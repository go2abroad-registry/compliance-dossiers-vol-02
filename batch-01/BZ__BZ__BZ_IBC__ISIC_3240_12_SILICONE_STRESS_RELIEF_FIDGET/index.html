Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service (Ministry of Finance, Government of Belize); online portal via https://taxservices.gov.bz

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities, including International Business Companies (IBCs), are not subject to income tax in Belize and are not required to obtain a domestic Tax Identification Number (TIN) for income tax purposes. For indirect tax purposes, an IBC that makes taxable supplies in Belize or engages in import transactions requiring customs clearance may be required to register for the General Sales Tax (GST) with the Belize Tax Service. The registration procedure entails filing Form GST-01 (Application for Registration), submitting a certified copy of the IBC certificate of incorporation, the IBC registered agent's letter, proof of registered office address, and a completed Business Profile form. Registration is done online through the Belize Tax Service portal. For customs purposes, the importer of record must obtain a Customs Declaration Number and may apply for a TIN from the Belize Tax Service where required for customs clearance. Exempt IBCs are expected to maintain records demonstrating their exempt status to justify non-registration for income tax, while still complying with any GST registration obligation should their taxable turnover exceed the registration threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Belize IBCs operate under the International Business Companies Act (Chapter 270). They are statutorily exempt from all Belize taxes (income tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, gift tax, and estate tax) for a period of 50 years from the date of incorporation. No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections are available for US federal tax purposes in Belize; an IBC would rely on its local statute to sustain its status as tax-exempt for Belizean tax purposes and may be treated as a corporation or disregarded entity under foreign tax law. There is no elective mechanism to alter the statutory exemption in Belize; an IBC cannot voluntarily elect to become a taxable Belizean domestic company unless it converts to an ordinary resident company under the Companies Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST registration is mandatory for businesses with an annual taxable turnover exceeding BZD 100,000 (Belize Dollars). For non-resident suppliers, a physical nexus or economic presence in Belize is required to create a GST registration obligation; supplies of goods made to Belizean consumers from abroad without a local establishment generally do not trigger local GST registration, but imports into Belize are subject to GST at the applicable rate and customs duties may be payable independently.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Belize General Sales Tax applies to the export of goods from Belize, directly exported by the supplier outside the country, and to supplies of internationally rendered services (services supplied to a non-resident recipient and delivered outside Belize in a manner unrelated to Belizean land or goods). For an IBC manufacturing and exporting food-grade silicone tactile sensory fidgets, the export of finished goods sold to foreign customers is zero-rated provided the exporter retains proof that the goods were physically removed from Belize and delivered to a foreign destination. Manufacturing inputs purchased locally are generally subject to GST at 12.5%, with the exporter entitled to claim input tax credits on the purchase of raw materials, consumables, and packaging exclusively used for the production of zero-rated exports. The export must be made directly by the registered taxable person; selling to a local intermediary who subsequently exports would not qualify for zero-rating unless the intermediary is also a registered GST person and the supply is treated as a taxable supply followed by the exporter's own zero-rated export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (submitted via ASYCUDA World)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Belize
  • Commercial Invoice reflecting FOB value and export terms
  • Packing List with commodity description, HS Code (HS 3926 for silicone articles, subheading for toys), and quantity
  • Certificate of Origin (optional but recommended for preferential treatment)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank statement or SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer)

Belize does not operate a national continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime. There is no API-based mandatory e-invoice clearance or digital stamping requirement for business-to-business transactions. However, GST-registered businesses are required to issue a standard tax invoice containing specified details (seller name and GST registration number, buyer name, date, description, quantity, price, and GST amount). Records and fiscal documentation must be retained. For import transactions, an electronic customs declaration is submitted through the Belize Customs and Excise Department's ASYCUDA World system, which processes import and export declarations electronically and assigns a customs declaration number. There is no mandatory blockchain-based stamping or SII-style real-time invoice clearance obligation.

Failure to substantiate an export zero-rating claim may result in disqualification of the zero-rated treatment and assessment of GST at 12.5% on the exports, together with interest at the statutory rate (12% per annum on underpaid tax) and penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded if fraud or intent to evade tax is established. Belizean customs law provides penalties for false export declarations, including confiscation of goods, monetary penalties up to three times the value of the goods, and imprisonment in serious cases. For IBCs, breach of the condition of export (such as diversion of goods back into the domestic market) would trigger a clawback of input tax credits previously claimed and may expose the IBC to loss of its exempt status for that transaction. The Ministry of Finance and the Belize Tax Service conduct post-clearance audits with a 6-year record retention obligation; fraudulent claims carry criminal penalties including fines of up to BZD 25,000 and imprisonment of up to 3 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belize dollar (BZD) is pegged to the United States dollar at BZD 2.00 = USD 1.00 with a +/-2% band maintained by the CBB under the Fixed Exchange Rate arrangements set out in the Central Bank of Belize Act. Belize maintains liberal current-account convertibility for resident transactions routed through authorized dealers (licensed commercial banks) subject to documentary support (invoices, customs declarations); capital-account transactions by Belize residents require CBB exchange-control approval. An International Business Company (IBC) incorporated under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270) is statutorily treated as a non-resident for exchange-control purposes and is therefore wholly outside the Exchange Control Act and the CBB's resident surrender and approval machinery; the IBC may freely hold multi-currency (predominantly USD) accounts, receive export proceeds, remit dividends, intragroup loans, and repatriate capital without CBB approval or withholding tax. IBCs are exempt from all Belize taxes, including income tax, capital gains tax, estate duty, inheritance tax, gift tax, and stamp duty (Cap. 270, s. 92); profit remittance hurdles are therefore de minimis. The primary practical friction is evidentiary: the IBC must maintain a current Certificate of Non-Resident Status/Incumbency issued by its registered agent and must avoid carrying on business with Belize residents or holding Belize real estate, as either act would trigger exchange-control, stamp-duty, and potential onshore tax exposure. Foreign-exchange transactions booked through the Belize banking system attract a 1.25% stamp duty on the sale/purchase of foreign currency for resident transactions; non-resident IBC accounts are outside this levy.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or conversion obligations apply to the IBC because it is classified as non-resident; export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in USD or any freely convertible currency and held in the IBC's offshore or Belize non-resident account without CBB permission. For a Belize-resident exporter (a structure this blueprint avoids), the Exchange Control Act and the CBB's Exchange Control (General) Regulations require the exporter to (i) obtain a Notice of Export/Export Declaration from the authorized dealer before shipment, (ii) collect proceeds from the foreign buyer within 180 days of shipment, and (iii) surrender the foreign currency to an authorized dealer for conversion into BZD at the official pegged rate, unless a specific exemption is granted. Because the IBC under the target commercial model (ISIC 3240 compression molding of silicone fidgets) is the principal seller to overseas distributors and platform marketplaces, the treasury architecture must book the export revenue at the IBC level as a non-resident sale; any Belize onshore tolling or logistics affiliate should invoice only domestic services in BZD and remain excluded from the export surrender regime. The board minutes and transfer-pricing documentation must confirm that the IBC assumes the commercial risk, controls the procurement and pricing, and executes all sales contracts offshore.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB), established under the Central Bank of Belize Act, Chapter 262 of the Laws of Belize; the CBB is the statutory exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act, Chapter 46, and administers the International Banking Act, Chapter 268, which governs the licensing of international banks authorized to accept non-resident foreign-currency deposits.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail; LOW as a primary treasury solution. Belize has no domestic e-money institution (EMI) licensing framework; the Central Bank of Belize Act and the International Banking Act reserve deposit-taking to licensed banks, so a Belize-licensed EMI does not exist. A regulated offshore EMI (e.g., a UK-authorised EMI under the PSD2 regime or an EU-authorised EMI with SWIFT/BIC access) is legally viable to receive Platform_Pay payouts (Amazon/Shopify marketplace settlement) in USD/EUR/GBP and to onward-transmit funds via MT103 to the IBC's Belize non-resident account. Legal constraints: (i) the IBC Act permits the IBC to conduct business freely outside Belize but prohibits carrying on business with Belize residents, so the EMI arrangement must be confined to non-resident collections; (ii) EMIs do not provide commercial LC, NDF hedging, or CBB-recognized authorized-dealer services; (iii) Belize banks will apply enhanced scrutiny to deposits originating from fintech wallets, requiring a definitive audit trail of marketplace settlement reports; and (iv) the IBC must not rely on a non-regulated wallet or on a crypto-adjacent fintech, because the low-risk standard classification (banking_aml_risk_tier: Low_Risk_Standard) would be degraded by unstructured third-party payer flows. The optimal architecture is a single treasury account at a licensed bank with the EMI used only as a per-market collection concentrator subject to daily sweep and full reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection must be executed by SWIFT MT103 from the contributing shareholder's own bank account (exact same remitter name as the IBC register) to the IBC's USD non-resident account at a Belize licensed bank, or to that bank's US correspondent using the correspondent ABA/routing number where the Belize bank maintains a US clearing presence. The wire instructions must state: (i) beneficiary styling exactly matching the IBC's legal name and the non-resident account number; (ii) a transparent purpose code - SWIFT category '109' (equity) or standard BOP code 'G110 - equity injection into a direct investment enterprise'; (iii) a reference field reading 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION - [SHAREHOLDER NAME] - RESOLUTION DATED [DD/MM/YYYY] - INVOICE/SUBSCRIPTION NO.'; and (iv) a short free-format narrative confirming the non-resident status of the IBC and that the remittance is not a Belize-source transaction. The bank will require, prior to posting credit, a KYC pack consisting of the share subscription agreement, the board resolution increasing issued capital, the incumbency certificate, and the source-of-funds documentation. No prior CBB approval is required for a non-resident IBC capital injection; however, if the remitter is a Belize resident or the funds originate from a Belize resident account, the CBB's exchange-control Form 10 procedure and a 1.25% stamp duty on foreign exchange would apply - such routing is strictly prohibited in this blueprint. After credit, the funds should be held in the capital-escrow sub-ledger and only transferred to the operational sub-ledger upon issuance of shares and filing of the register of members.The primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 from the IBC's non-resident USD account, cleared through the corresponding US or UK correspondent bank on a same-day or T+1 value basis. All supplier payments for food-grade silicone raw materials and compression molds must carry BOP purpose code 'B101 - Goods trade settlement' and a reference field quoting the purchase-order number and supplier invoice number to satisfy TBML screening. For large-value procurement (exceeding USD 50,000 or where the supplier requests bank-secured payment), the IBC should use a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by its Belize bank via SWIFT MT700, denominated strictly in USD, with confirmation at the advising bank (e.g., HSBC, Standard Chartered, or a tier-1 Chinese bank) to mitigate confirming-bank risk; Belize-dollar-denominated LCs are commercially unacceptable to Asian beneficiaries and must be avoided. For Platform_Pay rails (Amazon, Shopify, and similar marketplace settlements), the IBC must register its Belize non-resident USD account (or the regulated EMI collection account) as the payout destination and reconcile each deposit automatically against marketplace settlement reports; the treasury policy must mandate (i) a sub-USD 10,000 unconditional lower threshold for low-risk, pre-approved supplier disbursements, (ii) dual-signatory authorization for any payment above USD 10,000, and (iii) a prohibition on cash withdrawals, third-party beneficiary substitutions, and payments to Belize resident accounts. The IBC should also require its bank to assign a 'Commercial Marketplace' transaction profile and to maintain a watchlist of correspondent banks to ensure that all outflows are traceable via SWIFT GPI or equivalent. A daily liquidity report must project the USD net position across the operational and escrow sub-ledgers, with intraday credit lines (where granted by the Belize bank) used only to bridge timing mismatches between platform payouts and supplier payment deadlines.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) on BZD/USD: legally permissible for the non-resident IBC when executed with an investment-grade offshore dealer bank; in practice, availability is negligible because the BZD is hard-pegged at 2:1 within a +/-2% band and the CBB does not operate an active NDF market; the IBC should not rely on BZD hedging since all treasury flows are USD-denominated.Plain-vanilla FX forward contracts between USD and the revenue currencies of target markets (EUR, GBP, CAD, AUD): permitted when executed with the Belize bank's correspondent or with a licensed offshore dealer under an ISDA Master Agreement, and must be supported by a board resolution authorizing hedging-only (non-speculative) usage; settlement occurs by net delivery in USD against the underlying purchase order.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EUR or USD/CNY): permissible for the IBC where it holds multi-currency debt or long-dated procurement contracts with Chinese or European suppliers; requires an ISDA/CSA master agreement and an investment-grade counterparty, with the board documenting that the swap is a hedge of a specifically identified supply agreement and not a proprietary trading position.USD/CNY non-deliverable forward (NDF) for settlement of the Chinese contract-manufacturing 3240 supply chain: permissible with a qualified offshore bank, subject to the same evidenced-hedge requirement, and preferable to deliverable CNY forwards because the non-resident IBC does not maintain CNY clearing accounts.FX swap (swap of one leg of USD cash flow for EUR/GBP for platform-settlement conversion) is permissible as a linear, low-risk liquidity tool, but is restricted to matching known settlement cycles; options and exotic derivatives are expressly excluded from the approved treasury policy to preserve the Low_Risk_Standard AML tier and avoid speculation classifications.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail; LOW as a primary treasury solution. Belize has no domestic e-money institution (EMI) licensing framework; the Central Bank of Belize Act and the International Banking Act reserve deposit-taking to licensed banks, so a Belize-licensed EMI does not exist. A regulated offshore EMI (e.g., a UK-authorised EMI under the PSD2 regime or an EU-authorised EMI with SWIFT/BIC access) is legally viable to receive Platform_Pay payouts (Amazon/Shopify marketplace settlement) in USD/EUR/GBP and to onward-transmit funds via MT103 to the IBC's Belize non-resident account. Legal constraints: (i) the IBC Act permits the IBC to conduct business freely outside Belize but prohibits carrying on business with Belize residents, so the EMI arrangement must be confined to non-resident collections; (ii) EMIs do not provide commercial LC, NDF hedging, or CBB-recognized authorized-dealer services; (iii) Belize banks will apply enhanced scrutiny to deposits originating from fintech wallets, requiring a definitive audit trail of marketplace settlement reports; and (iv) the IBC must not rely on a non-regulated wallet or on a crypto-adjacent fintech, because the low-risk standard classification (banking_aml_risk_tier: Low_Risk_Standard) would be degraded by unstructured third-party payer flows. The optimal architecture is a single treasury account at a licensed bank with the EMI used only as a per-market collection concentrator subject to daily sweep and full reconciliation.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for International Business Companies (IBC) on all foreign-source income under s.75 IBC Act (Cap 270); domestic resident companies are taxable at 25% under the Income and Business Tax Act (Cap 55)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – dividends paid by an IBC to non-resident shareholders are wholly exempt from Belize withholding tax, with no notional WHT, no gross-up, and no filing obligation on the distribution

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File / Master File threshold exists in Belize law; best-practice threshold: prepare contemporaneous TP documentation once annual related-party procurement or resale flows for silicone fidget products exceed BZD 1,000,000 (approx. USD 500,000)

Economic substance scope: The Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 requires adequate premises, qualified employees, and annual expenditure in Belize only for listed relevant activities (banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, finance/leasing, headquarters, holding, IP holding, distribution and service centre). ISIC 3240 silicone-product manufacturing is not a listed activity; however, an IBC that acts as a purchasing/distribution hub for related fidget-toy companies may be recharacterized as a 'distribution and service centre' and must then satisfy the full substance test or face escalating penalties up to BZD 50,000 and strike-off

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham and Conduit Structuring

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at the 25% corporate rate plus interest and penalties; the general anti-avoidance provisions authorize the Tax Service to disregard the IBC and tax the economic owner on a look-through basis

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally assessable where assets are stripped or dividends improperly paid; criminal tax evasion conviction carries fines up to BZD 20,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years
Prohibited Domestic Trading / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Forfeiture of IBC exempt status, revocation of registration, fine up to BZD 10,000 under s.75(4) IBC Act, and strike-off with all assets vesting in the Registrar

Director personal liability: Directors who continue trading after strike-off become personally liable for all post-strike-off contract debts; court-ordered travel restrictions and asset freezes may be imposed in connected AML proceedings
Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Escalating administrative fines of approximately BZD 10,000 (first offence), BZD 25,000 (second), and BZD 50,000 (third); persistent non-compliance triggers automatic strike-off and exchange of information with OECD foreign tax authorities

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil recovery in Belize courts for unpaid penalties; each officer is subject to cumulative daily fines for continuing failure to file the beneficial ownership register
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the AML/CFT Act and IBC (Amendment) Act 2017: fine of BZD 10,000 plus BZD 1,000 per day of continued default; the Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze transactions and issue production orders against bank accounts

Director personal liability: Directors risk up to 3 years imprisonment for money-laundering predicate offences; cross-border fund movement linked to the evasion exposes directors to travel bans, extradition, and disqualification orders
