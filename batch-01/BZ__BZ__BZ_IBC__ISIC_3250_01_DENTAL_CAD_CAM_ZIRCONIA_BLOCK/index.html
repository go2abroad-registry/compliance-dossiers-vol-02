Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Services Department (TSD), Ministry of Finance, Government of Belize; Office of the Commissioner of Revenue; General Sales Tax (GST) administered by the TSD/GST Unit; export customs control by the Belize Customs and Excise Department. Corporate registry for IBCs: Belize Financial Services Commission (BFSC), Companies and Corporate Affairs Registry. Electronic portals: taxservices.gov.bz / etax.belize.gov.bz for tax filings, ASYCUDA World for customs declarations.

E-government filing portal

For a Belize International Business Company (IBC) incorporated under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270), there is no statutory obligation to obtain a Belize TIN because the IBC is exempt from income tax, business tax, capital gains tax, and GST on offshore operations. Procedure where a foreign-owned manufacturer needs a Belize tax profile for export operations: (1) incorporate via a licensed registered agent at the BFSC Companies Registry and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation; (2) if the IBC engages in any registrable activity, physical trade, or customs transactions in Belize, apply for registration with the Tax Services Department using the prescribed business/TIN registration form and evidence of incorporation; (3) register for Belize General Sales Tax if annual taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold or if voluntary registration is sought to recover input GST; (4) obtain a Customs Trader Registration and importer/exporter code from the Belize Customs and Excise Department; (5) obtain the municipal Trade License if a physical place of business or manufacturing facility is operated in Belize, notwithstanding the IBC's tax-exempt status; (6) for controlled goods such as medical/dental devices, obtain any health-related export/marketing authorization from the Ministry of Health and Wellness where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Belize IBC law contains no U.S.-style check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch election; the tax exemption is statutory and automatic under the IBC Act. Available elections and elective routes: (a) voluntary registration for General Sales Tax under the GST Act (Cap. 65) by a person making zero-rated exported supplies, enabling input GST credit recovery; (b) election under the IBC Act to convert, continue, or redomicile an IBC into a Belize domestic company under the Companies Act, thereby losing IBC tax-exempt status and becoming subject to Belize Business Tax and income tax on Belize-source income; (c) election by an IBC to carry on specified business activities with Belize residents after obtaining ministerial approval, which may expose limited activities to local taxation; (d) election to operate under the Export Processing Zones Act where the physical manufacturing site is located in a designated export processing zone, granting additional fiscal incentives; (e) do-nothing default: full statutory exemption from all Belize national taxes, including income, business, and GST, when the IBC conducts only offshore business outside Belize.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration applies to any person making taxable supplies in Belize exceeding BZ$100,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is available below the threshold and is relevant to an exporter of zero-rated goods wishing to recover input GST. Exports-only manufacturers whose supplies are wholly zero-rated are not compelled to charge GST on exported dental zirconia blocks but may apply for voluntary GST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported bioglass/zirconia dental CAD/CAM blocks qualify for zero rating under the General Sales Tax Act on the following cumulative criteria: (1) the goods are physically exported from Belize by the IBC within the time required by law after the supply — not later than 90 days unless extended by the Commissioner; (2) the sale is made to a non-resident purchaser outside Belize; (3) legal and beneficial ownership passes to the overseas buyer and the goods do not return to Belize, are not consumed in Belize, and are not used for a taxable purpose in Belize; (4) the exporter retains contemporaneous documentary evidence of physical export comprising the customs export declaration, transport document, commercial invoice, packing list, and proof of payment in a foreign currency; (5) for offshore services or sales processed through Belize, benefits of the zero rate extend only to supplies directly attributable to the exported goods, not to local ancillary services; (6) where the goods are supplied to a related offshore distributor, the exporter must demonstrate arm's-length pricing and economic substance for the transaction to withstand audit scrutiny.

  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing physical removal of the dental zirconia blocks from Belize
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Belize IBC to the overseas dental laboratory or distributor

  • Proof of foreign currency receipt — bank inward remittance advice, SWIFT MT103, or telegraphic transfer credit note to the IBC's Belize bank account

Belize does not currently mandate real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal device stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance. GST-registered persons must issue prescribed GST invoices containing the supplier's TIN, customer name/address, description of goods, price exclusive/inclusive of GST, and the GST amount. Monthly GST returns are filed electronically through the Belize Tax Services Department portal by the 15th day of the following month. All export declarations and customs clearances must be lodged electronically through ASYCUDA World (Automated System for Customs Data), including the Single Administrative Document (SAD/CusDec), with electronic risk assessment and customs release. No digital stamping of export documents is required.

The Commissioner of Revenue may audit zero-rated export claims for up to six years. Penalties for incorrect or fraudulent export zero-rating are severe: (a) misdescription or false declaration in customs documents attracts seizure and forfeiture of the goods and a fine of up to twice the customs value of the goods in addition to the assessed GST; (b) failure to substantiate physical export within the statutory period triggers a retrospective assessment of 12.5% GST on the gross invoice value plus interest at the statutory rate and a late payment penalty of 10% of the unpaid tax; (c) fraudulent evasion of GST, deliberate falsification of export documents, or collusive recycling of goods into Belize is a criminal offense punishable by a fine of up to BZ$25,000, imprisonment of up to two years, or both, together with clawback of all zero-rated relief and denial of input credit recovery; (d) failure of the exporter to repatriate and convert export proceeds into Belize dollars within 180 days may be treated by the Central Bank of Belize and the Tax Services Department as an incomplete export transaction, leading to reversal of the zero rating, assessment of GST at 12.5%, additional business tax penalties, and suspension or cancellation of customs trader privileges; (e) related-party export schemes lacking economic substance, arm's-length pricing, or proper board documentation are scrutinized as abusive arrangements and recharacterized as taxable domestic supplies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belize Dollar (BZD) operates under a fixed exchange-rate regime pegged to the U.S. Dollar at BZD 2.00 = USD 1.00, with the CBB maintaining the peg through a currency-board-style supply mechanism. Belize has accepted Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement, meaning current-account convertibility is unrestricted for residents and non-residents. Capital-account transactions are administratively controlled for Belize residents under the Exchange Control Act; however, the target entity, formed as a Belize International Business Company (IBC) under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270), is expressly carved out of the Exchange Control Act and enjoys unrestricted USD/EUR/GBP denominated current- and capital-account operations. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is legally unrestricted for IBCs provided that the IBC does not carry on business with Belize residents, does not hold real property in Belize, and maintains its accounting records in a foreign currency. In practical banking terms, the remaining hurdles are documentary rather than statutory: the corporate bank may require a board resolution approving dividend distribution, a solvency certificate, and a CRS/FATCA self-certification proving that the beneficial owners are tax-resident outside Belize. If the IBC were to convert to a Belize-resident operating company, the full exchange-control regime would attach, including CBB approval for outward capital remittances and mandatory conversion of export proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act and the CBB Exchange Control (Export) Regulations, a resident exporter must repatriate export proceeds within 180 days from the date of shipment and must surrender to an authorized dealer a prescribed portion (administratively applied at 50% for exports to non-CARICOM destinations and generally nil for CARICOM intra-regional exports) by selling that portion of the foreign currency to the commercial bank in exchange for BZD at the official peg rate. A Belize IBC is statutorily exempt from these surrender and repatriation rules for its entire corporate life, provided it operates outside the domestic Belizean economy and keeps no BZD functional currency. Therefore, the BZ_IBC engaged in manufacturing and exporting dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks (HS 6909.90 / HS 9018.49) has no statutory obligation to convert export proceeds into Belize Dollars and no 180-day encashment deadline. The IBC should, however, maintain an internal treasury policy confirming that all export sales are invoiced in USD/EUR and settled through non-resident correspondent clearing to preserve the exemption.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB), operating under the Central Bank of Belize Act (Cap. 61) and the Exchange Control Act (Cap. 62). The CBB is the sole exchange-control authority and the supervisor of all authorized dealers (commercial banks) in Belize.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

LOW-TO-MODERATE. Belize does not issue electronic-money-institution (EMI) licenses, and no deposit-taking fintech is licensed by the CBB. Offshore digital EMIs generally decline onboarding for Belize IBCs because of (i) correspondent-bank aversion to IBC privacy features, (ii) the absence of a real-time BZD USD settlement rail, and (iii) OFAC/SDN screening friction on Belize-registered corporate structures. Globally recognized EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) list Belize only sporadically; where onboarding is offered, account limits are restricted and USD clearing occurs via third-party correspondent banks in the United States, creating a two-hop wire risk and potential freeze exposure under U.S. anti-money-laundering review. Legal constraints stem from the Electronic Transactions Act (Cap. 235A) and the Banking Act (Cap. 101): a foreign EMI receiving deposits from Belize residents would require a CBB license, so an EMI may only be used to disburse the IBC's own funds to suppliers, never to collect funds from Belize-domiciled customers. Feasibility rating: MODERATE for payables-only disbursement via a fully licensed EU/UK EMI, and LOW for receivables concentration. Primary clearing should remain at Caye International Bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant inbound capital-injection rail is a SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer from the non-resident shareholder's bank directly to the IBC's USD operational account at Caye International Bank (or Heritage/Atlantic as secondary). Because Belize imposes no statutory wire-purpose code list on IBCs, the decisive compliance element is the structured remittance-information field (field 70): the narrative must read: 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION / CAPITAL CALL BR#2025-001 / DBCM BZ__BZ__BZ_IBC__ISIC_3250_01 / SHARE ISSUANCE AGAINST DENTAL ZIRCONIA BLOCK MFG'. If the correspondent intermediary bank (typically JPMorgan Chase or Bank of America in New York, or a U.S. Federal Reserve member wire bank) requires an IMF Balance-of-Payments purpose classification, the remitter should tag the wire as 'T0111' for equity injection or 'T0103' for intercompany shareholder loans. The receiving bank must pre-register the anticipated wire, and the capital injection must be accompanied by: (i) the board resolution approving the capital call; (ii) an updated register of members after issuance; (iii) a copy of the share certificate; (iv) for shareholder loans, an executed intercompany loan agreement with an arm's-length interest-rate index (e.g., SOFR + 250 basis points) and an agreed maturity date to avoid recharacterization as permanent equity; and (v) a certified source-of-funds statement. All inbound wires must be marked STP-eligible to prevent any downstream Belize-clearing correspondent from auto-converting USD into BZD and thereby triggering exchange-control overspill.Outbound disbursement rails are structured in a three-layer hierarchy. (1) Direct supplier payments: SWIFT MT103 from the IBC's Caye International Bank USD account to any global beneficiary, with field 70 containing the purchase-order number and commercial invoice reference; used for routine zirconium powder, milling-tool, sintering-furnace spare parts, and dental-lab consumable purchases below USD 25,000. (2) High-value trade settlements: commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP600 from the IBC's bank (issuing bank) in favor of the German, Japanese, or South Korean supplier of CAD/CAM blank blanks, confirmed by an international advising bank, payable at sight against presentation of commercial invoice, ISO 6872 conformance certificate, packing list, and transport document; this rail is mandatory for any single transaction above USD 100,000 to non-CARICOM counterparties. (3) Telegraphic Transfer (TT) payables and freight: same-day online-banking TT payments from the Caye International Bank portal for freight-forwarder charges, customs broker fees, and minor operational expenses; each TT must be matched to an already-registered supplier file in the bank's trade platform. The IBC's two-director signor matrix must be enforced on all MT103/LC outbound instructions above the USD 5,000 equivalent. MT202 COV is used exclusively for interbank cover payments when the beneficiary's bank is not a SWIFT BIC member or when requested by the receiving correspondent; the IBC should avoid direct MT202 for trade payments.Outright USD/EUR forward contract (deliverable) — used to hedge EUR-invoiced receivables from European dental laboratories while paying USD-denominated raw materials; tenor 30 to 180 days; executed with Caye International Bank's treasury desk or a U.S. correspondent under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) on USD/CNY — used exclusively for hedging Chinese zirconium-oxide feedstock invoices where physical CNY settlement is prohibited; executed with a licensed global bank (e.g., HSBC or Standard Chartered) clearing through the offshore CNH market, confirmed as non-deliverable and settled in USD.Cross-currency swap (USD/EUR) — permitted for six-to-eighteen-month tenors to convert the economic burden of EUR-denominated distributor contracts into synthetic USD exposure; requires board-approved treasury hedge policy and audited IBC financial statements; collateralized at 105%.BZD/USD currency swap — technically permissible but economically redundant because of the fixed 2:1 peg; the treasury blueprint prohibits entering BZD positions to avoid creating accidental onshore status.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: LOW-TO-MODERATE. Belize does not issue electronic-money-institution (EMI) licenses, and no deposit-taking fintech is licensed by the CBB. Offshore digital EMIs generally decline onboarding for Belize IBCs because of (i) correspondent-bank aversion to IBC privacy features, (ii) the absence of a real-time BZD USD settlement rail, and (iii) OFAC/SDN screening friction on Belize-registered corporate structures. Globally recognized EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) list Belize only sporadically; where onboarding is offered, account limits are restricted and USD clearing occurs via third-party correspondent banks in the United States, creating a two-hop wire risk and potential freeze exposure under U.S. anti-money-laundering review. Legal constraints stem from the Electronic Transactions Act (Cap. 235A) and the Banking Act (Cap. 101): a foreign EMI receiving deposits from Belize residents would require a CBB license, so an EMI may only be used to disburse the IBC's own funds to suppliers, never to collect funds from Belize-domiciled customers. Feasibility rating: MODERATE for payables-only disbursement via a fully licensed EU/UK EMI, and LOW for receivables concentration. Primary clearing should remain at Caye International Bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a Belize International Business Company (IBC Act section 4 exemption); otherwise the general Belize resident corporate rate under the Income and Business Tax Act is 25%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BZ$4,000,000 (approximately USD 2,000,000) in cross-border controlled transactions is the practical file maintenance threshold; Belize legislation codifies no formal Local File or Master File threshold, and OECD Country-by-Country reporting only applies when a parent multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds USD 850,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (International Business Companies) Act 2021 applies economic substance requirements to all Belize IBCs. Pure equity holding companies face reduced substance obligations, while entities conducting IP holding, distribution and service center, financing, or headquarters activities must prove sufficient employees, premises, and management in Belize proportionate to the relevant income. A dental ceramic block trading company holding intangibles in Belize without local activity fails the doctrine and forfeits its exempt status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of the tax evaded, interest at 1% per month on the undercharged Income and Business Tax, criminal fine up to BZ$10,000, confiscation of the proceeds of the dental block trade, and compulsory strike-off by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax; upon conviction they face imprisonment up to 2 years, court-imposed travel bans, and a 10-year disqualification from managing Belize registered entities.
Shell Company / Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of BZ$50,000, daily default penalty of BZ$1,000, automatic exchange of information with foreign tax authorities, deregistration, forfeiture of the corporate name, and mandatory restoration costs before any reinstatement.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced, exposing directors and shareholders to personal liability for the penalties; the court may issue a travel ban, civil asset freeze, a 10-year ban on incorporation, and up to 5 years imprisonment for knowingly filing false substance returns.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Unit can impose fines up to BZ$500,000; banking partners may close accounts; the Proceeds of Crime Act allows seizure of bank balances, milling equipment, and inventory traced to laundering; corporate dissolution follows non-payment.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers with knowledge face criminal liability of up to 14 years imprisonment, personal fines up to BZ$250,000, asset confiscation, travel bans, and extradition for cross-border laundering in connection with the dental consumables trade.
