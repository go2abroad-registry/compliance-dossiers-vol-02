Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Belize maintains the Belize Dollar (BZD) at a fixed official parity of BZD 2.00 = USD 1.00 and has accepted IMF Article VIII current-account convertibility; current-account payments are unrestricted. A Belize International Business Company (IBC) is carved out of the Exchange Control Act by section 14 (Exchange-Control Exemption) of the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270): an IBC may hold, credit, debit, and remit any currency without CBB prior approval and is treated as a non-resident for exchange-control purposes. Dividends, director fees, interest, and loan repayments may be repatriated to non-resident shareholders free of Belize withholding tax and without profit-remittance licensing because the IBC is tax-exempt. The practical constraint is bank-level: correspondent banks and US clearing agents require documentary proof of lawful source of funds, genuine trade substance in wholesale optical distribution, and a clean chain of title to inventory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act, a resident exporter must offer all foreign-currency export proceeds to an authorized dealer within a prescribed surrender window, with conversion to BZD generally mandatory. By statutory exemption, an IBC is not a 'resident' for exchange-control purposes; therefore, USD proceeds from the wholesale international distribution of acetate eyeglass frames are not subject to any surrender ratio, BZD conversion mandate, or CBB retention timeline. To preserve the exemption, the IBC must (i) maintain clearly designated non-resident USD accounts, (ii) never invoice in BZD, and (iii) ensure its records demonstrate that sales are executed through the international wholesale conduit; any onshore BZD-denominated sale to a Belizean buyer would re-expose the funds to the resident surrender regime.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB) is the monetary authority and exchange-control administrator under the Central Bank of Belize Act (Cap. 262) and the Exchange Control Act (Cap. 122). The CBB licenses banks under the Financial Institutions Act and the International Banking Act, issues prudential AML/CFT directives, supervises foreign-currency operations, and operates the BZD intervention peg. The International Financial Services Commission (IFSC) separately supervises registered agents and international financial services providers; both authorities coordinate on beneficial-ownership transparency and cross-border reporting.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No transfer pricing documentation threshold applies to a Belize IBC because the IBC is fully tax-exempt and outside the Belize corporate income tax base. Any uncontrolled interaction with a Belize taxable entity or a foreign related party is nonetheless expected to be supported by written contracts, board resolutions, and real economic flows if challenged by a foreign tax authority. Belize has not issued a specific local threshold in Belize currency for transfer pricing documentation.

Economic substance scope: Belize has not enacted a separate Economic Substance Regulation analogous to the BVI, Bermuda, or Cayman rules. However, Belize tax authorities may invoke the general anti-abuse doctrine and the central management and control test. Therefore the IBC should not be maintained as a pure shell. Even in an offshore wholesale eyeglass frames business, the IBC must be prepared to demonstrate real operational substance where it is used: contracts with the principal, a physical operational office in a jurisdiction outside Belize or validly outsourced functions, bank accounts, employee expenses, and board meeting minutes. A completely bare nominee IBC with no substantive activity is a fatal compliance red flag under international AML and tax transparency standards.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Exemption

Statutory sanction: Under the Belize Income Tax Act and the International Business Companies Act, the IBC may be assessed on 100% of its Belize-source income, plus penalties of 200% of the unpaid tax. The Registrar may revoke the IBC's tax exemption, and civil penalties up to BZ$20,000 may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, and liquidators who knowingly made false declarations become jointly and severally liable for restitution of unpaid tax and penalties. The Belize Supreme Court may issue a travel ban against directors who are judgment debtors. Fraudulent trading can result in personal civil liability without limit, disqualification from managing Belize companies, and, in egregious cases, criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Belize Financial Services Commission and the Registrar may treat the IBC as an inactive or non-operational shell. The IBC may be struck off, the registered agent may resign, and bank accounts may be closed under AML due diligence rules. Under the Money Laundering and Terrorism (Prevention) Act, administrative fines may be imposed on the corporate service provider and the company.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow a shell IBC to trade without oversight or sufficient records may be personally liable to external creditors if they acted in bad faith. The registered agent may file an AML report, and regulators may hold directors liable for failing to maintain accurate financial books and audit trails required by the International Financial Reporting Standards.
AML and Money Laundering Infractions

Statutory sanction: Belize's Money Laundering and Terrorism (Prevention) Act imposes criminal sanctions of up to seven years' imprisonment and fines up to BZ$500,000 for each offence. Corporate assets tied to money laundering are subject to confiscation under the Proceeds of Crime Act, including funds held in Belize banks.

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in laundering funds are personally criminally liable; foreign directors are exposed to Interpol red notices and arrest upon entering Belize or jurisdictions with mutual legal assistance treaties. Directors may also be subject to civil liability for knowingly allowing the company to facilitate crimes and for misrepresentation of the beneficial owner register.
Failure to File Annual Return and Striking Off

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may strike the IBC off the register under the IBC Act. The company's assets may vest in the Registrar and become forfeited to the Crown after the statutory time limit. Late penalties accrue at US$50 per month and the IBC loses its Certificate of Good Standing, making all foreign bank accounts and trade arrangements unusable.

Director personal liability: Once an IBC is struck off, directors remain personally responsible for obligations accrued before dissolution if they continued to trade after the company ceased to be in good standing. Financial institutions may blacklist the directors, and the Registrar may refuse future directorships in Belize, with the practical effect of a permanent travel restriction and banking embargo.
