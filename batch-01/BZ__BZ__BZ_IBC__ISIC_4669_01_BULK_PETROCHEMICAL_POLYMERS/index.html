Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service (BTS) under the Ministry of Finance, Government of Belize; official portal: https://www.belizetaxservice.gov.bz (electronic filing and registration via https://etax.belizetaxservice.gov.bz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Belize International Business Company (IBC) is generally exempt from national income tax and does not require a standard TIN for income tax purposes. However, for indirect tax (GST) and customs purposes, the entity must obtain a General Sales Tax (GST) Registration Number by filing Form GST-001 (Application for Registration) with the Belize Tax Service, accompanied by the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, proof of registered office address in Belize, and a completed Business Information Sheet. Foreign entities without a local establishment may still register for GST if they make taxable supplies in Belize. Non-resident entities that are not required to register for GST may obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) for customs clearance via the Belize Customs Department's Single Administrative Document (SAD) process. The Belize Tax Service issues a Taxpayer Identification Number for administrative purposes upon application, typically within 5-10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

International Business Companies (IBCs) under the International Business Companies Act, Chapter 270 of the Laws of Belize are exempt from Belize income tax, stamp duty, capital gains tax, and all other direct taxes for their offshore business activities. IBCs may elect, under Section 124 of the IBC Act, to be treated as a domestic company for tax purposes by filing a formal election with the Registrar of International Business Companies and the Belize Tax Service; such election triggers liability to Belize income tax on worldwide income. There is no general check-the-box entity classification regime akin to the US IRS; however, for Belizean tax purposes, an IBC may elect to convert to a domestic company under the Companies Act (domestication) by filing Articles of Continuance. For withholding tax and hybrid mismatch purposes, Belize has not enacted specific hybrid mismatch election rules under the OECD BEPS framework; entities may structure financing instruments and transfer pricing policies to avoid double non-taxation, subject to general anti-avoidance provisions under the Income Tax Act (Cap. 55).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

BZD 75,000 (Belize Dollars) of annual taxable turnover (taxable supplies) as the mandatory registration threshold. Persons whose annual taxable turnover exceeds BZD 75,000 must register for GST. Voluntary registration is permitted for persons making taxable supplies below the threshold or making zero-rated supplies. For cross-border digital or off-shore services, a foreign supplier may be required to register if the total value of taxable supplies to persons in Belize exceeds the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

100% of export sales of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules may be zero-rated for GST provided: (1) the exporter holds a valid GST registration number; (2) the goods are exported from Belize within 28 days after the date of supply (or within a longer period approved by the Commissioner); (3) the supplier retains documentary evidence of export as prescribed under Regulation 17 of the GST Regulations; and (4) the sale is made under a contract for the sale of goods where property passes to a buyer outside Belize. A Belize IBC that purchases goods from foreign producers and sells them offshore without physical import into Belize is entirely outside the Belize GST regime (no supply occurs in Belize), so no GST applies. If the IBC physically imports goods into Belize for temporary storage/repackaging and then re-exports, the import GST may be suspended under the customs warehousing regime, and the subsequent export is zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUSEXDEC) via ASYCUDA World, signed by the exporter or authorised agent
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment to the overseas buyer
  • Commercial Invoice showing the actual transaction value and noting the zero-rating
  • Packing List detailing product description, polymer grade, weights, and marks
  • Proof of export within the statutory 28-day window (e.g., port exit stamp, carrier receipt, or terminal log)
  • Foreign remittance / bank transfer advice or Letter of Credit realisation evidencing receipt of payment
  • Certificate of Origin (when required by buyer or for preferential tariff treatment)

Belize does not currently mandate real-time electronic invoicing or continuous fiscal clearance (no SAP-TMS-like model). GST-registered persons must issue tax invoices containing specified particulars (name, address, GST registration number, invoice date, description, quantity, price, tax amount) and retain records for at least six years. Electronic invoices are permitted, but there is no government-run e-invoicing clearance platform or digital stamping (RFID) requirement. Customs import/export declarations are filed electronically via the Belize Customs ASYCUDA World system, which functions as the fiscal clearance mechanism for cross-border goods. No API-based real-time tax clearance is required for domestic sales; GST returns are filed monthly or quarterly via the Belize Tax Service e-Tax portal.

The Belize Tax Service may scrutinise export zero-rating claims retrospectively for up to six years from the filing date. Where a zero-rating claim is disallowed because the exporter failed to provide sufficient documentary evidence, the GST becomes immediately payable at 12.5% together with interest at 3% above the prevailing bank rate, and a civil penalty of up to 150% of the tax due may apply under Section 77 of the GST Act. Fraudulent zero-rating claims (including false export declarations or fictitious invoices) are subject to criminal prosecution: fines up to BZD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals, and for companies a fine up to BZD 100,000, plus forfeiture of the goods. The Commissioner may also revoke the GST registration, enforce a security bond, and impose a 50% surcharge for late payment. Additionally, anti-money-laundering (AML) obligations under the AML/CFT regime require the IBC to maintain full audit trail of export proceeds; failure to repatriate foreign exchange within 180 days does not automatically invalidate the zero-rating, but the Central Bank of Belize may impose exchange-control penalties up to 10% of the un-repatriated amount and require written justification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Belize maintains a formally pegged exchange rate regime at BZD 2.00 = USD 1.00. Capital-account convertibility is liberal for non-resident entities: the International Business Company (IBC), being deemed non-resident for exchange-control purposes, may freely maintain and operate USD-denominated accounts, receive foreign equity/debt capital, and remit dividends and repatriated capital without CBB pre-approval. Current-account convertibility is unrestricted for IBCs provided no business is transacted with Belize residents and no domestic real property or prohibited local activity is undertaken. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal: an IBC must satisfy the Central Bank's 'economic substance-lite' expectations through documented corporate resolutions, audited or management accounts, and evidence that funds originate from genuine international trade, thereby avoiding constructive-residency triggers under the Income and Business Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters are statutorily required to repatriate export proceeds through an authorized dealer within 90 days of shipment and to surrender convertible foreign currency to the banking system subject to authorized-dealer conversion ratios; no statutory obligation to convert export receipts exists for BZD-domiciled resident traders beyond the 100% surrender policy. A Belize IBC is classified as non-resident for exchange-control purposes and is therefore exempt from surrender and mandatory conversion requirements: all export proceeds and trade receivables from the bulk polyethylene and polypropylene trading operation may be retained offshore or in designated USD commercial accounts, converted at the pegged rate if needed, and deployed freely for supply-chain payments without CBB surrender directives.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB) acting as the monetary authority and exchange control administrator, with the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) of Belize supervising AML/CFT compliance for banks and licensed financial institutions pursuant to the Banks and Financial Institutions Act and the AML/CFT Act framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Belize has no statutory EMI or digital-bank licensing category; offshore digital payment platforms (Wise, Mercury, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not recognized by the Central Bank of Belize as authorized dealers and cannot provide Belize-resident account relationships in the local regulatory sense. A Belize IBC may technically access offshore EMI rails for settlement, but such accounts carry high reputational and compliance risk: they typically require the IBC's existence certificate and UBO declarations, are subject to restrictive transactional thresholds, and may reject petrochemical-related trade flows due to sector-risk policies. Primary corporate treasury operations should therefore be anchored in a licensed Belize or international-license bank with direct SWIFT participation; EMI rails should be used only as secondary disbursement channels with pre-cleared source-of-funds documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound US Dollar (USD) capital injections from shareholders are routed via SWIFT MT103 credit transfers through the receiving Belize bank's correspondent bank (typically JPMorgan Chase or Bank of America in New York). Remittance instruction must state the purpose code 'Equity contribution to International Business Company' or 'Shareholder loan' and the IBC's corporate registration number; the bank will require a certified board resolution, shareholder resolution, and source-of-funds statement for any amount exceeding USD 50,000. Funds must originate from the shareholder's own bank account in the declared jurisdiction; for third-party funders, a notarized declaration identifying the beneficial source of funds and the absence of any PEP or sanctions nexus is mandated. No CBB exchange-control approval is required for inbound non-resident capital into an IBC operational or escrow account, but banks apply enhanced due diligence on wire originators and may request the original subscription agreement or loan agreement to classify the inflow as equity versus intercompany payable.Outbound disbursements to foreign polymer suppliers are cleared primarily through Irrevocable Documentary Letters of Credit (Commercial LC) issued by the Belize bank via SWIFT MT700, with MT400 amendment and MT754 acknowledgement rails supporting the LC lifecycle; sight or usance LCs at 30-90 days after bill of lading date are recommended for bulk resin procurement. Direct supplier prepayments or milestone payments are executed by SWIFT MT103 wires with the payment narrative referencing the sales contract number, invoice number, and Incoterm (CIF or FOB); the bank will require the presentation documents under the LC (commercial invoice, clean on-board bill of lading, packing list, certificate of analysis) before releasing funding. The rails must be structured so that title-document control remains with the Belize IBC until payment takes place, using 'against documents' settlement to prevent advance-funding risk; for high-volume same-supplier flows, a revolving LC facility or standby LC in favor of the supplier may be established under the bank's trade-finance limits.Plain-vanilla USD/BZD currency forwards contracted through international dealing desks and cleared via the Belize authorized dealerNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) on the BZD with offshore banks, given the fixed 2.00 peg, used to hedge BZD-denominated overhead exposureCross-currency swaps (USD/BZD) with tenor up to 12 months, permissible only where the counterparty is a licensed international bank or a qualified offshore financial institutionOption-based structures (call/put on USD/BZD) via investment-grade international banks, subject to board-level derivative authorization and collateral posting in USDCommodity-linked hedging, including forward fixed-price polymer purchase contracts with suppliers, which are permissible as commercial hedges distinct from financial FX derivatives and require no CBB license

0% for Belize IBCs (wholly exempt under the International Business Companies Act, Cap. 270, s. 5); the domestic Belize corporate income tax headline rate of 25% does not apply to an IBC in good standing.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Belize IBCs pay no withholding tax on dividends declared to non-resident shareholders; the distribution is wholly outside the Belize income tax net (IBC Act Cap. 270 s. 5(1)(c); Income Tax Act Cap. 58 s. 85 treats IBC distributions as non-Belize-source).

Form ESR-1 must be submitted within 12 months after the end of the IBC's financial period; audited financial statements are required for distribution and service centre relevant activities.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File/Master File threshold exists in Belize for IBCs as there is no dedicated transfer pricing law; however, the Belize Tax Service and ESR regime impose a de facto documentation threshold: any IBC engaged in intercompany polymer purchase or sale transactions exceeding BZD 1,000,000 (approx. USD 500,000) per reporting year must maintain contemporaneous TP documentation (functional analysis, benchmark study, and intercompany agreements) to satisfy the arm's-length condition of the Economic Substance Act. Prudent practice requires commencement of documentation at BZD 500,000 (USD 250,000).

Economic substance scope: The ESR Act 2019 applies to all IBCs carrying on 'Relevant Activities', including 'Distribution and Service Centre'. For the BZ_IBC bulk petrochemical trading entity, the doctrine requires that the IBC demonstrate, on an annual basis: (a) a physical office in Belize (owned or leased, not a virtual office); (b) full-time employees in Belize whose number is proportionate to the volume of the polymer distribution activity; (c) adequate annual operating expenditure in Belize relative to gross revenue (Registrar guidance: expenditure below 5% of turnover creates a rebuttable presumption of failure); (d) core income generating activity (CIGA) — purchase and sale contract negotiation, inventory risk management, logistics oversight, customer credit assessment, and payment authorization — performed from Belize. An IBC that is tax resident in a foreign jurisdiction with a valid TRC may certify exclusion from ESR, but it must still file Form ESR-1 with the foreign TIN and provide the foreign tax assessment notice; a mere claim of foreign residence without documentary proof is treated as continued ESR exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration to the Registrar

Statutory sanction: Fine up to BZD 100,000 per offence and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years (IBC Act Cap. 270 s. 93(7); ESR Act 2019 s. 24); mandatory strike-off of the IBC after the compliance notice; in addition, the Belize Tax Service may issue protective assessments recharacterizing up to 100% of the IBC's gross revenue as taxable Belize-source domestic income at 25%.

Director personal liability: Every director who signed the false return is jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid taxes plus interest at 15% per annum; the court may impose a travel ban pending investigation, a 5-year directorship disqualification, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment upon conviction. The doctrine of separate corporate personality is pierced for fraud.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Evasion

Statutory sanction: ESR penalty of BZD 20,000 initially, escalating to BZD 40,000 and BZD 100,000 for continued default; compulsory strike-off by the Registrar; the IBC loses limited liability status and contracts become personally enforceable against shareholders; the Belize Tax Service and foreign tax authorities (via CFC and mandatory disclosure rules) may recharacterize the structure as a sham.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal civil liability for all debts of the struck-off entity, restitution of any tax advantage, a 5-year ban from serving as a company officer, and criminal prosecution where evasion is established. Travel bans are routinely obtained by the Registrar to secure attendance for examinations.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Up to BZD 1,000,000 corporate fine for money-laundering-control failures; daily penalty of BZD 10,000 for each day the beneficial ownership information is not updated; the Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze the IBC's LC settlement accounts and file a restraint order over all polymer-trading proceeds traced to the breach.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers, MLROs, and directors are personally liable for failing to file suspicious transaction reports; personal fines up to BZD 250,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years; directors may be barred from holding banking or trust licences, and a travel ban may be issued where the beneficial owner is outside Belize and the investigation requires the director's physical presence.
Unlicensed Financial Activity / Illegal Local Business

Statutory sanction: Under the Banking and Financial Institutions Act: fine up to BZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 15 years, plus forfeiture of all funds collected; under the IBC Act: the IBC is automatically treated as a domestic company retroactively taxable at 25% on all income, and the Registrar may impose a strike-off order with publication of the entity's name on the public register of defaulters.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders are jointly and severally liable for restitution to every defrauded third party; the court may order an account of profits, confiscation of bank accounts, prohibitory travel bans, and imprisonment for senior officers of up to 15 years for the unlicensed deposit-taking offence, with no possibility of parole absent full restitution.
