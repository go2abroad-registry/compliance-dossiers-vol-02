Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service Department, Ministry of Finance, Government of Belize; e-filing and compliance portal: https://taxservices.gov.bz

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Belize International Business Company (IBC) is not required to apply for a domestic TIN. The IBC Act (Cap. 270) automatically confers a 50-year exemption from Belize income tax, business tax, capital gains tax, and stamp duty, and the Certificate of Incorporation together with the statutory tax exemption certificate serve as the entity's tax identity instrument. If the IBC were to make taxable supplies within Belize (generally prohibited without a special trading licence), it must apply for General Sales Tax (GST) registration at the Belize Tax Service by submitting the completed application form, certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation, registered office lease/address, full particulars of directors and shareholders, and evidence of foreign currency banking arrangements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Belize does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch election. Available elections are: (1) voluntary surrender/conversion of IBC status into an ordinary domestic company, thereby entering the Belize income tax and Business Tax net; (2) election to file an economic substance declaration under the International Business Companies (Amendment) Act 2021 to confirm that the IBC has no taxable presence or permanent establishment in Belize; (3) voluntary GST registration election for cross-border B2B or local supplies if a special trading licence is obtained; and (4) election to maintain foreign-currency accounts without Belize exchange-control restrictions for offshore e-commerce revenue.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

BZ$75,000 annual taxable turnover; voluntary registration is permitted below that threshold, but an IBC engaged exclusively in offshore internet retail is not obligated to register for Belize GST because its supplies are made to non-residents and are not consumed in Belize

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Direct physical export of outdoor camping equipment from Belize to a non-resident purchaser qualifies for the 0% GST rate under the General Sales Tax Act (Cap. 293) upon proof that the goods physically left Belize within the statutory export period, supported by a valid customs export declaration and a bill of lading or airway bill. Offshore e-commerce services, including the retail facilitation, marketplace intermediation, or logistics coordination for the cross-border sale of outdoor gear to non-resident customers, are also zero-rated where the services are supplied to non-residents and wholly consumed outside Belize. The zero-rating claim requires that the goods were located in Belize at the time of sale, the buyer is a non-resident, and the sales proceeds are received in a Belize-licensed or offshore foreign-currency account.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Belize Customs and Excise Department export entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure of goods from Belize
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank credit advice evidencing repatriation/settlement of proceeds

Belize has no real-time fiscal clearance regime, no mandatory API-based e-invoicing platform, and no digital stamping or pre-printing mandate for commercial invoices. E-commerce exporters may issue free-format commercial invoices and export documentation; GST returns are filed electronically on the Belize Tax Service portal on a quarterly basis, with supporting export evidence retained for audit.

Fraudulent or false export zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective GST reassessment, a surcharge/penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded, statutory interest, freezing of bank accounts, director and shareholder disqualification, and criminal prosecution under the General Sales Tax Act (Cap. 293) and the Customs and Excise Department rules. False declarations also expose the IBC to cancellation of its tax exemption certificate and strike-off under the IBC Act (Cap. 270), with clawback of all unpaid taxes and penalties for the entire period of non-compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belizean dollar (BZD) is legally pegged to the United States dollar at a fixed rate of 2.0000 BZD per 1.0000 USD administered by the Central Bank of Belize. Current account transactions are generally liberalized through authorized dealers, but capital account movements are subject to Central Bank approval and exchange control licensing. An International Business Company (IBC) domiciled under the Belize IBC Act is a non-resident entity for exchange control purposes and is exempt from Belize's domestic exchange control restrictions, enabling unrestricted holding and remittance of foreign currency through external accounts. Profit repatriation from an IBC is not subject to withholding tax or exchange surrender requirements provided that the transaction is recorded through a licensed external bank account and no transaction with a Belize resident is involved.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Belize Exchange Control Act and Central Bank of Belize directions, resident exporters must surrender export receipts in foreign currency to an authorized dealer within the statutory period, which is typically one month of the date of receipt. Mandatory conversion applies only to foreign exchange earned by Belize-resident entities; an IBC operating with non-resident status is outside the scope of surrender obligations and may retain export proceeds in a foreign-currency account at an authorized dealer without mandatory conversion to BZD.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low feasibility. Belize does not maintain a comprehensive electronic money institution (EMI) licensing framework, and offshore digital EMIs are not recognized as authorized dealers or participants in the Belize domestic clearing system. Cross-border payment service providers such as Stripe and PayPal can serve as merchant-facing collection rails but require a Belize-licensed bank account for settlement; most innovative cross-border EMIs are not accepted by Belize AML/CFT compliance teams due to the absence of a local license, potential ring-fencing issues, and the fact that reputable fintech platforms typically do not offer a Belizean merchant settlement option. The only viable structure is a Belize offshore bank account plus a supported global payment gateway account held in the name of the IBC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection should be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's or beneficial owner's bank to the IBC's external account at a Belize authorized dealer. The remittance must reference purpose code 'Investment in Belize IBC' or an analogous outward investment / intercompany loan coding accepted by the sending bank, and include supporting documentation at the receiving bank: board resolution authorizing allotment of shares or shareholder loan, completed capital intake form, certificate of incumbency, passport of signor, and a statement confirming non-resident status. The funds must be booked as equity or subordinated debt in the IBC's treasury ledger; conversion to BZD is neither required nor recommended, as IBC operations are intended to be conducted in USD and held in an external account exempt from exchange control.Outbound trade disbursements for imported outdoor camping equipment should be routed through SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier payments against pro-forma invoices, and through commercial letters of credit (irrevocable, confirmed or unconfirmed depending on supplier risk) for high-value purchase orders and large inventory replenishments. The paying bank requires presentation of commercial invoice, bill of lading or logistical evidence of shipment, packing list, and customs declarations when applicable.
