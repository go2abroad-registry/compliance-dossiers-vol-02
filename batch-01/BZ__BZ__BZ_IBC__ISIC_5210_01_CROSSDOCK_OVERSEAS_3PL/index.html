Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by law. The IBC Act requires only that the company has a registered office and registered agent in Belize; capital may be denominated in any currency and shares may be issued for such consideration as the board determines. No escrow account is legally mandated.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable — no fire safety certification, structural load inspection, or workplace clearance is required because the IBC does not operate any premises within the territorial jurisdiction of Belize.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service Department, Ministry of Finance, Economic Development and Investment (central tax administration and e-filing portal for VAT and Income & Business Tax); International Business Companies Registry, Companies and Corporate Affairs Registry, Ministry of Economic Development (IBC incorporation, annual license fee, and economic substance filings)

E-government filing portal

A Belize International Business Company (IBC), being non-resident and statutorily tax-exempt, is not required to obtain a Belize Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and is not registered for income tax, capital gains tax, or VAT. Its statutory corporate identifier is the IBC Registry Number and Certificate of Incorporation issued by the International Business Companies Registry. If the IBC were to carry on domestic Belize business (which is generally prohibited) or make taxable supplies in Belize exceeding the VAT threshold, it would be required to apply to the Belize Tax Service Department for a TIN/VAT registration on prescribed forms and file returns. Foreign entities supplying offshore cross-dock or 3PL fulfilment services have no mandatory Belize tax registration or TIN procurement obligation.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (IBC statutory exemption from income tax, capital gains tax, and stamp duty; foreign-source income is outside the territorial tax net; the entity is non-resident for Belize tax purposes and Belize-source income of a resident IBC would be assessable only under the Income and Business Tax Act) — A Belize International Business Company (IBC), being non-resident and statutorily tax-exempt, is not required to obtain a Belize Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and is not registered for income tax, capital gains tax, or VAT. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (IBC statutory exemption from income tax, capital gains tax, and stamp duty; foreign-source income is outside the territorial tax net; the entity is non-resident for Belize tax purposes and Belize-source income of a resident IBC would be assessable only under the Income and Business Tax Act)

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style entity classification election exists in Belize. An IBC may elect to convert or redomicile into an ordinary domestic company under the Companies Act, thereby losing IBC tax exemptions; conversely, an ordinary company may apply to be registered as an IBC if it satisfies the IBC Act conditions. An IBC carrying on a 'relevant activity' (including cross-border distribution and service centre operations) must file an annual economic substance return with the Registry and maintain adequate physical presence, directors, employees, and core income-generating activities in Belize, unless it is tax resident in a foreign jurisdiction with equivalent substance requirements. Belize has no domestic check-the-box, CFC, hybrid mismatch, or participation-exemption election regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

BZD 75,000 annual taxable turnover, assessed over any rolling 12-month period, for supplies made in the course of business in Belize; VAT registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, with voluntary registration available to exporters and other qualifying persons

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Belize and evidenced by customs documentation qualify for the 0% VAT rate, with the exporter entitled to credit or refund of attributable input VAT if VAT-registered. Certain international and offshore services, including international transportation and services physically performed outside Belize for a non-resident (such as cross-border e-commerce fulfillment, overseas warehouse storage, and cross-dock logistics supplied to foreign merchants), are outside the scope of Belize VAT or zero-rated. A Belize IBC is wholly outside the Belize VAT net and is not required to charge VAT on offshore 3PL income; the zero-rating claim and refund path is available only to Belize VAT-registered taxpayers.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry / Single Administrative Document filed with the Belize Customs Department)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation from Belize
  • Commercial Invoice and Sales Contract
  • Packing List (where applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent foreign-currency bank credit advice issued by a licensed Belize financial institution evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Belize does not maintain a mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, CDS/API, or digital stamping regime. VAT-registered persons must issue statutory VAT invoices and file periodic VAT returns through the Belize Tax Service Department e-filing portal; no government clearance or digital stamp is required before invoice issuance. Supplies of cross-border e-commerce fulfilment and overseas cross-dock logistics services rendered by an IBC outside Belize are outside the scope of Belize VAT and create no local invoicing or clearance obligation.

Belize Customs and the Tax Service audit export and zero-rating claims retrospectively. A VAT-registered exporter who fraudulently claims zero-rating, falsifies export documentation, or fails to substantiate physical export is liable to repayment of all input VAT credited or refunded, plus statutory interest and penalties of up to double the tax evaded; goods may be forfeited under customs law, and criminal prosecution with director and officer personal liability may follow under the VAT Act and applicable revenue statutes. An IBC that wrongfully conducts domestic Belize business or derives Belize-source income without registration risks loss of its statutory tax exemption, retroactive Income and Business Tax assessments, penalties, and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belize dollar (BZD) is pegged to the US dollar at BZD 2.00 = USD 1.00 under the Exchange Control Act (Cap. 244). Current-account convertibility is complete in practice: authorised dealers process bona fide trade and service cross-border payments without prior Central Bank approval. Capital-account convertibility is restricted: equity injections, intercompany loans, portfolio flows, and capital repatriation require prior Exchange Control approval where they involve Belize domestic BZD accounts; offshore external-account movements by a Belize IBC treated as non-resident under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270) fall outside the statutory exchange-control net. Profit remittance: a Belize IBC is tax-exempt and no withholding tax applies to dividends, interest, royalties, or capital gains; the operative remittance hurdles are therefore bank-level compliance rather than fiscal charges. The remitting bank requires a current certificate of good standing, a certified incumbency certificate, audited or management accounts evidencing the underlying commercial substance, and a board resolution authorising the distribution. Conversion into BZD is available on demand at the fixed official rate, but the IBC should preserve USD-ledger liquidity and avoid unnecessary conversion into BZD to minimise de facto regulatory friction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must repatriate export proceeds within six months of the date of shipment and surrender 100% of repatriated net proceeds to an authorised dealer at the fixed official rate unless a foreign-currency retention allowance has been granted in writing by the Central Bank of Belize for bona fide working-capital needs. Belize IBCs are statutorily treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270); therefore receipts from overseas e-commerce sales and cross-dock fulfillment generated offshore are not export proceeds subject to surrender or mandatory conversion. If such offshore funds are subsequently wired into a Belize domestic BZD account, the receiving bank applies inward-capital verification and standard source-of-funds due diligence rather than export-surrender conversion rules.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (Exchange Control Department), acting under the Central Bank of Belize Act (Cap. 262) and the Exchange Control Act (Cap. 244), with AML/CFT supervisory authority shared with the Belize Financial Intelligence Unit over authorised dealers and licensed financial institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a settlement sub-rail and Low as the primary bank account. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Payoneer, Currenxie, and WISE may lawfully be used by a Belize IBC because the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270) does not restrict the holding of foreign bank or EMI accounts; however, the EMI's own AML/KYC policy determines onboarding feasibility. Legal and operational constraints: most EMIs classify Belize IBCs as higher-risk due to beneficial-ownership opacity and will refuse onboarding absent full natural-person UBO disclosure, board minutes, and commercial trade contracts. EMIs are not licensed banks in Belize, cannot provide a Belize Central Bank settlement account, cannot credit BZD-denominated rails, and are generally limited to SWIFT, US ACH, and card-network settlement. E-commerce platform collections should be routed into the IBC's USD EMI wallet only as a temporary collection buffer, with daily sweeping into a licensed Belize international bank account for trade settlement. EMIs must never be used for capital escrow or statutory payment obligations.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial-ownership opacity caused by nominee shareholders, nominee directors, historical bearer shares, or a layered offshore structure.

Root cause: Beneficial-ownership opacity caused by nominee shareholders, nominee directors, historical bearer shares, or a layered offshore structure.

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation; current Certificate of Good Standing from the Registrar of IBCs; certified register of members and register of directors; natural-person UBO declaration containing full name, date of birth, nationality, passport copy, proof of residential address; a professional cover letter from the licensed Belize registered agent; and an organisational chart of the 3PL group evidencing the genuine commercial nexus.
Source of funds or source of wealth insufficiently documented for the capital escrow injection and initial funding.

Root cause: Source of funds or source of wealth insufficiently documented for the capital escrow injection and initial funding.

Defense evidence: Twelve months of bank statements from the contributing shareholder or parent company; audited or management financial statements of the parent group; underlying purchase and sale agreements for merchandise transiting the cross-dock; e-commerce platform receivables reports; tax payment receipts from the shareholder's jurisdiction; and a confirming letter from an independent certified accountant attesting to accumulated wealth.
Trade-flow mismatch where transaction volumes, counterparties, or payment tenor are inconsistent with a low-risk warehouse and fulfillment business.

Root cause: Trade-flow mismatch where transaction volumes, counterparties, or payment tenor are inconsistent with a low-risk warehouse and fulfillment business.

Defense evidence: Executed warehouse and order-fulfillment agreements; merchant onboarding contracts with e-commerce marketplaces; ERP inventory and order-level reporting; bills of lading proving physical goods movement; last-mile carrier contracts; customs declarations from destination jurisdictions; and a copy of the IBC treasury policy governing payment cycles.
Tax-residency and economic-substance suspicion arising from BEPS and global minimum tax spillover despite IBC tax-exemption.

Root cause: Tax-residency and economic-substance suspicion arising from BEPS and global minimum tax spillover despite IBC tax-exemption.

Defense evidence: Economic Substance Act 2019 notification and compliance confirmation; board minutes declaring non-residence and confirming no Belize-resident business activity; IBC tax-exemption certificate; certificate of tax residence from the beneficial owner's jurisdiction; and a written explanation of tax-transparency treatment to prevent dual-residency claims.
Sanctions, PEP hits, or adverse media affecting the IBC, beneficial owners, or counterparty merchants.

Root cause: Sanctions, PEP hits, or adverse media affecting the IBC, beneficial owners, or counterparty merchants.

Defense evidence: Commissioned World-Check or equivalent sanctions screening report at onboarding; sworn PEP declaration for each UBO; adverse-media explanations supported by contracts; confirmation that no party is listed by OFAC, EU, or UN sanctions; and standard OFAC no-sanctions clauses inserted into customer agreements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the beneficial owner's or parent company's own bank account to the IBC's USD current account at a licensed Belize bank. The wire must use the exact IBC legal name as shown on the Certificate of Incorporation, the beneficiary account number, and a precise Balance of Payments purpose code such as Equity other than reinvested earnings; the payment narrative should reference the underlying board resolution and commercial purpose to avoid generic codes. If the funds will be converted into BZD, the authorised dealer must complete the Central Bank inward-capital declaration; if kept in USD external account status, no conversion filing is required. For intercompany loans, an executed intercompany loan agreement at arm's-length interest, a board resolution, and the source-of-funds dossier must accompany the MT103. The bank will issue a SWIFT gpi tracker and MT103 confirmation; capital escrow accounts should carry a dual-signature disbursement mandate and a segregated-account designation to satisfy both Belize AML rules and destination-country source-of-funds audits.Outbound clearing rails for cross-dock overseas 3PL operations: (a) SWIFT MT103 for supplier and freight-forwarder payments above USD 1,000, using an accurate payment narrative referencing the commercial invoice and purchase order; (b) commercial letters of credit at sight for high-value destined inventory orders requiring title-document controls; (c) Fedwire or US ACH through a Belize bank correspondent for US local vendor expenses; (d) Local_ACH payouts through Airwallex or WISE APIs for buyer-facing marketplace settlements; (e) open-account trade with ERP invoice-matching for established suppliers. All outbound wires must include a matching commercial invoice, purchase order, bill of lading, and beneficiary-bank due diligence file retained for at least seven years. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold in Belize; IBCs are exempt from corporate tax and therefore not subject to Belize transfer pricing documentation requirements. However, if the IBC transacts with related parties in foreign jurisdictions with transfer pricing rules, a Local File and Master File may be required by the counterparty's tax authority. Under Belize's International Business Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations, IBCs conducting relevant activities must maintain records demonstrating economic substance. The monetary threshold is not defined; compliance is assessed on activity and proportionality. For an ISIC 5210 cross-dock overseas 3PL operation, the relevant activity is likely 'distribution and service center' or 'logistics hub', requiring physical presence, employees, and expenditure in the jurisdiction if the entity claims Belize tax residency.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Doctrine is codified in the Belize International Business Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations, 2019, aligned with the EU Code of Conduct Group requirements. IBCs engaged in 'relevant activities' — including distribution and service center activities, shipping, and logistics — must satisfy the full economic substance test in Belize if they seek Belize tax residency or wish to avoid adverse foreign reporting. The test requires: (a) core income-generating activities conducted in Belize; (b) adequate number of full-time employees in Belize; (c) adequate physical premises in Belize; (d) annual operating expenditure in Belize proportionate to income; and (e) senior management and board meetings held in Belize. A cross-dock overseas warehouse 3PL IBC that is managed and controlled from overseas and performs all operational functions outside Belize is not 'resident' in Belize for substance purposes; it must instead maintain records showing that its core activities are performed outside Belize and that it is resident for tax purposes in its real place of management. Filing the annual economic substance declaration with the IFSC is mandatory. Failure to satisfy substance requirements or to provide information can trigger financial penalties from BZD 50,000 up to BZD 150,000 per offense, and officers are liable to fines or imprisonment up to three years.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty of BZD 50,000 for initial non-compliance, escalating to BZD 150,000 per offense for continued non-compliance; possible striking-off or deregistration of the IBC; information exchange with foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers knowingly submitting false declarations face imprisonment of up to three years and fines; directors may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid penalties if the company is struck off; travel bans and freezing orders may be imposed in asset-tracing and anti-avoidance proceedings.
Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Under Belize's International Business Companies Act and Criminal Code, tax fraud penalties include fines and imprisonment up to five years; in the foreign jurisdiction, penalties may include back taxes, interest, penalties up to 200% of understated tax, and criminal prosecution; the IBC may be dissolved.

Director personal liability: If the corporate veil is pierced, directors, beneficial owners, and signatories are personally liable for all unpaid taxes and penalties; foreign courts may issue worldwide asset freezes, travel bans, and extradition requests; imprisonment in both Belize (up to five years) and the foreign jurisdiction is possible for willful evasion.
AML / Money Laundering Infraction

Statutory sanction: Under Belize's Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Act, fines up to BZD 500,000 for entities and imprisonment up to ten years for individuals; the IFSC may revoke the IBC license, impose administrative penalties, or refer the matter to the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Senior officers and directors are criminally liable for failure to implement AML compliance programs, including failure to conduct customer due diligence and file suspicious transaction reports; personal fines, confiscation of proceeds, and imprisonment up to ten years; travel bans and asset seizures are commonly imposed in cross-border cases.
Shell Company Abuse / Lack of Beneficial Ownership Disclosure

Statutory sanction: The Belize Beneficial Ownership Act mandates disclosure of beneficial owners to the registered agent and IFSC. Non-compliance attracts fines up to BZD 25,000 per offense and imprisonment up to two years; the IBC may lose its good-standing status and be struck off; information is exchanged under international tax transparency agreements.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and corporate officers who provide false, incomplete, or misleading ownership information are personally liable; they may be disqualified from acting as directors, subjected to fines and imprisonment, and face civil liability for taxes evaded by foreign tax authorities including piercing of the corporate veil.
Transfer Pricing and Permanent Establishment Exposure in Foreign Jurisdictions

Statutory sanction: In the foreign source jurisdiction, the tax authority may reassess profits attributable to the permanent establishment, impose withholding tax on unreported service and royalty payments, levy penalties of 10% to 100% of the adjusted tax, and assert interest; Belize itself has no transfer pricing penalty regime for IBCs, so the liability is external.

Director personal liability: Directors of the IBC who control the fulfillment network may be held personally liable for foreign tax debts in jurisdictions where the corporate veil is pierced, especially where the IBC is found to be a sham or a mere instrumentality; advisory fees, penalties, and legal costs may be personally assessed; travel bans may be issued in tax-debt enforcement cases.
