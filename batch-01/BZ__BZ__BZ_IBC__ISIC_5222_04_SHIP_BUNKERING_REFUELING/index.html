Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service Department, Ministry of Finance (portal: https://www.taxservice.gov.bz); Value Added Tax administered under the Value Added Tax Act, Chapter 63 of the Laws of Belize; Customs matters administered by the Belize Customs and Excise Department via the ASYCUDA World platform

E-government filing portal

An International Business Company (IBC) incorporated under the International Business Companies Act, Chapter 270 is tax-exempt and is not statutorily obligated to obtain a domestic Belize income-tax TIN. If the IBC nonetheless requires a TIN for administrative or bank-account purposes, the filing officer must submit Form TIN-01 (Application for Registration of a New Entity) to the Belize Tax Service together with the Certificate of Incorporation and a certified copy of the Memorandum and Articles of Association. VAT registration under the Value Added Tax Act is compulsory only for a person that makes taxable supplies exceeding BZD 75,000 per annum in Belize; a foreign-incorporated entity without a fixed establishment in Belize is generally outside the scope of VAT unless it elects or is required to register as a taxable person. No economic-presence registration applies to pure offshore bunkering suppliers serving non-resident vessels.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None applicable. Section 17 of the International Business Companies Act confers an unconditional exemption from all Belize income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, and stamp duty for a period of 50 years from incorporation for an IBC, and no check-the-box, fiscal-transparent, or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Belize domestic law. An IBC may not elect to be treated as a domestic Belize company, and Belize has not adopted OECD hybrid-mismatch rules affecting entity classification; the entity is classified at entity level as a non-resident tax-exempt corporation with no mitigating election mechanism available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

BZD 75,000 of annual taxable turnover (taxable supplies made in Belize); registration is compulsory within 14 days after the end of the month in which the threshold is exceeded; a person making only zero-rated or exempt supplies may apply for voluntary registration

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Value Added Tax Act for the supply of bunker fuel, marine lubricants, and ship stores to a qualifying vessel engaged in international traffic, provided the vessel is a foreign-going ship (i.e., departs Belizean waters for a destination outside Belize or is otherwise engaged in foreign trade) and the goods are physically exported or delivered to the vessel as ship's stores for use on the international voyage. The supplier must retain evidence that the supply is an export or an international transportation supply; supplies to vessels plying exclusively on domestic cabotage routes or to non-qualifying local vessels are subject to the standard VAT rate of 12.5%. The zero-rating claim is substantiated by the export declaration, delivery evidence, and proof that payment was received from a non-resident charterer, ship-owner, or operator.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World form C-EX / SAD declaration, including the unique consignment reference)
  • Bill of Lading or Inward/Outward Cargo Manifest issued by the shipping line
  • Signed Bunkering Delivery Note (BDN) or independent marine surveyor's report confirming quantity and grade delivered
  • Valid VAT invoice or tax invoice issued under the Value Added Tax Act showing the zero-rated supply code
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing foreign-exchange receipt from the non-resident counterparty
  • Ship's clearance papers, International Voyage Certificate, or certificate of foreign-going vessel status confirming the international character of the voyage

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API interconnect, or digital stamping regime is in force in Belize. Taxpayers must issue VAT invoices containing the prescribed particulars under the Value Added Tax (General) Regulations, whether in paper or electronic form; electronic invoices are permissible but are not cleared through any national e-invoicing platform. Export and customs documentation is processed through the ASYCUDA World single-window customs declaration system, which functions as the only digital transaction-clearance channel for exported goods.

The Belize Tax Service and Customs and Excise Department may conduct retrospective audits of zero-rated bunkering transactions for up to six years from the date of the export supply. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims attract civil reassessment at 12.5% VAT on the full supply value, interest at the statutory rate, and penalties of up to twice the tax evaded under the Value Added Tax Act, Chapter 63, whilst criminal conviction for deliberate misdeclaration under the Customs and Excise Act and VAT Act can expose the IBC to fines of up to BZD 50,000 and imprisonment of the responsible officer for up to three years. The Belize Tax Service may additionally claw back all input VAT credited in connection with the tainted exports, disallow refunds, suspend or revoke the bunkering supplier licence issued by the Belize Port Authority, and impose exclusion from future export bond or zero-rating arrangements upon a finding of systemic abuse.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belize dollar (BZD) is fixed under a currency board-like arrangement to the US dollar at BZD 2.00 = USD 1.00. Current-account transactions for an International Business Company (IBC) are freely permitted in foreign currency, but the IBC is statutorily required to conduct its banking business in a foreign currency or in a special foreign-currency account; conversion to BZD is not a pre-requisite for profit remittance. Capital-account inward investments into an IBC require only a reportable SWIFT purpose code, not prior CBB approval, provided funds are lodged as paid-in capital or shareholder loans. Profit remittance from BZD accounts is subject to standard exchange-control documentation and a 100% repatriation of the IBC's foreign-currency profits is available without additional exchange-control approval if accounted for in USD. Practical hurdles are limited to completing bank-required source-of-wealth documentation, as Belize does not impose profit remittance taxes on IBCs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Belize Exchange Control Regulations technically require residents to surrender export proceeds to an authorized dealer within 90 days; however, an IBC licensed under the International Business Companies Act, Chapter 270, is treated as a non-resident for exchange-control purposes and may retain 100% of marine bunkering export receipts in a foreign-currency account without mandatory conversion to BZD. The mandatory booking requirement is that all bunker sales invoices, port memoranda, and supporting bills of lading are denominated in USD and that the IBC maintains a clear trail of receipt into its designated foreign-currency account. No fixed statutory conversion ratio applies to the IBC's foreign-currency receipts; conversion to BZD, if voluntarily executed, must be done through an authorized dealer at the pegged rate of 2:1.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB) acting as the national monetary authority and ex officio Exchange Control administration under the Exchange Control Act, Chapter 36 of the Laws of Belize.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. Belize has no domestic electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime, so offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Mercury cannot function as a statutory banking rail for the Belize IBC. They may be used as ancillary USD service providers for supplier settlement, but the CBB-regulated anti-money laundering (AML) framework, the maritime bunkering license audit, and the IBC's corporate good standing requirements mandate a physical or reputable licensed Belize commercial bank account for operational liquidity and capital escrow. A foreign EMI may be accepted only as a supplementary collection channel if the primary account remains with a locally licensed authorized dealer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The standard inbound rail is an SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's corporate account, using the purpose code GCON (general capital) or APIC (additional paid-in capital) when the sending bank requires a disbursement code, plus the Belize IBC's full registered name, bank account number, IBAN-format identifier if applied, and a remark: 'Shareholder capital contribution to BZ IBC for marine bunkering treasury under the International Business Companies Act.' Prior to the wire, the IBC must file with the receiving bank a board resolution, a share certificate or subscription entry, and a confirmation from the local registered agent that the contribution is recorded in the IBC register. The clearing hub in Belize is SWIFT-connected through US correspondent banks; thus the receiving bank will verify the remitter against OFAC and CBB sanctions lists. Verification of beneficial ownership of the remitter is mandatory whenever the sending account is not in the name of a disclosed UBO or a publicly listed holding company. Proceeds must post to the designated Capital Escrow Account before being commingled with trade-operating funds.International bunker trade settlements should use SWIFT MT103 for direct payment of supplier invoices and an irrevocable standby letter of credit (SBLC) or documentary letter of credit governed by UCP 600 for deferred payment to major fuel terminals. For a standard MT103 supplier payment, the IBC must include the supplier's bank BIC, the beneficiary account number, the invoice number, and a purpose code such as trade-operated payment in foreign currency; the receiving bank will require the original purchase order, quantity certificate, and the bill of lading or barge receipt to be uploaded to the banking portal within five business days. A commercial LC is preferred when the bunker supplier requires certainty of payment against documents; the Belize issuing bank will transmit an MT700 to the beneficiary bank, with documents consisting of a commercial invoice, full set of clean bills of lading or a delivery receipt from the storage facility, a certificate of quantity and quality, a port authority anchorage log, and a certificate of origin. For same-day domestic settlement where the counterparty maintains a BZD-denominated account, the local clearing rail via the Belize Automated Clearing House may be used, but all marine bunker pricing and disbursement forecasts should be quoted in USD to avoid the 2:1 fixed peg mismatch.Spot purchase and sale of USD/BZD at the fixed pegged rate of 2.00 through an authorized dealer for transactional conversion onlyOutright forward contracts between BZD and USD with a licensed Belize commercial bank, provided the underlying exposure is a real trade payable or receivable and maturity is less than 365 daysCross-currency swaps between USD and EUR/GBP executed with an internationally licensed bank, but only for the purpose of hedging bunker procurement or scrap/recycling receivables of the marine support businessNDFs (non-deliverable forwards) and speculative currency options are not permitted in the Belize onshore market; no regulated BZD NDF market exists due to the fixed pegCurrency swaps on the same currency pair are permissible only as a treasury liquidity tool if they are booked as trade-related hedging, marked to market, and reported to the CBB through the authorized dealing bank

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a Belize International Business Company (IBC) under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270); the general Belize resident-company headline rate under the Income and Business Tax Act is 25%, but it has no application to an exempt IBC.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – dividends paid by a Belize IBC are statutorily exempt from withholding tax under Cap. 270; no WHT arises on distribution of profits to non-resident shareholders and no advance corporate tax credit is required.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before 31 August in each calendar year; the first-year license fee is prepaid at incorporation. Payments tendered after 31 August attract a US$25 late penalty; if the fee remains unpaid after 1 November, the Registrar may strike the IBC off the register, requiring court restoration thereafter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No gazetted Local File / Master File monetary threshold is published in Belize. The arm's-length standard applies to related-party transactions by statute. Best-practice operating threshold for a bunkering IBC: BZD 2,500,000 (approximately US$1,250,000) in aggregate annual cross-border related-party bunker procurement and sale turnover, above which contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation should be retained in Belize.

Economic substance scope: International Business Companies (Economic Substance) Rules 2019 apply: an IBC carrying on marine bunkering and lubricant replenishment (ISIC 5222) is engaged in a distribution and service centre relevant activity, with shipping substance exposure if it owns or operates barges. The IBC must file an Economic Substance return with the IFSC demonstrating that core income-generating activities (bunker contract negotiation, procurement, scheduling, invoicing, and UBO-level control) are performed in Belize through local employees and premises, or must validly rely on an available exclusion. A letterbox structure or failure to file invalidates the 0% tax status and triggers penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham Bunkering Transactions

Statutory sanction: Under the Income and Business Tax Act, tax evasion or gross negligence penalties up to BZD 10,000 plus double the tax evaded; the IFSC may revoke the IBC's tax-exemption certificate; under the IBC Act, false statements attract BZD 5,000 plus BZD 500 per day for continuing default.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the IBC functions as a mere instrumentality; directors face joint and several civil restitution for evaded taxes, clawback of distributions, travel and asset-restraint orders, and, upon indictment, imprisonment of up to 5 years for dishonest conduct.
Shell Company / Economic Substance and Beneficial Ownership Default

Statutory sanction: Under the 2019 ESR Rules, non-compliance attracts fines of up to BZD 10,000 per relevant activity, with escalating daily penalties for continued non-compliance after notice; under the IBC Act, BZD 5,000 plus BZD 500 per day for registry violations; persistent default triggers administrative strike-off, cancellation of the IBC license, and reclassification to the 25% resident-company tax regime.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent are jointly and severally liable for unpaid license fees and penalties; directors who continue trading after strike-off commit an offence and may be disqualified; courts may impose director-by-director monetary assessments, restitution orders, and disqualification from holding office in any Belize entity.
AML / Money Laundering and Sanctions Evasion (Bunker Trade Red Flags)

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering and Terrorism (Prevention) Act (Cap. 104), conviction upon indictment carries fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years, plus forfeiture of proceeds, vessels, and fuel cargoes; the Central Bank of Belize may impose supervisory fines on the IBC's Belize payment intermediaries and suspend SWIFT/LC access; treaty information exchange enables foreign asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and compliance officers who knew or suspected the laundering are criminally liable personally; civil forfeiture, travel bans, and extradition requests under TIEAs may be enforced; conviction carries custodial terms and disqualification from acting as a director of any Belize company.
