Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service Department, Ministry of Finance, Government of Belize (https://taxservice.gov.bz); customs brokerage licensing additionally regulated by the Belize Customs Department (https://customs.gov.bz) using the ASYCUDA World single-window platform.

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders incorporate the IBC through a licensed registered agent under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270), then submit the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum of Association, registered agent letter, and specimen signatures to the Tax Service Department on Form REG-1 to obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN). A customs brokerage IBC must also hold a Customs Broker Licence under s.22 of the Customs and Excise Duty Act, which requires a valid TIN, a licensed individual broker or qualified firm with a registered place of business in Belize, and professional indemnity cover. If annual taxable supplies in Belize exceed BZ$150,000, the IBC must additionally file Form GST-02 for Goods and Services Tax registration. A formal Tax Exemption Certificate may be issued to confirm the statutory exemption from income tax, capital gains tax, business tax, stamp duty, and dividend withholding. Domestic business with Belize residents is prohibited for IBCs absent ministerial consent under the IBC Act, and the tax exemption is preserved only where brokerage services are rendered to non-residents on offshore or transit cargo.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Belizean law. An IBC may voluntarily surrender tax-exempt status by converting to an ordinary Belize company pursuant to s.150 of the IBC Act and thereby become subject to chargeable income at entity level under the Income and Business Tax Act. Under the Economic Substance Act 2019, an IBC managed and controlled in Belize is deemed tax-resident for substance purposes and must file an annual economic substance notification; however, customs brokerage is not a designated relevant activity, so no substance test is triggered solely by this business line.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of goods from Belize and to services supplied to a non-resident for direct use and benefit outside Belize, including services directly performed upon or in connection with exported goods and services comprising international transit freight and bonded warehouse management (GST Act, Schedule 2). A customs brokerage IBC may claim 0% GST on brokerage fees, transit documentation, and bonded-warehouse handling invoiced to foreign principals for export-bound cargo, provided the services are not tied to local Belizean import consumption, the non-resident status of the recipient is documented, and the statutory export declaration and shipping evidence are retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing export from Belize
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Customs Broker Licence and client Power of Attorney
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Central Bank foreign-exchange conversion docket
  • GST export schedule and zero-rating declaration (Form GST-EX)

Belize does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API connectivity, or digital stamping regime for GST. Registered customs brokers must issue serially numbered tax invoices showing the broker's TIN and GST registration number, file monthly GST returns via Form GST-1 through the Tax Service e-filing portal, and retain invoice and customs records for six years. Customs export and import declarations are processed electronically through the ASYCUDA World customs clearance system, which operates separately from GST invoice controls.

The Tax Service Department and Customs Department conduct joint risk-based audits of export zero-rating claims. Non-compliant claims trigger reassessment, a civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded, monthly interest at 1.5%, and retrospective clawback of input tax credits previously refunded. Knowing or fraudulent presentation of false export documentation is a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to BZ$50,000 and/or imprisonment of up to three years, and carries mandatory suspension or revocation of the customs broker licence under the Customs and Excise Duty Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Belize maintains a de facto currency peg with a fixed official exchange rate of BZD 2.00 = USD 1.00, administered through the CBB under the Exchange Control Act. Current account payments are ordinarily convertible upon production of commercial documentation; capital account payments, profit repatriation, shareholder loan repayments, and head-office management fees require CBB exchange control approval, an authorization-to-purchase foreign currency, or a valid general permission. The Cayman-style pre-2023 IBC exemption from exchange control remains substantially operable only where the entity is designated as an offshore or USD-denominated account holder at a CBB-authorized dealer and does not create a domestic BZD credit balance with residents for prolonged periods. For this customs brokerage house, we recommend a dual-account architecture: (i) an onshore BZD operational account calibrated exclusively to customs duties, excise taxes, ASYCUDA World settlements, and local overheads; and (ii) a USD-denominated treasury account for freight forwarding, foreign sub-agents, reinsurance, and cross-border disbursements. Practical profit remittance hurdles to non-resident shareholders are low for fully tax-exempt or grandfathered IBCs, subject to filing of the annual economic substance declaration, confirmation of the beneficial ownership register, and segregation of income sourced from Belize customs services. The principal convertibility constraint arises when the company collects brokerage fees from domestic importers in BZD, which the CBB may treat as a Belize-source resident credit requiring authorization before conversion to USD; that risk is mitigated by denominating fees in USD under the master brokerage agreements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Exchange Control (General) Regulations require any person in Belize receiving foreign currency for exported services, such as customs brokerage fees from a foreign principal, to offer the proceeds to a CBB-authorized dealer in Belize dollars within the statutory period, generally not later than three months after shipment or, where the payment terms so provide, within one month after receipt. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% when the funds land in a domestic BZD-linked account; it may be reduced to 0% only where the CBB or the authorized dealer has approved a USD offshore account designation or a formal general permission for the IBC. The customs brokerage house must structure fee collections from foreign customs principals through the USD treasury account and file the applicable services export declaration, failing which the broker risks exchange control penalties and blocking of the payment rail. Exact percentage obligations and timelines should be reconfirmed with the CBB Exchange Control Department during the account authorization.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB), Belize City, being the central bank, currency board administrator, and exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act, Chapter 61 of the Laws of Belize, as applied and modified subsequent to the 2023 integration of International Business Companies into the Companies Act, Chapter 250.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Belize has no domestic EMI or standalone payment institution licensing class, and the CBB does not register foreign fintech EMIs as authorized dealers. Offshore digital EMIs, including Wise Business, Revolut Business, Currencycloud, and Airwallex, are not permitted to clear Belize customs duties through ASYCUDA World, cannot issue Belize-relevant tax documents, and are generally unwilling to onboard a Belize IBC with nominee shareholders and a medium-risk customs brokerage activity. They may be used only as auxiliary, low-value settlement channels for non-Belize supplier payments, and never as the primary treasury spine. The recommended architecture is a Belize commercial bank at the core, with the EMI serving solely as a secondary 'vendor aggregation' card or virtual account limited to minor foreign subscriptions; even then, fund flows must be reconciled monthly and tagged in the accounting system to preserve a clean audit trail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are executed by SWIFT wire transfer (MT103) from the shareholder's bank to the IBC's USD treasury account at the Belize commercial bank, with the beneficiary field identified by the company registration number, the payment reason using the CBB-recognized purpose code for 'direct investment / contributed capital', and the corporate resolution and share subscription documents attached to the bank's exchange-control verification. The credited funds are held in the approved USD offshore account designation; if the capital is to be converted into BZD for working capital, the bank will require the CBB authorization to purchase Belize dollars. For shareholder loan injections, the remittance must reference an executed loan agreement specifying the interest rate, maturity, and repayment schedule to avoid recharacterization as equity or unrepatriable funds. The bank will verify the inbound wire against the economic substance declaration, the Customs Brokers Licence, and the beneficial ownership register before releasing funds into the treasury pool; no cash withdrawal or onward transfer is permitted until the exchange-control records are completed.Customs duty, excise taxes, and port charges are settled through the ASYCUDA World e-payment module, which debits the BZD operational account at the Belize commercial bank by electronic funds transfer or ACH-equivalent debit, with an automated receipt serving as the customs entry release; oversized entries may be paid by wire to the designated Customs Department revenue account. Cross-border disbursements to foreign freight forwarders, marine insurers, sub-agents, and customs software suppliers are cleared via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with purpose codes corresponding to 'payment for transportation and customs services' or 'freight agent disbursement'. For high-value bonded merchandise, a commercial documentary letter of credit is issued through the Belize commercial bank under SWIFT MT700 and amended under MT707, payable through a confirming North American or European bank, ensuring that title documents are released only against compliant presentation of bills of lading, commercial invoices, packing lists, and customs entry certificates. The operative treasury protocol is a hard two-account segregation: the BZD account is limited to duties, taxes, local port fees, and statutory payments, requiring dual approver release, while the USD account handles all foreign-currency vendor settlements; the ACH rails are reserved for recurring US-based broker-client duty debits and are reconciled against ASYCUDA World entries to preserve the customs audit trail.USD/BZD currency forwards contracted with a CBB-authorized dealer on a commercial underlying (confirmed customs duty, freight, and bonded-transit obligations), subject to the fixed 2.00 peg with a negotiated premium or marginOffshore non-deliverable USD/BZD forwards (NDFs) with North American counterparties, permitted solely as a hedge of confirmed receivables and payables, not as a speculative position, and booked at the foreign parent or treasury intermediary level to avoid a Belize-resident derivative exposureEUR/USD, GBP/USD, and MXN/USD outright forwards and near-dated FX spots for non-USD sub-agent and ocean-freight settlements, executed through the Belize bank's correspondent or a licensed international bank subsidiaryCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) under ISDA 2002 negotiated outside Belize to match foreign-currency shareholder loans and capex financing, documented at the offshore treasury holding level so that the Belize IBC does not become a Belize-resident swap counterpartyNatural hedging instruments comprising same-day USD/BZD netting, segregated USD treasury accounts, pre-funded BZD term deposits warehousing duty cash-flow peaks, and contractual currency-of-settlement clauses in master brokerage agreements

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for International Business Company (IBC) under the International Business Companies Act (IBC Act), inclusive of full exemption from income tax, capital gains tax, business tax, withholding tax and stamp duty for 50 years. If the entity conducts taxable domestic operations in Belize without IBC exemption, the Income and Business Tax Act (IBTA) headline corporate rate is 25% on chargeable income, plus a quarterly Business Tax (gross receipts tax) at 1.75% applicable to general services.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Belize imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an IBC or by a domestic Belize company under the IBTA; dividend distributions from an IBC are statutorily exempt from all Belize taxes.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual licence fee and return payable on the anniversary of incorporation; standard administrative deadline is before 1 September each year, with a 30-day grace period; returns must be filed by the licensed registered agent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BZD 0 — Belize has no codified transfer pricing Local File or Master File monetary thresholds; however, the IBTA contains general anti-avoidance powers (s.48) to revalue non-arm's length transactions, and taxpayers engaged in cross-border related-party transactions with a Belize permanent establishment must maintain contemporaneous pricing records for 5 years and on demand produce them to the Tax Service Department.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act 2019 requires any Belize tax-resident company carrying on a 'relevant activity' (banking, insurance, fund management, financing, leasing, headquarters, shipping, IP, distribution and service centre) to demonstrate substantial economic presence in Belize, including physical premises, full-time employees and core income-generating activities in Belize. A customs brokerage or bonded transit management business (ISIC 5229) is not automatically a listed relevant activity unless it operates as a distribution/service centre; nevertheless, an IBC incorporated in Belize must file an annual economic substance declaration, comply with CRS/FATCA reporting, and if tax resident in Belize for any reason, maintain substance commensurate with its activity to avoid shell-company classification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: IBTA offences: fine up to BZD 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; civil penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded; interest at 12% per annum from the due date; forfeiture of customs broker licence and bond.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and the appointed corporate secretary are jointly and severally liable for unpaid Business Tax and PAYE/withholding taxes; the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent trading, exposing personal assets, travel bans, and director disqualification orders.
IBC Prohibited Local Business / Unlicensed Customs Brokerage

Statutory sanction: Violation of the IBC Act prohibition on local business: penalty up to BZD 10,000 and each offending transaction is void and unenforceable; unlicensed customs brokerage: fine up to BZD 1,000 and/or imprisonment up to 6 months, plus revocation of the customs surety bond and seizure of goods.

Director personal liability: Directors and the beneficial owner are personally liable for penalties and third-party losses; the IBC's separate legal personality is disregarded as a sham, and directors face criminal prosecution, personal restitution, and travel restrictions.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Act fines and annual declaration penalties up to BZD 20,000; CRS/FATCA penalties; administrative strike-off by the Registrar; the IBC loses limited liability and cannot enforce contracts after strike-off; foreign exchanges may de-risk and close accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are subject to AML-derived fines, asset freezing orders and travel bans; where the shell was used to disguise illicit trade flows, directors face personal civil restitution and up to 14 years' imprisonment under the Money Laundering (Prevention) Act.
Money Laundering and Trade-Based AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering (Prevention) Act 1996 (as amended), on indictment: fine up to BZD 500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 14 years; confiscation of proceeds; mandatory revocation of the Customs Brokers licence and deregistration of the IBC.

Director personal liability: The nominated Compliance Officer/MLRO, directors and any person in effective control face identical criminal penalties, joint and several civil liability for laundered amounts, international extradition exposure, and prohibition from holding any financial-services or customs licence.
Chronic Non-Filing and Administrative Strike-Off

Statutory sanction: Late penalties, licence forfeiture, automatic administrative strike-off under the Companies Act; the struck-off IBC's property vests in the Registrar and its contracts become unenforceable; reopening requires court restoration with a BZD 200 fee plus all arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors remain personally liable for all debts and taxes accrued after strike-off; the statutory limitation shield is lifted; individuals may be summonsed, subjected to a travel ban, and held in contempt for failing to produce corporate records to the Tax Service Department.
