Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service Department (BTSD), Ministry of Finance, Government of Belize; principal e-portal: https://taxservice.gov.bz (BTSD e-services for TIN, VAT and business tax filings); company records are maintained by the Companies and Corporate Affairs Registry (CCAR); export declarations are processed by the Belize Customs and Excise Department (Ministry of Finance) via ASYCUDA World.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Engage a licensed registered agent in Belize; the agent files the IBC incorporation application with the CCAR under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270, as amended by the IBC (Amendment) Act 2023). Step 2: Upon incorporation, the CCAR issues the Certificate of Incorporation and automatically generates the company's Tax Identification Number (TIN), recorded on the company's business registration record; a separate TIN application is not normally required for locally incorporated IBCs. Step 3: A foreign entity electing to continue or redomicile as a Belize IBC files the continuation application through the registered agent, and the BTSD assigns a Belize TIN upon issuing the Certificate of Continuation. Step 4: For CRS/AEOI and economic substance purposes, the registered agent must hold the TIN certificate, file the IBC annual return with CCAR, and submit the annual economic substance filing; the IBC must also maintain its beneficial ownership record. Step 5: If the IBC makes local taxable supplies in Belize exceeding the VAT threshold, it must separately register for VAT with the BTSD; the IBC is exempt from Belize business license fees and exchange controls, but the DG forwarding activity requires the relevant customs broker and dangerous goods transport permits in the operating country.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Belize does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime and offers no hybrid mismatch election for IBCs. Available statutory mechanisms are: (1) change-of-status election under the IBC Act, whereby an IBC may elect to convert into an ordinary Belize domestic company, becoming tax resident and subject to Belize corporate income tax at the 25% rate and business tax; (2) voluntary VAT registration election under the Value Added Tax Act 2004 for exporters making local taxable supplies below the compulsory threshold; (3) re-domiciliation and continuation in and out of Belize under the IBC Act to place the entity under a selected tax-neutral domicile; and (4) compliance-driven self-certification under the Economic Substance (Amendment) Act 2021/2022, under which a tax-exempt IBC deriving relevant activity income must demonstrate core income-generating activity and adequate physical substance in Belize or report to the BTSD. Treaty-based elections are unavailable because Belize's limited tax treaty network does not extend to tax-exempt IBC entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

BZD 75,000 annual taxable turnover (VAT Act 2004 compulsory registration threshold); voluntary registration is available for exporters. The offshore DG forwarding IBC is outside the Belize VAT net unless it supplies services to persons in Belize or carries on local taxable activity in Belize.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated when the goods are physically removed from Belize and the exporter holds a valid Customs Export Declaration. Services, including international freight forwarding of dangerous goods (ISIC 5229 / NAICS 488510), are zero-rated as exported services when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Belize at the time of supply, and the benefit of the service is received entirely outside Belize, or the service is directly in connection with goods exported from Belize. The DG forwarding IBC must evidence a genuine international transport or agency contract with a foreign principal, actual movement of the regulated hazmat cargo across a Belize or third-country border, and receipt of consideration in foreign currency. The service is not zero-rated if rendered to a Belize-resident person or if the cargo remains within Belize.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World export entry / SAD-format document) filed with the Belize Customs and Excise Department for the dangerous goods consignment
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing international carriage of the DG cargo, naming the IBC as shipper or principal and the foreign buyer as consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing collection of the freight revenue from the non-resident counterparty
  • Dangerous goods compliance file: IMO Dangerous Goods Declaration / IATA DGR Shipper's Declaration, Safety Data Sheet, and the applicable DG transport permit or emergency response documentation
  • Commercial freight invoice, signed forwarding or agency contract, Letter of Credit documentation, and the IBC's TIN certificate and VAT registration certificate where registered

No statutory real-time B2B/B2C fiscal clearance or API-based e-invoicing clearance regime is in force in Belize. Registered persons must issue compliant VAT invoices, whether paper or electronic, containing the prescribed particulars; file VAT returns electronically via the BTSD e-services portal; and retain electronic records for 5 years. Excise goods such as fuels, alcohol and tobacco are subject to physical excise stamps and digital fiscal marking under the Excise Duty Act. Export declarations and customs data are processed through the Belize Customs and Excise Department's ASYCUDA World system, which functions as the fiscal clearance point for export transactions.

The BTSD and Customs may audit export zero-rating claims and refunds within the statutory assessment window and beyond in cases of fraud or wilful default. Civil consequences include disallowance and retrospective clawback of the zero-rating applied to the exported services or goods, recovery of VAT at 12.5% with interest, and additional penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded for deliberate under-declaration or false refund claims under the VAT Act 2004. Fraudulent export claims additionally attract fines up to BZD 10,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years, per-company director and officer liability, and forfeiture of the goods or conveyances under the Customs and Excise Duty Act. IBC-specific risk: failure to maintain economic substance or transport substance records triggers fines up to BZD 25,000 per period and possible striking-off; false statements to tax or customs authorities are treated as fraudulent evasion with retrospective recovery of all input tax credits previously refunded.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belize dollar (BZD) is pegged to the US dollar at 2:1 under a fixed exchange-rate arrangement. Current-account convertibility (trade in goods, services, and financing relating to trade) is generally free for non-resident entities through authorized dealers without prior CBB approval. Capital-account transactions, including equity injections by residents, loan disbursements, and portfolio transfers, require case-by-case approval. Belgian International Business Companies (IBCs) are treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes, enabling them to hold foreign-currency accounts, receive/remit funds in USD/EUR/GBP, and repatriate profits without specific CBB license, provided that all counterparties are non-residents and no local currency conversion is involved. Profit-remittance hurdles arise only if funds are routed through the resident banking system or if the IBC is deemed resident for exchange-control purposes (management and control test).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Section 5 of the Exchange Control Act, resident exporters must surrender all export proceeds to an authorized dealer (bank) within 180 days of shipment and convert the proceeds into BZD at the official peg rate. Non-resident IBCs that do not operate 'in or from Belize' and do not maintain a resident place of business are legally exempt from surrender obligations; however, if the IBC's export invoices are cleared through a Belize onshore bank or if the CBB determines that the IBC has 'managed resident' status due to local management, the 180-day repatriation and mandatory conversion (100% of proceeds) will be enforced. For dangerous-goods forwarding, a letter from the licensed DG logistics authority may be required to document the non-resident status and to secure a formal exemption certificate from the CBB.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB), with the Ministry of Finance exercising final authority over exchange control matters under the Exchange Control Act (Cap. 40).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low. Belize has no resident EMI (E-Money Institution) licensing regime, and foreign EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-regulated or EU PSD2-licensed) generally refuse onboarding of Belize IBCs operating in the high-risk dangerous-goods forwarding sector due to correspondent-bank restrictions. EMIs that do accept offshore companies are typically restricted to non-regulated sectors and require full source-of-funds documentation, prevetting of each trade counterparty, and repayment of the IBC's freight charges through GMT (global money transfer) rails that may not support LC operations. Alternative: a licensed EMI in a third-party jurisdiction (e.g., the UK or Singapore) with a dedicated relationship manager and a willingness to accept Belize IBCs can be used as a sub-account for pre-funded distribution; however, primary clearing must be through a commercial bank to support letters of credit and documentary collections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols are mandated under the Belize Criminal Code (Money Laundering) and the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) regulations. For a capital injection into the IBC's escrow account, the sending institution must use a SWIFT MT103 with the field 70 (Remittance Information) populated as 'CAPITAL INJECTION - BELIZE IBC UNDER ISIC 5229 - DG FREIGHT' and field 59 (Beneficiary Bank Identification) referencing the IBC's exact legal name and registered office. The purpose code in the sender's foreign-exchange reporting system should be 'M14 - Initial Capital Contribution by Nonresident' or its equivalent. The Belize receiving bank will require a certified copy of the IBC's Certificate of Incorporation and a Board Resolution proving the source of funds (verified by a bank statement from the investing shareholder). Inbound capital in BZD is prohibited; all capital must be held in foreign currency (USD/EUR). If funds originate from a third-country related party, a written loan or equity agreement with an AIDE (Arm's-Length Intra-Group Documentation) statement must be appended to the payment instruction to prevent reclassification as a resident-company receipt.Primary clearing rails for DG forwarding settlements include (i) SWIFT MT103 for operational payments to carriers, agents, and insurers; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance 60-90 days) for high-value cargo where the importer's bank pays the IBC through a confirming bank in the United States or London; (iii) Documentary Collection (DP/DA) under URC 522 for lower-risk transactions. Each outbound payment instruction must cite the underlying invoice number and DG classification code (e.g., UN 1824 for sodium hydroxide). The Belize receiving bank's anti-money-laundering policies require payment matching against a pre-approved 'trade rationalization matrix' covering the UN transport window and the corresponding hazardous-materials endorsement. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low. Belize has no resident EMI (E-Money Institution) licensing regime, and foreign EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-regulated or EU PSD2-licensed) generally refuse onboarding of Belize IBCs operating in the high-risk dangerous-goods forwarding sector due to correspondent-bank restrictions. EMIs that do accept offshore companies are typically restricted to non-regulated sectors and require full source-of-funds documentation, prevetting of each trade counterparty, and repayment of the IBC's freight charges through GMT (global money transfer) rails that may not support LC operations. Alternative: a licensed EMI in a third-party jurisdiction (e.g., the UK or Singapore) with a dedicated relationship manager and a willingness to accept Belize IBCs can be used as a sub-account for pre-funded distribution; however, primary clearing must be through a commercial bank to support letters of credit and documentary collections.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — International Business Companies (IBCs) are statutorily exempt from Belize corporate income tax under Section 6(1)(a) of the International Business Companies Act (Chapter 270, Laws of Belize); the onshore standard corporate rate under the Income and Business Tax Act is 25%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — dividends paid by a Belize IBC to non-resident shareholders are wholly exempt from Belize withholding tax under the IBC Act exemption; no advance clearance or tax deduction is required for the distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No express statutory Belize Local File or Master File threshold exists. As a compliance benchmark, maintain full OECD-aligned transfer pricing documentation and arm's-length benchmarking wherever the IBC books annual cross-border related-party revenue above BZD 500,000 (approximately US$250,000), because the IFSC ESR questionnaire tests whether margins, headcount, and expenditure are proportionate to related-party freight commissions.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The IBC (Amendment) Act 2019 and the International Business Companies (Economic Substance) Rules 2019 treat specialized hazardous-goods forwarding (ISIC 5229) as a 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity. The IBC must demonstrate in each financial year: (a) direction and management exercised in Belize (real board meetings with quorum in Belize), (b) core income-generating activities performed in Belize, (c) adequate physical premises and a proportionate number of employees in Belize, and (d) adequate annual operating expenditure in Belize. The IFSC may exchange the substance data with foreign tax authorities and the OECD Global Forum under the exchange-of-information framework.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Mischaracterization of Belize-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the entire income at the 25% standard corporate rate under the Income and Business Tax Act; addition of a penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded; statutory default interest on the unpaid tax; prosecution for tax evasion with monetary fines and confiscation orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and the ultimate beneficial owner are jointly and severally liable for the reassessed tax and penalties; the corporate veil is pierced; courts may impose travel bans, asset-freezing orders, disqualification from serving as directors, and custodial sentences upon conviction.
Shell Company — Economic Substance Default

Statutory sanction: Rejection of the ESR; financial penalties per infraction year with additional daily penalties for continued default; mandatory reporting to the OECD Global Forum and the Belize Income Tax Department; administrative strike-off and dissolution of the IBC; loss of the 0% tax exemption with deemed assessment in gross.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or authorized a knowingly false ESR face personal criminal fines and imprisonment for false statements; restoration of a struck-off company is ordered only at the directors' personal cost; the IFSC may publish the directors' names and prohibit re-entry into any Belize regulated activity.
AML — Dangerous Goods Freight Fraud / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Money Laundering and Terrorism (Prevention) Act with substantial monetary fines, forfeiture of cargo and proceeds, suspension or revocation of the DG forwarding license, and revocation of the IBC registration by the IFSC for unlawful purposes; correspondent banks will treat the company as high-risk and may terminate banking relations.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability for the laundering predicate offences, including seizure of personal assets under preservation orders, travel bans, extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties, and custodial sentences; the corporate veil is pierced for civil recovery of any criminally derived proceeds.
Beneficial Ownership — False Declarations to the Registry

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties imposed on the company and its registered agent under the Belize Beneficial Ownership Act; deregistration of the offending agent; referral to the Financial Intelligence Unit; the IBC is flagged to the OECD as a non-compliant entity and subject to immediate strike-off.

Director personal liability: The beneficial owner and directors are personally liable for the concealment, with criminal fines and imprisonment for false declarations; regulatory authorities may issue director disqualification orders, travel restrictions, and civil restitution orders against the individuals who controlled the misrepresentation.
