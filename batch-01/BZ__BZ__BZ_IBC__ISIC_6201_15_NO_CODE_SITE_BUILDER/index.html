Applicable AML statute: The principal AML/CFT statute is the Money Laundering and Terrorism (Prevention) Act, 2019 ('MLTPA'), which repealed and replaced the Money Laundering (Prevention) Act (Cap. 104) and governs customer due diligence, reporting obligations, and the powers of the Financial Intelligence Unit of Belize. The IBC Act (in particular the provisions governing registers of directors, of members, and of beneficial owners, together with the beneficial-ownership amendment) requires every IBC to maintain at its licensed registered office a current Register of Directors, Register of Members, and Register of Beneficial Owners, and to produce such registers to the Registrar of International Business Companies or to the Financial Intelligence Unit upon lawful demand. The International Financial Services Commission ('IFSC') and the IBC Registry apply binding directives, guidance notes and risk-based AML/CFT rules issued under the MLTPA and the Financial Intelligence Unit Act, as amended.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is not permissible as the statutory registered office. The IBC Act and the IBC Registry require every IBC to maintain a physical registered office in Belize, which must be the office of a licensed registered agent appointed under the Act; the registered agent must hold the Register of Members, Register of Directors, Register of Beneficial Owners, and the company seal at that physical address. A virtual office or online-only correspondence address may be used only for ancillary business operations, such as customer support for the no-code website builder platform; it cannot satisfy the statutory registered-office or agent mandate. If the no-code SaaS business generates intellectual property income, the IBC must also document local substance (board meetings, local management, servers or contracted researchers in Belize) to meet the International Business Companies (Economic Substance) Act, 2019 requirements for intellectual property businesses or distribution and service centre activities, as applicable.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or paid-in capital deposit requirement. Standard practice sets authorized capital at US$50,000 (5,000 ordinary shares of US$10 each) as the zero-fee band; shares may be issued for cash, services, or promissory notes, and no capital duty is exigible at incorporation. One shareholder and one director are sufficient; non-resident ownership is expressly permitted.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Belize National Fire Service, structural load inspection by the Central Building Committee, and workplace clearance from the Labour Department under the Occupational Safety and Health Act are not triggered by a virtual registered office. If a physical office or server room is opened later, the IBC must obtain a building permit, fire certificate under the Fire Services Act (Cap. 237), and an occupational safety clearance before occupancy.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service, Fiscal Policy and Tax Administration Division, Ministry of Finance, Economic Development and Investment; e-Services portal: https://taxservices.gov.bz

E-government filing portal

A Belize International Business Company (IBC) is registered by its licensed registered agent with the Belize International Financial Services Commission (IFSC) under the International Business Companies Act. The IBC does not obtain a conventional Belize TIN because it is statutorily tax-exempt; instead, the IFSC issues the Certificate of Incorporation and the registered agent applies to the Belize Tax Service for a Certificate of Tax Exemption / Non-Resident Status when required by foreign banks or correspondent institutions. If the IBC subsequently undertakes taxable activity within Belize, the registered agent must file a VAT/Employer registration application with the Belize Tax Service to obtain a TIN and VAT registration number, using the same e-Services portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident International Business Company excluded from Belize income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, stamp duty on offshore transactions, and VAT on offshore digital services; no entity-level taxation in Belize).

Available election forms

Belize has no US-style check-the-box election and no statutory hybrid mismatch election. The IBC tax exemption is automatic under the IBC Act rather than elective. An IBC may elect in its constitutional documents to be non-resident and exempt from exchange control; the company may also voluntarily elect to register for VAT in Belize to recover input tax or facilitate zero-rated cross-border supplies, although such registration is not required for purely offshore business. Foreign parent entities may make their own transparency or CFC elections under their home-country tax law, but those elections are not recognized as Belize statutory tax elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

BZD 100,000 in annual taxable supplies (approx. USD 50,000); a non-resident IBC with no Belize-source supplies generally remains below the registration threshold and is not required to register for VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available to a VAT-registered supplier where the goods are exported from Belize within the statutory time window, or where services are supplied to a non-resident recipient and the benefit and place of supply are located outside Belize, including offshore digital and no-code software services offered by an IBC. The supplier must retain evidence that consideration is received from abroad and may claim a corresponding input VAT credit in Belize; the IBC's non-resident tax-exempt status further removes Belize VAT on foreign-derived SaaS and website builder revenue.

  • Validated Belize Customs Export Declaration / Export Entry
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed packing list
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or alternative transport evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice showing foreign exchange receipt
  • Non-resident customer service contract, certificates of residence, or place-of-consumption evidence for digital services

Belize does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, online invoice stamping, SAF-T, or API-based continuous transaction controls. Registered businesses must issue a full electronic or paper VAT invoice within 30 days, file VAT returns through the Belize Tax Service e-Tax portal, retain digital ledgers, and keep export documentation available for inspection. No Belizean government API clearance is required for B2B or B2C e-invoicing.

The Commissioner of the Belize Tax Service may issue an assessment for unreported VAT at 12.5%, plus statutory interest and an additional penalty up to 100% of the tax due. Fraudulent zero-rating or export-refund claims also attract criminal penalties of a fine not exceeding BZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to two years under the Value Added Tax Act. Any VAT input credit or export refund is clawed back retrospectively if the exporter fails to produce the full documentation bundle, fails to bring consideration into Belize within 180 days, or is found to have mischaracterized services as offshore to obtain zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belize dollar is convertible for current-account purposes under a rigid peg of 2.00 BZD = 1.00 USD, maintained by the CBB since 1976; capital-account transactions by BZD residents require CBB exchange-control approval. The non-resident IBC operates in a de facto unrestricted convertibility zone: the IBC Act (Chapter 270) disapplies the Exchange Control Act for foreign-currency dealings with non-residents, so the IBC may invoice in USD, hold USD/EUR/GBP accounts at authorized dealers, and remit dividends free of CBB approval and without Belize withholding tax. Residual hurdles are practical, not legal: all USD wires clear through US correspondent banks applying OFAC and sanctions screening; the IBC cannot fund in BZD from Belize residents; and annual IFSC license and registry filings must remain current to preserve non-resident, tax-exempt status. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, with no dividend withholding and no foreign-exchange surrender obligation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory surrender rules bind only BZD residents: resident exporters must repatriate proceeds within six months and sell the full foreign-currency amount (effectively a 100% conversion obligation) to an authorized dealer within the prescribed CBB window. The non-resident IBC is exempt from all surrender timelines and conversion ratios, provided no in-scope software revenue is received in BZD from Belize residents; any such receipt would trigger the resident rules and imperil the IBC exemption. The treasury must nonetheless demand USD-denominated settlements from Paddle and Stripe Billing, annotate inbound MT103 messages as 'Export of Software Services - ISIC 6201', and retain gateway settlement reports to evidence the source and service character of all proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB), constituted under the Central Bank of Belize Act (Chapter 262, Revised Edition 2020); the CBB administers the Exchange Control Act (Chapter 35), issues exchange-control notices, operates the fixed 2.00 BZD/USD peg, and supervises authorized dealers. The International Financial Services Commission (IFSC), constituted under the International Financial Services Commission Act (Chapter 272), independently registers and supervises the International Business Company (IBC) for annual-license, beneficial-ownership, and economic-substance compliance.

Compliant routing mandates USD-denominated SWIFT MT103 wires: equity injections from founders are tagged 'Capital Contribution to BZ IBC - ISIC 6201' and must be matched to the register of members with a board resolution and capital-contribution agreement; operating revenue arrives as MT103 settlements from Paddle (merchant of record) and, where a supported-country entity is used, from Stripe Billing, tagged 'Software Subscription Sales - No-Code Site Builder'. Each inbound credit is screened against CBB AML/CFT reporting thresholds; the receiving banker requires gateway settlement reports, underlying subscriber invoices, and a source-of-funds declaration for wires above the bank's reporting threshold. All injections are recorded as paid-in surplus and disclosed in the annual IFSC return to preserve the tax-exempt classification; the IBC must not receive BZD from Belize residents, and no cash currency should be physically transported into Belize.Outbound settlements for cloud hosting, domain renewals, subcontracted software development, IFSC agent fees, and professional services execute via SWIFT MT103 direct credits from the IBC's USD account through the Belize bank's US correspondent network with standard 1-3 business-day value dating; BZD clearing is unavailable to the non-resident IBC, and any local Belize-dollar expense must be converted through the authorized dealer at the 2:1 peg. Commercial letters of credit (MT700) are legally available but economically unsuitable for SaaS disbursements; the governing rail is open-account MT103 with a dated invoice and underlying contract retained for audit. Related-party payments require arm's-length documentation under Belize transfer-pricing principles, and all outbound wires above the bank's threshold demand board authorization recorded in minutes.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/EUR and USD/GBP to lock Paddle and Stripe settlement conversions where the gateway remits in non-USD currencyDeliverable outright FX forwards with the Belize authorized dealer for hedged conversion of excess EUR/GBP balances into USDCross-currency swaps with OECD-listed counterparties for matched funding of EUR-denominated operating expensesVanilla FX options and zero-cost collars on USD/EUR for long-dated subscription revenue exceeding USD 250,000 annual net settlementNatural hedging through a multi-currency (USD/EUR/GBP) sub-ledger to minimize conversion frequency and spread costs

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Constrained / Moderate-Low. Belize has no domestic electronic-money-institution or digital-bank licensing track, and the CBB does not charter pass-through fintech accounts; international EMIs generally classify Belizean IBCs as higher risk because of prior CFATF mutual-evaluation follow-up and global de-risking pressure, and the no-code model's intangible digital revenue without physical subcontracts compounds the concern. Wise and Airwallex will typically decline a pure Belize IBC without audited financials, directorship history, and 12 months of trading data; Payoneer offers limited Belizean residence onboarding but does not substitute for a corporate banking relationship. The implementable architecture is hybrid: Paddle serves as merchant of record (acquires customer cards, remits net USD by MT103), Stripe Billing is reached only through a supported-country group entity under arm's-length intercompany service agreements, and liquidity concentrates in the Belize tier-1 account to satisfy CBB supervisory expectations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BZD 2,000,000 (approximately USD 1,000,000) aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions within the IBC group - administrative OECD-aligned threshold used by the Belize Tax Service Department Transfer Pricing Guidelines; a Master File is expected where the ultimate group's consolidated revenue exceeds BZD 400,000,000 (approximately USD 200,000,000); country-by-country reporting applies on a best-efforts basis to MNE groups exceeding the OECD EUR 750,000,000 threshold

Economic substance scope: The Tax Information Exchange (Economic Substance) Act, 2019 applies to every Belize-resident IBC carrying on a 'relevant activity'. A no-code website builder holding proprietary software is an 'intellectual property business'; a template reseller or infrastructure-only operator may be a 'distribution and service center'. The IBC must show in Belize: (1) direction and management of the activity; (2) performance of core income-generating activities; (3) adequate employees, premises, and operating expenditure. IP businesses must also satisfy the OECD modified nexus test (nexus ratio of qualifying expenditure). Pure equity-holding IBCs benefit from a reduced substance test. A solo founder operating remotely with no Belize office, employees, or board meetings in Belize will fail the ESR test and trigger automatic information exchange

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Fine up to BZD 250,000 (about USD 125,000) for significant non-compliance; daily default penalties up to BZD 10,000; mandatory automatic exchange of information with the tax authority of the beneficial owner; administrative strike-off and loss of the 50-year tax exemption

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for economic substance offences, including imprisonment up to 3 years for false declarations; civil restitution orders may attach to personal assets; the registered agent may be deregistered and the director subject to a travel ban where the IBC founder is assessed as a shadow director
Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to BZD 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Income and Business Tax Act, 2023 and the IBC Act; forfeiture of the statutory exemption; retroactive assessment of 25% corporate tax on deemed Belize-source income plus interest and late-payment penalties

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors, shareholder-directors and shadow directors may be jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; civil recovery and confiscation proceedings under the Proceeds of Crime Act; travel bans, restraint orders and extradition exposure for aggravated evasion
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines from BZD 10,000 up to BZD 100,000 per infraction; suspension of the IBC certificate; mandatory disclosure to the Financial Intelligence Unit; blacklisting of the IBC's bank and payment-rail accounts; potential confiscation of funds held in the Stripe/Paddle settlement account

Director personal liability: Money laundering is a predicate offence carrying imprisonment up to 14 years and unlimited fines; directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability even where the corporate entity is struck off; travel bans and international freezing orders commonly accompany prosecution
Late Filing / Non-Payment of Government Fees

Statutory sanction: Late filing surcharge of BZD 1,000 with continuing monthly penalties; administrative dissolution of the IBC after continued default; after dissolution the company loses legal capacity to enforce its SaaS agreements, hold IP registrations, or maintain the Stripe/Paddle merchant contract

Director personal liability: Directors who contract on behalf of a dissolved IBC may be personally liable on those contracts; the registered agent may resign, triggering conversion to a non-compliant entity and exposure of the beneficial owner to personal liability for post-dissolution liabilities
