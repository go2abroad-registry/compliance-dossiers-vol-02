Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Belize operates a statutory dual-account exchange control regime distinguishing current account and capital account transactions. The Belize dollar (BZD) is contractually pegged to the US dollar at BZD 2.00 = USD 1.00 with convertibility for current account payments available upon documentary verification (invoice, contract, or tax filing) through any authorized dealer. Capital account outflows by Belize residents require CBB permission under delegated thresholds; non-resident persons and companies designated as non-resident are exempt. An International Business Company (IBC) qualifying as non-resident for exchange control purposes (i.e., with non-resident beneficial ownership and management and control exercised outside Belize) may freely maintain, convert, and repatriate USD balances without CBB permit or surrender duty. Profit remittances and dividend distributions by such an IBC are not subject to CBB approval, Belize withholding tax, or capital controls. The IBC loses this exemption if it is re-characterized as a Belize tax resident under the Income and Business Tax Act (as amended by the International Business Companies (Amendment) Act 2019) or if economic substance and management are relocated to Belize; in that circumstance capital account transfers and profit remittances fall within the resident control perimeter and require CBB authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Regulation 10 of the Exchange Control Regulations, Belize residents must receive the full export value of goods and services in foreign currency within the prescribed period (generally 180 days for goods and 90 days for services) and must offer the foreign currency to an authorized dealer in exchange for BZD at the official pegged rate within one month of receipt, unless the CBB grants an exemption or retention license. Non-resident IBCs are wholly exempt from this surrender obligation; the statutory basis is the IBC Act (Cap. 270) which deems qualifying IBCs non-resident and places them outside the Exchange Control Regulations. A PropTech/SaaS IBC receiving USD subscription revenues from global clients may therefore retain those proceeds indefinitely in a foreign currency account with a Belize licensed bank or an offshore correspondent without forced conversion into BZD. If, however, the CBB determines the IBC is managed and controlled in Belize, all USD export receipts from subscription software services (treated as international supply of services under ISIC 6201) must be surrendered to an authorized dealer within 90 days of receipt.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB), Exchange Control Department, acting under the Exchange Control Act (Cap. 264 of the Laws of Belize) and the Exchange Control Regulations 1963 (as amended). The CBB administers all exchange control approvals, issues authorized dealer licenses to commercial banks, and supervises compliance with the Money Laundering and Terrorism (Prevention) Act 2013 and CBB AML/CFT Guidelines.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for the Belize IBC entity directly; MEDIUM-HIGH via a U.S. or U.K. subsidiary structure. Global EMIs and neobanks (Wise Business, Mercury, Relay, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) do not accept Belize-incorporated IBCs as direct customers due to internal AML restricted-jurisdiction policies, U.S. money transmitter licensing constraints, and Stripe's merchant country coverage list which excludes Belize. Workable alternatives: (i) open a U.S. bank account for a Delaware LLC or Florida corporation owned by the Belize IBC, using the U.S. entity as the Stripe merchant of record, with treasury sweeps up to the Belize parent via SWIFT; or (ii) use a Belize-licensed bank's digital corporate portal (e.g., Atlantic Bank's electronic banking with ACH/Fedwire access) rather than an unregulated offshore EMI. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan funding must be conducted via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the Belize IBC's USD account with the authorized dealer, with narrative field 70 referencing 'EQUITY INJECTION - [IBC NAME]' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN - [IBC NAME]', and a uniform purpose code such as P010 (equity investment) or P080 (intercompany loan) under the IMF BPM6-aligned reporting framework. No CBB approval is required for non-resident IBC capital contributions, but the receiving bank will record the remittance on a Certificate of Inward Remittance and may apply a 21-day clearing hold for first-time remitters. Routing must avoid crypto exchanges, cash deposit desks, and jurisdictions on the FATF action list; the cleanest corridors are the United States (Fedwire via correspondent), United Kingdom, Canada, and EU/EEA banks licensed under CRD/MiFID. For vendor payments exceeding USD 50,000 or involving cross-border goods procurement, the recommended clearing rail is an irrevocable documentary letter of credit issued under UCP 600 by the Belize bank with confirmation from a New York or London bank, ensuring trade-validated settlement. Recurring USD vendor payments within the United States (e.g., AWS, Stripe, payroll processors) may be executed through FedACH Direct, with a daily aggregate de-minimis limit of USD 250,000 per account under the bank's internal exposure policy; any excess requires wire pre-funding and same-day authorization. Global EMIs and neobanks (Wise Business, Mercury, Relay, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) do not accept Belize-incorporated IBCs as direct customers due to internal AML restricted-jurisdiction policies, U.S. money transmitter licensing constraints, and Stripe's merchant country coverage list which excludes Belize. Workable alternatives: (i) open a U.S. bank account for a Delaware LLC or Florida corporation owned by the Belize IBC, using the U.S. entity as the Stripe merchant of record, with treasury sweeps up to the Belize parent via SWIFT; or (ii) use a Belize-licensed bank's digital corporate portal (e.g., Atlantic Bank's electronic banking with ACH/Fedwire access) rather than an unregulated offshore EMI. Legal constraints: Belize's International Financial Services Commission (IFSC) does not license standalone EMI businesses, and Belize banks act as the regulated gatekeepers for all electronic money movement; any unlicensed EMI facilitating Belize IBC payments would trigger enforcement under the Money Laundering and Terrorism (Prevention) Act.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (IBC statutory exemption; domestic Belize standard corporate rate is 25% but is inapplicable to an International Business Company in the Tax_Exempt_Zone).

0% — dividends and other distributions paid by a Belize IBC to its non-resident shareholders are statutorily exempt from Belize withholding tax under section 140 of the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270); no dividend WHT is imposed and the exemption is unaffected by the PropTech SaaS source of profits provided no Belize-resident trading occurred.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory BZD threshold applies: Belize imposes no OECD-aligned Local File or Master File documentation obligation on exempt IBCs. A residual arm's length rule applies only to domestic Belize-source trading income under the Income Tax Act, which is inapplicable to an exempt IBC. As a prudent practice, where intercompany SaaS royalties or management fees exceed BZD 10,000,000 annually, the IBC should prepare CUP or TNMM documentation to preempt a substance challenge or reclassification.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act, 2019 and its Regulations apply to every Belize IBC carrying on a relevant activity. A PropTech SaaS company providing rental screening and subscription management services to U.S. property managers will generally be classified as a distribution and service centre business or, where the software itself is the income-generating asset, an intellectual property business; both categories require demonstrable economic presence in Belize, including CIGA, premises, staff, and expenditure proportionate to profits. A pure equity holding IBC remains a relevant activity but benefits from reduced substance requirements. Non-compliance with ESR is a strict-liability offense and cannot be cured by retrospective board minutes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Forfeiture of IBC Exemption

Statutory sanction: Loss of IBC exemption with retroactive effect; all profits are re-taxed at the 25% domestic corporate rate with back assessments; penalties of up to 150% of the tax undercharged; default interest of 3% per month; the Registrar cancels the IBC registration and transactions executed during the tainted period become voidable.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the annual exemption declaration or ESR are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax through veil piercing; civil restitution may be sought by the tax authority; travel bans and departure controls may be imposed; imprisonment for up to 3 years applies to those who knowingly make false returns.
Shell Company and Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Fixed ESR penalty of BZD 10,000 plus a continuing default penalty of up to BZD 2,500 per day; the Competent Authority may compulsorily exchange information under CRS/FATCA-type mechanisms with foreign tax administrations; failure to remedy leads to automatic strike-off, loss of the right to enforce SaaS contracts in Belize, and disqualification of the entity.

Director personal liability: Each director is jointly and severally liable for the corporate penalty debt where non-compliance results from their gross negligence; courts may issue worldwide freezing orders over the directors' personal assets; imprisonment for up to 2 years applies for false substance declarations and directors may be permanently disqualified from acting as officers in Belize.
AML, Beneficial Ownership, and Financial Intelligence Unit Referral

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty of up to BZD 250,000 per violation under the Beneficial Ownership and AML/CFT framework, plus confiscation of hidden proceeds; referral to the Financial Intelligence Unit for money-laundering investigation; the IBC may be dissolved with assets escheated to the Crown and the entity blacklisted for 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of record are civilly liable for restitution to defrauded investors and for the corporate penalties; a money-laundering conviction carries imprisonment of up to 5 years, extradition exposure, and permanent loss of any Belize financial services license; non-resident controllers face targeted travel restrictions, denial of entry, and personal asset seizure orders.
