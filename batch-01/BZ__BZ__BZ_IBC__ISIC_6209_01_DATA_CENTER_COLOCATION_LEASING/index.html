Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service, Ministry of Finance, Government of Belize (statutory successor to the Income Tax Department and the GST Department); official e-services portal: https://www.taxservices.gov.bz.

E-government filing portal

An International Business Company (IBC) is incorporated by the Belize Companies Registry and simultaneously licensed by the Belize International Financial Services Commission (IFSC); as a tax-exempt person it receives no income-tax TIN automatically. Where a TIN is required (e.g., voluntary GST registration, economic substance filing, bank account opening, or treaty-residence election), the IFSC-licensed registered agent must submit a TIN application (Form TIN-01) to the Belize Tax Service attaching: (i) certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation; (ii) IFSC IBC licence and Certificate of Good Standing; (iii) memorandum and articles of association; (iv) registered agent consent letter and registered-office address proof; (v) notarized passport copies of all directors, officers, and ultimate beneficial owners; (vi) bank reference letter from the foreign account-holding bank; and (vii) a brief description of the colocation business model. Issuance takes approximately 3-5 business days at no statutory fee. For GST purposes the same TIN is used, and a separate GST registration application must be filed on the portal by a resident representative agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270) and its 2021/2022 amendments: (i) an IBC may elect to become tax resident in Belize, subjecting itself to domestic income tax at 30% on Belize-source income, in exchange for tax-residence certificates and treaty access; (ii) an IBC that is tax resident abroad must file an annual Economic Substance Declaration with the Belize Tax Service, demonstrating that its relevant activity (data-center rack/space leasing falls within the 'financing and leasing' and 'service centre' categories) is performed with adequate physical presence, local management, expenditure, and full-time employees in Belize; (iii) an IBC may voluntarily elect to register for GST notwithstanding its exempt status, in order to zero-rate its export colocation services and recover input tax; (iv) no 'check-the-box' entity classification regime exists in Belize law; conversion to a domestic ordinary Belize company is available but ends the 50-year statutory tax exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

BZD 150,000 per 12-month period for suppliers of goods; BZD 75,000 per 12-month period for suppliers of services. IBCs are treated as exempt persons under the GST Act and are not mandatorily registrable; voluntary registration is permitted only where the IBC makes zero-rated export supplies and wishes to claim input credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of exported goods is zero-rated where the goods are physically exported within 28 days of supply and export evidence is retained. A supply of services is zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident of Belize, the services are not supplied to an establishment in Belize, and the services are either performed wholly outside Belize or, if performed inside Belize, the benefit and use are exclusively outside Belize. For data-center colocation and bare-metal server hosting, zero-rating applies where: (i) the colocation contract names a foreign non-resident counterparty; (ii) the service is billed in foreign currency and paid from an overseas bank account; (iii) the rack space or dedicated servers are physically located in the Belize facility but are exclusively segregated and operated for the non-resident client; (iv) the IBC has voluntarily registered for GST; and (v) no Belize-resident person receives any benefit of the service. Local supply of rack space to Belize residents is taxable at 12.5% and would violate the IBC's non-resident business restriction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) covering any servers or equipment exported within the 28-day zero-rating window
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign-currency credit advice evidencing receipt from the non-resident client
  • Signed colocation service agreement and zero-rated tax invoice identifying the non-resident recipient
  • IFSC-issued IBC licence and Certificate of Good Standing, and where applicable the Belize Tax Service export-relief approval letter

Belize does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, government API validation, or digital stamping system for e-invoices. GST-registered persons must file GST returns and schedules electronically through the Belize Tax Service portal, and taxpayers issuing more than 10 invoices per filing period must upload an XML batch file. Tax invoices must be sequentially numbered, state the supplier's TIN and GST registration number, describe the supply, show the tax amount, and be retained for at least 6 years. Compliance obligations are therefore archival and e-filing based; no pre-clearance authorization exists.

The Belize Tax Service may audit zero-rating claims for up to 6 years retrospectively, extending indefinitely where fraud is suspected. A fraudulent or erroneous export zero-rating claim exposes the IBC to: (i) reassessment of the full 12.5% GST on the consideration; (ii) compound interest at 2% per month on the unpaid tax; (iii) a civil penalty up to 200% of the tax evaded; (iv) criminal fines up to BZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years for knowingly false declarations; (v) mandatory clawback of all input tax credits previously claimed on the zero-rated export class with adjusted assessments for each open quarter; and (vi) administrative revocation of the IBC's voluntary GST registration and, in egregious cases, suspension or striking-off of the IBC licence by the IFSC. Additionally, under the Exchange Control Act, failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days empowers the Ministry of Finance to compel surrender of the proceeds at the official exchange rate, impose exchange-control penalties, and preclude the exporter from future zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belize dollar (BZD) is pegged to the US dollar at a fixed rate of BZD 2.00 = USD 1.00, a peg administered and defended by the Central Bank of Belize since 1976. Current-account payments are freely permitted for non-residents and statutory exempt entities. An International Business Company (IBC) is classified as a non-resident for exchange control purposes and is expressly exempt from the Belize Exchange Control Regulations (Cap. 174); accordingly, it may hold, borrow, disburse, and transfer any foreign currency without prior central bank approval. Capital-account transactions, including shareholder capital injections, share transfers, and profit remittances, are unrestricted for the IBC. Profits, dividends, and liquidation proceeds may be remitted freely to non-resident shareholders with no Belize withholding tax, provided the IBC maintains its licensed registered agent, registered office, beneficial ownership register filing, and any applicable economic substance documentation. Residual friction is documentary rather than regulatory: Belize banks, acting under correspondent-bank pressure, apply enhanced due diligence to any IBC that cannot substantiate its ownership trail and real economic purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations (Cap. 174), resident exporters in Belize are statutorily required to repatriate foreign-currency export proceeds through an authorized dealer within a prescribed timeline (customarily within six months of shipment and subject to any conversion ratio mandated by the Central Bank of Belize). However, the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270) expressly exempts IBCs from the Exchange Control Regulations; consequently, export proceeds and operating revenues of the IBC are not subject to mandatory surrender or conversion. For the data centre colocation model, revenues invoiced in USD to international clients may be lawfully retained in USD, held offshore, or credited to the IBC's Belize USD account without any surrender obligation, subject only to the bank's internal transaction documentation requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB), acting through its Exchange Control Department and Banking Supervision Department.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low. Belize IBCs are generally ineligible for onboarding at leading US and EU digital EMI and neobank platforms (e.g., Mercury, Wise Business, and most UK EMIs) because Belize is classified as a higher-risk offshore jurisdiction by automated AML engines and because of correspondent banking restrictions. A limited number of international EMI platforms accept Belize IBCs in USD/EUR subject to enhanced due diligence, substantial documentary uploads, and full beneficial ownership disclosure; such relationships are fragile and may be terminated without cause. Legally, no provision of Belize law prohibits an IBC from using an offshore digital EMI; however, the superior execution path is to maintain the primary operating account at a tier-1 Belize commercial bank with direct USD correspondent relationships and, where commercially necessary, supplement it with a second account at a fully regulated foreign bank in a stable jurisdiction. Reliance on unregulated EMIs as the sole treasury rail is not recommended for a colocation business with recurring infrastructure expenditures.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: shareholder funds are transmitted from the investor's bank via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank transfers) to the IBC's USD account at the Belize tier-1 bank. The wire must carry a clear remittance reference, for example 'Share capital contribution - subscription No. [X] under board resolution dated [date]'. The bank's compliance team releases escrowed funds only after: (i) the board resolution and share subscription certificate are matched; (ii) source-of-funds documentation is verified; and (iii) internal name-and-country sanctions screening is cleared. Alternative compliant route: funds may be routed through a wholly owned foreign treasury intermediary before reaching the Belize account, provided full beneficial ownership disclosure is made to the Belize bank to avoid any structuring red flag. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — International Business Company (IBC) registered under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270, Laws of Belize) is wholly exempt from Belize income tax and business tax on worldwide profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Belize imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions made by an IBC to shareholders resident in any jurisdiction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in statutory zero-rate form: Belize's Income and Business Tax Act imposes no specified Local File/Master File threshold (in BZD or USD) on IBCs because IBCs are outside the charge to tax. The operative benchmark is the Economic Substance Act threshold: an IBC deriving gross colocation/leasing income from an ISIC 6209 data center activity must file ESR returns regardless of revenue amount (no monetary de minimis); where attributable income must be tested, Belize applies the OECD arm's-length standard by treaty interpretation, with a soft threshold of BZD 1,000,000 in controlled transactions requiring an internal ALP memorandum.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2019 (as amended) binds every Belize company and foreign company registered in Belize carrying on 'relevant activities' — a data center colocation and bare-metal server leasing operation is a leasing relevant activity. The IBC must demonstrate: (a) direction and management in Belize (board meetings and minute-taking in Belize); (b) adequate local employees, physical premises, and sufficient operating expenditure proportionate to the colocation rental income; and (c) the core income-generating activity (provision, maintenance, and billing of rack space and servers) performed in Belize or through a local delegation arrangement. Failure results in BZ$25,000 civil penalties plus BZ$5,000 per day, mandatory exchange of information with foreign revenue authorities, and — for repeat offenders — loss of the IBC's zero-tax status and striking-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abuse of Tax-Exempt IBC Status

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the IBC certificate and removal of the statutory exemption retroactively, leaving the IBC chargeable on worldwide profits at the domestic 25% corporate income tax rate plus business tax on gross receipts with interest on arrears; criminal prosecution under the Income and Business Tax Act (Cap. 275) for tax evasion exposing the IBC to fines up to BZ$100,000 and imprisonment of directors for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Belize courts may pierce the corporate veil and issue civil restitution orders requiring directors to pay the unpaid IBC taxes and administrative penalties where the company was a mere facade; directors face travel bans, disqualification from acting as directors for 10 years, and attachment of personal assets if the escaping tax is traceable to diverted colocation receipts.
Economic Substance Failure / Shell Company Designation

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of BZ$25,000 for the notification/return deficiency, plus BZ$5,000 for each day the failure continues after notice; repeat offenders receive a BZ$50,000 aggravated penalty, mandatory exchange of information with all CRS/OECD partner states, and administrative strike-off of the company with forfeiture of the IBC asset base.

Director personal liability: Under the Economic Substance Act, every director and manager knowingly concerned in the falsification is personally liable to the BZ$25,000 default penalty; the court may order restitution of the company's assets, impose a 3-year travel ban on directors pending the CRS exchange, and sentence directors to up to 2 years' imprisonment for false declarations in the ESR return.
Money Laundering (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: a fine up to BZ$500,000 and imprisonment up to 15 years for laundering, forfeiture orders over the rack equipment and server assets, and freezing/seizure orders by the Financial Intelligence Unit; the IBC's waiver of confidentiality in international mutual assistance removes the Belize privacy shield.

Director personal liability: Directors can be charged as principals, not merely corporate representatives, where they had knowledge of the tainted funds: the court may issue civil recovery orders for the full rental value laundered, asset freezing injunctions against the directors' bank accounts, disqualification from holding financial services roles for life, and extradition subject to the foreign warrant for predicate crimes.
Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Under the Companies (Amendment) Act 2017 and Beneficial Ownership Regulations: fines up to BZ$5,000 per officer plus a continuing penalty of BZ$500 for each day the register remains inaccurate; the registrar may strike off the IBC and execute a 'default declaration' allowing the Economic Substance authority to disclose the hidden owner to any requesting competent authority.

Director personal liability: The person in default (director or shareholder) is personally subject to the daily fine without the Company paying; courts may issue targeted sanctions, including a 5-year travel ban for the beneficial owner, seizure of the hidden share certificates, and imprisonment for up to 2 years for perjury in the BO declaration.
Unlicensed Financial Activity and Cross-Border Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Where financial business is performed without license: fines up to BZ$250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under the International Financial Services Commission Act; the IFSC may revoke the IBC's exemption certificate, impose operating restrictions on the Belize company, and require the winding up of the leasing business with distribution of assets only to verified creditors.

Director personal liability: Each director is jointly and severally liable for the BZ$250,000 fine and for restitution of amounts raised from the public; the IFSC and Central Bank may impose 10-year disqualification from managing any Belize financial or corporate entity and apply extraterritorially through supervisory colleges to foreign passport restrictions and travel bans.
