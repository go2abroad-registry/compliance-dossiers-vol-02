Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service (BTS), Ministry of Finance, Government of Belize — national revenue administration established under the Tax Management Act 2018; official taxpayer e-services portal: https://www.belizetaxservice.gov.bz (BTS e-Tax registration and filing); frontier and customs administration: Belize Customs and Excise Department operating the ASYCUDA World single-window declaration platform.

E-government filing portal

Standard operating procedure for a foreign-owned IBC: (1) incorporate the IBC at the Companies and Corporate Affairs Registry (CCAR) under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270) and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation; (2) appoint and maintain a licensed registered agent with a registered office in Belize; (3) the registered agent lodges the BTS TIN application attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, constitutional documents, registered-office certificate, and identification for directors and ultimate beneficial owners; (4) BTS issues a Tax Identification Number; a GST registration number is separately issued only where the IBC makes or intends to make taxable supplies in Belize exceeding BZD 100,000; (5) a pure offshore IBC with only non-resident users and foreign-currency app-store settlements is not GST-registered and files no income/business-tax returns in Belize, although the IBC Act's prohibition on trading with Belize residents must be observed; annual government fees and auditable records remain due at the registered office.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — a Belize IBC is a ring-fenced, non-resident offshore entity wholly exempt from Belize income tax, business tax, General Sales Tax, capital-gains tax, withholding tax, and stamp duties on its offshore income, assets, and transactions, provided it does not carry on business with Belize residents or hold Belize land without a licence.

Available election forms

Belize offers no US-style check-the-box election and no domestic hybrid-mismatch election. The statutory election mechanisms available are: (i) an IBC may elect by special resolution to convert into an ordinary domestic company under the Companies Act, and a domestic company may elect to become an IBC, triggering a change of tax posture; (ii) an IBC may elect to continue or redomicile into or out of Belize under the IBC Act with the approval of its members; (iii) constituent documents may elect the governing-law provisions, seat, and currency; and (iv) no fiscal unity or consolidation election exists for Belize tax purposes — each entity is taxed or exempted individually.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Belize operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with capital controls applicable to resident entities. Non-resident-owned IBCs are designated as 'international business companies' and are exempt from exchange control regulations under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270). IBCs maintain foreign currency accounts (USD/EUR/GBP) freely convertible outside the Belizean financial system. Profit remittance for IBCs is unrestricted; no prior CBB approval is required, but commercial banks may impose documentary verification for FATF compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to IBCs. Belize's Exchange Control Regulations require resident exporters to surrender foreign currency proceeds to authorized dealers within 90 days and convert to BZD for resident accounts. However, IBCs are statutorily excluded from the definition of 'resident' for exchange control purposes, so no surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio apply. Trade proceeds may be retained in original currency in offshore accounts.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderate feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Airwallex, Currenxie, or specialized Caribbean fintechs) generally decline Belize IBCs due to perceived high-risk jurisdiction and underwriting restrictions. However, several EMIs licensed in the UK, Lithuania, or Hong Kong accept Belize IBCs if the beneficial owner passes enhanced due diligence and provides certified corporate documents, bank reference letters, and source of funds. Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act, 2019 and the IBC (Economic Substance) Rules apply to every IBC carrying on a relevant activity including 'distribution and service center', 'intellectual property business', and 'financing and leasing' activities; a voice streaming application with in-app content delivery and royalty flows must demonstrate core income-generating activities in Belize, appropriate staff, physical premises, and direction and management, unless the company is tax resident in an EU-listed jurisdiction; annual Economic Substance Return and cost declaration are mandatory, failing which the company faces fines up to BZD 100,000 and potential striking-off for two consecutive years of non-compliance

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Filing Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under the Income and Business Tax Act, tax evasion penalties range from 100% to 200% of the tax undercharged, plus interest at 2.25% per month or part thereof; for Economic Substance Act violations, failure to file a return attracts a monetary penalty of BZD 10,000 for an initial offence and BZD 3,000 for each day the offence continues, with a maximum of BZD 100,000; knowingly providing false information attracts a fine of BZD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to two years

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly authorised or participated in evasion may be jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid taxes and penalties; the High Court may make a restitution order against personal assets, issue travel bans, and impose imprisonment not exceeding five years upon conviction; the corporate veil is pierced where the IBC is used for fraud or to defeat a public revenue claim
Shell Company in a Tax-Exempt Structure

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Act, the IBC shall be treated as non-compliant and subject to enforcement measures including a penalty of BZD 25,000 for failure to comply with a notice to file economic substance information; repeated non-compliance allows the Registrar to strike the company off the register; the company may also be disqualified from holding a valid tax exemption certificate

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally ordered to provide evidence of economic substance or face contempt proceedings; a court may hold directors personally liable for any tax that would have been payable had the shell status been disregarded, and may impose a travel ban pending compliance; deliberate use of a shell to avoid foreign tax obligations triggers cross-border exchange-of-information reporting and can lead to an Interpol red notice request where criminal tax evasion is established
AML / Know Your Customer Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorism Financing Act, Chapter 104, a regulated financial institution or money service business that fails to report suspicious transactions is subject to a fine of up to BZD 100,000 and imprisonment of up to five years; an unlicensed money transmitter operating without a licence faces a fine of up to BZD 250,000 and imprisonment of up to ten years; the IBC's assets may be seized under a restraint order

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are personally liable where they consented to or connived in an AML offence; the court may order disqualification from acting as a director for a period not exceeding 15 years, impose restitution of the proceeds of crime, and issue a custodial sentence; personal bank accounts of directors can be frozen, and travel abroad is subject to the permission of the Director of Public Prosecutions or the Supreme Court
