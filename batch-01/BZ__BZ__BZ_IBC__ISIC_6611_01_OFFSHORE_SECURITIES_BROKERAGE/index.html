Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an IBC; the Memorandum must state authorised share capital, which may be expressed in any currency, and shares may be issued partly paid. No capital escrow/deposit is required for incorporation. However, the IFSC international securities brokerage licence requires the licensee to maintain minimum regulatory capital, a segregated client funds account, and proof of financial resources at the licensing stage.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Belize National Fire Service, building occupancy clearance from the Belize Building Authority for the registered office premises, and workplace health and safety compliance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act. The premises must be equipped with fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and adequate ventilation, and the employer must file the required notice of business occupancy and maintain first-aid and evacuation protocols.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Income Tax Department (ITD) under the Ministry of Finance, Government of Belize; official online portal: https://itd.gov.bz

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Belize International Business Company (IBC) is generally exempt from Belize income tax and is not required to register for a national TIN solely by virtue of IBC status. However, to engage in licensed activities such as offshore securities brokerage with payment rails, the IBC must: (1) incorporate under the International Business Companies Act; (2) file an annual return with the Belize Companies and Corporate Affairs Registry; (3) obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) from the Income Tax Department only if the entity elects to be taxed as an ordinary resident company or generates Belize-source income; and (4) register for Value Added Tax (VAT) with the Belize Tax Service Department (GST/VAT Unit) only if it makes taxable supplies exceeding the registration threshold in Belize. For offshore brokerage activities expressly confined to non-resident clients and offshore securities, the standard procedure is: submit the IBC certificate of incorporation, registered office address, director and shareholder register, and a written declaration of non-residence to obtain an exemption certificate or confirmation letter from the ITD evidencing no TIN requirement. If the broker nevertheless opens a local correspondent account or hires local staff, it should request Form TIN-01 from the ITD and file it with supporting notarized corporate documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Under the International Business Companies Act (as amended), an IBC may irrevocably elect to be taxed as a domestic ordinary company by filing a written notice with the Belize Income Tax Department; this election subjects the IBC to Belize income tax at the standard corporate rate on its worldwide income and invalidates most IBC exemptions. No check-the-box regime analogous to the U.S. exists; however, an IBC can voluntarily register for VAT. Belize's hybrid mismatch rules are limited; the Income and Business Tax Act contains general anti-avoidance provisions but no formal hybrid-mismatch election. A foreign entity converting to an IBC may also elect carry-forward treatment of pre-existing tax attributes if permitted by the Income Tax Department; in practice, such an election requires advance written approval and may trigger exit taxation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

BZD $75,000 (Belize Dollars) of annual taxable supplies, per the Goods and Services Tax Act (Chapter 296); the equivalent USD threshold is approximately USD $37,500 at the fixed exchange rate of BZD 2 = USD 1.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for Belize VAT purposes applies to the export of goods (supply that is directly shipped out of Belize by the supplier or on their behalf) and to certain international services, excluding services physically performed in Belize. For an offshore securities broker, taxable brokerage services supplied to a non-resident client will qualify as zero-rated only if the benefit of the service is received outside Belize, the service does not relate to immovable property located in Belize, and the services are not connected with goods situated in Belize at the time of supply. The broker must obtain and retain written evidence of the client's non-resident status, including a declaration of foreign residence and supporting passport or business registration documentation. Services supplied to a Belize-resident person, or services that involve the management or administration of Belize-domiciled investment funds, are excluded from zero-rating. In addition, the IBC's underlying statutory exemption from all Belize taxes, including VAT, may apply to offshore services; however, where the IBC voluntarily registers for VAT, the general VAT export rules supersede the IBC exemption for supplies made within Belize.

Belize does not currently mandate real-time electronic fiscal clearance or API-based digital stamping for invoices. VAT-registered businesses must issue serially numbered paper or electronic tax invoices containing mandatory fields (seller name, TIN/VAT number, BRN, description, taxable amount, VAT charged, and invoice date). The Belize Tax Service Department (TSD) requires monthly electronic filing of VAT returns via the TSD online portal (https://tsd.gov.bz) and payment of net VAT by the 14th day of the following month. Although digital invoices are accepted, there is no clearance sandbox, no government-authorised continuous transaction control (CTC) model, and no mandatory e-invoice stamping currently enforced. However, the TSD has authority to require certain categories of taxpayers, including licensed financial institutions and international brokers, to file returns and summary schedules electronically in prescribed CSV or XML templates.

Belize law imposes strict penalties for fraudulent zero-rating claims. Under the Goods and Services Tax Act, a person who intentionally makes a false zero-rating claim or provides false documentation to obtain a refund is liable to a fine not exceeding BZD $25,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years. The Tax Service Department may retrospectively claw back any refund or VAT credit erroneously granted, add interest at the prescribed rate (currently 2% per month or part thereof on the unpaid tax), and impose penalties of up to 150% of the tax evaded in cases of fraud, wilful default, or neglect. In addition, the International Business Companies Act imposes penalties on the IBC for breach of its exempt status: if the company conducts business with Belize-resident persons or derives Belize-source income, the IBC's exemptions are retroactively voided, exposing the entire taxable income to Belize corporate income tax at the standard rate (currently 1.75% for offshore-commercial companies under the Income and Business Tax Act, but may be higher for domestic companies), plus penalties and interest for failure to file domestic tax returns. The Belize Financial Services Commission (FSC) may suspend or revoke the securities brokerage license for compliance failures, which concurrently triggers tax audit and clawback procedures. In practice, the 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline is enforced through the Central Bank of Belize's exchange control regulations; failure to repatriate within the deadline without an approved extension results in automatic disallowance of the zero-rating claim and immediate assessment of VAT at 12.5% on the gross fee, with interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belize dollar (BZD) operates under a de jure managed fixed peg of BZD 2.00 = USD 1.00. IBCs are statutorily deemed non-residents for exchange-control purposes and are expressly exempted from the Exchange Control Act, permitting unrestricted capital-account convertibility, foreign-currency account retention, and outward remittance of profits, dividends, principal, and brokerage settlement flows without CBB licensing. Current-account convertibility is equally unobstructed for non-resident IBC accounts denominated in USD; the only friction arises where a transaction converts into BZD or involves a Belize resident, in which case CBB Exchange Control Notice approval may be triggered. No withholding, capital-gains, or profits tax applies to IBC income under the International Business Companies Act (Chapter 270), so profit remittance is a purely banking-compliance exercise requiring a valid IFSC licence, certificate of good standing, and updated beneficial-ownership record.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender timeline or conversion ratio applies to an IBC: the non-resident IBC exemption permits 100% retention of export, commission, and brokerage receipts in any foreign currency. For Belize-resident commercial exporters, the CBB maintains a liberalized regime with only statistical declaration requirements under the Exchange Control Notice framework and no fixed inward-repatriation deadline; segregated client money of an IFSC-licensed securities broker is held in independent trust accounts and is explicitly excluded from any surrender, conversion, or set-off obligation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB), established under the Central Bank of Belize Act (Chapter 262), exercising monetary and licensing authority; residual exchange-control powers rest with the Ministry of Finance under the Exchange Control Act (Chapter 258), administered in practice by CBB.

Feasibility Rating: Low. Belize has no statutory e-money institution (EMI) or payment-services licensing framework; the IFSC does not issue EMI authorizations. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is SWIFT MT103 only, addressed to 'IBC Name – Client Trust Account (Segregated)' at Belize Bank Limited with a US correspondent (Citibank NY or Wells Fargo) and ABA/SWIFT BIC identifiers. The wire must carry an ISO 20022 purpose code indicating securities settlement (e.g., G104 'Financial investments' or 'TRADE') and the remitter description must match the KYC file. Required verification bundle: board resolution authorizing the account; compliance-officer pre-notification of the expected wire; matched CDD source-of-wealth file; and, for wires above USD 1,000,000, an authenticated SWIFT confirmation or tested-telex verification. Only commercial-bank-originated wires are accepted; third-party pass-through or cash-equivalent channels are an AML red-flag and will be rejected.Trade settlements and margin transfers to the executing prime broker use SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV from the IBC operational USD account cleared through the US correspondent network. Retail client withdrawals use SWIFT MT103 only to the client's own verified nominated account, with the beneficiary name exactly matching the CDD file. Commercial letters of credit are not applicable to securities settlements, but a Standby Letter of Credit (SBLC) issued by the Belize Bank to a clearing house or prime broker is permissible as collateral under an ISDA/prime-brokerage CSA. Global digital EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Payoneer, Currencycloud, Airwallex) systematically refuse Belize-domiciled, IFSC-licensed securities brokerages due to high-tier AML/CFT jurisdiction profiling. The legally sound and operationally reliable structure is a Belize Bank USD operational account connected to a US prime-brokerage sweep account via a US correspondent (Wells Fargo, JPMorgan, or Citibank) on the SWIFT network, rather than reliance on an offshore EMI payout rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BZD 0 - Belize has no codified Local File/Master File documentation thresholds; arms-length compliance is nevertheless enforced through the ESR return for all relevant activities regardless of transaction size, and the IFSC licence imposes arms-length pricing conditions on broker-dealer and referral arrangements

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Amendment) Act 2019 applies to all IBCs. Banking, insurance, financing and leasing, headquarters, shipping, distribution and service centre, intellectual property holding and pure equity holding are relevant activities requiring demonstrable local management, employees, physical premises, expenditure and Cayman-style CIGA performance in Belize. Retail securities brokerage for independent clients is not a listed relevant activity, but the IBC must still file an annual ESR return declaring 'none'; if the brokerage operates as a service centre for a foreign affiliate, the more onerous relevant-activity regime applies jointly with IFSC licence conditions. Failure to satisfy substance results in penalties, automatic exchange of information, and revocation of the tax-exempt status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unlicensed Regulated Activity - Securities Industry Act Cap.298A

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction, fine up to BZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 1 year; on indictment, fine up to BZD 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; continuing daily penalty for each day the offence continues; IFSC may seize brokerage records, freeze client assets and revoke any ancillary money service licence

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and shadow managers are jointly and severally liable for fines and civil restitution of commissions earned; individual imprisonment up to 2 years; travel bans and cancellation of work permits issued by IFSC; asset freezing orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act
Tax Evasion and Abuse of IBC Exempt Status - Income Tax Act Cap.55

Statutory sanction: Assessment to tax on the entire income at 25% with no loss relief; penalty of 100% of the under-assessed tax plus interest at 10% per annum; revocation of the 50-year exemption certificate; deregistration of the IBC; publication of the taxpayer's name in the Gazette

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false returns face fines up to BZD 100,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years per offence; civil restitution of unpaid tax with personal liability when the company is struck off; travel bans pending payment of tax arrears; disqualification from acting as director of any Belize company
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure - Economic Substance (Amendment) Act 2019

Statutory sanction: Initial penalty BZD 50,000; additional penalty BZD 50,000 on continuing default; daily default penalty up to BZD 300 per day; mandatory exchange of information with foreign tax authorities; dissolution of the company; loss of the IBC tax exemption with retroactive effect for the relevant period

Director personal liability: Directors held jointly and severally for penalties when default is attributable to their willful neglect; disqualification from acting as director for up to 5 years; inclusion on the IFSC enforcement watch-list; travel bans and restrictions on entry into Belize
Money Laundering and AML/CFT Breach - Money Laundering and Terrorism (Prevention) Act

Statutory sanction: For a body corporate, fine up to BZD 1,000,000 for money laundering; licensed financial institution subject to additional AML administrative penalties up to BZD 500,000 and licence suspension or revocation; confiscation and forfeiture of all laundered assets; freezing orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act

Director personal liability: Directors and the Money Laundering Reporting Officer personally liable to fines up to BZD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 15 years; travel bans and overseas asset tracing; extradition under BZ mutual legal assistance treaties; permanent loss of fit-and-proper status for engagement in any IFSC-regulated business
Registry Non-Compliance and Failure to File Annual Return - IBC Act Cap.270

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of BZD 500 per month; continued default results in strike-off/deregistration and dissolution after notice in the Gazette; restoration requires court application, payment of all arrears, penalties and legal costs; contracts entered into after strike-off are void and company property vests in the Registrar

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for penalties where non-compliance is deliberate; loss of limited liability protection for post-dissolution trading debts; registered agent may resign leaving the IBC without a legal service address, exposing directors to default judgments; travel bans and exclusion orders may be imposed pending insolvency investigations
