Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (IBC statutory exemption from all Belize income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, stamp duties, and GST for a 50-year period from incorporation under IBC Act Cap 270; treated as non-resident for exchange control and tax purposes)

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in Belize. A single statutory election is available: under IBC Act Cap 270, an IBC may elect to convert into a domestic company under the Companies Act Cap 250, thereby relinquishing tax-exempt status but gaining eligibility for Belize's limited tax treaty network (CARICOM agreement); the conversion is irrevocable and subject to CCAR approval.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST Act Cap 65 Seventh Schedule zero-rates (i) goods physically exported from Belize and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident recipient where the benefit is consumed entirely outside Belize, which includes cross-border money transmission and remittance commission services rendered to foreign principals under ISIC 6619. For an IBC, offshore supplies are additionally outside the scope of GST due to the statutory IBC exemption. Claimants must demonstrate: a written contract with a non-resident principal, SWIFT or agent settlement records, foreign-currency bank statements evidencing receipt of fees from abroad, and identification of the recipient as non-resident.

  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill for airfreight consignments)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 credit advice issued by an authorized bank in Belize)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based chaining, or digital stamping mandate is in force in Belize. The TSD operates the BTMS online portal for GST return filing, payment, and correspondence; taxpayers must issue GST invoices in paper or electronic form under GST Regulations and retain all records, including export substantiation, for at least 6 years for inspection by TSD officers.

The TSD annually reconciles customs export statistics against GST returns and remittance settlement records. A false zero-rating claim triggers a jeopardy assessment under GST Act Cap 65 recovering output VAT plus clawback of all related input credits with statutory interest; a civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded; and criminal prosecution with fines up to BZ$25,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years per offence. Fabricated customs declarations, bills of lading, or remittance advices are referred to the Financial Intelligence Unit for prosecution under the Anti-Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism Act and the Criminal Code forgery provisions, carrying substantial custodial sentences. Non-repatriation of export proceeds within the 180-day benchmark is treated as a presumption of unsubstantiated export, leading to retroactive VAT withdrawal, exchange-control penalties under the Central Bank of Belize, and, for an IBC, potential revocation of its tax-exempt status by the International Financial Services Commission.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belize dollar (BZD) is statutorily anchored to the United States dollar at BZ$2.00 = US$1.00, with the Central Bank of Belize operating an authorized-dealer exchange-control framework. Current-account items (trade in goods and services, interest, dividends, profit remittances) are generally convertible through authorized dealers upon presentation of supporting commercial documentation, while capital-account transactions by residents require prior Central Bank approval under the Exchange Control (General) Regulations. A Belize International Business Company (IBC) incorporated under the International Business Companies Act, Cap. 270 is statutorily designated as a non-resident enterprise and is exempt from the exchange-control surrender/approval machinery; it may therefore hold, settle, and remit freely in any currency through foreign-currency accounts. Profit and capital remittances by an IBC to non-resident shareholders attract zero Belize withholding tax and no exchange-control licence, provided the IBC is in good standing with the International Financial Services Commission (IFSC), has paid its annual licence fee, and has filed the annual return. The practical convertibility hurdle is therefore not Central Bank licensing but bank-level AML/KYC documentary clearance, especially the demonstration that the IBC transacts exclusively with non-residents and does not carry on regulated money-services business with Belize residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident exporters and service suppliers, the Exchange Control (General) Regulations require foreign-currency proceeds to be obtained within six months of the date of export/supply and to be surrendered to an authorized dealer in exchange for BZD within one month of receipt; the mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% of the invoice value, converted at the official pegged rate of BZ$2.00 per US$1.00. Non-resident IBCs are statutorily outside the surrender obligation and may retain export/settlement proceeds in USD or any foreign currency. For the IBC remittance business, this means: (i) USD settlement inflows from foreign correspondents may be kept offshore or in USD nostro accounts without mandatory BZD conversion; (ii) any local cash-pickup payout in Belize must be executed through a licensed Belize money-services business/authorized dealer that itself complies with the resident surrender rules when converting USD into BZD; and (iii) the IBC must ensure that its Belize treasury operations do not inadvertently create a permanent establishment or 'resident' exchange-control status by receiving BZD from Belize residents.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB), acting as the Foreign Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act, Cap. 145 of the Laws of Belize

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability with strict legal constraints. Belize does not maintain a domestic electronic-money-institution (EMI) or digital-payment-institution licensing regime under the Banks and Financial Institutions Act; the Central Bank of Belize does not charter fintech EMIs. Internationally domiciled EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Currencycloud, etc.) are not licensed in Belize and therefore cannot serve as the primary treasury bank for a Belize IBC; they may be used only as ancillary concentration/collection vehicles for corridor currencies, and even then most global EMIs decline onboarding of Belize-registered IBCs engaged in high-risk cross-border money transmission because of correspondent-bank de-risking and sanctions-compliance policies. The legally prudent structure is: principal capital escrow and operational accounts at a licensed Belize commercial bank or an IFSC-licensed offshore bank, with offshore EMIs used solely for downstream FX conversion and small-volume payouts where a named licensed payout partner is the account holder.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital must be routed over SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank concentrations) from the verified shareholder bank account to the IBC's capital escrow account at a licensed Belize bank. The wire instruction must quote the exact registered IBC legal name, the IFSC company number, and a purpose code clearly denoting equity capital contribution (e.g., 'G2 - Capital Transactions' or free-format 'EQUITY CAPITAL INJECTION – NON-RESIDENT IBC') accompanied by a board resolution authorising the opening and funding of the escrow account. The receiving Belize bank will conduct a Beneficial Ownership declaration, verify the remitter against the registered share register, and require the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles, Certificate of Good Standing, and registered-agent attestation before crediting. Funds arriving in BZD will be converted by the authorized dealer at the statutory pegged rate of BZ$2:US$1; USD-denominated injections are clearing-preferred because they avoid conversion friction and maintain the IBC's non-resident FX status. Escrowed capital must be released only against the board-approved disbursement schedule and only after the bank's AML compliance officer clears each drawdown.Outbound settlement uses three complementary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 direct wires for concentrated settlement to foreign licensed payout partners and for banked payees, with strict OFAC screening and purpose codes referencing 'cross-border remittance settlement'; (2) commercial letters of credit (sight LCs via SWIFT MT700/MT710) where the payout partner requires documentary security or where settlement backs a service-supply invoice for bulk corridors; and (3) local cash-pickup clearing executed through licensed money-services agents in recipient jurisdictions using file-based bulk payout authorizations rather than per-transaction wires, with daily same-day or T+1 reconciliation against the IBC operational account. All outbound flows must be denominated in USD or destination-currency; BZD outflows are restricted because the IBC holds no Belize resident-surrender licence. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium viability with strict legal constraints. Belize does not maintain a domestic electronic-money-institution (EMI) or digital-payment-institution licensing regime under the Banks and Financial Institutions Act; the Central Bank of Belize does not charter fintech EMIs. Internationally domiciled EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Currencycloud, etc.) are not licensed in Belize and therefore cannot serve as the primary treasury bank for a Belize IBC; they may be used only as ancillary concentration/collection vehicles for corridor currencies, and even then most global EMIs decline onboarding of Belize-registered IBCs engaged in high-risk cross-border money transmission because of correspondent-bank de-risking and sanctions-compliance policies. The legally prudent structure is: principal capital escrow and operational accounts at a licensed Belize commercial bank or an IFSC-licensed offshore bank, with offshore EMIs used solely for downstream FX conversion and small-volume payouts where a named licensed payout partner is the account holder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold is gazetted for IBCs: Belize imposes no mandatory Local File or Master File obligation (BZ$0.00 benchmark); the operative gatekeepers are the Economic Substance Regulations and the source-country TP documentation rules of the remittance corridor (e.g., US IRC 482 comparability and the transfer pricing file in the principal's country)

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (International Business Companies) Regulations 2019 apply to all IBCs. For the ISIC 6619 remittance business, the activity will be treated as financing and leasing or distribution and service center if it earns income from foreign related parties, requiring in Belize: (a) employees, (b) premises, (c) board meetings and local management, and (d) core income-generating activities. A pure equity-holding IBC enjoys reduced substance. The annual Economic Substance Return (Form ES-01) is due within 12 months of the reporting period end; a false return carries BZ$25,000 to BZ$100,000 penalties, daily default fines, strike-off, and director disqualification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion, Fraudulent Concealment, and Offshore Secrecy

Statutory sanction: Belize Income and Business Tax Act: penalty of double the tax evaded plus interest at the statutory rate from the due date; criminal conviction liability to a fine of up to BZ$10,000 and imprisonment of up to 3 years. In source and OPM countries: FATCA and CRS evasion penalties (up to 75% of the understatement in the US; UK offshore penalties up to 200%) and criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the IBC veil where the entity is the UBO's alter ego: directors and UBOs are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution, worldwide asset-freezing orders, travel bans, extradition to the residence country, and custodial sentences of up to 5 years for tax fraud in the corridor jurisdiction
Unlicensed Money Service Business and AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT Act (Cap. 104) and Central Bank directives: fines up to BZ$100,000, imprisonment up to 7 years, license revocation, freezing of settlement accounts, forfeiture of float funds, and compulsory winding-up of the IBC by the Registrar

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally criminally liable without right of corporate indemnity; courts issue travel bans and international arrest warrants; mutual legal assistance requests expose directors to extradition, and personal confiscation orders attach to assets held inside or outside Belize
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance (IBC) Regulations 2019: administrative penalties of BZ$25,000 to BZ$100,000, daily default fines, Registry strike-off and dissolution, public naming of non-compliant companies, and notification to competent authorities of other states

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the administrative penalty, disqualified from acting as directors for up to 5 years, and foreign courts treat the IBC as a facade, making directors personally liable under agency and restitution doctrines for the remittance book
Nominee Director Misuse and Breach of Fiduciary Duty

Statutory sanction: IBC Act (Cap. 270) and general company law: equitable compensation, rescission of tainted contracts, fines up to BZ$25,000, and imprisonment up to 2 years for false declarations by a director or agent

Director personal liability: The nominee is personally liable for restitution to creditors and shareholders; courts impose travel bans and asset freezes; cross-border insolvency orders require the nominee to contribute personal assets to cover the deficit
Failure to Maintain Accounting Records

Statutory sanction: IBC Act, s. 69: fine of up to BZ$10,000 and imprisonment up to 1 year per officer for each day of default; the Tax Services Department may recharacterize profits and impose the onshore 25% rate by default assessment

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for the daily fines; in liquidation, the court pierces the veil and orders directors to account for the missing records and pay compensation measured by the concealed remittance flows
Wire Stripping and Remittance Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT Act and Central Bank directions: revocation of the high-risk special license, fines up to BZ$100,000, seizure of settlement funds, removal from correspondent banking networks, and OFAC sanctions exposure in USD clearing corridors

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, travel bans, and extradition; the veil is pierced to recover funds at the remittance destination; sanctions violations trigger personal designation (SDN listing) and asset freezes in the US and EU
