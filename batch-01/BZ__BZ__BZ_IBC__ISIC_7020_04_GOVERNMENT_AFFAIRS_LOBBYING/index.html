Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service (BTS), the unified tax administration established under the Tax Administration Reform Act, 2021 (TARA), operating under the Ministry of Finance, Economic Development and Investment; electronic filing portal: Belize Tax Service e-Tax portal at https://etax.belizetaxservice.gov.bz; the International Financial Services Commission (IFSC) remains competent for IBC licensing, annual fees and certificates of compliance; Her Majesty's Belize Customs Department (customs administration within BTS for ASYCUDA World export clearance).

E-government filing portal

For a Belize IBC: (1) incorporate through a licensed registered agent under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270) via the Belize Companies Registry and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation; (2) the registered agent files the IFSC registration and pays the flat annual government license fee (currently US$1,000), obtaining the Certificate of Good Standing / Certificate of Compliance; (3) apply to the Belize Tax Service for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) by submitting the Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, IFSC certificate, registered agent confirmation and proof of the Belize registered office; BTS validates the taxpayer profile and issues the TIN; (4) register in the 'International/Tax Exempt' taxpayer category under TARA and file the required annual information/returns notwithstanding tax exemption; (5) for GST, lodge a separate GST registration application with BTS only if making taxable supplies in Belize; a pure cross-border policy advisory and foreign-stakeholder lobbying IBC with no Belize-resident clients is not required to register for GST because its supplies are zero-rated or outside the scope of Belize GST.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — the Belize IBC is a separate legal person subject to entity-level taxation exclusively on income derived from business carried on in Belize with Belize-resident individuals or companies; under section 39 of the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270), as amended by TARA, all foreign-source income of the IBC is exempt from Belize income tax, business tax, capital gains tax (Belize levies no general capital gains tax) and stamp duty; Belize-source business income of an IBC is taxed at the penal rate of 40% under the Income and Business Tax Act (Cap. 55); no pass-through, partnership or transparent treatment is available to an IBC for Belize tax purposes. — For a Belize IBC: (1) incorporate through a licensed registered agent under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270) via the Belize Companies Registry and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation; (2) the registered agent files the IFSC registration and pays the flat annual government license fee (currently US$1,000), obtaining the Certificate of Good Standing / Certificate of Compliance; (3) apply to the Belize Tax Service for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) by submitting the Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, IFSC certificate, registered agent confirmation and proof of the Belize registered office; BTS validates the taxpayer profile and issues the TIN; (4) register in the 'International/Tax Exempt' taxpayer category under TARA and file the required annual information/returns notwithstanding tax exemption; (5) for GST, lodge a separate GST registration application with BTS only if making taxable supplies in Belize; a pure cross-border policy advisory and foreign-stakeholder lobbying IBC with no Belize-resident clients is not required to register for GST because its supplies are zero-rated or outside the scope of Belize GST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the Belize IBC is a separate legal person subject to entity-level taxation exclusively on income derived from business carried on in Belize with Belize-resident individuals or companies; under section 39 of the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270), as amended by TARA, all foreign-source income of the IBC is exempt from Belize income tax, business tax, capital gains tax (Belize levies no general capital gains tax) and stamp duty; Belize-source business income of an IBC is taxed at the penal rate of 40% under the Income and Business Tax Act (Cap. 55); no pass-through, partnership or transparent treatment is available to an IBC for Belize tax purposes.

Available election forms

Belize has no IRS-style check-the-box election. The relevant statutory elections for an IBC are: (i) elective conversion of the IBC into a domestic Belize company by resolution and IFSC consent, thereby ceasing IBC status and entering the general corporate tax regime (25% income tax, 12.5% GST, financial statements and audit obligations); (ii) voluntary surrender of the IBC tax-exempt status with notice to the Belize Tax Service, effective from the following year of assessment; (iii) economic substance self-certification election under the IBC Act, whereby the IBC may certify that it does not carry on a 'relevant activity' (cross-border trade policy analysis and foreign stakeholder lobbying are not listed relevant activities, so a nil/non-applicable substance filing is appropriate); (iv) no hybrid mismatch, group relief or consolidation elections are available because Belize law does not recognize tax groups for IBCs; (v) the IBC may elect its accounting year-end and reporting currency with BTS for TARA compliance purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

BZ$50,000 (fifty thousand Belize dollars, approximately US$25,000) of annual gross taxable turnover in Belize; mandatory GST registration applies to any person carrying on a taxable business whose taxable supplies exceed BZ$50,000 in any period of twelve months; a person making exclusively zero-rated exports of goods or services may apply for voluntary registration in order to recover input GST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of export services is available under the General Sales Tax Act (Cap. 64) and applicable zero-rated supplies provisions where: the recipient of the services belongs outside Belize; the services are supplied for use or benefit wholly outside Belize; and the supplier retains contemporaneous documentary evidence of the foreign supply and remittance of proceeds. Cross-border trade policy analysis, government-relations advisory and foreign stakeholder lobbying services rendered by the Belize IBC under retainer agreements to non-resident clients qualify for the 0% GST rate, or are outside the scope of Belize GST where the supply is not made in Belize; input GST on costs exclusively attributable to such zero-rated exports is creditable, and refunds are payable by BTS if input credits exceed output GST in a prescribed period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export entry lodged in ASYCUDA World, showing the export entry number, date of export and customs officer validation)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill and packing list (for goods); for zero-rated services, a signed service agreement, detailed scope of work, deliverables report and proof of delivery/performance to the foreign recipient
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency proceeds into the IBC's bank account within the repatriation deadline
  • Commercial invoice with full description of services, declared consideration, currency, GST treatment code, IBC TIN and reference to the underlying contract
  • Foreign-resident status evidence of the client (foreign VAT/TIN certificate, incorporation document, passport or address proof) supporting the export zero-rating claim

Belize has not implemented a real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping or mandatory continuous-control e-invoicing regime for GST. The present legal mandate requires: (i) every registered person to issue sequential pre-numbered tax invoices or simplified tax receipts that include the supplier's TIN and GST registration number for all taxable supplies; (ii) GST returns to be filed electronically through the Belize Tax Service e-Tax portal (monthly or as directed by BTS); (iii) export consignments to be declared electronically through the ASYCUDA World customs clearance system; and (iv) proper preservation of source documents, input tax invoices and export evidence. Any e-invoicing modernization is at the ministerial/administrative stage and does not presently impose a fiscal clearance obligation.

BTS and Belize Customs audit export zero-rating claims by cross-matching the ASYCUDA World export declaration, the executed service contract, bank remittance evidence and foreign-client documentation; failure to receive or substantiate foreign-currency proceeds within the 180-day window creates a rebuttable presumption that the supply was domestic, triggering GST at 12.5% plus clawback of any refunded input tax. Fraudulent zero-rating, false declarations or forged documents attract criminal penalties under the General Sales Tax Act (Cap. 64) of up to BZ$10,000 and/or imprisonment for up to three years; TARA additionally authorizes civil penalties of up to 30% of the tax understated in cases of gross negligence or fraud, statutory interest on unpaid or erroneously refunded tax, and retrospective assessment/clawback of refunds within the applicable limitation period of up to six years; the IBC's registered agent and directors may be disqualified, and the IFSC may suspend or strike off the IBC for non-compliance with filings, economic substance notifications or the IBC Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belize dollar is pegged to the US dollar at BZD 2.00 = USD 1.00. Current-account transactions under the Exchange Control Act are routinely approved for bonafide commercial and advisory fees, and profit remittance is permitted after verification of underlying service/retainer invoices, tax clearance, and the payment of any applicable domestic taxes. Capital-account transactions, equity injections, and intercompany loans require formal Central Bank authorization. Belize IBCs are treated as non-resident entities for exchange-control purposes, so their external account funding is generally honoured; however, any transaction touching a Belize-resident counterparty or a Belize-source payment for lobbying/government affairs activity triggers resident-account scrutiny and may require a local business licence and CBB approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Belize exchange-control rules require exporters to surrender foreign-currency export proceeds to an authorized dealer bank not later than the 90th day after export shipment or receipt, and the BZD equivalent must be credited at the official pegged rate unless the Central Bank has granted a specific retention or waiver. For a non-resident IBC engaged exclusively in cross-border government affairs advisory and lobbying, no mandatory conversion of non-Belize-sourced revenue arises, but any sum received into a Belize domestic account from a Belize exporter or local trade transaction will be subject to prescribed surrender timelines and conversion requirements. Administrative practice permits foreign-currency retention in designated external accounts where the IBC is non-resident and the funds do not represent Belize current-account export earnings.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB), acting under the Exchange Control Act, Chapter 11 of the Laws of Belize, together with the International Business Companies Act, Chapter 270; the CBB also administers the licensing and supervision of all commercial banks and external trade payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Belize does not maintain a fintech or electronic-money-institution licensing regime for offshore digital banks or EMIs. Offshore EMIs can be used as a supplementary collection rail only when the ultimate settlement account is held at a licensed Belize commercial bank or at a well-regulated foreign private bank. Belize-licensed banks generally reject direct EMI-to-Belize funding as a primary clearing rail without an underlying correspondent banking relationship and a physical KYC-complete corporate account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from shareholders or group treasury must be routed as SWIFT MT103, or for high-value treasury sweeps as MT202 COV, from the originating bank to the Belize commercial bank's USD correspondent account, typically through a US clearing bank. The payment must reference the IBC registration number, the specific capital-contribution purpose, and the applicable Exchange Control narrative such as 'equity subscription' or 'shareholder loan capital'; supporting board resolution, capital account allocation, and the final beneficial-owner identification must be delivered under cover of the bank's KYC file before the credit clearing is made irrevocable. The remitter's bank must include the source-of-funds reference and the underlying retainer/trade-policy advisory agreement when the funds represent disbursement from an international client, since Belize banks treat ambiguous third-party funding as a high-risk trigger.Outbound trade disbursements and retainer payments for cross-border government-relations services are cleared over the US dollar clearing network. The standard instruction rail is SWIFT MT103 showing the final beneficiary's bank and a narrative describing 'professional advisory retainer' or 'cross-border trade policy analysis fee'. Correspondent-to-correspondent interbank settlements without final beneficiary details use MT202 for treasury/cash-management purposes only, never for original underlying client payments. Commercial letters of credit are issued on SWIFT MT700 through a Belize licensed bank or its foreign correspondent when the trade policy engagement is tied to payment against deliverables; documentary collections can be used for invoice-backed service disbursements but are less common in lobbying advisory structures. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BZD 1,000,000 in annual related-party cross-border transactions mandates full Master File and Local File preparation for a Belize taxable entity. An exempt IBC that has no Belize corporation-tax exposure is not subject to the mandatory filing, but the IBC must maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation if the Economic Substance Unit or Belize Customs requires a functional analysis of the advisory fee arrangement. A prudent minimum documentation threshold for such an IBC is any intercompany fee in excess of BZD 250,000 annually because that is the level where the ESU begins to scrutinize functional substance.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (International Financial Services) Act 2021 and the associated International Financial Services (Economic Substance) Regulations apply to IBCs carrying on 'relevant activities'. ISIC 7020 (management consultancy, policy advisory, and government-affairs lobbying) will be treated as a relevant activity if the Belize Registered Agent determines the entity receives income from advisory, technical, or trade-policy services. The doctrine mandates that an IBC must demonstrate real physical presence, a proportionate number of full-time employees, and core income-earning operations in Belize if that activity is managed and controlled from Belize. A pure offshore advisory shell with no Belize substance can remain exempt, but must not represent that the activity is economically managed or performed in Belize. Tax havens' regime-restructuring commitments require the ESU to exchange information on any entity that fails to meet substance; therefore the IBC must document its non-Belize management and operating premises clearly.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Sham Non-Residence

Statutory sanction: Under Sections 129 and 142 of the Income Tax Act, the Commissioner may treat the IBC as a resident company, assess the entire relevant income to tax at up to 25%, impose a fine of BZD 5,000, and levy an additional penalty of up to three times the tax evaded. Criminal tax evasion carries imprisonment of up to two years.

Director personal liability: Where the IBC is found to be a façade or sham, the Supreme Court of Belize will pierce the corporate veil: every director, officer, and beneficial owner becomes jointly and severally liable for assessed taxes, penalties, interest, and costs; travel bans may be issued at the Attorney General's request, and offending shareholders may be stripped of distribution rights and subjected to restitution orders.
False Economic Substance Declaration

Statutory sanction: The Economic Substance Unit may impose an administrative penalty of BZD 30,000 for each inaccurate return, or BZD 100,000 for a deliberate misrepresentation. The Income Tax Department can additionally recharacterize all offshore revenue as Belize-source income and apply full corporate tax plus interest from the original filing date.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent signing a false return are personally liable for the penalty; the court can order disqualification from holding directorships for a period of up to 10 years, issue a personal restitution order for all financial benefits derived from the misrepresentation, and in egregious cases impose custodial sentences of up to 5 years for fraudulent false certification.
Shell Company Abuse and Substance-Dopped IBC

Statutory sanction: Under the Companies (Amendment) Act and Economic Substance Act, the Registrar may strike off the IBC without further notice, and the ESU may report the entity to the OECD Global Forum as a non-compliant 'shell'. The entity's assets may escheat to the Crown; any foreign parent company will lose the ability to rely on the Belize entity's tax-exempt status.

Director personal liability: A Director who knowingly operates the IBC as a shell becomes liable for the expenses of liquidation and for any fraudulent preference; travel bans may be imposed, and the Belize courts may order the transfer of Indian or other foreign assets back to the ultimate beneficial owner; an international freezing order may be granted in aid of foreign asset-recovery proceedings.
AML / Countering Financing of Terrorism Red Flags in Lobbying Fees

Statutory sanction: The International Financial Services Commission Act and the Proceeds of Crime Act impose a fine of up to BZD 250,000 and imprisonment of up to seven years for a single failure to conduct adequate enhanced due diligence on a politically exposed person. The IBC's license may be suspended, foreign correspondent banking relationships may be terminated, and the entity may be placed on the CRS non-cooperative list.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and persons 'acting in the business' of the IBC are personally liable for money-laundering offenses when they had knowledge that the retainer represented the proceeds of crime. The Belize Asset Recovery Agency may invoke Proceeds of Crime Act powers to seize the directors' personal property, impose a domestic travel ban, and issue an order restricting them from acting in any financial sector for a 12-year period; extradition may be sought under mutual legal assistance treaties where the lobbying activity generated corrupt-source assets.
