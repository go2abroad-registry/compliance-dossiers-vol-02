Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is not acceptable as the statutory registered office for an IBC. The IBC Act (Cap. 270, s. 7) requires a physical registered office in Belize maintained at the office of a licensed registered agent. Remote management and virtual board meetings are permitted, provided the registered agent can evidence commercial substance, holds UBO/AML files on site, and maintains a physical Belizean compliance presence. For an ISIC 7490 trade-remedy defense consulting IBC, a non-resident principal may operate wholly from abroad, but the Belize registered agent and (if applicable) a locally licensed attorney engaged for legal-defense services must be in place.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service, Ministry of Finance of Belize (portal: https://taxservice.gov.bz; e-filing portal: https://easytax.gov.bz; customs and excise administered by Belize Customs and Excise Department via ASYCUDA World: https://www.customs.gov.bz)

E-government filing portal

An International Business Company (IBC) is formed under the Belize International Business Companies Act and obtains its corporate registration number from the Belize Companies and Corporate Affairs Registry. For indirect tax/GST purposes, a foreign-owned IBC that undertakes a Belize taxable local activity must apply to the Belize Tax Service for a Tax Identification Number using the GST registration application package, submitting: certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, directors and shareholders register, registered office confirmation, local business address, description of services, projected annual turnover, bank confirmation and authorized signatory identification. If the IBC remains purely offshore, no income tax registration is required and the Registry-issued IBC number is used for corporate identity; however, most banks require a Belize Tax Service TIN for account opening and wire transfers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Belize. An IBC is statutorily tax-exempt under the International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270): it is exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, stamp duty and all other Belize taxes on international business operations, with a 50-year grandfather protection if Belize tax law changes. The only relevant elections are (i) voluntary registration for General Sales Tax if the IBC makes taxable local supplies; (ii) application to Customs/GST authorities to zero-rate qualifying export supplies; and (iii) hybrid mismatch elections are not available because the IBC sits outside the Belize income tax net.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

BZD 75,000 annual taxable turnover; businesses whose taxable supplies exceed this threshold must register for GST, and suppliers of zero-rated export services may register voluntarily to reclaim input GST

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods and services are zero-rated under the General Sales Tax Act. Goods qualify if they are exported by the supplier in the course of business and the supplier retains proof of physical export. Services qualify if supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Belize when the service is performed, with principal exceptions for services directly connected with land or tangible movable property located in Belize and services received by a person through a Belize permanent establishment. For anti-dumping trade-defense consulting rendered from Belize to a foreign client, the service is generally zero-rated as an offshore export service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Belize Customs Form/Customs Export Entry filed via ASYCUDA World)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export (for goods shipments)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Belize authorized dealer evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Service-exporter substantiation file: signed consulting agreement, zero-rated invoice, SWIFT wire credit confirmation, foreign client TIN or tax residency certificate, and written certification that the service recipient is non-resident

Belize does not currently mandate a real-time fiscal clearance e-invoicing API or digital stamping platform. GST-registered businesses must issue prescribed tax invoices showing TIN, registration number, description, rate and GST amount. Customs declarations are submitted electronically through ASYCUDA World, which is the authorized electronic clearance system for exports and imports; no real-time domestic GST clearance API is imposed at present.

Zero-rating claims are subject to GST and Customs audit scrutiny. Claimants must retain export and FX repatriation documentation for at least six years. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to assessment of the uncollected GST at 12.5%, interest, penalties of up to 100%-200% of the tax evaded, fines, criminal prosecution, and clawback of refunds previously paid. False customs declarations additionally attract forfeiture, monetary penalties and suspension of export privileges under the Customs and Excise Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belize dollar (BZD) is pegged to the US dollar (USD) at BZD 2.00 = USD 1.00. Current account convertibility is broadly unrestricted for licensed commercial banks, but capital account transactions remain subject to Exchange Control approval unless conducted by an International Business Company (IBC). An IBC incorporated under the International Business Companies Act, Chapter 270, is statutorily exempt from Belize exchange control regulations for its foreign-currency operations. This allows an IBC engaged in cross-border anti-dumping trade defense consulting to receive USD fees from foreign clients, maintain USD bank accounts, and remit profits to non-resident shareholders without the usual Central Bank authorization hurdles. The practical constraints are primarily bank-level CBC/FATF due diligence rather than exchange control approvals. Dividend and profit distributions to non-resident beneficial owners are not subject to Belize withholding tax or exchange control restrictions for IBCs, provided the IBC is not classified as carrying on business within Belize under the relevant legislation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement applies to an IBC qualifying under the International Business Companies Act. IBCs providing professional trade-defense consulting services are treated as non-resident for currency control purposes; their foreign-currency receipts may be retained in full, with 0% mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory surrender deadline. If, exceptionally, an IBC is deemed resident due to prohibited economic activity inside Belize, the general Exchange Control regime may require export proceeds to be surrendered to an authorised dealer within a limited period; however, the standard IBC banking structure avoids this outcome by maintaining non-resident status and USD-denominated accounts.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB), operating under the Exchange Control Act, Chapter 34 of the Substantive Laws of Belize, and the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) for AML/CFT supervisory coordination.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for fully offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Airwallex, or Payoneer) as the primary treasury rail. Belize has no standalone Electronic Money Institution licensing regime; the CBB licenses only banks and authorized dealers. A Belize IBC may legally open an account with a licensed foreign EMI, but such EMIs often classify Belize IBCs as high-risk or unbankable due to perceived opacity and lack of local regulatory equivalence. The legal constraint is contractual rather than statutory: foreign EMIs generally require clean corporate registry extracts, certified UBEO declarations, and may prohibit Belize-registered entities entirely. Therefore, the optimal execution path is a licensed Belize commercial bank account used as the master account, with foreign EMI accounts used only for sub-collection in specific client jurisdictions where the Belize bank lacks swift/road connectivity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident shareholders must be routed through licensed international banks using SWIFT MT103 or MT202 messages. Recommended destination account is a segregated USD capital account with the Belize bank's correspondent bank in New York or Miami. The IBC must provide to the remitting bank the following purpose codes: for share capital or contributed surplus, use IMF Balance of Payments code 'E511' (Equity Securities) or, for shareholder loans, 'C430' (Other Investment Liabilities – Loans). The bank will require the board resolution authorizing issuance of shares or loan drawdown, the subscription agreement, copy of the UBO declaration, and the filing of a capital returns form with the Belize Companies Registry as applicable. Funds derived from shareholders must first be subject to source-of-funds verification; if the injection exceeds USD 50,000, enhanced due-diligence and FIU reporting thresholds may be triggered. The most compliant route is: shareholder's domestic bank → international correspondent → CBB's payment system → Belize IBC account with full annotation of 'Board Resolution No.__, Share Subscription Agreement dated __'.Outbound trade disbursements for anti-dumping defense consulting are primarily service payments to client companies, expert witnesses, dossier filing fees, legal counsel in foreign jurisdictions, and regulatory agents. The primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for vendor payments and attorney fee remittances. For larger fee settlements, a Belize bank can issue a documentary Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) payable to an overseas consultant after presentation of invoices and signed progress reports, although this is uncommon in services. Given the IBC's operation in professional services, the recommended practice is to disburse via MT103 only after invoice matching and, where the destination requires it, including a purpose code such as 'IC220' (Other Business Services) or 'SF420' (Legal Services). Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (IBC statutory tax exemption; domestic Belize companies pay 25%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for IBC distributions; no Belize withholding tax on dividends paid by an IBC to any shareholder, resident or non-resident.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

IBC Annual License Renewal: on or before the anniversary of incorporation (or the Minister-designated due date); ESR-1 due within six months after the financial year end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Belize dollar threshold for Local/Master File under IBC law; prudent mandatory documentation threshold is BZD 3,000,000 (USD 1,500,000) aggregate related-party revenue or any cross-border intra-group payments exceeding BZD 1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Belize International Business Companies (Amendment) Act 2019 introduces substance requirements for entities undertaking any relevant activity. A trade-remedy defense consultancy (ISIC 7490) is not a listed relevant activity per the OECD substance matrix, but is treated as a 'distribution and service centre' when it generates fee income from cross-border anti-dumping litigation support; where in scope, the IBC must maintain in Belize: (a) board meetings and management functions, (b) adequate number of employees and premises, (c) minimum annual operating expenditure, and (d) a physical presence proportionate to the activity. Filing is made via ESR-1; failure to satisfy substance may result in exchange of information with OECD and sanctions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Status Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under Income and Business Tax Act and IBC Act: 100% to 200% penalty on tax evaded plus daily interest of 2%; forfeiture of assets; strike-off; supply of information to foreign tax authorities under exchange-of-information provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners and managing officers are jointly and severally liable for outstanding taxes, penalties and costs; Belize courts may pierce the veil, extend liability, impose travel bans, asset prohibiting orders, and custodial sentences of up to 10 years.
Shell Company and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: IFSC may impose civil penalties up to BZD 100,000 per entity, cancel the IBC license, strike off the company, report to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU), and pursue confiscation proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified for up to 15 years; personal fines of BZD 25,000; travel restrictions and mutual assistance measures; beneficiaries deemed 'shadow directors' exposed to equity compensation and criminal fraud liability.
AML and Anti-Dumping Trade Defense Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under Money Laundering (Prevention and Control) Act: fines up to BZD 500,000 and imprisonment for money laundering for up to 15 years; corporate confiscation of the whole offense-related benefit; FIU freezing orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Individual officers face separate criminal prosecution alongside the entity; funds traced to directors are subject to civil recovery and restraint orders; travel bans may be imposed during investigation; directors aiding anti-dumping fraud in foreign jurisdictions can be extradited for trade/financial fraud.
Unlicensed Professional/Regulatory Activity

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed activity is an offense subject to fines and imprisonment under the Belize Legal Profession Act and IFSC licensing regulations; contract voidableness; confiscation of unlawful fees; public admonition and license revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors and principals are personally liable for restitution of all unlawful fees; court may issue permanent restriction orders and prohibit opening new business; custodial sentence not exceeding 10 years for illegal practice.
