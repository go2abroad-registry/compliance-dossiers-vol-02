Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Belize Tax Service Department (part of the Ministry of Finance) is the national tax administrator for VAT, income tax, business tax, and stamp duties; e-services are transacted through the official portal at https://etax.taxservice.gov.bz, with primary administrative guidance published at https://www.taxservice.gov.bz.

E-government filing portal

An International Business Company (IBC) organized under the Belize International Business Companies Act (Cap. 270) is not automatically issued a domestic TIN or VAT number because the entity enjoys a statutory exemption from Belize income tax, business tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, and stamp duty. The IBC's primary statutory identifier is the certificate of incorporation issued by the Belize Companies and Corporate Affairs Registry through a licensed registered agent. If a TIN or VAT ID is required for operational reasons such as opening a local bank account or executing a local services contract, the foreign enterprise must file a Business Taxpayer Application with the Belize Tax Service Department, submitting the IBC certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing, a board resolution authorizing the local application, copies of the register of directors and shareholders, due diligence documentation on the ultimate beneficial owners, and a letter from the registered agent confirming the IBC's exempt status. The Tax Service Department will assign a tax identification number flagged as 'IBC Exempt' without admitting the entity to domestic taxability. No minimum capital contribution or local physical presence is required to complete the registration procedure for this exempt classification.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (statutory exemption for International Business Companies). Income, gains, and profits derived from sources outside Belize fall entirely outside the Belizean tax net, and the IBC is generally not treated as a Belize tax resident. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (statutory exemption for International Business Companies). Income, gains, and profits derived from sources outside Belize fall entirely outside the Belizean tax net, and the IBC is generally not treated as a Belize tax resident. The exemption is valid for a fixed statutory period of 50 years from date of incorporation, subject to compliance with annual registry filings and any applicable economic substance reporting obligations.

Available election forms

Belize does not maintain an elective check-the-box regime comparable to United States Treasury Regulations, and the IBC Act does not allow an IBC to elect pass-through taxation while retaining its exempt IBC character. However, an IBC may elect to convert into a domestic Belize company by resolution and approval of the Financial Secretary and the Registrar; such conversion cancels the IBC tax exemption and makes the entity liable to domestic income tax on worldwide income. An IBC may also voluntarily register for Belize VAT where it intends to make taxable supplies within Belize, and may elect to adopt formal international financial reporting standards for substance presentation. Hybrid mismatch elections are not separately provided for in domestic legislation; nevertheless, an IBC may make a procedural election to apply the Economic Substance (Implementation) instructions for calendar-year reporting to preserve documented economic substance in a non-tax-privileged manner.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

BZD 75,000 (Belize dollars) of annual taxable turnover in any consecutive twelve-month period. A foreign entity exclusively conducting an exempt IBC operation without domestic supplies is not required to register; once the entity commences a local taxable supply of goods or services, the BZD 75,000 threshold applies and the entity must file a VAT registration application within 21 days of exceeding the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available on the export of goods where export is evidenced by customs documentation. For services, zero-rating applies where the recipient of the supply belongs outside Belize and the direct benefit and economic enjoyment of the service occurs outside Belize. In the context of a global Employer of Record and cross-border contractor payroll platform, payroll processing, employment compliance, and contractor management services provided to non-Belizean clients will qualify for the zero rate only where the service is not consumed or used within Belize, the consideration is received in foreign currency, and the supplier retains documentation demonstrating performance of payroll and EOR obligations for offshore workforces. Services supplied to a Belizean resident or services with beneficial use in Belize remain subject to the standard 12.5% VAT rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUS-EXP) issued by the Belize Customs Department, required when the EOR operation includes physical export of goods, software media, or contract equipment; not applicable to pure service supply.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual export movement, retained for goods-based transactions or hybrid goods-and-services arrangements.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an authorized foreign exchange dealer in Belize demonstrating that the foreign currency consideration for the zero-rated export has entered the Belize financial system.
  • VAT zero-rated invoice reflecting the taxation supply category, the supplier's VAT registration number, a statement that the supply is zero-rated, and the statutory invoice particulars.
  • Documentation of the recipient's non-resident status, including a certificate of tax residence, foreign company registry extract, or equivalent evidence that the client belongs outside Belize.
  • Executed service agreement, payroll registers, contractor time records, and disbursement reports evidencing that the EOR services were enjoyed wholly outside Belize.

Belize has not introduced a real-time electronic invoicing clearance mandate, government fiscal stamping, or API-based transaction reporting as of the applicable assessment period. Taxable persons are required to issue standard sequential VAT invoices containing prescribed information, retain delivery documentation, and transmit VAT returns via the Belize Tax Service electronic filing portal. There is no pre-clearance or real-time transaction authorization requirement under the current indirect tax framework.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to post-clearance audit by the Belize Tax Service Department, with a heightened statutory examination of EOR arrangements based on the apparent mismatch between Belize-domiciled invoicing and foreign workforces. Where zero-rating is incorrectly claimed, the authority may retroactively assess VAT for a period of up to three years, extended to six years in cases of deliberate concealment or false documentation. Interest accrues at the rate of 20% per annum, and civil penalties may reach 100% of the tax evaded; criminal prosecution may additionally expose directors, officers, and the registered agent to fines up to BZD 10,000 and imprisonment. For an IBC, proof of fraudulent export zero-rating or submission of false statements in continuing registration filings triggers loss of the statutory tax exemption, immediate strike-off risk by the Registrar, and retroactive cancellation of the IBC's tax-exempt certificate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Belize Dollar (BZD) operates a hard peg of US$1 = BZ$2.00 (in effect since 1976), with the CBB maintaining full convertibility through authorised dealers. The Exchange Control Act restricts residents; however, an International Business Company is statutorily exempt under section 22 of the IBC Act (Cap. 270), subject to the condition that it does not carry on business with Belize residents. Consequently, the EOR IBC can freely receive client payroll funding, hold USD/EUR accounts, and remit fees or dividends without CBB capital-account permits. Profit remittance is untaxed under the IBC tax-exemption regime and faces no exchange-control gate; bank-level AML filters and internal remittance caps remain the only practical hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident goods exporters must repatriate proceeds to an authorised dealer within 180 days and convert the BZD equivalent at the official peg unless CBB retention approval is obtained. A non-resident IBC conducting global EOR payroll services is not a 'resident exporter' and enjoys the section 22 IBC Act exchange-control exemption; no mandatory surrender or conversion of payroll collections applies. The IBC must, however, ensure zero BZD-denominated transactions with Belize residents, as any such activity would trigger surrender obligations and nullify the exemption.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Belize (CBB) under the Central Bank of Belize Act (Cap. 262), administering the Exchange Control Act (Cap. 247) and Exchange Control Regulations; Financial Services Commission of Belize (FSC) supervises licensed financial services auxiliary to the banking function.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-Low. Belize is outside the SEPA reachable jurisdiction list and is deprioritised by most UK/FCA and EU-regulated EMI providers (Revolut Business, Payoneer, MultiPass), which treat Belizean IBCs as elevated-risk due to legacy IBC opacity and EU/OECD listing history. Wise is functionally viable only as a last-mile payout overlay for low-value contractor disbursements, not as the primary settlement spine, because Wise does not issue BZD-resident local account details and cannot independently onboard a Belize IBC without a referenced commercial bank relationship. Recommended architecture: a Belize commercial-bank USD settlement account as the backbone; Wise (or comparable EMI) as a secondary payout leg only, segregated from client funds and subject to a hard treasury flow cap.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All external funds must arrive via SWIFT MT103 from an OECD-licensed credit institution into the IBC's USD settlement account in Belize; no CBB exchange-control approval is required for the IBC exemption, but payment narratives must carry explicit purpose descriptors such as 'Capital Contribution - Belize IBC incorporation', 'Pre-seed funding for Global EOR implementation', or 'Client payroll prepayment under MS-2024-xxx', together with the payer's LEI where available. The receiving bank's compliance unit will request the corporate resolution authorising the capital increase, share-allotment confirmation from the Belize registered agent, and a directors' attestation that none of the funds derive from Belize resident activities; USD balances remain unexposed to conversion spread due to the fixed 2:1 peg.Clearing rails are structured in four tiers: (i) primary settlement via SWIFT MT103 from the Belize USD account to an international payroll aggregator or partner bank; (ii) local payroll direct debit executed through in-country payroll entities or licensed processors using ACH/SEPA/local instant rails; (iii) Wise Direct for low-value contractor payouts as a supplemental last-mile rail; and (iv) commercial letters of credit, which are inapplicable to an EOR services book and would be rejected by the bank's trade desk as goods-trade instruments unsuited to operational staffing floats.Outright FX forwards on USD/EUR, USD/GBP and USD/CAD with tenors up to 12 months, executed with a Belize-licensed commercial bank or OECD-regulated counterparty for payroll-rate certainty.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on illiquid regional currencies including BZD and other Caribbean floaters, capped at one-month of payroll exposure to fix conversion cost for tail-currency payout books.Cross-currency swaps and rolling forward structures limited to a notional ceiling of three months of forecast payroll liabilities, preserving hedging character and avoiding speculative classification under the IBC treasury policy.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

1.75% Business Tax on gross receipts (general non-retail corporate rate under the Income and Business Tax Act, Cap. 55, Laws of Belize). A residual 25% Income Tax applies to corporate investment income and items not within the Business Tax net. Belize International Business Companies (IBCs) in good standing remain exempt on foreign-source income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Belize does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions, whether paid to resident or non-resident shareholders, and whether in cash or in shares.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Belize has no statutory Local File or Master File thresholds. The Commissioner may re-determine related-party pricing under section 5(4) of the Income and Business Tax Act and the general anti-avoidance rule. Best-practice threshold: prepare and retain a Local File where intercompany transactions, including salary pass-through, management fees, and EOR margin charges, exceed BZD 1,000,000 (approximately USD 500,000) per year. Prepare a Master File where the multinational group reports consolidated turnover above BZD 750,000,000, the OECD country-by-country reporting threshold adopted in Belize's international tax commitments. In practice, a 5% to 15% mark-up on payroll pass-through for a low-risk EOR agent is considered arm's length where the Belize IBC assumes no market, credit, or employment risk.

Economic substance scope: The International Business Companies Act, Cap. 270, as amended in 2018 and 2019, together with the Income and Business Tax Act, imposes economic substance requirements aligned with EU and OECD standards. A Belize IBC conducting relevant activities, including distribution, service center, financing, and intellectual property holding, must demonstrate in Belize: (i) a physical office and premises; (ii) board meetings and strategic management; (iii) adequate employees and operational expenditure proportionate to the activity; and (iv) core income-generating activities performed in Belize. A global EOR staffing platform routed through a Belize IBC must maintain at least one Belize-resident director, a local payroll compliance officer, and a registered agent with full accounting records. A paper IBC without substance will lose the foreign-source exemption, be reclassified as a domestic taxable company at 1.75% Business Tax on worldwide gross receipts, and be denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Business Tax Filings

Statutory sanction: Best-of-judgment assessment of the full understated tax; penalty of 100% of the evaded tax; interest at 1% per month; criminal fines up to BZD 10,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Income and Business Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax arrears and penalties where they signed false returns or directed the fraud; civil restitution orders, travel bans, and seizure of personal assets are available to the TSD.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Breach

Statutory sanction: Strike-off from the Registry after 3 months of default on the annual return; restoration penalty up to BZD 1,000; retrospective reassessment of worldwide receipts at 1.75% Business Tax plus 10% late surcharge; denial of benefits under the Belize-UK treaty.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally responsible for debts incurred during the struck-off period, for reinstatement filings, and for any misrepresentation made to the Registrar; directors may be disqualified for up to 5 years and barred from serving on any Belize company.
AML, Money Laundering, and Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering and Terrorism (Prevention) Act and the Beneficial Ownership Act 2020: fines up to BZD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years; asset freezing orders, confiscation orders, and compulsory liquidation of the IBC.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and registered agents face personal fines, travel bans, and imprisonment; the Belize Financial Intelligence Unit may blacklist the company and its principals, triggering correspondent bank account closures and reputational collapse.
Prohibited Domestic Trade and Unlicensed EOR Activity

Statutory sanction: The IBC Act prohibits an IBC from carrying on business with Belize residents except for prescribed offshore functions. Sanctions include deemed conversion to a domestic company, retrospective assessment at 1.75% to 6% Business Tax plus 25% Income Tax on local profits, license fines up to BZD 5,000, closure orders, and mandatory unpaid wage restitution to placed workers.

Director personal liability: Directors are individually and jointly liable for unpaid wages and statutory fines of locally placed workers; they face immigration exclusion from Belize, imprisonment for illegal placement of foreign workers, and personal liability for all contracts entered into in violation of the IBC Act.
