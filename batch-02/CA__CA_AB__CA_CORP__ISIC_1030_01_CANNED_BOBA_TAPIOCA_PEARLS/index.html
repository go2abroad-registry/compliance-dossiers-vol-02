Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); corporate tax, business-number, and GST/HST services are administered through CRA online services, including My Business Account and Business Registration Online. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under an agreement with the Province of Alberta.

E-government filing portal

A corporation carrying on business in Canada generally obtains a nine-digit CRA Business Number (BN) and registers the applicable program accounts, including a corporate income tax account (RC) and, when required, a GST/HST account (RT). Registration is available through CRA Business Registration Online where eligible, or by submitting Form RC1 or contacting the CRA. A non-resident corporation may apply for a BN and required accounts through the CRA's non-resident registration procedures; it should provide its legal name, jurisdiction and date of incorporation, registered address, Canadian business activities, directors, and authorized representative information. GST/HST registration is generally required once the corporation is no longer a small supplier or elects voluntary registration. Alberta does not issue a separate provincial corporate income tax identification number for corporations filing under the federal-provincial administration agreement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election and no general election for a corporation to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity. A Canadian corporation is generally a separate taxpayer. Available elections are limited to specific statutory matters, including certain tax-deferred reorganizations, amalgamations, and intercorporate transfers. Canadian hybrid-mismatch and cross-border rules may deny deductions or impose inclusions in specified arrangements; they do not provide a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, subject to statutory rules and exceptions. A person exceeding the threshold must register and collect GST as required. Voluntary registration may be available below the threshold to eligible persons making taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are generally zero-rated where the statutory conditions are met, including that the goods are exported from Canada and are not acquired for consumption, use, or supply in Canada before export, subject to applicable exceptions. The supplier must retain evidence sufficient to establish export and eligibility. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated where the specific statutory conditions are met; exclusions apply, including certain services relating to Canadian real property, tangible personal property, or specified individuals or transactions in Canada. Zero-rating is not automatic merely because the customer or payer is outside Canada. A registrant may generally claim input tax credits for eligible GST paid or payable on inputs used in commercial activities, subject to documentation and statutory limitations.

  • Canada export declaration filed through the applicable Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) process when an export declaration is required; retain the declaration or applicable proof of reporting.
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order or sales contract, and records identifying the goods, purchaser, destination, and transaction terms.
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, carrier receipt, or other transport documentation showing shipment from Canada to the foreign destination.
  • Proof of delivery or receipt outside Canada, where available, and customs import or entry records from the destination country, where available.
  • Accounting records and correspondence supporting the recipient's non-resident status and the nature and place of supply for zero-rated services.
  • Bank records or foreign-exchange records may support the transaction but an Inward Remittance Certificate is not a generally prescribed Canadian GST export document.

Canada has no general real-time e-invoice clearance, fiscal-stamping, or mandatory government e-invoicing system for ordinary domestic business-to-business transactions. GST registrants must issue invoices or receipts containing prescribed information when required and retain adequate electronic or paper records. CRA electronic filing and payment obligations apply to specified registrants and returns; they do not constitute transaction-level invoice clearance.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST, deny unsupported zero-rating or input tax credits, recover resulting tax, and assess interest and applicable penalties. False statements or omissions may attract statutory penalties; gross-negligence penalties can generally equal the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit, subject to the governing statutory provisions. Exporters should retain records for the applicable statutory retention period and be able to substantiate export, recipient status, and transaction characterization. There is no general Canadian export-VAT refund regime requiring foreign-currency repatriation as a condition of zero-rating.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, tax rules, and financial-institution due diligence. There is no general requirement to obtain prior approval to inject or repatriate corporate capital, pay foreign suppliers, or convert Canadian dollars. Dividends, interest, royalties, and certain other payments to non-residents may be subject to Canadian withholding tax, generally 25% under domestic law unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty and supported by required documentation. Dividends must be lawfully declared from funds available under the governing corporate statute. Cross-border related-party financing, transfer pricing, thin-capitalization rules, and interest-deductibility limitations may affect payments and deductions. Non-resident investors should also assess the Investment Canada Act and applicable national-security review requirements; these are not general FX controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no general statutory deadline for converting export receipts into Canadian dollars and no mandatory export-proceeds surrender ratio. Exporters may retain foreign-currency receipts and settle them through Canadian or offshore accounts, subject to sanctions, tax, contractual, and bank compliance requirements. Canadian financial institutions must report qualifying international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC under applicable rules; this is a reporting obligation, not a surrender or conversion requirement. Exporters should separately assess customs, export-control, tax, and sector-specific reporting requirements.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Canada is Canada's central bank and conducts monetary policy; it does not administer general foreign-exchange controls. Canada has no general capital controls or mandatory foreign-exchange conversion regime. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers relevant anti-money-laundering reporting requirements under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated financial institutions. The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) administers applicable tax and withholding obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary collections, payments, and foreign-currency services where the provider is legally authorized to serve Canadian business customers and supports the required currencies and transaction corridors. An offshore electronic-money institution account is not a substitute for a Canadian operating bank account, does not itself confer Canadian deposit insurance, and may be restricted by the provider's licensing, correspondent-bank access, sanctions screening, and risk policies. Verify Canadian registration or authorization where applicable, safeguarding and insolvency terms, settlement arrangements, and the provider's ability to support Canadian-dollar payments and trade documentation. Avoid routing capital contributions or material trade flows through personal or inadequately documented accounts.
