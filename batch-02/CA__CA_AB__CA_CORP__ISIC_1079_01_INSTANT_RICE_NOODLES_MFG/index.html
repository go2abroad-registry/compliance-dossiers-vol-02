Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) at https://www.canada.ca/revenue-agency (GST/HST registration and T2 corporate income tax administration for federal Part I tax and Alberta provincial corporate income tax under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; Alberta provincial business registration is effected through Service Alberta / Alberta Corporate Registry (www.alberta.ca/corporate-registry) under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta)).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned manufacturing corporation must obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) from CRA using Form RC1 or the Business Registration Online (BRO) service, and simultaneously open a GST/HST account (BN + 'RT' program identifier) via CRA My Business Account; if the corporation is incorporated federally, an extra-provincial registration in Alberta under the Business Corporations Act is required through Service Alberta before carrying on business in Alberta; the BN is the universal tax identification number used for corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and customs purposes, and the Alberta corporate income tax account is opened automatically upon filing the T2 return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Business Corporation is a separate taxable person under the Income Tax Act (Canada) with no US-style check-the-box pass-through treatment; a corporation incorporated in Canada is resident in Canada by statute (s. 250(4) ITA) and is subject to federal Part I corporate tax (12% general rate for 2025; small business deduction rate of 9% restricted where non-residents or public corporations control the corporation) and Alberta provincial corporate income tax at a flat 8%, computed on taxable income allocated to Alberta permanent establishments; a non-resident-owned branch is taxed under Part I plus the 25% branch tax under Part I.1/Part XIV (reduced by treaty).

Available election forms

Canada relies on statutory elections rather than check-the-box: (i) the s. 216(1) ITA election permits a non-resident to file a net-basis return on Canadian rental/investment income and avoid Part XIII gross withholding; (ii) the s. 219 ITA branch-tax election governs profit extraction from a non-resident's Canadian branch; (iii) GST/HST group registration under ETA s. 270(1) and the closely-related-corporations election under ETA s. 156(1) can neutralize inter-corporate tax on intra-group supplies; (iv) s. 85(1) and s. 86.1 rollovers are available at low tax cost for capital reorganization; (v) hybrid mismatch results are constrained by the hybrid arrangement rules in ss. 18.4-18.5 ITA, and the ETA applies special rules for supplies between a non-resident head office and Canadian permanent establishment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold is measured by worldwide gross revenues (exclusive of GST/HST) from taxable supplies in the current calendar quarter and the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once this threshold is exceeded, registration becomes mandatory; voluntary registration is available to a non-resident exporter to claim input tax credits and keep zero-rated flows clean.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported packaged instant/retort rice noodles qualify as zero-rated supplies under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the goods are supplied to a non-resident or to a Canadian recipient for export, shipped by the supplier to a destination outside Canada, and exported in commercial quantities; the exporter must be GST/HST registered to claim the zero rating and the corresponding input tax credits; related advisory and logistics services supplied to non-residents and used wholly outside Canada are also zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V; domestic sales of the product may additionally qualify for zero-rating as basic groceries, but restaurant-style meals sold for immediate consumption are taxable supplies.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) filed via the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) or GTR online reporting system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list describing the goods and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 credit advice, or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Signed sale contract / purchase order evidencing transfer of title and risk outside Canada
  • CRA proof-of-export record package (e.g., carrier certificate, freight forwarder certificate, or seller's own shipment records) conforming to GST/HST Memorandum 4-4-2

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime comparable to Latin American CTC systems; e-invoicing is permissible, and CRA prescribes the GST/HST tax invoice content (registrant's BN and legal name, date, invoice number, recipient identification, tax status, and amounts) under ETA s. 169(4) and accompanying regulations; a GST/HST registrant must be able to produce electronically stored invoices and records acceptable to CRA's Electronic Evidence Assessment Tool (E-EATS); registration, return filing, and amendments must be made electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST EDI via CRA My Business Account; the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoice framework is voluntary, and low-value/high-volume exporters may transact through CRA's machine-to-machine (API) channels under the Business Gateway.

CRA audits zero-rated export claims under GST/HST Memorandum 4-4-2 and will reassess the supply at 5% GST if the exporter fails to maintain the prescribed proof-of-export documentation; the reassessed tax carries daily compound interest under ETA s. 280.1 plus late-filing penalties and, for false statements or omissions on returns or customs documentation, a penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax understated under ETA s. 285(1); deliberate misstatements attract gross-negligence penalties under ETA s. 285.1 and Income Tax Act s. 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated federal/Alberta tax, with possible criminal prosecution for fraud; failure to repatriate proceeds within the 180-day benchmark creates foreign-exchange gains taxable as ordinary income and may trigger CRA transfer-pricing review or shareholder-benefit treatment under ITA ss. 90/15 if proceeds are retained offshore by a related non-resident.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; no prior approval is required for foreign direct investment, dividend remittance, trade settlements, or loan repayments. Remittance hurdles are purely compliance-driven: 25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends (reducible under treaty), FINTRAC sanctions and AML screening, and proof of export proceeds only for customs and audit purposes

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio exists; the Exchange Control regulations were suspended in 1951 and Canada operates a free foreign-exchange system. Export receipts in any currency may be retained, converted, or repatriated without central bank approval; only FINTRAC cross-border transaction reporting applies at the CAD 10,000 threshold

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Bank of Canada Act); exchange administration and cross-border currency reporting rest with FINTRAC and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), with prudential supervision of banks conducted by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Payoneer, Stripe) are treated as Money Service Businesses and require FINTRAC registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act plus licensing under Alberta's Money Services Businesses Act when operating locally. They are viable supplementary collection and vendor-payment rails, but cannot serve as the primary CAD clearing bank, hold regulated deposits, or issue documentary letters of credit; a Schedule I bank account remains mandatory for the treasury structure

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 100,000 aggregate reportable non-arm's length transactions per non-resident taxpayer (Form T106); country-by-country reporting required for consolidated group revenue exceeding CAD 750 million; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be completed by the tax return due date.

Economic substance scope: Alberta is not a zero-tax regime, so offshore-style ESR does not apply directly; however, Canada's GAAR, OECD substantial-activity standards, and DTA LOB provisions require the corporation to maintain real payroll, manufacturing assets, and operational risk in Alberta to support arm's length profitability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment; gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax; criminal fines up to CAD 10,000 plus 100% to 200% of evaded tax; imprisonment up to five years under Income Tax Act s.239.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST under ss.227.1 and 323.1 of the Excise Tax Act; passport seizure under Canada's travel-bans program; criminal prosecution where directors knowingly participate.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment to arm's length price; transfer pricing penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment; denial of correlative relief.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable if they sign false certifications or consciously cause the corporation to fail to maintain contemporaneous documentation.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to recharacterize the structure, deny treaty benefits, impose Part XIII withholding on unrepatriated amounts; Alberta corporate registry may dissolve the company under the Business Corporations Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders may be subject to creditor claw-back actions under fraudulent conveyance statutes and CRA director liability assessments where the corporate veil is pierced on a sham basis.
AML and International Trade Controls

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act penalties up to CAD 2 million; sanctions violations under SEMA and export controls; suspension or revocation of required licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal charges for failing to report suspicious transactions; civil asset forfeiture; travel bans and personal restitution orders in connection with sanctioned-party dealings.
