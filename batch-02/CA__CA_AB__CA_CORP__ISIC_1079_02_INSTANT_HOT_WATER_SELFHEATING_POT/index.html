Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Ottawa — national tax administration and agent of the Government of Alberta for corporate income tax under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act (RSA 2000 c C-21); federal GST administration under the Excise Tax Act (RSC 1985 c E-15). Portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/business-number-registration.html; CRA My Business Account; Alberta Corporate Registry at https://www.albertacorporate.com; CBSA (Canada Border Services Agency) administers export declarations via eManifest and CARM/GCA.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Business Corporation incorporated federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the Alberta Business Corporations Act and carrying on business in Alberta: (1) file Articles of Incorporation, obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, and comply with director-residency rules (ABCA requires a majority of resident Canadian directors; CBCA requires 25% resident Canadian directors); (2) obtain the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1, and simultaneously open program accounts: RC (corporate income tax — dual federal/Alberta T2 filing), RT (GST/HST), RM (CBSA import/export), and RP (payroll if employees are engaged); (3) GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters or may be elected voluntarily under ETA s. 240(3); (4) if incorporated under the CBCA, complete extra-provincial registration in Alberta (EPC compliance) with the Alberta Corporate Registry; (5) file the annual T2 corporate income tax return within six months of each taxation year-end (electronic filing mandatory where gross revenue exceeds CAD 1 million) with GIFI and Alberta Schedule 5 allocation of taxable income to the Alberta permanent establishment, applying the 2% small-business rate on the first CAD 500,000 of eligible income and 8% general Alberta rate; (6) maintain records in English or French for six years and disclose reportable cross-border transactions on Form T106 and foreign affiliates on Form T1134.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Canadian-incorporated Business Corporation is a statutory resident of Canada under ITA s. 250(4)(a) and is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income (federal general rate 15%, federal small-business rate 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of CCPC income, plus Alberta general rate 8% and Alberta small-business rate 2%). It is the GST/HST registrant liable for 5% GST on taxable supplies; no pass-through or check-the-box flow-through election exists for a Canadian-incorporated corporation; non-resident shareholders derive income only as dividends subject to Part IV tax and dividend refund mechanics, and the corporation is the sole taxpayer on operating profits.

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a check-the-box regime for domestic corporations. Available statutory and administrative elections relevant to this structure include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier under ETA s. 240(3) to claim input tax credits and effect export zero-rating; (ii) election under ITA s. 83(2) to deem dividends paid out of the capital dividend account as non-taxable; (iii) election under ITA s. 93(1) on dispositions of shares of foreign affiliates to access exempt surplus; (iv) designation of a foreign affiliate under ITA s. 5907(1) and related surplus computation elections; (v) treaty-residence deeming under ITA s. 250(6)/(4.1) where the Canada-United States treaty tie-breaker assigns residence to the United States, limiting Canadian taxation of the corporation to Canadian-source income; and (vi) hybrid-mismatch outcomes, which are mandatory and cannot be elected around — ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5 and 18.6 automatically deny or defer deductions arising from imported hybrid mismatches and other double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — aggregate worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made by the person in Canada during any four consecutive calendar quarters, measured under the small-supplier rules of ETA s. 123(1) and the registration requirement of ETA s. 240(1). Once the threshold is exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory; a non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada must register only if carrying on business in Canada, subject to the same CAD 30,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating arises under Schedule VI, s. 20 of the Excise Tax Act for supplies of goods manufactured in Canada and sold for export: the registrant must possess documentary evidence of physical export (contract, commercial invoice, CBSA export declaration, bill of lading or waybill); the goods must be shipped to a destination outside Canada and exported 'as soon as is reasonably possible' after delivery; and supplies made in Canada to non-resident purchasers for subsequent export may also be zero-rated where the goods are in fact exported within a reasonable time. For the Self-Heating Instant Hot Water Pot: when sold domestically in Alberta, the basic-food meal component may be zero-rated as basic groceries under Schedule VI, Part III, while the chemical self-heating pot and utensil components are taxable at 5% under composite-supply analysis; when the complete self-heating meal kit is exported, the entire assembled product, including the exothermic heating element and food carton, is zero-rated at 0% under s. 20. Ancillary services performed on or in respect of the exported goods, such as contract manufacturing, packaging, and freight, may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, ss. 21-23; the zero-rating applies to the whole composite supply where the predominant element is the exported good.

Canada does not currently implement mandatory real-time e-invoice clearance, government fiscal stamping, or API pre-approval of invoices. Commercial invoices may be paper or electronic if they satisfy ETA s. 221(2) validation requirements (vendor name, BN/RT number, GST/HST charged). Mandatory digital compliance includes: (i) all GST/HST registrants must file GST/HST returns and remittances electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE or CRA-approved software for reporting periods beginning after 2022 (paper filing has been phased out); (ii) export declarations to the CBSA must be transmitted electronically through eManifest or CARM/GCA for commercial goods; (iii) zero-rating documentation must be retained in machine-readable format for at least six years; and (iv) any GST/HST net remittance exceeding CAD 10,000 must be remitted by electronic funds transfer.

CRA verifies export zero-rating claims by cross-matching GST/HST returns with CBSA export declarations, carrier manifests, customs broker records, and banking SWIFT inflows. Where documentation is deficient or false, CRA may reassess the supply as taxable at 5% in full, deny related input tax credits, and impose: (i) late-filing penalties under ETA s. 282 equal to 1% of the net tax due plus 0.25% per month up to 12 months; (ii) a 20% culpable-conduct penalty under ETA s. 285.1 for gross negligence in making false statements or omissions; (iii) a 100% penalty with a minimum CAD 2,000 under ETA s. 285 for false statements or wilful evasion; (iv) prescribed-rate interest compounded daily on the reassessed tax (2025 CRA rates approximately 8-10%); (v) criminal prosecution under ETA s. 289 with fines up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment up to two years for knowing participation in export-fraud schemes; and (vi) retrospective clawbacks over the four-year normal reassessment period, extended to eight years where misrepresentation is established. Alberta may also reopen corporate income tax assessments where the permanent- establishment allocation was understated. Where export proceeds remain offshore beyond the 180-day benchmark, ITA ss. 17 and 247 empower CRA to impute deemed interest and make transfer-pricing adjustments, adding corporate tax, penalties, and interest to the indirect-tax clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible. Canada imposes no general exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions; profit remittances by way of dividends are freely permitted without Bank of Canada approval. Applicable constraints are fiscal rather than exchange-control: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (NR4) on dividends and interest, section 247 transfer-pricing documentation, section 18(4) thin-capitalization rules on debt-to-equity limits, and FINTRAC reporting of large cross-border transfers (CAD 10,000 or more). For a self-heating-food manufacturer, royalties for licensed chemical-thermal technology are also freely remittable subject to withholding tax and treaty relief (e.g., Canada–China, Canada–Korea protocols).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no requirement to repatriate foreign-currency receipts within a prescribed timeline. Exporters may open and retain foreign-currency accounts at Canadian banks. Reporting obligations apply only for statistical purposes: exports exceeding CAD 2,000 per shipment must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the B13A Export Declaration, and aggregated data flows to Statistics Canada. Proceeds may be received in any currency and settled via the bank's correspondent network.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting as fiscal agent of the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act; there is no standalone foreign-exchange control authority. Canadian banks are prudentially supervised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and all cross-border wire transfers are monitored by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium (conditional). Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer and Currencycloud are not licensed banks in Canada and cannot provide CDIC-insured deposit accounts, bankers' acceptances, or commercial letters of credit. They may operate in Canada only as registered money services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC or, for payment services, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) effective November 1, 2024. Recommended use: secondary collection and small-ticket vendor disbursement rail only. Primary liquidity and LC issuance must sit with a Schedule I Canadian bank. Alberta-based food exporters using EMIs as the sole treasury rail face significant compliance rejection at the banking level due to lack of regulatory capital and audit trail depth.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity and debt capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103/202 from the investing entity's own bank account to the Canadian beneficiary account, with the following compliance protocol: (i) reference the beneficiary bank SWIFT BIC (e.g., ROYCCAT2 for RBC, TDOMCATTTOR for TD, NOSCCATT for Scotiabank, BOFMCAM2 for BMO, CIBCCATT for CIBC); (ii) include the beneficiary legal name and Alberta Registration Number; (iii) state purpose code for direct-investment equity injection (BPM6 code DI/611 or the receiving bank's equivalent capital-contribution code); (iv) attach a certified board resolution approving share issuance or shareholder loan; (v) provide a share-subscription agreement and, for parent-guarantee debt, an executed loan agreement. For inbound amounts greater than CAD 10,000, FINTRAC reporting is generated by the bank; amounts greater than CAD 50,000 in a 24-hour window trigger enhanced due-diligence verification and a likely compliance interview. USD-denominated injections clear via the bank's US correspondent through Fedwire or CHIPS into the USD account, after which spot conversion to CAD is optional and must be documented with a client FX dealing ticket.Trade settlements for raw materials (self-heating containers, desiccants, food ingredients, packaging film) and contract manufacturing will use: (i) regular supplier payments via SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer with full 56A/57A/59 beneficiary details and corresponding purpose code (e.g., trade payables); (ii) irrevocable confirmed commercial letters of credit (L/C at sight) for large shipments from Asia, issued by the Canadian bank, advised/confirmed through the seller's bank, with Incoterms 2020 (FOB or CIF) and required documents including commercial invoice, full-set clean-on-board bill of lading, packing list, certificate of origin, health certificate, and weight certificate; (iii) documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for mid-risk suppliers; and (iv) domestic CAD disbursements through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the emerging Real-Time Rail (RTR). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium (conditional). Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer and Currencycloud are not licensed banks in Canada and cannot provide CDIC-insured deposit accounts, bankers' acceptances, or commercial letters of credit. They may operate in Canada only as registered money services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC or, for payment services, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) effective November 1, 2024. Recommended use: secondary collection and small-ticket vendor disbursement rail only. Primary liquidity and LC issuance must sit with a Schedule I Canadian bank. Alberta-based food exporters using EMIs as the sole treasury rail face significant compliance rejection at the banking level due to lack of regulatory capital and audit trail depth.
