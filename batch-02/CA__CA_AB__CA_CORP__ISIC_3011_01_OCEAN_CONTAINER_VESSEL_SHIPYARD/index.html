Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta). The articles must specify the authorized share structure, and shares may be issued with or without par value; any par value shares must be fully paid before issuance. No statutory escrow account is mandated for incorporation. However, the commercial model (maritime installment payments and letter of credit) contemplates contractual escrow and refund guarantee arrangements, which the board will formally authorize at the post-incorporation organizational meeting.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online / My Business Account portal (www.canada.ca/taxes-business)

E-government filing portal

A foreign shipyard entity must first obtain a nine-digit CRA Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 (Application for Registration) or through Business Registration Online if eligibility criteria are met. To collect, remit, or recover GST/HST, the entity must concurrently apply for a GST/HST program account (RT) on the same registration. For maritime goods crossing borders, an Import/Export (RM) account is required. Non-resident applicants must provide certified copies of foreign incorporation documents, a foreign registry extract, and proof of a Canadian representative (Form RC59). Approval is generally issued within 2–4 weeks, after which the entity receives the BN and program account confirmation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with Canadian-resident worldwide tax liability; no tax-exempt zone treatment and no pass-through eligibility for a standard Business Corporation; Alberta does not maintain a separate provincial sales tax but corporate income tax is administered by CRA under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement.

Available election forms

Canada has no direct check-the-box election for corporate entities; CRA determines classification under domestic law and tax treaties. Applicable elections include: (i) corporate tax residence tie-breaker under the Canada-US and other treaties; (ii) capital cost allowance (CCA) and class elections under the Income Tax Act; (iii) rollover elections for amalgamations and asset transfers under sections 85, 86 and 87; (iv) GST/HST filing frequency election (monthly, quarterly, or annual); and (v) a voluntary GST/HST registration election for exporters to claim input tax credits. Hybrid mismatch and anti-arbitrage rules under section 18.4 apply automatically and cannot be overridden by an election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in combined annual taxable supplies in Canada for domestic persons; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada must register if Canadian-source taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, and a shipyard exporter should nevertheless voluntarily register to claim input tax credits on purchases attributable to zero-rated export sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported by the manufacturer directly to a non-resident are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. For ocean-going container vessels, zero-rating applies when the vessel is sold to a non-resident buyer and is either delivered into the buyer's foreign navigation or exported as part of the supply within 60 days; attendant services such as design, engineering, and offshore testing are zero-rated if performed wholly outside Canada or in direct respect of the exported vessel. The zero rate can be claimed only with contemporaneous export documentation demonstrating the physical export of the vessel and the non-residence of the purchaser.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent ocean transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of receipt of foreign currency payment from the non-resident buyer)

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or API-enabled commercial software on a periodic basis; invoices must contain prescribed information including vendor BN, HST registration identification, invoice date, and tax status. E-invoicing is permitted but not mandatory, and there is no pre-clearance API approval from the CRA for individual export invoices.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated supply to taxable status if the export documentation is incomplete or does not establish that the goods left Canada and the payment was received. Any denied zero-rating results in full collection of unreported GST/HST plus arrears interest. A false or negligent statement in support of an export zero-rating claim carries a gross negligence penalty of 25% of the amount assessed; fraudulent conduct may trigger criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. CRA retains a four-year normal reassessment window, but the period is unlimited where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default is established. The exporter must retain all supporting export documents for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions, and Canada accepts IMF Article VIII obligations. There is no blocking of profit remittance, no non-convertibility approval requirement for dividends, loan repayments, royalties, or technical fees, and no obligation to obtain ministerial consent for operational remittances. The only statutory costs and conditions on outward remittance are: (i) Income Tax Act Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, reduced to typically 5-15% under applicable tax treaties such as the Canada-US Convention); (ii) the corporate-law solvency test (ABCA/CBCA) that must be satisfied before a dividend or capital reduction is made; and (iii) standard CRS/FATCA beneficial-ownership self-certification and FINTRAC EFT reporting by financial intermediaries. Capital injections into a Canadian corporation from foreign shareholders are likewise unrestricted, subject to the Investment Canada Act review thresholds where the transaction involves acquisition of control rather than a subscription into an existing controlled entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation, conversion ratio, or holding-period rule on export receipts. A Canadian shipyard may retain export proceeds in foreign-currency accounts in Canada or at offshore banks without restriction. The only associated obligations are (i) filing a Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration (B13A) for goods exported above the prescribed reporting threshold; (ii) maintaining complete books and records of export transactions for income tax and customs audit; (iii) obtaining EIPA export permits where the goods are on the Export Control List (including certain dual-use or military-capable vessel designs); and (iv) automatic financial-institution reporting to FINTRAC of any cross-border electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, which is an AML/CFT intelligence measure and does not constitute a limitation on free disposition of funds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada maintains no general foreign-exchange-control statute: the former Exchange Control Act was allowed to lapse in 1951, and the Canadian dollar floats freely without central-bank conversion licensing or registration for individual foreign-exchange transactions. The Department of Finance Canada holds foreign-exchange policy responsibility; official international reserves are held in the Exchange Fund Account and managed by the Bank of Canada as fiscal agent. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally chartered banks; FINTRAC administers cross-border transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); and Global Affairs Canada enforces economic sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA), the United Nations Act, and export controls under the Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury layer, and viable only as a secondary concentrate-and-disburse rail for low-value payments. A foreign electronic-money institution (EMI) such as Wise, Airwallex, or similar overseas-licensed providers may process B2B cross-border payments into Canada only if it is registered as a money services business with FINTRAC and, to the extent it provides payment functions to Canadian end-users, complies with the Bank-of-Canada-administered Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime. Foreign EMIs are not deposit-taking Canadian banks, cannot issue or receive documentary letters of credit, and cannot operate shipbuilding escrow accounts or maritime installment guarantees. A compliant structure is for the EMI to settle through API integration with a Tier-1 Canadian bank, but the shipyard's capital-injection, installment LC, and large trade-disbursement rails must remain at a regulated domestic bank. Feasibility rating: Medium for subcontractor payroll, refunds, and visa/expense reimbursements; Low for escrow, LC, and high-value term settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.008) from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the shipyard's CAD commercial operating account at a Tier-1 Canadian bank, settled in central-bank finality over Payments Canada LYNX (RTGS). No Bank-of-Canada exchange-control approval is required; the receiving bank requires the wire's remittance information to state 'share subscription / paid-in capital' (or 'intercompany loan' where debt-funding is used) and the originator's bank must identify the ultimate beneficial owner. The verification bundle consists of: board resolution authorizing issuance; updated Alberta share register; CRS/FATCA self-certifications for all UBOs; and an origin-bank reference. For wires of CAD 10,000 or more the receiving bank automatically files an Electronic Funds Transfer report with FINTRAC as an information-only AML measure. Funds may be retained in a USD sub-account without conversion; if conversion to CAD is elected, the treasury desk should pre-book a market-rate forward or spot transaction with the same bank to lock the rebalancing cost, using the bank's standard FX confirmation and settlement-risk controls.Primary clearing rails, in order of prevalence: (1) SWIFT MT103 supplier wires for discrete invoices covering steel plate, castings, marine engines, electrical switchgear, and subcontract works, each carrying the invoice number and project code in field 70; (2) commercial documentary letters of credit under UCP600 (SWIFT MT700 series) with the presenting documents being commercial invoice, clean on-board bill of lading, insurance certificate, and inspection certificate, per the issuing bank's terms; (3) maritime installment LC or stage-payment escrow-release protocol driven by the shipbuilding contract milestones (keel laying, launch, delivery) and evidenced by classification-society progress certificates; (4) domestic CAD disbursements executed through Payments Canada ACSS/EFT, and through the Real-Time Rail where credit-risk terms permit; (5) USD obligations routed via the Canadian bank's US correspondent over Fedwire or CHIPS. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury layer, and viable only as a secondary concentrate-and-disburse rail for low-value payments. A foreign electronic-money institution (EMI) such as Wise, Airwallex, or similar overseas-licensed providers may process B2B cross-border payments into Canada only if it is registered as a money services business with FINTRAC and, to the extent it provides payment functions to Canadian end-users, complies with the Bank-of-Canada-administered Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime. Foreign EMIs are not deposit-taking Canadian banks, cannot issue or receive documentary letters of credit, and cannot operate shipbuilding escrow accounts or maritime installment guarantees. A compliant structure is for the EMI to settle through API integration with a Tier-1 Canadian bank, but the shipyard's capital-injection, installment LC, and large trade-disbursement rails must remain at a regulated domestic bank. Feasibility rating: Medium for subcontractor payroll, refunds, and visa/expense reimbursements; Low for escrow, LC, and high-value term settlements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of reportable transactions (T106/Schedule 88) with each non-resident related party; contemporaneous Local File / Master File documentation recommended when goods transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 or services / interest / royalties / intangibles exceed CAD 500,000 per non-resident affiliate.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) denies tax benefits from transactions lacking genuine economic substance, and treaty limitation-on-benefits clauses require demonstrable decision-making, personnel, risk retention, and premises within the Alberta shipyard.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1) and Criminal Code s. 380: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understatement under ITA s. 163(2); compounding interest on unpaid balances at CRA prescribed rates.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source withholdings, GST/HST, and related penalties under ITA s. 227.1; criminal conviction may result in travel bans, asset seizure, director disqualification under the CBCA, and extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA re-determination under ITA s. 247(2) increases taxable income; penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment; interest on recomputed tax; loss of reliance on valuation ranges unless documentation is contemporaneous and demonstrates best-method use.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or approve materially false TP documentation expose the corporation to penalties and may be personally assessed for unremitted remittances; professional disciplinary exposure for signing certificates of compliance.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA s. 245 annuls the tax benefit and re-characterizes income; Part XIII deemed-dividend withholding of 25% imposed on indirect distributions; treaty benefits denied under limitation-on-benefits clauses; extended reassessment periods apply for misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil liability under tax-circumvention doctrines; knowingly executing false residency certificates constitutes forgery and can lead to imprisonment, permanent travel restrictions, and disqualification from corporate office.
AML / Proceeds of Crime Violation

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per first violation and CAD 2,000,000 for repeat violations; Criminal Code money-laundering charges (s. 462.31) carrying up to 10 years imprisonment; seizure and forfeiture of vessel, escort account, and shipyard assets; licence revocation under the Canada Shipping Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers are personally assessable for FINTRAC penalties; criminal conviction triggers asset-freezing orders, travel restrictions, and imprisonment; directors can be disqualified from public company board service.
