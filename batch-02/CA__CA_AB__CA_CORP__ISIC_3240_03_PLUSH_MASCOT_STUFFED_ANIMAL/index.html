Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first complete any required federal or provincial corporate registration (e.g., extra-provincial registration in Alberta or federal incorporation via Corporations Canada), then apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or through the Business Registration Online service. The BN serves as the statutory TIN; separate GST/HST, payroll, and corporate income tax program accounts are established during the same e-registration process. Non-resident applicants without a Canadian presence may use the CRA's non-resident registration channel and may be required to designate a representative or provide an address in Canada for service of documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; a business corporation incorporated in Canada is automatically subject to entity-level corporate income tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act, whether organized federally or under the Alberta Business Corporations Act. Relevant elections and anti-hybrid rules include the section 18.4, 18.5, and 18.6 hybrid mismatch elections (OECD BEPS Action 2), the subsection 256(2) deemed acquisition-of-control election, the section 216(1) non-resident rental election, and the CCPC status elections available on CRA prescribed forms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold (measured on taxable supplies made worldwide in any single calendar quarter and in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters); a non-resident supplier carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada may be required to register regardless of the threshold under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate exports of tangible personal property where the goods are supplied in Canada and delivered by the supplier to a common carrier, customs broker, or the recipient for export, and the goods are exported from Canada as part of that supply. The registrant must retain documentary evidence of export within the prescribed period (generally 60 days for the zero-rating to be claimed on that return). Services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated when performed wholly outside Canada, or when supplied directly in furtherance of a non-resident's commercial activity, subject to the exclusions in Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) accepted and processed by the CBSA
  • Bill of Lading issued by the common carrier or freight forwarder evidencing shipment from Canada to the foreign customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Settlement Statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds in CAD

Canada does not impose a federal or Alberta real-time e-invoicing clearance mandate, API requirement, or digital stamping regime. GST/HST registrants may issue paper, PDF, or EDI invoices. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million, and all registrants may file via CRA NETFILE or API-enabled digital services. No pre-clearance of invoices is required by fiscal authorities.

CRA may reassess to recover uncollected GST/HST plus interest and applicable penalties on a retrospective basis if export documentation is inadequate or the supply is found not to be zero-rated. Penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act for false statements or omissions generally equal the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the net tax assessed; section 285 carries additional penalties for wilful evasion or fraud, and criminal prosecution may follow. In cases of fraudulent export zero-rating, the CRA may also invoke joint liability of the exporter and purchaser, impose a 6-year reassessment period for misrepresentation, and issue fines for failure to retain records for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains free convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Canadian dollar has a floating exchange-rate regime and there is no general restriction on conversion, no registration of FX contracts, and no central-bank prior approval for inbound investment, dividend declarations, interest payments, royalty payments, loan repayments, or repatriation of equity sale proceeds. For the Alberta business corporation engaged in ISIC 3240 plush-toy OEM, outward profit remittance to a non-resident shareholder is legally unrestricted but subject to Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory 25% or a lower applicable Canada-U.S./Canada-U.K./other treaty rate, requiring a properly executed Form NR301/NR302 waiver evidence package, Form NR4 filings, and CRA payment. Additional constraints include Canada Revenue Agency transfer-pricing documentation under section 247, thin-capitalization limits under section 18(4)-(6), and the requirement that material cross-border commercial transactions satisfy the arm's-length standard. The Investment Canada Act notification thresholds do not apply to a passive export-oriented service entity unless acquiring control of a Canadian business above the monetary threshold; nevertheless, a share-subscription wiring memorandum should be maintained to evidence foreign direct investment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no Canadian statutory surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no timeline for repatriation of export proceeds. A Canadian exporter may retain foreign currency in a domestic foreign-currency bank account or in an offshore account, may convert at negotiated commercial rates, and may use those proceeds directly for offshore supplier payments. For corporate tax and GST/HST reporting, all export revenues and purchases must be translated into Canadian dollars using the CRA published annual average exchange rate for cash-basis or accrual-basis entries, or the spot rate on the transaction date where the CRA method permits. No Form T2125 reporting category is required for export proceeds merely because they are earned in USD, but the transaction must be reflected in the accounts with traceable USD CAD conversion records, supplier invoices, and bank statements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential banking regulation is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing supervision is exercised by FINTRAC. Canada does not operate a dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority. The Minister of Finance has broad residual powers under the Currency Act and the Bank Act, but current policy imposes no centralized exchange-control license.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary payment collection, Low as the primary treasury account. Canada does not recognize the European EMI licence. An offshore digital EMI that offers multi-currency IBANs may lawfully service a Canadian corporate client as a money-services business only if it is registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, maintains a Canadian agent or authorized representative, and executes fund flows through a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank or an approved clearing agent. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act, foreign payment service providers that acquire Canadian end-users are expected to register with the Bank of Canada once the retail payments framework is fully in force. For a CA_AB plush-mask mascot manufacturer, a foreign EMI should be used only for low-value platform collections, not for capital contributions, trade settlements over CAD 10,000, or payroll disbursements, because the local bank will not extend commercial credit or trade finance against an EMI balance. All funds held at an EMI remain outside Canadian deposit insurance and are not covered by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant inbound equity-injection protocol is as follows: (1) the parent shareholder executes a board resolution and share subscription agreement in the exact legal name of the Alberta corporation; (2) the bank account at the source bank must be in the same legal name as the shareholder; (3) the remittance wire must be sent using SWIFT MT103 with beneficiary name matching the Alberta corporate registry extract, beneficiary account held at a Canadian Schedule I bank, and the following purpose-code/text unambiguously stated in Field 70 or 72: 'Share capital contribution / foreign direct investment / ISIC 3240 plush-toy OEM operations'; (4) the funds must be wired directly to the designated Capital Escrow Account, never combined with intercompany loan amounts or trade receivables; (5) the bank's KYC file must contain the source-of-funds evidence, a certificate of the corporate secretary confirming issuance of shares, and confirmation that the shareholder is not a resident of an OFAC/EU-embargoed jurisdiction; (6) once the wire clears and the bank's foreign-exchange conversion is booked, the company's accounting system must record capital at the exact CAD book rate, and any premium/discount on conversion recorded in equity or comprehensive income. For values exceeding C$10,000, the bank will submit its mandatory FINTRAC Report; therefore the corporate file should include a copy of the MT103 and a document trace showing the complete chain from parent board minute to share register entry to bank ledger entry.For outbound trade settlements, the following clearing rails are permitted and recommended: (a) SWIFT MT103 for open-account supplier payments, using beneficiary banks acceptable to the Canadian bank and full Incoterms references; (b) commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600 through SWIFT MT700/MT707, with deferred payment or sight-draft tenors, for large purchases of polyester fiber and embroidery materials; (c) SWIFT MT202/COV for cross-border bank-to-bank cover payments where the Canadian bank's corresponding relationship requires; (d) Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for high-value Canadian-dollar settlements, and EFT/automated clearing for routine payroll and domestic supplier invoices; (e) SWIFT gpi tracking for end-to-end status confirmation; and (f) approved platform settlement (PayPal, Stripe) for low-value direct-to-consumer collections, with daily sweeps to the main CAD account. The major Canadian banks maintain USD correspondent relationships through their U.S. branches; for RMB payments to Chinese contract manufacturers, a USD-denominated MT103 with the final Chinese beneficiary receiving USD through a correspondent bank is often simpler than a direct RMB clearing, unless the Canadian bank has an approved RMB clearing arrangement.
