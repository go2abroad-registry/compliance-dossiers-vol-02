Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both the current and capital account. There are no substantive exchange controls or administrative approvals required for profit remittance, dividend distributions, loan repayments, or repatriation of invested capital. Non-resident investors remain subject to Part XIII withholding tax on dividends, interest, and certain royalties, and to the Investment Canada Act notification or review thresholds for large acquisitions. Daily settlement of CAD transactions flows through Payments Canada's Lynx system, and cross-border payments are unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender, repatriate, or convert export proceeds into CAD. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts, and there is no mandatory conversion timeline. FINTRAC reporting obligations apply only where a cross-border transaction or wire transfer exceeds CAD 10,000, or where cash transactions exceed CAD 10,000 for a single or linked sequence.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) serves as the central monetary authority; exchange control and prudential supervision are administered by the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada operates a liberalized, market-based foreign exchange framework under the Currency Act and the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for ancillary payment collection and disbursement, but low suitability as the primary operating account. Legal constraints: any electronic money institution (EMI) or payment service provider serving Canadian residents must be registered as a money services business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and with FINTRAC. Offshore digital EMIs without a Canadian MSB registration are not permitted to maintain a settlement account at a Canadian bank or clear through Payments Canada. Unregistered processors expose the corporation to potential AML non-compliance, account freezes, and restriction on CAD clearing access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound investment funds from a parent entity or investor are routed over SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the corporate operational account at a Canadian Tier-1 bank. The remitting instruction must include the ISO 20022 settlement purpose code 'INV' or 'S0301' for direct investment, or 'S0101' for intercompany loans. Canadian financial institutions will report cross-border funds transfers above CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC. No exchange control approval is required. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined (15% federal general corporate rate + 8% Alberta general corporate rate); 11% for Canadian-controlled private corporations on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (9% federal + 2% Alberta).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents; Alberta imposes no separate dividend withholding tax because provincial tax is administered federally under the collection agreement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the corporation’s fiscal year-end (e.g., a December 31 year-end requires filing by June 30). Payment of the balance of tax is due 2 months after year-end for CCPCs claiming the small business deduction, and 3 months after year-end for all other corporations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border transactions with non-resident related parties exceeding CAD 1 million in aggregate gross value require contemporaneous Local File documentation. Master File and Country-by-Country Report obligations apply to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of approximately CAD 1 billion (≈ EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply separate Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, Canada relies on the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act section 245), the central-management-and-control residency test, and anti-treaty-abuse provisions in DTAs to deny tax benefits to shell companies lacking genuine commercial activity. The CRA may disregard an intermediary without substance if it is used to shift profits or avoid a permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under Income Tax Act subsection 163(2) of 50% of the tax understated plus interest; criminal prosecution under subsection 239(1) carries a fine of 50%-200% of the evaded tax (summary conviction) or 100%-200% (indictment) plus imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or acquiesce to evasion may lose limited liability protection, be jointly assessed for source deduction and GST/HST liabilities, and face criminal conviction, restitution orders, travel restrictions, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR (ITA s.245) to deny all tax benefits, reassess income upward, levy a 50% gross-negligence penalty, and add compound interest. If the structure is paper-only for treaty relief, CRA may refuse treaty rates and impose statutory 25% Part XIII withholding on deemed distributions.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who sign false declarations may be prosecuted for misrepresentation; directors may be personally liable for unpaid wages and source deductions when an empty shell is operated to avoid those liabilities.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable income under ITA s.247(2), assess interest, and impose a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment when the taxpayer fails to prepare or provide contemporaneous documentation by the filing due date.

Director personal liability: Directors must certify the corporate return; knowingly signing false transfer-pricing documents can trigger personal civil penalties and criminal liability for aiding tax evasion, with CRA enforcement through mutual agreement procedures.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Under PCMLTFA, failure to report or false reporting carries fines up to CAD 500,000 for a first offence and up to CAD 1 million for a repeat offence, with up to 5 years imprisonment for knowingly making false statements. Export and Import Permits Act violations carry imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for corporate penalties if they acquiesce; separate Criminal Code money-laundering charges can result in imprisonment, asset freezes, and a prohibition against acting as a director or officer.
