Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the ABCA; shares may be issued for cash or other consideration determined by the directors, and no paid-up capital escrow or bank lock-up account is required for an Alberta provincial business corporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification obtained under the Alberta Fire Code and the Safety Codes Act after inspection of the molding floor, storage and egress; structural load inspection certifying the floor capacity for injection molding presses; electrical installation clearance from an accredited safety codes agency; and an Occupational Health and Safety clearance under the Alberta OHS Act, Alberta Occupational Health and Safety Code, covering machine guarding, noise reduction, lockout/tagout and workplace hazard controls.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the ABCA; shares may be issued for cash or other consideration determined by the directors, and no paid-up capital escrow or bank lock-up account is required for an Alberta provincial business corporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Canada's federal tax administration for GST/HST and corporate income tax; Alberta provincial corporate income tax is also administered by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; official portals include CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online; export administration is shared with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta business corporation must first incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act or the Canada Business Corporations Act, satisfy registered-office and resident-director requirements, and obtain its Alberta corporate registry file. The corporation then applies for a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) and the required program accounts for GST/HST, payroll, and import/export through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1. A separate GST/HST registration account uses the BN followed by an 'RT' account code. Non-resident corporations without a Canadian permanent establishment may also apply for a non-resident GST/HST account under the Excise Tax Act, using Form RC1 and supporting foreign-entity documents, if they carry on business in Canada or voluntarily register to claim input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide United States-style check-the-box elections or a separate hybrid-mismatch entity election. A legal corporation is always treated as a separate taxpayer subject to entity-level corporate income tax and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Relevant statutory elections in this context include: Income Tax Act section 85 rollover elections for tax-deferred transfers of property into the corporation, GST/HST related-party elections under ETA section 156 for closely related corporations, and certain capital cost allowance pooling elections. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are constrained by Canadian residency rules, the general anti-avoidance rule under Income Tax Act section 245, and transfer-pricing rules; no elective Canadian domestic hybrid-mismatch check-the-box regime exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the GST/HST small-supplier threshold measured by global taxable supplies, including those of associated persons, in the four consecutive calendar quarters ending before registration; exporters exceeding this threshold while carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered Alberta exporter may zero-rate custom mechanical keyboard keycaps under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, as tangible personal property exported in a continuous journey to a destination outside Canada, or delivered to a common carrier for export. The supply must be made to a non-resident buyer and physically leave Canada. Supporting services supplied to the non-resident buyer in relation to the exported goods may also be zero-rated if they are not performed in Canada or are not in connection with Canadian real property or Canadian tangible personal property. The exporter claims input tax credits for GST paid on manufacturing inputs on its regular GST/HST return; no separate export refund application is required. Although Canada imposes no explicit statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, 180 days after shipment is the standard commercial and audit benchmark for receiving export proceeds; extended non-payment or related-party delayed settlement materially increases CRA scrutiny of whether a genuine export sale occurred.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A or Canadian Automated Export Declaration data) where the shipment value exceeds CAD 2,000 or the goods are controlled, regulated, or require a permit
  • Commercial invoice in English or French describing the keycaps, quantity, export classification, country of origin, seller, buyer, and consideration
  • Packing list showing exact shipment contents, weights, and destination
  • Bill of lading or airway waybill evidencing physical carriage from Canada to the foreign consignee
  • Inward remittance evidence from the foreign buyer, such as a SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or letter of credit settlement record
  • Customs broker or carrier electronic release/export confirmation confirming actual exit of the goods from Canada

Canada has no real-time tax authority invoice clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory Peppol-style e-invoicing regime as of the current tax period. CRA accepts electronic invoices and books and records where they are retained in readable format. GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed tax-invoice information and may file returns electronically through CRA's GST/HST Internet File Transfer service. There is no API-based per-invoice fiscal approval obligation; however, for export transactions the registrant must retain shipment and payment documentation sufficient to substantiate the zero-rated supply on audit.

CRA may reassess a GST/HST return within four years from the date originally filed, and the limitation period is unlimited where the registrant has made a misrepresentation or committed fraud or wilful neglect. If zero-rating is disallowed for lack of export evidence, the 5% GST is clawed back from the Canadian exporter as uncollected net tax, plus prescribed daily compound interest. False or grossly negligent statements can trigger penalties under ETA section 280, generally the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net-tax shortfall, with additional penalties under the Customs Act for inaccurate export declarations. Knowingly evading GST/HST or facilitating a false export claim is a criminal offence under ETA section 327 and may result in substantial fines and imprisonment, in addition to full recovery of tax, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The Canadian dollar is a freely floating and widely traded currency. There are no exchange control, surrender, or approval requirements for capital inflows, current payments, or profit remittances by non-residents. The only legal constraints arise from U.N./automated sanctions screening, FINTRAC large-transaction reporting (C$10,000), and Canada Revenue Agency rules on the functional currency of foreign-denominated earnings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rule or mandatory repatriation timeline exists for Canadian exporters. Export receipts may remain in foreign currency, though they must be valued in Canadian dollars for income tax and GST/HST purposes. FINTRAC reportable transactions apply at C$10,000 per payment. Payments from sanctioned jurisdictions or involving dual-use goods are restricted under the Special Economic Measures Act and Export and Import Permits Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority, with foreign exchange policy administered under the Currency Act; FINTRAC serves as the financial intelligence and AML/CTF supervisor for authorized foreign exchange and money services businesses.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-high operational viability as a supplementary settlement corridor, but low legal viability as a full replacement for a Schedule I Canadian chartered bank. Offshore EMI accounts (e.g., E.U./U.K.-based EMI licenses) are not recognized as Canadian bank accounts; they may be used only for payment collection in foreign platform ecosystems, with fund flows passed into a regulated Canadian account. Stripe and Shopify Payments are the preferred fintech rails, subject to their own KYC and merchant underwriting, but a domestic bank account and FINTRAC registration are still necessary for treasury control and large-volume settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from foreign shareholders are routed as SWIFT MT103 wires into a Canadian Chartered Bank account, using the exact legal corporate name and the CC (Canadian clearing code) along with the Bank/Institution, Branch, and Account number. Remittance instructions should reference the share subscription agreement or shareholder subordinated loan; the note field must identify the transaction as 'Capital Injection' or 'Equity Contribution'. Wires equal to or above C$10,000 trigger FINTRAC large-transaction reporting. For larger foreign investments, a post-closing Investment Canada Act notification may be required; capital injection into a wholly-owned Alberta operating company is generally exempt from pre-merger review.Productive cross-border disbursements for tooling moulds, double-shot PBT resin pellets, keycap stems, and specialized fixtures are cleared through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers to supplier accounts, with CAD-to-USD execution provided at the bank’s competitive rate. For high-value custom molds, a commercial documentary letter of credit under UCP600 with an advising bank in the toolmaker’s country is recommended.
