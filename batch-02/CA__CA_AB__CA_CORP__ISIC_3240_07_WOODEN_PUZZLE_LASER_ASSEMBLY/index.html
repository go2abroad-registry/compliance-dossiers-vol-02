Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST harmonized federal and Alberta corporate income tax administration via the My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html); Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration handles only provincial fuel/tobacco/tourism levies, not corporate income tax, which is collected by CRA under the Tax Collection Agreement with Alberta.

E-government filing portal

A foreign or newly incorporated entity obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 or using the Business Registration Online (BRO) service. The GST/HST program account suffix 'RT' is registered upon incorporation or voluntarily once taxable supplies in Canada (excluding zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 per any four consecutive calendar quarters. Non-resident registrants must also file Form RC4592 or provide an authorized representative authorization (Form RC59) if engaging an agent; extra-provincial Alberta registration (under the Business Corporations Act) is a prerequisite for carrying on business in Alberta. CRA registration is instantaneous for incorporated entities and the GST account can be back-dated to the date supply activity commenced.

For GST/HST purposes, the corporation is treated as a separate registrant; no fiscal unity or consolidated GST filing is allowed unless an election under ETA subsection 156(2) or section 191 is made. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A federally or provincially incorporated Business Corporation is a distinct person and resident taxpayer under the Income Tax Act; it is taxed at entity level on worldwide income (no flow-through or partnership classification for Canadian corporations). For GST/HST purposes, the corporation is treated as a separate registrant; no fiscal unity or consolidated GST filing is allowed unless an election under ETA subsection 156(2) or section 191 is made. Alberta has no special tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Canadian income tax law; however, the following indirect tax elections are available: (i) ETA subsection 156(2) election for shared corporate costs, (ii) ETA section 191 election treating supplies among closely related corporations as nil, (iii) Quick Method election for small suppliers (lower remittance), (iv) annual vs. monthly remittance elections, and (v) ETA section 211 election for deemed supplies. Hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.2 and 18.3 apply mandatorily with no elective override; CRA respects US check-the-box disregarded entities only to the extent they are fiscally transparent under the Canada-US treaty tie-breaker rules, requiring a separate legal and documentary analysis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — measured as total worldwide taxable supplies made in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters, excluding zero-rated exports and financial services; a non-resident supplying advertising services to Canadian recipients has a nil threshold and must register immediately once any such supply is made. Voluntary registration is permitted for entities making zero-rated exports (such as the subject wooden puzzle exports) to claim input tax credits on Canadian inputs, with a minimum registration period of one year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies when tangible personal property (including wooden puzzles) is exported and title passes to a non-resident buyer, under ETA Schedule VI Part V sections 1 to 7, provided the goods leave Canada within a reasonable time (CRA guidance commonly accepts 60 days from invoice, but flexible on documentary proof). The supplier invoices at 0% GST and may claim full Input Tax Credits for GST embedded in inputs (wood, laser consumables, freight). Services directly in respect of goods shipped outside Canada (e.g., design or assembly services provided to non-residents) may also be zero-rated under section 7 where the service is performed for a non-resident and relates to goods situated outside Canada at the time the service is performed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A via the Canadian Export Reporting System/CERS) or ACI eManifest confirmation referencing the export transaction and date of departure
  • Carrier-issued Bill of Lading or Air Waybill plus a commercial invoice bearing the supplier's GST/HST number and explicit 'zero-rated export' notation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement record (SWIFT MT103) evidencing payment in foreign currency from the non-resident buyer to substantiate the export sale proceeds

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping in Canada. CRA requires every GST invoice to include prescribed data (supplier name and GST/HST number, invoice date, taxable amount, rate of tax, and description of goods, per ETA Schedule V Part IV). Electronic records must be retained in a readable, extractable XML/CSV format for 6 years and produced to CRA upon audit; participation in PEPPOL is voluntary. Export transactions should be supported by a recognized digital Customs export filing (CERS or ACI eManifest) to facilitate e-commerce fulfillment with Amazon Pay and Stripe.

CRA may reassess GST amounts within 4 years of the filing due date (ETA section 298); no limitation period applies in cases of fraud or wilful default. If export zero-rating is denied due to insufficient documentation, the full 5% GST becomes collectible retroactively on the original invoice date with daily compound arrears interest (CRA quarterly prescribed rate, typically 8% to 10% in 2025). Failure to collect/remit attracts a 6% automatic penalty (ETA section 280), plus an additional 6% penalty for a second occurrence; knowingly making a false statement or omission subjects the registrant to a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded (ETA subsection 285(4)). Wilful evasion is a criminal offence under ETA section 327, punishable by fine up to CAD 25,000 and/or 12 months' imprisonment, and the Crown may seek forfeiture; CRA also performs input-output matching audits on laser-cut precision export sectors, reviewing CERS declarations against invoice timing and the 180-day FX settlement benchmark.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current and capital account. There is no exchange-control legislation, no central bank approval requirement for capital inflows or outflows, and no restriction on trade-related remittances or profit repatriation. A Canadian-incorporated exporter may receive equity injections, shareholder loans, dividends, and trade receivables in any convertible currency. Profit remittance barriers are limited to tax rules: non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax on eligible dividends at the statutory rate of 25 percent, generally reduced under an applicable income tax treaty, and a non-resident-owned Canadian corporation may be subject to branch tax under section 219 of the Income Tax Act. The Investment Canada Act may require notification or net-benefit review where a non-Canadian acquires control of a Canadian business above prescribed thresholds, but ordinary capital contributions to an existing operating corporation do not require ministerial approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender rule, no minimum conversion ratio, and no statutory deadline for repatriation or conversion of export receipts. An Alberta exporter may lawfully retain foreign currency balances in resident or non-resident bank accounts and may convert to CAD at a commercially reasonable time. The only regulatory thresholds are FINTRAC reporting requirements for cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and CBSA reporting for physical currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more. Supporting trade documents such as invoices, bills of lading, packing lists, and customs declarations should be retained for at least six years for Canada Revenue Agency verification, but no surrender certificate or central bank clearance is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; prudential supervision of chartered banks and federally regulated financial institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Cross-border transaction monitoring is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and Payments Canada operates the domestic clearing and settlement infrastructure. The Department of Finance Canada is responsible for exchange rate policy and for the administration of the Investment Canada Act review process.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for auxiliary collection and settlement routing, Low for primary capital escrow and treasury concentration. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut Business may be used as thin settlement conduits for Amazon and Stripe proceeds, but they are not Schedule I Canadian banks and cannot offer deposit accounts with Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation coverage. An offshore EMI that solicits retail payment services in Canada generally must register as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada and must comply with FINTRAC AML/ATF obligations. Funds held through an unlicensed offshore EMI are exposed to sub-custody, pooling, and counterparty risk, and Tier-1 Canadian banks may apply enhanced due diligence to incoming wires originating from such EMI accounts. The recommended architecture is to use EMIs only for low-value disbursement and daily sweeping of balances into a Tier-1 Canadian operational account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a permitted shareholder loan or paid-in capital contribution, route the payment through SWIFT MT103 using the beneficiary CAD account at a Canadian Tier-1 bank. Field 50K must identify the exact remitter, Field 59 must show the Canadian beneficiary name and account number, and Field 70 should contain a clear purpose-of-payment narration such as 'Shareholder capital contribution', 'Paid-in capital', or 'Intercompany working capital loan' together with a reference to the corporate authorization and subscription agreement. Canada does not maintain a standard statutory purpose-code schedule, but banks will map the narration to their internal economic purpose classification and may request supporting documentation including the certificate of incorporation, board resolution, loan note, UBO confirmation, source-of-funds evidence, and confirmation of compliance with the Investment Canada Act. FINTRAC reporting applies to cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more, so the corporation should maintain a full trail of the originating bank, intermediary bank, SWIFT references, and foreign-exchange conversion records. The receipt of capital is credited to equity or shareholder loan records and is not subject to central bank approval.Outbound supplier payments for wood, laser consumables, packaging, and freight should be settled through SWIFT MT103 for open-account transactions, with the invoice number and supplier reference in the remittance field. For high-value or first-time procurement from overseas suppliers, use a commercial documentary letter of credit issued by the Canadian bank via SWIFT MT700; amendments are transmitted via MT707, and beneficiary negotiation is settled through SWIFT MT202/MT202COV to the presenting bank. CAD domestic disbursements may be executed through Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system for large values or the Automated Clearing Settlement System for batched lower-value items. For USD payables, the Canadian bank will clear through its US correspondent, Fedwire, or CHIPS network, or through its own US branch. The company may also use commercial documentary collections with bills of exchange for lower-risk trade relationships. Turnover: Under ABCA s. 151, only a distributing corporation (publicly traded securities) must appoint an auditor. A non-distributing private corporation is fully exempt if all shareholders, including non-voting shareholders, consent in writing to waive the auditor appointment. Alberta imposes no gross-revenue or asset threshold for audit exemption. For tax purposes, the CRA requires complete financial statements (balance sheet, income statement, retained earnings) with the T2 only for corporations with gross revenues exceeding CAD 1,000,000 or taxable income exceeding CAD 500,000, but this does not by itself compel an independent audit; a lender, investor, or board policy may nonetheless require an audit or review engagement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined federal/provincial general corporate rate (15% federal Part I + 8% Alberta general rate); 11% combined effective rate for active business income eligible for the federal small business deduction (9% federal + 2% Alberta small business rate on first C$500,000 of eligible income, subject to proration and associations)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, subject to reduction under an applicable tax treaty (commonly 5% for a shareholder holding at least 10% of voting shares, and 15% in other portfolio cases under the Canada–US and Canada–China treaties)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$500,000 – mandatory filing of Form T106 for reportable cross-border transactions with non-resident related persons when the aggregate fair market value exceeds this amount in a taxation year; Country-by-Country Report is required for MNE groups with consolidated annual revenue of C$750 million or more; Canada does not prescribe a separate statutory threshold for Local File/Master File but imposes contemporaneous documentation obligations under the CRA administrative policy to avoid transfer-pricing penalties

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime; however, the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245) and specific anti-avoidance provisions require that transactions be entered into for a genuine business purpose and not solely to obtain a tax benefit. A Canadian resident shell company that lacks substantive functions, assets, and risks relative to its claimed intra-group transactions may be disregarded, with profit reattribution based on the actual economic activities performed and risks borne (consistent with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and the 2019 revised treatment of hard-to-value intangibles)

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under section 239 of the Income Tax Act and sections 327/380 of the Criminal Code: fine of the higher of 50% of the evaded tax or C$5,000 plus imprisonment up to five years; civil reassessment with gross-negligence penalty of 50% under subsection 163(2) and compound interest at prescribed rates

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate in evasion are personally liable as parties to the offence; civil personal liability for unpaid tax and penalties may be imposed under subsections 227.1 and 160(1) for avoidance of tax; criminal courts may issue travel bans, prohibitions on management, restitution orders, and custodial sentences
Shell Companies / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of payments and recharacterization under GAAR (section 245); denial of treaty benefits; transfer-pricing adjustment with 10% penalty of the net adjustment under subsection 247(3) if the adjustment exceeds C$50,000 and reasonable documentation is absent; retroactive reassessments and interest

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unremitted source deductions, GST/HST, and Part XIII withholding under subsection 232.1 (the Act references section 227.1 for 100% liability); where the shell arrangement is used to commit fraud, directors risk criminal liability for conspiracy to evade tax and may face travel restrictions pending prosecution
AML / Trade-Sanctions Violation (Proliferation Financing)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative monetary penalties up to C$1 million per violation (per minor violation C$50,000, per major violation C$500,000, and C$1 million for noncompliance with compliance program requirements); under the United Nations Act and Special Economic Measures Act, fines and up to ten years' imprisonment for unauthorized dealings with sanctioned entities

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly fail to implement an effective COMPLIANCE program or who enable trade-based money laundering can be personally prosecuted under the Criminal Code for laundering proceeds of crime (maximum 10 years' imprisonment); directors' assets can be frozen under restraint orders; personal liability assessments can be issued under section 462.44 for offense-related property
Non-Resident Dividend and Royalty Stripping

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the payment as a deemed dividend under section 84; liability for unpaid Part XIII tax equal to 25% of the gross distribution; interest from the date of withholding and penalties for failure to withhold (a taxpayer is liable for the amount that was not withheld); CRA may issue a garnishment or security demand against property held by Canadian payers

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholdings and GST/HST if the amounts were not deducted and remitted (subsection 227.1); directors who knowingly cause a corporation not to withhold can be assessed personally and may be subject to director's liability travel bans under tax enforcement measures; in aggravated cases, personal criminal liability for aiding abetting tax evasion carries up to five years' imprisonment
