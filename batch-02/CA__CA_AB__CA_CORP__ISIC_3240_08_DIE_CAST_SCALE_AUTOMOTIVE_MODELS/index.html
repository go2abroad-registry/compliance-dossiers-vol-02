Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Conditionally permitted. Under ABCA s 101, the registered office must be a physical street address in Alberta where process may be served; post-office boxes and pure non-physical virtual addresses are prohibited. A serviced physical office or a registered-agent address satisfies the statutory seat and mail-forwarding function for a non-licensed die-cast model importer/manufacturer (ISIC 3240 / NAICS 339930), but the entity must be able to receive notices and produce records at that address; a separate production or warehousing address in Alberta is commercially advisable to demonstrate genuine economic substance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online portal; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA on behalf of Alberta through the T2 corporate return process.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA or through the Alberta Corporate Registry when incorporating. Non-resident corporations may file Form RC1S (Non-Resident Corporation Account Registration) and are assigned a GST/HST account where the legally required threshold is exceeded; a Canadian representative for service of notices must be named.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA or through the Alberta Corporate Registry when incorporating. Non-resident corporations may file Form RC1S (Non-Resident Corporation Account Registration) and are assigned a GST/HST account where the legally required threshold is exceeded; a Canadian representative for service of notices must be named.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. Business corporations are automatically taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed by ss. 18.2 through 18.4 of the Income Tax Act; no statutory election can re-classify the corporate entity. GST/HST registrants within a corporate group may elect to file consolidated returns under s. 238.1 of the Excise Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any period of four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada may be required to register regardless of the threshold if carrying on business in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods (such as die-cast scale automotive replicas) is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 20 of the Excise Tax Act if the supplier is a GST/HST registrant, title transfers to a non-resident recipient, and the goods are delivered to a destination outside Canada. Shipping terms (e.g., FOB, EXW) must substantiate that ownership and risk pass outside Canada; ancillary services performed on the goods may also be zero-rated where statutory tests are met.

  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Canada customs export declaration or proof that an export declaration was not required (CBSA export reporting confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing departure from Canada and consignee outside Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment confirmation showing receipt of foreign currency from the foreign importer

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. CRA accepts electronic invoices provided they are legible and retained; GST/HST returns are required to be filed electronically for most registrants; no API-based clearance or digital stamping is enforced.

CRA may reassess export zero-rating within the normal assessment period (four years or seven years where zero-rating was reported), deny the benefit, and collect unpaid GST/HST with interest. False or misleading statements attract penalties of the greater of CAD 100 or 25% of the amount sought to be evaded under s. 280 of the Excise Tax Act, plus civil penalties and criminal prosecution under s. 327 carrying a maximum penalty of five years imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. There are no exchange-control requirements or administrative approval hurdles for profit repatriation or capital injections. Dividends and management fees may be remitted without central-bank consent; the principal technical constraint is Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (e.g., 25% with applicable treaty reduction), plus filing obligations for foreign-owned taxpayers under section 216 and prescribed reporting forms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement to repatriate or surrender export proceeds. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency accounts in Canada or abroad. Mandatory conversion ratios: none. Reporting obligations flow from Statistics Canada's Balance of Payments survey forms and FINTRAC's electronic funds transfer reports for international remittances exceeding CAD 10,000 in a 24-hour period; no exchange-control permission or conversion timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision by Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). No dedicated foreign-exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal caveats. Foreign fintechs and electronic-money institutions ('EMIs') are not authorized deposit-taking institutions in Canada under the Bank Act and cannot accept call or demand deposits without a federal banking license. They may operate as money-service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, and can provide cross-border payment facilitation, sub-account structures, or payment gateways in partnership with a chartered bank; however, such channels are not acceptable as the main treasury clearing rail for capital preservation or segregated escrow under Alberta corporate law. Use of unlicensed offshore EMIs is not viable for compliance with OSFI expectations and can generate enhanced scrutiny.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident beneficial ownership with opaque multi-tier holding structure

Root cause: Non-resident beneficial ownership with opaque multi-tier holding structure

Defense evidence: Certified corporate charters in each jurisdiction; an updated register of directors and shareholders; an organizational chart showing every intermediate legal entity with ownership percentages; a sworn ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) declaration; source-of-wealth evidence (banked tax returns, sale contracts, audited financial statements); and a board resolution authorizing the Alberta subsidiary account.
Unexpected or implausible source of initial export-trade proceeds or large cash-like funding

Root cause: Unexpected or implausible source of initial export-trade proceeds or large cash-like funding

Defense evidence: Copies of signed purchase orders, bills of lading, customs entry documents (for the die-cast scale replicas), proforma commercial invoices, container freight manifests, and a logical explanation of the manufacturing cost-per-unit and wholesale margin; lender name and facility agreement if the injected capital is a shareholder loan; proof of payment flow from an existing corporate bank account.
Jurisdiction risk or lack of local business substance

Root cause: Jurisdiction risk or lack of local business substance

Defense evidence: Alberta corporate registry certificate, registered office and records address, Canadian Business Number, real lease agreement for Alberta manufacturing or administrative office, payroll evidence of Alberta resident employees, a substantive business plan for zinc alloy die-casting and tampo printing under ISIC 3240, and a tax compliance statement confirming no shell-indicia.
Tight timing for emergency disbursement to a counterparty in a watchlisted destination

Root cause: Tight timing for emergency disbursement to a counterparty in a watchlisted destination

Defense evidence: KYC package completed at onboarding; due-diligence screening list for restricted parties; a written purpose statement for the disbursement (e.g., purchase of artwork? in this case, raw zinc alloys or printing consumables); underlying contract and proforma invoice identifying the ultimate end-user; certification that no goods are military-grade or strategic-controlled; readiness for enhanced transaction monitoring to be applied.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses a Canadian chartered bank receiving an incoming SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank, with the field 50K shareholder name, field 59 Canadian payee account, field 70 reference as 'Equity Contribution / Shareholder Loan — Alberta Corp.' The Canada Revenue Agency and FINTRAC do not impose exchange-control approval, but the bank will record the transaction under its Suspicious Transaction Report obligations if it exceeds CAD 10,000. The corporate taxpayer should file a prescribed Foreign Subsidiary Reporting or statement of share capital; for payments below CAD 25,000 no special 'purpose code' exists under the SWIFT system, but the Canadian bank's front-office onboarding form will capture the Statistics Canada classification code for direct investment (e.g., 'Equity injection, direct investor' under the Balance of Payments survey). For high-value inflows exceeding CAD 1 million, use Lynx (the Canadian high-value payment system) via the bank's treasury desk, and obtain a remittance confirmation with bank reference number.Primary clearing rails are (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct settlement with overseas suppliers and distributors; (2) commercial letters of credit (L/C) opened by the Alberta bank for the import of zinc alloy ingots and tampo-pad consumables; and (3) documentary collections for smaller cross-border transactions. Low-value customer pre-payments may use PayPal's commercial payments side or similar money-service business rails, but for amounts above CAD-line threshold the bank's standard wire channel is recommended. For CAD-dollar domestic clearing within Canada, use Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system; for USD-denominated trade settlements, the bank will route through a corresponding US bank using MT103 or internal sweep.
