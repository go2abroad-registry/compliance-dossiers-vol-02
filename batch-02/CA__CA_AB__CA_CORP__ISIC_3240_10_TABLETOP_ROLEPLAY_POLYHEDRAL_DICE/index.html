Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC) is the federal competent tax authority; operational portals are Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration, GST/HST NETFILE, and My Business Account (MyBA). Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA on behalf of Alberta under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act and the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement; Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax and has no separate sales-tax administration.

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1 - Incorporate the business corporation under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (preferred) or federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act with extra-provincial registration in Alberta; no minimum capital is required. Step 2 - Obtain the federal Business Number (BN), the statutory tax identifier, by applying online through BRO or by submitting Form RC1 to the CRA; simultaneously request the Corporate Income Tax (RD), GST/HST (RC0001), and Import/Export (RM) program accounts. Step 3 - For a foreign entity not incorporated in Canada, appoint a Canadian authorized representative, file Form RC59 (Authorizing or Cancelling a Representative), and submit Form RC1; the CRA will issue a BN and register the non-resident for GST/HST under ETA s. 240(3.1), subject to the same CAD 30,000 small-supplier analysis under ETA s. 148. Step 4 - Register the RM customs program account with the CRA/CBSA for export reporting capability through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS). Step 5 - Maintain the BN program accounts through MyBA, retain all CRA notices of assessment, and keep the GST/HST registration status current for the Kickstarter/Stripe/PayPal revenue streams. No separate Alberta corporate income tax registration is required because Alberta participates in the federal Tax Collection Agreement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident corporation taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income, plus Alberta corporate income tax of 8% general rate and 2% small-business rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income; no pass-through eligibility for a corporation; dividends paid to foreign shareholders are exposed to 25% Part XIII withholding unless reduced by treaty).

        
Available election forms
No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canadian law; corporate taxpayer status is fixed by incorporation and deemed residence under ITA s. 250(2)(a). Available statutory mechanisms: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration for non-residents under ETA s. 240(3.1); (ii) ITA s. 216(1) election permitting a non-resident to file a Part I return on Canadian rental/royalty income instead of Part XIII withholdings; (iii) Part XIV branch-tax planning under ITA s. 219 for a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada through a permanent establishment, with a 25% branch tax on after-tax Canadian business income subject to treaty relief and the s. 219(3) election; (iv) hybrid-mismatch transitional elections under ITA ss. 18.4-18.7 effective 1 July 2023, relevant where the corporate group uses foreign transparent or check-the-box structures; (v) no election exists to reclassify a corporation as a partnership or disregarded entity for Canadian indirect-tax purposes.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
5%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1): a person is not required to register for GST unless total taxable supplies made in Canada (plus worldwide taxable supplies for non-residents carrying on business in Canada, aggregated with associates as applicable, per CRA policy) exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters. Voluntary registration is available at any time through ETA s. 240(3.1); Kickstarter pre-sales to Canadian backers count toward the threshold, as do Canada-destination direct sales through Stripe or PayPal.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exports of the polyhedral resin tabletop dice qualify as zero-rated supplies under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 20 ETA where the vendor sells and physically exports the goods in a commercial shipment, commercial convoy, or commercial container immediately following the supply, or mails/couriers the goods to a destination outside Canada; the goods must leave Canada within 60 days of the supply per CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17-2, and the supplier must retain transport and customs evidence. Ancillary services (e.g., custom engraving or design services) supplied with export goods may be zero-rated under Sch VI s. 23 where the benefit is realized outside Canada or no part of the service is performed in Canada; royalties or licensing fees from foreign distributors remain subject to 25% Part XIII withholding unless treaty-reduced. Export-heavy operations claim input tax credits (ITCs) on GST paid for resin, molds, packaging, and freight through the GST/HST return, producing a net refund where creditable ITCs exceed output GST.

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government-API tax-clearance regime at the federal or Alberta level. Commercial invoices may be paper or electronic but must contain the prescribed ETA information, including the supplier legal name, BN, description of the goods, consideration, rate of GST charged, and zero-rated or exempt status notation. GST/HST returns must be filed through GST/HST NETFILE, and payments are remitted via MyBA or participating financial institutions. Canada participates in the voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing (PCEI) framework aligned with the Peppol BIS 3.0 four-corner model; the Quebec RPCI B2B e-invoicing mandate rolling from 2026 binds only Quebec-perspective suppliers and is a watch item for multi-province expansion. No pre-clearance approval or digital stamp is required before issuing invoices from Alberta.

      
ETA s. 296(1) allows CRA reassessment within four years of the return due date, extended to seven years in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud; if the export zero-rating is reversed, 5% GST is assessed on the full consideration plus compound prescribed-rate daily interest from the original filing date. ETA s. 285.1 imposes a false-statement penalty equal to the greater of CAD 500 and 25% of the understated net tax or overstated refund; evading-payment prosecutions under ETA s. 280 can yield fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment of up to five years on indictment. CRA verification programs data-mine payment-processor (Stripe/PayPal) and Kickstarter settlement records to test export substantiation; common audit triggers are missing B13A/CERS export filings, absent waybills, mismatched customer-residence data, and foreign proceeds collected more than 180 days after shipment without a commercial explanation. The CRA may also impose sanctions for failure to retain proper records; all export evidence, FX receipts, and GST/HST ledgers must be retained for at least six years under ETA record-keeping rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility; since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act (1951) there are no prior-approval, quota, or licence requirements for dividend remittance, profit repatriation, intercompany loans, capital redemption, or foreign-currency account balances. Practical frictions: (i) every international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the bank and may be held pending source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership documentation; (ii) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposes 25% gross withholding on non-resident dividends, interest, and royalties unless reduced by treaty (Canada-US 15%/5% for direct shareholders, Canada-China 10%; treaty relief requires Form NR301 and shareholder residency certificates); (iii) OFAC and Canadian sanctions list screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act can freeze flows until cleared; (iv) CDIC deposit insurance covers only CAD 100,000 per depositor per insured institution, so treasury balances above that level require multi-bank spreading.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline; exporters may retain USD, EUR, or CNY balances in bank or EMI multi-currency accounts and convert at commercially agreed spot rates. Compliance boundaries: FINTRAC large-EFT reporting applies to any outbound or inbound wire of CAD 10,000 or more with 24-hour aggregation of structured transfers; foreign-exchange gains and losses are recognized under section 39(1) of the Income Tax Act; GST/HST on qualifying exported goods and services to unregistered non-resident consumers is generally zero-rated when export documentation is maintained.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and note-issuing institution; prudential banking supervision and prudential FX balance-sheet oversight rest with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); exchange-control policy is vested in the Minister of Finance/Governor in Council under the Currency Act and the Emergencies Act (dormant); AML/ATF surveillance is executed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Stripe
            Fit: High
          

          
Viability rating: C+ (shadow rail only, not treasury-grade). Wise Business, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, and PayPal Business operate legally in Canada as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and can aggregate Kickstarter/Stripe/PayPal card settlements, but they cannot satisfy Canadian deposit-insurance expectations (no CDIC protection on wallet balances), cannot issue direct SWIFT MT103 messages with capital-injection purpose codes, and their balances represent unsecured claims on the EMI. Legal constraints: MSB registration requires the EMI to maintain a Canadian presence and to comply with PCMLTFA record-keeping, suspicious-transaction, and large-EFT rules; Canadian banks treat third-party EMI payouts as upstream money movements and will request the originator's full platform-reconciliation package before crediting the operating account. Recommended strategy: use the EMI layer as a low-balance pass-through for consumer card capture only, and sweep platform payouts daily into the RBC/TD/BMO operational account.

        All capital injections must arrive via SWIFT MT103 using SWIFT purpose code GFSR (financial/equity capital) with field 70 reading 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - CA_AB__ISIC_3240 ESCROW ALIAS - BOARD RESOLUTION [DATE]'. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability rating: C+ (shadow rail only, not treasury-grade). Wise Business, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, and PayPal Business operate legally in Canada as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and can aggregate Kickstarter/Stripe/PayPal card settlements, but they cannot satisfy Canadian deposit-insurance expectations (no CDIC protection on wallet balances), cannot issue direct SWIFT MT103 messages with capital-injection purpose codes, and their balances represent unsecured claims on the EMI. Legal constraints: MSB registration requires the EMI to maintain a Canadian presence and to comply with PCMLTFA record-keeping, suspicious-transaction, and large-EFT rules; Canadian banks treat third-party EMI payouts as upstream money movements and will request the originator's full platform-reconciliation package before crediting the operating account. Recommended strategy: use the EMI layer as a low-balance pass-through for consumer card capture only, and sweep platform payouts daily into the RBC/TD/BMO operational account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory threshold in CAD; however, CRA's administrative policy waives full contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for SME taxpayers with gross revenues below CAD 10 million and cross-border non-arm's length transactions below CAD 1 million. Above these de minimis thresholds, a local file, master file, and CbC report are required. Transaction-level records and a reasonable-efforts contemporaneous documentation must always exist for any related-party transactions.

      
Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer an Economic Substance Regulation equivalent to offshore centres (e.g., ESR UAE), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the doctrine from the Supreme Court in *Canada Trustco* deny tax benefits for transactions that are primarily tax-motivated and lack substantial economic substance. A corporate payer must demonstrate a real business activity—such as actual casting, shipping, and fulfilment functions in Alberta—if it wishes to claim cross-border treaty benefits or intercompany allocations. Treaty-shopping arrangements are attacked under the treaty anti-abuse provisions, and the CRA expects permanent-establishment countries to correspond to the actual flow of sales revenue from RPG dice manufacturing.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s.239(1), a fine ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 24 months on summary conviction, and up to 5 years if prosecuted by indictment; provincial penalties and gross negligence penalties under s.163.2 may add an additional penalty of up to 50% of the benefit.

        
Director personal liability: Directors can be pierö correlative: civil restitution for the tax and penalties, unlimited personal liability for unremitted Part XIII or payroll withholdings upon bankruptcy, and criminal charges as parties to the offence; travel restrictions can be imposed by a court under the bail or recognizance regime pending prosecution.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Sham / Lack of Substance
        
Statutory sanction: The CRA will apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) to negate non-recognition transactions, disallow treaty benefits, and reassess the true beneficial owner; a sham may be collaterially voided and the corporate veil would be pierced if the entity is a facade. Penalty of 50% of tax benefit under s.163.2 if the offence amounts to careless representation.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid tax and penalties under directors' liability certificates; in fraudulent conveyance cases, the court may set aside the corporate limitation and order restitution from director/director's estate; serious cases lead to travel restrictions and imprisonment under criminal fraud provisions.

      

      
      

        Corporation Tax Record-Keeping Failure / Failure to File
        
Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of 5% and 1% monthly; failure to provide books and records can lead to a compliance order, criminal contempt, and a default assessment with arbitrary income. For failure to file for more than two years, CRA may issue an automatic assessment and a 5% penalty on underpaid tax.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow a default in filing or who are jointly responsible for the failure can be held personally liable for the corporate tax debt under s.227.1 only if funds were not remitted; in wilful cases, personal prosecution as an accessory may attract imprisonment.

      

      
      

        Money Laundering (AML) Transaction Red Flags
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), administrative penalties up to CAD 1 million for non-registration or non-compliance; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code s.462.31 carries imprisonment up to 10 years and unlimited fines; false statements to FINTRAC attract penalties up to CAD 500,000 for small businesses.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct, authorize, or acquiesce in money laundering are criminally liable; civil restitution and asset seizure orders can reach their personal property, and under the Criminal Code the court may order a prohibition from leaving Canada if there is a risk of flight pending sentence.
