Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary filing and registration portal is My Business Account (MYBA) at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html; Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered by the CRA on behalf of Alberta, and Alberta does not levy a general provincial sales tax.

E-government filing portal

For a federally or provincially incorporated Business Corporation, the CRA issues the Business Number (BN) automatically upon incorporation via the corporate registry (Corporations Canada under the CBCA, or Alberta Corporate Registry under the ABCA). If a BN has not already been issued or is lost, the corporation must file CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) using the CRA online registration service or by completing the RC1 in paper form. To act as a GST/HST registrant, the corporation adds a program account with prefix RT to the BN (e.g. BN + RT0001); payroll accounts use prefixRP, and corporate income tax accounts use prefix RC. Non-resident corporations registering for GST/HST may register online or through a Canadian agent and will receive a CRA Web Access Code for subsequent administration.

In addition, the Alberta corporate income tax is levied on the same taxable income base, while the GST/HST system applies to taxable supplies at the entity level. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Canada). It computes federal Part I tax on worldwide income when resident in Canada and is not transparent for Canadian income tax purposes. In addition, the Alberta corporate income tax is levied on the same taxable income base, while the GST/HST system applies to taxable supplies at the entity level. There are no tax-exempt zones in Alberta; the only provincial tax distinction is the absence of an Alberta provincial sales tax, meaning only the 5% federal GST generally applies.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a U.S.-style check-the-box entity classification regime. A CBCA or Alberta Business Corporation is not eligible to elect pass-through treatment for federal income tax purposes; partnership transparency is reserved to partnerships. Certain federal elections are available in cross-border situations, including: s.216 of the Income Tax Act for non-resident film-production services; ss.93(1) and 93.3 legacy surplus elections for shares of foreign affiliates; s.256 rollover elections for amalgamations; and the qualified small-business corporation share election. Canada has enacted a hybrid mismatch statutory framework (ss. 2(7.1)-(8), 93.3, 94.2, 95.3 and 247) that operates by statutory deeming and disallowance rather than by elective check-the-box; no free-standing election can convert an entity-level taxed corporation into a pass-through entity for Canadian tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD over any single calendar quarter or any period of 12 consecutive months, measured by a person's worldwide taxable supplies including export sales; once a supplier exceeds $30,000, GST/HST registration is mandatory under Division V of the Excise Tax Act. For non-resident suppliers making direct-to-consumer supplies of tangible personal property and prescribed digital services in Canada, separate simplified GST/HST rules also set the registration threshold at $30,000 CAD over 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods, including carbon-fiber FPV racing drone components and fully assembled drones, that are physically exported from Canada as part of a qualifying supply to a non-resident recipient. The sale must be a taxable supply and the goods must have been exported within a reasonable time under s.1, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act. Certain services exported to non-residents are also zero-rated when they are not for use in Canada and are not supplied to a Canadian resident, including engineering, design, software development, logistics and after-sales support directly related to the exported drones. To sustain the 0% rate, the registrant must retain documentary proof of export at the time the tax is claimed, including customs evidence showing the goods were actually moved out of Canada, and ordinary commercial supporting evidence. If the registrant is under the $30,000 threshold and not required to register, the same zero-rating may apply only after the registrant either registers voluntarily or becomes successfully registered.

  • Canada Customs Coding Form B13A / Canada Customs Export Declaration (mandatory for exports of commercial goods above $2,000 CAD, transmitted electronically through the Canada Export Reporting System (CERS))
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / highway or rail carrier waybill showing physical movement from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice certifying the product description, quantity, HS classification and value of the FPV drones or components
  • Inward remittance certificate, bank statement or payment confirmation evidencing settlement of the export sale in foreign currency
  • Sale contract or purchase order and, where applicable, a certificate of origin or non-proliferation declaration for unmanned aerial vehicle exports

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance platform, a standardized tax-authority API for invoice validation, or a digital stamping regime for GST/HST. There is no legal requirement to pre-clear invoices with the CRA. Tax invoices issued by a GST/HST registrant must meet the informational requirements of s.169 and 221 of the Excise Tax Act, including the supplier's legal name or operating name, the supplier's GST/HST registration number, the date of supply, the description, the total consideration, and the amount of tax. Large filers (generally $1M+ in annual taxable supplies) must file electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI. Voluntary electronic invoicing is available through the Canadian Peppol Authority (PEPPOL BIS 3.0), and CRA can request electronic format records during audit, but no mandatory clearance mandate exists.

CRA auditors review export zero-rating at the documentary stage and may reassess under s.169(2) and s.296(1) of the Excise Tax Act if proof of export is not obtained within the required time, collecting the uncollected 5% GST plus arrears interest. Civil penalties apply for failure to collect or remit, and a false zero-rating claim can be subject to a gross-negligence penalty of up to 50% of the tax misstated, together with a 12% late-filing or failure-to-report penalty component plus interest on the deficiency. For deliberate falsification, the person faces prosecution under s.239 of the Income Tax Act and applicable offences in the Excise Tax Act, with fines of up to 200% of the evasion amount and imprisonment. CRA's normal reassessment period is four years, extended to seven years or without limit where neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud is established. Reversed zero-rating findings render input tax credits on related purchases clawed back retroactively with compound statutory interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange control permits, no restrictions on the purchase or sale of foreign currency, and no statutory approval requirements for cross-border profit remittance or dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders. Capital account liberalization allows direct equity injections, shareholder loans, and repatriation of capital without trade control consent. Legal hurdles are limited to Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) non-resident withholding tax on dividends (normally 25% absent treaty reduction, e.g., 5-15% under the Canada-U.S. Treaty with proper NR301/NR302 filings), the requirement to maintain a Canadian resident agent for service in Alberta under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta), and documentary compliance for Treaty-based relief. Corporates must also observe FINTRAC reporting thresholds: a large cross-border transaction report for physical currency movements exceeding CAD 10,000, and an electronic funds transfer report (EFTR) for international EFTs exceeding CAD 10,000. Profit remittance to foreign parents or shareholders is otherwise unfettered, and there is no stamp duty, capital duty, or branch equalization levy on the conversion itself.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no required timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Exporters of FPV racing drones (HS 8806 / 9503 parallel classifications; ISIC 3240; NAICS 339930) may hold foreign currency proceeds in CAD or USD operating accounts at their discretion. Record-keeping obligations require retention of export invoices, bills of lading, Customs Self-Assessment (CSA) program declarations if applicable, and underlying contracts for at least five years for CRA and FINTRAC audit purposes. Where export documents reflect letters of credit, the International Trade and Transportation division of Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) expects consistent commercial documents. No conversion certification requirement exists, but for financing purposes a bank may request evidence of underlying export transactions to support USD natural hedge lines.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and financial system regulator, with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential supervisor of federally licensed banks and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/CTF supervisory authority. Foreign exchange policy is administered by the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act, with settlement infrastructure overseen by Payments Canada. Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime under IMF Article VIII and does not operate a separate centralized foreign exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (complementary, not substitutionary). Fully digital international money services businesses and electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, or US-domiciled EMIs) may be onboarded as ancillary payment conduit accounts, but they cannot replace a Schedule I or Schedule II Canadian bank account for treasury-clearing purposes under the Bank Act (Canada) and the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) framework. EMIs are not licensed to take deposits; funds held with such providers are not eligible for Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) coverage. Cross-border digital EMIs may execute settlement in CAD via Payments Canada clearing only if they hold a Canadian payment service provider registration or operate through an authorized Canadian bank partner. Rod: for this low-risk drone parts and retail integration enterprise, the optimal structure is a primary Canadian bank account for capital, payroll, and taxes, with one EMI corridor (e.g., Airwallex or Wise) used solely for incoming customer collections from PayPal/Stripe sweeps, multi-currency receivables concentration, and vendor payments in USD/CNY/EUR. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined federal and Alberta general corporate income tax rate (15% federal + 8% provincial); 11% for eligible active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation within the small business limit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident recipient.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate for cross-border non-arm's length transactions requiring Form T106 filing; CAD 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue threshold for Country-by-Country Report (CbCR).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, CRA applies the OECD transfer pricing principles and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s.245, requiring demonstrable Canadian economic activity (employees, assets, risks, decision-making) to support intercompany pricing and treaty entitlement. Shell or letterbox structures are vulnerable to treaty-benefit denial, deemed-dividend recharacterization, and TP adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Under s.239 of the Income Tax Act: imprisonment for up to 5 years and fines up to 200% of the tax evaded; gross negligence penalty of 50% on understated tax; CRA may impose immediate asset freezes and garnishments.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or participate in fraudulent filings may be jointly and severally liable for gross negligence penalties; courts can pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a facade, imposing civil restitution, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Inadequate Documentation

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess to arm's length profits and impose a 10% penalty on the net transfer pricing adjustment under s.247(3); additional gross negligence penalty of 50% if documentation is knowingly false; interest runs from the original filing deadline.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve deficient TP forms or misleading economic analyses may face personal liability for the 10% TP penalty; in cases of deliberate sham pricing, courts may impose fraudulent conveyance remedies, restitution orders, and travel bans.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR under s.245 to deny tax benefits, recharacterize payments as deemed dividends subject to 25% Part XIII WHT, impose transfer-pricing penalties, and assess interest; treaty benefits may be denied under anti-abuse provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders may be liable for unwithheld Part XIII amounts; civil courts may pierce the corporate veil to recover assets defrauded from CRA, impose disgorgement, travel bans, and, where criminal intent is established, imprisonment.
Late Filing / Withholding Remittance Failures

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalties: 5% of unpaid balance plus 1% per month up to 12 months (repeat failure adds 10%); failure to withhold makes the corporation liable for the gross unwithheld amount plus interest; a CRA demand may trigger an additional 10% penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under s.227.1 ITA for unremitted source deductions and withholding taxes; this liability can be collected through personal bank account freezes, garnishment, and seizure of personal assets, with travel bans enforced where fraudulent non-compliance is proven.
