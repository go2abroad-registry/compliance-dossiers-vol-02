Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Primary portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), CRA My Business Account (MyBA), and GST/HST NETFILE/EFILE. The Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered and collected by the CRA under the Canada–Alberta Tax Collection Agreement; Alberta has no provincial retail sales tax (no PST/HST provincial component).

E-government filing portal

Apply for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts 'RC0001' (GST/HST) and 'RT0001' (Corporate Income Tax) via Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' with the CRA. Foreign non-resident corporations must use the non-resident BN series, designate a Canadian agent/representative for service of documents, and provide: certificate of corporate incorporation (federal or under Alberta Business Corporations Act), registered office address, director/officer particulars, and bank account confirmation. GST/HST registration is mandatory once the person is no longer a 'small supplier' (worldwide gross taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the current or preceding four consecutive calendar quarters); otherwise registration is voluntary. The corporate income tax account is opened automatically upon incorporation or upon filing the first corporate return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian 'business corporation' is a separate taxable person under the Income Tax Act, taxed on worldwide income if resident and on Canadian-source business income if non-resident. Alberta-resident corporations: federal general rate 15% + Alberta general rate 8% (total 23%); Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPC) eligible for the small business deduction: federal 9% + Alberta 2% (total 11%) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada are taxable under Part I on Canadian-source income and under Part XIII on passive Canadian-source payments, with branch tax under section 219. No pass-through/fiscally transparent treatment is available for an incorporated entity; the corporation cannot be treated as a disregarded entity for Canadian tax purposes.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' regime; entity classification follows the legal form under the CBCA/ABCA. Available statutory elections: (1) subsection 216(1) ITA election allowing a non-resident corporation with Canadian rental/royalty gross revenue to file a Part I return and be taxed on net Canadian-source income instead of Part XIII withholding; (2) hybrid mismatch designations/structured arrangement elections and documentation under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 implementing BEPS Action 2 (relevant to outbound hybrid financing by the non-resident parent); (3) Excise Tax Act section 156 election among closely related corporations for nil-consideration GST/HST supplies, plus joint elections under s. 244(1); (4) GST/HST 'quick method' election for small corporations (generally available to CCPCs with taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000); and (5) subsection 45(2) capital gains deferral on change-of-use of property. CRA administrative policy also recognizes fiscally transparent foreign entities (e.g., US single-member LLCs) for Canadian tax purposes, but a Canadian-incorp corporation cannot elect transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in gross worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies and some financial services) in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and in the current quarter; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory from the following month. As of July 1, 2021, non-resident suppliers and marketplace facilitators selling goods delivered in Canada (including in-bond warehousing at Canadian postal/courier depots) are deemed to be carrying on a business in Canada for GST/HST purposes where taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold; platform sellers transacting through Etsy Direct Pay may rely on the platform's deemed-supplier collection, but a Canadian seller remains independently required to register and charge GST/HST on Canadian sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V (paragraph 1, 'Exports') to supplies of tangible goods (hard enamel lapel pins, ISIC 3240; HS 7326.90 / 7117.90) where the supplier or the recipient on the supplier's behalf exports the goods to a destination outside Canada and the goods are delivered via common carrier, mail, courier, or freight. Evidence that the goods left Canada within 60 days of the invoice is required. Services supplied in respect of exported goods (e.g., die-struck brass polishing, cloisonné enamelling, design and fulfilment for pin merchandise) supplied to a non-resident recipient are also zero-rated where the recipient is outside Canada and the service is acquired for consumption wholly outside Canada; the zero-rating of goods permits full input tax credit (ITC) recovery for related inputs, including raw materials, brass, enamel, and logistics.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13/G49 or Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS)) for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 or for controlled goods; for low-value postal/courier shipments under CAD 2,000, a commercial invoice suffices
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Canada Post or courier consignment note showing destination outside Canada
  • Commercial Invoice (with HS 7326.90 or 7117.90, description, unit values, and seller/BN)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank wire confirmation, or Etsy/Stripe settlement statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency
  • Purchase order or sale contract with the non-resident buyer, plus packing list

No federal real-time clearance mandate, no government API validation, and no digital stamping of invoices at the CRA level. Invoices issued by GST/HST registrants must contain prescribed information under ETA s. 3(a)(i) of Schedule V and the Excise Tax Information Regulations (supplier BN/legal name, date, recipient name/address, description of goods, tax charged in Canadian dollars). Mandatory electronic filing (NETFILE/EFILE) and mandatory electronic remittance apply to registrants with net tax over certain thresholds (generally gross revenue over CAD 1,000,000). Quebec's Revenu Québec is phasing in mandatory B2B e-invoicing from 2026/2027 for large enterprises (revenue above C$50M), but this does not affect Alberta-based SME exporters of enamel pins; there is no requirement to use a certified invoicing platform or PEPPOL in Alberta.

CRA can reassess within the standard four-year period, extended to ten years for wilful misrepresentation or gross negligence. If export zero-rating is denied, the tax becomes collectible with arrears interest and penalties: ETA s. 280 (failure to register: penalty of 10% of the amount plus CAD 10 per day), ETA s. 285 (false statements: penalty generally 25% of the tax evaded or misstated, and gross negligence penalty of 25% of the net tax under s. 285.1), plus retrospective clawback of input tax credits claimed against the fabricated export sale. Criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 327(1) carries fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years. CBSA enforcement for inaccurate export declarations triggers administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) under the Customs Act (ss. 109.1–109.5) and the Export Control Act. CRA also has the power to revoke BN/GST registration, audit foreign-currency receipts, and issue Part XIII/Part I reassessments for unreported income where the FX repatriation does not match declared export proceeds within the prescribed 180-day period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and permits unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. A CA_AB-resident exporter may receive USD revenue, maintain foreign-currency operating accounts, and remit dividends, management fees, intercompany loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds abroad without exchange-control approval. The only frictions are domestic: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on payments to non-residents (25% statutory default, reduced under Canada-China double taxation treaty to 10% on dividends where the beneficial owner owns at least 10% of voting shares, 5% for substantial treaty-qualified shareholdings, 0% on interest and royalties in certain conditions), the Alberta Business Corporations Act solvency test for distributions, and general transfer-pricing documentation under the Income Tax Act s. 247 for cross-border related-party transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory repatriation ratio, or surrender obligation for export receipts; a financial corporation engaged in ISIC 3240 enamel pin sales may retain proceeds in CAD, USD, or other freely convertible currencies at a Schedule I bank without restriction. The only reporting duty is indirect: for each international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, the financial institution must file a FINTRAC EFT report including remitter, beneficiary, and transaction purpose code; exporters may also elect functional-currency reporting under Income Tax Act s. 261 solely with prior CRA approval and maintain dual books in the elected currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange administration is decentralized across the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential oversight, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT and cross-border EFT reporting, and the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control board because the capital account is fully liberalized and the Canadian dollar is freely floating.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a non-resident shareholder injection: instruct the remitting bank to issue an SWIFT MT103 in CAD or USD to the Canadian bank's correspondent, with beneficiary line showing the exact legal name of the CA_AB corporation and its Alberta registry number, and a payment purpose code denoting direct investment equity capital (BPM6 code for direct investment equity injection). The receiving bank will require a board resolution authorizing the issuance of shares, a share subscription agreement, source-of-funds evidence from the remitter, and confirmation that no OFAC/FINTRAC/UN sanctions matches exist. CAD funds settle same-day via Lynx (Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement system operated by Payments Canada) where the receiving bank is a direct participant, or via ACSS batch processing for lower-value transfers; USD funds clear over Fedwire or CHIPS through the bank's U.S. correspondent and are booked on the following business day. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (annual aggregate value of transactions with non-resident related parties) triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; also required if the corporation is part of a multinational group with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. For cross-border transactions, the CRA may challenge arrangements lacking economic substance, and the OECD's BEPS actions (e.g., Action 6) are incorporated into domestic law.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239: fine up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties: 50% gross negligence penalty on understated tax; interest on arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax (including penalties and interest) if they failed to withhold or remit source deductions (s.227.1); travel bans and asset freezes possible under criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize transactions, and apply GAAR to reassess; penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; interest; potential criminal charges for fraudulent misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes arising from GAAR adjustments if they participated in the scheme; civil restitution and potential criminal liability for fraud.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for non-compliance with record-keeping and reporting; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participate in or fail to prevent AML violations; travel bans and asset forfeiture possible under criminal proceedings.
