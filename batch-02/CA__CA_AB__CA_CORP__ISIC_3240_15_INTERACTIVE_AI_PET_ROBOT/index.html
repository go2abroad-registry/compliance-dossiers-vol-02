Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC), federal tax administrator for corporate income tax and GST/HST, operating through CRA My Business Account portal at https://www.canada.ca/revenue-agency; Alberta Corporate Registry responsible for provincial registry and Alberta Corporate Access Number at https://corporate-registry.gov.ab.ca.

E-government filing portal

A business corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Business Corporations Act must register for its federal Business Number (BN) and related program accounts. Online registration is performed via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). Where paper registration is used, Form RC1, Request for Business Number and Certain Accounts, is filed with CRA. A GST/HST account (account type RT) is opened automatically or upon application for a taxable registrant. For a non-resident foreign entity, a Canadian representative with valid power of attorney is required; CRA Form RC464A or Form RC59 may be used to confirm the agent authorization. Alberta incorporation is evidenced by the Corporate Access Number issued upon registration under the Alberta Business Corporations Act. GST/HST registration is mandatory once Canadian and worldwide taxable revenues exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, without regard to the place of supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A 'Business Corporation' incorporated in Canada is a separate legal person and resident corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act if incorporated in Canada. Such corporation is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income for Canadian federal purposes and for Alberta provincial purposes under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement. For a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada, Canadian-source business income is subject to Part I tax on a territorial basis, and remittances out of Canada may attract Part XIV branch tax. The AI-pet-robot manufacturer would be a CRA registrant with BN, RT, RC, and RZ accounts as applicable.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide an elective 'check-the-box' regime for domestic income tax purposes. Every corporation is classified as a taxable corporation under the Income Tax Act; no disregarded-entity election is available domestically. Canadian tax elections are available for corporate reorganizations and transfers under ss. 85, 86, 87, 88, 97, 51 and 52 of the Income Tax Act, as well as for the s. 216 non-resident rental election. For hybrid instruments and hybrid mismatch rules, ss. 18.4 and 18.5 operate to deny deductions in respect of interest or dividend payments that give rise to a deduction/non-inclusion mismatch. Alberta does not prohibit unlimited liability corporations; a ULC may be classified as disregarded for U.S. federal income tax purposes and may elect status under U.S. check-the-box, but that foreign-election classification does not change the entity's Canadian tax classification for CRA purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — a person qualifies as a small supplier and is exempt from mandatory GST/HST registration only while worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) do not exceed CAD 30,000 in the current or previous four consecutive calendar quarters; mandatory registration is triggered immediately when the threshold is exceeded, and voluntary registration is available to exporters and other zero-rated suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported goods applies under section 1, Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. The supplier must be a GST/HST registrant; the goods must be physically removed from Canada, exported within 60 days after delivery to an exporter or carrier, or delivered by common carrier, mail, or courier to a foreign destination. For a bionic companion robotic animal classified under ISIC 3240 / NAICS 339930, the export zero-rating applies if the goods leave Canada and the registrant maintains evidence of export. Supplies of services that are performed wholly outside Canada and supplied to a non-resident are also zero-rated, as are services performed on exported tangible personal property. No Canada Border Services Agency export licence or special product clearance is required for this category.

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or mandatory digital stamping obligation. Registered persons must file GST/HST returns electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE and remit net tax by the statutory due date. E-invoicing is voluntary and may be exchanged through the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Infrastructure using the PEPPOL network. Taxpayers must retain all books of account and supporting invoices and records for six years from the end of the relevant tax year; where exports are zero-rated, CRA expects exporters to keep the documentary support and customs communication contemporaneously available for audit.

CRA may reverse a zero-rated export where the supplier fails to retain or produce complete documentation proving physical export and foreign consideration. Upon reassessment, 5% GST is assessed on the selling price, with interest in accordance with the Excise Tax Act from the date the tax would have become payable. Gross-negligence penalties may be imposed at the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax understated, and false statements or omissions may trigger s. 285.1 penalties of up to 50% of the total understated tax. Criminal enforcement may apply for deliberate evasion, with fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to five years. The 180-day benchmark reflected in this dossier is an operational documentary safeguard; Canadian law does not impose a fixed statutory date for foreign exchange repatriation, but CRA and customs auditors assess foreign sales proceeds evidence, and a failure to show the remittance within a reasonable 180-day window increases the risk of denial of zero-rating and the imposition of penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency under IMF Article VIII; there are no capital-account or current-account exchange controls and no requirement for official approval on profit remittance. Dividend repatriation is subject to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax of 25%, treaty-reduced (e.g., 5% under the Canada-U.S. DTA with 10% voting-control shareholding), and the payout must satisfy the solvency test under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (dividends only out of retained earnings or surplus). Inbound foreign investment is screened only under the Investment Canada Act (net-benefit review for acquisitions of control of an existing Canadian business at or above C$1.152B enterprise value for WTO investors); the establishment of a new Alberta corporation is not a reviewable transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio (0%); Canadian-resident exporters may hold foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts indefinitely. FINTRAC requires reporting of any cross-border electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more within 24 hours, and physical currency movements of C$10,000 or more across the border must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Any conversion into CAD is purely treasury policy, not law.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the central bank; there is no centralized foreign-exchange administration in Canada, as the Foreign Exchange Control Act has been repealed. Cross-border banking and fund-movement oversight is shared by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF and cross-border electronic funds transfer (ETR) reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-Moderate feasibility. The EU/UK EMI licence has no direct Canadian analogue; an offshore EMI cannot accept deposits or maintain a resident operating account without becoming a federally regulated bank under the Bank Act or registering as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC. Offshore digital payment platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer) may serve as collection/disbursement corridors and contingent liquidity tools, but the primary treasury spine must remain a Schedule I bank operating account. Legal constraints: foreign fintechs transacting in Canada must register as MSBs under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act regulations, appoint a Canadian AML compliance officer, maintain Canadian books and records, and comply with provincial licensing regimes (e.g., Alberta's Money Lenders Act).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be maintained if the taxpayer's aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000; Master File required where the multinational group's consolidated gross revenue is at least CAD 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulation applicable at the Alberta or federal level in a manner analogous to the Gulf ESR; however, CRA applies GAAR (ITA s.245) to disregard transactions lacking commercial substance, and treaty-limitation-on-benefits tests plus base-erosion and anti-treaty-shopping rules (e.g., s.94.1–94.3) deny treaty or deduction benefits to genuinely artificial structures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded under s.239(1); imprisonment up to five years; additional civil penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or connive in the fraud are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt and penalties; criminal conviction can lead to director disqualification, asset freezes, travel bans, and extradition.
Shell Company / Sham Transaction

Statutory sanction: CRA reassesses the corporation at arm's length; GAAR applies with 50% penalty on the tax benefit; treaty relief rejected under Limitation-on-Benefits; penalties under s.238(2) and criminal charges under s.239 for deliberate misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Promoters and nominees may be held personally liable for the tax benefit as tax shelter sellers; in severe cases, directors' personal bank accounts can be attached and court-ordered exit bans imposed for non-payment.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment; penalty under s.247(3) of 10% of the net adjustment if reasonable efforts not made; flat penalty of CAD 24,000 for failure to provide documentation; interest and compounded arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed under s.227.1 if they knowingly approved non-arm's length transactions that caused withholding obligations; in fraud contexts, officers may be criminally liable and subject to travel restrictions.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 1.5 million per violation; criminal fine up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years for knowingly dealing with proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and compliance officers personally liable for FINTRAC penalties if they knowingly consented to or failed to stop the infraction; their assets may be seized under a restraint order, and foreign travel may be barred pending criminal process.
