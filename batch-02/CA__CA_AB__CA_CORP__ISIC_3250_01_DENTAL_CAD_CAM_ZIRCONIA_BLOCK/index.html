Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Canadian Business Corporation is a statutory taxpayer (Income Tax Act RSC 1985 c 1 (5th Supp.), s. 2 and deemed resident under s. 250(4) by incorporation in Canada) taxed on worldwide income; there is no pass-through or check-the-box regime. Federal general corporate rate 15% plus Alberta 8% (combined 23%); a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) earning active business income up to CAD 500,000 is eligible for the Small Business Deduction (federal 9% plus Alberta 2%, combined 11%). Export income is taxable in Canada without territorial exemption; double taxation relief is granted by foreign tax credits (ITA ss. 113, 126). No tax-exempt zone applies. Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax, so indirect taxation is limited to the 5% federal GST.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box entity classification election; corporate residence and taxability are fixed by statute. Available elections affecting an exporting manufacturer: (1) ITA s. 85(1) rollover to transfer contributed intellectual property or equipment at tax cost; (2) fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 permitting a non-calendar year (maximum 53 weeks); (3) associated-CCPC election on T2 Schedule 4 allocating the CAD 500,000 Small Business Deduction limit among associated corporations; (4) ETA amplified accounting elections — annual/quarterly/monthly return frequency and the Quick Method for small registrants; (5) foreign-affiliate elections (ITA ss. 93(1) and 113(2)) to characterize dividends from and dispositions of foreign distribution subsidiaries; (6) worldwide or separate-country foreign accrual property income elections (ITA s. 95(2)-(7), only in specified circumstances); (7) hybrid-mismatch rules follow BEPS Action 2 (ITA ss. 18.2-18.8) and admit no elective opt-in; the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) and ETA s. 274 remain backstops.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully freely convertible under a market-determined floating exchange rate regime. No exchange control legislation, capital permit system, or prior central bank approval applies to current account (trade in goods/services, royalties, management fees) or capital account (share subscription, intercompany loans, dividends) flows. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted and is subject only to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (typically 5-25% depending on the relevant tax treaty and beneficial ownership substance). For this Alberta business corporation (ISIC 3250/NAICS 339114 dental ceramic block manufacturer), there are zero statutory convertibility hurdles, no investment screening thresholds applicable at this sub-50% non-controlling ownership profile, and no requirement to obtain a Certificate of Compliance from the Canada Revenue Agency before wire transfers. The sole practical requirements are bank-level customer due diligence and FINTRAC large-transaction reporting (wires of CAD $10,000 or more), which do not constitute substantive currency impediments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes NO mandatory export proceeds surrender, NO statutory timeline for repatriation, and NO prescribed conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency bank accounts (e.g., USD clearing sub-accounts), may convert at commercial interbank rates, and may net or pool export receivables for treasury optimization. Only statistical reporting obligations apply: (i) the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration for goods exceeding CAD 2,000, and (ii) Statistics Canada trade data compilation; neither creates exchange control compliance duties. For commercial invoicing to global dental distributors and milling centers, proceeds can be collected in USD, EUR, or CNY without restriction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating in conjunction with OSFI for prudential bank regulation and FINTRAC for AML/ATF surveillance; there is no standalone foreign exchange administration authority in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as supplementary rail, LOW as primary treasury infrastructure. Globally licensed fintech EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer) are permitted in Canada as registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under FINTRAC (PCMLTFA registration), but they are not chartered banks and cannot issue letters of credit, provide committed FX lines, or hold settlement clearing memberships. For this dental consumables manufacturer (low AML risk tier), such EMIs are acceptable only for sub-CAD 50,000 supplier disbursements, small inbound customer advance payments, and employee expense reimbursements. All principal USD/CAD settlement, capital injections, and trade finance must be anchored at a Schedule I Canadian bank to preserve legal certainty, direct SWIFT membership, and OSFI-regulated custody of operating liquidity. Any offshore unregistered EMI is treated as LOW viability and inadmissible for this treasury structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an inbound capital injection from a non-resident corporate parent or investor into the Alberta corporation: (1) Execution must be a SAWN wire (SWIFT MT103) from the remitter's bank through a US/UK clearing correspondent into the beneficiary's CAD/USD account at the Tier-1 Canadian bank; (2) Purpose code field must state explicitly 'Capital Contribution / Share Premium' — Canada has no central bank coding system, but the remitting bank and beneficiary bank require the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' or 'CNCB' and a free-text note matching the entity's PUC; (3) Beneficiary line must show the Alberta corporation's legal name, account number, and the bank's 9-digit routing (institution + transit) verified via the Canadian Payments Association directory; (4) The same-day FINTRAC large-international-transaction report (C$10k+ threshold) is filed by the bank, with no prior governmental approval required; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as supplementary rail, LOW as primary treasury infrastructure. Any offshore unregistered EMI is treated as LOW viability and inadmissible for this treasury structure.
