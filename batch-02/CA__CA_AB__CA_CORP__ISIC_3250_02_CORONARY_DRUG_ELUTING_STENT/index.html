Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); My Business Account portal (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Alberta corporate income tax is assessed and collected by the CRA on behalf of Alberta Treasury Board and Finance under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act administration agreement.

E-government filing portal

The statutory tax identifier is the nine-digit Business Number (BN). A corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Alberta Business Corporations Act obtains the BN via Business Registration Online (BRO), adding program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) and RM (import/export for CBSA customs accounting). Registration must be completed before the first filing due date; GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold). A non-resident exporter without Canadian incorporation may register for GST/HST on Form RC1 solely to claim input tax credits and zero-rate exports; the CRA may require a Canadian address for service, a resident representative, or security as a condition of registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A corporation incorporated in Canada is resident in Canada under common law and taxable on worldwide income under section 2 of the Income Tax Act; Alberta levies an 8% provincial corporate income tax on income allocated to Alberta. Export-only business conducted through foreign affiliates may be relieved by the foreign affiliate exemption for active business income, narrowing the effective territorial scope to Canadian-source and treaty-exposed income.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box regime. Available elections and mandatory alignment mechanisms include: (1) functional currency election under ITA s.261; (2) joint nil-consideration election for closely related corporations under ETA s.156; (3) offshore services election under ITA s.93.1(2) for services performed for a foreign affiliate in a treaty country; (4) non-resident section 216 election for Canadian-source rental or timber royalty income; and (5) hybrid mismatch deduction-denial rules under ITA ss.18.4 to 18.6 with the accompanying inclusion rule in s.12.7.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (small supplier threshold under ETA s.148). Registration is voluntary below the threshold and compulsory once exceeded; a non-resident carrying on commercial activity in Canada is subject to the same threshold rules and may register voluntarily to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of coronary drug-eluting stents is zero-rated under section 1 or section 2 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the goods are shipped to a destination outside Canada designated in the contract of carriage and are not acquired for consumption, use or supply in Canada. Services rendered to a non-resident may be zero-rated under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI where the services are performed wholly outside Canada or otherwise meet the statutory conditions and do not relate to Canadian real estate or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The exporter must export the goods as soon as commercially reasonable after delivery and retain contemporaneous transport, customs, inventory and payment records to sustain the zero-rating claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration - electronic filing through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CBSA CAED) for exports exceeding CAD 2,000 or controlled, regulated or permitted goods
  • Bill of Lading - original or certified negotiable bill of lading / airway waybill evidencing physical carriage to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - SWIFT MT103 credit advice or bank confirmation of foreign currency proceeds actually received from the offshore purchaser

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing Standard (PINT), with CRA-supported Peppol interoperability. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants with annual gross revenue above CAD 1.5 million and to T2 corporate income tax filers with gross revenue above CAD 1 million.

CRA audits will test the documentary chain, timing of dispatch, and destination of goods; failure to substantiate export under the ETA triggers reassessment of uncollected 5% GST plus daily compound interest. Gross negligence or false statements attract a 50% penalty on the understated net tax under ETA s.285.1; willful evasion under s.327 is punishable on indictment by fines of 100% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of responsible officers for up to five years. The normal reassessment period is four years, but the limitation period is unlimited where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, willful default or fraud. Canada does not prescribe a mechanical 180-day repatriation term, but proceeds retained offshore without active business deployment may be recharacterized under Part 1 foreign affiliate rules and transfer-pricing adjustments under ITA s.247, with consequential clawback of export margins.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility; the Canadian dollar (CAD) is free-floating and freely convertible with no exchange controls, no conversion quotas, and no prohibition on foreign-currency deposit accounts, USD clearing accounts, or offshore settlement rails. Profit remittance constraints are tax-driven rather than FX-driven: non-resident shareholders face Part XIII withholding tax (default 25%; treaty-reduced to 5% or 15% on dividends under the Canada-China DTA), and the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) Section 116 clearance-certificate procedure applies to the disposition of taxable Canadian property by a non-resident. The Canadian treasury must file NR6 and Part XIII remittances, maintain arm's-length transfer pricing documentation under Section 247 of the Income Tax Act for intercompany royalties and management fees, and comply with CRS/FATCA reporting; no CAD amount or conversion requirement applies to repatriations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None: Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in USD or any foreign currency in a Canadian Schedule I bank account, a provincial credit union, or an OSFI-regulated foreign branch, subject only to FINTRAC electronic fund transfer (EFT) reporting for transfers of CAD$10,000 or more (administrative reporting, not a restriction). All exports must be declared to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for Statistics Canada's Canadian International Merchandise Trade data under HS Code 9021.39.00 (coronary stents and similar implantable devices), and the Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA) export permit requirements do not apply to standard coronary drug-eluting stents absent a listing on the Export Control List.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting as fiscal agent for the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bank of Canada Act. Canada maintains no general foreign exchange control statute; prudential regulation of banking and payments is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while transaction-level reporting and AML/CFT surveillance are enforced by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as a primary liquidity rail; MEDIUM-HIGH as a supplementary disbursement and collections rail. Under Canadian law, an offshore digital EMI (e.g., an EU/UK-licensed electronic money institution) is not a bank and cannot lawfully accept Canadian customer deposits in the banking law sense; any non-bank entity holding funds in Canada must be registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and operate under a full PCMLTFA compliance program. The Alberta medtech entity may use an EMI for foreign-currency trade receipts and supplier payments on an agency basis, but the statutory capital escrow and investment-grade treasury functions must remain with a Schedule I bank or a regulated Schedule II bank/credit union to satisfy lenders, insurers, and corporate auditors. Each EMI deployment requires a board resolution, a documented treasury control policy with bank-to-bank sweeps, and full EFT reporting of all EMI-originated wires.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection from the PRC/HK parent into the Alberta entity proceeds in two documented legs. Leg 1 (China side): the parent remits through its SAFE-registered ODI structure via a Chinese bank (Bank of China or a cross-border RMB clearing bank) using SAFE international receipts and payments purpose code 622 (outward direct investment capital) and attaching the ODI certificate and foreign-exchange registration record. Leg 2 (Canada side): the receiving Schedule I bank executes the credit as an SWIFT MT103 with remittance information fields (fields 70/77B) quoting the Alberta corporate registry number, the payment purpose 'equity subscription / capital contribution', and the exact legal name of the contributing shareholder. The bank will apply PCMLTFA EFT reporting (CAD$10,000 threshold) and may require the share subscription agreement, board resolution approving issuance, wire receipt evidence, and a CRA Section 116 clearance certificate if the capital is later repatriated or the shares disposed. A USD-denominated account is recommended so funds remain in USD and FX conversion is deferred until the first trade disbursement. For CAD settlement, the clearing rail is Payments Canada Lynx (real-time gross settlement, ISO 20022, formerly LVTS); for USD settlement, the Canadian bank's New York correspondent clears via CHIPS/Fedwire.Outbound trade settlement uses three parallel rails: (i) SWIFT MT103/MT202 direct transfers to suppliers via Payments Canada Lynx (CAD, RTGS) or via the Canadian bank's US correspondent through CHIPS/Fedwire (USD), both under SWIFT gpi for track-and-trace; (ii) commercial letters of credit governed by UCP 600 (and ISP98 for standby credits) issued by the Schedule I bank's trade desk — typically irrevocable and sight for initial supplier engagements, with the advising and confirming bank in the beneficiary's country handling document verification; and (iii) documentary collections (DP/DA) and open-account settlements with bank acceptance or supply-chain-finance support once a 3-4 shipment cycle history exists with the supplier. Under Canadian law, an offshore digital EMI (e.g., an EU/UK-licensed electronic money institution) is not a bank and cannot lawfully accept Canadian customer deposits in the banking law sense; any non-bank entity holding funds in Canada must be registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and operate under a full PCMLTFA compliance program. The Alberta medtech entity may use an EMI for foreign-currency trade receipts and supplier payments on an agency basis, but the statutory capital escrow and investment-grade treasury functions must remain with a Schedule I bank or a regulated Schedule II bank/credit union to satisfy lenders, insurers, and corporate auditors. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 gross annual value of controlled transactions in property or services; CAD 250,000 for controlled transactions involving intangible property, royalties, or interest. Provincial compliance with Alberta does not alter these federal thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a named Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime (as in UAE or Barbados), but CRA applies: (i) s.247(2) Transfer Pricing Adjustment requiring allocation of confirmed risks to entities with tangible capital and employees in Canada; (ii) s.245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule disallowing without-bona-fide-purpose transactions; and (iii) treaty benefit rules (e.g., Limitation on Benefits provisions in recent DTAs) requiring active conduct of an enterprise through permanent establishment in Canada. For a medical device manufacturer, key substance evidence: legal title to IP, plant and machinery in Alberta, technical R&D staff, and space leased for cleanroom laser micromachining.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under s.239(1) Income Tax Act: fine equal to 50% to 200% of the tax evaded (or CAD 1,000 minimum), plus imprisonment for a term of up to 5 years if prosecuted by indictment, or up to 2 years on summary conviction. CRA may also levy a civil gross negligence penalty under s.163(2): 50% of the understated tax. Alberta may suspend the corporate certificate of status for failure to remit provincial tax.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted payroll and dividend withholding under s.227.1 ITA (due diligence defence available). In fraud cases, a court may pierce the corporate veil, imposing restitution orders for the full deficiency plus interest; directors face travel bans (via arrest warrants/asset freezes), mandatory surrender of passports, and imprisonment if convicted of conspiracy to evade tax.
