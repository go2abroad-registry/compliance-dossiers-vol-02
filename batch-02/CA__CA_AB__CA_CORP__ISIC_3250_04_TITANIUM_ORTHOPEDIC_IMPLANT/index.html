E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Alberta-incorporated or federally incorporated corporation is a 'taxable Canadian corporation' under ITA s. 89(1), taxed at the entity level under Part I on its worldwide income (not Territorial_Scope); the general federal rate is 15%, Alberta's general provincial rate is 8%, and the Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) small-business rate applies to active business income up to CAD 500,000, subject to provincial/federal aggregation and passive-income grind rules; no pass-through, partnership, or disregarded-entity election is available; consolidated filing is prohibited; the permanent establishment allocation to Alberta is the sole provincial nexus; a non-resident-owned Canadian corporation remains fully entity-level taxable.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a U.S.-style check-the-box election, and hybrid-mismatch rules are mandatory with no opt-out. Available elections: (i) ETA s. 156 closely-related-corporation election (Form GST25) to treat inter-affiliate supplies of implant production services and goods as being for nil consideration; (ii) ETA s. 71 cash-basis election (Form GST74) for eligible small registrants; (iii) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST74) for registrants with worldwide taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000; (iv) ETA s. 191(1) joint election for taxable transfers of property among registrants; (v) ITA s. 83(2) capital-dividend-account election (return-based, relevant only post-earnings); (vi) mandatory hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4–18.6 and 20.2 for payments made or incurred on or after July 1, 2022, disallowing deductions or requiring inclusion where the counterparty jurisdiction has no corresponding inclusion; (vii) mandatory Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) under ITA ss. 18.2, 18.21 and 18.22 for corporations and large partnerships with net interest/ financing expenses exceeding CAD 250,000 for taxation years commencing on or after October 1, 2023, limiting net interest deductions to 30% of tax EBITDA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the statutory small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1), computed on the total consideration for taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies) made in Canada in any single calendar quarter or cumulatively over the four preceding calendar quarters; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory under ETA s. 240(1); a manufacturer making exclusively zero-rated exports is not required to register but should register voluntarily under ETA s. 241 to claim input tax credits on plant, tooling, and overhead, and to support zero-rated export refund mechanics.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of the titanium orthopedic implants qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, para. 1 (exported tangible personal property supplied by way of sale) where: (i) the purchaser is a non-resident of Canada; (ii) the goods are delivered by the supplier to a common carrier, freight forwarder, or mailed by Canada Post for direct export from Canada; (iii) possession of the goods is transferred before export and the goods are exported as soon as is feasible; (iv) the goods are not altered, consumed, or incorporated into other goods in Canada after the supply; (v) the goods are not imported back into Canada prior to export; and (vi) contemporaneous documentary proof of export is retained. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident (e.g., engineering design for the implant) are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, para. 7 only where the service is not performed in Canada in the course of the supplier's Canadian business and does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; therefore, manufacturing/passivation services physically performed at the Alberta plant for a non-resident principal may remain taxable, and the safe structure is title-transfer sale of the finished implant for export rather than a Canadian-rendered service. Additional zero-rating for medical devices under Schedule VI, Part II may apply to implantable devices supplied to a named individual, but export zero-rating at 0% is the operative mechanism for the offshore distribution model.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts under a free-floating CAD regime; no exchange-control legislation restricts dividend, interest, or capital remittances. Outbound profit repatriation is subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reduced, e.g., 5% for a 10%+ corporate shareholder under the Canada-US treaty). Inbound investment for this medical-device manufacturing scope is reviewable only under the Investment Canada Act net-benefit test for acquisitions above the WTO threshold (C$1.045B in 2023); a greenfield Alberta plant requires no pre-clearance. No central-bank approval is required for wire transfers, and no conversion certificates are mandated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds; exporters may retain and hold foreign currency abroad indefinitely. Compliance obligations are limited to (i) CBSA export declarations under the Customs Act via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for goods leaving Canada, (ii) Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys (monthly/quarterly) for entities with annual foreign receipts above C$30M, and (iii) Investment Canada Act annual reporting where applicable. Medical-device exports also require a Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) under the Food and Drugs Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential regulation by OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions), AML/ATF supervision by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada), and balance-of-payments statistical reporting by Statistics Canada. Canada maintains no standalone foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Foreign electronic-money institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut) may operate in Canada only as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses under the PCMLTFA; they are statutorily barred from accepting demand deposits, a privilege reserved for Schedule I/II chartered banks, credit unions, and trust companies under the Bank Act. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility. Foreign electronic-money institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut) may operate in Canada only as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses under the PCMLTFA; they are statutorily barred from accepting demand deposits, a privilege reserved for Schedule I/II chartered banks, credit unions, and trust companies under the Bank Act.
