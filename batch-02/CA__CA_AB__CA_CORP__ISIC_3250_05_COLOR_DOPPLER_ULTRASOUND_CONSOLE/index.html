Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — registration and filing administered through Business Registration Online (BRO) and the CRA 'My Business Account' portal (canada.ca); customs export administration: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the Canadian Single Window / Canadian Export Reporting System (G-13); Alberta provincial corporate registry: Alberta Registries (Alberta Corporate Registration System), which transmits corporate identity data to the CRA.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta Business Corporation obtains its statutory tax identity as follows: (1) incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act or register the federal corporation as an extra-provincial company in Alberta; (2) obtain the corporate registration number from the Alberta Corporate Registry; (3) apply to the CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and the Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) account (RT) through BRO or by filing Form RC1 (Request for Business Number Registration); (4) open the CBSA Import-Export (RM) account so that future export declarations for the medical ultrasound consoles can be transmitted through the G-13 Canadian Export Reporting System; (5) for a non-resident corporation, designate a Canadian representative and follow the ETA s.240(1) non-resident registration path, including the deemed-residency rule where applicable; (6) elect the GST/HST reporting period and fiscal year-end at registration; (7) verify the registration by searching the CRA GST/HST Registry.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: a federal/Alberta Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is an independent taxpayer under both the Income Tax Act (ITA) and the Excise Tax Act (ETA); no pass-through treatment applies to the corporation; the Alberta permanent establishment is subject to Alberta corporate income tax at 8% in addition to the federal corporate rate (as applicable), while GST/HST is administered federally at the entity level. — A foreign-owned Alberta Business Corporation obtains its statutory tax identity as follows: (1) incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act or register the federal corporation as an extra-provincial company in Alberta; (2) obtain the corporate registration number from the Alberta Corporate Registry; (3) apply to the CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and the Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) account (RT) through BRO or by filing Form RC1 (Request for Business Number Registration); (4) open the CBSA Import-Export (RM) account so that future export declarations for the medical ultrasound consoles can be transmitted through the G-13 Canadian Export Reporting System; (5) for a non-resident corporation, designate a Canadian representative and follow the ETA s.240(1) non-resident registration path, including the deemed-residency rule where applicable; (6) elect the GST/HST reporting period and fiscal year-end at registration; (7) verify the registration by searching the CRA GST/HST Registry.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a federal/Alberta Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is an independent taxpayer under both the Income Tax Act (ITA) and the Excise Tax Act (ETA); no pass-through treatment applies to the corporation; the Alberta permanent establishment is subject to Alberta corporate income tax at 8% in addition to the federal corporate rate (as applicable), while GST/HST is administered federally at the entity level.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election; corporate classification follows legal central management and control and the written constitutional documents. Relevant statutory elections for this exporter include: (a) the ETA s.156 joint election to treat taxable supplies between closely related corporations as made for no consideration, simplifying intercompany export-related flows; (b) the ETA s.273 election for a qualifying non-resident registrant to self-assess and claim rebates of GST/HST paid on Canadian goods and services; (c) the ITA s.93(1) foreign affiliate election for capital-gain purging where a foreign sales subsidiary is used; (d) Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status and Small Business Deduction elections made on the T2 Schedule 17 where the ownership and control tests are met; (e) hybrid-mismatch deductions are constrained by ITA ss.18.4-18.6 and Part VI.1, and no election is available to override those matching rules for import-of-IP or reverse-hybrid structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (the CRA small-supplier threshold under ETA s.148); a non-resident supplier making taxable supplies in Canada ceases to be a small supplier once worldwide gross revenue exceeds CAD 30,000, which triggers mandatory GST/HST registration under ETA s.240; voluntary registration is nevertheless available to the exporter to support input tax credit recovery and the application of the 0% export rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of the Color Doppler Ultrasound Console (ISIC 3250 / NAICS 334510) are zero-rated at 0% GST under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, Item 1 (supply of goods exported from Canada). To claim the zero rate, the supplier must: (i) sell the goods under a written export contract with a non-resident purchaser, (ii) physically ship the goods to a destination outside Canada, and (iii) hold contemporaneous documentary evidence of export within the two-year period mandated by ETA s.169(4). Where the console qualifies as a medical device for human use under the Medical Devices Regulations, domestic supplies to qualifying hospitals and healthcare institutions may also qualify for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part II; however, for cross-border sales the Part V export zero-rating route governs.

  • Canada Export Declaration (G-13 / CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System) containing the applicable Canadian Export Code for diagnostic imaging equipment
  • Commercial Invoice with export contract value, Incoterms, and a clear description of the Color Doppler Ultrasound Console
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Control Document evidencing physical departure of the goods from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign exchange from the overseas buyer
  • Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) or export permit where the ultrasound console is controlled as a medical device

Canada currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory fiscal API, and no digital stamping for GST/HST invoices; electronic invoicing is permitted and is being aligned on a voluntary basis with the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) adopted through the Digital Governance Council; all GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE), and invoices and export records must be retained in an accessible readable format for at least six years; exports must be reported to CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System (G-13) or an approved service provider, but no tax-authority pre-approval or digital clearance seal is required before export.

CRA scrutinizes zero-rated export claims through the Exports Verification Program and cross-checks customs declarations through CBSA. If export substantiation fails within the ETA s.169(4) two-year period, CRA deems the supply taxable at 5% GST, reassesses the unremitted tax with daily interest under ETA s.280, and claws back the input tax credits (ITCs) apportioned to the export; a gross-negligence penalty equal to 25% of the assessed tax amount (minimum CAD 250) applies under ETA s.285.1; knowingly false export documentation also exposes the exporter to penalties under ETA s.296 and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Criminal Code s.380. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within a commercially reasonable period (benchmarked at 180 days) may lead CRA to recharacterize the foreign receivable for income tax purposes, with transfer-pricing reassessment under ITA s.247(3) and associated adjustment penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full Article VIII IMF convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Profit remittance (dividends, interest, royalties, management fees) is unrestricted and requires no Bank of Canada authorization; the only statutory gate is Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (25% statutory, treaty-reduced to 5%–15% on dividends and typically 10%–15% on interest and royalties), administered via NR4 reporting and NR301/NR302 treaty forms. No foreign-investment review under the Investment Canada Act applies to this medical-device manufacturing scope, no minimum holding periods bind capital accounts, and foreign-currency balances may be held freely with domestic authorized banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain USD or other currencies in domestic or foreign bank accounts indefinitely. Compliance obligations are limited to: (i) documentary evidence retention under income and sales tax rules; (ii) Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA) licensing and destination screening where the buyer is sanctioned or the technology is controlled; and (iii) Statistics Canada export reporting for shipments above CAD 2,000. Medical imaging devices additionally require a Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) attesting to compliance with the Food and Drugs Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and systemic authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator for Schedule I/II banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/ATF supervisor under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). No dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration exists; exchange-rate and capital-flow policy is coordinated by the Department of Finance Canada under Canada's freely floating CAD regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Canada has no 'Electronic Money Institution' licence; foreign digital EMIs cannot access the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or Payments Canada clearing membership directly. An offshore EMI may operate through a sponsoring Schedule I bank (banking-as-a-service) or register as a money-service business (MSB) with FINTRAC for money transmission, but cannot take deposits or settle CAD. For this exporter, a direct Schedule I bank relationship is legally and operationally superior; the EMI channel is acceptable solely for ancillary receivables collection or merchant acquiring if FINTRAC MSB registration and Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) payment-service registration with the Bank of Canada are in place.
