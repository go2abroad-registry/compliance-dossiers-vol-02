Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada); e-portal: www.canada.ca/taxes — Business Registration Online (BRO); provincial counterpart: Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) at www.alberta.ca/taxes (Alberta corporate income tax is administered federally by CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Business Corporations Act via Corporations Canada or the Alberta Corporate Registry (RegistryX); obtain the certificate of incorporation and the Alberta Corporate Access Number. Step 2: Obtain a federal Business Number (BN) — the statutory business TIN — automatically upon integrated registration, or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or RC460 (non-resident accounts) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). Step 3: Activate the RT program account for GST/HST and the RP program account for corporate income tax within the same BN (BN15 format: 9-digit BN + 2-letter program code + 4-digit reference). Step 4: For importation of manufacturing components, activate the RM import/export program account with CRA/CBSA. Non-resident shareholders are permitted; no minimum capital requirement; no separate Alberta tax ID — the single CRA BN covers both federal and Alberta tax obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian resident corporation is a distinct taxpayer; worldwide income taxed federally at 15% general corporate rate plus Alberta at 8% general corporate rate, combined 23%, before applicable manufacturing/processing deductions; no pass-through regime and no tax-exempt zone in Alberta).

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (i) ITA s. 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfer of property to a corporation; (ii) ITA s. 216 election for non-residents on net rental income; (iii) ETA s. 273 election for closely related corporations (100% common ownership) to treat intercompany supplies as zero-rated for GST/HST purposes; (iv) treaty fiscally-transparent entity election under Article IV(6) of the Canada–US Tax Treaty (e.g., US check-the-box LLCs); (v) hybrid-mismatch deduction limitation/recharacterization under ITA s. 18.2/18.4; (vi) associated-corporation allocation of the Small Business Deduction under ITA s. 256.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148, computed on total taxable supplies made in Canada (including associates) in the four immediately preceding consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is triggered; voluntary registration is available to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Part I of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: (a) s. 1 — supply by way of sale of goods exported by the supplier; (b) s. 2 — supply delivered to a non-resident recipient for export; (c) s. 10 — goods exported within 60 days of delivery; (d) s. 5 — zero-rated supplies of goods to non-residents under specified conditions. Ultra-HD 4K rigid endoscopes / surgical laparoscopy towers also qualify as zero-rated medical devices under Part II, Schedule VI (para. 44, medical devices designed solely for human use) when supplied domestically, subject to CRA Device Availability listing. Export invoices must bear 0% GST, and input tax credits on embedded Canadian manufacturing inputs remain fully recoverable.

  • B13A Exports Declaration (filed electronically via CBSA eManifest/EDI) for controlled or high-value (>CAD 2,000) shipments
  • Commercial Invoice (zero-rated, 0% GST, HS 9018.90 medical instruments classification)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air) or Truck Waybill / freight forwarder pickup receipt
  • Packing List and country-of-origin certificate
  • Proof of Export (carrier's signed delivery receipt / customs stamp at destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — SWIFT MT103 / TT wire credit advice evidencing settlement of export proceeds

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based clearance mandate exists in Canada for B2B/B2G invoicing. CRA accepts standard EDI/XML/PDF invoices. Canada is progressively migrating federal procurement to Pan-Canadian e-invoicing under the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 framework (optional for general B2B). Export consignments require CBSA eManifest reporting and an electronic B13A Exports Declaration (via CBSA EDI/portal/API) for controlled goods or non-controlled exports exceeding CAD 2,000; all zero-rating evidence must be retained in the taxpayer's books.

CRA scrutiny focuses on bona fide export evidence, timely shipment (60-day export window), and arm's length pricing (ITA s. 247 transfer-pricing documentation). Where zero-rating is disallowed, CRA reassesses the full GST at 5% plus arrears interest and a s. 280 failure-to-remit penalty of 10%; false or grossly negligent export claims attract a s. 285 penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the misreported net tax. Retrospective clawback covers up to four years for misrepresentation and is without limitation period in cases of fraud or wilful evasion; criminal conviction under ETA s. 327 may carry fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years. Customs non-compliance is separately penalized under the AMPS regime (up to CAD 25,000 per violation).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted the obligations of Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, and the Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. There is no exchange-control approval for dividend remittances, shareholder loans, royalty payments, or capital repatriation. The operative remittance hurdles are: (i) 25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends (treaty-reduced, typically 5% to 15% under an applicable tax treaty); (ii) 25% branch profit tax under subsection 219(1) if operating through an Alberta branch; (iii) thin-capitalization and transfer-pricing rules under sections 18(4), 247, and 78 of the Income Tax Act; and (iv) FINTRAC reporting of every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more. Ultra-HD 4K rigid endoscopes (HS 9018.90) are freely importable and exportable and trigger no capital-account licensing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. Canadian law imposes no statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation threshold for export proceeds. An exporter may hold receipts indefinitely in USD, EUR, or CAD. Statutory compliance is limited to: (i) filing a Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for shipments of CAD 2,000 or more per line; (ii) FINTRAC reporting of any cross-border electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) retention of underlying commercial invoices, bills of lading, airway bills, customs entry records, and bank confirmations for CRA audits under section 230 of the Excise Tax Act and corresponding Income Tax Act records rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange regulatory oversight is shared with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting. Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange administration or capital-control licensing body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium for standalone treasury use. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Platform, Airwallex, and neobank virtual account providers cannot lawfully act as the primary clearing bank for an Alberta corporation unless they hold a FINTRAC money services business (MSB) or foreign money services business (FMSB) registration, operate a compliant AML/ATF program under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, appoint a Canadian agent for service, and settle through a Schedule I chartered bank. An offshore EMI cannot accept deposits or constitute a deposit-taking institution under the Bank Act without an OSFI federal banking licence. The treasury blueprint therefore mandates an RBC/TD/BMO/Scotiabank operational account as the primary rail, with EMIs permitted only as ancillary receivables-collection software for cross-border distributors, never as the legal settlement ledger.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign parent equity subscription or shareholder loan should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the home-country bank to the Schedule I operational account in Alberta. In the SWIFT narrative field, state the exact purpose: Capital contribution under the Alberta Business Corporations Act and board resolution dated [date], or Shareholder loan agreement dated [date]. Use Statistics Canada Balance of Payments direct investment purpose classification and include the corporate LEI in fields 50 and 52. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium for standalone treasury use. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Platform, Airwallex, and neobank virtual account providers cannot lawfully act as the primary clearing bank for an Alberta corporation unless they hold a FINTRAC money services business (MSB) or foreign money services business (FMSB) registration, operate a compliant AML/ATF program under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, appoint a Canadian agent for service, and settle through a Schedule I chartered bank. An offshore EMI cannot accept deposits or constitute a deposit-taking institution under the Bank Act without an OSFI federal banking licence. The treasury blueprint therefore mandates an RBC/TD/BMO/Scotiabank operational account as the primary rail, with EMIs permitted only as ancillary receivables-collection software for cross-border distributors, never as the legal settlement ledger.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 required where non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000 (or certain categories exceed CAD 50,000); Local File documentation required where controlled transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000; Master File required where controlled transactions exceed CAD 5,000,000; CRA may request documentation for any cross-border transaction regardless of thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies an economic substance doctrine equivalent to ESR through GAAR under s.245, transfer pricing recharacterization under s.247(2), place-of-effective-management (POEM), thin capitalization rules, mandatory disclosure rules, the BEPS MLI principal-purpose test, and CRA functional/risk analysis under the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; a shell entity without Canadian manufacturing substance will be recharacterized and profits reassessed to the Canadian permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Indictable offence under ITA s.239(1): fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction: fine up to 200% and imprisonment up to 2 years less a day; gross negligence penalty under s.163(2): 50% of understated tax; daily compounding interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted personally for fraud or tax evasion; personal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years; travel bans and restitution orders may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Recharacterization and GAAR

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under s.247(2), 10% transfer-pricing penalty, T106 penalties, documentation penalties up to CAD 50,000, GAAR 25% penalty on tax benefit; interest on all deficiencies.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for unremitted withholding and source deductions; corporate veil piercing for fraud or sham; criminal prosecution for fraudulent schemes.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Re-attribution of income to a Canadian permanent establishment, denial of treaty benefits, GAAR reassessment and 25% penalty, s.160 transfer liability, and collection against directors who authorized misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution under Alberta Business Corporations Act; liability under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted source deductions and s.155.1 for withholding amounts; imprisonment for fraud-based shell scheme.
