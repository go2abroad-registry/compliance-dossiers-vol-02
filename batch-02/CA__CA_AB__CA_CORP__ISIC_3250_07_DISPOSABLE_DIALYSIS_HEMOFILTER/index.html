Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the My Business Account portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html. For Alberta corporate income tax purposes, CRA acts as the collecting administrator for the Province of Alberta; there is no separate Alberta corporate tax filing portal for federally incorporated corporations carrying on business in Alberta.

E-government filing portal

After incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Alberta Business Corporations Act, the corporation's accountable officer must apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN), which is the statutory TIN. The application is completed through Business Registration Online (BRO) or CRA Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts. The CRA issues a 9-digit BN and program account suffixes, such as RT for GST/HST, RC for payroll, and RM for import or export activities. A non-resident corporation must evidence its Canadian legal existence or extra-provincial registration, may appoint a Canadian representative or agent, and must obtain the BN before carrying on taxable commercial activity in Canada. The same BN covers CRA-administered federal GST and Alberta corporate income tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian tax law does not permit a U.S.-style check-the-box election for a Business Corporation. A corporation incorporated under federal or Alberta law is an opaque, separate taxpayer for income tax purposes. Available elections relevant to a manufacturing corporation include an Income Tax Act subsection 85(1) rollover election for transfers of property to the corporation, a subsection 186(1) election to avoid dividend taxation on certain intercorporate payments, and a GST/HST closely-related corporation election under subsection 156(2) of the Excise Tax Act to treat supplies between qualifying related corporations as made for nil consideration. No elective mechanism exists to neutralize hybrid mismatches; the hybrid mismatch provisions apply mandatorily without an election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies. More precisely, a corporation is a small supplier and not required to register for GST/HST unless the total consideration for its taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports and excluding capital property and financial services, exceeds CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or CAD 30,000 in the preceding four calendar quarters. A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada that exceeds this threshold must register and charge 5% GST on taxable supplies made in Canada. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold, subject to CRA approval, and allows input tax credit recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a supply of goods is zero-rated when the goods are physically exported by the supplier or delivered outside Canada pursuant to a contract of sale, or are supplied to a common carrier, courier, or freight forwarder for shipment to a non-resident purchaser. For disposable dialysis hemodialyzers and hemofilters classified as medical devices, the sale qualifies for zero-rating only if the goods are shipped to a destination outside Canada and leave Canada in substantially the same condition as supplied. The commercial invoice and transport documents must identify the non-resident customer, the foreign port of discharge, and the true export value of the PES hollow-fiber dialyzers. Medical device services performed in Canada, such as sterilization, quality testing, warehousing, freight, or installation consulting that are performed for the foreign buyer, may be zero-rated as exported services only if all applicable statutory conditions are satisfied; if any part of the service is performed in Canada for assets located in Canada at the time, 5% GST generally applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA export declaration in the eManifest / Canadian Export Reporting System, formerly B13A), including the HS classification and export value of disposable hemodialyzers
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the foreign consignee and destination port or facility outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent foreign exchange payment evidence, including the SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or official banking record showing receipt from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice prepared in accordance with CRA invoice requirements and expressly marking the supply as zero-rated GST/HST export
  • Packing list with lot numbers, quantities, and product codes matching the export declaration for the dialysis hemofilter shipment
  • Carrier certificate, freight forwarder report, or port records confirming actual physical export and removal from Canada

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control system. There is no statutory electronic invoicing mandate under federal GST/HST law or Alberta law. CRA accepts paper, PDF, and EDI invoices, and optional API-based filing is available through CRA web services. GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed information: supplier legal name and Business Number, date of invoice, description and quantity of goods or services, consideration payable, and either the GST amount charged or, for zero-rated exports, an express statement that the supply is zero-rated. Exported invoices may be transmitted electronically to foreign buyers, but export documentation must be retained for six years.

CRA actively audits export zero-rating claims and may reverse the 0% rate if the exporter cannot produce the required export evidence. A reverse of zero-rating results in reassessment of 5% GST on the full invoice value, statutory interest from the original supply date, and ordinary late-filing or failure-to-register penalties. Gross negligence in maintaining false or inadequate export records can attract penalties equal to a significant percentage of the tax deemed collectible, in addition to the tax itself. Deliberate false statements causing a GST under-assessment may lead to prosecution under the Excise Tax Act, monetary fines, and criminal liability for persons knowingly participating in export fraud. The normal CRA reassessment window is four years from the assessment date, but is extended where misrepresentation, negligence, or wilful default is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized capital and current account convertibility regime under the IMF Articles of Rights and Obligations (Article VIII). There are no general exchange controls, no restrictions on the purchase or repatriation of foreign currency for trade or investment purposes, and no differentiated treatment of resident and non-resident capital accounts. Profit remittances in the form of dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees are permitted provided transfer pricing and withholding tax requirements are satisfied. For Alberta, there is no provincial currency convertibility restriction; however, fund transfers must be documented to comply with Canadian anti-money laundering and tax reporting rules for transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 and with the Canada Revenue Agency reporting thresholds for foreign accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in Canada for exporters to surrender export proceeds to a central authority, no mandatory conversion ratio to Canadian dollars, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency in authorized Canadian chartered bank accounts or in offshore accounts, provided the accounts are reported under the Foreign Income Verification Statement (T1135) where aggregate cost exceeds CAD 100,000 at any time in the year, and all transactions comply with the Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting Regulations for cash or bearer instruments exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Department of Finance Canada; exchange control functions are conducted within the framework of the Canadian Payments Act and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Canadian-regulated payment service providers and fintech entities offering foreign currency accounts and cross-border payment corridors may be used for operational disbursements and receivables collection. Non-resident Electronically Money Institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as banks in Canada and cannot provide a domestic Canadian clearing account or a Canadian banking license; however, foreign EMIs may open Far Eastern or U.S. dollar nostro-style accounts through Canadian chartered banks. The legal constraint is that a foreign EMI without a Canadian money-services-business (MSB) registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and provincial money-services licensing may not offer payment services to Canadian residents. For the Alberta dialysis filter manufacturer, a registered MSB or a Canadian Schedule I bank subsidiary is required for any deposit-taking or payment-account activity within Canada.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident equity or shareholder-loan capital must be wired through a Canadian Schedule I or Schedule II bank via SWIFT MT103 or SWIFT MT202, using the purpose code consistent with the Canada Revenue Agency and Bank of Canada reporting frameworks. Recommended wire instructions: correspondent-bank-to-Canadian-bank routing through major U.S. clearing hubs (JPMorgan, BNY Mellon, or HSBC) for U.S. dollars, or direct EUR/CAD payment via Canadian correspondent. The remitter must specify the beneficiary account as the Alberta corporate escrow or operating account and include the corporate legal name, Alberta corporation number, and the purpose code 'CND - Capital Contribution/Equity Injection' or the IMF Balance of Payments code for direct investment (DI). For amounts above CAD 10,000, the bank will complete FINTRAC large-transaction reports. For amounts above CAD 100,000, the Canadian resident corporation must report the foreign-source capital under T1135 if held in foreign accounts, but no prior approval from the Bank of Canada is required. The routing should avoid third-party aggregation accounts; the beneficial-owner name must match the Canadian bank account title to prevent returns.Outbound settlements for raw materials, equipment, and trade liabilities should use SWIFT MT103 for high-value direct wire transfers with full invoice references and Incoterms. Open-account trade settlements may be executed via SWIFT MT103 for amounts below CAD 200,000 or via Continuous Linked Settlement (CLS) for same-day Canadian dollar and major FX currencies. All outbound wires must include an ISO 20022 purpose code, for example 'TRADE' for trade-related payments, and must quote the underlying invoice and purchase-order numbers to avoid AML review of unexplained outflows.
