Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — National Headquarters, 555 MacKenzie Avenue, Ottawa, Ontario K2P 2K1; online business portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and GST/HST NETFILE (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/gst-hst-netfile.html). Alberta Corporate Income Tax is also administered by the CRA under a single federal-provincial reporting system, with the Alberta tax administered through the same Business Number.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation manufacturing or supplying sterile examination nitrile gloves into Canada must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Procedure: (i) prepare Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts', designating the federal income tax corporate account, GST/HST account, and Alberta corporate tax account; (ii) file RC1 online through Represent a Client, by fax, or by mail to Instruction Office or International Tax Services Office (1241 Michel North, Ottawa, ON K1A 0E9); (iii) provide supporting corporate existence or registration documents such as the foreign certificate of incorporation, articles, and notarised registration in the Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES); (iv) receive the BN and tax program identifiers, including the GST/HST account number; (v) register a Canadian agent or representative with T600 authority for notices. A non-resident vendor located outside Canada may also use the simplified non-resident registration process under the CRA Online Registration Service, effective within 10 business days; however, if the vendor supplies goods from a Canadian inventory or under a permanent establishment in Alberta, immediate mandatory registration applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a business corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Alberta Business Corporations Act is an opaque separate taxpayer; no pass-through eligibility is available for an ordinary Inc./Corp. in Canada; non-resident corporations are taxed under Part I on Canadian-source business income and on the disposition of Canadian business or taxable Canadian property, while an Alberta-incorporated resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income).

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. All Business Corporations are categorically taxable entities under the Income Tax Act. Relevant statutory elections affecting corporate tax identity include: (i) section 85 rollover election for non-residents transferring property to a Canadian corporation (Form T2057); (ii) section 216 election allowing a non-resident to be taxed on a net basis for Canadian rental income; (iii) subsection 256(9) elections concerning foreign-owned investment corporations and deemed residency where a Canadian corporation is created solely for tax abatement purposes; (iv) hybrid mismatch reporting and allocation elections governed by sections 18.2–18.7 for cross-border arrangements, including determination notices for a hybrid entity or branch. Under the Alberta corporate tax regime, no additional provincial entity elections exist beyond the CRA-federal program accounts; however, the corporate allocation of taxable income for interprovincial and treaty purposes is determined through the permanent establishment formula.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold). A non-resident supplier of medical nitrile gloves is required to register for GST/HST if the aggregate value of worldwide taxable supplies and those arising in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in any consecutive four-calendar-quarter period. Where the non-resident conducts business through a permanent establishment in Alberta or holds an Alberta inventory for retail sale, mandatory registration arises irrespective of the CAD 30,000 threshold; for suppliers of remote services and digital products, CRA also applies the CAD 30,000 threshold to non-resident sellers through the simplified cross-border GST/HST registration framework.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, a supply of sterile examination nitrile gloves is zero-rated for GST/HST purposes where (i) the supplier is a GST/HST registrant, (ii) the gloves are sold to a non-resident recipient or shipped to a non-resident address outside Canada, (iii) the goods are actually exported from Canada within the prescribed export timeline, generally within 60 days after the time of supply for ordinary goods (CRA administrative practice), and (iv) the supplier retains proof of export in the form of transport or customhouse documentation. Medical gloves exported directly by a rubber-type manufacturer or via an export agent also qualify where the supplier or agent actually removes the goods; zero-rating is not available for goods that are subsequently brought back into Canada or substantially altered in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Form G49 'Canadian Export Declaration' filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) / Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) portal, including the 17-digit export reference number and HS code 4015.12 or 4015.19 not otherwise covered by a zero-rating exception)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air waybill, with the destination address outside Canada, port of exit and consignee details evidencing physical removal from Canadian jurisdiction)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (a bank receipt or SWIFT/MT103 documentation showing payment in foreign currency or Canadian dollars from the non-resident buyer, matched against the export contract and customs declaration)

Canada does not maintain a real-time fiscal clearance system or digital stamping/continuous control regime. The Excise Tax Act requires that taxable suppliers provide invoices showing the seller's Business Number, the words 'Reg/GST' with the applicable GST registration number, the transaction date, vendor and purchaser names, the total amount, and the stated GST/HST charged. GST/HST returns are filed through the CRA's GST/HST NETFILE protocol using XML/API submission, or by using third-party accounting software connected to CRA's Represent a Client (RaC) service. There is no pre-approval, invoicing licence, or central validation of invoice numbers; however, electronic invoices and the audit trail must be rendered in a technology-neutral format that an auditor can convert to text. Canadian exporters must keep all customs and commercial evidence electronically accessible for the seven-year CRA retention period.

The CRA may disallow a zero-rating claim where the exporter cannot provide satisfactory proof of physical export, freight terms, legal custody transfer, or remittance from outside Canada; the result is an assessment of 5% GST on the gross selling price, plus interest and administrative penalties. Under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act, a gross negligence penalty of 25% of any tax understated may be assessed; for fraudulent claims or dishonest statements, the Income Tax Act section 239.1 penalties include fines of up to 50% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. CBSA can impose administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 25,000 per export transaction for non-reporting, false declarations, or illegal pass-backs, including seizure and forfeiture of goods. Reassessments for ordinary misrepresentations can reach back to the previous four calendar years; the limitation period is unlimited where fraud or negligence is proven, enabling retrospective clawback of refunds, interest and penalties for zero-rated exports that fail substantive compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada affords full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; the Canadian dollar (CAD) is free-floating with no exchange-rate approval, profit-remittance permit, or non-resident capitalization requirement. Practical hurdles for a foreign parent are tax-driven rather than FX-driven: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on outbound dividends (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., Canada-US 15%/5% and Canada-China 15%/10% depending on ownership); thin-capitalization and transfer-pricing recharacterization of non-arm's-length shareholder debt (CRA Form T106); and the general anti-avoidance rule. Sale proceeds, loan repayments, and capital reductions are freely repatriable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Canada. Exporters of sterile examination gloves may retain, invoice, and hold proceeds in CAD or any foreign currency indefinitely. Reporting obligations are limited to: (i) FINTRAC large-cash and cross-border EFT reports for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) CBSA export documentation and Statistics Canada trade reporting for controlled or valued goods; (iii) CRA Form T106 transfer-pricing disclosure where exports are made to related non-resident distributors; and (iv) IFRS/CAS financial-statement recognition of monetary assets.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; prudential supervision of chartered banks rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); FINTRAC enforces anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administrative body; FX freedom is grounded in the Currency Act and the Bank Act, with market-determined exchange rates.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-Low. Canadian-regulated non-bank fintechs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Corpay) may operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) or, from November 2024, as licensed Retail Payment Activity (RPA) providers. They are legally unable to accept deposits as chartered banks, issue letters of credit, provide real credit facilities, or hold segregated escrow; funds reside in pooled bank accounts with structural counterparty risk. They remain viable only for low-to-mid-value operational payables/receivables and cross-currency conversion, not as the primary treasury, LC, escrow, or hedging rail for a medical-PPE manufacturer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder debt from a foreign parent is wired via SWIFT MT103 with a transparent purpose narrative mapped to ISO/UN CEFACT categories (e.g., 'CAPS - Capital Subscription', 'FDLT - Shareholder Loan Disbursement'). Canada does not operate a Chinese-style purpose-code or FX conversion system; the receiving bank evaluates the narrative, supporting corporate resolutions, and the issued-share ledger for FINTRAC-based EDD. Where investment acquires control of an existing Canadian active business, an Investment Canada Act notification is required (WTO-investor net-benefit review threshold is approximately CAD 1.3-1.4 billion, making a mid-sized glove plant notifiable but not reviewable). Payments route to the institution-transit-account plus bank BIC (no IBAN exists in Canada). Funded amounts must be booked as paid-up capital or formal shareholder loans, with Part XIII withholding and Form NR301 treaty-relief documentation applicable on subsequent interest or dividend distributions.Outbound clearing rails: (i) open-account SWIFT MT103 wires to suppliers with purpose codes such as 'GDDS' or 'Purchase of Goods'; (ii) irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) for capital equipment (nitrile dipping lines, ovens, chlorination arrays), commonly 60-120 days sight with presentation of commercial invoice, clean-on-board bills of lading, packing list, and inspection certificate; (iii) SWIFT MT760 standby LCs for advance-payment guarantees; (iv) documentary collections (CAD basis) for recurring latex and polymer shipments; and (v) domestic CAD settlement via Lynx (large-value) and ACSS (retail), with USD transactions cleared through the Canadian bank's US correspondent via CHIPS/Fedwire. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for goods, CAD 500,000 for services, CAD 200,000 for intangibles; also required if gross revenue exceeds CAD 10 million and transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) applies to deny tax benefits from transactions lacking economic substance. Substance over form is required for intercompany arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s. 227.1) if they failed to withhold or remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for willful evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit, and potential criminal charges for fraudulent misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to the arrangement; travel bans and asset freezes under tax enforcement.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly failed to implement compliance programs; civil penalties and potential criminal liability.
