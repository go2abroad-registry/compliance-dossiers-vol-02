Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is permissible for the registered office element of an Alberta business corporation, provided the registered office address is located in Alberta, mail-forwarding is operational, and corporate records are accessible at that address during business hours. A virtual office alone will not satisfy Health Canada licensing, product liability insurer, or commercial bank KYB expectations. At minimum, a leased manufacturing, QA, and warehousing facility in Alberta is required for substantive commercial presence.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; provincial corporate income tax for Alberta is also collected by the CRA under a tax collection agreement; Alberta Corporate Registry for extra-provincial corporate registration; CBSA for customs and export declaration processing.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must: (1) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 or applying online through the 'My Business Account' portal; (2) register for GST/HST if worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or if the entity voluntarily registers; (3) if operating in Alberta through a physical presence or permanent establishment, complete extra-provincial registration under the Alberta Business Corporations Act and obtain an Alberta Corporate Access Number; (4) where importing or exporting goods, open a CBSA import/export account (linked to the BN) and obtain a Canadian customs client identifier; (5) for non-resident corporate status, submit relevant treaty/withholding documentation to the CRA if a waiver of Part XIII withholding is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not permit a U.S.-style check-the-box election; legal form determines tax classification. A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to federal and provincial income tax. Available statutory elections include: (i) GST/HST joint election under ETA s. 273 to treat related corporations as a single registrant; (ii) ETA s. 156 election for closely related persons to have taxable supplies at nil consideration; (iii) S. 216 election for non-residents with Canadian employment income reporting; and hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.5 apply to recharacterize certain cross-border arrangements, but no elective hybrid classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports?) measured over any calendar quarter; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. Small suppliers are exempt from mandatory registration until the threshold is breached. Voluntary registration is available for businesses making zero-rated exports to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported by the supplier to a non-resident person are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (ETA) if the supplier has evidence of export within the prescribed time frame (generally 60 days). Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated where the service is acquired for use mainly outside Canada and is not performed in Canada to a substantial extent (or is performed only for a non-resident). For medical device components such as amorphous silicon cesium iodide flat panel detectors, zero-rating applies only where the goods are exported as part of a commercial activity and are not excluded by Schedule VI Part IV (some medical devices may be exempt when supplied domestically, but exports are generally zero-rated).

  • Commercial invoice / Canada Customs Invoice
  • Canadian Export Declaration (via CBSA CERS or authorized service provider)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Cargo control and customs clearance records
  • Proof of payment (SWIFT MT103, letter of credit, telegraphic transfer advice) and inward remittance record where applicable
  • CRA Form GST11 or GST189 for refund applications (where input tax credits exceed liabilities), and internal audit trail of the zero-rating election

Canada has no mandatory government real-time invoice clearance or digital stamping regime at the federal level. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information (ETA s. 3, Sch. V? actually s. 223 and Regulations). Electronic invoicing is permitted; records must be retained and accessible electronically for CRA inspection. For exports, the CBSA requires electronic pre-export reporting via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) under the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) platform, with export declarations transmitted through the Canadian Customs Commercial System.

CRA may reassess to disallow zero-rating where export documentation is insufficient or the export does not occur within the required period, resulting in uncollected GST/HST liability, interest, and penalties. Under ETA s. 280, failure to collect or remit can attract penalties up to 15% of the amount; under s. 285, false statements or omissions may incur penalties equal to 10% of the net tax (or greater for knowingly fraudulent filings). Willful evasion can lead to fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount (under s. 327, a fine up to CAD 10,000 per count for summary conviction or CAD 50,000 per count for indictable? actual statutory max? To be safe: significant fines) and imprisonment. Retrospective clawback periods can extend up to 4 years for ordinary reassessments, or beyond 4 years where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is shown.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. There is no exchange-control approval for profit remittances, dividends, or cross-border capital movements. Repatriation of earnings is free in CAD or any permitted foreign currency, subject only to corporate-law solvency tests, AML/KYC verification, and Part XIII Canadian non-resident withholding tax (typically reduced by tax treaty).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender or conversion ratios. No mandatory timeline or conversion obligation applies to export receipts; proceeds may be retained in CAD or any foreign currency. Export documentation (customs declarations, commercial invoices, bills of lading) must however be retained, and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) requires accurate trade records for duty and tax verification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); no dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority due to Canada's liberal exchange-control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMI entities are not permitted to take deposits or hold CAD clearing accounts as deposit institutions under the Bank Act. They may operate in Canada only as registered payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) or applicable provincial money-services licensing, with FINTRAC AML obligations. Such accounts can support settlement flows but cannot serve as statutory capital escrow or payroll accounts for an Alberta-incorporated business corporation.
