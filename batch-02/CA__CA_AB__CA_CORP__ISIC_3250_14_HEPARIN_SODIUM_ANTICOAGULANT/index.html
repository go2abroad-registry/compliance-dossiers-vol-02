Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Agence du revenu du Canada; electronic portal: 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html); for customs/export control: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Alberta business corporation: (1) incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (or extra-provincially register a foreign corporation if applicable) to obtain a Certificate of Incorporation and corporate access number; (2) apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 via My Business Account or by mail; (3) activate the required program accounts: RT (corporation income tax), RC (GST/HST), RP (payroll if employees), and RM (import/export) as needed; (4) for a foreign entity that itself carries on business in Canada as a branch, submit certified foreign registration documents, a Canadian address for service, and a resident representative/agent under Income Tax Act s.170; CRA will issue a BN plus program accounts. A non-resident shareholder that only holds shares of the Canadian corporation is not required to obtain a Canadian TIN, but if it receives dividends it is subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax administered through CRA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a resident corporation is taxed separately from its shareholders on worldwide income (with foreign tax credits); no pass-through treatment; Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered by CRA under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Available elections include: (i) Excise Tax Act s.156 GST/HST group registration for closely related corporations (single registrant); (ii) ITA s.216 election allowing a non-resident to file a Canadian return on gross Canadian-source income instead of Part XIII withholding; (iii) ITA s.217 election for non-residents on certain Canadian-source payments; (iv) ITA s.85 rollover election when transferring property into a corporation (e.g., reorganisation of a heparin manufacturing business); (v) ITA s.256 election to allocate the small business limit among associated CCPCs. For hybrid mismatches, Canada applies the OECD hybrid mismatch rules codified at ITA s.18.6; there is no elective departure from the default opacity/transparency alignment, and designated taxpayers must file prescribed information for reportable arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of gross worldwide taxable supplies in any single calendar quarter or over the four immediately preceding consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); a non-resident that is not a small supplier and carries on a business in Canada must register. Exceeding the threshold triggers mandatory GST/HST registration for the Canadian corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, a supply of tangible personal property (including heparin sodium API) is zero-rated where the registrant ships the goods to a destination outside Canada by its own or a common carrier, or supplies them to a non-resident person for export, and retains evidence of export. Also zero-rated: services supplied to a non-resident if not in respect of Canadian real estate or tangible personal property located in Canada and performed outside Canada. Canada does not legislate a fixed FX repatriation deadline; a 180-day documentation benchmark is applied by exporters as good commercial practice to preserve the zero-rating file.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic CEES/CAED filing)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air/rail/road waybill)
  • Packing Slip
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or authenticated SWIFT wire / Letter of Credit confirmation evidencing receipt in Canadian currency
  • Certificate of Analysis and Customs/Health Canada export licence, if applicable for heparin API

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. GST/HST invoices (electronic or paper) must contain the supplier's name and BN, invoice date, buyer name/address, description of supplies, tax amount and consideration. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE. For exports, CBSA requires electronic export reporting via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CEES/CAED) for goods valued above CAD 2,000; CBSA supports API/EDIFACT integration for authorized exporters. CRA does not pre-clear invoices; compliance is enforced through post-filing audits and electronic records access.

CRA may challenge zero-rated export claims within the normal 4-year reassessment period (unlimited where fraud or gross negligence/misrepresentation is established). If the documentary evidence is insufficient, CRA reassesses the supply at 5% GST, recomputes input tax credits, charges interest at prescribed rates, and imposes penalties under Excise Tax Act s.285 (greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax sought to be avoided) for false or misleading statements; gross negligence penalties can rise further under the Income Tax Act. Retrospective clawback can include denial of export-related input credits. CBSA can also penalize failure to file electronic export declarations under the Customs Act, with fines up to CAD 25,000 or the undeclared export value.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations (since 1951); the Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts with no foreign exchange control legislation in force since the abolition of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951. The Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and Bank of Canada Act establish CAD as legal tender, but parties may freely contract payments in any currency. There is no requirement for prior regulatory approval, licensing, or registration for capital inflows, dividend remittances, intercompany loans, or royalty payments. Profit remittance is nevertheless subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) at a statutory rate of 25%, generally reduced to 5%–15% under an applicable tax treaty (e.g., Canada–U.S. treaty for dividends). Heparin API export receipts may be retained in foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, conversion ratio, or nostro account requirement on export proceeds. Export filings under the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) are required for goods shipped out of Canada valued at over CAD 2,000 per transaction, and the exporter must classify Heparin Sodium under HS Chapter 30 (3001.90, pharmaceutical preparations for therapeutic use). Records supporting export valuations must be retained for six years under section 230 of the Income Tax Act, and all cross-border financial transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 initiated or received in a 24-hour period are reportable to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), 234 Wellington Street, Ottawa, ON K1A 0G9, operating under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); prudential regulation of banks and deposit-taking institutions is performed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); AML/ATF supervision and financial intelligence are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA, S.C. 2000, c. 17).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury use; Medium as a supplementary corridor for small operational FX. Canadian payment service providers (PSPs) must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23), with mandatory registration in force from September 30, 2025; unregistered digital EMIs are barred from providing payment services in Canada. Offshore EMIs (EU-licensed or UK-licensed) cannot accept CAD deposits or open Canadian settlement accounts unless licensed or authorized under the Bank Act, and are treated as unregulated foreign money transmitters, triggering elevated FINTRAC scrutiny and capital preservation risk. Chartered bank rails (SWIFT MT103, LC) are required for export proceeds and trade disbursements; EMIs such as Wise can support minor corridor conversions below 10% of monthly receipts but must not be used for capital escrow, LC settlement, or hedge collateral.

Offshore EMIs (EU-licensed or UK-licensed) cannot accept CAD deposits or open Canadian settlement accounts unless licensed or authorized under the Bank Act, and are treated as unregulated foreign money transmitters, triggering elevated FINTRAC scrutiny and capital preservation risk. Chartered bank rails (SWIFT MT103, LC) are required for export proceeds and trade disbursements; EMIs such as Wise can support minor corridor conversions below 10% of monthly receipts but must not be used for capital escrow, LC settlement, or hedge collateral.
