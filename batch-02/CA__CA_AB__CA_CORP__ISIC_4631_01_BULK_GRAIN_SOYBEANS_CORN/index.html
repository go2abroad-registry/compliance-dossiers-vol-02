Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC). Primary portals: 'My Business Account' (corporate tax and GST/HST accounts), 'GST/HST NETFILE' (electronic return filing), 'Business Registration Online' (BN/RT/RC account creation), and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) 'CARM client portal / Canadian Single Window (G7)' for customs and export declarations. Alberta corporate income tax (11% provincial rate) is administered by CRA on behalf of Alberta under the Tax Collection Agreement; Alberta has no provincial sales tax and no separate sales-tax authority. Corporate registry: Alberta Corporate Registry (Corporate Access Number system).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta business corporation: (1) incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (or Canada Business Corporations Act) through the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain legal existence and a Corporate Access Number; (2) apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN), which is the statutory TIN for all federal tax purposes, using Business Registration Online or Form RC1; (3) open CRA program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll, if staff employed); (4) no separate Alberta corporate tax registration is required because CRA collects Alberta corporate income tax under the Tax Collection Agreement; (5) file ownership/non-resident disclosure and maintain a Canadian registered office and resident agent; no minimum capital requirement; (6) if acting as a grain dealer or elevator operator, obtain a Canadian Grain Commission licence under the Canada Grain Act (a pure brokerage commission model without title transfer does not require such licence); (7) the RT program account number (BN + 'RT' + 4-digit suffix) functions as the VAT/GST registration ID and must appear on all invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (ITA), with no pass-through classification available. It is taxed under Part I on worldwide income at the federal general corporate rate of 15% (38% basic rate less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction), plus Alberta corporate income tax at 11%, for a combined headline rate of 26% on active business income. Non-resident shareholders are not taxed on Canadian active business profits except through Part XIII dividend withholding (25% statutory, subject to treaty reduction). Interprovincial profit allocation to Alberta follows the Alberta Corporate Tax Act and the Alberta Allocation Regulation. — For a foreign-owned Alberta business corporation: (1) incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (or Canada Business Corporations Act) through the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain legal existence and a Corporate Access Number; (2) apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN), which is the statutory TIN for all federal tax purposes, using Business Registration Online or Form RC1; (3) open CRA program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll, if staff employed); (4) no separate Alberta corporate tax registration is required because CRA collects Alberta corporate income tax under the Tax Collection Agreement; (5) file ownership/non-resident disclosure and maintain a Canadian registered office and resident agent; no minimum capital requirement; (6) if acting as a grain dealer or elevator operator, obtain a Canadian Grain Commission licence under the Canada Grain Act (a pure brokerage commission model without title transfer does not require such licence); (7) the RT program account number (BN + 'RT' + 4-digit suffix) functions as the VAT/GST registration ID and must appear on all invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (ITA), with no pass-through classification available. It is taxed under Part I on worldwide income at the federal general corporate rate of 15% (38% basic rate less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction), plus Alberta corporate income tax at 11%, for a combined headline rate of 26% on active business income. Non-resident shareholders are not taxed on Canadian active business profits except through Part XIII dividend withholding (25% statutory, subject to treaty reduction). Interprovincial profit allocation to Alberta follows the Alberta Corporate Tax Act and the Alberta Allocation Regulation.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory. Pertinent elections and mandatory regimes: (1) ITA section 261 functional-currency election (permit a Canadian corporation to compute Canadian tax in US dollars); (2) ITA subsection 216(1) election for a non-resident to file a Part I return on net Canadian rental income instead of Part XIII withholding; (3) Excise Tax Act (ETA) section 156 election among closely related registrants to treat qualifying inter-corporate supplies as non-supplies (eliminating double GST/HST); (4) ETA subsection 240(2.1) voluntary GST/HST registration for small suppliers, including non-residents, to enable zero-rating and input tax credit recovery; (5) ETA Part IX elections for annual/quarterly filing and the quick method of accounting; (6) hybrid mismatch neutralization is mandatory and non-elective - the excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) under ITA sections 18.2 and 18.3 (effective for tax years beginning after 30 September 2023) and hybrid instrument/branch mismatch rules under ITA sections 18.4 and 18.5, with no opt-out election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small-supplier threshold under ETA subsection 148(1): an Alberta corporation must register for GST/HST once its total taxable supplies made in Canada (including zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 over any four consecutive calendar quarters. Registration is mandatory above the threshold and voluntary below it; voluntary registration is strongly advisable for an exporter to recover input tax credits and to document zero-rated export sales. Non-resident suppliers of cross-border digital services and short-term accommodation are subject to a parallel CAD 30,000 registration threshold under the ETA Division IV.1 simplified regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property (bulk soybeans, HS 1201; yellow corn, HS 1005) are zero-rated under ETA section 145 and Schedule VI Part V when the supplier, or the supplier's agent, physically exports the grain 'as soon as is reasonable' after the supply (CRA administrative practice accepts documentary proof of shipment typically within 60-120 days). Conditions: (a) sale by the Canadian vendor to a non-resident purchaser - sales to Canadian residents for foreign export do not automatically qualify; (b) commercial terms, title/risk transfer, and payment evidence consistent with a genuine export sale; (c) physical removal from Canada, including through-transit shipments via US ports, which must be documented with US customs in-bond records and outbound vessel manifests to the final foreign destination - US warehousing without a clear in-bond transit will disqualify zero-rating; (d) vessel loading of bulk grain in conformity with CBSA outbound reporting and Canadian Grain Commission official inspection/weighing obligations. Commission/brokerage services supplied to non-resident grain principals are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V section 7 where the services do not relate to real or tangible property situated in Canada and the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST.

  • CBSA electronic export declaration (Form B13A / CARM portal outbound report, e-manifest) endorsed by CBSA
  • Original negotiable Bill of Lading (marine) or multimodal waybill naming the non-resident buyer/consignee and foreign discharge port
  • Commercial invoice (Canada Customs Invoice dataset) with full commodity description, HS 1005/1201, grade, weight, and FOB value
  • Canadian Grain Commission official inspection certificate and, if applicable, CGC export permit / grain dealer licence data
  • Loading-port survey report, vessel nomination, sail date certificate, and outbound cargo manifest from the Canadian load port
  • Payment evidence: SWIFT MT103 credit and copy of the irrevocable transferable letter of credit, linking proceeds to the specific export invoice
  • Freight forwarder's certificate of export or carrier's certificate evidencing physical shipment from Canada within a reasonable period
  • Bank inward remittance advice / inward remittance certificate and foreign exchange confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • For through-US transit exports: US Customs entry or in-bond (IT) number, US outbound ocean manifest to the final foreign port, and foreign port arrival/discharge certificate

Canada does not mandate continuous transaction control (CTC), real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or tax-authority invoice approval. Since 2023 the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing standard is available on a voluntary basis for procurement interoperability. Mandatory electronic reporting applies to: (1) GST/HST returns, which must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or EDI for most registrants; (2) customs and export declarations, which must be filed electronically with CBSA through the CARM client portal / Canadian Single Window (outbound cargo reports, e-manifest, Form B13A data); (3) GST/HST invoices, which must contain the supplier's BN/RT number, invoice date, description, tax amount, and total under ETA subsection 223(1). No digital stamping or pre-clearance certification is required.

CRA export verification programs and CBSA export-report cross-checks heavily audit bulk-commodity zero-rating. Where export substantiation fails, goods are diverted or re-imported, or grain is warehoused in the US without in-bond transit, CRA will reassess GST/HST at 5% of the full selling price and add compounding daily arrears interest from the reporting-period due date. Penalties include: (1) 20% false-claim penalty under ETA section 281.1 (minimum CAD 100); (2) gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the tax, minimum CAD 250, under ETA section 285.1; (3) evasion penalties - fine of 50%-200% (summary) or 100%-250% (indictment) of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment under ETA section 280; (4) retroactive reassessment beyond the normal four-year limitation period up to ten years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default (ETA subsection 298(2)); (5) denial and repayment of input tax credits on grain purchases and related freight; (6) administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 25,000 per false CBSA export declaration under the Customs Act AMPS regime; (7) Canada Grain Act offences for false documentation, suspension/revocation of grain dealer/elevator licences, and forfeiture of grain or proceeds. Related-party export sales are additionally tested under transfer-pricing arm's-length rules (ITA section 247), and intercompany receivables outstanding beyond 180 days may trigger benefit or deemed-interest assessments under ITA sections 15, 17, and 80.4, independent of the GST/HST audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada imposes no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions. Foreign direct investment, dividend distributions, profit remittances, interest payments, royalties, and liquidation proceeds may be freely converted and remitted in CAD or foreign currency. Compliance burdens remain exclusively AML/KYC and sanctions-related; there are no convertibility approval gates or repatriation quotas.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada does not impose statutory surrender timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds. Grain, soybean, and corn exporters may retain receivables in USD or other trading currencies, repatriate at commercial maturity, and convert through authorized Canadian banks without central-bank permission.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; monetary policy and bank regulation are led by the Bank of Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while FINTRAC enforces anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium practical viability only as an ancillary payment conduit; Low viability as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs cannot act as Canadian deposit-taking institutions or issue letters of credit unless they operate through a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank. Platforms registered in Canada as money services businesses (MSBs) are FINTRAC-reporting entities but are not Lynx/SWIFT clearing members and are not CDIC-insured. Such services are acceptable for low-value FX transfers and expense payments, but bulk grain and soybean settlements should remain on fully regulated bank LC and SWIFT rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital in CAD or USD must be routed by SWIFT MT103 using the Canadian beneficiary bank's SWIFT BIC (e.g., ROYCCAT2, TDOMCATTTOR, BOFMCAM2). Field 70 narrative should clearly state 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION CAPITAL INJECTION' and include remitter legal name, address, and contract or resolution reference. The receiving bank will require a board resolution authorizing share issuance, subscription agreement, applicable federal/Alberta securities filing or exemption, updated UBO declarations, and source-of-funds evidence. For documentary shipments, Canadian Schedule I banks issue or advise LCs, and the presenting bank receives grade certificates, phytosanitary certificates, vessel freight documents, weight certificates, and commercial invoices. High-value CAD obligations settle in real time via Lynx, Bank of Canada's RTGS successor to LVTS. USD payments are cleared through US correspondent banking accounts of the Canadian bank; EUR payments clear via TARGET2.
