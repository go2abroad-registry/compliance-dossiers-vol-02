Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Alberta Business Corporation; shares may be issued with or without par value for the consideration fixed by the directors under ABCA s. 34. No escrow or guarantee account is required for a non-reporting issuer; subscription funds may be held in a solicitors' trust account but must be transferred to the Corporation promptly at closing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; primary portal: My Business Account (MyBA) at canada.ca; customs accounting and export declarations via Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CARM/CAERS digital platforms; Alberta provincial corporate tax administered jointly with CRA under the Provincial Tax Collection Agreement.

E-government filing portal

Register through the CRA 'Register My Business' web service or file Form RC1 to obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN). The entity opens the 'RT' GST/HST program account (suffix 'RT0001') at the same time; where physical imports/exports occur, activate the 'RP' Import/Export account through the CBSA. Non-resident corporations may self-register via the CRA Non-Resident GST/HST portal; a non-resident is required to register when carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies in excess of the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, and may voluntarily register to zero-rate export supplies and claim input tax credits.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Register through the CRA 'Register My Business' web service or file Form RC1 to obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN). The entity opens the 'RT' GST/HST program account (suffix 'RT0001') at the same time; where physical imports/exports occur, activate the 'RP' Import/Export account through the CBSA. Non-resident corporations may self-register via the CRA Non-Resident GST/HST portal; a non-resident is required to register when carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies in excess of the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, and may voluntarily register to zero-rate export supplies and claim input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box exists in Canada; entity classification follows the Income Tax Act and provincial corporate statutes. Relevant elections: (i) s. 216(1) ITA election for non-resident owners of Canadian rental real property to be taxed on net rental income; (ii) GST/HST elections for voluntary registration effective date, annual filing and quick method (s. 227 ETA); (iii) s. 18(4) ITA anti-hybrid election and structured-financing elections under ss. 18.2/18.3 ITA prospectively neutralizing hybrid mismatch outcomes; (iv) treaty residency/business-income elections under Article IV of the Canada-US Tax Treaty for dual-resident corporations; and (v) the CRA's factual 'entity classification' ruling process for foreign limited liability vehicles, which does not involve elective check-the-box filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000; s. 148(1)(a) ETA small-supplier threshold measured over a single calendar quarter or any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters of worldwide taxable supplies. Registration becomes mandatory the day the threshold is exceeded, though persons whose supplies are exclusively zero-rated can remain unregistered; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST if the threshold is met, and may register voluntarily to recover input tax credits on export-related costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Raw cane sugar exported by vessel qualifies for 0% GST/HST under Item 1, Part V, Schedule VI ETA where the supplier is a GST/HST registrant, the tangible personal property is exported as part of the sale (i.e., delivery to the carrier for export is in the sale terms), and the goods are exported within 60 days of the sale, or within one year where the registrant took reasonable measures to export. VHP sugar imported under a customs bonded warehouse (ss. 87/88 ETA) can be withdrawn for export without GST payable. Ancillary services - international freight, stevedoring, weighing, vessel-loading inspection, and polarization/ICUMSA lab analysis performed in Canada in respect of the exported parcel - are separately zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI (ss. 5-7) where supplied to a non-resident. Supplies made to a non-registered Canadian purchaser of imported sugar are taxable at 5% GST unless the sugar is immediately exported.

  • CBSA electronic Canadian Export Declaration (CAERS/e-manifest) - Form B13A equivalent
  • Ocean Bill of Lading evidencing vessel shipment from a Canadian port to the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and sale contract referencing the seller's GST/HST registration (BN-RT)
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank SWIFT or LC settlement advice showing foreign currency proceeds credited to a Canadian bank account
  • CBSA Form A4 / bonded-warehouse export withdrawal record for imported VHP sugar re-exported in bond
  • Third-party polarization certificate and weight certificate (draft survey/port certificate of weight) for the vessel parcel

No federal real-time fiscal clearance/e-invoicing mandate exists in Canada; CRA does not operate an API-based continuous transaction control system. GST/HST invoices must contain the supplier's BN/RT number and statutory particulars required by s. 223 ETA. For all vessel exports, CBSA requires electronic export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) and, for import/export accounting and duty deferral, participation in the CBSA CARM (Assessment and Revenue Management) single-window platform. Quebec's phased B2B e-invoicing mandate (Revenu Quebec, effective 2026-2027 for large enterprises) is not applicable to Alberta-based entities.

CRA Export Verification, Refund Integrity, and GST/HST post-assessment review programs scrutinize zero-rated exports for documentation sufficiency. A false or spurious zero-rating claim triggers: (i) gross negligence penalty of 20% of the net tax under s. 285(1) ETA; (ii) a 25% false-statements penalty under s. 285.1 ETA; (iii) interest under ss. 280-281 ETA on reassessed tax; (iv) extension of the normal four-year reassessment period for up to five additional years, or indefinitely where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default (ss. 298-298.1 ETA); (v) denial of ITCs and export refunds with retroactive clawback; and (vi) criminal prosecution for evasion under s. 239 ETA (fine to CAD 200,000 and/or imprisonment) and parallel ITA penalties under ss. 163(2)/163.2 for income-tax misstatements. Documentary integrity is the sole substantive defence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with unrestricted convertibility for current account transactions. Capital account transactions are generally convertible subject to Bank of Canada reporting thresholds of CAD 10,000 for resident entities. Profit remittance is permitted without capital controls, subject to withholding tax obligations and FINTRAC beneficial ownership reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender of export proceeds under Canadian law. Exporters may retain funds offshore or repatriate voluntarily; however, all transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 involving designated persons or suspicious activity must be reported to FINTRAC. Voluntary repatriation is recommended for liquidity optimization and audit compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
