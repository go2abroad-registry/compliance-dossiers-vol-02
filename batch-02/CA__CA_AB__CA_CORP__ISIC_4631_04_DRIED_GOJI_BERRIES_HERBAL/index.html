Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — central administrator of federal corporate income tax and federal GST/HST; Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) — provincial corporate registration and extraprovincial licence; tax filing portal: CRA My Business Account / Business Registration Online.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must register with the CRA. Procedure: (1) obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO) using Form RC1 or RC2; (2) activate the GST/HST program account ('RT') and, if applicable, the corporate income tax account ('RC'); (3) when registering in Alberta, file an extraprovincial registration or Articles of Incorporation with the Alberta Corporate Registry and obtain the Alberta corporation number; (4) appoint a resident agent for service where required. Canada does not operate a separate VAT ID registry; the CRA BN plus RT account code serves as the GST/HST registration number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident corporation taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporation taxed only on Canadian-source business income and certain capital gains; GST/HST operates at entity level and is not a pass-through tax) — A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must register with the CRA. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident corporation taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporation taxed only on Canadian-source business income and certain capital gains; GST/HST operates at entity level and is not a pass-through tax)

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections relevant to this structure include: GST/HST election for closely related corporations under ETA s. 156, quick-method election for certain GST/HST registrants, and non-resident elections under Income Tax Act s. 216 for net-basis tax on Canadian rental/royalty income; s. 219 branch tax applies automatically to non-resident corporations. Hybrid mismatch rules under ss. 18.4, 18.5, 94, 94.1, 94.2, and 94.3 do not permit elective recharacterization of an entity's tax status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available to exporters seeking to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Dried goji berries and herbal botanicals are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier: (i) exports the goods directly from Canada; (ii) ships them to a destination outside Canada; or (iii) delivers them to a common carrier for export within a reasonable time. The exporter must maintain documentary evidence proving physical export, such as transport documents, CBSA export declarations, freight bills, and destination-country import records. Because the supply is taxable at 0% rather than exempt, the exporter may claim input tax credits for GST paid on acquisitions related to the export business.

  • Form B13A / B13B Export Declaration filed via the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or automated export declaration portal
  • Commercial invoice showing exporter, foreign buyer, description, quantity, HS code 0813.40 for dried goji berries, and value in Canadian dollars
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or Cargo Control Document / PARS highway equivalent) evidencing dispatch to the foreign destination
  • Packing list and transport/freight invoices
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • Certificate of origin and phytosanitary inspection records where required for herbal botanical imports in the destination jurisdiction

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing approval system exists in Canada. GST/HST invoices must contain CRA-required particulars, including supplier name, GST/HST registration number, invoice date, description of goods, amount, tax rate, and tax collected; electronic invoices are permissible if legible and accessible to the CRA. Export documentation is transmitted electronically to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), not through a tax-authority clearance engine.

CRA may reverse a zero-rating treatment, disallow related input tax credits, and reassess GST/HST plus interest where the exporter cannot substantiate physical export within the statutory record-retention period (six years). False statements or misrepresentations attract penalties under Excise Tax Act ss. 280(1)-(2) (5% immediate penalty plus 1% per month on unremitted amounts) and Income Tax Act s. 163(2) (50% of the additional tax for gross negligence); fraudulent export claims may also attract criminal prosecution. Canada imposes no formal foreign-exchange repatriation statute, but receipt of export proceeds within 180 days and preservation of bank remittance records are the practical audit benchmark; failure to collect does not alone trigger clawback if genuine export is established, although missing payment evidence materially weakens the export-credibility review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada has accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement and imposes no exchange-control licensing, no capital-repatriation approval, and no restriction on dividend remittance, loan servicing, or share-capital restitution for an Alberta business corporation. Residual frictions are non-FX in nature: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on outbound dividends, interest, and royalties; FINTRAC reporting of electronic funds transfers at or above CAD 10,000; and bank-level KYC/AML evidentiary requirements triggered by non-resident beneficial ownership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation requirement on export proceeds and prescribes no mandatory timeline for collection of foreign receivables. The exporter may hold any currency balance in Canada or abroad. Compliance obligations are limited to routine FINTRAC reporting of EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more, CBSA export documentation where required, and accurate inclusion of foreign-currency revenue in taxable income under the Income Tax Act, using either the transactional or functional-currency translation rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Functional supervision is distributed across OSFI (prudential regulation of banks and insurance), Global Affairs Canada (export controls under the Export and Import Permits Act), FINTRAC (AML/CFT monitoring of cross-border electronic funds transfers under the PCMLTFA), and Payments Canada (clearing and settlement rail ownership, including Lynx).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium — limited. The European EMI designation has no direct statutory analogue under Canadian law; digital payment providers operating in Canada are regulated as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) and must register with FINTRAC. Platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are workable as secondary rails for mid-value operational settlement, multi-currency concentration, and automated reconciliation, but they cannot issue commercial letters of credit, act as reimbursing banks under MT700, or provide documentary trade finance. They must therefore complement, not replace, a Schedule I chartered bank as the primary clearing counterparty for bulk agricultural export flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity subscriptions and shareholder loans arrive via SWIFT MT103 or internal bank wire. Instruction fields must carry the beneficiary legal name, institution number, transit number, account number, and a purpose narration such as 'Equity Injection - Share Subscription' or 'Shareholder Loan Disbursement'. No exchange-control approval or government purpose-code declaration is required because both current and capital account payments are unrestricted in Canada. The receiving financial institution automatically reports EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC. For smooth clearing, the remitter should reference the receiving bank's direct-clearing BIC (e.g., ROYCCAT2, TDOMCATTTOR, BOFMCAT2) and keep the beneficiary address in the field-50K/59 block. Equity injections must be documented by board resolution and share subscription, and intercompany loans by demand promissory note, so that the legal characterization supports the tax treatment under the Income Tax Act and prevents reclassification of the injection as foreign business income.Supplier settlements are executed on three principal rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for open-account telegraphic transfers, including GPI with a Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR) for real-time tracking; (ii) MT202COV for USD reimbursement legs where clearing passes through US Fedwire or CHIPS correspondents; and (iii) documentary trade instruments, namely commercial letters of credit (MT700 issuance, MT707 amendment, MT754/MT799 advice, MT756 settlement claim) and documentary collections (MT400/MT410), used when counterparties require LC security. CAD leg clearing runs on Lynx, Payments Canada's real-time gross settlement system, with immediate finality; USD legs clear through the Canadian bank's US correspondent account network. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 and transfer-pricing documentation are mandatory where the total annual value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceeds C$1,000,000; local file and master file content must be made available within 3 months (6 months for master file) of a CRA demand; Country-by-Country reporting is required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least C$750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer formal Economic Substance Regulations; instead, economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), the common-law sham and substance-over-form doctrines, and the 'central management and control' residency test. Transactions lacking commercial rationality or genuine business purpose may be recharacterised, denied tax benefits, and subjected to gross-negligence penalties.
