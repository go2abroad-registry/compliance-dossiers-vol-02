E-government filing portal

Foreign corporations must first obtain a statutory Business Number (BN) from the CRA via the Online Business Registry or Form RC22 (Application for Non-Resident Business Account) before engaging in commercial activity in Alberta. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal or Alberta law. Entity classification for foreign and domestic corporations is determined by CRA administrative rules and applicable tax treaties. Available narrow elections include: GST/HST closely related corporation election under Section 156 of the Excise Tax Act; discretionary joint election under Section 61 for a supply of taxable real property; and Section 216 elective filing for Canadian-source rental income. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 gross annual taxable supplies in Canada as determined over the current and previous four calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); non-residents deriving taxable revenue from Canadian commercial activity may be required to register regardless of threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported matcha powder requires that the exporter holds documentary evidence of physical removal from Canada, title to the goods passes to a foreign purchaser, and the goods are exported within a reasonable period (normally within 12 months) under Excise Tax Act Section 39. Services performed directly on the goods or ancillary to export may zero-rate under Division IV Regulations. Foreign purchasers must be non-residents and the goods must not be returned to Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA eManifest / B13)
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean or Air Waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of FX repatriation, where required)

No federal mandate for real-time e-invoicing, fiscal stamping, or government clearance of invoices. Invoices must contain mandatory GST/HST information prescribed under Section 223 of the Excise Tax Act and Input Tax Credit Information Regulations. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via NETFILE when revenue exceeds thresholds. Export transactions require electronic submission of customs documentation through CBSA eManifest / Canadian Export Reporting (CCLDP) in the prescribed format.

CRA may reassess within four years (or longer in cases of misrepresentation or fraud), deny zero-rating status, and apply 5% GST plus interest. Penalties for false statements, deliberate evasion, or overclaimed refunds under Section 280.08 of Excise Tax Act and Sections 285/288 can amount to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax evaded. Criminal prosecution under Section 239 ETA may result in fines up to CAD 500,000 and/or imprisonment. Retrospective clawback of input tax refunds is enforceable through civil reassessment and revenue prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible; both current and capital account transactions are freely settled without exchange-control license. Cross-border wire transfers and foreign currency purchases in excess of CAD 10,000 are reported by financial institutions to FINTRAC. Profit and dividend remittances are unrestricted, subject to Canada Revenue Agency withholdings (Part XIII tax on payments to non-residents, unless reduced by treaty) and corporate compliance. No anti-avoidance 'forced conversion' applies to export receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds applies in Canada. Exporters may retain funds in any currency for an indefinite period, or convert to CAD at commercial bank rates. Exporters must maintain full documentation (customs declarations via CBSA, commercial invoices, and settlement records) for six years. FINTRAC reporting thresholds (e.g., cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more) remain applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority). The foreign exchange control framework in Canada is administered by the Department of Finance Canada, with day-to-day enforcement under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), supervised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). No exchange-control statute imposes prior approval on foreign exchange transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) and global fintech payment platforms (Wise, Payoneer) are not licensed as Canadian banks but may operate as money service businesses (MSBs) where activities occur in Canada, requiring FINTRAC MSB registration. Their multi-currency accounts and lower transaction fees provide medium feasibility as supplemental rails for unit disbursements, but they generally do not offer formal overnight liquidity, trade finance facilities, or a stable 'settlement hub'. For sustained cross-border procurement, a tier-1 Canadian bank account is mandatory.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

High-frequency cross-border wires with multiple third-party payor/payee configurations not referenced in the underlying trade agreement.

Root cause: High-frequency cross-border wires with multiple third-party payor/payee configurations not referenced in the underlying trade agreement.

Defense evidence: Complete commercial contract chain (purchase order, pro-forma invoice, final invoice), quality/certificate analysis for matcha (food safety certificate, organic certification), customs declarations (cargo control list from CBSA), transport documents (bill of lading / air waybill), and bank statement evidence showing that all payees/payors are named in the transaction records.

Defense evidence: Full shareholding registry, register of individuals with significant control (ISC Registry), corporate organizational chart, notarized passport of directors/UBOs, and a legal opinion letter explaining ownership structure and source of wealth.
Invoice anomalies, including over/under-invoicing or invoicing from a shell entity in a non-cooperative jurisdiction.

Root cause: Invoice anomalies, including over/under-invoicing or invoicing from a shell entity in a non-cooperative jurisdiction.

Defense evidence: Independent product valuation certificates (lab testing and organic certification), inspection company (SGS/Bureau Veritas) report, prior trade history and banking transaction patterns, and supplier due diligence (business registration, tax status, operational address).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection to CA_CORP (Alberta) arrives via SWIFT MT103 from the parent shareholder (or investor) to the bank's commercial account. Routing must include full remitter and beneficiary names (not merely account numbers), the corporate identification (Company's incorporation number), and the bank's BIC code. For FINTRAC, the purpose should be stated as 'Inbound Investment / Capital Call' or, if procurement finance, 'Payment for Import Settlement'. The bank should be pre-notified with the capital contribution agreement, a board resolution from the applicant, and an asset/liability schedule to accelerate verification.For settlement with the Japanese matcha supplier (or other suppliers), the primary rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfer where the supplier accepts an open account; (2) a commercial letter of credit (LC) issued by the Canadian bank under UCP600, confirmed by the seller's bank, for large-value and single-shipment purchase; and (3) check/documentary collection when commercially prudent. SC packing invoices and exchange rates are used for CAD/JPY conversion.
