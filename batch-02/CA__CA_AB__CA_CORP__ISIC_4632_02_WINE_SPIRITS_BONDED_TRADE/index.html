Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the federal Business Registration Online (BRO) service and My Business Account (MyBA) portal; provincial Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement, while the Alberta Corporate Registry manages corporate registration. Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) is the co-administrator for customs and duty-deferral (bonded) program accounts through the CRA Business Number (RM).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) before commencing activity in Canada. Registration is initiated through BRO or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), with program accounts allocated as RT for GST/HST, RC for corporate income tax, RM for customs import/export, and RP for payroll. For Alberta, the entity must also register or extra-provincially cross-register with the Alberta Corporate Registry; the CRA-issued BN is the single federal tax identifier. Non-resident corporations without Canadian physical presence may use the non-resident simplified GST/HST registration process and must obtain a CBSA importer/exporter code under the RM account for bonded-warehouse transactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-incorporated entity must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) before commencing activity in Canada. Registration is initiated through BRO or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), with program accounts allocated as RT for GST/HST, RC for corporate income tax, RM for customs import/export, and RP for payroll. For Alberta, the entity must also register or extra-provincially cross-register with the Alberta Corporate Registry; the CRA-issued BN is the single federal tax identifier. Non-resident corporations without Canadian physical presence may use the non-resident simplified GST/HST registration process and must obtain a CBSA importer/exporter code under the RM account for bonded-warehouse transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime and does not permit consolidated tax filing; a corporation is taxed as a separate legal person. Relevant non-resident elections include Income Tax Act section 216 (net-basis return on Canadian real-property rental income), section 217 (net-basis return on Canadian employment income), and the treaty-based election to file a Part I return on a net basis where a permanent establishment exists. Hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically; there is no domestic hybrid-mismatch election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies from Canada (small supplier threshold); registration is mandatory once exceeded. Non-resident importers and bonded-warehouse operators of wine/spirits are expected to hold a GST/HST account (RT) because imported alcoholic beverages are subject to GST at release and the zero-rated export chain must be documented by a GST-registered person.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of wine/spirits is zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act where the supplier transfers ownership to a non-resident and the goods are physically exported as part of the same supply, with continuous custody through the licensed bonded warehouse to the exporting carrier and no consumption, use, or resale in Canada. Supplies to a non-resident under an excise-bonded transfer for export are 0% GST/HST if the exporter holds an excise licence and compliant CBSA bonded-warehouse/export records. Documentary proof must exist at the time of the return to support the zero-rating claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CBSA electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement out of Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank proof of cross-border payment and repatriation

Canada currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing API, or digital stamping for commercial invoices. GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE / MyBA) and retain full source documentation. For bonded wine/spirits, CBSA eManifest and ACI/PAN data, the B3-3 customs accounting document, and CRA/RM program-account records govern the import/export clearance and duty-deferral chain; no continuous transaction control is applied at the point of sale.

CRA may disallow an export zero-rating claim if the exporter lacks contemporaneous proof of export and can reassess the uncollected GST/HST as tax payable by the exporter, together with arrears interest and penalties. A false or negligent export claim triggers the gross-negligence penalty under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, generally 50% of the understated net tax, plus additional fines and imprisonment for knowing evasion. CBSA can further assess excise duty under the Excise Act 2001, seize or forfeit bonded alcoholic beverages, and cancel duty-deferral (excise warehouse) privileges with retrospective duty claw-backs for any goods diverted to domestic use.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility with no material restrictions. Canada meets IMF Article VIII obligations: both current-account and capital-account transactions are free from exchange-control approval, registration, or surrender requirements. Inbound equity injection, intercompany loans, dividend and profit remittance, supplier payments and liquidation proceeds are lawful and unrestricted. The CAD is a fully deliverable two-way currency in deep wholesale markets. The only frictions are fiscal and corporate-law driven: non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (dividends, interest and royalties, reduced under applicable treaties such as the Canada-US, Canada-UK and Canada-France treaties), the thin-capitalisation rules of ITA s. 18(4), the CBCA / Alberta Business Corporations Act solvency tests for distributions, and transfer-pricing documentation under ITA s. 247. None of these is an FX-licensing hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil surrender obligation. Canada imposes no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no statutory custody requirement for export receipts. A bonded-wine/spirits wholesaler may lawfully retain USD, GBP or EUR sale proceeds offshore or domestically without CBSA or Bank of Canada involvement. The only connected rules are tax-driven: ITA s. 39(1)/(2) realisation of foreign exchange gains and losses, the ITA s. 261 functional-currency election, the controlled-foreign-affiliate accrual rules of ITA ss. 91-95, and the requirement to retain GST/HST zero-rating exemption evidence for exported goods. These are not exchange-surrender controls.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), functioning jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential supervisor of federally incorporated Schedule I clearing banks, and with FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) as the AML/ATF reporting and financial-intelligence unit. Canada does not operate a separate exchange-control administration; the Department of Finance Canada retains oversight of Payments Canada infrastructure (LVTS and Real-Time Rail) but exercises no exchange-control veto over cross-border commercial payments or capital movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for the core treasury; conditional only for small administrative disbursements. Payments Canada access is restricted to Canadian chartered banks and credit unions, so offshore digital EMIs and money-service businesses (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Payoneer, Banking Circle) cannot join LVTS/Real-Time Rail, cannot issue commercial letters of credit or customs bonds, and generally maintain 'restricted goods' policies that decline alcohol and bonded-commodity settlement. At most, a FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB may be used for low-value GBP/EUR voucher-style disbursements below CAD 25,000 with a complete invoice set transmitted to the principal Schedule I bank. All bonded-duty payments, LC margins, and primary trade-flow liquidity must remain at an OSFI-regulated bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

SWIFT MT103, or MT202 COV where the upstream remittance clears through a US or New York correspondent, is the compliant rail. Canada has no central-bank purpose-code registry, so the Remittance Information field (Field 70) must carry a true commercial label, e.g. 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - BONDED WINE SPIRITS OPCO' or 'INTERCOMPANY LOAN - STAGE 4 TREASURY'; vague labels such as Consulting, Travel or Miscellaneous are immediate AML flags. The receiving bank's verification package must include a board resolution of the Canadian business corporation and of the foreign shareholder, an updated share register, the AGLC licence and CBSA bond engagement, a source-of-funds letter, and, for shareholder-loan injections, an executed loan agreement with arm's-length interest and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. Equity injections carry no Part XIII withholding; a loan is subject to the Part XIII interest-withholding regime unless treaty-reduced, and must be documented to avoid recharacterisation as a s. 15(2) shareholder benefit.(a) Swift Wire (SWIFT MT103): the primary supplier-payment rail for Scotch and Bordeaux sellers; the Canadian issuing bank debits the operational CAD account, converts at the bank's trade rate to GBP or EUR, and clears through its London or Paris correspondent; Field 70 must carry the invoice number and Incoterms 'CIF CALGARY', with dual-signature release. (b) Commercial Letter of Credit: SWIFT MT700 issued under UCP600, available by sight payment or 30-day deferred terms, advised and confirmed by the seller's UK/EU bank, payable against presentation of the commercial invoice, full set of bills of lading, insurance certificate, and an expert quality certificate for aged wines. (c) Bonded Transfer: duty-deferred movement between CBSA-licensed warehouses is executed through the CBSA customs-broker platform (CAB import-release modules) rather than a commercial SWIFT rail; excise duty is paid by electronic transfer to the Receiver General for Canada. (d) Domestic CAD disbursements to duty brokers, freight forwarders, and Alberta logistics partners settle through Payments Canada LVTS for large-value items or the Automated Funds Transfer system for retail items. The treasury must apply a strict 'pay-to-contract' rule: no outgoing wire is released without the underlying commercial contract, invoice, and arrival or conveyance documentation.Outright deliverable FX forward contracts in CAD/GBP and CAD/EUR (tenors up to 24 months) executed through an OSFI-regulated Schedule I bank dealing desk under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Credit Support Annex; no NDF is necessary in the fully convertible CAD corridor.FX vanilla call and put options on CAD/GBP and CAD/EUR, with premiums settled in CAD, used to protect supplier-commitment and duty-remittance exposure.FX spot transactions and rolling T+1 conversions of GBP/EUR revenue into CAD for same-day or next-day excise-duty, payroll, and bond-margin funding.Cross-currency interest-rate swap (CAD fixed-floating versus GBP/EUR floating) aligned with LC-margin and inventory-financing lines, documented under the same ISDA/CSA master.USD/CAD or CAD/GBP foreign-exchange swap (roll) for intra-month liquidity management between revenue collection and the excise escrow accounts.Permissible non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only where a lawful physical-delivery market is absent for a third-country transaction currency; for all CAD, GBP, EUR, and USD exposures, physical deliverable forwards are the permitted and lower-cost instrument.All hedging strategies must be pre-approved through a board-adopted treasury policy, limited to genuine commercial exposures not exceeding 100% of forecast trade flows, booked only with the primary relationship bank, and supported by contemporaneous transaction documentation to satisfy OSFI and internal risk-appetite standards.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for the core treasury; conditional only for small administrative disbursements. Payments Canada access is restricted to Canadian chartered banks and credit unions, so offshore digital EMIs and money-service businesses (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Payoneer, Banking Circle) cannot join LVTS/Real-Time Rail, cannot issue commercial letters of credit or customs bonds, and generally maintain 'restricted goods' policies that decline alcohol and bonded-commodity settlement. At most, a FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB may be used for low-value GBP/EUR voucher-style disbursements below CAD 25,000 with a complete invoice set transmitted to the principal Schedule I bank. All bonded-duty payments, LC margins, and primary trade-flow liquidity must remain at an OSFI-regulated bank.
