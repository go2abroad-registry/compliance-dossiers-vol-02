Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online portal and My Business Account portal; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA on behalf of the Province of Alberta under a single federal-provincial tax administration agreement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN). The entity files Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or registers online via Business Registration Online (BRO). Upon approval, CRA issues the BN, a GST/HST program account (RC0001), and a corporate income tax program account (RT0001) where applicable. Non-resident registrants must provide Canadian agent or representative details in prescribed forms and may need to certify treaty residence. The GST/HST account must be obtained before claiming input tax credits or zero-rating exports.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a federally or provincially incorporated corporation is a separate taxable Canadian corporation if incorporated in Canada; for tax purposes it is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income, with territorial limitation for non-resident corporations. Alberta does not recognize check-the-box pass-through classification for corporations.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian income tax law. Corporate tax status follows the legal form of incorporation. Available elections relevant to indirect tax include the streamlined GST/HST election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for closely-related corporations to treat supplies as excluded from nil consideration, and annual GST/HST election for filing frequency. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Canadian rules under paragraphs 18(1)(q), 18(1)(r), and 18(1)(t) address disallowed deductions on hybrid arrangements; no elective hybrid regime is available to convert entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 threshold for the small supplier registration relief; a non-resident making a supply in Canada can be required to register once worldwide taxable supplies from Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter and over the preceding four quarters; registration is mandatory if any single supply or series of supplies for a promotion exceeds CAD 30,000. Voluntary registration is available to recover input tax credits for export-oriented operations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 9 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a supply of goods made by a supplier is zero-rated if the goods are exported directly by the supplier or delivered by the supplier to a common carrier for export, and evidence of export is held. For a non-resident wholesaler distributing Chinese baijiu from bonded storage, zero-rating applies when the goods are exported from Canada and the supplier maintains documentary proof of export; the supply must be made to a person outside Canada or for use solely outside Canada. Services directly related to export goods (e.g., logistics, storage, freight) may also be zero-rated, but the direct selling and buying of baijiu by an export distributor must satisfy the export evidence rules.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (via Canada Border Services Agency through the Canadian Export Reporting System, including the AES Export Declaration number and low-value shipment reporting where applicable)
  • Commercial invoice and purchase contract evidencing the foreign buyer; B/L (Bill of Lading or air waybill) showing exporter, consignee, port of loading, and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / banking records proving receipt of foreign currency consideration from the Alberta exporter to the Canadian vendor

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is in force in Canada. CRA supports Interac e-transfer for tax payments and electronic records, but e-invoicing is voluntary. The CRA launched a voluntary 'E-invoice' registry based on the Peppol network in 2024, but there is no legal mandate for XML invoice clearance or prior CRA approval of invoices. GST return filing must be done electronically for registrants with mandatory electronic filing thresholds, and prescribed information must appear on invoices to substantiate zero-rating.

CRA requires exporters to keep prescribed books and records for six years, including export documentation, to substantiate zero-rating of supplies. Absent such proof, the supply is taxable at 5% GST/HST and the registered exporter may be reassessed for all uncollected tax plus arrears interest and penalties. False export claims giving rise to refunds are subject to penalties under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, including a penalty of CAD 100 plus 25% of the amount wrongly refunded or erroneously sought; knowing misstatements can lead to criminal prosecution. CRA may also claw back input tax credits previously claimed on exported goods and impose no-form-of-relief civil penalties for significant failures under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. The Canadian dollar floats freely, and no exchange-control authorization is required for trade settlements, dividend repatriation, loan servicing, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance from an Alberta business corporation is unrestricted at the FX level; the operative barriers are fiscal and procedural only: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory rate, treaty-reduced to 10% on dividends generally and 5% on qualifying intercorporate dividends where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 10% of the voting shares under Article 10 of the Canada-China DTA (1986)), transfer-pricing documentation under section 247 of the Income Tax Act, thin-capitalization limitations under section 18(4) to 18(8), and CRA information returns (T1134 for foreign affiliates and T1135 Foreign Income Verification Statement where the cost amount of specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000). Dividends may be paid in CAD or any freely convertible currency without Bank of Canada approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline on export proceeds. A licensed Alberta exporter may retain foreign currency balances in CAD, USD, CNY, or other acceptable currencies at any Canadian Schedule I bank, may hold funds offshore, and may convert at commercial market rates. The only binding obligations are reporting thresholds, not surrender: all financial institutions must report incoming and outgoing electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, together with large-cash-transaction reports and suspicious-transaction reports, to FINTRAC. The commodity-level licensing controls applicable to liquor export (Alberta Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Act permits and the federal Importation of Intoxicating Liquors Act) relate to lawful movement of goods, not to foreign-exchange surrender.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (1935), acting with the Department of Finance Canada on exchange-rate policy and with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) on prudential bank supervision. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange administration agency; the Minister of Finance retains residual intervention powers under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) that are not exercised in ordinary market conditions. Anti-money-laundering and cross-border funds-flow supervision is performed by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary clearing rail and LOW if proposed as the sole treasury infrastructure. Canada does not license offshore digital EMIs as deposit-taking institutions; such entities must register as money services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, where applicable, register as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act administered by the Bank of Canada (registration framework effective November 2024). International EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer and LianLian Global typically classify alcohol distribution as a restricted or higher-risk goods category and apply enhanced review, and their balances are not CDIC-insured. Sales leg: the Alberta entity presents export documents under the buyer's letter of credit, with proceeds settled by MT202 or MT103 cover. Contingent rail: standby letters of credit (MT760) guarantee performance and prepayment obligations. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary clearing rail and LOW if proposed as the sole treasury infrastructure. Canada does not license offshore digital EMIs as deposit-taking institutions; such entities must register as money services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, where applicable, register as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act administered by the Bank of Canada (registration framework effective November 2024). International EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer and LianLian Global typically classify alcohol distribution as a restricted or higher-risk goods category and apply enhanced review, and their balances are not CDIC-insured. PRC-oriented cross-border payment platforms usually condition service on the merchant holding a Chinese business registration, which does not fit a Canadian-incorporated baijiu exporter. An EMI may be used for low-value FX conversion and supplier payments, but the primary corporate treasury and trade-finance functions must remain with a Schedule I bank that can issue documentary credits and provide regulatory-grade reporting.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if gross revenue exceeds CAD 20 million and cross-border transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million (per transaction or aggregate). Master File required if gross revenue exceeds CAD 100 million. Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions) must be filed if total transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions require that transactions have a bona fide business purpose and economic substance. For treaty benefits, the beneficial ownership test and principal purpose test apply. A shell company without real business activities may be denied treaty relief and face penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act, gross negligence penalty of 25% of the understated tax; criminal tax evasion penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded, fines up to CAD 1 million, and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts if they failed to withhold or remit source deductions (e.g., payroll) or if they authorized fraudulent filings. Civil restitution, travel bans, and personal bankruptcy may result.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reassessment of income, and penalties under GAAR (25% of the tax benefit). CRA may also impose a 10% penalty for failure to file T106. In severe cases, criminal charges for conspiracy to defraud.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in a sham arrangement. They may face civil restitution, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for up to 5 years.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations, and criminal penalties up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for AML violations if they directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the non-compliance. They may face fines, imprisonment, and travel bans.
