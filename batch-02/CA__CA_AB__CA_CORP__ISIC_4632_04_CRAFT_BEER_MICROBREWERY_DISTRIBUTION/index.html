Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal corporate income tax and GST/HST administered via CRA My Business Account portal (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); Alberta Corporate Income Tax (ACT) is collected by the CRA for Alberta Treasury Board and Finance under the Tax Collection Agreement (Alberta Corporate Tax Act); liquor and excise licensing for beer distribution is split between the federal Excise Act, 2001 regime (CRA Excise Duty) and the Alberta Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Commission (AGLC) for intra-Alberta sales.

        
E-government filing portal
A Canadian resident Business Corporation already holds a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) obtained at incorporation (via Corporations Canada or the Alberta Corporate Registry). For the craft beer wholesale/export mandate, the corporation must: (1) activate a GST/HST program account (RP suffix) through CRA My Business Account or Form RC1 once taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or voluntarily earlier; (2) activate a corporate income tax program account (RC suffix) to file T2 returns; (3) register with AGLC for a Manufacturer's Licence and, if selling to Alberta retailers, comply with AGLC mark-up and distribution rules (Alberta's privatized liquor model permits direct wholesale supply to licensed retailers); (4) obtain a federal excise licence and excise warehouse licence under the Excise Act, 2001 for beer removal, including zero- rated export removals; (5) if depositing funds from non-resident buyers, keep the FFI/IRP tax account in order for T106/T1134 filings where applicable. Non-resident shareholders do not require a Canadian TIN to hold shares, but non-resident corporate lenders/suppliers invoicing the Canadian entity require a CRA BN to avoid Part XIII gross-up issues.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: a Canadian-resident corporation under common law residency or the deeming rule in ITA s. 250(4)(c) is subject to Part I federal tax on worldwide income (base federal rate 15%; reduced to 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income for a Canadian-controlled private corporation) plus Alberta provincial corporate tax (general rate 8%; 2% on the small business limit of CAD 500,000 for qualifying CCPCs). Non-resident corporations with a Canadian permanent establishment are subject to Part I tax on Canadian-source business income and Part XIV branch tax (25%, reducible by treaty). The corporation is not a pass-through entity and is not eligible for US-style check-the-box; entity classification follows the statute and corporate law, with no elective transparency.

        
Available election forms
Canada does not provide check-the-box election; classification is statutory. Available mechanical elections include: ITA s. 85 rollover election on transfer of property to the corporation on a tax-deferred basis; ITA s. 88(2) winding-up election to crystallize subsidiary assets; ITA s. 256(9) associated-corporation designations affecting the CAD 500,000 SBD limit; ITA s. 216 election for non-residents on rental income; ITA s. 219 election for a non-resident corporation to be taxed under Part I instead of Part XIV branch tax; GST/HST elections include ETA s. 156 (associated persons deemed single supply), ETA s. 21 (closely related corporations election), and ETA s. 22 (zero-rated export election for non-resident registrants). Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.4 and 18.6) apply to disallow deduction/non-inclusion outcomes; no elective de-hybridization regime exists, and denied amounts are not reversible by taxpayer election.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
5%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CAD 30,000 (small supplier exemption) measured on total worldwide taxable supplies, including those of associated persons, over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; threshold applies to both resident suppliers and non-resident suppliers of digital services or tangible personal property under the 2021 e-commerce measures; a person whose only supplies are zero-rated exports may apply to be a 'zero-rated exporter' and remain unregistered; proposed increase to CAD 50,000 has been announced but is not yet in force as of the current statute.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating applies to a supply of tangible personal property (craft beer kegs) made by the exporter where the supplier ships or delivers the goods to a place outside Canada, or makes the goods available to a common carrier for export under ETA Schedule VI, Part I, s. 5; the purchaser must be a non-resident and the property must leave Canada in the ordinary course of trade as a bona fide export. For services incidental to the beer export (e.g., cold-chain logistics, marketing fees), zero-rating applies if the service is supplied to a non-resident and is performed either entirely outside Canada or is not received in Canada under the place-of-supply rules. The exporter must obtain 'satisfactory evidence of export' at or before the time the goods are shipped and retain it for six years, per CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17.6.2, including bills of lading and customs export documentation; failure to hold contemporaneous documentary proof will cause the CRA to reassess the supply at 5% GST.

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoice authorization mandate exists in Canada. CRA accepts paper, PDF, EDI, and PEPPOL-compliant e-invoices; the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework operates voluntarily on the PEPPOL network. Exporters must file electronic export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) within 15 days after the goods are exported to the CBSA.

      
Canada imposes no statute-codified FX repatriation deadline; 180 days is the operational benchmark under which the CRA expects to observe return consideration for export substantiation, but the absence of any receipt for a longer period will trigger audit scrutiny. CRA may deny zero-rating and assess 5% GST on the full export consideration, plus arrears interest (prescribed quarterly rate) and a civil penalty of 25% of the net tax under ETA s. 285; false or fraudulent statements attract a gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 280.1 and, on conviction, fines up to 200% of the tax evaded or imprisonment up to five years (ETA s. 327.1). Income tax understatements from fictitious export revenue carry a 50% gross-negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2). CRA audit of export zero-rating focuses on evidence contemporaneity, the exporter's status (licensed excise warehouse), and actual movement of kegs; beer exported without proper excise removal under the Excise Act, 2001 triggers excise duty assessment and potential seizure by the CBSA. Exporters failing to file the B13A within 15 days are subject to CBSA penalties under the Customs Act and the Export Reporting Regulations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Canada provides full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no foreign-exchange controls, prior-approval mechanisms, profit-remittance barriers, or obligatory conversion requirements imposed on a federally incorporated Alberta business corporation exporting craft beer. Domestic and cross-border payments settle freely in CAD. Dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory rate, potentially reduced to 5-15% under an applicable income tax treaty); managerial or service fees must withstand CRA re-characterization risks but are not blocked by exchange-control law.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory conversion ratio, and no deadline for repatriation or conversion of export receipts. Exporters must file electronic export declarations with the Canadian International Trade Single Window (CBSA) where applicable, file FINTRAC large-transaction/EFT reports for each cross-border wire of CAD 10,000 or more, and retain underlying shipping and invoice records for GST/HST and customs compliance. No central-bank approval is required to receive, hold, or convert USD export proceeds.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency; exchange-control-related supervision is distributed among FINTRAC (anti-money-laundering and cross-border transaction reporting), OSFI (prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions), Statistics Canada (balance-of-payments direct-investment reporting), and Global Affairs Canada (trade controls under the Export and Import Permits Act).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium-to-Low. Canada does not recognize the European-style EMI (Electronic Money Institution) as a distinct banking licence. A non-resident digital payment provider may lawfully serve the Alberta craft-beer distributor only if it: (i) registers with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business for each Canadian service location; (ii) complies with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration and risk-management rules once fully in force; and (iii) refrains from deposit-taking in the manner of a bank. Offshore EMIs cannot clear CAD directly into Payments Canada ACSS/Lynx rails without a Schedule I bank sponsor or a FINTRAC-registered pure-play MSB partner; accordingly, fintech rails are suitable for supplementary FX conversion and disbursements but not as the primary treasury backbone.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound equity capital must be routed by SWIFT MT103 to the Alberta operating account using the Canadian clearing structure: 3-digit institution number, 5-digit transit number, and 12-digit account number, together with the beneficiary bank's SWIFT/BIC. Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
23% combined (15% federal general rate + 8% Alberta general rate); reduced to 11% combined (9% federal + 2% Alberta) on the first CAD 500,000 of qualifying active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) under the Small Business Deduction.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% Part XIII withholding tax on the gross amount of dividends, deemed dividends, and surplus distributions paid to a non-resident shareholder from Canadian sources. Under the Canada-US Tax Treaty, the rate is reduced to 5% for a US resident beneficial owner that is a company holding at least 10% of the voting stock of the Canadian payer, and to 15% in all other cases; Alberta does not impose a separate provincial dividend withholding tax, so the Part XIII federal rate is the sole statutory rate.

    

    

      
Mandatory Return Breakdown

Filing deadline is no later than 6 months after the fiscal year-end (e.g., a December 31 year-end must file by June 30 of the following calendar year). Payment of the final balance of tax is due no later than 2 months after fiscal year-end for a non-CCPC and 3 months after fiscal year-end for a CCPC claiming the small business deduction; instalments are due quarterly for most CCPCs and monthly for larger corporations (taxable capital above CAD 10 million).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: CAD 250,000: Form T106 (Transfer Pricing Return) must be filed for reportable transactions with each non-arm's length non-resident when the aggregate value of transactions within any category (goods, services, interest, royalties) exceeds CAD 250,000 in the fiscal year. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (equivalent to the OECD Transfer Pricing Documentation Master File and Local File) must be prepared and retained by the taxpayer when this threshold is exceeded and an arm's length price is relied upon. Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting under Form T1173 is mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of CAD 1 billion or more in the preceding fiscal year. The Alberta-based craft beer distributor should treat any sales of kegs, licensing of brewing trademarks, or intercompany distribution services with US affiliates as reportable transactions subject to the CAD 250,000 threshold.

      
Economic substance scope: Canada and Alberta do not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the Income Tax Act contains an equivalent anti-avoidance architecture: the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, s. 245) nullifies tax benefits from 'avoidance transactions' lacking genuine economic substance, business purpose, and a bona fide non-tax purpose. The CRA applies a substance-over-form analysis to disregard shell corporations, treaty-shopping conduits, and circular financing arrangements. For a craft beer distribution entity, the CRA will examine whether the Alberta corporation has real employees, physical premises, actual inventory, cold-chain distribution activity, and arms'-length contracts; a 'zombie' entity with no operational substance and only intercompany paper invoices is vulnerable to GAAR reassessment, re-attribution of profits to the Canadian shareholder, and denial of treaty benefits.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act s. 239 and Criminal Code s. 380)
        
Statutory sanction: On indictment: a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment for up to 5 years; on summary conviction: a fine of up to CAD 50,000 plus imprisonment for up to 2 years. The CRA will also assess a 50% gross negligence penalty on the understated income and charge compound daily interest on all unpaid amounts from the due date until payment is made.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct fraudulent conduct are personally exposed: the CRA lifts the corporate veil under s. 227.1 for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII amounts, and under s. 160 for transfers to non-arm's length parties. Directors may face civil restitution, garnishment of personal assets, travel bans via CBSA flagging on outstanding tax debts, and imprisonment for fraud over CAD 5,000 under Criminal Code s. 380.

      

      
      

        Failure to Withhold and Remit Part XIII Non-Resident Tax
        
Statutory sanction: The Canadian payer is liable for the full unwithheld Part XIII amount plus arrears interest and a 10% penalty on the amount not withheld; daily penalties apply for failure to remit or report on Form NR4; the CRA may also assess the non-resident directly but will not discharge the payer's liability until recovery is exhausted.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for the unremitted Part XIII withholding if the corporation fails to pay; the CRA can issue a compliance hold on bank accounts, garnish receivables, and pursue the directors' personal bank accounts and real property without a court order when the 2-year limitation window has passed.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (s. 247(3))
        
Statutory sanction: A penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment is imposed when the adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 250,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue; the penalty is in addition to the reassessed tax, interest, and any 50% gross negligence penalty; the CRA can also impose penalties under s. 162 for failure to file T106 or for incomplete information.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false or misleading T106 declaration or who knowingly set non-arm's length prices that strip profits out of Canada can be held personally liable where the corporation is insolvent; the CRA will look through the corporate shield when the company is a mere conduit and the directors have engaged in a sham pricing arrangement.

      

      
      

        AML / Proceeds of Crime (PCMLTFA and Criminal Code s. 462.31)
        
Statutory sanction: Administrative Monetary Penalties (AMPs) of up to CAD 1 million for a corporation and up to CAD 200,000 for individuals, imposed per violation with no requirement of criminal intent; failure to report a large cash transaction or a suspicious wire transfer is a separate violation; criminal money laundering charges carry imprisonment of up to 10 years on indictment.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly fail to implement AML controls can be personally named in FINTRAC AMPs and prosecuted under Criminal Code s. 462.31; a conviction leads to criminal fines, imprisonment, forfeiture of corporate assets, and a travel ban enforced by the Canada Border Services Agency. Although the standard risk tier is low, the entity must document the risk assessment and annual training records.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / GAAR and Economic Substance Failure
        
Statutory sanction: The CRA applies GAAR (s. 245) to deny the tax benefit and reassess on a substance-over-form basis; a 15% GAAR penalty may be imposed on the denied tax benefit under the 2024 proposed amendments; the sham doctrine permits the courts to void the entire arrangement; the corporation's residence and PE status will be re-analyzed and all cross-border payments recharacterized as taxable Canadian-source income.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who cause the corporation to be a 'zombie' shell are exposed to personal liability under s. 160 for the tax benefit transferred, s. 227.1 for any unremitted amounts, and the general law of fraudulent conveyance when the veil is pierced; the CRA may also obtain a travel ban under s. 226.1 and criminal prosecution for tax evasion where the shell was a deliberate artifice to conceal taxable income.
