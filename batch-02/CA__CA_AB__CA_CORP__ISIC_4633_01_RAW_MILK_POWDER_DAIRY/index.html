Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and the Alberta Ministry of Finance (provincial corporate income tax administered by CRA); primary portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html) and GST/HST NETFILE.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or Alberta through a permanent establishment must: (1) obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) from the CRA; (2) register for a GST/HST program account using Form RC1 if total taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 over the current and preceding four calendar quarters or if a voluntary registration is desirable; (3) open an Import/Export Program Account with the CBSA for dairy commodity shipment clearance; and (4) file a T2 corporate income tax registration and annual return if resident or non-resident carrying on business in Canada. Non-resident entities are eligible for the BN and GST/HST registration directly through CRA's non-resident registration process.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or Alberta through a permanent establishment must: (1) obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) from the CRA; (2) register for a GST/HST program account using Form RC1 if total taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 over the current and preceding four calendar quarters or if a voluntary registration is desirable; (3) open an Import/Export Program Account with the CBSA for dairy commodity shipment clearance; and (4) file a T2 corporate income tax registration and annual return if resident or non-resident carrying on business in Canada. Non-resident entities are eligible for the BN and GST/HST registration directly through CRA's non-resident registration process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Not applicable for the standard Canadian Business Corporation; Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election. All corporations are taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act, and no pass-through classification is available to a capital-stock company. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Canada's domestic rules (section 18.2 and related provisions) and the Multilateral Instrument may restrict double-dip payments. Standard tax elections available to corporations include the section 85 asset rollover and the small business deduction elections, but these are not entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of total taxable supplies made in Canada over the current calendar quarter and the four immediately preceding calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold). A non-resident supplier is required to register if it carries on business in Canada and exceeds this threshold; voluntary registration is permitted. Importing dairy commodities does not itself trigger GST registration, but GST applies at the border on the importation of goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a supply of tangible personal property (including whole and skimmed milk powder) that is exported from Canada, or delivered outside Canada to a non-resident, in accordance with Division III of the Excise Tax Act and Schedule VI, Part V. The zero-rating claim is available when the supplier obtains adequate evidence of exportation within the prescribed time. For dairy commodity trading, the sale contract with a foreign buyer, delivery outside Canada, and proof that the goods were physically removed from Canada must be retained. Export services directly in respect of the exported goods may also be zero-rated if the conditions are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Export Reporting System – CERS) or the applicable B13A/B15A form, as required for commodity exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the dairy commodity was shipped from Canada to the foreign consignee
  • Commercial Invoice and sales contract reflecting the buyer, product description, quantity, and agreed export terms (Incoterms)
  • Customs broker or carrier confirmation of physical exportation from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/FX settlement record evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada. GST/HST registration and filing are electronic: registrants must generally use GST/HST NETFILE or an approved third-party electronic filing service if eligible. Invoices may be paper or electronic, and CRA requires that electronic records and e-invoicing data be retained in a readable format for audit. No API-based government invoice clearance is currently imposed on commercial invoices for export transactions.

Canada does not prescribe an express statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; however, CRA and commercial bank compliance practices treat the 180-day window as the reasonable export settlement benchmark. If an exporter claims a 0% zero-rated export and fails to substantiate physical export or receivables settlement, the CRA may reassess the supply at 5% GST, recover any previously paid refunds or claimed Input Tax Credits, and charge daily compound interest. Penalties under Sections 280 and 281 of the Excise Tax Act may apply for knowingly making false statements, with penalties up to 25% of the misstated amount. In egregious cases, criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and the Criminal Code can be instituted, and retroactive clawback of refunds plus interest is standard for fraudulent export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar floats freely, and neither the Currency Act nor the Bank of Canada Act imposes convertibility restrictions, pre-approval requirements, or currency quotas on commodity-trade settlements. Profit and dividend remittances are unimpeded, subject solely to Part XIII withholding tax under sections 212 and 214 of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.)), generally reduced to 5%–15% under an applicable tax treaty for dividends and 0%–15% for arm's-length interest. No Investment Canada Act notification is required for wholesale dairy trading below the WTO net-benefit review thresholds, and no capital-control screening applies to ordinary trade flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory deadline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation of foreign-currency earnings. A resident exporter may lawfully hold offshore or onshore USD/EUR balances indefinitely, subject only to general tax documentation, Customs Act record-keeping, and FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting (CAD 10,000 or higher) for incoming wire transfers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) under the Bank of Canada Act, with prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money-laundering/terrorist-financing oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control licensing body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for LC-based dairy commodity treasury. Canada does not recognise the European e-money institution (EMI) licensing model; non-bank payment-service providers must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c.23, s.176) supervised by the Bank of Canada, but they cannot issue or operate SWIFT documentary letters of credit (MT700), lack direct access to the Lynx high-value settlement system, and face constrained AML/CFTC compliance architecture. Use of a fintech corridor is acceptable only for incidental operating expenses below CAD 25,000 per transaction; the principal trade, FX and liquidity rails must remain on Schedule I bank infrastructure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of non-arm's length transactions with non-residents in the taxation year (triggering Form T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation per CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R). Master File and Local File required for MNE groups at the documented threshold; Country-by-Country Report (RC4649) and notification (RC4648) required where consolidated group revenue exceeds the CAD equivalent of EUR 750 million (approximately C$1.1B).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations code (unlike UAE/Cayman); however, economic substance is decisive under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test, and permanent establishment attribution rules. A shell Alberta corporation with no employees, no premises, no Canadian bank account, and no decision-making function will be treated as a conduit: CRA will re-attribute the milk powder trading profit to the jurisdiction where substantive business decisions are actually made, deny treaty benefits, and reassess Part XIII tax at 25% with interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1): on summary conviction, fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, fine of 50% to 200% plus imprisonment of not less than 2 years and up to 5 years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2): 50% of the understated tax, plus daily compound interest at the CRA prescribed rate.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and agents who directed, assented to, or participated in the offence are personally liable under ITA s.242 to the same fines and imprisonment. CRA may register a civil judgement, obtain a travel ban against the individual, and invoke extradition treaties where funds routed through Canada-China payment rails are tainted by fraud.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: T106 late-filing penalty C$100/day per form (minimum C$100, maximum C$2,500); failure to produce transfer-pricing documentation within 3 months of CRA request: C$1,000 per month per person; ITA s.247(3) transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment where the aggregate adjustment exceeds the greater of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue and documentation was not complete, accurate, and contemporaneous.

Director personal liability: Directors are not strictly liable for the 10% transfer-pricing penalty, but where the resulting corporate tax remains unpaid, CRA may assess directors under ITA s.227.1 for deemed-trust amounts. Directors who knowingly sign false T106 declarations face criminal liability under s.242 and potential extradition exposure in cross-border schemes.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR (ITA s.245) re-characterization of the arrangement; denial of the Canada-China DTA 10% dividend/interest/royalty rates; reassessment at the statutory 25% Part XIII rate; compound arrears interest; 50% gross-negligence penalty; application of the MLI Principal Purpose Test to deny treaty benefit where one of the principal purposes of the structure is to obtain treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who authorize or sign the scheme are personally liable under s.242 as participants; civil recovery may pierce the corporate veil at common law where the company is a mere façade. CRA may request a compliance order or an international travel prohibition where the director attempts to leave Canada with unreported corporate funds.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalty up to C$500,000 for a corporation and up to C$250,000 for an individual; failure to register as an MSB where required carries additional monetary penalties of up to C$500,000; criminal sanctions include imprisonment up to 5 years. FINTRAC intelligence may also be shared with CRA to trigger reassessment of hidden revenue.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who 'direct, authorize, assent to, acquiesce in, or participate in' the violation are liable to the same AMPs as the corporation. Canadian courts will lift the corporate veil where the entity is used to conceal the true principal; travel bans and restraint orders over personal assets are available in money-laundering prosecutions.
Unremitted Withholdings and Deemed Trust Liability

Statutory sanction: ITA s.227(8.4): 10% penalty plus daily compound interest on unremitted amounts; the Crown holds a deemed trust over all corporate assets from the moment the withholding obligation arises, giving CRA priority over secured lenders; CRA may immediately garnish bank accounts and appoint a receiver.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally personally liable for the full unremitted amount, interest, and penalties under ITA s.227.1. The only defense is having exercised reasonable care, diligence, and skill to prevent the failure, which is applied strictly; personal bankruptcy, CRA collection against personal assets, and criminal exposure under s.242 are realistic outcomes.
