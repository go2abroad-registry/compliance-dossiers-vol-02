Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – 'My Business Account' portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Business Registration Online (BRO); CBSA CARM portal for customs accounts

E-government filing portal

Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must (1) apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account (RT) via Business Registration Online (BRO), Form RC1, or Form RC1A (non-resident); (2) register for an Import/Export (RM) Program Account with the CBSA through the CARM portal for customs transactions; (3) provide authenticated corporate documents, director resolutions, and proof of permanent establishment/agent for service if applicable; CRA normally issues the BN/GST/HST account within 5-10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Worldwide_Resident_Scope / Non-Resident_Taxation_On_Canadian_Source_Income / No_Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' pass-through election exists under Canadian law; available elections include: (i) Income Tax Act s. 216 election (net-basis Canadian rental income for non-residents); (ii) s. 217 election (net-basis employment income for non-residents); (iii) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST74) and simplified non-resident GST/HST accounting (Form GST507); (iv) hybrid mismatch elections under ITA ss. 18.2/18.3 (BEPS Action 2, effective 2023-2024) to deem hybrid mismatches and reverse deduction/non-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold for total taxable supplies made worldwide in the preceding four calendar quarters); threshold is cumulative global turnover, not just Canadian sales

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods (e.g., pre-packaged hotpot soup base bricks) is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V when the goods are exported by the supplier (or at the supplier's direction) on or before the last day of the calendar month following the month in which the supply is made, and the supplier retains conclusive documentary evidence of export (qualified for 0% GST and full input tax credit entitlement). Services supplied to a non-resident recipient for use wholly outside Canada are also zero-rated under Part V, s. 7.

  • CERS/CBSA Export Declaration (B13A) with approved export confirmation
  • Transport document (Ocean Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Receipt)
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms, HS classification (HS 2103.90 – sauces/preparations), and declared value
  • Packing list with product/serial identifiers for the hotpot soup base bricks
  • Evidence of export delivery (carrier certificate, terminal receipt, or customs-stamped proof)
  • Bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice / Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing the overseas receivable

No real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies ≥ CAD 1.5M must file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE / CRA-approved EDI). Export declarations must be submitted to the CBSA through CERS (Canadian Export Reporting System) or integrated EDI/API channels under the CARM platform. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary under the optional Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework.

CRA verifies export evidence and may claw back the 0% rate by reassessing to 5% GST within 4 years (unlimited window for misrepresentation/fraud), with compound daily interest and penalties. Civil false-statement penalties equal 25% of the tax understated (minimum CAD 100) under ETA s. 285.1; gross negligence and conspiracy penalties may also apply (ITO s. 163.2 – greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated amount). Criminal evasion under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 is subject to fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or up to 5 years imprisonment. Records must be retained for 6 years. Note: Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; 180 days is the customary trade-finance and CRA audit benchmark for export proceeds substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current and capital account convertibility, consistent with IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange controls, no licensing procedures, and no dividend or profit remittance restrictions. The Canadian dollar trades freely on foreign exchange markets; corporate election of CAD, USD, CNY, or other invoicing and settlement currency is allowed. Only reporting obligations apply, such as CRS/FATCA and FINTRAC International Electronic Funds Transfer reports for transactions at or above CAD $10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or minimum repatriation timeline. Exporters may hold funds in foreign currency accounts in Canada, convert at any time, or retain proceeds offshore. Documentary support (export invoice, bill of lading, customs declaration) must be available to satisfy FINTRAC, CBSA, and the entity's Schedule I bank for large or high-frequency foreign-currency credits.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Department of Finance Canada. Canada does not operate a dedicated central foreign exchange control authority; currency matters are governed by the Bank of Canada Act, Currency Act, and the free-market framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium as primary treasury solution; feasible only as a supplementary disbursement channel. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not regulated deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act; they may provide payment initiation and settlement services if registered with FINTRAC as a money services business and, once fully effective, licensed under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). An EMI account cannot substitute for a corporate bank account holding commercial deposits; all material capital inflows and trade settlement under Letters of Credit should be routed through a Tier 1 Canadian chartered bank to preserve compliance and LC issuance capacity.

The T2 filing deadline is six months after the taxation year-end. For an Alberta corporation with a 31 December fiscal year end, the return is due on 30 June of the following calendar year. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate controlled transactions with non-resident related parties in a taxation year is the commonly applied Canada Revenue Agency transfer-pricing record-keeping threshold under section 247(4) and related regulations.

Economic substance scope: The United Arab Emirates-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) do not formally apply in Canada. Instead, the Canada Revenue Agency applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), the beneficial-ownership tests under treaty provisions, and OECD 'place of effective management' concepts to attack shell or conduit corporations. An Alberta distribution entity holding no real employees, no warehouse or storage risks, no operational direction and no commercial substance will be vulnerable to treaty-benefit denial, GAAR reassessment and potential penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under section 163(2) of the Income Tax Act, a civil penalty of 50% of the understated tax applies, plus compounded daily interest. Criminal prosecution under section 239(1) can result in a fine between 50% and 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years on indictment, or up to two years on summary conviction. Restitution orders and asset forfeiture may also be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct or participate in the filing of false returns may be jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax, penalties and interest. The corporate veil may be pierced by courts if the entity is an instrument or sham used to defeat CRA claims; personal civil restitution, freezing orders and travel restrictions may follow in criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The CRA may adjust the taxable income under section 247(2); a transfer-pricing penalty under section 247(3) equal to 10% of the greatest amount of income understatement or expense overstatement applies, in addition to the underlying tax and interest. The taxpayer bears the burden of demonstrating arm's-length pricing and reasonable efforts.

Director personal liability: Directors of the controlled Canadian corporation may be assessed for inducing, aiding or participating in a culpable transfer-pricing arrangement. If the corporation is insolvent or the arrangement is found to be a sham, directors may face personal restitution rights in favour of creditors, civil damages, and travel bans where criminal misconduct is established.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Tax (Part XIII)

Statutory sanction: The payer remains liable for the full 25% Part XIII withholding on payments made to non-residents, plus arrears interest. Under section 227(10), a penalty of 10% applies for failure to remit withholdings unless due to reasonable error, and 20% applies where the failure was deliberate. Repeat failures can trigger increased assessments and audits.

Director personal liability: Under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act, directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding taxes if the corporation fails to deduct and remit. The director liability survives corporate dissolution and can be enforced directly against personal assets; a due-diligence defence requires proof of all reasonable care in ensuring remittance.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245(2) can unwind tax benefits through reassessment by re-attributing income to the true economic owner. Treaty benefits may be denied where the beneficial ownership/purpose test is failed. False material facts can attract a 50% penalty, and an Alberta corporation that fails to maintain substance may be struck off under the Alberta Business Corporations Act for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who curate a facade without operations may be personally liable for unpaid taxes under fraudulent-disposition or discretionary-avoidance rules. Courts may order compensation, declare the corporation a sham, and impose travel restrictions where the structure is part of a tax and money-laundering scheme.
AML and Regulatory Licensing Failures

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative penalties can reach CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 5 million for entities, per violation. Food/CFIA export licensing violations may result in permit suspension, product detention and customs penalties. Alberta corporate registry non-compliance leads to administrative strike-off or dissolution.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for participating in or authorising unlawful regulatory or AML conduct; they may be subject to monetary penalties, court-ordered compensation, restitution and disqualification from serving as directors. Criminal or quasi-criminal cases can impose travel bans and personal seizure of proceeds.
