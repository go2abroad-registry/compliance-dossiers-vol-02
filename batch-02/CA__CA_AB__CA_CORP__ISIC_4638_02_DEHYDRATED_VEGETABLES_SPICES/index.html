Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal administrator of GST/HST, corporate income tax, and customs program accounts; digital portal: My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE via www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Alberta does not levy a provincial sales tax or administer provincial corporate income tax; provincial tax is filed through the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act. Alberta corporate registry matters are handled by Service Alberta / Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or Alberta Business Corporations Act (ABCA) and obtain the Alberta corporate registry file number. Step 2: Apply to the CRA for the 15-digit statutory Business Number (BN) online, by telephone, or by filing Form RC1 Request for a Business Number. Step 3: Activate program accounts under the BN: RT for GST/HST, RC for federal corporate income tax, and RP for payroll where applicable; obtain an RM customs importer/exporter program account through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Step 4: Where the corporation is controlled by non-residents, designate a Canadian representative and comply with any forms required by the CRA for non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada. Step 5: Register on the CBSA CARM portal (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) to transmit commercial export declarations and e-manifests electronically. Step 6: No separate Alberta provincial tax ID is required; the federal BN and program accounts constitute the complete statutory tax identity for GST and income tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A CBCA/ABCA Business Corporation is a Canadian-resident corporation subject to federal and Alberta corporate income tax under Income Tax Act Part I and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; no pass-through or check-the-box classification is available. Canadian-resident corporations are taxed at the entity level on worldwide income, while non-resident shareholders are not subject to entity-level Canadian tax on dividends. Alberta applies a flat provincial corporate rate with no provincial capital tax and no provincial sales tax, but the entity remains an exclusive GST/HST registrant rather than a flow-through vehicle.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists for CBCA/ABCA corporations; entity-level taxation is mandatory. Material elections available to an export trader include: (a) voluntary GST/HST registration under Excise Tax Act s. 240(3) for exporters of zero-rated goods to claim input tax credits even below the CAD 30,000 threshold; (b) joint election under ETA s. 156 for closely related corporations to treat intercompany taxable supplies as made by a single registrant; (c) non-calendar fiscal period election under ITA s. 11; (d) associated-corporation allocation election under ITA s. 256 for income attribution. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2 to 18.7 are mandatory and are not subject to taxpayer election, consistent with OECD BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold under ETA s. 123(1) and s. 148; mandatory registration under ETA s. 240(1) once exceeded). Zero-rated export supplies count toward the threshold, so a bulk dehydrated spice exporter must register even if all customers are non-residents. Voluntary registration under ETA s. 240(3) is available below the threshold to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 (supply of tangible personal property sold for export) and s. 2 (supply to a non-resident person for subsequent export). Bulk dehydrated garlic flakes (HS 0712.90) and crushed chili pepper (HS 0904.21/0904.22) qualify where: (i) the supplier is a GST/HST registrant; (ii) possession of the goods transfers to a non-resident purchaser in Canada; (iii) the goods are exported as part of the supply within 60 days after delivery without substantial use or alteration in Canada; (iv) the supplier retains documentary proof of export (customs declaration, bill of lading, commercial invoice, payment evidence); and (v) the transaction is made on commercial terms (Incoterms FOB, CIF, or EXW) transferring risk and title to the foreign buyer. International freight services directly linked to the exported goods are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part VII. Refunds are secured by claiming input tax credits in the exporter's GST/HST return; a net refund arises where zero-rated exports exceed taxable purchases.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (B-13/A8A or A8A/B1) transmitted through the CARM commercial portal
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing direct shipment to the non-resident purchaser
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or TT bank credit advice confirming foreign currency settlement
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and Certificate of Origin referencing HS 0712.90 or HS 0904 classification
  • Executed export contract and proof of non-resident purchaser status (purchase order, Incoterms, foreign registry data)

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, biometric invoicing, or government-API pre-approval requirement. Registered suppliers must issue and retain invoices satisfying ETA s. 220 to s. 224 and the Input Tax Information Regulations, including supplier name, Business Number, invoice date, description, and zero-rating status; CRA accepts PDF, XML, EDI, and other electronic formats in English or French. Pan-Canadian Peppol BIS electronic invoicing is voluntary under the Digital Interchange Canada pilot and not yet mandatory. For exports, the binding mandatory electronic filing is the CBSA CARM portal, which requires approved customs partners to transmit B-13 export declarations, e-manifests, and cargo-level data electronically before or at departure.

The CRA and CBSA Export Verification Unit may audit export substantiation for up to seven years (ETA s. 298(1); the normal four-year reassessment period is extended to seven years for misrepresentation and is unlimited in fraud cases). Where the required customs documentation bundle is incomplete or inconsistent, CRA reassesses the supply at 5% GST, disallows associated input tax credits retroactively, and imposes civil penalties: ETA s. 280 failure-to-file penalty of 5% plus 1% per month; ETA s. 285 gross negligence penalty of 25% of the understated net tax; and compound interest under ETA s. 280. Fraudulent export zero-rating schemes involving false bills of lading, recycled customs declarations, or fictitious non-resident buyers trigger criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327.07 and ITA s. 239, with fines up to 200% of the evaded amount, imprisonment, and permanent denial of the exporter's GST/HST refund entitlement.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full Canadian-dollar convertibility on both current and capital account. No Bank of Canada licence, FX permit, or approval is required for conversion or cross-border remittance of funds. Profit remittances (dividends, interest, royalties, branch profits) are unrestricted at the monetary level and are subject only to: (i) Part XIII withholding tax of 25% with treaty reductions, for example 5% for a substantial corporate holder under the Canada-United States treaty; (ii) FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) the remitting bank's KYC/EDD assessment of underlying commercial documentation. Practical constraint is bank validation of the trade contract and corporate resolution; no currency-quota or conversion-ticket obstacles exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no surrender mandate, no mandatory CAD conversion ratio, no retention ceiling, and no repatriation window on foreign-currency export proceeds. Exporters may hold proceeds indefinitely in USD or other permitted foreign accounts. Statutory obligations: (i) CBSA export declaration form B13A filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) for all shipments valued at CAD 2,000 or more and for controlled goods regardless of value; (ii) FINTRAC EFT reports for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (iii) Statistics Canada international merchandise-trade reporting; and (iv) Canadian corporate income-tax recognition of foreign-exchange gains and losses per the Income Tax Act functional-currency rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority. Statutory foreign-exchange administration is distributed across the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF reporting, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export declarations, the Department of Finance Canada for Investment Canada Act administration, and Payments Canada (PayNet) for clearing and settlement. Canada maintains no statutory exchange-control commission; exchange controls were repealed in 1951.
