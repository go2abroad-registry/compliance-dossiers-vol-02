Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available for Canadian corporations. The Alberta Business Corporation is categorically subject to entity-level federal and provincial income taxation. Available statutory elections include GST/HST group registration under Excise Tax Act s.156 for closely related corporations, GST/HST branch or division registration under s.179, and the election to use the net tax adjustment for financial institutions. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized under Canadian domestic law; however, the Income Tax Act includes deductible hybrid mismatch rules (legislative proposals) that restrict deductions where treaty or structural mismatches arise. An Alberta corporation may also elect a fiscal year end and choose the method of capital cost allowance, but no optional pass-through tax classification exists at the federal level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies threshold for GST/HST small supplier treatment; however, non-resident corporations making taxable supplies in Canada that are not small suppliers must register for GST/HST regardless of whether they have a permanent establishment, and certain foreign entities may be required to register when they solicit orders, advertise, or carry on commercial activity in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of finished garments are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act. The zero rating applies when tangible personal property is supplied to a non-resident and is exported from Canada, or is delivered outside Canada, as part of the supply. Services, such as brokerage, logistics, or off-price resale services relating to overseas clients, may be zero-rated if provided to a non-resident person and supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST. The taxpayer must retain documentary evidence that ownership passes outside Canada and that the goods were exported within a reasonable time.

  • Authorized Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration (B13A or Canada Export Reporting System confirmation)
  • Bills of lading / airway bills / freight forwarding evidence
  • Commercial invoice with terms of sale and FOB export destination
  • Customs export declarations and tracking proof
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank transfer confirmation showing foreign-currency payment
  • Foreign buyer purchase order and non-resident status documentation

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital furnace control. Tax invoices must contain prescribed information such as vendor identity, BN/GST/HST registration number, date, description, and tax amount. Electronic invoices are accepted, but taxpayers must retain audit evidence. The CRA requires point-of-sale records for retail sales and may inspect accounting systems. No government approval of invoice software is currently required; however, digital bookkeeping and electronic records must be preserved for audit in accessible and legible format.

CRA audits export claims strictly. If zero-rating is ineligible or export documentation is missing, GST/HST becomes payable at 5% with interest from date of supply. The CRA may assess a penalty up to 25% of the tax amount if false statements or careless omissions are made, and may impose gross negligence penalties up to CAD 5,000 or more, plus 50% of tax improperly refunded. Intentional fraud can lead to criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. Retroactive clawbacks are not limited to a four-year reassessment period where misrepresentation is involved; an unlimited reassessment may occur for fraudulent returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted currency convertibility under IMF Article VIII; there are no controls on current account transactions, capital account movements, or dividend/royalty/profit remittances for non-resident shareholders. Profit repatriation is subject solely to commercial solvency requirements under the Canada Business Corporations Act and relevant withholding tax obligations (typically 25% unless reduced by treaty, e.g., 5%-15% under the Canada-United States tax treaty). No prior exchange control approval is required for denominating transactions in foreign currency or settling invoices from offshore counterparties. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely floating; banks and licensed foreign exchange dealers provide market-rate conversion without central bank intervention.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements in Canada. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency, convert them at market rates, or use them for direct offshore disbursements. Receipts from apparel off-price wholesale liquidation may be held in USD, EUR, or other currencies in offshore or domestic accounts without mandatory repatriation or compulsory conversion. Entities must only ensure proper bookkeeping and tax reporting in CAD for income tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT enforcement

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for ancillary treasury functions; Low as a primary bank for capital escrow. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Stripe Treasury, Payoneer) may be used for commercial cross-border payments and FX conversion, but they are not chartered banks in Canada. To serve Canadian clients, such EMIs must generally register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and comply with Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canadian banks will normally require a domestic operating account as the anchor, with EMIs used solely for settlement efficiency, not custody of bulk liquidity. Legal constraint: an unregistered offshore EMI that accepts Canadian-domiciled funds may itself violate Canadian AML regulations and expose the corporate treasury to compliance risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the Alberta corporate treasury must be routed over SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for direct wires with full remitter/beneficiary information. The payment purpose should be encoded as 'Foreign Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (CA purpose code reference: 7FCG or equivalent under the Bank of Canada Balance of Payments coding) to distinguish from trade settlement. Verification steps include: (a) confirmation that the sending financial institution is not subject to Canadian sanctions under OFSI/OSFI vetting standards; (b) matching the remitter name with the shareholder registry; (c) adherence to FINTRAC reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000 for electronic funds transfer) using the Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR); (d) settlement in CAD via LVTS/Lynx or a conventional wire transfer by the receiving Canadian bank. No exchange control approval is required.
