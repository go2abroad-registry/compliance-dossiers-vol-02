Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; principal portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement, so no separate Alberta filing portal is used for corporate tax.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) (ABCA) or the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) and obtain the certificate of incorporation; if incorporated in another Canadian jurisdiction, file extra-provincial registration in Alberta through the Alberta Corporate Registry. Step 2: Register for the federal Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1; the BN is the sole statutory tax identifier in Canada and carries program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll). Step 3: Register for GST/HST (RT account) when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or, for non-residents, when carrying on business in Canada; voluntary registration is available and advisable for an exporter of APIs to zero-rate export sales and claim input tax credits. Step 4: No separate Alberta corporate tax registration is necessary: the T2 corporate return filed with the CRA allocates income to the Alberta permanent establishment and applies the Alberta 8% general rate (2% small business rate on the first CAD 500,000). Step 5: A non-resident corporation carrying on business through a Canadian branch must also file T2 and may face branch tax under ITA s. 219. Step 6: Before commercial wholesale of APIs in or from Canada, obtain a Health Canada Drug Establishment Licence (DEL) and, where relevant, an export permit under the Food and Drug Regulations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a resident Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no pass-through regime applies; Canada taxes resident corporations on worldwide income with foreign tax credits; Canada has no territorial election and no tax-exempt zone. Non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source income and may be subject to Part I.14 branch tax.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box election. Relevant elections: (i) ETA s. 156 election to treat supplies between closely related corporations as made for no consideration (useful for group API wholesale flows); (ii) ETA s. 273(1) election to form a GST/HST group; (iii) ITA s. 85(1) rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of property into the corporation; (iv) ITA ss. 216 and 216.1 elections (if the corporation has Canadian rental or pension-source income). Hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6) apply compulsorily, with no elective carve-out, and disallow or defer deductions for deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies measured over the current calendar quarter and the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA small-supplier rule). A person who is not a small supplier must register; non-residents carrying on business in Canada must register even below the threshold where they make taxable supplies in Canada. Suppliers below the threshold may register voluntarily to zero-rate export sales and recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under ETA ss. 39-40 and Schedule VI. Part V (goods): supply of APIs by sale to a non-resident who will export the goods, provided the goods are shipped to a destination outside Canada by the supplier, the purchaser, or a common carrier; goods delivered to a common carrier must be exported within 60 days (ETA s. 39(2)). Part VII (services): services supplied to a non-resident (e.g., warehousing, logistics, brokerage) are zero-rated if not performed in relation to real property located in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply and the recipient is not a registrant. Note: APIs without a Canadian Drug Identification Number do not qualify for the Schedule VI Part I drug zero-rating by product status alone; the export rules of Part V are the operative route for cross-border API sales. The supplier bears the burden of proving physical export through contemporaneous commercial and customs documentation; absent such proof, the supply is taxable at 5% GST and net tax is reassessed.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) / ACI eManifest for exports with a value of CAD 2,000 or more (or any controlled goods), citing the appropriate HS subheading for the API
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or CERS cargo report evidencing shipment to the non-resident destination outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice and executed sales contract (Incoterms terms) identifying the non-resident purchaser
  • Inward remittance evidence: SWIFT MT700 letter of credit and bank wire advices from the non-resident buyer evidencing payment settlement
  • Health Canada export permit and licence-to-deal documentation where the API is listed as a controlled or regulated drug substance

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API clearance, or digital stamping regime. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE/EDI) is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1.5 million; all other registrants may file online voluntarily. E-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Pan-Canadian interoperability framework based on the international PEPPOL/UBL standard; ordinary B2B invoices satisfying ETA s. 169/223 and the Information Regulations (recipient, date, description, consideration, GST amount or zero-rating statement) remain legally sufficient, with a six-year record-retention rule.

CRA will reverse (claw back) the zero-rating and assess 5% GST on the full selling price, plus daily compound interest, where CERS/BOL documentation, the 60-day export rule, or recipient-residency criteria are not met; ITCs claimed on inputs to the failed export may also be denied. General reassessment windows: 4 years for GST/HST (ETA s. 298) and income tax (ITA s. 152(4)), extended to 6 years for large corporations and non-arm's-length or non-resident-related transactions. Civil penalties: ETA s. 280(1) - CAD 500 plus 1.5% of the disallowed tax for false statements or misrepresentations. Criminal penalties (ETA s. 285(1) / ITA s. 239): on indictment, fines from 100% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST of a corporation under ETA s. 323. Non-repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days does not itself negate zero-rating, but may trigger transfer-pricing recharacterization and reassessment under ITA s. 247 where the settlement is not arm's length.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible and Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement, meaning both current and capital account transactions are generally free from exchange control restrictions. Dividend, management fee, interest and royalty profit remittances are permitted without Bank of Canada approval, but cross-border payments remain subject to transaction-level reporting thresholds ($10,000 CAD or equivalent for cash, and FINTRAC large virtual currency transaction reporting), regulatory compliance screening and withholding tax obligations on payments to non-residents under the Income Tax Act and applicable income tax treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or to effect mandatory FX conversion. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency; however, exporters must comply with trade reporting obligations, including customs declarations to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), the maintenance of supporting commercial documentation under the Customs Act, and FINTRAC record-keeping rules for every transfer received from outside Canada of $10,000 CAD or more in the course of business, in addition to applicable economic sanctions screening.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank); financial sector regulatory oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada has no separate dedicated foreign exchange control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating for cross-border treasury reliance on offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) is Low-to-Medium. Canada does not issue an 'Electron Money Institution' licence; non-bank payment service providers fall under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and must register with the Bank of Canada, while fintech transfer entities operating as Money Services Businesses must be registered with FINTRAC. Offshore EMIs cannot definitively act as primary clearing conduits without a Canadian presence or applicable provincial money-services licensing and, for pharmaceutical API wholesale operations, corporate treasury should treat EMIs only as supplemental rails for low-risk disbursements, preserving prime cross-border clearing on a tier-1 domestic commercial bank and SWIFT.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The financial institution will apply source-of-funds verification, FINTRAC transaction monitoring where the transfer exceeds $10,000 CAD, sanctions screening, and may require proof of the inbound corporate resolution from the ultimate parent, audited financial statements, banking references and a certificate of incumbency. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate tax rate (federal 15% + Alberta 8%); for qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations, the federal small business deduction reduces the combined rate to 11% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% general withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder; treaty reductions apply, e.g., 5% under the Canada-US DTA for a US corporation owning at least 10% of the Canadian payer's voting stock, and 10% under the Canada-China DTA for portfolio dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but anti-avoidance doctrines under s.245 (GAAR), common law sham doctrine, and the OECD Multilateral Instrument’s Principal Purpose Test can invalidate structures lacking real commercial substance. A parent company in a low-tax jurisdiction receiving API distribution profits from Alberta must demonstrate substantive management, employees, assets, and risk allocation; otherwise profits may be recharacterized as Canadian-source business income or dividends subject to Part XIII withholding.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements (Criminal)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under section 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fine of not less than 50% and up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment for up to five years; also a civil penalty under s.163(2) equal to the greater of 50% of the understated tax or CAD 100, depending on the neglect.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or knowingly direct illegal expense claims can be personally assessed under the Corporations Act/Income Tax Act; CRA may freeze personal bank accounts, issue a taxpayer’s exit block notice, and seek restraint orders. Criminal fraud can trigger passport surrender or a travel ban until resolution of proceedings; officers may face restitution orders for amounts evaded plus interest and penalties.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under s.247(3), a penalty of 10% of the total transfer-pricing adjustment for corporations where the adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 5,000 and 10% of gross revenue; in addition, s.163(2) gross negligence penalties may apply up to 50% of the resulting tax deficiency. If no TP adjustment is made but T106 is unfiled, penalties remain as detailed above up to CAD 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are not automatically liable for corporate TP penalties, but they can be personally exposed for authorizing filings that intentionally misrepresent transactions; CRA may use shareholder/director liability clauses in cross-border judgments and seek asset freezes against related non-resident individuals. In egregious cases of abusive intra-group pricing, the CRA can reassess the foreign affiliate classification or deem dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, exposing directors to broader fiduciary litigation.
Artificial Shell Company / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: The CRA will apply s.245(2) to void the tax benefit and reassess the Canadian corporation as if the transaction had not occurred; a 5% penalty plus 110% of the appropriate prescribed rate interest on the tax deficiency may apply. The Court may also overturn treaty relief under the Principal Purpose Test (MLI) and levy Part XIII tax at the full 25% default rate.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve shell transactions known to be abusive can be personally liable for the corporation's unremitted withholding tax liabilities under s.227.1 or s.227.2. A director’s personal assets can be garnished, and a director who participates in a scheme to strip Canadian-source profits by forging a foreign intermediary may be charged with conspiracy to evade tax, resulting in a travel ban and imprisonment.
Withholding Tax / Non-Resident Distribution Default

Statutory sanction: For unremitted Part XIII withholding, the Crown may assess the Canadian payer under s.215(6) for the full amount, plus interest, and may exact a penalty of 10% or more under s.227(8); failure to remit source deductions can attract an additional 10% civil penalty and Directors' liability assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 for withholding taxes the corporation failed to deduct and remit, including dividends paid abroad; CRA can issue a requirement and levy directors’ personal assets. Where the director knowingly structured payments into a tax haven without remittance, criminal prosecution can result in travel bans, extradition proceedings, and up to seven years' imprisonment under related Criminal Code fraud provisions.
