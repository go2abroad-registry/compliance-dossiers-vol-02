Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), operating through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at canada.ca, the My Business Account portal, and GST/HST NETFILE; Alberta corporate income tax is administered concurrently by the CRA under the Canada-Alberta Tax Collection Agreement (single T2 return, no separate provincial TIN or portal).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (federal) or the Alberta Business Corporations Act (provincial) and obtain the incorporation certificate or letters patent from the Alberta Corporate Registry or Corporations Canada, noting the Alberta registered office address. Step 2: Obtain the federal Business Number (BN) online via Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts.' Step 3: Attach the required program accounts to the BN: 'RP' for corporate income tax (T2), 'RT' for GST/HST, 'RC' for payroll if the entity will employ Canadian staff, and 'RM' for import/export linkage to the CBSA customs account. Step 4: A non-resident foreign corporate shareholder that is 'carrying on business in Canada' must disclose its foreign incorporation jurisdiction, foreign registration number, the date it commenced Canadian business, and the address of its Canadian permanent establishment; the CRA issues the BN and GST/HST account without requiring a Canadian postal address, but a Canadian agent or registered address is recommended. Step 5: Register the Alberta corporate number with the Alberta Corporate Registry (extra-provincial registration) if incorporated federally, and file the annual return. Step 6: Use the BN to file the T2 Corporation Income Tax Return within six months of the fiscal year-end and to file GST/HST returns electronically via NETFILE or the My Business Account API. Step 7: Retain all CRA notices of assessment and maintain a Canadian permanent establishment address for service of process and audit correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Canadian Business Corporation (federal or Alberta) is a separate legal taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act; it is taxed on worldwide income as a resident corporation if incorporated in Canada (subsection 2(1)), and a non-resident corporation is taxable on Canadian-source business income under subsection 2(2). No pass-through or transparent status is available to a corporation in Canada; corporate losses do not flow through to shareholders. Territorial scope: a Canadian-resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income with a foreign-tax credit under section 113; branch profits of a non-resident corporation are subject to federal and provincial Part I tax plus the 25% Part XIV branch tax, generally reduced to 5% under the Canada-US tax treaty (Art. XIII) for a US parent. Tax-Exempt Zone: Not applicable to Alberta; no free-trade-zone income tax holiday or qualifying offshore regime exists in the province.

Available election forms

Canada does not offer an Internal Revenue Code-style 'check-the-box' classification election; however, the following statutory elections are available to a Canadian wholesale distributor: (i) Excise Tax Act section 156 election for closely related corporations to treat intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration, eliminating GST on wholesale transfers of IVD reagents between affiliated Canadian entities; (ii) ETA section 156.2 special method election for selected closely related corporations; (iii) ETA section 167 joint election on the sale of a business as a supply of a business carried on by the transferor with deemed nil consideration; (iv) Income Tax Act section 85 rollover election for transferring Canadian property into the corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (v) ITA subsection 256(2) acquisition-of-control election to preserve loss carry-forwards; (vi) the GST/HST 'Quick Method' election (Form GST74/RC4) for small supplier wholesale distributors under ETA sections 221-223; and (vii) hybrid mismatch election relief under subsection 18.2(2) for synthetic branch mismatches with a US affiliate, to align Canadian and foreign recharacterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters (the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA subsection 148(1)); once crossed, GST/HST registration is mandatory within one month after the end of the quarter in which the threshold was exceeded. A foreign supplier located abroad but making supplies to Canadian customers may be required to register if it carries on business in Canada; voluntary registration is available to a non-resident wholesale distributor even below the threshold to recover input tax credits on Canadian inventory purchases and to affirmatively zero-rate exported diagnostic assays.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V. Section 1 zero-rates tangible personal property (TPP) supplied by the vendor for delivery to a destination outside Canada, provided the supplier possesses documentation proving actual exportation within 60 days after the supply. Section 7 zero-rates a service supplied to a non-resident where the service is performed wholly outside Canada or is rendered in respect of or in transportation of TPP exported from Canada. For wholesale distribution of clinical diagnostic ELISA assay kits and IVD reagents, the operative relief is Part V section 1: the vendor must ship the reagents to the non-resident buyer's foreign address, retain proof of physical removal from Canada (airway bill, ocean bill of lading, courier proof of delivery, Cargo Control Document), and evidence of settlement. Domestic supplies of the same reagents to Canadian hospitals remain taxable at 5%, unless the product qualifies under Schedule VI Part III (drugs/medical devices) as a licensed health product for human use, in which case a prescribed diagnostic reagent may be zero-rated when supplied for human administration; however, export zero-rating under Part V is the primary and more defensible basis for this pipeline.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) filed electronically through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) for goods valued at CAD $2,000 or more, citing HS code 3822.19 (diagnostic reagents, IVD) or 3002.15, with the CBSA exit confirmation or port stamp
  • Commercial invoice on company letterhead stating the seller and non-resident buyer, Incoterms (FOB, CIF, or EXW), unit values, and total consideration in CAD and foreign currency
  • Bill of Lading, outbound airway bill, or courier Cargo Control Document evidencing shipment to a foreign destination with named carrier and consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank SWIFT MT103 advice, wire transfer receipt, or TT settlement confirmation showing receipt of funds from the non-resident buyer, matched to the invoice value
  • Packing list with ELISA reagent lot numbers, kit counts, cold-chain temperature logs, and certificate of analysis for biomedical products
  • Certificate of Origin (if required by destination customs) and, for regulated IVD products, the Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence or drug/device notification reference when zero-rating is claimed under Schedule VI Part III
  • Freight forwarder's certificate of export or exporter's signed declaration confirming that goods left Canada within 60 days of transfer of possession

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls (CTCs) at the federal level; the CRA accepts electronic or paper tax invoices meeting ETA sections 221-223. For taxable supplies over CAD $30, the invoice must show the supplier's GST/HST registration number; for supplies exceeding CAD $150, the invoice must also state the invoice date, vendor and purchaser identity, description of the goods, and consideration. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or via the CRA My Business Account API; no pre-clearance or fiscal authorization code is required. For export shipments, all goods valued at CAD $2,000 or more must be reported in advance to the CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) using a B13A Export Declaration, which functions as the electronic clearance record; licensed customs brokers and freight forwarders use edoc/API interfaces for real-time filing, and the CBSA issues an exported-goods confirmation that substantiates zero-rating.

CRA audit scrutiny of export zero-rating claims is substantial. If the supplier cannot produce the B13A, shipping documents, and payment evidence, the CRA will reassess the export as taxable at 5% GST, disallow the corresponding input tax credits (ITCs) claimed on the exported inventory, and issue a refund clawback under ETA subsection 296(2), with prescribed-rate compounded daily interest running from the original return due date. Statutory penalties: false or fraudulent statements attract a civil penalty of up to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded under ETA section 280.1; gross negligence penalties under ETA section 280(2) and ITA subsection 163(2) apply where culpable conduct is shown. Reassessment limitation: normal reassessments of a GST/HST return may be raised within 4 years (6 years for a refund claim); the limitation period is suspended indefinitely where the misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. Criminal exposure: ETA section 327.3 authorizes summary conviction fines up to CAD $10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 1 year; indictable offences carry fines up to CAD $100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; where the false export scheme exceeds CAD $5,000, prosecution under the Criminal Code (fraud) may attract imprisonment up to 14 years. Directors and senior officers may be jointly and severally liable under the ETA for refunds obtained in error. Record-keeping is mandatory for six years under ETA section 232; CRA may additionally obtain CBSA customs transactional data and forwarder records for cross-border reconciliation and business-benefit audits. Compliance recommended: timestamped export evidence, matched FX receivables within the 180-day benchmark, and pre-claim review by a Canadian indirect tax advisor.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. No central-bank approval, licence, or exchange-control filing is required for conversion of CAD, payment for imported goods, profit remittance, dividend distribution, or inbound equity injection. The only statutory friction on outward profit remittance is Part XIII of the Income Tax Act: 25% non-resident withholding tax, treaty-reduced to as low as 5% for eligible corporate shareholders. Banking-level compliance, including FINTRAC wire reporting for transactions of C$10,000 or more, sanctions screening, KYC, and source-of-funds verification, is the operative gate on fund flows. Export or import controls under the Export and Import Permits Act, the Special Economic Measures Act, and the United Nations Act apply to certain goods and counterparties but do not restrict currency convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no surrender mandate, mandatory conversion ratio, or statutory timeline for export proceeds. A Canadian exporter may retain USD, EUR, or other foreign-currency receipts in domestic foreign-currency bank accounts and convert to CAD at free-market rates at its discretion. No Bank of Canada or OSFI filing is required on export receipts. For FINTRAC-reporting consistency and customs valuation alignment, the company must retain export invoices, bills of lading, and CBSA customs entries matching each bank credit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision of chartered banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); payment clearing infrastructure by Payments Canada (Lynx and ACSS).
