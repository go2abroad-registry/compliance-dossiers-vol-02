Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) is the competent federal authority for Part I corporate income tax and for GST/HST under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act (ETA). The primary registration and filing portals are Business Registration Online (BRO), CRA My Business Account, and the CRA Business Gateway API for third-party filers. Alberta corporate income tax (8% general rate) is administered and collected by the CRA on behalf of Alberta under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act (RSA 2000, c. C-28); the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) is the provincial counterpart responsible for non-income tax compliance programs.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Alberta Business Corporations Act; appoint a Canadian resident registered office and, if the beneficial owner is foreign, a resident agent for service of process. (2) Apply for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number', selecting the program accounts: RP (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RM (CBSA import/export), and RC (payroll, if Canadian employees are hired). (3) GST/HST registration is compulsory for any person engaged in a commercial activity in Canada whose worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148); an export-only wholesaler should nevertheless register voluntarily to recover input tax credits on Canadian warehousing, logistics, and overhead costs. (4) File the T2 Corporate Income Tax Return and the Alberta corporate tax allocation annually through the same BN; GST/HST returns are filed electronically via My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE, or the Business Gateway API. (5) Maintain the BN program accounts in good standing even in years of nil revenue; CRA imposes late-filing penalties under ITA s. 162 and ETA s. 280 and may suspend refund offsets.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A corporation incorporated in Canada is resident in Canada under common law and section 250 of the Income Tax Act; it is therefore taxable on worldwide income and no pass-through treatment is available. The small business deduction (9% federal plus 2% Alberta on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income) is restricted to Canadian-controlled private corporations and is unavailable where 50% or more of the equity is held by non-residents. Territorial scope is worldwide taxation for resident corporations; a non-resident corporation carrying on business through a permanent establishment in Canada would be taxed only on Canadian-source business income, but that model does not apply to the Canadian-incorporated subsidiary contemplated here. No tax-exempt zone exists in Alberta.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box classification regime. The relevant statutory elections are: (a) ITA s. 85 rollover election (Forms T2057/T2058) to incorporate contributed property at tax-deferred agreed amounts; (b) ITA s. 249.1 election to adopt a non-calendar taxation year; (c) ministerial discretion under ITA s. 256(9) regarding associated-corporation determinations; (d) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations to deem intercorporate supplies to be made for nil consideration; (e) ETA s. 180(1) election permitting a non-resident to appoint a resident accredited agent, making agent and principal jointly and severally liable for GST/HST - relevant for foreign-owned intra-group brokerage structures; (f) hybrid-mismatch outcomes are addressed automatically by ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5, 20.2 (effective for years beginning on or after July 1, 2022) with no protective election available to override them; (g) the GST/HST quick-method election is available only to resident small suppliers and is generally disadvantageous for zero-rated exporters.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully free-floating currency regime with unrestricted current and capital account convertibility. The Canadian Dollar (CAD) is freely convertible without exchange controls. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation may proceed without foreign-exchange administration approval, subject to corporate solvency tests under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) and applicable Canadian withholding tax on payments to non-residents (typically 25% statutory rate, subject to tax treaty reduction). Eligibility to obtain treaty benefits requires tax-residence certificates, and CA-AB corporate residency must be substantiated with Canadian board meetings, major supplier decisions, and inventory sourcing protocols. Cross-border wire transfers and large-cash transactions above CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Funds originating from, or destined to, sanctioned jurisdictions are prohibited under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act. No central-bank approval is required for converting export receipts or making foreign currency payments; however, Schedule I banks may apply their own compliance overlays on OFAC-listed or high-risk counterparties

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirements on export proceeds. There is no mandated timeline to bring export receipts into Canada, nor any mandatory conversion of foreign currency export earnings into CAD. Exporters may retain funds in foreign currency accounts with Canadian authorized banks. Nevertheless, the PCMLTFA requires that records of all export/import payments and associated wire transfers be retained for at least five years. Exporters must also report suspicious transactions and large cash or virtual-currency transactions to FINTRAC. The bank's internal AML policies may require proof of shipment within a reasonable commercial period to avoid trade-based money-laundering red flags, particularly in the spot semiconductor wholesale sector. To mitigate, the CA_AB treasury function should maintain fully reconciled digital ledgers of export trade documents and wire histories per transaction

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with supervision by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML/ATF regulation by FINTRAC

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderate feasibility with strict legal constraints. Non-resident Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) cannot provide electronic money services or payment accounts to Canadian residents unless registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where applicable, authorized under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with Bank of Canada oversight. Unregistered offshore EMIs holding Canadian client funds are non-compliant and expose the entity to bank de-risking. Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex may be used under a Canadian-partnered regulated model, but must not serve as the primary treasury custody or settlement layer. The recommended architecture uses a Schedule I bank as the primary clearing account, while regulated FINTRAC-registered payment service providers facilitate low-value vendor payouts and expense disbursements. A compliance package must include the EMI's FINTRAC MSB registration number, RPAA authorization or exemption, and contractual confirmation that funds are held in segregated settlement accounts at a Canadian or equivalent regulated financial institution

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate feasibility with strict legal constraints. Non-resident Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) cannot provide electronic money services or payment accounts to Canadian residents unless registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where applicable, authorized under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with Bank of Canada oversight. Unregistered offshore EMIs holding Canadian client funds are non-compliant and expose the entity to bank de-risking. Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex may be used under a Canadian-partnered regulated model, but must not serve as the primary treasury custody or settlement layer. The recommended architecture uses a Schedule I bank as the primary clearing account, while regulated FINTRAC-registered payment service providers facilitate low-value vendor payouts and expense disbursements. A compliance package must include the EMI's FINTRAC MSB registration number, RPAA authorization or exemption, and contractual confirmation that funds are held in segregated settlement accounts at a Canadian or equivalent regulated financial institution
