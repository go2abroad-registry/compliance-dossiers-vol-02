Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Head Office (Ottawa) and Calgary Tax Services Office for Alberta registrants; online portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (My BA), GST/HST NETFILE; Alberta provincial corporate tax is administered by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act, RSA 2000, c C-28, and no separate Alberta tax authority exists for income or GST purposes.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Extra-provincial registration of the foreign or interprovincial corporation in Alberta under the Business Corporations Act, RSA 2000, c B-9, s. 22 via the Alberta Corporate Registry (alberta.ca / Corporate Access), obtaining an Alberta Corporate Access Number (CAN). Step 2: Obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with program accounts from the CRA through BRO, Form RC1, or the Alberta one-stop online business registration window; the BN is the statutory TIN for GST/HST (RT0001), corporate income tax (RC0001), payroll (RP0001), and customs (RM0001) accounts. Step 3: A Canadian-resident operating corporation (CA_CORP) must file T2 corporate income tax returns (ITA s. 150(1)(a)); a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Alberta must also obtain a BN and file T2 under ITA s. 150(2)(a). Step 4: Register the GST/HST account once taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (ETA s. 240(1)); voluntary registration is available under ETA s. 240(3) and is recommended for used-machinery export traders to claim input tax credits (ITCs) on purchases. Step 5: Maintain the Alberta Corporate Registry annual return and update CRA BN program-account information concurrently; no separate provincial tax identifier is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian Business Corporation is a resident taxpayer under ITA s. 2(1) taxed on worldwide income; combined federal plus Alberta general corporate rate is 23% (federal 15% under ITA s. 123(1) plus Alberta 8%), with the Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) small business rate reduced to 11% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (federal 9% under ITA s. 125 and Alberta 2%); no pass-through or flow-through eligibility for corporations; no territorial carve-out (foreign business income is taxable subject to foreign-accrual-property-income rules and foreign tax credits); no tax-exempt zone status exists in Alberta.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box pass-through election; the CRA applies its own entity classification, and a US check-the-box election is disregarded for Canadian tax purposes. Available elections and regimes include: (i) ITA s. 85(1) corporate rollover election on transfers of property to a corporation in exchange for shares; (ii) ETA s. 156 joint election for nil consideration supplies between closely related corporations (e.g., parent and used-equipment trading subsidiary); (iii) ITA s. 216 election allowing non-residents to file a net-basis return on Canadian rental or timber income; (iv) ITA s. 217 election for non-residents with Canadian employment income; (v) Part XIV branch tax under ITA s. 219 (25% on non-resident corporation Canadian business profits, subject to treaty relief); (vi) mandatory hybrid-mismatch deduction-denial rules under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 (BEPS Action 2), which are non-elective; (vii) ITA s. 93(1) election with respect to dividends from foreign affiliates.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1): a person whose worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports, but excluding supplies of capital property, financial services and goodwill) in the current calendar quarter plus the preceding four calendar quarters do not exceed CAD 30,000 is exempt from mandatory GST/HST registration (ETA s. 240(1)); once the threshold is exceeded, registration is mandatory effective the first day of the following calendar month; voluntary registration under ETA s. 240(3) permits full ITC recovery and zero-rating of export sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of used construction excavators and rollers (HTS 8429.52) is available under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 1-3: (a) sale of tangible personal property delivered or sent by the supplier to a destination outside Canada (Part V s. 1); (b) sale under which the goods are shipped to a destination outside Canada (Part V s. 2); or (c) goods delivered in Canada to a non-resident purchaser and exported by that purchaser within 60 days of delivery (Part V s. 3), in each case provided the supplier obtains evidence satisfactory to the Minister of Export (CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17.4). Offshore commercial services on the equipment performed wholly outside Canada are zero-rated under Part V s. 7. The supplier charges no GST on zero-rated exports but claims full ITCs on acquisition costs under ETA s. 169; used goods acquired from non-registrants may attract a notional ITC equal to 5/105 of the purchase price under ETA s. 176. Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter in this dossier reflects the administrative audit benchmark for substantiating receipt of foreign-currency consideration from the non-resident buyer.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Declaration (Form B13A / CAED) filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) for all commercial exports valued above CAD 2,000, or an approved service provider record;
  • Bill of Lading (ocean: master/house B/L; land: carrier manifest referencing Canadian exit and US ACE e-manifest with US importer of record entry number);
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 payment advice matching the invoiced foreign-currency consideration and evidencing receipt from the non-resident buyer;
  • Commercial invoice with full machine serial/VIN, model, engine hours, condition report, and HTS 8429.52 classification;
  • Proof-of-export bundle under ETA s. 169 and CRA Memorandum 17.4: carrier certificate, freight-forwarder packing list, terminal receipts, US Customs entry summary, and photographic evidence of cross-border delivery.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital tax-invoice mandate exists in Canada; electronic invoicing is voluntary and the Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework (Peppol PINT standard, announced September 2024) is being phased in as the interoperable standard; mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1M (GST/HST NETFILE), and mandatory electronic payment applies to direct debits exceeding CAD 25,000; tax invoices under ETA s. 223 must disclose the supplier BN, invoice date, vendor and purchaser names, total consideration, GST charged, or an express indication that the supply is zero-rated.

On audit, the CRA may deny zero-rating where export evidence is deficient, reassess 5% GST on the full selling price, and charge compound daily interest under ETA s. 280.1; late-filing penalties under ETA s. 280 equal 6% of the unremitted net tax plus 0.2% per full month outstanding up to 24 months; false or fraudulent statements attract a gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 285 equal to 50% of the net tax understated; criminal prosecution for tax evasion under ETA s. 327 carries a fine up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to two years on indictment; on the income tax side, an unwarranted export claim may support a 50% gross-negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2), transfer-pricing adjustments under ITA s. 247 with a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment, and extension of the reassessment period to four years for non-resident corporations under ITA s. 152(4)(a)(i); the CRA may also cancel the exporter's GST/HST registration under ETA s. 242 and claw back previously claimed input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange-licensing requirements, no administrative approvals on routine remittances, and no statutory barriers on profit repatriation beyond standard Part XIII withholding tax (which may be reduced by tax treaty). Cross-border wire transfers equal to or exceeding C$10,000 are reported to FINTRAC, but this is surveillance only and does not constitute exchange-control authorization. Capital inflows for corporate formation, commercial debt, and equity investment are freely permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada does not impose any statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline. Exporters may hold foreign currency in domestic or non-resident accounts. For Alberta corporate income tax, export revenues and receivables must be translated into Canadian dollars under the Income Tax Act, generally using the relevant spot rate or a valid functional-currency election under section 261.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); transaction monitoring by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs are not recognized as banks under the Bank Act (Canada); they may operate only as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act for payment services. They cannot accept deposits, issue letters of credit, or hold escrow in a bank-grade manner. Practical viability is acceptable for FX conversion and small-value payout rails, but they are inadequate as a primary clearing bank for capital escrow, documentary collections, or commercial LC transactions involving used heavy machinery.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 disclosure threshold: CAD 50,000 aggregate value for transactions in each category (goods, services, interest, royalties) involving a non-arm's-length cross-border relation. Contemporaneous documentation must demonstrate arm's-length results under subsection 247(4) to avoid the 10% transfer-pricing penalty, which applies where a net adjustment in excess of CAD 50,000 is required or documentation is absent.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not implement a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime similar to offshore centres. The CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule (s.245) denying the tax benefit from pure tax-avoidance transactions lacking real business purpose, and recharacterises sham transactions under judicial doctrine. For used machinery wholesaling, substantive sales, inventory, bank accounts, personnel, and risk control must be provable in Canada to maintain treaty residence and Canadian sourcing integrity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (s.239 ITA)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to 200% of evaded tax plus up to CAD 10,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; civil assessments with 50% gross negligence penalties on the understated tax; CRA can freeze bank accounts and seize assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who assert control over the corporation are liable jointly and severally for Part XIII withheld amounts (s.227.1) and for unpaid wages under other statutes; for tax evasion, directors may be subject to direct criminal liability, restrictions on cross-border travel by order imposed pending prosecution, and on conviction, imprisonment.
Shell Company with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the corporation, reassess the shareholder or beneficiary on a look-through basis, deny treaty benefits, impose the 50% gross-negligence penalty, and issue notices of direction for withholding. Progressive civil penalties under Part XV may also be applied.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for corporate taxes and penalties if they deliberately benefit from the shell facade or fail to cause the corporation to collect remittable taxes; courts will lift the corporate veil where the corporation was a sham and a director exercised total control.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment if the adjustment exceeds CAD 50,000 and the taxpayer has not made reasonable efforts to document the arm's-length amount; additional interest on deficiency and daily late penalties on Form T106 non-filing.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for transfer-pricing penalties, but personal liability can arise if they approved false invoices or directed mispricing to avoid tax and thereby acted in gross negligence or wilful participation.
Money Laundering / AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under the Canadian Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, a record-keeping violation and failure to report a suspicious transaction can attract administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 500,000 for corporations; criminal charges for laundering proceeds of crime are punishable by up to 5 years imprisonment; FINTRAC can publish administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate, direct, or fail to implement reasonable controls to prevent double-bookkeeping and shell trade can be prosecuted as principals or accomplices; personal travel bans to countries with DTA enforcement may be imposed under criminal recognizance orders, and civil restitution is available in favour of corporate creditors.
