Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), the national tax administration responsible for federal corporate income tax and GST/HST, and (under the Canada-Alberta tax collection agreement) for Alberta provincial corporate income tax. Primary portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html), Business Registration Online (BRO) and the new CRA business registration service, together with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export reporting systems (Canadian Export Reporting System and related CBSA portals).

E-government filing portal

Step-by-step SOP for a foreign entity establishing a tax identity for an ISIC 4661 export wholesale operation in Alberta: (1) Obtain or confirm the Alberta extra-provincial registration through the Alberta Corporate Registry so the foreign corporation is lawfully carrying on business in Alberta; (2) Apply to CRA for the statutory Business Number (BN), a 9-digit tax identifier, using Form RC1 (Application for a Business Number) filed online via Business Registration Online or by mail; the application should request the RT program account for GST/HST and the RC program account for corporate income tax; because Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA, no separate provincial TIN is required; (3) If the entity is a non-resident corporation without a permanent establishment or physical presence, designate an authorized Canadian representative or agent for service and file the CRA non-resident registration package; (4) Register with the CBSA as an exporter for filing B13A / Canada Export Declaration, and open a Canadian-dollar settlement account with a Schedule I bank (or a recognized foreign branch) to receive Swift LC and TT Wire proceeds; (5) If the exporter will claim input tax credits on zero-rated exports, complete the voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240 even if below the small supplier threshold; (6) No minimum capital, no statutory license, and no bonding requirement attach to this wholesale commodity trading activity in Alberta.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a federal or Alberta business corporation (CA_CORP, ELF 8615) is treated as a separate entity-level taxpayer: resident in Canada under Income Tax Act s. 250(4) if incorporated in Canada, and liable for Part I federal corporate income tax (15% general federal rate) plus Alberta Part 4 corporate tax (8% general rate, 2% small business rate where eligible), both assessed on worldwide income subject to foreign tax credits; no partnership-style pass-through is available to the corporation. Territorial scope note: income is allocated to Alberta for provincial tax purposes using an agreed statutory apportionment formula where the corporation has establishments both inside and outside Alberta. No tax-exempt zone applies in Alberta or Canada; only targeted incentive regimes (e.g., SR&ED, investment tax credits, and certain Alberta Crown incentive agreements) can reduce tax. Non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax on dividends from this corporation: statutory 25%, reduced under applicable treaty (e.g., 5% for significant shareholdings and 15% for portfolio dividends under Canada-US Treaty Article 10).

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification regime. The statutory elections and designations relevant to a bitumen/asphalt trading corporation are: (a) ETA s. 156 GST/HST non-application election for taxable supplies between closely related corporations to eliminate GST leakage on intercompany logistics, storage, and financing; (b) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240 for zero-rated exporters, enabling input tax credit recovery for Canadian freight, warehousing, and container-handling costs; (c) ITA s. 219 branch tax parity election, relevant only if a non-resident parent operates a Canadian branch rather than the local corporation; (d) ITA foreign affiliate designations and s. 93 election where the Canadian wholesaler holds foreign selling affiliates; and (e) hybrid mismatch analysis under the BEPS Multilateral Instrument and bilateral treaty entitlement rules, under which a fiscally transparent foreign entity (e.g., a US LLC) may be disregarded for Canadian treaty benefits, but the Canadian corporation itself has no elective entity-classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small supplier threshold for GST/HST registration, computed as worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports and foreign sales) in the immediately preceding four calendar quarters plus the current quarter; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada or soliciting orders in Canada must register once this threshold is exceeded and charge GST/HST on Canadian domestic supplies; certain non-residents making taxable supplies of Canadian real property must register regardless of threshold; a zero-rated export-only trader may register voluntarily under ETA s. 240 even below the threshold to obtain input tax credits and recover GST paid on Canadian inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating eligibility under ETA Schedule VI, Part V for drummed/bagged penetration-grade bitumen sold by the Canadian wholesale corporation to non-resident buyers: (i) common-carrier export — the supplier sells to a non-resident and the goods are delivered to a common carrier (truck, rail, ocean, or air) for export from Canada, with exportation occurring as soon as is reasonably possible (CRA generally expects export within 60 days of delivery); (ii) recipient-export — the goods are made available to the non-resident buyer and are actually exported by the buyer within a reasonable time; (iii) non-resident export within 12 months — goods in Canada for less than 12 months before being exported by a non-resident person, applicable to containerized inventory held in Canadian transit hubs or temporary consignment warehouses; and (iv) zero-rated services — trading, logistics, marketing, and freight-forwarding services supplied to the non-resident buyer are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 as services supplied to a non-resident person, subject to carve-outs for services performed in Canada in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property or supplied to individuals present in Canada. The exporter must hold contemporaneous records proving the goods left Canada and reached the destination market; GST paid on export freight, drayage, terminal handling, and storage is recoverable as input tax credits under ETA s. 169 only where the exporter is a GST/HST registrant.

  • Bill of Lading (ocean, rail, or truck B/L with the Canadian port or rail exit, foreign discharge or destination point, shipment date, and INCO term)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-generated proof of inward remittance, e.g., SWIFT MT103 / TT credit advice, evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the Canadian corporate account from the non-resident buyer)

No real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no universal e-invoicing mandate currently applies in Canada. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns by all registrants with annual worldwide taxable supplies above CAD 1,000,000 (threshold lowered from CAD 1,500,000 for reporting periods ending after 2021), and requires that net tax remittances exceeding CAD 10,000 be remitted electronically. API and EDI integration is available through CRA digital services (GST/HST NETFILE, third-party software, and the GST/HST Internet File Transfer service). Separately, the CBSA requires electronic filing of export declarations (B13A / Canada Export Declaration) through the Canadian Export Reporting System for export shipments; no national clear-the-invoice type mandate exists.

The export zero-rating file is a CRA priority audit area for commodity traders (GST/HST Memoranda and Policy P-222). Where the documentary bundle is incomplete, the transactions are circular, or the goods never leave Canada, CRA will re-characterize the supply as taxable domestically and assess: (1) GST/HST at 5% on the full selling price plus arrears interest under ETA s. 280.1 (prescribed daily compound interest); (2) a false-statement penalty under ETA s. 285(1) equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded for each false or misleading statement made knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence; (3) additional penalties under ETA s. 280 and, for income tax, ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalties equal to 50% of the understated tax; (4) criminal tax evasion charges under ETA s. 327 and Criminal Code fraud, with fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years; (5) retrospective clawback and reassessment within the normal four-year period, extended to ten years where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, including reversal of zero-rating, denial of input tax credits, and immediate collection of net tax, penalties, and interest; and (6) personal liability of directors and officers under ETA s. 323 and ITA s. 227.1 for unremitted GST/HST and payroll amounts. To mitigate exposure, the exporter should treat the 180-day proceeds-repatriation benchmark and the full export document bundle as a compliance floor, retain records for at least six years, and maintain a reconciliation of each container export to bank remittances and destination-market customs entries.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the OECD liberalisation codes. There is no foreign exchange control, no restriction on the acquisition of CAD/USD, and no advance approval for profit remittance, dividend distribution, royalty or intercompany repayments. Non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax on dividends and certain payments (generally 25% unless reduced by treaty), and the remitting company must issue an NR4 statement. Convertibility of CAD to USD is unconstrained; banks apply standard negotiated spreads and deal at prevailing market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, conversion ratios, or repatriation deadlines in Canada. Proceeds may remain in foreign currency, be routed through a Canadian financial institution, or be converted at commercial bank rates. That said, any electronic funds transfer (EFT) of CAD 10,000 or more into or out of Canada must be reported to FINTRAC in accordance with PCMLTFA; physical import/export of currency or bearer monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000 must be declared to the Canada Border Services Agency at the border.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange monitoring and AML/ATF supervision fall under FINTRAC and OSFI, with no dedicated central bank exchange control branch.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for ancillary payments, Low for primary capital escrow or settlement accounts. Offshore digital banks and e-money institutions (EMIs) cannot accept Canadian deposits or provide a banking licence; if they provide virtual payment services in Canada, they must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, depending on products, obtain a money transfer license from a Canadian province or be exempt as foreign remittance companies. An EMI offering CAD-denominated account equivalents without Canadian registration is inadmissible. For corporate treasury in Alberta, a Tier 1 bank account is the only reliable rail for escrow, trade settlements and FX hedging.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections are executed by the non-resident parent or shareholder via SWIFT MT103 from a regulated commercial bank to the Canadian corporate account. The standard routing is: originating bank -> SWIFT network -> Canadian bank's correspondent (e.g., RBC's New York correspondent for USD) -> receiving account. For CAD payments, LVTS provides real-time gross settlement; for USD payments, Fedwire/CHIPS settlement through New York. Remittance instructions must include: purpose code 'FDIN' (financial direct investment) or 'INTC' (intercompany funds), a reference to the subscription agreement or shareholder resolution, and the invoice/corporate account identifier. The Canadian bank will verify the source of funds and may require a board resolution, signed authority matrix, certificate of incorporation, and a source-of-wealth questionnaire before the financing activities can commence.Outbound disbursements for bitumen/asphalt trade settlements are executed through: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct telegraphic transfer (TT) for open-account and prepayment structures; (ii) documentary collections under MT400/MT422 on D/P or D/A terms; (iii) commercial letters of credit (L/C) issued via SWIFT MT700 under UCP600, with advising, confirmation, and negotiation through a bank in the seller's country; and (iv) standby L/C in MT760 as performance security. For containerized freight, documentation includes a full set of clean, on-board container bills of lading, standard commercial invoice, packing list, third-party inspection certificates, and certificate of origin. Payment debits flow through Canadian bank's LVTS for CAD and CHIPS/Fedwire for USD. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium for ancillary payments, Low for primary capital escrow or settlement accounts. Offshore digital banks and e-money institutions (EMIs) cannot accept Canadian deposits or provide a banking licence; if they provide virtual payment services in Canada, they must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, depending on products, obtain a money transfer license from a Canadian province or be exempt as foreign remittance companies. An EMI offering CAD-denominated account equivalents without Canadian registration is inadmissible. For corporate treasury in Alberta, a Tier 1 bank account is the only reliable rail for escrow, trade settlements and FX hedging.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined: 15% federal general corporate rate + 8% Alberta general corporate rate. Qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) may use the small business reduced rate of 11% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (9% federal + 2% provincial).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; Alberta imposes no separate dividend withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no standalone statutory Local File/Master File monetary threshold; contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is required for all cross-border non-arm's-length transactions. Form T106 is mandatory when the aggregate value of reportable non-arm's-length transactions with a non-resident exceeds CAD 50,000 in the fiscal year; CRA expects full OECD-aligned documentation, including a transfer-pricing policy and benchmarking study, proportionate to the risk.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the offshore-centre sense are not applicable to Alberta, Canada. Canada instead applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act section 245), transfer-pricing rules (section 247), and the central-management-and-control residency test to deny treaty benefits or reassess transactions where the corporate structure lacks real economic substance, employees, premises, or risk-bearing control.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Invoicing or Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239(1): summary conviction carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and up to 2 years imprisonment; indictment carries a higher fine and up to 5 years imprisonment. CRA also reassesses the full tax, penalties, and compounded daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or participate in false filings may be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution, assessed personally under section 227.1 for unremitted source deductions; courts can impose travel bans and imprisonment where fraudulent intent is proven.
Shell Company / Artificial Treaty Interposition

Statutory sanction: CRA can disregard the structure under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and section 246, deny treaty relief, reclassify excess payments as deemed dividends subject to Part XIII withholding tax, and apply section 163.2 penalties for misrepresentation in a tax shelter/planning arrangement, up to CAD 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and promoters risk personal civil restitution, disgorgement of fees or profits, travel restrictions, and criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code for fraud or dishonest deprivation where the shell company is used to avoid Canadian taxes.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA adjusts the transaction under section 247(2). If the net adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 50,000 and 10% of gross revenue, a 10% transfer-pricing penalty applies under section 247(3). Interest, gross-negligence penalties, and retroactive reassessments may also follow.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fabricated transfer-pricing studies or knowingly implement false pricing are exposed to personal penalties under section 163.2, civil claims for restitution by the company or CRA, and criminal liability for fraudulent manipulation of cross-border trade documents.
Failure to File and Unremitted Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalties up to 17% or 20% of unpaid tax; failure to file information returns yields daily penalties up to CAD 2,500 per form. Unremitted Part XIII withholding tax is recoverable from the payer, plus a 10% penalty for failure to withhold under section 227(9) and prescribed-rate interest.

Director personal liability: Under section 227.1, directors are personally, jointly, and severally liable for all unremitted withholding taxes and source deductions, including interest and penalties; CRA can issue director-liability assessments, and courts may impose travel bans or imprisonment for deliberate non-filing.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act allows administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation; Criminal Code money-laundering offences carry imprisonment up to 10 years and forfeiture of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly facilitate trade-based money laundering face personal criminal liability, asset seizure and forfeiture, travel bans, disqualification from acting as directors, and piercing of the corporate veil for restitution to affected counterparties or the Crown.
