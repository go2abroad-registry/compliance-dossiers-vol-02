Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administration authority; official business registration portal is CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account (MyBA). Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA for the Government of Alberta (Alberta Ministry of Treasury Board and Finance) under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement. Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs import/export accounts through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) single-window platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate federally or extra-provincially register in Alberta under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) to obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number; Step 2 — Apply for a Federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), submitting articles/certificate of incorporation, registered office address, director and officer particulars, and appointing a Canadian tax representative (Form RC59) if no individual ordinarily resident in Canada is available; Step 3 — Open a GST/HST program account (RT) under the same BN, mandatory once taxable worldwide supplies in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; Step 4 — Register for a CBSA importer/exporter account (RM) for customs reporting; Step 5 — Register for a Corporate Income Tax account (RC) for the federal T2 return; Alberta requires no separate corporate tax registration — Alberta Corporate Income Tax is reported on the federal T2 return accompanied by the Alberta corporate tax schedule. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Alberta must file a T2 and may be subject to Part I federal tax, Part XIV branch tax, and the Alberta branch tax equivalent.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian incorporated business corporation is a separate taxable person under the Income Tax Act, subject to entity-level tax on all of its income; no pass-through treatment applies to this corporate vehicle. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian incorporated business corporation is a separate taxable person under the Income Tax Act, subject to entity-level tax on all of its income; no pass-through treatment applies to this corporate vehicle. Federal Part I general corporate rate is 15% (after the 10% provincial abatement), combined with Alberta general corporate income tax at 8%, yielding a total combined rate of approximately 23% on active business income; the Canadian-controlled private corporation small-business rate is reduced (Federal 9% plus Alberta 2% = 11%) on the first CAD 500,000 of qualifying active business income. Territorial_Scope: resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxed only on income from Canadian sources, plus an additional 25% branch tax on unremitted Canadian business profits. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable — Alberta has no tax-free zone for wholesale petrochemical trading; the Alberta Petrochemicals Incentive Program applies only to qualifying direct capital investment in new petrochemical manufacturing capacity, not to wholesaling or distribution of virgin polymer granules.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a United States-style 'check-the-box' election; entity classification is determined by Canadian tax law. Key elections and designations available in this context include: Section 85 rollover election for transfer of property to a corporation on a tax-deferred basis; Section 261 functional currency election permitting Canadian business income to be computed in a currency other than the Canadian dollar; Section 216 election for non-residents to file a Canadian tax return on certain Canadian-source rental or employment income; GST/HST elections under the Excise Tax Act, including the Quick Method election for small suppliers, joint elections among closely related registrants to apply the close-association rules, and the election to treat a branch office as a separate person for GST/HST purposes; and the hybrid mismatch and excess foreign tax credit generator rules (implemented domestically to give effect to the OECD/G20 BEPS Action 2 recommendations for taxation years beginning after 2022), which are applicable as mandatory anti-avoidance rules rather than elective mechanisms, although certain transitional designations and disclaimers may be available to restructure offshore check-the-box hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the GST/HST small-supplier threshold under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act. A person (including a non-resident carrying on commercial activity in Canada) must register for GST/HST once total worldwide taxable supplies, including supplies by associates, exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. The same CAD 30,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of intangible property and digital supplies under the simplified GST/HST registration procedure. Voluntary registration is permitted for any person engaged in commercial activity and enables input tax credit recovery and zero-rating of export supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of the supply of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules is available under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier exports the tangible personal property (TPP) as part of its commercial activity or delivers the TPP to a common carrier for export outside Canada. The goods must be exported before the goods are consumed, used, or installed in Canada and substantially all of the goods must be shipped within 60 days of delivery (or within a longer period accepted by the Minister of National Revenue). The supply to a non-resident purchaser is eligible only if the supplier retains documentary evidence that exportation occurred, including customs clearance data and proof of shipment to a foreign destination. Ancillary services rendered directly to a non-resident in respect of the exported goods (e.g., freight, handling, inspection, storage in transit) may also be zero-rated where the requirements of Schedule VI are satisfied. If the goods are supplied to a GST/HST registrant who is a non-resident but does not take possession in Canada, the sale may also qualify as a zero-rated export supply, rather than a taxable supply, provided proper evidence of foreign destination is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Form B13A filed electronically through the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) / CBSA single-window portal, showing HS 3901 (polyethylene) or HS 3902 (polypropylene), quantity, total value in Canadian dollars, and the exporter's Business Number
  • Commercial Invoice describing the polymer grade, quantity, unit price, Incoterms, value, and declaring the supply as zero-rated GST/HST export
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing shipment from a Canadian port/terminal to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Credit Advice (SWIFT or wire transfer confirmation) evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Packing List and freight forwarder delivery/warehouse release documentation linking the physical goods to the declared export transaction
  • Export Permit or Area Control List declaration where the goods are destined to sanctioned or controlled territories (virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules are not ordinarily subject to the Canadian Export Control List; a permit is required only in exceptional end-use scenarios)

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance mandate, government pre-approval of invoices, digital stamping, or API-based continuous transaction controls at the federal level. GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed GST/HST information on invoices and retain source records, including electronic invoices, in a readable machine format for at least six years. The CBSA mandates electronic presentation of export and import trade data through the CARM single-window environment and the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) system for goods requiring reporting. Pan-Canadian e-invoicing adoption under the PEPPOL standard is voluntary at the federal level; however, Québec has enacted mandatory B2B e-invoicing obligations for taxable suppliers registered for Québec QST with staged implementation from 2024 onward, which may indirectly affect national GST/HST compliance for affected head offices; Alberta has no such provincial e-invoicing mandate.

CRA may reassess a GST/HST return within the normal four-year limitation period, extended to twelve years where the exporter has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default; fraud-based misrepresentation may be reassessed without time limit. A zero-rated export claim without the required statutory export documentation is disallowed, and the 5% GST is recovered from the exporter with interest compounded daily at the CRA prescribed rate (9% per annum for corporate assessments as of 2025). Civil penalties include: the GST/HST false-statement penalty under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act (generally the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded); the federal income tax gross-negligence penalty under section 163(2) of the Income Tax Act equal to 50% of the amount of tax understated; and transfer-pricing penalties under section 247(3) of the Income Tax Act up to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment. Criminal liability under section 239 of the Income Tax Act for wilful evasion may result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years upon summary conviction. Where exported goods are subsequently returned to Canada or the foreign purchaser is found to be a shell entity, CRA may claw back the zero-rating, deny the related input tax credits, and recharacterize the transaction as a sham for both GST/HST and income tax purposes. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day treasury deadline creates heightened audit risk, as CRA may treat such receivables as uncollectible, reclassify them as contributions to foreign affiliated entities, or impose transfer-pricing adjustments with stacked interest and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII status; the Canadian dollar is a free-floating currency. No license, prior approval, quantitative limit, repatriation constraint, or conversion mandate applies to dividends, management fees, royalties, interest, loan principal repayments, or share capital repatriation from an Alberta Business Corporation to non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance hurdles are purely documentary: Canadian banks and payment intermediaries screen for sanctions under the Canadian Special Economic Measures Act and US OFAC regulations, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) requires the bank to issue a FINTRAC report for any incoming or outgoing transfer of CAD 10,000 or more. A bulk polymer wholesale distributor must also evidence commercial substance for every cross-border payment to avoid characterization as a sham or a transfer-pricing adjustment under subsection 247(2) of the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts for ISIC 4669 bulk petrochemical products. The Alberta distributor may retain 100% of USD or other foreign-currency export proceeds in a foreign-currency account with a Canadian Schedule I bank, may invoice and settle in any freely convertible currency, and may deploy export receipts directly for future feedstock purchases, freight, warehousing, or intercompany funding without Bank of Canada notification or approval. If foreign-currency positions are converted or held as part of treasury operations, the resulting foreign-exchange gains or losses must be accounted for in accordance with Canadian GAAP and reconciled with the taxpayer's functional currency election for Income Tax Act purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating as the monetary authority, financial stability supervisor of retail payment service providers, and lender of last resort; the Minister of Finance and Department of Finance Canada jointly administer Canada's Exchange Fund Account and official international reserves. Canada maintains no statutory foreign exchange control agency, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates the chartered banks that execute cross-border payments and derivative activities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM only as a secondary rail, LOW as the sole treasury banking layer. A foreign fintech EMI or e-money institution is not a chartered bank under the Bank Act and cannot take deposits, issue drafts, or act as an issuing or confirming bank for documentary letters of credit. Since September 2024, payment service providers handling Canadian retail payments must be registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act and supervised by the Bank of Canada; in addition, any digital platform performing money transfers must hold FINTRAC money services business (MSB) registration. For an ISIC 4669 bulk polymer trading entity, a foreign EMI may be used for low-value supplier disbursements and FX-netted settlements, but the HSBC-style 'bank-primary, EMI-secondary' architecture is the only commercially defensible structure: all operational liquidity, escrow capital, and LC traffic must remain with a CDIC-insured Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the investing parent's bank to the Alberta entity's Capital Escrow Account or Operational Account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, with field 72 remittance information clearly stating 'Equity Contribution / Shareholder Subordinated Loan — Trade Working Capital for Bulk PE/PP Granules Distribution under CA_AB scope'. The Canadian banking leg settles through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) and large-value transfer system (LVTS) for same-day CAD receipt. For any order amount at or above CAD 10,000 the bank automatically files the large cross-border wire transaction report with FINTRAC; no prior regulatory approval is required. To avoid a discretionary hold, the bank must receive before the wire: source of funds documentation, a corporate resolution authorizing the account and purpose, and a copy of the executed share subscription agreement. There is no CUSMA or customs-related approval for money movement, only AML/KYC verification.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 customer transfers for open-account payments, freight, insurance, customs duties, and intra-group treasury settlement; (2) irrevocable commercial documentary Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 by the Alberta entity's Schedule I bank in favour of polymer producers, with the seller's bank acting as advising or confirming bank under UCP600; (3) Standby Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT760 as performance or payment guarantees to feedstock suppliers; and (4) SWIFT MT101 or EDI interbank transfers for domestic CAD settlement with Canadian downstream customers. The fundamental rule for 4669 bulk petrochemicals is documentary LC as the default for high-value feedstock purchases, with MT103 wire transfer reserved for verified open-account balances and small cash disbursements. Each outward payment requires commercial invoice, bill of lading, proof of customs entry, and sanctions screening certification embedded in the bank's trade finance workflow.Outright CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/CNH spot and forward contracts under an ISDA Master Agreement or Canadian FX Master Agreement, freely executable without Bank of Canada approval given the floating exchange rate regime.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or future-convertible emerging-market currencies such as USD/CNH, USD/INR, and USD/BRL, transacted under the bank's derivatives authorization and subject to OSFI counterparty credit risk guidelines.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CAD/USD) to convert USD-denominated LC and purchase-price exposure into CAD-based cash flow performance.Vanilla FX call and put options and zero-cost collars on CAD/USD and CAD/EUR used to hedge polymer purchase contract price risk and buyer remittance conversion risk.FX cyclically settled swaps and forward liquidating swaps executed under the bank's derivative master, subject to the Bank Act derivative powers and OSFI corporate governance and margining requirements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM only as a secondary rail, LOW as the sole treasury banking layer. A foreign fintech EMI or e-money institution is not a chartered bank under the Bank Act and cannot take deposits, issue drafts, or act as an issuing or confirming bank for documentary letters of credit. Since September 2024, payment service providers handling Canadian retail payments must be registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act and supervised by the Bank of Canada; in addition, any digital platform performing money transfers must hold FINTRAC money services business (MSB) registration. For an ISIC 4669 bulk polymer trading entity, a foreign EMI may be used for low-value supplier disbursements and FX-netted settlements, but the HSBC-style 'bank-primary, EMI-secondary' architecture is the only commercially defensible structure: all operational liquidity, escrow capital, and LC traffic must remain with a CDIC-insured Schedule I bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal basic rate plus 8% Alberta provincial rate, for a combined statutory headline rate of 23% (before any small business deduction or general rate reductions).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder, unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty.

Due 6 months after fiscal year-end. For a December 31 year-end, the filing deadline is June 30 of the following year. Payment of tax is due on the filing deadline; instalments may be required for large corporations (monthly or quarterly).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory T106 disclosure and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File). Master File requirements apply above CAD 10,000,000 in aggregate revenue or related-party transactions. CRA may request documentation regardless of threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the economic substance doctrine via the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under ITA s. 245, which denies tax benefits from avoidance transactions lacking genuine commercial substance. For wholesale distribution, the Canadian entity must have real functions, assets, and risks. A 'contract distributor' or 'commissionaire' arrangement that merely passes title without operational substance is vulnerable. The CRA may apply transfer pricing recharacterization under s. 247(2) if economic substance is missing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act s. 239)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties on indictment: up to 5 years imprisonment and fines up to 200% of the tax evaded (minimum CAD 5,000). Summary conviction: up to 2 years imprisonment and fines up to 200% of tax evaded. Civil penalties: gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax plus interest and arrears penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize, participate in, or acquiesce to evasion may be convicted personally. They may also be jointly and severally liable for unremitted corporate tax withholdings under ITA s. 227.1. Courts may issue travel bans, freezing orders, and civil restitution. Criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The CRA may disregard the corporate entity, reallocate income to the actual economic owner, and assess tax plus penalties under s. 247(2). The GAAR may apply to deny benefits. A court may 'pierce the corporate veil' in serious cases, resulting in unpaid tax assessed against the shareholder or parent. Penalties can be 50% of the adjusted amount.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate tax if the shell is found to be a sham, especially if they signed false financial statements or knowingly contributed to the mischaracterization. Civil liability for restitution, potential director bans, and in criminal cases imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing (ITA s. 247)

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess using arm's-length price, impose a 10% quantum penalty on the transfer pricing adjustment (if net adjustment exceeds CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue), charge interest, and penalize late documentation. Penalties up to CAD 5,000 per month for late TP documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors are responsible for ensuring reasonable TP documentation exists. If a deliberate mispricing scheme is proven, directors may face gross negligence penalties, civil liability for restitution, and in criminal cases (fraud/evasion) imprisonment.
Money Laundering and AML Deficiencies (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative penalties: up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and CAD 1,000,000 for entities per violation, increased to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 10,000,000 for entities under recent amendments. Criminal penalties under the Criminal Code for laundering: up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who knowingly facilitate money laundering face criminal liability and imprisonment. FINTRAC can impose monetary penalties on senior officers. Directors may also face civil claims by creditors or regulators. Travel bans and asset freezes are possible under MLAT proceedings.
