Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at canada.ca/business-registration; ongoing account administration via CRA My Business Account (MyBA) for GST/HST returns and correspondence.

E-government filing portal

A foreign or Canadian-incorporated Business Corporation obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus a GST/HST program account (RT prefix) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or, for non-residents, Form RC1A (Request for GST/HST Registration of a Non-Resident), submitted electronically through BRO or by mail to the CRA Sudbury Tax Centre. Required particulars: certified certificate of incorporation or foreign equivalent, registered office address in Canada or agency address, full director/officer list, anticipated Canadian taxable revenue (CAD 30,000 threshold analysis), high-level business activity description (NAICS/ISIC mapping), and appointment of a Canadian tax representative if the non-resident carries on business in Canada. The CRA issues the BN/RT account within 5-20 business days; the account must be quoted on all sales invoices, customs documents, and GST/HST returns. Authorization of an accountant or legal counsel is completed via Represent a Client (RAC) with the Business Number and CRA user ID.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. Under the Income Tax Act (Canada), a Business Corporation (Federal/Provincial) is a separate taxpayer filing a T2 corporate income tax return; no pass-through election exists. General federal Part I rate is 38% reduced by the 10% provincial abatement and general rate reduction to 15%; Alberta corporate income tax is 8% flat (2025). The small business deduction (9% federal on first CAD 500,000) is restricted to Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs), so a non-resident-controlled e-commerce entity generally pays the full 15% general federal rate plus 8% Alberta rate. GST/HST registration is a separate entity-level collection identity. Territorial scope: tax applies to supplies made in Canada, including imported digital and tangible supplies; exported goods and certain offshore services are zero-rated (Schedule VI, ETA). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. Under the Income Tax Act (Canada), a Business Corporation (Federal/Provincial) is a separate taxpayer filing a T2 corporate income tax return; no pass-through election exists. General federal Part I rate is 38% reduced by the 10% provincial abatement and general rate reduction to 15%; Alberta corporate income tax is 8% flat (2025). The small business deduction (9% federal on first CAD 500,000) is restricted to Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs), so a non-resident-controlled e-commerce entity generally pays the full 15% general federal rate plus 8% Alberta rate. GST/HST registration is a separate entity-level collection identity. Territorial scope: tax applies to supplies made in Canada, including imported digital and tangible supplies; exported goods and certain offshore services are zero-rated (Schedule VI, ETA). No tax-exempt zone regime exists in Alberta for e-commerce operators.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections relevant to this structure include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration and streamlined non-resident registration (ETA s. 240(1)); (ii) functional currency election (ITA s. 261) permitting Canadian tax computations in USD; (iii) election to deem inventory sent cross-border to not be a taxable self-supply when goods remain owned by the vendor and move between Canadian and foreign jurisdictions; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 (draft rules applicable to taxation years beginning on or after October 1, 2025) disallow deductions on hybrid-financing and reverse-hybrid arrangements with no elective opt-out, though taxpayers may file structural re-characterization elections on prescribed forms; (v) Part I vs. Part XIV branch tax elections for non-resident insurers/carriers (not applicable to general retail); and (vi) GST/HST quick method election (ETA s. 226(1)) for eligible resident registrants, generally unavailable to non-residents, so full input tax credit (ITC) accounting is the default.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier exemption under ETA s. 148(1) when taxable supplies in Canada over four consecutive calendar quarters do not exceed CAD 30,000); mandatory registration for non-resident digital/e-commerce sellers and marketplace operators once taxable sales into Canada exceed CAD 30,000 per quarter, under the July 1, 2021 e-commerce supplier/marketplace regime (ETA s. 240(1.1) and associated distribution rules).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, item 1 where the registrant supplies tangible personal property by way of sale and exports the goods in accordance with regulatory evidence requirements. For an Amazon FBA generalized retail operation, the Canadian registrant may zero-rate goods shipped from Alberta inventory to foreign Amazon fulfillment centers (US/EU/UK) provided: (i) title and risk pass to a non-resident purchaser or the goods are removed permanently from Canada; (ii) continuous and contemporaneous proof of export is maintained; and (iii) the goods are not in Canada at the time of the underlying consumer sale. Cross-border digital services and intangible supplies to non-residents may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, items 22-23, excluding services delivered to an individual physically present in Canada. Domestic Alberta FBA sales are subject to 5% GST; FBA deliveries into HST provinces (ON 13%, NS 15%, etc.) attract the destination province rate under place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA electronic B13A filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System - CERS, mandatory for exports valued over CAD 2,000; lower-value shipments evidenced by waybill and shipper's records)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / multimodal transport document showing origin in Alberta and foreign destination port/fulfillment center

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government API pre-clearance, or digital stamping for GST/HST invoices. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records if complete, legible, and retained for six years (ETA s. 286(1); IC-05-1). The Pan-Canadian business-to-business e-invoicing framework (Peppol PINT/UBL standard, administered by the Canadian Peppol Authority) is voluntary. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE for residents, GST/HST MEMO for non-residents), with monthly/quarterly remittance schedules based on revenue thresholds; third-party marketplace platforms (e.g., Amazon) must report seller data under the expanded GST/HST reporting regime and the new mandatory platform information reporting rules (CRA Information Return on Web Platforms) effective 2025.

Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline in the ETA; the 180-day benchmark operates as the internal treasury and documentation protocol used to demonstrate a bona fide commercial export and is expressly referenced because CRA treats extended unrepatriated receivables as an indicator of off-book or non-export structuring. CRA audit triggers include persistent zero-rated refunds exceeding input tax credits, repetitive export claims with photocopied waybills, and shipments to foreign fulfillment centers without corresponding final consumer sale records. Penalties: (i) failure to register/remit (ETA s. 280) - CAD 250 simple; greater of CAD 250 and 25% of net tax if knowingly; (ii) gross negligence penalty (ETA s. 285) - 25% of the net tax falsely claimed; (iii) false statements and omissions (ETA s. 327.1) - CAD 2,500, or greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax for knowing conduct; (iv) criminal tax evasion (ETA ss. 327-333) - fines up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to five years; (v) reassessment limitation extended beyond the standard four-year window to any period where misrepresentation is established (ETA s. 298); and (vi) retrospective clawback of all zero-rated amounts with interest compounded daily through arrears, together with denial of future refunds and public disclosure under CRA's Voluntary Disclosures Program only if disclosure precedes audit contact.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for residents and non-residents under a freely floating exchange-rate system. There are no restrictions on profit remittances, dividend distributions, or repatriation of invested capital for an Alberta business corporation. Dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to non-resident withholding tax of 25%, potentially reduced under applicable tax treaties (e.g., Canada-US Treaty may reduce to 5-15% depending on ownership levels). This withholding obligation is a revenue-collection measure only and does not limit the convertibility or transferability of funds. Transfers are subject to standard AML reporting thresholds but require no administrative authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory surrender requirement on export proceeds. Exporters are free to retain foreign currencies, hold receipts in offshore or onshore foreign-currency accounts, and are not bound by any conversion ratio or mandatory repatriation timeline. Export proceeds received through Canadian banks are subject to FINTRAC reporting when an international electronic funds transfer equals or exceeds CAD 10,000, and must be recorded for corporate income tax and GST/HST purposes, but no foreign-exchange surrender obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the central monetary authority; however, Canada imposes no comprehensive foreign-exchange control regime. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) is the financial intelligence unit and AML/ATF supervisor under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. No separate government agency grants exchange-control permissions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High with legal caveats. Cross-border electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Payoneer, and Stripe are widely used by Amazon FBA sellers and may lawfully hold funds for Canadian corporate clients. If an EMI provides money-services business (MSB) activities from within Canada, it must be registered with FINTRAC and may need provincial licensing in select provinces. Canadian corporations may maintain settlement accounts with offshore EMIs without breaching exchange controls, but they should not rely on an unregistered offshore EMI as the sole banking channel because it cannot provide Canadian-dollar clearing via ACSS or Lynx, nor direct payroll or tax remittance in CAD. Use of an EMI as a supplementary foreign-currency collection and disbursement layer is viable and common.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders injecting equity or shareholder loans should route funds via SWIFT MT103 with the corporate account details, stating the purpose as 'contribution to share capital' or 'shareholder loan' in the payment narrative. For amounts at or above CAD 10,000, the remitting bank must identify the sender; the receiving Canadian bank will request a source-of-funds attestation and may complete a FINTRAC large international electronic transfer report. No government pre-approval is required for ordinary foreign direct investment; however, if the injection exceeds CAD 50 million, Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting is mandatory. Funds should be converted through the bank's FX desk only after arrival, using the corporate spot rate, and then credited to the CAD operating account via the ACSS for quick availability. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% (federal Part I 15% after general rate reduction plus Alberta flat 8%); 11% combined rate for Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPC) eligible for the federal small business deduction (9%) and Alberta small business deduction (2%) on the first C$500,000 of active business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax under ITA s.212(2) on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; no separate Alberta dividend withholding because Alberta corporate tax is federally administered under the Tax Collection Agreement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due within six months after fiscal year-end; balance-due date is two months after year-end, extended to three months for a CCPC claiming the small business deduction; instalments are computed on prior-year, current-year, or graduated estimates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 – Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) is mandatory when the aggregate annual value of reportable cross-border related-party transactions equals or exceeds C$1,000,000, or when outstanding related-party loans/indebtedness exceed C$1,000,000; contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation is legally expected above this threshold; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 (approximately C$1.1 billion).

Economic substance scope: Alberta and Canada do not apply Gulf-Cooperation-Council-style Economic Substance Regulations; equivalent safeguards operate through the central management and control residency test, the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s.245, including the 25% GAAR surcharge), and substance-over-form recharacterization; an Amazon FBA shell corporation with no Canadian office, employees, or board decision-making power is exposed to (a) reassessment of income to the real economic owner and (b) denial of treaty benefits under the OECD MLI principal-purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False or Omitted Returns

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of tax plus compounded interest; criminal fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment (up to 2 years on summary conviction) under ITA s.239(1); civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under ITA s.163.2; additional late-filing penalties of 5% to 20%.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or acquiesce in false returns commit an offence; Canadian courts may pierce the corporate veil for fraudulent conduct; CRA can pursue personal garnishment and restitution orders; tax debts above C$50,000 can trigger passport denial or revocation procedures; directors also face civil fiduciary claims from the corporate estate.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: 10% penalty on the net transfer-pricing adjustment under ITA s.247(3); T106 late-filing and failure penalties in addition to reassessment; disallowance of expense deductions and corresponding interest on deficient instalments.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatic insurers of transfer-pricing accuracy, but personal liability attaches where they knowingly sign false documentation; CRA may assert secondary liability under ITA s.160 against shareholders or directors who receive diverted corporate value; criminal exposure arises if TP manipulation is part of a fraudulent tax-evasion scheme.
