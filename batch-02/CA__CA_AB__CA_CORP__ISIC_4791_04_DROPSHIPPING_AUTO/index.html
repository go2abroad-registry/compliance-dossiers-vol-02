Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Alberta provincial corporate tax administered under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act via CRA under the Alberta/TFS agreement

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; the GST/HST account is procured under the BN program as a separate program account (RT identifier, format BN-RT0001 with a four-digit account suffix). For Alberta registration, the entity must incorporate with the Alberta Corporate Registry (via Corporate Registry online portal; Form REG-01) or obtain an extra-provincial registration number if federally incorporated; the Alberta corporate income tax account is aligned to the federal BN. Dropshipping entities should open the GST/HST account concurrently with the BN request to enable immediate sales tax compliance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — an Alberta Business Corporation (provincial or federal) is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; it is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income if Canadian-resident, with no US-style pass-through classification available under Canadian federal or Alberta provincial law. — Foreign entity must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; the GST/HST account is procured under the BN program as a separate program account (RT identifier, format BN-RT0001 with a four-digit account suffix). For Alberta registration, the entity must incorporate with the Alberta Corporate Registry (via Corporate Registry online portal; Form REG-01) or obtain an extra-provincial registration number if federally incorporated; the Alberta corporate income tax account is aligned to the federal BN. Dropshipping entities should open the GST/HST account concurrently with the BN request to enable immediate sales tax compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Alberta Business Corporation (provincial or federal) is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; it is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income if Canadian-resident, with no US-style pass-through classification available under Canadian federal or Alberta provincial law.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Canada; hybrid mismatch elections are not available under the Income Tax Act or Excise Tax Act. Relevant elections for cross-border e-commerce include: (i) GST/HST election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for nil consideration supplies among closely related corporations; (ii) joint venture elections under ETA section 273 and ITA section 216(6); (iii) section 216 ITA election for non-resident income from real property or timber; (iv) transfer pricing contemporaneous documentation election (Form T106, treaty-based waivers); (v) capital cost allowance elective classes; and (vi) functional currency election under ITA section 261 for Canadian dollar reporting.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small supplier threshold measured over any four consecutive calendar quarters (GST/HST); a non-resident dropshipping vendor carrying on business in Canada must register once worldwide taxable supplies made to Canadian customers exceed CAD 30,000 in a 12-month period. Under the non-resident GST/HST registration framework effective July 1, 2021, deemed registration and collection obligations may also arise for platform operators and non-resident suppliers of goods supplied to Canadian recipients via fulfillment or marketplace models, with a corresponding threshold of CAD 30,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under ETA Schedule VI for goods that are exported by the supplier (delivered by the supplier to a destination outside Canada, removed from Canada within prescribed time) and for specified services supplied to non-residents (ETA Schedule VI, Part V s. 7 and s. 8). Automated dropshipping: (i) goods physically shipped from Canadian fulfillment centers to a foreign consumer qualify for 0% GST/HST where the supplier maintains documented proof of export (carrier waybill, customs export declaration) and the goods leave Canada within 60 days; (ii) goods sourced by the foreign vendor from a foreign fulfillment location and delivered directly to a foreign consumer are outside the Canadian GST/HST system entirely; (iii) goods shipped to a Canadian consumer (including through a Canadian marketplace) are taxable at 5% GST and cannot be zero-rated merely due to the seller's foreign residence. The dropshipper must be a GST/HST registrant to claim input tax credits and to issue a zero-rated invoice for export goods; voluntary registration is required if total supplies are below the CAD 30,000 threshold.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (CUSDEC / CCA — Canada Customs Automated Export Declaration) filed within 15 days of export for controlled or regulated goods; required for goods valued above CAD 2,000 and for special export permits
  • Commercial Invoice (detailed description, HS classification, stated export sale terms) and purchase order
  • Bill of Lading (through or direct, evidencing foreign destination beyond Canada) or air waybill
  • Carrier cargo control document or customs broker outbound summary evidencing export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice / PayPal-Stripe settlement statement documenting foreign consideration and FX repatriation

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, cryptographic stamping, or pre-clearance e-invoice API regime. GST/HST registrants must issue tax invoices containing prescribed content under Excise Tax Act sections 221–223 (supplier BN and GST/HST account number, date, description, and tax payable); simplified invoices are permitted for non-taxable small supplies. Returns are filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or CRA My Business Account within one month of the fiscal quarter-end (monthly filers: one month); e-invoicing in EDI/PDF/XML is commercially accepted but not legally mandated. Alberta adds no provincial invoicing overlay.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST generally within four years of the return date; the limitation period is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default is established. False export zero-rating claims are subject to penalties under ETA section 280 (greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the net tax) and section 285 for false statements; ITA section 163(2) imposes a 25% gross negligence penalty on tax reported for which the taxpayer made false statements knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence. Retrospective clawbacks include full reassessment of tax at 5% GST plus arrears interest (prescribed compound interest) and, in aggravated cases, criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act and Criminal Code, director liability for corporate GST/HST remittances under ETA section 323, and denial of input tax credits for documentation failures. CRA audit focus areas for dropshipping include verification of warehouse-in origin, legitimate foreign delivery, documentary chain of custody, and matching of remittance evidence to export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully and freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance is unrestricted for non-resident shareholders, subject to domestic withholding tax on dividends (typically 25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, reduced by treaty), provincial/federal corporate good standing, tax clearance, and reporting obligations under the Investment Canada Act for non-resident acquisitions above statutory thresholds. No prior exchange-control approval or surrender requirement exists for offshore transfer of capital, dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds in foreign currency. Exporters may retain, convert, or disburse foreign currency receipts freely; statutory reporting is limited to information required by FINTRAC for large transactions and by Statistics Canada for trade-balance compilation, not exchange-control permission.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration and has not imposed exchange-control restrictions under the Currency Act or the Bank of Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. Offshore digital EMI institutions (e.g., EU-authorised e-money institutions) do not receive deposit-taking recognition under the Bank Act and cannot provide Canadian-designated payment accounts as banks. Such entities may operate only as money-service businesses (MSB) after FINTRAC registration and, in some provinces, licensing under money-services statutes; they can support card acquiring and payout aggregation, but core treasury liquidity must reside with a Schedule I bank. Enhanced due diligence applies to all non-regulated or offshore payment intermediaries because Canada treats quasi-banking as carrying on business in Canada, potentially triggering provincial licensing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing in Canada uses SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) through the receiving Bank of Canada participant, with mandatory KYC source-of-wealth documentation attached to the remitting instruction. Recommended purpose code in the SWIFT field 70/72 must describe equity participation or loan funding, e.g., 'Share subscription CA_AB corporation', 'Shareholder loan (non-resident)'; remittance must be mirrored by a securities filing under the Alberta Business Corporations Act, directors' resolution and share register entry; non-resident shareholder contribution exceeding CAD amounts relevant to Investment Canada Act triggers notifiable acquisition review. Money-laundering proceeds cannot enter via PayPal or card aggregators; high-value injections must clear through Lynx (CAD RTGS), and equivalent USD wire may settle via SWIFT or Fedwire through the Canadian bank's New York correspondent.Outbound supplier payments are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 customer transfers in USD/EUR/CNY/HKD and, where speed is not critical, through commercial documentary collections. Open-account dropshipping settlement is best served by SWIFT MT103 to suppliers with Straight-Through Processing, or by global payment rails such as Visa B2B, or bank-integrated mass-payout APIs for repetitive automated disbursements. A documentary letter of credit may be used for large, intermittent inventory procurements but is atypical for automated retail dropshipping due to high transactional friction and STP latency. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23.0% combined federal and Alberta general corporate income tax rate (15% federal Part I rate plus 8% Alberta general rate).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Income Tax Act subsection 212(2) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Alberta imposes no separate dividend withholding tax; the federal Part XIII withholding system governs.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed no later than six months after the end of the corporation's fiscal period. For a December 31 year-end, the due date is June 30 of the following calendar year. The balance of tax payable is due no later than two months after fiscal period end, or three months for an eligible CCPC claiming the Small Business Deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000. Under Income Tax Act section 247, a Canadian taxpayer must prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for all non-arm's length cross-border transactions when the aggregate value of those specified transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the taxation year. This serves as the local file. A master-file orientation applies for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 1 billion subject to country-by-country reporting to CRA. Alberta imposes no separate local threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the general anti-avoidance rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, which denies a tax benefit from an avoidance transaction unless the transaction has a genuine economic substance and a bona fide business purpose. Alberta has no offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations, but CRA also applies common law substance-over-form and sham doctrines. An automated dropshipping corporation incorporated in Alberta but managed and operating solely through a tax-haven shell with no employees, no office, and no meaningful activities in Alberta will be treated as lacking economic substance; CRA can reallocate income and impose penalties under GAAR, transfer-pricing rules, and gross-negligence provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under Income Tax Act section 239(1): on indictment, a fine of 100% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years; on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 100% plus imprisonment up to 2 years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) is 50% of the understated tax, and the normal reassessment period becomes unlimited. Interest accrues on all assessed amounts.

Director personal liability: A director who authorizes, participates in, or knowingly acquiesces to the false reporting can be individually prosecuted as a party to the offence. CRA may compel civil restitution, pierce the corporate veil for fraud, and seek personal liability for assets improperly transferred out of the company. Criminal charges and bail conditions can prevent international travel, and conviction results in imprisonment.
Shell Company and Sham Transactions

Statutory sanction: CRA will ignore the shell under the sham doctrine or apply section 245 GAAR to deny the tax benefit. The taxpayer is reassessed for the full Canadian tax, gross-negligence penalties of 50% may be added, interest runs from the return due date, and the promoter of such an arrangement can be penalized separately under section 237.1. In criminal flag cases, asset seizure and forfeiture may follow.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause or participate in the sham may be held jointly and severally liable where courts pierce the veil for actual fraud or misrepresentation. Promoter penalties can be imposed personally on professionals or directors who market or execute the shell structure, and criminal exposure can trigger travel restrictions pending trial.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable income under subsection 247(2). Subsection 247(3) imposes a penalty equal to 10% of the net upward transfer-pricing adjustment unless the taxpayer demonstrates reasonable efforts. Failure to file T106 attracts CAD 100 per day, to a maximum of CAD 25,000. Interest is charged on reassessed tax, and treaty mutual agreement relief may be denied if documentation is absent.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct false information returns can be prosecuted for making false statements. Where diverted funds are transferred to a director's personal account, CRA can assess that director as a transferee under section 160 and collect the unpaid tax and penalties from personal assets. Travel restrictions may be imposed in the context of criminal proceedings for fraud.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance and Intentional Structuring

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties against a corporation can reach CAD 500,000 per violation. Criminal money laundering under Criminal Code section 462.31 is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years. Cross-border physical currency violations under the Customs Act can result in seizure and forfeiture, and unreported foreign transfers may trigger CRA undisclosed-income penalties equal to 50% of the benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and de facto managers who knowingly structure or direct transactions can be charged as principals or accessories. Courts may lift the corporate veil to seize funds hidden in mixed personal or foreign accounts. Civil restitution is recoverable from directors personally, and bail conditions or criminal travel restraints may be imposed during prosecution.
