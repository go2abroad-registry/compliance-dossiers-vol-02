Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital portal: My Business Account via https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA on behalf of Alberta under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a statutory Business Number (BN) by: (1) registering online through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or submitting paper Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number); (2) creating the applicable program accounts (RT for GST/HST, RC for payroll, RP for corporation income tax); (3) for non-residents operating through a Canadian digital distribution platform, GST/HST registration is required where taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold or where the platform is deemed to make supplies in Canada under the July 1, 2021 e-commerce framework; (4) the BN is generally issued within 5-10 business days for online filings; (5) a separate Alberta corporate income tax account is not required because Alberta's corporate income tax is collected federally.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; a Business Corporation incorporated in Canada is a resident corporation taxed under ITA Part I on worldwide income. Available statutory elections include: (1) GST/HST annual filing and quick-method elections for qualifying small suppliers; (2) ITA section 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of property to a corporation; (3) elections under ITA ss. 216/216.1 for non-residents to file on a net basis for Canadian-source rental and service income; (4) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4-18.7 apply with mandatory adjustment rather than elective opt-out; (5) for entities fiscally transparent in a foreign jurisdiction, Canada determines classification by domestic law, with no mirror election, and treaty/domestic characterization governs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier exemption); non-resident digital platform operators and sellers of taxable goods/services through a Canadian distribution platform are deemed to make taxable supplies in Canada and may be required to register regardless of a Canadian physical establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act applies to: (a) tangible personal property exported from Canada by the supplier or at the supplier's direction within 60 days of delivery, supported by satisfactory export evidence; (b) services supplied to a non-resident person where the service is not performed in Canada or, if performed in Canada, is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; and (c) intangible personal property (including platform access, software, and marketplace listing or intermediation services) supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada. A B2B cross-border marketplace platform supplying listing, subscription, or commission-based intermediation services to non-resident merchants is generally eligible as a zero-rated supply of services to a non-resident under Sch. VI Part V, s. 7, unless the service relates to real property in Canada or to goods situated in Canada when the service is performed.

  • Customs export declaration (electronic filing via CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System or Form B13A) for goods valued above CAD 2,000 or controlled/prohibited goods
  • Commercial invoice or sales contract evidencing the sale and export obligation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier naming the Canadian supplier and the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/SWIFT wire confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Cargo control documents or destination-country customs entry documentation where available

No real-time fiscal clearance regime, no mandatory e-invoicing, and no digital stamping or API clearance requirements. The CRA does not pre-authorize invoices; taxpayers must retain all electronic and paper sales and export records for six years, and any e-invoices must be accessible in readable format. Suppliers must display their GST/HST registration number on invoices, and B2B platform operators must maintain auditable transaction trails for supplies facilitated in Canada.

CRA audits zero-rating claims by requiring proof of actual export and receipt of payment. Failure to maintain satisfactory books and export evidence results in reassessment at 5% GST, plus arrears interest and penalties. Knowingly false statements or gross negligence attract a penalty under ETA s. 285 of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax understated, and exposing the exporter to ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalties (50% of understated income tax). CRA may reassess beyond the normal four-year period where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default. Export proceeds are expected to be repatriated within 180 days of shipment as a commercial and documentary norm; failure to repatriate does not automatically claw back zero-rating, but it weakens commercial substance in subsequent audit challenges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There are no surrender, licensing, or prior-authorization requirements for inward or outward capital transfers. Profit remittances (dividends, management fees, royalties, interest) are freely repatriable, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, which may be reduced under the Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement (CUSMA) or an applicable tax treaty, and subject to supporting transfer-pricing and corporate-documentation requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in Canadian or offshore accounts without surrender; however, all international electronic funds transfers (EFTs) and incoming/outgoing wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC and are subject to AML/KYC documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (the monetary authority and fiscal agent for the Government of Canada); foreign exchange administration lies with the Department of Finance (Canada) through the Exchange Fund Account. Canada maintains no standalone foreign-exchange-control authority, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates federally chartered banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low to Medium: Canada does not have an Electronic-Money-Institution licensing framework. Fintech cross-border payment providers (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, or similar) generally operate as money-service businesses registered with FINTRAC rather than as banks; they are acceptable for low-risk operational collections and disbursements, but they cannot legally hold client funds as a deposit-taking escrow account. Capital escrow and treasury settlement should be maintained with a Schedule I or Schedule II chartered bank operating in Canada.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection from a non-resident parent into the Canadian corporate treasury is executed via SWIFT MT103 with standard international remittance purpose codes: 'EQUI' (equity injection), 'LOAN' (intercompany loan), or 'EXTR' (capital account credit) as required by the receiving bank. The bank must receive a board resolution approving the injection, a subscription agreement or loan agreement, source-of-funds documentation, and a beneficial owner certificate. Settlement is made through the Canadian large-value payment system Lynx (RTGS) or same-day ACH under Payments Canada rules; all wire amounts equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 are reported to FINTRAC, and the transaction is recorded as a direct investment with Statistics Canada under the CPA program if thresholds are met.Outbound trade settlements for the B2B marketplace use: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier and cross-border platform disbursements; (ii) commercial letters of credit issued under UCP600 and confirmed by a Canadian clearing bank for larger bulk-import orders; and (iii) CAD and USD wire transfers through RTGS Lynx and CHIPS/ACH correspondent hubs. For Chinese suppliers, a dual-track rail is available using either standard USD SWIFT clearing or cross-border RMB (CNH) through CIPS with the onboarding bank maintaining a RMB correspondent account; for domestic Canadian vendors, payments execute via Payments Canada (E-mail Money Transfer for low-value items, or EFT/ACH for batch disbursements). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23.0% combined standard headline rate for a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) located in Alberta: 15% federal general corporate rate plus 8% Alberta provincial general corporate rate (Alberta rate held at 8% since the 2019-2020 reduction schedule was paused).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax (ITA s.212(2)) on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder; a lower treaty rate must be proven before the payment is made or the excess can only be recovered by filing a Canadian non-resident tax return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 — aggregate value of all non-arm's length cross-border transactions with the related non-resident in the fiscal period (including platform-service fees, intercompany commissions, royalty and interest flows) triggers the s.247(4) requirement to prepare and, upon CRA request, provide contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. A Master File and Country-by-Country (CbC) report are required where the MNE group's consolidated revenue reaches the C$750M (€750M-equivalent) CbC reporting threshold; for the typical B2B marketplace operator below that threshold, the Local File / T106 documentation set is the controlling obligation.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but an equally binding substantive-equivalence regime operates through: (1) ITA s.245 GAAR, which denies tax benefits arising from avoidance transactions lacking bona fide economic substance/subjective purpose; (2) ITA s.247(2), permitting the CRA to re-price or re-characterize intercompany transactions that do not reflect what arm's-length parties would have agreed (substance over form); (3) common-law sham/agency doctrine, which can collapse a hollow Alberta corporation into its Chinese parent; and (4) case-law beneficial-ownership and LOB-style analysis under the Canada–China DTA, refusing treaty protection to shell recipients with no direction/disposition of the income. The Alberta corporation should maintain a physical head office, employees in Alberta, real board meetings, active bank management, and contractual ownership of the platform's Canadian customer contracts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / willful non-declaration

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1) criminal conviction — on summary conviction, a fine of 50%–200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment of up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of 100%–200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax is applied as an alternative or in addition to interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or authorize the false return are personally prosecuted (imprisonment terms apply to individuals). The CRA can also pierce the corporate veil where the company was used as a sham, and directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unremitted source deductions and GST/HST (s.227.1 / ETA s.323) even without intent.
Failure to file / chronic late filing (T2, T106, T1135, GST/HST)

Statutory sanction: T2 late-filing penalty of 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month (max 12 months), doubled (10% + 2%/month, max 20 months) for a repeat failure within 3 years; daily compounding arrears interest at the CRA prescribed rate (base T-bill rate + 4%); T1135 information penalty of C$25/day (up to C$2,500, rising to C$12,000 for 24-month+ defaults); T106 penalty of C$25/day per form (up to C$2,500).

Director personal liability: Directors of an insolvent company are assessable under s.227.1 for source deductions and under ETA s.323 for GST/HST remittances (a director used as a shield cannot avoid liability). A travel/criminal-hold risk arises where the CRA commences a tax evasion inquiry alongside the civil assessments.
Transfer-pricing documentation failure / non-arm's-length pricing

Statutory sanction: s.247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment (applied where the adjustment exceeds the greater of C$5,000 and 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue, and the taxpayer failed to make reasonable efforts/documented positions); full tax reassessment at the 23% combined rate plus arrears interest; non-deductibility of 'unreasonable' intercompany charges.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed an intercompany invoice scheme that artificially relocated Alberta profit expose themselves to s.239 criminal prosecution if the understatement is deliberate; civil director liability can arise for unlawful distributions made after the company became unable to pay its tax debt.
Shell company / artificial interposition / GAAR abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s.245) denies the abused tax benefit (SBD, treaty relief, loss utilization) and allows reassessment without a limitation-period extension (the 'avoidance transaction' standard); the CRA may also collapse the shell as an agent/sham under common law, and a s.247 re-characterization can impute revenue directly to the platform principal.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the veil where the corporation is a mere agent/shadow of the Chinese parent; directors of the shell face civil restitution for diverted corporate opportunities and are exposed to the penalties described for tax evasion where the interposition was known and intended to deceive the CRA.
AML / FINTRAC non-compliance in cross-border payment rails

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties (FINTRAC) of up to C$500,000 per violation for corporations (and C$1M+ for repeat/aggravated violations); Criminal Code s.462.31 laundering-of-proceeds offence: on indictment, imprisonment up to 10 years and fines at the court's discretion for the corporation; failure to register as a money services business in a province where applicable attracts separate provincial enforcement.

Director personal liability: FINTRAC holds directors/officers to a 'reasonable measures' standard; the responsible AML compliance officer can be personally named in enforcement actions, and deliberate failure to report suspicious transactions can trigger personal criminal liability (up to 10 years in prison) for officers directing the conduct.
