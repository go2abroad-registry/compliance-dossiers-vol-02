Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); Business Registration Online and My Business Account portal at www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes.html, with GST/HST NETFILE filing and BN (Business Number) issuance via the central registry.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian-incorporated Business Corporation obtains its 9-digit BN automatically through the corporate registry (Federal Corporations Canada or Alberta Corporate Registry via ABConnect). The BN is activated for income tax upon incorporation and for GST/HST by filing Form RC1A or through Business Registration Online (BRO). A foreign-entity seller without a Canadian incorporation files Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with CRA, designating the GST/HST program (RT) and providing evidence of foreign registration, a Canadian address for service or authorized representative, and a description of the Canadian commercial activity; non-resident sellers may also apply electronically under the simplified GST/HST non-resident registration module before effecting taxable supplies in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Inc./Corp. is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act; it is not pass-through, and all income is computed and taxed at the corporate level before any dividend distribution, with Alberta corporate income tax levied on the same taxable income. No territorial election is available for this structure; Canadian-resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income, while capital-gains and foreign-affiliate rules apply.

Available election forms

No United-States-style check-the-box election exists. Relevant Canadian elections for this cross-border resale structure include: (i) the GST/HST closely related corporation election under section 150 of the Excise Tax Act to treat taxable supplies among related group members as non-supplies; (ii) the section 216 election permitting a non-resident corporation to compute Canadian-source rental or timber income on a net basis; (iii) the section 219 deemed non-resident taxation election for Canadian subsidiaries of foreign parents and associated surplus-dividend elections under subsection 112(1); (iv) optional streamlined GST/HST registration and non-resident rebate elections (e.g., public service body rebate equivalents) for the foreign vendor; and (v) hybrid-mismatch alignment elections and documentation for fiscally transparent foreign entities (including US LLCs) under the hybrid mismatch rules of the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies and financial services) in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; exceeding the threshold mandates registration and remittance. For non-resident sellers of tangible personal property (including secondhand luxury goods) facilitated through a distribution platform deemed to be the supplier, the platform operator bears registration and collection duties, and the foreign seller is exempt from GST/HST registration for those platform-facilitated supplies. Direct sales by the foreign vendor to Canadian consumers outside a platform, or importations of goods for local resale, attract the standard threshold and separate import GST obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, supplies of tangible personal property delivered by the vendor (or by the purchaser taking the goods abroad) to an address outside Canada are zero-rated at export. Eligible vintage-luxury resale items (handbags, watches, jewellery, collectibles and apparel) qualify when: (i) the online vendor ships or delivers the goods directly to a non-resident buyer outside Canada; (ii) title and risk of loss pass to the non-resident buyer; and (iii) export occurs as soon as reasonably practicable (CRA administrative practice generally requiring export within 60 days of sale). Services supplied to a non-resident that are performed entirely outside Canada, or that directly relate to goods situated outside Canada at the time of supply (e.g., sourcing/consignment agent services for an offshore seller), may also be zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Canada Customs B13A Export Declaration (or authorized customs broker export report) filed with CBSA via the Canadian Economic Development Agency or the Export Reporting System, identifying the goods, value, and destination country.
  • Bill of Lading or International Transport Evidence: ocean Bill of Lading, airway bill, or postal/courier waybill (Canada Post, FedEx, UPS) with complete tracking data and customs release stamps showing delivery to an address outside Canada within the export window.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC): bank-issued documentary evidence of foreign-currency settlement (SWIFT MT103 or escrow/payment processor settlement ledger from Stripe or Escrow Pay) demonstrating receipt of payment from the non-resident buyer and matching the invoice, foreign currency, and invoice amount.

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or API-based invoice clearance regime at the federal level. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE when annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million or in all cases for newly registered bank/credit-union cases; electronic remittance is compulsory for registrants with monthly or quarterly reporting. The CRA is a participant in the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and the PEPPOL network, requiring PEPPOL e-invoices for federal B2G procurement; additionally, Revenu Québec mandates EDI (e.g., ANSI X12 or EDIFACT) or equivalent electronic invoicing for selected large suppliers and for certain B2B transactions in Quebec. No digital stamping or prior approval of invoices exists for B2C e-commerce export resale.

Export zero-rating is a high-risk audit profile for the CRA. In the absence of a valid B13A, transport document, and IRC, the CRA disallows the 0% rate, reassesses the sale at 5% GST with compounding daily arrears interest, and extends the normal four-year reassessment window indefinitely where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation. Civil penalties include a failure-to-file penalty of 1% of the unreported net tax plus 25% on repeated failures, a 'gravely negligent' penalty of 25% of the net tax avoided under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, and denial of refunds. Knowingly creating fraudulent export declarations (fabricated bills of lading, simulated shipments, sham consignment partners) constitutes an offence under section 327, punishable by fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment for up to five years; CRA may also publicly name and debar offenders, seize bank accounts, and apply the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) to transaction recharacterization.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating Canadian dollar with unrestricted convertibility on both current account and capital account. No exchange controls, no capital-transfer approvals, and no profit-remittance restrictions exist for a federally incorporated Alberta business. Dividends to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (generally 25%, treaty-reduced to 5-15% depending on recipient jurisdiction), but remittance itself is unencumbered. Statutory institutional constraints are limited to FINTRAC reporting on CAD 10,000+ international electronic funds transfers and, where non-resident investors acquire control, Investment Canada Act notification/review thresholds. This enables the luxury-resale treasury to hold USD/EUR proceeds in segregated accounts and to pay consignors freely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No Canadian exchange-control law imposes export-proceeds surrender; there is no statutory timeline and no required conversion ratio. Vintage luxury resale exports may be settled and retained in USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency. The only binding obligation is the PCMLTFA duty on financial institutions to report international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more, and the corporate obligation to keep full invoice, customs, authentication, and payment records for seven years for CRA and OSFI audit verification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign-exchange administration duties shared by the Department of Finance Canada and prudential reporting to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); transaction monitoring and cross-border currency-flow reporting are enforced by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a regional settlement accelerator, but Low as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business) are not authorized under the Bank Act to accept demand deposits and cannot provide the regulatory stack required for FINTRAC-compliant corporate treasury in Alberta. A Canadian-controlled private corporation may use an EMI solely as a collection gateway for low-margin, low-UK/Europe corridors, provided the main Canada bank is disclosed, the EMI never holds capital-escrow balances, and transaction volumes remain below CAD 25,000 per payment. Legal constraint: any hold of CAD customer funds by an unlicensed EMI triggers a compliance breach and may cause the charter bank to de-risk the primary account.
