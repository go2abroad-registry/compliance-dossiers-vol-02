Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement for Alberta business corporations. Shares may be issued at par value stated in the Articles of Incorporation; no escrow account or paid-up capital deposit is required under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian corporation is a resident taxpayer separately assessed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income; Alberta levies no provincial corporate income tax, so the effective federal rate is 15% general (9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income under the small business deduction); inter-corporate dividends are deductible to the recipient; no special attribution regime applies to active retail income; controlled foreign affiliate rules may apply where a foreign subsidiary earns passive income.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US check-the-box regime; entity classification follows the legal form of the organization. Statutory elections available to corporate taxpayers: (a) subsection 216(1) ITA election to report Canadian-source net rental income; (b) subsection 217 ITA election for non-residents to compute tax on Canadian employment income on the Part I basis; (c) joint GST/HST election under subsection 273(1) ETA to treat closely related corporations as a single registrant; (d) hybrid mismatch arrangement rules under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Budget Implementation Act 2022, effective for payments on or after July 1, 2023) which disallow deductions or impose inclusions to neutralize deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction mismatches; no formal entity classification election is available to override the default corporate characterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — a supplier becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant when worldwide taxable supplies by the entity and its associates exceed CAD 30,000 in one calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive quarters; small-supplier voluntary registration is available with an input tax credit recovery window starting one month before registration. Non-resident vendors supplying digital goods or services to Canadian residents must register and collect GST/HST without the small-supplier threshold under the July 1, 2021 simplified regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property, including cosmetics shipped to foreign consumers, are zero-rated under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act for supplies of goods delivered by the supplier or its agent to a destination outside Canada; retail direct-to-consumer export sales qualify if the supplier retains proof of export and the goods leave Canada within a reasonable time. Services performed wholly outside Canada and supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST are also zero-rated; online orders fulfilled from a Canadian warehouse must have shipment documentation naming the foreign destination; no zero-rating is available for goods consumed in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA B13A export report or electronic Canadian Export Declaration filed at the port of exit for commercial shipments
  • Bill of Lading, Waybill or courier proof of delivery (Canada Post, UPS, FedEx, DHL) showing export routing and foreign consignee address
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order or contract documenting the cosmetics product description, quantity, unit value, exporter identity and foreign customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer / foreign exchange credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the corporate bank account within the repatriation window

No statutory real-time clearance or digital stamping mandate. Canada operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) by registrants whose annual taxable supplies, including those of associates, exceed CAD 1,500,000. Audit-grade electronic document retention is mandatory; invoices are not pre-cleared by CRA but must be issued and retained in a legible, machine-readable format for the duration of the reassessment window.

CRA may reassess GST/HST within four years of the date of the notice of assessment, and indefinitely for misrepresentation, fraud or wilful default, to claw back tax not substantiated; zero-rating documentation must be produced within 30 days of an export-verification audit request. False or grossly negligent claims trigger a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax understated under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, plus prescribed daily-compounded arrears and instalment interest at CRA prescribed rates (currently 7% per annum). Knowingly false export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act, prosecutable as a criminal offence with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment; CRA also applies export clawback red-flags where foreign receipts remain unrepatriated beyond 180 days.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates under a fully convertible and free-floating exchange-rate regime for the Canadian dollar (CAD) pursuant to the Currency Act, following the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951. There is no legal bifurcation between capital-account and current-account convertibility; profit remittance, dividend distributions, royalty payments, branch repatriations, and intercompany payments are unrestricted and do not require central-bank consent. Operational frictions are limited to (i) FINTRAC reporting obligations for all cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, (ii) Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding tax on non-resident remittances (statutory 25%, typically reduced under an applicable tax treaty), (iii) bank source-of-funds documentation for large inbound credits, and (iv) commercial FX spread and processing fees imposed by the clearing bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no fixed conversion ratio in Canada. An exporter of cosmetics and personal-care products may freely hold, convert, invest, or repatriate foreign-currency proceeds. Compliance obligations are confined to: (i) income-tax conversion of foreign receipts at the Bank of Canada daily published noon exchange rate, (ii) GST/HST zero-rating for qualifying exports under the Excise Tax Act, (iii) FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000) applicable to foreign currency exchange and cross-border electronic fund transfers, and (iv) customs audit obligations under the Customs Act (B3 export declarations for controlled or high-value outbound shipments).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating jointly with Payments Canada (clearing and settlement systems including LVTS, ACSS, and Real-Time Rail), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential regulation, and FINTRAC for anti-money-laundering transaction reporting. Canada does not require any central-bank license or prior FX authorization for corporate treasury operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a principal settlement bank. Offshore digital EMIs and neobanks cannot directly clear CAD obligations through Payments Canada LVTS unless they obtain Payments Canada membership or, in practice, rely on a Schedule I Canadian chartered bank sponsor. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection from a non-resident shareholder is routed by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the Tier 1 Canadian bank account. The remittance narrative must specify 'inbound capital subscription - issuance of shares under the Canada Business Corporations Act' or 'paid-up capital contribution'. No Bank of Canada approval is required; the receiving bank performs OFAC sanctions screening, beneficial-ownership verification, and FINTRAC cross-border wire reporting where the amount equals or exceeds CAD 10,000. The source-of-funds dossier must include the board resolution authorizing the share issuance, the subscription agreement, the share certificate, the wire advice, and the paid-up-capital table. Profit repatriation by subsequent dividend is unrestricted but ordinarily subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% (treaty-reduced where applicable).
