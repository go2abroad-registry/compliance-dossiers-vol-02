Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (MyBA) for corporate income tax (RC program account) and GST/HST (RT program account); Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered by the CRA on behalf of Alberta under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act (Tax Administration Agreement), so a single federal T2 filing covers both levels; provincial registration is via Alberta Corporate Registry (Corporate Access Point / ALCS).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Business Corporations Act via Corporations Canada / Alberta Corporate Registry (NUANS name search; Articles of Incorporation; registered office; non-resident directors permitted — federal corporations require at least 25% Canadian-resident directors, Alberta has no residency requirement); obtain the Certificate of Incorporation. 2) Apply to the CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts — online via Business Registration Online (BRO), by telephone, or by filing Form RC1E (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts); the RC (corporate income tax) and RT (GST/HST) program accounts are established in the same application; the BN is the statutory TIN for all federal and Alberta tax filings. 3) Register within 30 days of incorporation and before the first T2 return is due (6 months after fiscal year-end). 4) Provide Articles, By-laws, Certificate of Incumbency and proof of registered office when onboarding non-resident directors, opening bank accounts, and activating Stripe/PayPal merchant accounts. 5) Maintain the BN/RT accounts, file annual T2 corporate income tax returns and periodic GST/HST returns through MyBA, and keep Alberta Corporate Registry and Corporations Canada records current.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Alberta Business Corporation is a Canadian-resident corporation taxed at the entity level under ITA Part I (federal) and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; combined federal-Alberta general corporate income tax rate is 23% (federal 15% + Alberta 8%); an 11% combined small-business rate (federal 9% + Alberta 2%) applies to the first CA$500,000 of active business income only while the corporation qualifies as a Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC); because the dossier permits non-resident ownership, CCPC classification and the small-business deduction are available only if the corporation is not controlled, directly or indirectly, by non-residents; the entity has worldwide (territorial-plus) scope with foreign tax credits for foreign-source business income; no tax-exempt zone status applies in Alberta.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box or entity-classification election; a Canadian business corporation is irrebuttably an entity-level taxpayer. Available statutory elections relevant to this structure include: (1) ITA s. 125/125.1 — election to allocate the small-business limit among associated CCPCs (T2 Schedule 23 and associated-company allocation agreements); (2) ITA s. 18.2/18.21 (EIFEL) and hybrid-mismatch limitation elections for cross-border group debt structures; (3) ETA s. 156 — optional election for closely related corporations to treat intra-group supplies as taxable for GST/HST planning; (4) ETA s. 245 — election to file GST/HST on an annual basis with periodic remittances, and the quick-method election for qualifying small businesses; (5) ITA s. 216 — net-income election for specific rental/royalty income streams (not applicable to active e-commerce retail; noted for completeness). No Tax_Exempt_Zone or passthrough election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CA$30,000: mandatory GST/HST registration is triggered when total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies such as exports) exceed CA$30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 240); the same CA$30,000 threshold applies to worldwide specified supplies of non-resident digital-economy vendors; Alberta does not impose any separate provincial sales tax registration threshold, and e-commerce sales through Stripe/PayPal count toward the threshold when charged to Canadian customers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (pet supplies and pet-care devices) are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 where the Alberta corporation sells the goods and exports them from Canada directly (or through a common carrier/forwarder) as soon as is reasonably possible, and the goods are not acquired for use or consumption in Canada; international courier shipments to non-resident customers qualify. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under Part V, s. 7 where not on the excepted list. Goods shipped to a foreign fulfilment centre may be zero-rated only if exported within 60 days of delivery to the carrier; goods delivered from a Canadian warehouse to Canadian customers are taxable at the destination-province GST/HST rate and are not eligible for zero-rating.

  • Canada Export Declaration via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) / Form G13 for commercial exports valued at CA$2,000 or more (low-value e-commerce parcels are covered by the carrier's electronic export manifest and commercial invoice)
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill / international courier waybill (UPS, FedEx, DHL, Canada Post) with tracking history showing the goods left Canada and were delivered to the non-resident address
  • Payment evidence: Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) from the Canadian bank or payment-rail settlement reports from Stripe/PayPal (payout statements, foreign-card-acquirer settlement, SWIFT MT103/credit advice) showing export proceeds received from non-resident buyers

None — Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or API-based invoice validation comparable to CFDI or SDI regimes. A GST/HST-registered supplier must issue an invoice with the prescribed particulars under ETA s. 221 (supplier name, BN, invoice date, description of supply, tax amount or tax-included notice); simplified invoices are acceptable for supplies under CA$150. The CRA is implementing a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard (certified access points); participation is optional. Stripe/PayPal sales records must be retained in a legible, machine-readable format for six years, and imports entering Canada are processed electronically through the CBSA customs platform, which is distinct from any seller-side invoice clearance requirement.

Zero-rating claims are subject to CRA audit of contemporaneous export evidence; if documentation is deficient or the supply is re-characterized as delivered in Canada, the CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period (ETA s. 298(1)), without time limit in cases of misrepresentation, neglect or fraud, and collect unremitted GST/HST at the applicable rate plus daily compounded prescribed-rate interest. Penalties: ETA s. 280 — false amounts on returns, greater of CA$250 and 25% of the tax (50% on repeat occurrences); ETA s. 285 — false statements, up to 100% of the tax; ETA s. 327 — criminal tax evasion, fines of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to 5 years; retrospective clawbacks reverse previously claimed input tax credits on related purchases and may trigger gross-negligence penalties under ITA s. 163.2; import/export misdeclarations may also attract CBSA administrative monetary penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible. Both current and capital account transactions are unrestricted, with no general approval regime for inward or outward remittances. Profit remittances (dividends, management fees, royalties) may be made freely, subject to corporate law solvency tests, tax withholding under the Income Tax Act (e.g., Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-residents), and FINTRAC reporting for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more. No exchange control approval is required; however, cross-border wire transfers must include accurate purpose codes and comply with sanctions screening under the United Nations Act and the Special Economic Measures Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists in Canada. Exporters of goods and services may retain foreign currency balances indefinitely. For tax and reporting purposes, all foreign currency gains/losses must be computed in CAD using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or a permitted equivalent, and proceeds from e-commerce sales are subject to GST/HST on taxable supplies into Canada and must be documented for income tax accounting. Cross-border electronic transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC via a Large Virtual Currency Transaction Report or EFT Report, as applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI, for banking supervision); the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers anti-money laundering compliance for cross-border payments and currency reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Foreign digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) must register as money service businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC before operating or maintaining a business account in Canada unless they rely on a licensed Canadian financial institution as a partner. Provinces may also require money transmitter licenses (e.g., Alberta’s Money Lenders Act and regulations, though Alberta generally requires registration only for payday lending; material Canadian presence may trigger provincial securities or consumer protection rules). A fully remote, non-Canadian EMI without FINTRAC registration is not viable as the sole banking rail. A prudent approach is to use a FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB (e.g., a licensed payment service provider) or open a direct account at a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward equity or shareholder loan injections should be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian operating account using the bank’s standard foreign remittance instructions. The sender must include the corporate legal name, the Alberta corporate registration number, and a clear payment purpose code, e.g., 'Equity Injection' (or the bank’s proprietary purpose code for capital transfers). For amounts above CAD 10,000, the Canadian receiving bank will report the EFT to FINTRAC. To avoid delays, the bank should be notified in advance, and the underlying corporate resolution authorizing the borrowing or equity contribution must be available. If the injected funds originate from a foreign jurisdiction with currency controls, the sending bank may require a certificate of inward remittance; Canada does not impose any such certificate requirement.Trade settlements for imported pet supplies and inventory purchases should be executed through the corporate bank’s direct SWIFT service, using MT103 payment instructions or, for confirmed orders with documentary compliance, a commercial letter of credit (LC) under UCP600. For regular high-volume purchases to low-risk suppliers, MT103 telegraphic transfers are preferred. When the supplier requires payment assurance before shipment and the purchase value exceeds an internal threshold, an irrevocable LC may be issued by the Canadian bank, with the bank’s global trade finance department handling document examination and settlement. All outbound payments require a proper purpose code, such as 'Goods - Imports' or 'Trade Settlement', and must be subjected to sanctions screening.
