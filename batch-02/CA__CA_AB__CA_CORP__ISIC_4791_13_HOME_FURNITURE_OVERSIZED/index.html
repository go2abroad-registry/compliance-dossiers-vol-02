Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), with administrative portal 'CRA My Business Account' and GST/HST NETFILE; provincial corporate tax is administered by the Alberta Finance and Department of Treasury Board and Finance through ATBF Corporate Tax portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business in Canada or a Canadian resident corporation must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA via online registration, identifying the legal entity and all program accounts. For GST/HST, the corporation registers either as a resident or non-resident registrant by completing GST/HST account enrolment through the CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) service. The CRA issues separate program accounts for corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and customs imports; a valid TIN comparable is the BN plus two-letter program account suffix (e.g., BN/RM for GST/HST). Non-resident corporations with no Canadian presence must also consider whether permanent establishment triggers Part III tax and mandatory registration under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a 'check-the-box' or free-standing entity classification election for federal corporate tax purposes; a business corporation is per se a resident taxpayer subject to Part I tax at the entity level. Available elections relevant to a newly incorporated federal/provincial corporation include: (i) the family or ownership continuity election under subsection 256(1) and associated associated-corporation elections; (ii) section 85 rollover election upon transfer of property to a corporation, subject to hybrid mismatch restrictions; (iii) the section 216(4) election for non-resident persons earning rental income to compute tax on a net basis; (iv) the capital dividend account (CDA) election under subsection 83(2) to distribute tax-free capital dividends; and (v) optional GST/HST quick method and simplified method elections for registrants, provided the entity qualifies. All elections must be filed within prescribed time limits and are subject to anti-avoidance, hybrid mismatch, and general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) scrutiny.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada over any four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); for non-residents, registration may be required where commercial activity in Canada exceeds this threshold, or voluntary registration may be available for persons making zero-rated supplies to claim input tax credits

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property, including oversized furniture and home decor sold online to foreign customers, are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act provided the goods are supplied by way of sale and delivered to a destination outside Canada. The zero-rating applies only when evidence satisfactory to the CRA establishes that the goods were exported as part of the supply chain, generally within 60 days of delivery to an exporter or carrier. Services that are directly tied to the export (e.g., freight, handling, and customs brokerage services for goods delivered outside Canada) may also be zero-rated if the service is performed for a non-resident or in respect of exported tangible property and is not expressed to be for consumption in Canada. Online retailers selling from Canada to foreign customers may not charge GST/HST if the goods are shipped directly from Canada to a foreign address, provided the export and proof-of-export documentation are maintained; zero-rating does not apply to goods delivered to a Canadian consumer or to goods subsequently re-imported into Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), or documented exemption code
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill indicating the foreign destination and evidencing departure of the goods from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement / SWIFT payment evidence proving export proceeds were received in the ordinary course
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, and packing slip showing the foreign purchaser and final delivery address
  • Carrier or freight provider confirmation and any customs border crossing report (e.g., highway or marine exit) supporting physical export

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime exists at the federal level. Canada does not mandate the use of certified e-invoicing or continuous transaction controls (CTC). However, GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed invoices containing specified information, and returns and remittances must be transmitted electronically via the CRA's GST/HST NETFILE service or authorized third-party electronic data interchange (EDI). A registrant that exceeds the threshold is required to file returns electronically and to remit amounts by electronic funds transfer. CRA applies strict record retention rules for electronic invoices and underlying supporting documents, including export evidence, but does not require pre-clearance or governmental API validation of each invoice.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rated supply to GST/HST taxable at 5% if the exporter fails to produce adequate proof of export within a reasonable time after written request. For fraudulent export zero-rating claims, the Excise Tax Act imposes penalties, including a penalty of 50% of the net tax deficiency under section 285, plus interest; assessments may be imposed retroactively. Under the Income Tax Act, subsection 163(2) provides for a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated income tax or overstated refund/ITC, and section 239 criminal penalties apply to wilful evasion, including fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment of up to five years. The CRA also maintains targeted audits of e-commerce exporters and may claw back input tax credits previously claimed and use customs export data from CBSA to cross-verify zero-rated claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial and financial transactions. Canada generally has no capital-account approval requirement, mandatory repatriation rule, general foreign-exchange licensing requirement, or restriction on remitting dividends, interest, principal, or sale proceeds to non-residents. Remittances remain subject to corporate-law solvency and dividend rules, applicable withholding tax, transfer-pricing requirements, thin-capitalization rules, treaty eligibility, sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, bank source-of-funds review, and accurate tax reporting. Cross-border payments may require Canadian income-tax withholding, commonly 25 percent under domestic law before treaty reduction, depending on the payment type and recipient status. Intercompany financing should be supported by arm's-length terms, corporate approvals, beneficial-ownership evidence, and transfer-pricing documentation. Non-resident ownership of an Alberta or federal corporation is generally permitted, subject to sector-specific laws that do not ordinarily apply to online furniture retail.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, convert export receipts into Canadian dollars, or convert a prescribed percentage of receipts. There is no general statutory deadline for conversion of export proceeds. The corporation may retain foreign-currency balances, subject to its bank agreement, accounting records, tax reporting, sanctions compliance, customs documentation, and anti-money-laundering requirements. Export transactions should be supported by commercial invoices, sales contracts, shipping records, customs filings, proof of delivery, and reconciliation of settlement receipts. Currency gains and losses must be recorded under the corporation's applicable financial-reporting framework and Canadian tax rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; Canada has no separate foreign-exchange administration imposing general exchange controls. Relevant supervisory and enforcement bodies include the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, the Canada Revenue Agency, the Canada Border Services Agency, Global Affairs Canada, and the Alberta Securities Commission where applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to High for payment acceptance, foreign-currency collection, and payout support, but not a substitute for a Canadian operating bank. An offshore electronic-money institution does not automatically have the right to provide deposit-taking, payment services, money transmission, or consumer-credit services in Canada. The provider must establish its Canadian regulatory basis, provincial money-services registration where applicable, safeguarding and settlement arrangements, sanctions and AML controls, and contractual responsibility for chargebacks and merchant funds. Canadian operating revenue should be settled to a corporation-controlled account with complete reconciliation. Stripe may provide payment-processing services subject to eligibility, underwriting, reserves, prohibited-business rules, and Canadian settlement terms. Affirm availability depends on merchant approval, product, province, consumer-credit structure, and the applicable Canadian program; it should not be assumed solely from incorporation or use of Stripe.
