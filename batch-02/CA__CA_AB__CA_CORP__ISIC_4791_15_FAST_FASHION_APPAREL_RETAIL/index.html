Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC) - National tax administration for GST/HST, federal and Alberta corporate income tax; registration portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and the Non-Resident Registration portal (RC391)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity seeking to operate in Alberta must first register as an extra-provincial corporation with the Alberta Corporate Registry and, if federally incorporated, obtain federal incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act. The CRA assigns a nine-digit Business Number (BN) upon incorporation or upon application. For GST/HST purposes, the non-resident entity must complete Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts', or use the Non-Resident Registration form RC391. Where no physical presence exists but taxable sales in Canada are made, the entity may apply directly for a BN and a GST/HST account (RT program account). For fast-fashion e-commerce, registration is elective below the threshold but is generally obtained before making taxable supplies to obtain input tax credits (ITCs) on Canadian inventory and operating costs. The entity must also obtain an Alberta Corporate Tax account, which is administered and serviced by CRA under the Alberta corporate income tax administration agreement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have a 'check-the-box' classification regime for federal or provincial corporate law; a Business Corporation is automatically a taxable corporation under section 89 of the Income Tax Act. For GST/HST purposes, no entity-classification election exists; however, certain statutory elections are available, including: (i) the closely related corporation election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act to treat supplies between GST-registered closely related corporations as zero-rated; (ii) the joint venture election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act allowing a designated operator to account for the JV's GST/HST; (iii) the Social Insurance Number / BN program account selection for separate business divisions; and (iv) for hybrid mismatch rules, Canada's anti-hybrid provisions (sections 212.3 and 18.4 of the Income Tax Act) disallow certain deductions, but there is no elective entity reclassification for tax purposes. A non-resident corporation may elect under domestic law to be treated as a corporation for treaty purposes, but this does not create pass-through status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD in cumulative worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) over the preceding four calendar quarters; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is required for any person making taxable supplies in Canada in the course of a commercial activity; for non-residents carrying on business in Canada, the threshold applies to supplies in Canada. A fast-fashion e-commerce seller must register once Canadian sales exceed this threshold, unless they voluntarily register earlier to claim ITCs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported directly by the supplier or by a common carrier to a non-resident recipient at an address outside Canada, provided the supplier retains conclusive evidence of export within the prescribed time frames under section 158 of the Excise Tax Act. For fast-fashion e-commerce, goods delivered by mail or courier to international destinations qualify for 0% GST if the shipment manifest, airway bill, and customs export documentation show delivery outside Canada. Services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated if they fall under Schedule VI Part V, including services in respect of exported goods or services performed for a non-resident person. The seller must be a GST registrant to claim ITCs attributable to zero-rated exports; a non-resident may recoup Canadian input taxes paid via the CRA rebate for non-residents.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Reporting System / CBSA export report, or courier manifest equivalent for low-value shipments)
  • Bill of Lading / Master and House Airway Bill (or courier waybill) showing foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing Slip with foreign consignee name and address
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency / cross-border payment
  • Proof of foreign delivery (tracking delivery confirmation or signed proof of delivery)
  • Customer order records and e-commerce shipping log linking the order to the shipment
  • Canadian Payment Rails Output (Shopify Payments / Klarna BNPL) transaction reconciliation

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control is currently mandated by CRA. Canada does not require API-based e-invoicing transmission to the tax authority for GST/HST compliance. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records in a standard machine-readable format (PDF, XML, EDI, Peppol) for audit purposes. The federal government has adopted the Pan-Canadian Peppol (PEPPOL) e-invoicing standard for B2G procurement, but it is not mandatory for private sector GST registrants. For fast-fashion e-commerce, invoices issued via Shopify or other platforms must contain the GST registration number and 5% GST charged on domestic Canadian supplies; digital records must be retained for at least six years.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims rigorously; insufficient documentary proof results in the supply being recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST, with retroactive assessment, interest, and penalties. Under the Excise Tax Act, penalties include: failure to register penalties under section 280; false statements or omissions penalties up to 25% of the net tax at issue under section 285; and tax evasion penalties up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded under section 283. The normal assessment period is four years, but in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, the CRA may reassess without time limit. Export zero-rating fraud (e.g., shipping to domestic addresses but reporting exports) is treated as serious tax evasion and may lead to criminal prosecution, monetary fines, and imprisonment. The exporter bears the burden of proof; digital evidence of foreign delivery and foreign-currency remittance must be retained for six years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized exchange-control regime with unrestricted convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both the current and capital accounts. The Currency Act, the Financial Administration Act, and Canada's binding commitments under the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements preclude any exchange-control approvals, residency-based FX quotas, or repatriation licensing. A foreign-owned Alberta business corporation may freely convert CAD, USD, EUR, and offshore CNH, may hold multiple foreign-currency bank accounts at Schedule I and II banks, and may remit dividends, management fees, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds without Bank of Canada or Department of Finance authorization. The only frictions are fiscal: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at the statutory 25% on dividends and certain remittances (reduced to 15% under the Canada-US Treaty and to 5% for treaty-qualified corporate shareholders), CRS and FATCA reporting obligations to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), and GST/HST compliance on e-commerce sales. No capital-control taxes, conversion delays, or prior-approval mechanisms apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules in Canada: no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no requirement to receive export earnings through a designated local account. Fast-fashion retail exporters may retain USD, EUR, CNY/CNH, or other proceeds indefinitely in foreign-currency business accounts at Canadian chartered banks and may deploy them directly toward offshore supplier settlements. The only applicable duties are statistical and customs-related: (i) CBSA export declarations under the Customs Act for merchandise shipped out of Canada exceeding CAD 30,000 per transaction, including e-commerce parcel exports; (ii) Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys under the Statistics Act; and (iii) CRA record-keeping for transfer-pricing and CRS substantiation. None of these obligations imposes a conversion ratio or a deadline for converting receipts into CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-9); prudential supervision of chartered banks delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); clearing and settlement infrastructure administered by Payments Canada; AML/ATF supervision and financial intelligence administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional/Medium. No foreign Electronic Money Institution (EMI) is recognized as a deposit-taking institution under the Bank Act and none may lawfully accept Canadian client deposits or create deposit liabilities. Non-bank payment service providers operating in Canada must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (SC 2021, c. 5), whose registration regime is administered by the Bank of Canada and fully in force since November 1, 2024, and must separately register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. A registered cross-border PSP such as Wise or Payoneer is legally permissible for automating supplier disbursements and optimizing FX tickets, provided the provider holds active FINTRAC MSB and Bank of Canada RPAA registrations and settles through a Schedule I bank. Structural constraint: EMIs offer no CDIC deposit insurance, are not covered by ODBP or OSFI prudential oversight, and introduce nesting risk that may trigger enhanced due diligence by the clearing bank. Recommended architecture: use a Schedule I chartered bank as the treasury anchor and use a registered PSP exclusively as a disbursement rail with full reconciliation reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Routing is executed by SWIFT MT103 (for customer transfers) with an MT202 cover where an intermediary is used, credited to the Alberta operating account at a Schedule I chartered bank and settled in real time through Lynx, Canada's ISO 20022 high-value RTGS system. Protocol: (1) pre-notification to the relationship manager with a funding memorandum; (2) corporate board resolution authorizing the injection, account title, and signing authorities; (3) wire narrative identifying the transaction as 'Inbound direct investment — equity contribution for working capital of fast-fashion e-commerce operations,' with the ISO 20022 purpose narrative mapped to the Statistics Act balance-of-payments category for direct investment in Canada; (4) CRS and FATCA self-certifications filed before funding; (5) source-of-funds documentation preserved for five years plus one day to satisfy FINTRAC record-keeping requirements. Foreign-currency injections are converted to CAD at the bank's published spot rate under a written FX ticket, or pre-hedged with a forward contract where timing is known. The receiving bank will also verify that the destination account is not held pending unresolved beneficial-ownership attestations before releasing funds for deployment.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 with a UETR field using SWIFT gpi tracking for open-account telegraphic transfers to apparel suppliers across China, Vietnam, Bangladesh, and the United States; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit issued under UCP600, and URR725 for reimbursements, employing sight or deferred 30-90 day drafts for first-time or higher-risk suppliers; (iii) cross-border USD automated clearing or Canada-US drawing arrangements for US-based fulfillment and logistics partners; and (iv) domestic payments via Lynx and the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for Canadian suppliers and refunds. For Klarna BNPL and Shopify Payments flows, refund and merchant settlement disbursements remain inside the acquiring settlement cycle, with the operating bank maintaining a direct-payables ledger that validates merchant ID and beneficiary matching. All outbound disbursements settle from the Schedule I operating account to preserve a clean audit trail for CRA, transfer-pricing, and banking compliance reviews; FX conversion for disbursements is executed through the bank's treasury desk or a registered PSP FX module with full reconciliation.Outright deliverable CAD foreign-exchange forwards: purchase and sale contracts for USD, EUR, and offshore CNH against CAD, tenors typically 1-12 months, documented under the ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and the bank's credit facility.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs): permitted for restricted or offshore non-deliverable currencies, including USD/CNY NDFs referencing the CFETS Shanghai fixing, used exclusively to hedge actual trade payables and revenue exposures.Cross-currency swaps: fixed-floating and fixed-fixed structures for long-term intercompany loans and capital injections denominated in USD or CAD.FX swaps and swap-fix roll transactions: permitted for short-dated liquidity matching between CAD operating balances and USD disbursement requirements.Vanilla FX options: plain-vanilla call and put options on USD/CAD and USD/CNH for asymmetric hedging of supplier payables and Shopify revenue receipts.Regulatory note: all instruments are OTC derivatives permissible under the Bank Act and OSFI guidelines for commercial hedging and are subject to Canadian trade-repository reporting and counterparty-limit documentation if mark-to-market thresholds are met; speculative position-taking is outside the approved treasury mandate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$1,000,000 (specified transaction threshold for the transfer-pricing penalty regime); a Master File is expected when the multinational group’s consolidated gross revenue exceeds CA$1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone ESR; however, CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule (s. 245) and the doctrine of substance over form, and s. 247 TP rules require a functional, economic, and risk analysis. A non-resident with an e-commerce warehouse or dependent agent in Alberta may trigger a permanent establishment and become subject to income tax and Alberta corporate income tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 239: upon indictment, fine of 50% to 200% of evaded tax plus imprisonment up to five years; on summary conviction, imprisonment up to two years less a day and same fine range. Civil gross-negligence penalty under s. 163.2: 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or authorize false filings may be criminally prosecuted; s. 160 transfer liability attaches to personal assets where corporate property was transferred at undervalue; travel bans and pre-trial restraints can be ordered for indictable offences.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA may ignore the shell under GAAR, reallocate income to Canada, impose interest and penalties, and deny treaty benefits; shell directors may be fined under CBCA or provincial securities laws if false declarations are made. E-commerce proceeds may be reassessed with a 50% gross-negligence penalty.

Director personal liability: Nominees who sign false certificates may face civil restitution, director bans, and criminal liability for fraud; beneficial owners may be personally assessed under s. 160 for dividends or property received from the shell.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Section 247(3) penalty: 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment if the transaction exceeds CA$1 million and contemporaneous documentation is absent; plus interest and a possible s. 163.2 gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Canadian entity who cause or acquiesce in false or unreasonable TP filings can be assessed personally for unremitted withholdings; they may also be liable for conspiracy to defraud the Crown and be subject to travel restrictions in criminal investigations.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Breach

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties up to CA$1,000,000 for an organization; Criminal Code s. 462.31 money-laundering offence carries a maximum 10-year imprisonment; property is subject to restraint and forfeiture under s. 462.37.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and beneficial owners who authorise, direct or knowingly receive laundering proceeds face criminal prosecution, personal forfeiture orders, and travel bans; FINTRAC may issue monetary penalties directly against persons with authority over the entity.
