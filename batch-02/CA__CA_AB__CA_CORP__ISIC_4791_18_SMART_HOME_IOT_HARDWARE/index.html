Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or similar elective regime exists in Canada. All resident corporations are subject to mandatory entity-level taxation under federal and provincial rules, subject to GAAR and ATAD hybrid mismatch provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual taxable supplies for GST registration; equivalent CAD 30,000 threshold for PST registration

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST is zero-rated on exports of goods from Canada and on services supplied to non-residents located outside Canada. Eligibility requires verifiable proof that the goods were exported or the services were supplied offshore.

  • Export Invoice
  • Shipping Documents (Bill of Lading or equivalent)
  • Proof of Export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API integration, or digital stamping requirements apply to standard taxpayers. GST and PST returns must be filed electronically through CRA or Alberta government portals.

Zero-rating claims must be supported by documentation; fraudulent or unsubstantiated claims may result in penalties up to 200% of the GST amount, plus interest and potential criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully open capital and current account under IMF Article VIII status; no exchange controls, no prior approval for foreign exchange transactions, and no restrictions on dividend or profit remittance, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax where applicable

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Canada; exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds indefinitely in domestic or offshore foreign-currency bank accounts without repatriation obligation

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy is administered by the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act and the Financial Administration Act, with FINTRAC overseeing AML/ATF obligations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Moderate for ancillary collection rails, but EMIs are not chartered banks and are not treated as the primary clearing account; funds held with EMIs may lack CDIC deposit insurance and are subject to the home-jurisdiction regulatory framework; a Schedule I bank account remains mandatory for capital escrow, export settlement, and FX lines

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of all transactions with non-arm's length non-residents in the taxation year triggers the formal documentation requirement and Schedule 57 filing; the same threshold applies to the foundation for contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File may also be expected for high-value intercompany activities).

Economic substance scope: No formal Economic Substance Regulations exist in Alberta or federal Canada; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and the Multilateral Instrument's principal purpose test require a financial and operational substance threshold in cross-border arrangements. Enterprises carrying on retail and distribution activity must demonstrate real presence through physical inventory, personnel, bargaining power, and substantive payroll; otherwise CRA may challenge entitles as conduits or sham arrangements under the common law doctrines of agency business realism or tax-avoidance misuse.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under subsection 239(1) and (1.1) of the Income Tax Act: a fine of 50% to 200% of the amount of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, plus civil reassessment penalties of 50% of the understated tax under subsection 163(2); CRA may impose third-party penalties up to CAD 24,000 for facilitating one tax evasion

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or authorize tax evasion may be personally assessed for taxes, interest, and penalties; the corporate veil will be pierced and civil restitution suits by shareholders and creditors may attach personal assets; serious criminal convictions can ground travel restrictions and preclude cross-border travel
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits, assess federal and provincial taxes on deemed undistributed income, apply GAAR offset more than 25% of the avoided tax and impose a penalty equal to 50% of the benefit where the avoidance is due to a transaction entered into primarily for tax purposes; failure to maintain corporate records may lead to dissolution or restoration penalties under the Alberta Business Corporations Act

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for statutory source deductions and GST/HST that the entity failed to remit; if the shell is used as a fraudulent instrument, courts can pierce the veil and order directors to compensate creditors or the tax authority for the full unpaid liabilities of the corporation
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act of up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations and CAD 1,000,000 for individuals; criminal liability for laundering the proceeds of crime under Criminal Code sections 462.31; in extreme cases the corporate registration may be cancelled and assets seized

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers personally liable for knowingly participating in or directing laundering transactions; personal asset freezing orders, forfeiture of directors' interests in the entity, travel sanctions, and imprisonment of up to fourteen years are possible for willful involvement
