Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary portal is CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under a single-administration agreement with the Alberta Department of Finance, using the same federal Business Number; no separate provincial registration is required for corporate tax.

E-government filing portal

The entity must register for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (RT suffix) with the CRA. Procedure: (1) Obtain the incorporating instrument - for a federal corporation, Articles of Incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (auto-issued BN upon federal incorporation); for an Alberta corporation, a Certificate of Incorporation from the Alberta Corporate Registry (no Capital Tax deposit, no minimum capital requirement) followed by an online BN application; (2) Complete Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or use the CRA Online Registration service, indicating 'GST/HST' under program accounts and the status as a Canadian-controlled or other-resident corporation; (3) Provide corporate details: registered office address in Alberta, board of directors' names and addresses, foreign parent entity's tax identification number if any, and business activity ISIC 4791 / NAICS 441310 (Automotive Parts and Accessories Retailers); (4) If a non-resident director or shareholder is involved, include Form NR4 or section 116/Part XIII relevant declarations; (5) Submit electronically for immediate BN issuance, or by fax/mail via RC1 with 4-6 weeks processing; (6) Register for a CRA My Business Account online credential and Set Up a GST/HST account with a filing frequency (monthly or annual based on revenue); (7) For payment settlement, open an Alberta bank account and file a T2 corporate tax return within 6 months of fiscal year-end. No statutory license is required for auto parts e-commerce, but valid sectoral AML/KYC occurs through payment processors Stripe/PayPal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; every incorporated entity is treated as a corporation absent an explicit joint election. Available relevant elections for this structure: (i) non-calendar taxation year election under subsection 249.1(1) of the Income Tax Act; (ii) GST/HST Quick Method election (available for registrants with taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000 in any four consecutive quarters, allowing simplified remittance at a reduced effective rate); (iii) affiliated corporation loss transfer election under section 156 of the Income Tax Act for losses within an associated group for federal and Alberta corporate tax purposes; (iv) hybrid mismatch designations and the anti-hybrid 'exclusion election' under section 18.2(4) applicable where a foreign subsidiary or partnership member is treated differently for foreign law (relevant if a US parent has a disregarded LLC member holding shares of this CA_CORP); (v) section 212(3) or Part XIII withholding adjustments for dividends/cross-border payments to non-resident shareholders (not applicable unless distributions occur); (vi) election for a corporation to be treated as a 'Canadian-controlled private corporation' status change on Schedule 50 to the T2 return. All elections must be filed with, or before, the due date of the relevant T2 or GST/HST return to be valid.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small supplier threshold under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act. A Canadian-resident corporation must register for GST/HST within 30 days after its taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters exceed CAD 30,000, or as soon as it cannot be considered a small supplier; for the CA_CORP e-commerce model, where sales originate in Alberta and are delivered to Canadian consumers or exported, registration from first revenue is recommended, and voluntary registration is permitted to claim input tax credits. Alberta has no separate economic nexus or sales/use tax threshold; remote sellers outside Canada but selling into Canada may also trigger Goods and Services Tax obligations under the same CAD 30,000 rule.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of automotive replacement parts and tuning accessories qualify for the 0% GST rate under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (exported goods, in particular Section 5), provided: (i) the supply is made in the ordinary course of the seller's business; (ii) the purchaser is a non-resident; (iii) the goods are delivered outside Canada by the supplier or on its behalf (shipped directly to a foreign address via common carrier/courier); (iv) the invoice clearly identifies the seller's Alberta origin, foreign destination and a statement such as 'zero-rated export - Excise Tax Act Schedule VI Part V'; and (v) the goods are exported within a reasonable time (CRA administrative practice expects export within 60 days of delivery to the carrier). Dropship arrangements where the goods travel from a Canadian warehouse to a non-resident consignee are also zero-rated. A registered GST collector is eligible to claim input tax credits (ITC) for inventory, courier, marketplace and Stripe/PayPal processing fees; where ITCs exceed output GST, a net refund is payable via the GST/HST return (e.g., Form GST34-1) or a RC4500 refund application. Export shipments with a value exceeding CAD 2,000 must have a CBSA electronic export declaration (CARM/ACI eManifest) to preserve zero-rating substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA CARM or ACI eManifest submission; mandatory for goods exceeding CAD 2,000 in value)
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill (original or electronic, showing CA_CORP as exporter and foreign destination)
  • Commercial invoice (with zero-rated export declaration, HS classification 87.08, ISIC 4791 - automotive replacement parts and tuning accessories description)
  • Proof of delivery to the non-resident (courier manifest, delivery confirmation, signed waybill from Canada Post, Purolator, DHL, UPS, FedEx)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or bank confirmation evidencing settlement in foreign or Canadian currency from the non-resident buyer
  • CRA schedules reflecting ITC claims and the GST/HST return reconciliation for the corresponding reporting period

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API pre-approval is imposed by the CRA. Canada has adopted a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the PEPPOL network, and the CRA accepts electronic invoices (EDI, XML, PEPPOL BIS 3.0) on condition they contain all statutory data elements prescribed under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act (vendor BN, recipient, description, value, GST rate, ITC amount). There is no requirement for a government clearance code. However, the CBSA (Canada Border Services Agency) mandates electronic export declarations via the CARM (Assessment and Revenue Management) system or ACI/export eManifest for goods exported from Canada with a value above CAD 2,000; invoices must be digitally archived for 6 years and be capable of being produced in an auditable, legible format.

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is adopted here as the prudent CRA/BEPS substantive documentation cycle and as a defense against transfer-pricing and General Anti-Avoidance Rule challenges where settlement is materially delayed. On audit, if zero-rating is found to be falsely claimed, the supplies are re-characterized as taxable at 5% GST and the previously refunded amounts are clawed back with arrears interest at the CRA quarterly prescribed rate (compounded daily). Specific penalties: Excise Tax Act section 280 - false statement penalty of 25% of the deficiency (50% for repeat violations); section 285 - evasion penalty of up to 200% of the tax attempted to be evaded plus imprisonment upon conviction; subsection 286(1) - failure to maintain adequate export books and records, with a penalty of CAD 4,000 for failure to comply; Income Tax Act subsection 152(4) - the CRA may reassess beyond the normal 4-year period for 7 years where negligence, carelessness, or fraud exists; subsection 163(2) - gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated income. Where fraudulent bills of lading, sham export invoices, or artificially extended settlement beyond 180 days are detected, the entire GST/HST refund history (4-year window) is subject to retrospective clawback with interest, and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act applies parallel assessment and penalty provisions for misstated taxable income attributable to the zero-rated export revenue.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with full current-account and capital-account convertibility: trade in goods and services, ecommerce settlements, intercompany fees, royalties, equity injections, shareholder loans, dividends, and capital repatriation are all free from prior exchange-control approval. Practical convertibility hurdles are limited to (i) Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding on non-resident dividend remittances, statutory 25% and treaty-reduced to 5-15% for corporate beneficial owners; (ii) FINTRAC international electronic-funds-transfer reporting for amounts equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000; (iii) arm's-length interest and thin-capitalization rules under ITA ss.17 and 18(4), which impose a 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ceiling on debt owed to specified non-residents; (iv) sanctions and proscribed-entity screening under Canadian federal legislation; and (v) Investment Canada Act review, which is not triggered for this new online retail auto-parts entity because the 2024 reviewable enterprise-value threshold is CAD 1.335 billion and no cultural-business sensitivity applies. No sectoral negative list or pre-approval screening applies to online retail of automotive replacement parts in Alberta.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada maintains no export-proceeds surrender regime: the historical Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio, no statutory timeline for repatriating proceeds, and no obligation to sell export receipts into Canadian dollars. The auto-parts ecommerce exporter may hold USD, EUR, or CNY receivables indefinitely in domestic foreign-currency deposit accounts or offshore accounts. Residual reporting obligations are not exchange-control surrender rules: (i) international wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the bank; (ii) physical-goods exports may invoke CBSA export declaration or Statistics Canada G-45 trade reporting when shipped by commercial modes or when the goods appear on the Canadian Export Control List; (iii) all export transactions must be screened against Canadian and allied sanctions lists, but no portion of the proceeds must be converted into CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority and sole issuer of Canadian dollar notes; prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering surveillance and international EFT reporting are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); economic sanctions and export controls are administered by Global Affairs Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act and the Export and Import Permits Act. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-administration agency and no exchange-control permit system; the Canadian dollar floats freely.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium: viable only as a secondary collection/payout rail, never as the primary treasury bank. The EU 'electronic money institution' categorization does not exist in Canadian law; the nearest Canadian regulated categories are Money Services Business under the PCMLTFA and Payment Service Provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act, which the Bank of Canada supervises. Wise Canada Inc., Payoneer Canada Inc., and similar digital cross-border treasury platforms can onboard this Alberta corporation remotely, receive USD/EUR remittances, convert to CAD, and pay out to the primary bank DDA; however, their balances are not CDIC-insured, they cannot issue commercial letters of credit, they do not extend credit, they lack an OSFI prudential licence, and settlement ultimately depends on sponsor chartered banks. Legal constraint: concentrated uninsured float at an MSB raises counterparty risk flags and fails most bank underwriting eccentricity tests. Feasibility rating: Medium for receiving card-payouts and paying small foreign suppliers; Low as a money-concentration or liquidity-hedging locus. Daily sweep of Wise/Payoneer balances into an OSFI-regulated Schedule I D-SIB account is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for non-resident equity injection: (1) pass a board resolution designating the injection as paid-up capital subscription, which is preferred for tax because it attracts no Part XIII withholding and no thin-capitalization imputation; if the injection is instead a working-capital shareholder loan, execute a written loan agreement at an arm's-length interest rate and continuously monitor ITA s.17 excessive-interest rules and the 1.5:1 debt-to-equity thin-capitalization ceiling of s.18(4); (2) issue share certificates and update the corporate register and share register; (3) route funds by SWIFT MT103 from the overseas bank to the company's recipient bank using the recipient Institution BIC and account number, with remittance field 70 stating 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION / EQUITY CONTRIBUTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN DISBURSEMENT'; Canada does not employ numeric EU-style purpose codes and does not use IBAN, but the free-text purpose must be explicit because the bank is statutorily obliged to record the transaction purpose under FINTRAC rules; (4) when the injection is denominated in USD, CNY, or EUR, convert to CAD at the bank's spot rate and preserve the conversion ticket and any negotiated FX pricing for audit; (5) the receiving bank will screen the counterparty and beneficial owner against sanctions lists, verify consistency with the KYC file on file, and file a Suspicious Transaction Report or international EFT report for amounts of CAD 10,000 or more; (6) confirm FATCA/CRS status of the shareholder before wiring, obtaining W-8BEN-E or equivalent self-certifications where applicable; (7) Investment Canada Act review is not triggered for a newly established ecommerce business because the 2024 enterprise-value review threshold is CAD 1.335 billion and the activity is non-cultural. Prohibited practices: funneling through a personal account, splitting transfers to evade the CAD 10,000 EFT disclosure threshold, or using cryptocurrency-derived funds as equity without a complete auditable trail; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium: viable only as a secondary collection/payout rail, never as the primary treasury bank. The EU 'electronic money institution' categorization does not exist in Canadian law; the nearest Canadian regulated categories are Money Services Business under the PCMLTFA and Payment Service Provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act, which the Bank of Canada supervises. Wise Canada Inc., Payoneer Canada Inc., and similar digital cross-border treasury platforms can onboard this Alberta corporation remotely, receive USD/EUR remittances, convert to CAD, and pay out to the primary bank DDA; however, their balances are not CDIC-insured, they cannot issue commercial letters of credit, they do not extend credit, they lack an OSFI prudential licence, and settlement ultimately depends on sponsor chartered banks. Legal constraint: concentrated uninsured float at an MSB raises counterparty risk flags and fails most bank underwriting eccentricity tests. Feasibility rating: Medium for receiving card-payouts and paying small foreign suppliers; Low as a money-concentration or liquidity-hedging locus.
