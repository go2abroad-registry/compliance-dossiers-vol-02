Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax Program, accessed primarily through the CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online portals. Alberta corporate income tax administration is generally integrated with the CRA through the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration framework; Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration provides provincial tax administration services through Alberta tax portals.

E-government filing portal

A corporation carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN), normally through Business Registration Online, incorporation-related registration, or Form RC1 where online registration is unavailable. The corporation must then open the applicable CRA program accounts, including an RT account for GST/HST and, where relevant, RP for payroll and RC for corporate income tax filing administration. A non-resident corporation must provide its legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, registered address, Canadian business activities, authorized representative information, effective registration date, expected taxable supplies, and details of Canadian agents, fulfillment arrangements, or inventory. GST/HST registration is generally mandatory when the corporation is not a small supplier, including where taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 under the applicable statutory measurement period. A BN and GST/HST account are not the same as a provincial business licence, municipal licence, Alberta corporate registry number, or customs importer/exporter account. A business importing or exporting commercial goods should also obtain a Canada Border Services Agency importer-exporter RM account through the Canada Revenue Agency portal and comply with the Canada Border Services Agency CARM system. Non-resident businesses may need to appoint a Canadian customs broker or qualified representative, but appointment of a representative does not generally transfer the taxpayer's GST/HST liability.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a general United States-style check-the-box election for corporations, and an Alberta or federal corporation is ordinarily taxed as a separate legal entity. Corporate tax treatment follows Canadian legal form, residence, source, and applicable treaty rules. Available elections are transaction-specific rather than entity-classification elections, including section 85 income tax elections for qualifying transfers to a taxable Canadian corporation, section 86 share reorganization elections, section 51 conversion rules, capital dividend account elections, and GST/HST elections where statutory conditions are met. Canadian hybrid mismatch rules can deny deductions, impose withholding consequences, or otherwise neutralize outcomes involving hybrid instruments or entities; they do not create a general pass-through classification election.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current and capital account transactions with no central bank approvals, exchange quotas, or repatriation licenses. Capital injections, trade settlements, and e-commerce collections via Stripe or Shopify Payments may flow in and out in CAD, USD, or any freely convertible currency without Bank of Canada authorization. The principal profit-remittance hurdle is federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders: 25% statutory rate, reduced under the Canada-US Treaty to 5% for a beneficial owner holding at least 10% of voting shares, and under the Canada-China DTA to 10% for a 10% corporate shareholder (otherwise 15%). An NR301/NR302 tax declaration must be completed with the remitting bank before cross-border dividend payments. Alberta imposes no provincial capital tax or dividend withholding. Deposits at CDIC-member banks are insured up to CAD 100,000 per eligible account. FINTRAC large-value international EFT reporting applies at the CAD 10,000 threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable — Canada imposes no statutory surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio (effective mandated conversion is 0%), and no repatriation timeline for export proceeds. All proceeds from online retail exports, including USD receipts settled via Shopify or Stripe, may be retained in original currency in a Canadian foreign-currency account or held offshore indefinitely. Residual compliance obligations: (i) CBSA electronic export reporting under the Customs Act for outward shipments above CAD 2,500; (ii) FINTRAC international EFT Report for each single wire transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; (iii) IFRS revenue recognition and FX revaluation on foreign-denominated receivables; (iv) alignment of supplier invoices with cleared bank funds to avoid trade-restriction and AML flags.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) — prudential regulator of Schedule I/II banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) — AML/ATF and international EFT reporting; Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) — Investment Canada Act filings. Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration; exchange controls were abolished in 1951, and Canada fully subscribes to IMF Article VIII obligations. The Canadian dollar is a freely usable, floating reserve currency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM — permissible only as a supplementary collection or disbursement rail, not as the primary treasury bank. Canada has no licensed EMI deposit-taking category; cross-border digital payment firms operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, and Nuvei may not take deposits, must place client funds in omnibus trust accounts at a Schedule I bank, cannot issue Canadian IBANs, and their balances are not CDIC-insured. They are viable for low-value disbursements to Asian resin and 3D printer component suppliers in CNH, USD, or EUR at competitive FX spreads, and for merchant settlement aggregation. Mandatory preconditions: verified active FINTRAC MSB registration, a Canadian correspondent banking relationship, and the absence of any arrangement that would cause the EMI to hold CA_CORP operating funds overnight in excess of a pre-approved float.
