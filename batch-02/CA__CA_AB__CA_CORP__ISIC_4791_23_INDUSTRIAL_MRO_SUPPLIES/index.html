Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Alberta law; the corporation may be formed with zero issued capital and shares may be issued for money, property, or past services (ABCA s. 23). No escrow account, deposit, or capital certification requirement applies. Non-resident ownership is permitted without restriction for this MRO e-commerce business.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html); Alberta provincial corporate tax is administered by CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act through the Tax Collection Agreement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by completing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) either through Canada Revenue Agency's Business Registration Online gateway, by fax, or by mail to the registered office in Ottawa, and must appoint a resident Canadian representative or agent if the entity has no physical presence in Canada. Once the BN is issued, the entity must register separately for a GST/HST account (if annual Canadian taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold), a corporate income tax program account, and any payroll or import/export program accounts if applicable. Alberta does not issue a separate provincial tax identification number; Alberta corporate income tax compliance is effected through the federal BN and CRA assessment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box or full entity-classification election comparable to the United States. A Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act. Particular statutory elections affecting recognition and amounts include: s. 85(1) tax-deferred transfer of property to a corporation; s. 256 associated-corporations elections for allocation of the federal small business deduction; s. 39(1)(a) and s. 39(11) elective dispositions; and Excise Tax Act s. 156 election to treat closely related corporations and partnerships as a single person for GST/HST purposes. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act and the Income Tax Conventions Interpretation Act apply to deny deductions or adjust characterization where tax arbitrage arises with foreign jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (before GST/HST) made in Canada over a single calendar quarter or over the four most recent consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident persons soliciting orders in Canada may be deemed to carry on business in Canada. Persons making taxable supplies exclusively 0-rated or supplies of real property by non-residents may still require registration through ordinary application.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies are zero-rated under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when goods are exported from Canada by or on behalf of the supplier as commercial shipments, or supplied to a non-resident person for delivery outside Canada. Services, including electronic catalogue and MRO-related services, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident person, unless the service is performed wholly or partly in Canada and the supplier does not satisfy the exception relating to services to non-resident individuals present in Canada. Zero-rating is not an exemption from registration but permits an input tax credit recovery for GST/HST paid on Canadian business inputs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A for non-CBSA-processed exports) or the proof of export transmitted through the Canadian Export Reporting System
  • Bill of Lading (airway bill, truck bill, ocean bill)
  • Commercial invoice / sales contract evidencing the foreign buyer and export sale
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement showing receipt of foreign currency transfer in the exporter's Canadian bank account
  • Packing list identifying goods and shipping marks
  • Logistics evidence such as port terminal receipts, carrier certificate, or freight forwarder confirmation; for dropship arrangements, third-party declaration of delivery outside Canada

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate is imposed under Canadian federal law or in Alberta. A GST/HST registrant must issue an official sales invoice containing prescribed information (vendor name, BN/GST/HST registration number, tax status, amount of tax, consideration, date); invoices and records must be maintained for six years. Filing of GST/HST returns and remittance is mandatory electronically for registrants with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1,500,000, and GST/HST returns can otherwise be filed electronically through the GST/HST Internet Filing Service.

CRA and CBSA may verify export documentation and cross-match customs declarations with GST/HST returns. False zero-rating claims may trigger reassessment, disallowed input tax credits, revocation of registration, gross negligence penalties of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax understated under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act, or income tax penalties and interest under sections 162, 163 and 164 of the Income Tax Act. In addition, wilful evasion, false statements, and conspiracy can attract criminal fines and imprisonment under section 380 of the Criminal Code and the Income Tax Act. Export proof must generally be obtained within no fixed statutory Customs window, but CRA administrative practice requires adequate contemporaneous documentation; the absence of such evidence will cause the zero-rated supply to be reclassified as taxable, with resultant GST/HST, interest, penalties, and potential clawback of GST/HST refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility in accordance with IMF Article VIII obligations. The Canadian dollar (CAD) trades under a free-floating exchange rate regime with no exchange controls, no prior authorization requirements, and no central bank approval for capital injections, dividend remittances, or profit repatriation. Dividend and interest payments to non-residents are subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, typically treaty-reduced under Canada's tax conventions) and do not require foreign-exchange licensing. No hurdle exists beyond standard corporate and banking KYC compliance; the Bank of Canada does not screen or approve cross-border payment flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no timeline for repatriation of export proceeds. A Canadian exporter of industrial MRO supplies may retain invoiced foreign currency indefinitely in foreign-currency-denominated bank accounts (USD, EUR, CNY) maintained at a domestic chartered bank or an authorized foreign bank branch, and may convert to CAD at its sole discretion. Only FINTRAC electronic fund transfer reporting applies, mandating an EFT report for every international transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, which is an informational compliance obligation, not an exchange-control restriction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada; foreign exchange policy is administered by the Minister of Finance through the Exchange Fund Account; prudential banking regulation is the mandate of the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision is conducted by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium viability as a secondary settlement rail only. Canada does not maintain a dedicated electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing class; cross-border digital payment firms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) operating in Canada must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. An MSB may facilitate foreign-currency payouts and card settlement, but cannot accept deposits, offer chequing accounts, or provide CDIC-insured balances, and is unsuitable as a primary treasury or capital-receipt account. A foreign-incorporated EMI without FINTRAC registration or a Canadian branch cannot lawfully provide payment services to a Canadian corporation.
