Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under the ABCA or the federal CBCA. Shares may be issued for nominal consideration; stated capital is recorded as a book-entry account under ABCA s. 24. Non-resident share ownership is unrestricted. Board composition must satisfy the ABCA s. 101 resident-Canadian director rule (default majority rule; if incorporated federally under the CBCA, at least 25% of directors must be resident Canadians).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the National Fire Code - 2019 Alberta Edition for the registered office and shore terminal; municipal building occupancy permit with structural load certification for the office and Ro-Ro staging facilities; Alberta Workers' Compensation Board (WCB) Employer Account in Good Standing clearance for workplace injury coverage; Transport Canada marine safety inspection clearance (Safety Management Certificate) covering the vessel and the Designated Person Ashore.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); online services include CRA My Business Account and Represent a Client. Alberta administers provincial corporate income tax, while the CRA administers federal corporate income tax and GST. Alberta has no general provincial sales tax.

E-government filing portal

A corporation generally obtains a nine-digit CRA Business Number (BN) and the applicable program accounts, including an RT GST/HST account, by registering with the CRA online where eligible or by submitting the applicable CRA registration form. A non-resident corporation should first determine whether it is carrying on business in Canada or is otherwise required to register; it may apply for a BN and GST/HST account through the CRA's non-resident registration procedures. Provide legal-entity, incorporation, business-activity, address, director and authorized-representative information, and any supporting documents requested. A GST/HST account number is the BN followed by the RT program identifier and account suffix. Registration does not itself determine income-tax residence or establish that a permanent establishment exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Canadian corporation is generally a separate taxpayer under the federal Income Tax Act and applicable Alberta law. Canada has no general U.S.-style check-the-box election allowing a corporation to elect partnership or disregarded-entity treatment. Certain statutory elections and entity-specific regimes may apply, but they do not create a general elective classification system. Cross-border hybrid-entity treatment depends on the relevant foreign law and applicable Canadian anti-hybrid and treaty rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, measured under the small-supplier rules over a single calendar quarter or the applicable four-consecutive-calendar-quarter period. The calculation generally includes relevant supplies of associated persons and excludes specified supplies. Special rules may require a non-resident to register even where the ordinary small-supplier threshold is not met; applicability depends on the non-resident's activities and the registration regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods may be zero-rated where the statutory conditions are met, including evidence that the goods were exported and compliance with the applicable purchaser, delivery, and use conditions. Certain services supplied to non-residents, and qualifying international freight transportation services, may be zero-rated under specific Excise Tax Act provisions; exclusions and detailed conditions apply. A deep-sea Ro-Ro carrier should determine the GST treatment separately for each freight, ancillary, port, and domestic leg and retain evidence supporting the statutory category and destination.

  • Canadian export declaration or other applicable CBSA export-reporting evidence, where required
  • Ocean bill of lading, booking confirmation, cargo manifest, and proof of loading and delivery at the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, freight contract, and records identifying the shipper, recipient, cargo, route, and consideration
  • Evidence of foreign-currency receipt or other payment records where relevant to substantiate the transaction; Canada does not generally require an Inward Remittance Certificate as a prescribed GST zero-rating document

Canada has no general GST real-time invoice-clearance, mandatory tax e-invoicing, API submission, or digital-stamping regime for ordinary commercial supplies. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices or receipts containing prescribed information when required, maintain adequate books and records, and file returns electronically where statutory requirements apply.

Canada imposes no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The supplier bears the burden of substantiating zero-rating and must retain records for the applicable statutory period. Unsupported or incorrectly zero-rated supplies may be reassessed for GST, with interest and applicable penalties. A false statement or omission may attract a gross-negligence penalty generally equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax understated or overstated credits claimed; other penalties or prosecution may apply for serious or deliberate conduct. Reassessment periods and available defenses depend on the facts and statutory rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Dividends, interest, royalties, and repatriation of capital are freely transferable in any convertible currency, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (e.g., 25% statutory, reduced by treaty) and corporate solvency tests under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Business Corporations Act. There are no exchange permits, surrender requirements, foreign-exchange budgets, or delays on profit remittance for Alberta-resident RoRo ship-operating companies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose mandatory surrender, repatriation, conversion, or holding-period rules on export proceeds. RoRo freight revenue may be credited to foreign-currency bank accounts in Canada or abroad, and may be retained indefinitely; any conversion is at arm's length commercial rates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange-control and AML supervision delegated to OSFI, FINTRAC, and Payments Canada; no dedicated exchange-control ministry.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium as a primary banking layer; Medium as an ancillary payment channel. Foreign offshore digital EMI accounts are not recognized as insured Canadian deposit accounts and cannot provide a full transactional account for an Alberta operating corporation. MSBs and RPAA payment service providers require registration, must not hold principal funds in an operating account, and are not substitutes for Canadian clearing membership; use Canadian chartered bank accounts for core escrow and operations, and EMI only for payment efficiency.
