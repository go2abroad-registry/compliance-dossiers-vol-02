Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST, corporate income tax, and payroll administration; provincial corporate income tax for Alberta is administered federally by CRA under a tax collection agreement. Business portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on commercial activity in Alberta must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1. The BN serves as the statutory TIN and is used for GST/HST, payroll, corporation tax, and import/export accounts. A separate provincial Alberta corporate tax number is not required because CRA collects Alberta corporate income tax under the federal administration agreement. Non-residency does not absolve the entity from registration if it is engaged in a commercial activity in Canada.

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Elections available under the Income Tax Act (Canada), including the section 216 election (non-resident rental income), section 217 election (non-resident payroll remuneration), section 115(1) treaty-based return election, and the GST/HST joint venture election under Excise Tax Act s. 273. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made within a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters, excluding zero-rated supplies; mandatory GST/HST registration is required once this threshold is surpassed. Non-residents engaged in commercial activity in Canada must register regardless of threshold if they make taxable supplies in Canada unless a specific exception applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under GST/HST Schedule VI to supplies of goods that are exported as part of the supply and for which the supplier maintains evidence of export; to services supplied to non-residents where the services are performed wholly or partly outside Canada or are in respect of goods exported from Canada; and to certain transportation and international freight services. The supplier must hold sufficient documentary evidence to support the export within the required retention period to claim input tax credits and refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Declaration using the Canadian Export Reporting System or Form B13A where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement out of Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of receipt of foreign consideration from the buyer
  • Commercial invoice and sale contract identifying the foreign purchaser and Canadian origin of goods
  • Carrier certificate or customs house broker report confirming export from Canada

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, national e-invoicing clearance API, or digital stamping regime is in force in Canada or Alberta. Businesses may use electronic invoicing voluntarily, but must retain complete and legible transaction records. GST/HST returns generally must be filed electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE or other electronic filing channels when revenue exceeds statutory thresholds; electronic payment of large remittances is also mandated. Exported supply documentation must be retained in paper or digital form for six years.

If a taxpayer zero-rates supplies or claims input tax credits without sufficient export documentation, the CRA may reassess the supply as taxable, deny the refund, and charge interest and arrears. False or negligent statements attract penalties under the Excise Tax Act s. 280, including 25% of the amount of net tax sought to be evaded for gross negligence, 50% of the refund claimed where a refund is claimed with false statements, and misconduct penalties of up to CAD 25,000 to CAD 100,000 for tax evasion offences under s. 327. The CRA may also claw back unjustified refunds for a retrospective period, impose director liability under s. 323, and pursue criminal prosecution for fraud which may include additional fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full currency convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar is a freely floating currency; there are no statutory exchange controls administered by the Bank of Canada, no prior approval requirements for capital inflows or outflows, and no legal differentiation between resident and non-resident access to the domestic FX market. Dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, and service payments may be remitted cross-border without exchange control approval. All cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable by financial institutions to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and banks may require underlying commercial documentation for payment instructions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender rule, mandatory conversion ratio, or imputed repatriation deadline in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts indefinitely in foreign-denominated accounts and may choose when or whether to convert to Canadian dollars. The Bank of Canada does not require export settlement documentation for FX purposes; however, the receiving financial institution must report cross-border electronic funds transfers above the CAD 10,000 threshold to FINTRAC, and Statistics Canada may request balance-of-payments survey data.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing surveillance by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Foreign non-bank electronic money institutions and payment fintechs are not authorized under the Bank Act to take deposits or maintain Canadian domestic clearing accounts with Payments Canada. Such firms may operate in Canada only as money services businesses (MSB) registered with FINTRAC, with compliance obligations under PCMLTFA. Airwallex, when properly registered and following Canadian AML requirements, can provide cross-border payment initiation, FX conversion, and sub-account settlement in limited jurisdictions, but CAD-denominated clearing must ultimately settle through a Canadian Schedule I bank. Offshore EMIs without FINTRAC registration are not viable as the sole treasury clearing rail for an Alberta-based operating corporation; a domestic Schedule I bank account should remain the primary settlement account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan funding should be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent's financial institution to the Canadian company's account at a Schedule I bank. The SWIFT payment must include the beneficiary legal name, account number, the purpose description 'Equity Injection / Capital Contribution' or 'Shareholder Loan for Working Capital,' and the beneficiary financial institution identifier (BIC). There is no central bank use-of-proceeds declaration; however, the receiving bank will verify the corporate KYC file and may request the shareholder resolution and source of funds. For amounts equal to or greater than CAD 10,000, the sending or receiving Canadian financial institution will file an Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC. CAD funds become immediately available via Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) after bank processing; if USD are received, the company may convert to CAD at the prevailing spot or pre-negotiated forward rate through the bank's FX desk. All injection documentation, including executed subscription agreement, board minutes, bank statements, and SWIFT confirmation, must be retained to support future upline audits.Routine supplier and logistics payments in Canada should be cleared through Payments Canada's Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for ACH-style debits/credits, or through LVTS for high-value same-day CAD wires. Cross-border USD and regional payments should be effected by SWIFT MT103 from the Canadian bank to the beneficiary's bank, with the remittance information containing the invoice number, contract reference, and purpose of payment. For international merchandise imports where the kitting assembly is part of a deferred postponement strategy, a commercial documentary letter of credit issued by the Canadian bank may be used for pre-shipment or post-shipment financing, but the preferred treasury rail for open-account trade is SWIFT bank payment obligation or plain MT103 wire. Box 400 Total tax payable (federal + provincial) Sum of federal and provincial taxes; ensure all credits applied. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month up to 12 months; late payment interest at current prescribed rates (currently 8% for corporations).
Box 500 Instalment payments made during the year Non-residents with Canadian-source income must make quarterly instalments if tax exceeds CAD 3,000. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month up to 12 months; late payment interest at current prescribed rates (currently 8% for corporations).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services; CAD 5 million for other transactions; Master File required for entities with gross revenue over CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but applies OECD BEPS principles; economic substance is required to obtain treaty benefits and to avoid the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties up to 200% of tax evaded, fines up to CAD 50,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes under Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act, including civil restitution and potential travel bans.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or participate in such arrangements may be held personally liable for penalties and taxes.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations per violation; administrative penalties for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be subject to fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for willful non-compliance.
