Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A corporation must maintain a registered office in Alberta and a records office as required by the Business Corporations Act. A commercial virtual office may satisfy the address requirement only if it is a genuine service address capable of receiving legal documents, maintaining or accessing corporate records, and supporting the registered agent's statutory functions. A mail-forwarding address or accommodation with no documentary control, personnel, or records capability is unsuitable. For offshore supply-vessel leasing and operation, a virtual office does not by itself establish operational substance, management and control, maritime compliance, tax residence, transfer-pricing support, or eligibility for banking and vessel-finance onboarding. The business should maintain documented Canadian decision-making, accounting records, contracts, insurance, vessel-management arrangements, safety and compliance records, and appropriately qualified personnel or independent service providers.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC) — sole federal tax administrator; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export declarations. Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html), GST/HST NETFILE, and CBSA CARM export reporting portal.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — no pass-through eligibility for corporations. A corporation incorporated in Canada is resident in Canada and taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income (federal general rate 15%, small-business rate 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income for a Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation; Alberta provincial general rate 8%, small-business rate 2%). Territorial_Scope: non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source business and property income, and are subject to an additional 25% Part XIV branch tax (treaty-reduced where applicable) on Canadian-branch after-tax earnings. Tax_Exempt_Zone: none in Alberta; Indian Act exemptions are limited to specific First Nations personal/band property and do not apply to a commercial vessel lessor.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian law. Available elections and regimes: (1) ETA s. 156 election among closely related corporations to treat supplies of taxable property/services as nil consideration; (2) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident to compute Canadian real-property rental income on a net basis; (3) ITA s. 219 election regarding branch tax computation for non-resident corporations; (4) ETA election for a small-supplier to voluntarily become a GST/HST registrant; (5) CCPC subgroup small-business-deduction elections under ITA s. 125; (6) hybrid mismatch ordering elections and deduction-disallowance rules under ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5, 18.6, 20.2, and 94 effective 1 July 2022, applying BEPS Action 2 outcomes to cross-border charter-payment structures involving the vessel-leasing affiliate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: GST/HST registration is mandatory where total worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. For a foreign vessel-leasing entity, charter fees attributable to Canadian users or to vessel operations within Canadian territorial waters count toward the threshold. Voluntary registration below threshold is permitted and commercially advisable to claim input tax credits and zero-rate export charters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An Alberta-resident corporation leasing offshore supply vessels may claim 0% GST on charter revenue where: (a) the charter/bareboat contract is with a non-resident and the vessel is delivered, positioned, and operationally employed wholly outside Canada (zero-rated under ETA, Schedule VI, Part V, including the general services-to-non-resident export rule and the provisions for commercial vessel/transportation services); (b) the vessel is actually used in international commercial transportation or on the high seas in offshore oil-and-gas support beyond Canada's territorial sea, with only incidental calls in Canadian ports; (c) the charterer's residence is documented and the vessel is not made available for domestic Canadian cabotage; and (d) for support services related to offshore drilling, the activities are performed outside Canadian jurisdiction or qualify under the zero-rated offshore mineral activity provisions of ETA Schedule VI, Part V.7. If the charterer is a Canadian-resident oil company or the vessel operates substantively within Canadian waters, GST at 5% applies unless the specific statutory exemption for international commercial vessels is satisfied.

  • Charter party agreement (bareboat, time-charter, or voyage-charter) inclusive of flag-state and vessel-classification certificates

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime exists at the federal or Alberta level. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1,500,000 must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE; all registrants must maintain books and records in readable and accessible format, including electronic records acceptable to CRA. Canada is implementing a voluntary PEPPOL-standard pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework expanding from business-to-government pilots; there is no statutory requirement for B2B/B2C e-invoicing or CRA invoice clearance.

Canada imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is adopted as the CRA administrative evidentiary standard matching ETA s. 169(4) input-tax-credit timing rules and the expectation that export consideration be collected within a reasonable commercial window. If zero-rating is disallowed, CRA may reassess charter fees as taxable at 5% GST within four years (unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is proven), with compound daily interest at prescribed rates. Penalties include ETA s. 280 (false statements or omissions) and s. 285 (gross negligence) at 25% of the tax sought to be evaded, and criminal prosecution under ETA ss. 327-333. On the income tax side, ITA s. 163(2) imposes a gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax, and ITA s. 247(3) applies a 10% transfer-pricing penalty where charter-hire terms or intercompany rates to a foreign affiliate are not arm's length. Fabricated residency evidence or forged certificates could result in prosecution under ITA s. 239, carrying fines up to CAD 5,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Exporters must retain the complete evidentiary chain, including contemporaneous AIS data, vessel logs, and residency declarations, for at least six years from the end of the relevant taxation year.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current account and capital account. There is no exchange-control regime, no prior approval for foreign direct investment into Canada except national-security review under the Investment Canada Act for certain foreign investments, and no restriction on profit remittance. Cross-border dividends and interest are subject to non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (typically 25% statutory, reduced under applicable tax treaties, e.g. Canada–United States treaty to 5%–15% for dividends). Repatriated charter revenue and capital repayments may be remitted freely through any authorized bank in CAD or foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion into CAD, and no time limit for holding foreign currency earned from exports. For offshore supply vessel leasing under ISIC 5222, USD and other foreign-currency charter hire may be retained in domestic or offshore accounts indefinitely, or used directly to settle trade liabilities. Only standard Canadian tax filing, transfer pricing documentation, and FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting thresholds apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) is the central bank; foreign exchange and currency administration are carried out under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act, with banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Anti-money laundering surveillance is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control authority or separate FX approval body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium – Limited Viability for use as a primary treasury venue. Canada regulates retail payments providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), administered by the Bank of Canada; all payment service providers offering services in Canada must be registered, including cross-border digital EMIs. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g. foreign-domiciled money transmission services) can clear CAD settlements only through a registered domestic payment service provider or a partnership with a Schedule I or Schedule II bank. For a Medium_Risk_Enhanced maritime chartering entity, a foreign EMI may serve as an auxiliary FX conversion and small-vendor payout rail, but inbound charter hire and escrow liquidity should sit with a top-tier domestic bank. Legal exposure arises if the EMI is not RPAA-registered or if the bank's correspondent network flags marine-sector beneficial ownership.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the investor or parent foreign entity, with unambiguous Field 50K (ordering party), Field 56A/57A (intermediary and beneficiary bank in Canada), and Field 70 (remittance information). Use purpose code 'CAPITAL INJECTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN' and note the corresponding ISIC 5222 vessel charter project. For equity injections in excess of the Investment Canada Act scrutiny threshold, a notification must be filed with Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada before closing. For intercompany loans, maintain a signed facility agreement, an arm's-length interest rate respecting the CRA prescribed rate, and IFRS-compliant documentation for the Canadian bank's source-of-funds review.Outbound disbursements for vessel leasing operations should settle over SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payouts, profit repatriations, and intra-group settlements. Commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) are the preferred clearing rail for vessel acquisition, bunker procurement, and major dry-dock services, with documents negotiated through a domestic bank's trade finance desk. For USD-denominated disbursements from a Canadian USD account, use SWIFT MT103 routed via a U.S. intermediary bank (Fedwire or CHIPS), while CAD disbursements clear over the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). All wire releases require dual-authorization, ACH/vendor bank validation, and call-back verification.Deliverable Over-the-Counter Foreign Exchange Forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR) under an ISDA Master Agreement with a Canadian-Chartered BankForeign Exchange Swaps (FX Swaps) for managing near-term charter-hire mismatchesCross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) for long-term vessel financing hedgesVanilla Foreign Exchange Options (FX Options) purchased to cap currency risk on USD/EUR charter portfoliosSerial Forwards and Average Rate Forwards for re-occurring offshore payroll and operating expenditureNo-NDF policy: non-deliverable forwards are not practical for CAD because deliverable forward liquidity is robust; NDFs may be used only for exotics (e.g. NOK, BRL) in a separately margin-documented trade

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium – Limited Viability for use as a primary treasury venue. Canada regulates retail payments providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), administered by the Bank of Canada; all payment service providers offering services in Canada must be registered, including cross-border digital EMIs. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g. foreign-domiciled money transmission services) can clear CAD settlements only through a registered domestic payment service provider or a partnership with a Schedule I or Schedule II bank. For a Medium_Risk_Enhanced maritime chartering entity, a foreign EMI may serve as an auxiliary FX conversion and small-vendor payout rail, but inbound charter hire and escrow liquidity should sit with a top-tier domestic bank. Legal exposure arises if the EMI is not RPAA-registered or if the bank's correspondent network flags marine-sector beneficial ownership.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (ABCA), every corporation must appoint an auditor at each annual general meeting unless all shareholders, including non-voting shareholders, pass a unanimous resolution to dispense with the audit; such waiver is valid only for the fiscal year covered and must be renewed annually. A distributing corporation (publicly traded or issuing securities to the public) is statutorily ineligible for the waiver. There is no revenue- or asset-size threshold in the ABCA regime; however, marine mortgage lenders, insurance underwriters, and multi-year OSV charter contracts typically impose an independent audit as a contractual threshold where annual charter revenue exceeds CAD $2 million.
