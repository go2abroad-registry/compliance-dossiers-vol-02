Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — sole national tax administrator for federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, and payroll remittances; also collects Alberta provincial corporate income tax (8%) under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement on behalf of Alberta Treasury Board and Finance / Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration. Primary portal: CRA 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (federal) or Alberta Business Corporations Act (RSA 2000, c B-9); the corporation is deemed resident in Canada (ITA s. 250(4)(a)). (2) Apply for the CRA Business Number (BN) via BRO, Form RC1, or by phone (1-800-959-5525); the 9-digit BN is the universal tax identifier, with program accounts: RC0001 (corporate income tax), RT0001 (GST/HST), RP0001 (payroll). (3) Foreign/non-resident ownership is fully permitted; provide the Canadian registered office address, director declarations, and, if no Canadian address exists, Form RC460-1 (Canadian Resident Address Information). (4) Register for GST/HST (RT account) once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000, or voluntarily to recover input tax credits on export-related inputs. (5) No separate Alberta corporate tax account number is required; the CRA administers the Alberta corporate income tax under the same BN. (6) If federally incorporated, file an extra-provincial registration with the Alberta Corporate Registry. (7) Register for a payroll account (RP) before employing personnel in Canada; withhold federal income tax, CPP, and EI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income (ITA ss. 2, 3, 9), with foreign tax credits under s. 126; non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source business income, employment income, and gains on taxable Canadian property. Standard combined federal + Alberta general corporate rate is 23% for 2025 (15% federal + 8% Alberta); the federal rate is legislated to rise to 16% (2026) and 17% (2027). No Tax_Exempt_Zone exists in Alberta. A Canadian-incorporated entity controlled by non-residents is not a CCPC and therefore cannot claim the Small Business Deduction (combined small-business rate 11%: 9% federal + 2% Alberta on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income). Export service revenue is accrued on the receivable basis.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; foreign-entity classification follows legal form and CRA administrative guidance (Income Tax Folio S4-F6-C1). Available mechanisms: (a) the Canadian corporation cannot elect pass-through or disregarded status; (b) GST/HST elections: 'Quick Method' (ETA s. 258) and closely-related corporation election (ETA s. 156); (c) non-resident branch tax under ITA s. 219 (25%, treaty-reduced) applies automatically; (d) non-residents with Canadian rental/royalty income may elect net-basis taxation under ITA s. 216(4); (e) ITA s. 113 provides a 90–100% exemption for dividends received from foreign affiliates; (f) proposed/deferred hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2, 18.21, per the 2021 Fall Economic Statement and subsequent draft legislation, BEPS Action 2) would restrict deductions on hybrid mismatch arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — 'small supplier' threshold under ETA s. 239(1): GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once the value of worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in any calendar quarter or in the four most recent consecutive calendar quarters. Zero-rated export supplies count toward the threshold. Voluntary registration is available to non-residents to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Ship crew recruitment and crewing-management services (ISIC 5222 / NAICS 488390) supplied to a non-resident shipowner or operator qualify for GST/HST zero-rating under s. 23(1), Part V, Schedule V, ETA where: (a) the recipient is a non-resident person not registered for GST/HST; (b) the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada; (c) the service is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed (a pure personnel-procurement service is not customarily 'in respect of' the vessel); and (d) the service is not supplied to an individual present in Canada. No 5% GST is charged on qualifying exports; input tax credits on Canadian overhead are recovered through the net-tax return, producing a refund when credits exceed tax. Risk note: if the contract bundles crew management with services performed on a vessel located in Canadian waters, CRA may recharacterize part of the supply as taxable; contracts should clearly sever pure recruitment services.

  • Executed crewing service agreement/contract naming the non-resident recipient, vessel(s), and service scope
  • ETA-compliant GST/HST invoice bearing the supplier Business Number and stating zero-rating under Schedule V, Part V, s. 23(1)
  • Evidence of recipient non-resident status (foreign certificate of incorporation, foreign tax identifier)
  • Crew placement manifests and seafarer employment contracts demonstrating services rendered for the non-resident principal
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • For any ancillary physical goods exports (e.g., marine equipment samples over CAD 2,000): B13A Canadian Export Declaration via CAERS/CITSW, Commercial Invoice, and Bill of Lading
  • GST/HST net-tax calculations and input tax credit working papers

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory government API clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime is in force in Canada. The CRA operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework based on the international Peppol standard (first certified service providers launched 2023). GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed data (supplier BN, invoice date, recipient identity, description, consideration, tax amount) under ETA ss. 221–223; books and records must be retained for six years.

CRA may reassess within the normal 4-year period (ETA s. 298(1)); no limitation period applies for misrepresentation attributable to fraud, neglect, or willful default (ETA s. 298(4)). A false or grossly negligent zero-rating claim triggers a penalty under ETA s. 285.1 equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax; refunds/input tax credits wrongly claimed are clawed back with daily compounded arrears interest (ETA s. 280). Wilful evasion is an offense under ETA s. 327 and Criminal Code s. 380, punishable by fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years (indictable). For income tax, false export-revenue reporting attracts the 50% gross-negligence penalty (ITA s. 163(2)) and prosecution under ITA s. 239. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is an audit reasonableness benchmark — export receivables remain taxable when 'receivable' under the accrual method, artificial deferral through non-resident receivables may trigger imputed interest under ITA s. 17, and cross-border pricing must satisfy the ITA s. 247 arm's-length standard.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Canada accepts IMF Article VIII obligations, with no restriction on payments for current international transactions, dividends, or profit remittances. The capital account is fully liberalized; share capital injection and shareholder loans into an Alberta corporation are permitted without exchange-control approval. There is no withholding or approval outside the tax regime; outbound profit remittances are subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends (generally 25% under the Income Tax Act, commonly reduced to 5-15% under Canada tax treaties) and applicable CRS/FATCA filing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender rule exists in Canada. There is no statutory timeline or conversion ratio for export receipts. The company may lawfully retain foreign currency proceeds in a Canadian dollar, US dollar, or multi-currency account. The only obligations are trade reporting to Statistics Canada via the Canadian International Merchandise Trade (or the Trade in Services) system and adherence to general FINTRAC record keeping.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange surveillance overseen by the Department of Finance Canada and bank supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium as a supplementary liquidation rail; unacceptable as the sole treasury bank. An offshore digital EMI is not a bank under the Bank Act; to provide payment/FX services to Canadian clients it must be registered with FINTRAC as a money services business (MSB) under the PCMLTFA, meet provincial money lending/transfer registration in Alberta if applicable, and maintain a physical compliance presence or agent relationship. Most non-regulated offshore EMIs fail provincial/AML suitability and are rejected at onboarding. A FINTRAC-registered EMI (e.g., Wise Business, or a chartered-bank-backed payment service provider) can serve crew payroll disbursements in small-value batches but must not hold capital escrow.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal general corporate rate 15% + Alberta general corporate rate 8% = combined 23%; reduced combined rate for a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income: 9% federal + 2% Alberta = 11%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed no later than 6 months after the corporation's fiscal year-end (December 31 year-end = June 30 filing deadline); balance-due date for non-CCPCs is 2 months after year-end and for CCPCs claiming the SBD is 3 months after year-end; instalments are due on the last day of each month or quarter under the 2-month/3-cycle rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 Information Return must be filed when aggregate non-arm's length transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000 in a fiscal year (no minimum threshold for transactions with residents of designated non-treaty countries); CRA expects contemporaneous Local File documentation for controlled transactions exceeding CAD 5,000,000; a Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated audited revenue of at least CAD 1,000,000,000; the 'reasonable efforts' defence under s. 247(3) requires that the taxpayer prepare and retain contemporaneous documentation before the filing-due date.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a UAE-style ESR label; instead, the Income Tax Act and the OECD-BEPS framework require real economic substance through: (1) the Place of Effective Management (POEM) test for corporate residency; (2) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, s. 245 ITA); and (3) the 'business carried on in Canada' permanent-establishment doctrine under s. 253 ITA. For an Alberta crew recruitment agency, actual employees, office premises in Alberta, payroll administration functions, and commercially rational decision-making are required; a letterbox or shell structure directing core management functions offshore invites re-characterization, deemed Canadian residency, and reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful False Statements

Statutory sanction: Section 239(1) ITA: on summary conviction, a fine of 50%-200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of 30 days to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of 100%-200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civilly, the gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s. 163(2) and compound interest at the prescribed rate.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are treated as parties to the offense under s. 242 ITA and can be personally fined and imprisoned up to 5 years; CRA may also issue a departure-from-Canada travel ban where the tax debt exceeds CAD 50,000; criminal convictions can trigger additional Criminal Code money-laundering charges (up to 10 years imprisonment).
Shell Company / GAAR / Residency Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment disregarding the abusive transaction, denial of treaty or SBD benefits, reallocation of income to the Alberta corporation, penalties under s. 163(2) at 50% of the reassessed tax, interest from the original due date, and loss of loss-carryforward utilization.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false T2 returns can be charged under s. 239; personal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA can pierce the corporate veil under s. 160 for non-arm's length transfers of property, making directors jointly and severally liable for the market value transferred.
Unremitted Payroll and Non-Resident Withholding Taxes

Statutory sanction: Deemed trust under s. 227(4) over all corporate assets with super-priority over secured creditors; failure to remit penalties of 10% plus 1% per month up to 20 months; interest at the prescribed rate; wilful failure to withhold is an offense punishable by a fine up to CAD 25,000 and/or 12 months imprisonment under s. 227(9); CRA may freeze bank accounts and register a lien against the corporation.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for all unremitted source deductions and GST/HST regardless of the corporate veil; the only defense is the exercise of reasonable due diligence, which is assessed strictly; personal liability extends to travel restrictions, seizure of personal assets, and bankruptcy proceedings initiated by the CRA.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance on Cross-Border Payroll Flows

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, failure to report a large cash or wire transaction is punishable by administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 500,000 for entities; criminal money laundering under s. 462.31 of the Criminal Code carries imprisonment of up to 10 years and unlimited fines; the CRA and FINTRAC can each pursue concurrent sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly direct, authorize, or acquiesce in structuring transactions are personally chargeable with money laundering offenses; penalties include imprisonment up to 10 years, confiscation of personal and corporate proceeds, and director bans under Canadian corporate law.
