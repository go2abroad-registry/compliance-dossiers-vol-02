Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), administered via the 'My Business Account' corporate portal and the Business Registration Online (BRO) gateway at https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business.html. Alberta corporate income tax is imposed under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act but is assessed, collected, and administered exclusively by the CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement; no separate Alberta corporate tax filing portal exists for income tax, although an Alberta Corporate Access Number is required from the Alberta Corporate Registry for extra-provincial registration.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian-incorporated business corporation (federal or Alberta provincial) obtains its primary tax identifier through the CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1-Request for a Business Number. The CRA issues a unique 9-digit Business Number (BN), with program account suffixes: 'RC' for corporation income tax, 'RT' for GST/HST, and 'RP' for payroll source deductions. A foreign-inuring organizational detail: if the operating entity is a non-resident-owned corporation, it must also file Form RC464 (Registration and Election Information for a Corporation) and the articles of incorporation or continuance must be endorsed by the Alberta Corporate Registry (Corporate Access Number) before the BN is activated. Registration must be completed before invoicing for taxable supplies, since the GST/HST registration number must appear on every invoice.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation (Federal/Provincial) is a resident taxpayer under Income Tax Act s. 2(2), taxable at the corporate level on worldwide income, and no pass-through or US-style 'check-the-box' flow-through classification is available. The combined corporate rate in Alberta is 23% (federal 15% + Alberta 8%). The vessel-operating subsidiary is not a 'Canadian-controlled private corporation' (CCPC) if controlled by non-residents, eliminating the small business deduction; the federal general rate and the Alberta 8% rate therefore apply. Territorial scope is worldwide for corporations resident in Canada (central management and control test), subject to foreign tax credit and treaty relief. — A Canadian-incorporated business corporation (federal or Alberta provincial) obtains its primary tax identifier through the CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1-Request for a Business Number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation (Federal/Provincial) is a resident taxpayer under Income Tax Act s. 2(2), taxable at the corporate level on worldwide income, and no pass-through or US-style 'check-the-box' flow-through classification is available. The combined corporate rate in Alberta is 23% (federal 15% + Alberta 8%). The vessel-operating subsidiary is not a 'Canadian-controlled private corporation' (CCPC) if controlled by non-residents, eliminating the small business deduction; the federal general rate and the Alberta 8% rate therefore apply. Territorial scope is worldwide for corporations resident in Canada (central management and control test), subject to foreign tax credit and treaty relief.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box entity classification regime; classification is statutory. Available elections materially affecting tax identity: (i) Income Tax Act s. 216 election for a non-resident to file on net income basis and reduce Part XIII withholding; (ii) s. 219 election for a non-resident corporation to pay branch tax on Canadian-source income; (iii) Excise Tax Act ss. 156/157 joint elections for closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for no consideration (easing GST/HST cash-flow on intercompany towage/salvage contracts); (iv) Income Tax Act s. 85 rollover election on asset transfers into the Canadian subsidiary; (v) hybrid mismatch integrity rules under Income Tax Act ss. 12.8 and 18.4 (post-BEPS) requiring counterparty consistency in respect of foreign hybrid entities and denying duplicate deductions; (vi) GST/HST election under ETA s. 240(3) related to deeming a non-resident to be carrying on the business of making taxable supplies in a listed financial institution context, though inapplicable to ordinary maritime services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the annual worldwide gross-revenue 'small supplier' threshold under ETA s. 148(1); once exceeded, registration is mandatory within 30 days (ETA s. 240(1)). A non-resident or domestic corporation supplying deep-sea towage and salvage services to foreign vessel owners is generally entitled (and often obliged) to register once total taxable supplies exceed this threshold; voluntary registration may still be advisable to reclaim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V to a registered Canadian corporation supplying a service to a non-resident person who is not registered under the ETA and who is outside Canada at the time the supply is made, where the service is supplied in respect of tangible personal property (a vessel) situated outside Canada when the service is performed. Deep-sea towage and emergency salvage services rendered on the high seas or in foreign territorial waters to a foreign-flagged vessel operated by a non-resident owner therefore qualify for 0% GST/HST. To preserve zero-rating the supplier must prove the offshore situs of the service and the foreign status of the recipient; towing services that begin in Canadian waters and terminate at a foreign port, or services performed in respect of a Canadian-documented vessel for a Canadian-resident custodian, are taxable at 5% GST unless the vessel is used primarily in international commercial transportation. The zero-rated supplier recovers all ITCs on fuel, port dues, spare parts, and contracted subcontractor towing charges through the net GST/HST return, creating the refund claim requiring export substantiation.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed under the Customs Act for any exported goods exceeding CAD 2,000 in value, with supporting inward/outward manifest data transmitted through the Canadian Export Reporting System (ERS); where the service involves no physical export of goods, a service contract specifying the international towage route and offshore salvage location suffices
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or towage waybill / Lloyds Open Form (LOF 2011) salvage contract evidencing the carriage of the towed vessel or cargo from a Canadian port to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank MT103/SWIFT wire confirmation or letter of credit settlement record evidencing receipt of foreign-currency payment from the non-resident shipowner or P&I club; CRA does not issue its own certificate but the treasury remittance record must be maintained as primary evidence

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance regime, API-based e-invoicing stamping, or digital fiscal reporting equivalent to PEPPOL continuous transaction controls; the Pan-Canadian B2B/Government e-invoice framework (PEPPOL) is voluntary. GST/HST invoices are governed by ETA s. 223 information requirements: supplier BN (RT account), invoice date, description, tax rate, GST payable, and total consideration must be stated. Failure to show the GST/HST registration number triggers a penalty under ETA s. 280(2). For zero-rated export supplies the registrant need not reflect tax on the invoice, but must retain supporting export documents electronically in a form that satisfies CRA's record-keeping audit trail under ETA s. 286 and Income Tax Act s. 230.

CRA audit scrutiny focuses on the factual export nexus: auditors will require a complete documentary chain linking the service to a non-resident recipient and a foreign-situated vessel; failure to produce the B13A, bill of lading/towage contract, and remittance evidence triggers re-assessment of the entire refund as taxable at 5% GST, with arrears interest computed on a daily-compound basis. False export claims attract civil penalties under ETA s. 281.2 (the greater of CAD 100 and 10% of the tax avoided), s. 280(1) compliance penalties, and prosecution under ETA s. 285 or s. 286 for evasion, with fines up to CAD 25,000 on summary conviction or CAD 100,000 on indictment, plus imprisonment in egregious cases. The normal 4-year reassessment window is extended indefinitely where the CRA establishes misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default; clawback is retrospective to the first zero-rated claim included in any open return.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency regime with full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. There are no general exchange controls, foreign-currency rationing, or prior approvals for inward or outward remittances. Profit repatriation is not subject to exchange-control limits; however, cross-border dividend and interest remittances attract Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, typically 25% statutory, reducible under an applicable tax treaty (e.g., Canada–US treaty to 5% on dividends for substantial holders and 15% otherwise). No capital account authorization is required for foreign direct investment into an Alberta business corporation, but reporting thresholds under the Canada Business Corporations Act and Investment Canada Act may apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Canadian law does not require exporters to surrender foreign currency to the central bank or to any designated dealer. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts held in Canada or abroad, subject to Canadian reporting obligations for foreign property and to FINTRAC reporting for cross-border currency transfers exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential banking supervision delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange monitoring and wire-reporting administration is shared with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Foreign digital banking and electronic money institutions are not permitted to operate in Canada without registration as a money services business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and, if providing retail payment services, as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act. An offshore EMI without a Canadian MSB registration cannot maintain a domestic settlement account or originate CAD-denominated direct debits. A registered and compliant EMI may be used as a supplementary payment rail, but Tier 1 banks remain necessary for escrow, letters of credit, and high-value cross-border wires.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols require the remitter to transmit funds through the SWIFT network using MT103 for high-value injections. The remitter must use a proper purpose code; for equity injections into an Alberta corporation, use 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or the OECD purpose code 'Equity Capital' (e.g., BOP code 6X or equivalent per the transmitting bank's classification). The receiving Canadian bank will require a Beneficial Ownership Declaration and may require proof of source of funds for amounts exceeding CAD 100,000. The funds must be deposited into the designated Capital Escrow Account before being released to the Operating Account upon completion of corporate verification. To avoid triggering the Investment Canada Act review, the inbound injection should be structured either as equity below the monetary threshold or as a direct loan not exceeding the reviewing threshold for acquisition of control of a Canadian business.Clearing rails for maritime towage and salvage trade settlements are primarily SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers, the Canadian Wire System operated through Payments Canada for CAD-denominated wire payments, and Commercial Letters of Credit (Documentary Collections) where vessel owners or charterers require bank-backed settlement. For salvage awards paid under the Lloyd's Open Form, disbursements are commonly made through MT103 to a designated law firm escrow account in London or to the London P&I Club correspondent. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general rate (federal 15% + Alberta provincial 8%); Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPC) benefit from a reduced combined rate of 11% on the first CA$500,000 of active business income (federal small business deduction 9% + Alberta small business deduction 2% on the first CA$600,000 of Alberta-allocated income).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders unless a treaty rate applies. Dividends paid to a Canadian-resident corporate or individual shareholder are not subject to tax at source. Dividends from a 'foreign affiliate' may be deductible from net income under ITA s. 113 and are not subject to Part XIII.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within six months after the fiscal year-end (e.g., December 31 year-end is due June 30). The final balance of tax payable is due two months after the fiscal year-end (three months for a CCPC with federal taxable income not exceeding CA$500,000). Large corporations (federal taxable capital exceeding CA$10,000,000) are ineligible for the three-month payment extension.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory monetary threshold; Canada's TP rules under ITA s. 247 apply to every non-arm's-length cross-border transaction. CRA maintains an administrative audit-focus threshold where the aggregate annual value of non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceeds CA$1,000,000 (goods) or CA$250,000 (services); however, documentation is strictly required contemporaneously for all such transactions regardless of size, and a 10% penalty applies for a TP adjustment exceeding CA$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue unless complete and credible documentation is filed with the T2/T106 within 90 days of a CRA request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulation akin to the UAE, but the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) at ITA s. 245 permits reassessment of any avoidance transaction lacking genuine economic substance, business purpose, and bona fide non-tax purpose. For offshore towage and salvage, the CRA vigorously scrutinizes shell-flag structures in Panama, Marshall Islands, and Malta: a Canadian parent that derives towage fees through a zero-tax foreign subsidiary and repatriates earnings as exempt dividends under s. 113 may be reassessed under GAAR or, where foreign accrual property income (FAPI) applies, the passive/incident income is taxed in Canada on accrual. The foreign vessel-owning entity must demonstrate substantive crew, vessel control, contracts, and actual salvage response operations outside Canada. A bare registration of flag without employees, offices, and commercial decision-making will be disregarded on a sham/substance-over-form basis, triggering full Canadian taxation of the entire towage revenue on a net basis plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent International Towage Structuring

Statutory sanction: CRA criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1) and Criminal Code fraud offenses: fines of 50-200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to five years on indictment; in civil reassessment, the full unreported income is taxed at the 23% combined rate with gross negligence penalties of 50% under s. 163(2), daily interest, and the disallowance of all deductions attributable to the undisclosed foreign vehicle.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable as signing officers for the corporation's tax debt attributable to unremitted source withholdings and non-resident tax under ITA s. 227.1, including interest and penalties; the CRA will issue a director's liability assessment directly against individual directors and may obtain a Federal Court charging order on personal assets; criminal conviction in tax fraud cases commonly results in restitution orders, ineligibility to act as a director under provincial corporate legislation, and imprisonment, which necessarily prevents international travel without judicial consent.
Shell Company / GAAR Abuse and Economic Substance Disregard

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under s. 245 disallows the tax benefit and imposes interest from the original filing date; a 50% gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2) may be additionally applied where the misrepresentation was knowing; for a large corporation, Part I.3 and Part XIII withholding on dividends paid to the shell may be reinstated at 25% plus an intentional-withholding penalty of 10% under s. 227(9).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participated in the shell arrangement face joint and several liability for all reassessed taxes and penalties, including interest compounding, and are subject to CRA's asset-freezing orders. Civil fraud proceedings by minority shareholders or lenders may pierce the corporate veil, and criminal liability for conspiracy to evade tax can result in up to five years incarceration and a formal finding preventing future cross-border travel.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax and Information Return Failures

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s. 227(8), the payer is liable for the full un-withheld tax, interest, and a penalty equal to 10% of the amount (plus a further penalty of 20% where the failure was knowingly made); failure to file the information returns incurs the daily penalties and the 10% TP documentation penalty on the adjusted gross amount (minimum threshold of CA$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue is only a mitigating condition).

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under s. 227.1 for the unremitted withholding tax amounts, including all interest and penalties, unless they exercised due diligence; the CRA enforces director's liability aggressively in the maritime sector irrespective of the corporation's insolvency; a criminal finding of wilful non-withholding carries imprisonment up to five years.
Late Filing / Chronic Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Section 162(1) late filing penalty of 5% plus 1% per month up to 12 months; repeated failures trigger an additional 10% penalty; failure to file for 24 months or more triggers a criminal prosecution for tax evasion with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment; CRA will issue an arbitrary assessment based on external data, which becomes payable immediately.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to cause the corporation to file timely returns or remit installments are jointly liable for the unpaid tax and any installments plus interest; a director may request relief under the 'due diligence' defense only if all reasonable steps were taken; repeat offenders face personal seizure of bank accounts, garnishment of towage receivables, and CRA's ability to require a judicial order preventing departure from Canada during pending criminal proceedings.
