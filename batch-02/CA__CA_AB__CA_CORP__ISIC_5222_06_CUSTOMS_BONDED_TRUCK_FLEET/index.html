Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA/Agence du revenu du Canada), portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; Alberta has no provincial sales tax authority for GST purposes.

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation carrying on business in Alberta must first complete extra-provincial registration with Alberta Corporate Registry under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta), then apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN). The application is made using Form RC1, Request for a Business Number, together with certified incorporation documents and Alberta extra-provincial registration evidence. The BN is a 9-digit number and the GST/HST account is added as a 2-letter program account code 'RT' plus a 4-digit reference number. The same BN serves for corporate income tax (account 'RC'), payroll (account 'RP'), and GST/HST. Non-resident applicants may file electronically through an authorized representative or by mail; CRA issues the BN and GST/HST registration effective on the date the registrant begins commercial activity or the requested effective date.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A federally or provincially incorporated Business Corporation is mandatorily classified as a corporation for Canadian income tax and is not eligible for pass-through treatment. Hybrid mismatch provisions under the Income Tax Act (including sections 18.4 to 18.6) may deny deductions for amounts arising under hybrid arrangements or fiscally transparent foreign entities; no voluntary election is available to alter the entity-level taxation of a Canadian corporation. For GST/HST, no entity-classification election exists; registration is mandatory when the taxable-supply threshold is exceeded, and a GST-registered supplier may elect to use the quick method in limited situations, but that does not affect income-tax entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (total worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, over the current calendar quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters, or any single calendar quarter exceeding CAD 30,000 triggers registration; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada is subject to the same threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated where they are supplied by way of sale and are exported as part of the supply, or where the supplier or an agent physically exports the goods, and the supplier retains contemporaneous documentary proof of export. Services, including international freight transportation of exported goods by a customs-bonded road feeder truck fleet, may be zero-rated under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act when the supply is a freight transportation service or ancillary service provided in respect of goods being exported from Canada, or where the service is supplied to a non-resident and is not for use in Canada. The road feeder segment from an inland Canadian warehouse to an international airport or to a U.S. point can qualify only if it forms part of the continuous international movement of the goods and is supported by the required customs and commercial documentation; purely domestic warehousing, handling, or terminal services do not qualify for zero-rating.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) / customs export report validated by CBSA or via the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export system
  • Through Bill of Lading or Cargo Control Document (PARS/PDI) evidencing the customs-bonded movement and the international destination of the goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/ACH credit evidence showing receipt of export sale proceeds in the Canadian entity's bank account within the applicable foreign-exchange repatriation timeline

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal invoice clearance, digital stamping, or CRA pre-authorization of invoices. A GST/HST registrant must issue invoices or simplified documentation containing the supplier's BN, tax rate, total amount, and tax amount in compliance with Excise Tax Act documentary requirements. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,500,000 and for registrants designated by the CRA; GST/HST NETFILE is the accepted electronic channel. No API-based clearance or government invoice repository is imposed.

CRA may reassess claimed zero-rated export supplies within the normal four-year limitation period, and indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. If documentary proof of export is deficient, CRA disallows zero-rating, recaptures unreported GST, and charges interest at the prescribed rate. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a failure-to-file penalty of 5%, plus 1% per month up to 10%, and a false-statement penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 15% of the tax understated in cases of negligence or wilful misconduct. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating claims may lead to criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act, with fines and imprisonment, including the clawback of export tax refunds and associated input tax credits. CRA audit programs frequently target high-risk export sectors, including customs-bonded trucking, and request B13A records, cargo manifests, customs declarations, bank remittance evidence, and internal freight movement logs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating exchange-rate regime with full current-account and capital-account convertibility and observes IMF Article VIII obligations. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible; there are no exchange-control licences, quantitative limits, prior-approval mechanisms, or repatriation authorizations applicable to trade receipts, dividends, loan servicing, or equity flows. Profit-remittance friction is fiscal rather than regulatory: dividends distributed by an Alberta business corporation to a non-resident shareholder are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory rate 25%, typically reduced to 15% or 5% under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention and to comparable levels under other OECD treaties), and interest deductions on intercompany debt are constrained by Canada's thin-capitalization rules (Income Tax Act s. 18(4)-(6)) and the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) rules. No Bank of Canada authorization or foreign-investment permit is required for the incorporation and capitalization of a new Alberta trucking entity; any Investment Canada Act filing obligations are post-establishment notifications only and do not suspend operations. Convertibility risk is therefore negligible for both inbound capital injection and outbound cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, forced conversion, or holding-period requirement exists in Canada. The Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and no current federal or Alberta statute imposes mandatory conversion ratios, designated-account requirements, or collection timelines for export or freight proceeds. Customs-bonded road-feeder revenues invoiced in U.S. dollars may be retained in a Canadian or offshore USD account, converted at the operator's discretion, or physically remitted into Canada without restriction. The only compliance duties are: (i) FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting by the bank for any cross-border wire of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) Statistics Canada / Bank of Canada International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) statistical reporting for international service transactions above the applicable aggregate threshold; and (iii) Income Tax Act recognition in the entity's reporting currency, with a functional-currency election available under ITA s. 261. CBSA does not supervise currency conversion or foreign-exchange retention in connection with customs-bonded transportation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), which acts as monetary authority and retail-payment-activity supervisor under the Retail Payment Activities Act; prudential regulation and bank licensing are administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering surveillance is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); customs-bond compliance is supervised by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration because the currency is fully convertible.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium, and acceptable only as a secondary treasury rail. A foreign or Canadian fintech payment-service provider must be registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) and, once the Retail Payment Activities Act licensing regime is fully in force, must hold a retail-payment-activity licence issued by the Bank of Canada. Digital-only accounts (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Ebury, Payoneer, and similar) are viable for non-critical vendor disbursements, sub-contractor settlements, and opportunistic FX conversion, but they are not a substitute for a Schedule I bank relationship because they cannot provide LVTS clearing, CBSA bond-collateral custody, credit or overdraft facilities, or the correspondent chain required for commercial letters of credit. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder's equity subscription should be wired via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire where the remitter is in the U.S.) and credited to the corporation's operational multi-currency account at the chosen Canadian bank. Canadian law imposes no statutory purpose code or FX approval, but the bank's compliance form must carry a narrative remittance description reading 'equity contribution / subscription price for common shares' with reference to the executed subscription agreement and directors' resolution. The bank is obliged to file a FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer report for every cross-border wire of CAD 10,000 or more. For injections above CAD 1,000,000, the compliance desk should be pre-notified with a source-of-funds file comprising audited or reviewed financial statements, bank statements of the subscribing shareholder, and confirmation that no conduit financing is involved. USD-denominated injections are cleared through the bank's U.S. correspondent and either internally converted to CAD at the prevailing market rate or retained in a USD twin account if the fleet's export freight receivables are predominantly USD-dominated; CAD settlement occurs same-day through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS).Domestic CAD disbursements clear through Payments Canada's LVTS for high-value wires and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for ACH/direct deposit. Cross-border trade settlements use: (1) SWIFT MT103 wires for fuel, tolls, maintenance, insurance, and sub-carrier payments to non-resident payees; (2) the USD bulk exchange (U.S. ACH through the Canadian bank's U.S. branch or correspondent) for recurring monthly USD obligations; (3) Fedwire for urgent airport cargo-gate settlements; (4) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) for capital equipment, aircraft cargo containers, or collateralized fuel procurement of significant notional value; and (5) the CBSA CANPAD electronic payment platform for all customs duties, import GST/HST, and bonded-warehouse charges. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate income tax (15% federal Part I + 8% Alberta provincial corporate income tax); active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation up to CAD 500,000 is taxed at an effective 11% combined federal/Alberta rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on all taxable dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident individual or corporation; statutory rate applies on gross dividend without deduction of expenses

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is required when a Canadian resident (or PE) is a participant in a non-arm's length cross-border transaction that exceeds CAD 1,000,000 for goods and CAD 500,000 for services, rents, royalties, and interest; T106 information return must be filed for related-party cross-border transactions exceeding these thresholds; Master File and Local File contemporaneous documentation should be prepared for any controlled transaction with high-value intercompany services (e.g., fleet dispatch fees, trucking management fees); Country-by-Country reporting is required when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 750 million

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to UAE ESR, but CRA will deny benefits where transactions are artificial or lack commercial substance using the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) (Income Tax Act s.245); for airport feeder trucking, commercial substance requires a physical Canadian office, employees, vehicles registered in Alberta, a permanent establishment location, and operational contracts; a shell corporation cannot substantiate customs bonded carrier licensing or CRA GST/HST registrations, and CRA will recharacterize revenue if the Canadian entity exists solely as a conduit to move profits offshore

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Omission

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the net amount of tax understated; criminal sanction on summary conviction under Income Tax Act s.239: fine between 50% and 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and up to 2 years imprisonment; also CRA may revoke the T2 status and issue an arbitrary assessment

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize evasion can be personally liable under s.239 for the corporate fine; CRA can issue a director's liability assessment under s.227.1 for unremitted source deductions, travel bans may be issued by CRA (Contraventions Act), and a criminal record blocks entry to the US for trucking operations
Shell Corporation / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA can disallow the management fee deductions, add gross negligence penalty of 50%, and apply Part XIII withholding tax on the fees if deemed a dividend; PA/NS? GAAR may require the transaction to be ignored for tax purposes; customs authorities may suspend the bonded carrier licence for not maintaining a real place of business

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid Part XIII withholding remittances even if the corporate entity is bankrupt; CRA may pierce the corporate veil in cases of fraud or sham; exclusion from the US customs-trusted trader program for the individual director
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment by CRA plus interest; penalty under s.247(3) is 10% of the aggregate adjustment when taxpayer lacked or failed to provide reasonable documentation; T106 late-filing penalties up to CAD 100 per day (CAD 2,500 minimum per return); CRA may issue a lifetime prohibition from the authorized dealer program for repeat offenders

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false T106 schedules or who direct the offshore pricing structure can be jointly and severally liable for the 10% penalty under s.247(6) and for the unpaid withholding tax if fees are recharacterized as dividends
Profit Repatriation Without Withholding

Statutory sanction: 25% Part XIII withholding tax assessed on the total gross amount recharacterized, plus arrears interest and a 50% gross negligence penalty; failure to withhold makes the Canadian resident payer liable for the full 25% tax even if the non-resident never reported the amount

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unpaid Part XIII withholding amount under s.227.1(1); CRA can freeze the director's personal bank accounts and sell personal assets to satisfy the tax debt; repeated intentional violations trigger travel ban requests under the Federal Court
