Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; corporate income tax and GST/HST are administered under the Income Tax Act (ITA) and Part IX of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) through the CRA Business Registration Online portal and My Business Account portal. Alberta corporate income tax is collected by the CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement, so the same federal Business Number and corporate tax program account simultaneously satisfy the Alberta filing and remittance obligations; the Alberta Ministry of Finance / Alberta Corporate Registry handles only corporate legal registration, not tax assessment.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) and, if federally incorporated, extra-provincially register in Alberta to obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number or federal corporation number. Step 2: Apply for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1; the BN is the single national tax identification number and, in standard cases, is issued immediately upon electronic application. Step 3: Enroll for the required program accounts under the BN: RC0001 for federal and Alberta corporate income tax and RT0001 for GST/HST, adding RP0001 payroll only if the Alberta office hires employees. Step 4: Compulsory GST/HST registration is triggered once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is strongly recommended for a zero-rated engine lessor to permit recovery of input tax credits embedded in engine acquisition, financing, storage, and maintenance inputs. Step 5: File T2 corporate returns and GST/HST returns electronically through CRA NETFILE or EDI/XML services and remit payments via CRA My Payment or financial institution rails; non-resident shareholders require no CRA tax number unless a Part XIII withholding return becomes necessary.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation resident in Canada and is assessed under Part I of the ITA on worldwide income; there is no partnership or pass-through eligibility and no territorial remittance regime. The combined Alberta and federal corporate rate is approximately 23 percent for general corporations (federal general rate 15 percent plus Alberta flat rate 8 percent); the Alberta small business rate of 2 percent on the first CAD 500,000 applies only to a Canadian-controlled private corporation and will normally be unavailable to a special-purpose cross-border aircraft leasing vehicle that is foreign controlled. The entity is not situated in any tax-exempt zone and no Alberta general capital tax applies to the leasing corporation.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or entity classification election; classification is statutory, although the GST/HST rules permit certain elective treatments. Relevant elections for the engine lessor include: (i) the ETA s.156 closely related corporations election, which removes intercorporate supplies from the GST net where the 90 percent control test is satisfied; (ii) the GST/HST filing frequency election via Form GST20 for registrants with annual taxable supplies under CAD 400,000; (iii) the ITA s.85(1) rollover election to transfer aircraft engines, lease contracts, or escrow rights into the leasing corporation without immediate tax; (iv) the election under ETA s.169 to allocate input tax credits and any election to file zero-rated export documentation on a transaction-by-transaction basis; and (v) treaty-residence certifications such as Form NR301 to substantiate the foreign lessee's status for zero-rating purposes. Hybrid mismatch elections under BEPS Action 2 are not yet operational in Canada for this structure, but cross-border lease consideration must respect OECD treaty characterization as business profits under Article 7 rather than royalties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — registration with the CRA for the GST/HST program account (RT0001) becomes compulsory once taxable supplies made in Canada, including zero-rated aircraft-part leases, exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters; a person under the threshold is a small supplier and need not register, but voluntary registration is recommended for a zero-rated lessor to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Eligibility derives from Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, which zero-rates a supply of an aircraft part to a non-resident person not registered under ETA s.240 for GST/HST. A turbofan aircraft engine qualifies as an aircraft part. The CRA will test: (i) the lessee is a non-resident airline or operator with no GST/HST registration; (ii) the engine is not supplied for use primarily in Canada and is in fact deployed on the lessee's international fleet, as evidenced by registry, flight, and redelivery records; (iii) the lease is a bare or dry lease without crew, maintenance integration, or operational services that would constitute a separate taxable supply; (iv) delivery and redelivery terms move the engine to locations outside Canada by air waybill or, if the engine is already abroad, title and location records evidence a foreign situs; and (v) lease documentation contains a signed zero-rating certificate and the rental consideration is settled through normal cross-border banking rails. A finance lease containing a nominal purchase option may be recharacterized as a sale under ETA s.136.1, but the zero-rating analysis is unchanged. If the engine is leased to a Canadian-registered carrier for purely domestic sector operations, the lease is taxable at 5 percent GST.

  • CBSA customs export reporting: Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) export declaration or eManifest export report for goods valued over CAD 2,000 evidencing departure of the engine from Canada, or the exporter's own low-value shipment records where no formal declaration is required.
  • Air waybill or bill of lading (AWB/BOL) covering the physical international movement of the engine to the foreign lessee's delivery point.
  • Executed dry-lease agreement, engine redelivery certificate, and title or custody records identifying the engine by manufacturer serial number and confirming foreign registry or foreign operational deployment.
  • Non-resident status evidence: tax residence certificate of the foreign lessee, ICAO operator code or Part 129 approval, and a signed declaration that the lessee is not registered for GST/HST under ETA s.240.
  • Inward remittance evidence: Swift MT103 or MT202 wire records, escrow account statements, and foreign exchange conversion documents demonstrating rental consideration in the contract currency was received from outside Canada.
  • GST/HST invoices identifying the supply as zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the ETA, together with any engine logbook or maintenance tracking records supporting the foreign deployment characterization.

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance regime and has no invoice digital stamping, government clearance API, or continuous transaction control mandate comparable to Latin American or EU clearance models. E-invoicing in PDF, XML, or EDI format is accepted if the invoice satisfies the statutory content requirements of ETA s.223, including supplier name and Business Number, date of supply, description of the engine lease, consideration, and either the GST/HST charged or a clear statement that the supply is zero-rated. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million, using NETFILE, EDI, or XML API channels; remittances are processed through CRA My Payment or participating financial institutions, but no transaction-level fiscal authorization or stamp is required.

The ETA does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign exchange repatriation deadline; however, 180 days operates as the CRA's de facto audit benchmark in cross-border leasing. Rental receivables outstanding beyond approximately 180 days are treated as red flags suggesting a disguised loan, profit shifting, or a fictitious export, and the CRA may reverse the zero-rating and assess 5 percent GST on the entire lease stream with compound daily interest at prescribed rates. Penalties include ETA s.280(1) for false statements or omissions, being the greater of CAD 100 and 25 percent of the tax that would have been assessed; ETA s.282 for failure to charge or remit tax; and criminal prosecution under ETA s.327 with fines and imprisonment of up to five years. On the income tax side, ITA s.163(2) imposes a gross-negligence penalty equal to 50 percent of any understated tax if the lease revenue is underreported or diverted. The ordinary reassessment window is four years, but it remains open indefinitely where the taxpayer has been guilty of carelessness, false representation, or fraud. Unsupported zero-rating claims also trigger clawback of input tax credits previously claimed, plus interest and penalties, if the supporting export documentation is not contemporaneous with the supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has a fully liberalized exchange control regime consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. Current-account items (dry-lease rentals, maintenance reserves, management fees, dividends, and interest) and capital-account items (equity injections, intercompany loans, acquisition consideration, and repatriation of invested capital) are freely convertible without Bank of Canada approval or FINTRAC exchange licensing. The Canadian dollar floats freely; USD/CAD transactions are unrestricted, and corporations may hold foreign-currency bank accounts domestically or abroad without conversion mandates. Profit remittance by non-resident shareholders is subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25 percent, generally reduced by treaty, e.g., 5 to 15 percent dividends under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention depending on the holding structure) and represents a tax obligation, not a capital-control restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline, surrender requirement, or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts or lease rental proceeds. USD receipts from aircraft-engine dry-leasing may be retained indefinitely in Canadian or offshore USD accounts, swapped into CAD at commercial rates, or used directly to fund USD-denominated liabilities without any realization or repatriation deadline. For Canadian income tax purposes, an eligible corporation may elect Section 261 of the Income Tax Act functional-currency reporting; foreign-currency gains and losses are accounted for under the enterprise's functional currency with no capital-control dimension.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and settlement authority; prudential banking regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering and terrorist financing supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); wholesale clearing and settlement operated by Payments Canada through the Lynx high-value payment system with final settlement on Bank of Canada books; Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange administration or exchange control licensing body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Medium as an ancillary payment rail; Low as the primary treasury anchor. Canada does not issue EMI licenses comparable to the United Kingdom FCA or EU EMD regimes. Non-bank fintechs operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) and/or as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which is supervised by the Bank of Canada for the retail payments oversight regime. Legitimate foreign EMIs (e.g., UK/EU-licensed electronic money institutions) may support inbound and outbound CAD/USD payments only through Canadian partner banks with an FINTRAC MSB registration or RPAA registration in place. Foreign EMIs cannot access Lynx settlement directly, cannot offer deposits protected under the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) framework, and generally lack escrow custody capabilities. For an Alberta aircraft-engine lessor requiring a USD capital escrow, statutory security deposits, and engine-purchase disbursement controls, a Tier-1 Schedule I bank mandate is the operative structure, with fintech rails reserved for low-value vendor payments and tenant/lessee collection convenience.

Compliant protocol: (1) shareholder equity or intercompany subordinated debt is wired from the offshore investor via SWIFT MT103 directly to the Alberta corporation's USD collection account at a Schedule I Canadian bank; (2) the wire narrative must state 'capital contribution' or 'share subscription' together with a board-resolution or subscription-agreement reference number to avoid mischaracterization as trade proceeds; (3) the Canadian receiving bank will validate the underlying board resolution and ultimate beneficial ownership information under its PCMLTFA program and may request a FINTRAC large cash or virtual currency report only if the transaction is cash-based; (4) where the investor is a regulated non-resident fund, the entity must provide fund registration documents, audited net-asset-value statements, and FATCA/CRS self-certifications; (5) for amounts exceeding counterparty know-your-customer triggers, the treasury team should hold source-of-funds evidence for at least five years; (6) on receipt, funds may be deployed to a capital escrow account for engine purchase or swept into an interest-bearing USD operational account. No Canadian exchange control authorization is needed, but the bank may impose a 24-hour hold for enhanced due diligence reconciliations on first deposits.Settlement architecture: (a) operational disbursements including sub-lease commissions, maintenance reserves, insurance premiums, and professional fees are executed by SWIFT MT103 messages through the Canadian bank's US correspondent network or its US subsidiary (RBC, BMO, TD, CIBC, Scotiabank each maintain US clearing subsidiaries or correspondents), with the CAD leg settling T+0 through Payments Canada's Lynx and the USD leg settling via CHIPS or Fedwire in the United States; (b) aircraft-engine purchase settlements are structured as irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) issued by the Canadian bank for the seller's benefit, ensuring payment against presentation of the bill of sale, title transfer, and airworthiness release; (c) escrow-backed disbursements release capital only upon delivery of the lawyer's or engineer's certification confirming bill of sale, FAA or Transport Canada registration, and supplier invoice; (d) lease-rental collections flow inbound via lessee MT103 into the designated USD collection account; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Medium as an ancillary payment rail; Low as the primary treasury anchor. Canada does not issue EMI licenses comparable to the United Kingdom FCA or EU EMD regimes. Non-bank fintechs operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) and/or as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which is supervised by the Bank of Canada for the retail payments oversight regime. Legitimate foreign EMIs (e.g., UK/EU-licensed electronic money institutions) may support inbound and outbound CAD/USD payments only through Canadian partner banks with an FINTRAC MSB registration or RPAA registration in place. Foreign EMIs cannot access Lynx settlement directly, cannot offer deposits protected under the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) framework, and generally lack escrow custody capabilities. For an Alberta aircraft-engine lessor requiring a USD capital escrow, statutory security deposits, and engine-purchase disbursement controls, a Tier-1 Schedule I bank mandate is the operative structure, with fintech rails reserved for low-value vendor payments and tenant/lessee collection convenience.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA requires Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm’s Length Transactions with Non-Residents) for controlled transactions aggregating above C$50,000 in a taxation year. Full contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation, including OECD-aligned Local File and Master File, is expected when aggregate related-party transactions exceed approximately C$5,000,000. Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above C$750,000,000. Alberta does not maintain a separate transfer-pricing filing regime; the federal rules apply.

Economic substance scope: Neither Alberta nor the federal Crown has enacted Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations. Canada instead applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245), common-law sham and substance-over-form doctrines, and treaty Limitation-on-Benefits provisions. A Canadian engine-leasing company must demonstrate genuine economic substance through actual acquisition decisions, board meetings, lease negotiation and default-management staff, physical or digital asset tracking, and a real profit motive. A mailbox or invoice-conduit entity will be recharacterized, denied treaty benefits, and exposed to Part XIII withholding reassessments.
