Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal tax administrator; Alberta corporate income tax is collected by CRA under a Tax Collection Agreement. Portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MBA), GST NETFILE; export declarations are filed through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Declaration (CAED) portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the Business Corporation with the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain the provincial incorporation number. Step 2: apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts via the one-step BRO service or Form RC156 (non-resident corporations by mail/fax to CRA Sudbury Tax Centre); mandatory program accounts are RC (federal and Alberta corporate income tax, combined T2 return) and RT (GST/HST), with RP added if Canadian payroll is withheld. Step 3: open the CBSA import/export account (RM) linked to the same BN for ocean cargo declarations. No physical presence in Canada is required; online BN issuance is immediate with acknowledgement, paper applications take 4-6 weeks. The GST/HST account effective date is the date the CAD 30,000 threshold was exceeded, or the requested voluntary registration date.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (mandatory C-corp-equivalent; Pass_Through_Eligible: false). A Business Corporation is a separate taxable person under the Income Tax Act (Part I) and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; no US-style check-the-box exists. Territorial_Scope: Canadian-resident corporations pay federal tax on worldwide income, while non-resident-controlled Canadian subsidiaries pay tax only on Canadian-source business income attributable to a permanent establishment plus gains on taxable Canadian property. If the NVOCC subsidiary is a Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC), the federal small business deduction (9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income) and Alberta's 2% small business rate apply; foreign-controlled corporations pay the federal general rate of 15% plus Alberta's 8% general rate (23% combined) with no small business rate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (mandatory C-corp-equivalent; Pass_Through_Eligible: false). A Business Corporation is a separate taxable person under the Income Tax Act (Part I) and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; no US-style check-the-box exists. Territorial_Scope: Canadian-resident corporations pay federal tax on worldwide income, while non-resident-controlled Canadian subsidiaries pay tax only on Canadian-source business income attributable to a permanent establishment plus gains on taxable Canadian property. If the NVOCC subsidiary is a Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC), the federal small business deduction (9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income) and Alberta's 2% small business rate apply; foreign-controlled corporations pay the federal general rate of 15% plus Alberta's 8% general rate (23% combined) with no small business rate. No Tax_Exempt_Zone applies in Alberta.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime is available. Relevant statutory elections: (i) ETA s. 240(1)(c) voluntary GST/HST registration before exceeding the CAD 30,000 threshold, enabling immediate input tax credit recovery on Canadian container drayage, terminal handling, and warehouse costs; (ii) ITA s. 216 election permitting a non-resident to file a net-basis Part I return for Canadian real-property rental income (only for ancillary Canadian warehouse real estate, not freight revenues); (iii) ITA s. 217 election for non-resident employees to be taxed on net Canadian employment income (rare for NVOCC advisory or crew staff); (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s. 18.2/18.4 apply mandatorily with no elective carve-out, disallowing deductions arising from otherwise-hybrid offshore back-to-back freight arrangements; (v) Alberta corporate tax is integrated into the federal T2 via CRA-administered collection, so no separate provincial election is required.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 240 small-supplier threshold); zero-rated export supplies are excluded from the threshold computation, so an NVOCC whose only supplies are zero-rated exports may remain unregistered but should register voluntarily to recover input tax credits on Canadian operating costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V: (i) s. 3 zero-rates a supply of a service of transporting goods to or from Canada, covering the ocean leg and the NVOCC's international freight charges; (ii) s. 7 zero-rates a supply of an export-related service to a non-resident performed in Canada in respect of goods that are exported, covering NVOCC booking, container stuffing, drayage to the mother vessel, and export documentation when performed in the direct export chain and the goods actually leave Canada; (iii) services in the purely domestic chain (warehousing of goods not committed to export, trucking within Canada, import customs brokerage) remain taxable at 5% GST. The NVOCC must hold contemporaneous documentary evidence of export before applying the 0% rate; Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline, but the 180-day benchmark referenced below is the evidentiary window CRA auditors generally accept as proof that export documentation is contemporaneous and reliable.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (formerly Form B13A) filed via CBSA CAED showing destination country and the CAED acknowledgment number
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (master or NVOCC house bill) evidencing Canadian port of loading, foreign port of discharge, container number, and onboard date
  • Customs commercial invoice and packing list matching the B/L cargo description, weight, and container seal number
  • Container gate-in/gate-out report and sailing certificate from the steamship line or marine terminal operator
  • SWIFT MT103 or inward remittance certificate evidencing freight settlement from the non-resident shipper/consignee in CAD or foreign currency
  • Destination port outturn report or consignee delivery receipt proving physical arrival and customs clearance of the container abroad
  • Written exporter attestation confirming that the goods were not diverted back into Canada after export

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, government-side API stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing for GST; B2B e-invoicing (Peppol format) is voluntary. Mandatory electronic procedures: (i) GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1 million must file returns electronically (GST NETFILE or XML API) and remit via EFT; (ii) CBSA requires all exports of goods valued over CAD 2,500 (or controlled goods of any value) to be reported electronically in the CAED/G7 export reporting system, generating the acknowledgment number used in GST zero-rating audits; (iii) carriers and NVOCCs reporting freight manifest data use the CBSA ACI/eManifest protocol.

CRA freight-forwarding industry verification projects target NVOCCs where export evidence is back-dated, the exporter is a shell entity, or freight proceeds are routed offshore; unsupported zero-rating claims are recharacterized as taxable at 5% and reassessed within the normal 4-year limitation period (ETA s. 298) with interest (ETA s. 222). Consequences include: (i) false-statement penalty under ETA s. 280 of not less than CAD 250 and up to 25% of the net tax that would have been payable; (ii) gross-negligence penalty under ITA s. 163.2 up to 50% of any overstated refund or credit; (iii) criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 (tax evasion) with fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment up to 5 years; (iv) director joint-and-several liability for unremitted GST under ETA s. 323; and (v) suspension of the RT program account and future zero-rating authority until compliant export evidence is resubmitted and accepted by CRA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully floating exchange-rate regime without general exchange controls. Current-account and capital-account items are freely convertible for residents and non-residents. Profit remittances, dividends, and interest payments may leave Canada without central bank approval, subject to Part XIII withholding tax and treaty eligibility. Cross-border electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC and require standard anti-money-laundering verification procedures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender or convert export proceeds into Canadian dollars. Exporters may retain foreign currency in external accounts, and no mandated repatriation timeline applies. For Canadian income tax purposes, foreign-currency income and balances must be translated into Canadian dollars using the rate prevailing at the relevant valuation date, and material cross-border currency movements must be tracked under CRA guidance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC); foreign exchange and AML/CFT supervision are co-administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Department of Finance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating for a regulated Canadian money-services business (MSB) is Medium; for unlicensed offshore e-money institutions (EMIs) the rating is Low and legally impermissible. An offshore digital EMI must either obtain a FINTRAC MSB registration or operate as a payment-facilitator under a licensed Schedule I bank partnership to offer cross-border wire and payment services to Canadian corporates. Without such registration, the EMI cannot legally facilitate NVOCC disbursements or collections in or into Canada.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque beneficial ownership or offshore shareholder structures

Root cause: Opaque beneficial ownership or offshore shareholder structures

Defense evidence: Corporate register extract, Alberta Certificate of Incorporation / Continuance, complete share register, signed beneficial-ownership declaration, source-of-funds affidavit for each 10%+ shareholder, and organizational chart with ultimate beneficial owners.
Discrepancy between declared freight-forwarding business and high-volume payment flows to unrelated trade beneficiaries

Root cause: Discrepancy between declared freight-forwarding business and high-volume payment flows to unrelated trade beneficiaries

Defense evidence: Valid NVOCC license or bonding evidence under the Federal Maritime Commission (FMC) or Canadian registration as a freight forwarder, signed contracts with shippers/carriers, commercial invoices, packing lists, bill-of-lading drafts, and a written treasury management policy explaining payment flows.
Sanctions or restricted-country exposure in shipping routes

Root cause: Sanctions or restricted-country exposure in shipping routes

Defense evidence: Automated sanctions-screening procedures and logs, trade-compliance manual, denied-party screening reports, charter-party/freight-forwarding terms incorporating OFAC/CBSA/UN sanctions clauses, and evidence of shipping-route review against restricted-party lists.
Large unrelated-party cash deposits or rapid in-and-out movement (structuring risk)

Root cause: Large unrelated-party cash deposits or rapid in-and-out movement (structuring risk)

Defense evidence: Bank statements showing documented freight collections, customer invoices and remittance advices, aged receivables report, and written 'no-cash policy' for freight collections; AML risk assessment and training certificates.
High-risk jurisdiction counterparties or ocean-carrier payment discrepancies

Root cause: High-risk jurisdiction counterparties or ocean-carrier payment discrepancies

Defense evidence: KYC file on each overseas agent/carrier, evidence of beneficial ownership of the foreign payee, shipping contracts with clear payment terms, vessel/schedule confirmation, and a standard settlement/clearing-process letter.
