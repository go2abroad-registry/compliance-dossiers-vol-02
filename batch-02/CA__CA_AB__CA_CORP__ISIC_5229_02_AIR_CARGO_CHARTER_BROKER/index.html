Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, with no general statutory restriction on converting or transferring funds into or out of Canada. Ordinary commercial payments, capital contributions, intercompany loans, dividends, and other distributions can generally be settled through authorized financial institutions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, tax rules, corporate-law solvency requirements, contractual restrictions, and bank documentation. Dividends require legally available funds and compliance with the Alberta Business Corporations Act or the Canada Business Corporations Act, as applicable. Payments to non-residents may require Canadian withholding-tax review; treaty relief generally requires appropriate residency and beneficial-ownership documentation. Related-party financing and cross-border transactions may also engage transfer-pricing, thin-capitalization, interest-deductibility, and reporting rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no general export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or statutory deadline for converting or repatriating export receipts. Receipts may be retained in Canadian dollars or foreign currency, subject to bank account terms, sanctions, tax and accounting requirements, and any applicable contractual or sector-specific rules. Maintain customer contracts, invoices, transport and customs records, and settlement evidence to substantiate trade-related receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange control authority imposing routine exchange controls; the Bank of Canada conducts monetary policy and oversees designated payment systems. The federal Department of Finance is responsible for financial-sector policy, while FINTRAC administers applicable anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing reporting obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine eligible payments through a Canadian-regulated payment service provider; low as a substitute for a Canadian business deposit account or as the sole treasury bank. Confirm the provider's Canadian regulatory status, permitted activities, safeguarding and insolvency treatment, supported currencies and corridors, transaction limits, and whether it can process the corporation's aviation-related counterparties. A foreign electronic-money institution is not automatically authorized to take deposits or provide regulated payment services in Canada. Maintain a Canadian bank account for operating liquidity, payroll, tax payments, and settlement needs. Cross-border payment services may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act and FINTRAC obligations, depending on the provider and activity.

Defense evidence: Current certificate and articles of incorporation; Alberta or federal corporate registry profile; registers of directors and shareholders; ownership chart through all intermediate entities to natural-person beneficial owners; government-issued identity and address evidence; corporate authorizations; and explanations and supporting records for nominee or trust arrangements, if any.

Defense evidence: Board resolutions and subscription or loan agreements; capital-call or financing documents; source-of-funds and, where requested, source-of-wealth evidence; originating-account statements; wire advice; beneficial-owner details of the remitter; and a reconciliation to the corporation's general ledger.

Route shareholder equity and documented shareholder or third-party loans by SWIFT to an account in the corporation's legal name at a Canadian bank. Use the bank's required payment-purpose and originator/beneficiary fields; Canada does not impose a universal foreign-exchange purpose-code system for ordinary commercial wires. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for transactions with non-residents (services, goods, intangibles) requiring Form T1062 and contemporaneous documentation; CAD 5 million for other transactions. Master File required for transactions exceeding CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty shopping provisions under the OECD BEPS framework. For treaty benefits, the entity must have economic substance (e.g., real business activity, employees, premises) in the jurisdiction of residence. The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) may deny treaty benefits if the main purpose is to obtain treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of tax evaded plus interest; criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239 with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate tax, source deductions, and GST/HST under s.227.1 of the Income Tax Act. Criminal liability may extend to directors who authorize or acquiesce to evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the entity under GAAR, reassess tax, impose penalties of 25% of tax avoided, and deny treaty relief. Criminal charges for tax evasion may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in the sham arrangement.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for money laundering under the Criminal Code up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly fail to implement compliance programs or report suspicious transactions.
