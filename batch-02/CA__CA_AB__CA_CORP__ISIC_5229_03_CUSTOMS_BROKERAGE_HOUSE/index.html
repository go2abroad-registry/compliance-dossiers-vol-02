Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and federal corporate income tax administration via My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered under a collection agreement by the CRA.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident foreign entities must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' either by mail or through CRA's Business Registration Online portal. The entity must simultaneously request a GST/HST program account (program type RT). A licensed customs brokerage operation in Canada also requires a CBSA Customs Broker License (application pursuant to section 9, Customs Act), and must register for the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) system to transmit electronic data. For Alberta corporate income tax purposes, no separate registration is required because the CRA administers the Alberta corporate tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Non-resident foreign entities must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' either by mail or through CRA's Business Registration Online portal. The entity must simultaneously request a GST/HST program account (program type RT). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a 'check-the-box' pass-through election; a business corporation incorporated federally or in Alberta is automatically a separate taxpayer subject to entity-level income tax. Available elections include: joint GST/HST election under ETA section 273 for closely related corporations; provincial permanent establishment allocation formula elections; and Part XIV non-resident corporation elections under ITA section 219. Hybrid mismatch and excessive interest/financing rules under ITA sections 18.2 and 18.3 restrict deduction stripping and cannot be overridden by corporate elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies and financial services) measured in the current calendar quarter and the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; mandatory registration is required once the threshold is exceeded, and non-residents otherwise may be required to register if they are carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Canada may be zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act provided the supplier has documentary evidence that title, possession, or real risk of loss/insurance transferred to a non-resident buyer and that the goods left Canada within the statutory time frame. Services are zero-rated if performed wholly outside Canada, or if supplied to a non-resident in direct connection with tangible personal property exported from Canada, if the service is unrelated to Canadian real property, and the recipient is a non-resident person. Customs clearance brokerage services rendered to an importer of record in respect of goods entering Canada are generally subject to 5% GST; services provided on exported goods to qualifying non-resident clients may be zero-rated where all export documentation is retained by the broker.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13) or CBSA CARM electronic export report
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Commercial Invoice describing goods, value, and consignee
  • Bank wire transfer instruction or foreign exchange repatriation receipt (SWIFT MT103/PDF bank confirmation) evidencing inward remittance from the foreign purchaser

Canada has no universal real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate for GST/HST. CRA requires all GST/HST registrants to maintain electronic records and files on a timely basis; GST/HST returns must be filed and paid electronically through CRA-approved channels when reporting thresholds are exceeded. For customs brokerage, CBSA mandates electronic data transmission through CARM for import/export declarations; authorized brokers use web services APIs or prescribed EDI formats. E-invoicing in B2B/B2G commerce may follow PEPPOL standards, but this does not constitute a GST clearance endorsement.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess GST/HST at 5% (plus provincial HST where applicable) with arrears interest, where export documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or unavailable. Penalties range from 10% for repeated failure in reporting, 25% under ETA section 285(1) for knowingly making false statements, to 50% for gross negligence penalties under ETA section 285(1)(b) parallel to ITA section 163(2). Fraudulent export zero-rating claims can trigger criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act with fines of up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment plus full clawback of any claimed refunds, and liability may extend to directors and officers for knowing participation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada sustains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under a free-floating exchange-rate regime, with no exchange-control authorization, conversion mandate, or repatriation licensing for trade or investment flows. Profit remittances by a customs brokerage corporation are unrestricted, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties (statutory 25%, reduced under applicable income tax treaties to 5%–15%), plus FINTRAC reporting of large cross-border fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or equivalent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canadian law imposes no export-proceeds surrender ceiling, statutory conversion ratio, or mandatory repatriation timeline for customs brokerage service fees or export-related foreign-currency receivables. The brokerage may freely hold, convert, or disburse foreign currency; only Income Tax Act records-and-attribution rules and FINTRAC transaction-reporting thresholds apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) functions as the central monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally chartered banks; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers cross-border currency and wire reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Canada has no designated foreign-exchange control administration or approval body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for offshore digital EMI or challenger-bank accounts. A foreign EMI carrying on business in Canada as a money services business or foreign money services business must register with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and must maintain AML/KYC, record-keeping, and suspicious-transaction reporting programs. An unlicensed offshore EMI lacks a Canadian Schedule I bank charter, cannot access CBSA duty-remittance clearing rails, and its use as a primary settlement account would raise enhanced due diligence flags at domestic banks. Such rails should be used only as secondary, low-velocity liquidity adjuncts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Canada imposes no FX conversion approval, surrender, or capital-import licensing gate; funds may be credited to a CAD or foreign-currency account in Canada freely. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate rate (Canada federal Part I 15% + Alberta provincial general rate 8%; qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation active business income is subject to an 11% combined rate: 9% federal + 2% provincial on the first CAD 500,000)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder under subsection 212(2); tax treaty reductions apply: generally 15% for portfolio shareholdings and 5% for shareholdings of 10% or more of voting stock, with certain exemptions under specific DTAs.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Alberta corporate tax is reported on the same T2 return; tax balances are due two months after the fiscal year-end for CCPCs and three months for other corporations, subject to instalment rules for larger taxpayers.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions with any single non-resident for Local File purposes; Master File required where total Canadian group consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 50,000,000. Contemporaneous documentation must be completed before the T2 filing due date and available to the CRA within three months of a request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code; however, Section 245 of the Income Tax Act (General Anti-Avoidance Rule), treaty abuse clauses including the Principal Purpose Test, and the requirement of a genuine Canadian permanent establishment impose a quasi-economic-substance standard. For a customs broker, valid CBSA licensing under Section 9 of the Customs Act and ongoing operational presence (licensed broker on staff, physical office, substantive transaction records) are minimum predicates to tax recognition; a mailbox-only shell corporation will be disregarded, and transactions it enters may be reassessed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 2 years on indictment; s.240 for false statements; Customs Act false declaration offences carry additional penalties up to CAD 25,000 and potential licence revocation; CRA civil penalties under s.163.2 and s.152(6) apply for gross negligence.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally participate in or authorize the fraud are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unremitted withholdings and may face direct civil claims; courts can order restitution, impose travel restrictions, and incarceration; the CRA can assess directors personally when the corporation is a sham or the director knowingly furthered the deception.
Part XIII Withholding Failure and Profit Repatriation Abuse

Statutory sanction: Section 215(1) liability: the payer is liable for the full amount of tax not withheld, plus arrears interest and penalties; s.227(9) imposes gross negligence penalties up to CAD 100,000; transfer pricing penalties under s.247(3) equal 10% of the net adjustment. Continued non-compliance triggers CRA lien registrations and prosecutions.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII amounts under s.227.1 without proof of intent if they knew or should have known; CRA files certificates and may enforce against personal assets; imprisonment remains possible where the withholding omission is part of a tax evasion scheme.
Sham Corporation and Economic Substance Absence

Statutory sanction: Under s.245 GAAR, the arrangement is voided for tax purposes and the taxpayer is reassessed on substance; treaty benefits are denied under the Principal Purpose Test; the Alberta Registrar may dissolve the corporation; creditors and CRA may pierce the corporate veil.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham company may be held personally liable for corporate tax debts, tort claims, and unpaid wages under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta); courts may issue asset freezing orders, travel bans, and contempt incarceration where directors obstruct discovery; criminal tax charges may follow lifestyle and revenue audits.
AML and Customs Broker Licensing Violations

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 110,000 for individuals and CAD 500,000 for corporations, with additional penalties for repeat violations; illegal brokerage operation penalties under the Customs Act; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code s.462.31 punishable by up to 10 years in prison; seizure of corporate assets and licence revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be convicted where they knowingly direct, authorize or acquiesce in money laundering; they face imprisonment up to 10 years, personal asset confiscation, and travel restrictions; courts may order civil restitution to victims or the Crown, and regulators may ban senior management from holding licences or directorships.
