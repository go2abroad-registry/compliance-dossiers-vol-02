Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required. Shares may be issued without par value; stated capital is determined by the first board resolution. No escrow account rule applies for ordinary share subscriptions. If directors exercise the authorizing power to issue shares for a non-cash consideration under s. 24(3) ABCA, the value of the consideration must be fixed by a board resolution to establish paid-up capital.

Funding & unlock path: Workplace safety clearance under the Alberta Occupational Health and Safety Code (AR 87/2009), including first-aid and hazard communication for field coordinators. Leased office and warehousing premises require fire safety certification under the Alberta Fire Code issued through the Safety Codes Act permitting process, plus a structural load inspection for any proposed laydown yard floor loading. No hazardous static installation is contemplated.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via the 'My Business Account' portal; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the Alberta Department of Finance under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation with Canadian permanent establishment or carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or paper Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'. A separate GST/HST account is opened under the same BN. Non-resident applicants must provide a designated agent for service, evidence of Canadian business presence, and applicable authorizations; CRA normally issues the BN within 15 business days after complete filing.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a federal or provincial business corporation is a separate Canadian taxpayer filing Part I corporate income tax on worldwide income; no general pass-through eligibility exists). — A foreign corporation with Canadian permanent establishment or carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or paper Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a federal or provincial business corporation is a separate Canadian taxpayer filing Part I corporate income tax on worldwide income; no general pass-through eligibility exists).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box system exists. Available statutory elections include the Excise Tax Act (ETA) section 156 closely related corporation election, ETA section 273 joint venture election, Income Tax Act section 85 corporate rollover election, and, for eligible non-residents, Income Tax Act section 216 net rental election. Hybrid mismatch rules operate under ITA Part VI.1 and section 18.4; limited elections may be available under those rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold is measured on world-wide taxable supplies made in Canada (including zero-rated supplies) over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Exceeding CAD 30,000 mandatorily triggers GST/HST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to tangible personal property exported from Canada under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, where the supplier obtains documentary evidence confirming direct export of the goods by the supplier or at the supplier's direction. For out-of-gauge heavy project cargo, zero-rating also applies to freight transportation services for exported goods where the goods leave Canada to a foreign destination. The supplier must demonstrate a causal link between the invoiced supply, the export shipment, and the eventual foreign destination.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or electronic equivalent filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System
  • Original or carrier-issued Bill of Lading / through bill of lading covering out-of-gauge heavy project cargo
  • Commercial invoice incorporating the GST/HST registration number, sub-item descriptions, and export zero-rating clause
  • Packing list and out-of-gauge weight/measurement certificate
  • Proof of export exit/release (border clearance stamp or carrier terminal departure confirmation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of the milestone wire transfer / letter of credit settlement

No federal real-time e-invoicing clearance, digital stamping, or central API fiscal clearance is currently mandated. CRA requires complete, legible invoices and supporting records for at least six years; B2B electronic invoices are acceptable if systematically retained and convertibly readable. CRA enforces GST/HST compliance through data matching, pre-audit risk scoring, and desk examinations rather than real-time fiscal approval.

CRA may reassess GST/HST within four years for ordinary filings, with no limitation period where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect has occurred. Knowingly participating in false export statements attracts civil penalties under ETA section 280 (including percentages of tax amounts plus assessment interest) and potentially criminal prosecution. Gross negligence penalties may also apply under Income Tax Act section 163(2). If export zero-rating is claimed without satisfactory export evidence, CRA denies the zero-rate, recovers the net tax unpaid, charges arrears interest, and may claw back related input tax credits. Directors and responsible officers may face personal liability in egregious cases.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and maintains fully liberalized current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange-control approvals for payment of trade-related invoices, freight settlements, dividend remittances, or equity repatriation. Profit/equity remittances are subject to Canadian Part XIII or Part I tax and applicable treaty withholding rates (typically 5–25%), and cross-border EFT transactions over CAD 10,000 are reported to FINTRAC by the financial institution. No Bank of Canada license, exchange quota, or prior government authorization is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None — Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or deposit timeline on export proceeds. Exporters and logistics providers may retain USD and other currencies indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts. For income tax purposes, export revenue must be computed in CAD using the Bank of Canada daily rate or an acceptable average annual rate; realized FX gains/losses are recognized in accordance with CRA rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign exchange control administration; the Bank of Canada is the monetary and financial-stability authority, with FINTRAC and OSFI providing AML/CFT and prudential regulatory controls over cross-border transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-to-Low for primary treasury use; feasible as a supplemental payment channel. An Alberta corporation may lawfully instruct offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) for small administrative disbursements and global payroll, provided such EMIs are registered or licensed where required in Canada. However, under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), unregistered EMI operations into Canada create statutory compliance risk, and major Schedule I banks generally restrict high-value project cargo settlements from moving through non-bank EMI rails. Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 100 / Gross Revenue Enter all gross business revenue from out-of-gauge heavy industrial project cargo logistics, freight forwarding, haulage, and ancillary handling services. Non-resident corporation carrying on business in Alberta must report only Canadian-source business income attributable to its Alberta permanent establishment in accordance with federal and provincial allocation rules. Late filing penalty equal to 5% of unpaid balance plus 1% per complete month up to a maximum of 12 months; repeated failure is penalized at 10% plus 2% per complete month up to 20 months; daily compound interest on arrears accrues at the CRA prescribed rate plus applicable gross negligence penalties of 50% for false statements or omissions.
Box 150 / Operating Expenses Report deductible operating expenses including subcontractor costs, fuel, equipment leases, insurance, demurrage, driver wages, repairs, maintenance, and Alberta health premiums if applicable. Claim only expenses reasonably allocable to the Canadian permanent establishment; intra-group charges must satisfy the arm's length standard under section 247 of the Income Tax Act. Late filing penalty equal to 5% of unpaid balance plus 1% per complete month up to a maximum of 12 months; repeated failure is penalized at 10% plus 2% per complete month up to 20 months; daily compound interest on arrears accrues at the CRA prescribed rate plus applicable gross negligence penalties of 50% for false statements or omissions.
Box 910 / Taxable Income State total taxable income after adjustments, amalgamations, loss carryovers, and any permanent establishment allocation. Non-resident corporations must complete the Alberta corporate income tax allocation schedule to ensure income is not apportioned to another jurisdiction without a treaty-respected taxable presence. Late filing penalty equal to 5% of unpaid balance plus 1% per complete month up to a maximum of 12 months; repeated failure is penalized at 10% plus 2% per complete month up to 20 months; daily compound interest on arrears accrues at the CRA prescribed rate plus applicable gross negligence penalties of 50% for false statements or omissions.
