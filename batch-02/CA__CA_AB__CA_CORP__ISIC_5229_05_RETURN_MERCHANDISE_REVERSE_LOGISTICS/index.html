Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for an Alberta private corporation. Shares may be issued for such consideration as fixed by the directors, and no escrow or share capital deposit is required at incorporation. The articles must set out the authorized share structure.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification per the Alberta Fire Code; structural load inspections for racking and work platforms under the Alberta Building Code; workplace health and safety clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act; and Workers' Compensation Board (WCB-Alberta) registration is mandatory once employees are engaged.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) -- the sole federal tax administrator for the Income Tax Act and Part IX of the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST). CRA collects Alberta corporate income tax on behalf of the Province of Alberta under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement, so a single registration portal applies: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-portal.html). The provincial counterpart for Alberta-specific tax policy is the Alberta Ministry of Treasury Board and Finance, Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA), which does not operate a separate registration or filing portal for corporate taxpayers.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned entity organized under the CBCA or ABCA must: (1) obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or Form RC1; (2) activate the corporate income tax program account (RT suffixed, e.g., BN RT0001) and the GST/HST program account (RC suffixed); (3) file the RC1 together with a certified copy of the certificate of incorporation and, for a non-resident shareholder, a completed CRA non-resident declaration; (4) provide a Canadian registered office address for service; and (5) receive CRA confirmation of registration and the GST/HST effective date. CRA issues a BN within 5-10 business days of a complete application; GST/HST registration becomes mandatory on the first day a registrant exceeds the CAD $30,000 small-supplier threshold, with CRA able to backdate registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A CBCA/ABCA business corporation is resident in Canada (ITA s. 250(4)(a)) and is a separate taxpayer subject to Part I federal and Alberta provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income, with no pass-through or check-the-box election available. Applicable federal rates: 15% general, 9% small-business deduction rate where CCPC status is met; Alberta rates: 8% general, 2% Alberta small-business rate. Dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to 25% Part XIII withholding tax, reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US treaty. The entity is also subject to a 38 1/3% federal Part I.3 capital tax? No -- the large corporations tax was repealed; no further federal capital tax.

Available election forms

Available statutory elections include: (a) GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s. 156); (b) closely-related corporation election for nil-consideration intercompany supplies (ETA s. 273); (c) non-resident net-basis elections for rental and service income (ITA ss. 216 and 216.1); (d) tax-deferred corporate rollover elections (ITA s. 85) and share-for-share exchanges (ITA s. 85.1); (e) functional-currency election (ITA s. 261) permitting Canadian tax reporting in a qualifying foreign currency; (f) non-calendar fiscal period election (ITA s. 249.1(4), with CRA concurrence); and (g) branch tax / deemed dividend elections (ITA Part XIV) where a non-resident parent operates a Canadian branch in parallel. Canada permits no US-style hybrid 'check-the-box' entity classification for corporations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000. A person becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant when its worldwide taxable supplies (including associated related persons and permanent establishments) exceed CAD $30,000 either in a single calendar quarter or over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters. For a Canadian-resident corporation, the threshold is global: once crossed, GST/HST registration becomes compulsory even if Alberta-specific Canadian-source supplies are below the threshold. Voluntary registration is available and is commonly used by exporters to recover input tax credits attributable to zero-rated export activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating may be claimed under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 of the Excise Tax Act where the supplier provides a service to a non-resident person in respect of tangible personal property that is or is to be exported from Canada, which covers receiving, inspecting, quality grading, reconditioning, repackaging and reconsigning returned goods, provided the property is physically exported and the service is supplied to a non-resident legal entity (not an individual consumer). International freight-arrangement services tied to direct export are separately zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part VII (s. 5) as 'transfer of goods' transportation services. The exporter must document that no taxable Canadian use of the goods occurred (e.g., no sale into Canadian commerce) and that the goods left Canada within the normal course of the reverse-logistics cycle.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (B13A / CERS electronic export report) filed through the CBSA Export Reporting System, including the Unique Shipment Reference Number and border exit confirmation
  • Negotiable Bill of Lading (ocean or truck) or House Air Waybill (HAWB) naming the foreign consignee and foreign port of destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List identifying the returned goods, condition code, and value applicable to the zero-rated export sale
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice (wire transfer / ACH statement from Airwallex or local bank) evidencing foreign-currency payment to the Canadian entity within the invoiced term
  • Warehouse receiving / inspection logs, GPS tracking reports and final delivery confirmation from the foreign carrier proving physical receipt abroad

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based pre-clearance of B2B invoices (contrast Latin American CFDI/NF-e regimes). Mandatory electronic obligations are limited to: (a) e-filing GST/HST returns via CRA NETFILE/GST/HST TELEFILE where revenue exceeds CAD $1.5 million; (b) retaining invoices (paper or digital) containing the statutory prescribed information under s. 223 of the ETA (vendor BN, date, consideration, GST/HST charged, buyer identity); and (c) electronic export reporting through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS). The Peppol-compliant Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing network is available on a voluntary B2B basis and is increasingly requested by large retail and logistics counterparties for returned-merchandise reconciliation.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated refund claim within 4 years where export documentation is incomplete, and indefinitely where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default. Penalties include: (a) a s. 280 ETA false-claim penalty equal to the greater of CAD $250 and 25% of the net tax; (b) interest under s. 296 on clawed-back input tax credits; (c) a 50% penalty under ITA s. 163(2) for false statements on income tax; and (d) criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 or ITA s. 239 (up to 5 years' imprisonment). Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation clock; the 180-day metric is the commercial banking and audit-prioritization benchmark CRA uses to scrutinize prolonged offshore retention, but non-repatriation itself does not automatically trigger clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada imposes no foreign-exchange controls. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance requires no central-bank or foreign-exchange approval; the only hurdles are standard tax withholds (e.g., Part XIII withholding on dividends at 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty) and FINTRAC monitoring thresholds for cross-border funds transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirement for export receipts in Canada. Exporters may retain proceeds in CAD or foreign currency in any Canadian-deposit-taking institution. However, international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or above must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; receipts must also be recorded consistently for Canadian corporate income tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) / Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Digital cross-border EMI platforms such as Airwallex are legally viable as supplementary payment rails for operational FX and working-capital flows, provided they are registered under Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada and obtain all required FINTRAC reporting compliance. EMIs are not permitted to replace a Schedule I chartered bank for Canadian-dollar clearing or federal deposit-taking activities; they cannot offer CDIC-insured Canadian-dollar accounts and may be subject to additional non-resident correspondent-bank due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol requires an inbound SWIFT MT103 from a foreign corporate or individual investor to a Canadian Schedule I bank account held in the name of the Alberta Business Corporation. The wire narrative must explicitly indicate 'Share Subscription – Capital Contribution' or 'Equity Injection' and reference the corresponding board resolution and share subscription agreement. No central-bank approval is required; however, for amounts equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, the receiving bank will record the international electronic fund transfer for FINTRAC. The corporate KYC pack must include the incorporation certificate, Alberta corporate dash, current certificate of directors, share register, and the beneficial-ownership declaration before funds crediting.Outbound trade payments for reverse-logistics settlements are executed primarily via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers from Canadian corporate bank accounts to the beneficiary's overseas bank, or via MT202 for interbank cover payment if required. For larger strategic purchases of returned merchandise, a documentary commercial letter of credit (UCP600) is the preferred clearing rail, allowing title transfer against compliant transport and inspection documents. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general rate (Federal 15% + Alberta 8%); 11% combined small business rate (Federal 9% + Alberta 2%) on the first CA$500,000 of active business income for a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return must be filed within 6 months after fiscal year-end. Balance-due payable within 2 months after year-end generally; 3 months for a CCPC eligible for the Small Business Deduction. Instalments due when total net tax exceeds CA$3,000 in a year.
