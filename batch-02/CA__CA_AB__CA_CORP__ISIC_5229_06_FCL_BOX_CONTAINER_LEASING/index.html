Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to federal Part I corporate income tax and Alberta provincial corporate income tax. It is not fiscally transparent for income tax purposes. A resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation is taxed on Canadian-source income only. For GST/HST, the entity becomes a registrant once the applicable threshold is met or upon voluntary election.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available in Canada. Applicable elections include: (i) GST/HST related-party joint return election under Excise Tax Act s.156; (ii) associated-corporations election for Canadian-controlled private corporation status under Income Tax Act s.256(2); (iii) hybrid mismatch arrangement rules under ss.18.3-18.4 and ss.12.5-12.6 of the Income Tax Act, which contain elective ordering adjustments; (iv) voluntary GST/HST registration election for a non-resident exporter. For an ISO container leasing corporation, no entity classification pass-through election is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold based on total worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies (including associated persons) for a calendar year. Non-residents carrying on business in Canada and exceeding this threshold must register for GST/HST. Voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. Alberta does not set a separate provincial threshold because it does not impose a provincial sales tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, a supply of tangible personal property (e.g., an ISO shipping container) is zero-rated when: (a) the lessor exports the container as part of the supply for direct shipment outside Canada; (b) the lessee is non-resident and the container is not used in Canada; or (c) the lease qualifies as an export under the applicable place-of-supply rules for goods. Offshore services related to the leasing arrangement may be zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V if the service is supplied to a non-resident not registered for GST/HST and the service is primarily performed outside Canada. The lessor must possess credible export evidence evidencing that the goods left Canada within a reasonable period (CRA administrative practice generally looks for documented export within 60 days) and must file a GST/HST return claiming the zero-rated status and any applicable input tax credits.

  • Canada Customs B13A Export Declaration (where required, typically for goods valued over CAD 2,000 or controlled goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill showing final destination outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice or lease agreement indicating zero-rated status
  • Carrier or freight-forwarder export proof (e.g., signed waybill, container gate receipt, terminal exit stamp)
  • Documentary evidence of non-resident lessee status (e.g., foreign address, jurisdiction of incorporation or residence)
  • Inward remittance record from a Canadian bank confirming payment received in a foreign currency

The CRA may reassess a claimed zero-rated export as taxable if the exporter lacks sufficient export evidence, causing uncollected GST/HST to become payable with interest from the original filing deadline. Civil penalties under Excise Tax Act s.280(1)(c) for knowingly making a false or deceptive statement — or gross negligence — amount to 50% of the tax sought to be avoided. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with full current account convertibility for trade-related payments, lease receipts, and profit remittances. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment and portfolio flows, are subject to oversight under the Investment Canada Act and Bank of Canada reporting thresholds. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders require beneficial ownership verification, economic substance compliance, and adherence to tax treaty provisions; hurdles include CRA reporting obligations and potential delays for enhanced due diligence on opaque structures. All significant FX transactions must be reported to the Bank of Canada for statistical purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies in Canada. Exporters must report all export receipts to the Bank of Canada within 30 days of receipt for statistical compilation. Banks process foreign exchange conversions without statutory conversion ratios or surrender timelines, provided full AML/KYC documentation, remittance advice, and supporting trade documents are submitted. Compliance with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act is mandatory.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

A gross negligence or false statement penalty of 50% of the understated tax may be assessed under ITA s.163(2). Interest at the prescribed rate is applied to unpaid balances and unpaid installments.
