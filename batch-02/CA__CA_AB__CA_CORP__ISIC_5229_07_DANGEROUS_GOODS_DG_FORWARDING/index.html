Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Federal GST/HST and Income Tax administration; business portal is 'My Business Account' (canada.ca), with Business Registration Online (BRO) for BN acquisition. Alberta corporate income tax is legislated under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act and administered by the CRA under the federal-provincial single tax administration agreement; Alberta has no provincial sales tax. Customs and cargo fiscal clearance is handled by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via eManifest/ACI and the Canadian Export Reporting System.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign or Alberta extra-provincial business corporation: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act, or register the foreign corporation as an extra-provincial company with Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number; (2) apply to the CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account with the 'RT' suffix using Form RC1 or BRO; (3) for non-resident applicants, provide a resident agent/Canadian representative, proof of a Canadian place of business (terminal, office, or registered agent address), a Canadian bank account, foreign incorporation certificates, and certify an intention to carry on business in Canada; (4) add program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RP (corporate income tax), RC (payroll); (5) if the entity is below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold but needs to recover GST on Canadian overheads, file the voluntary registration election under ETA s. 240(2). The BN-RT must be disclosed on all GST invoices (ETA s. 223).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation is a taxable corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income at federal 15% general effective rate plus Alberta 8% (2% small-business rate for a Canadian-controlled private corporation on up to CAD 500,000 of active business income). Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada are taxed on Canadian-source income only, plus a 25% branch tax under ITA s. 219, treaty-reduced (e.g., 15% for U.S. corporations). No tax-exempt zone applies in Alberta. — For a foreign or Alberta extra-provincial business corporation: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act, or register the foreign corporation as an extra-provincial company with Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number; (2) apply to the CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account with the 'RT' suffix using Form RC1 or BRO; (3) for non-resident applicants, provide a resident agent/Canadian representative, proof of a Canadian place of business (terminal, office, or registered agent address), a Canadian bank account, foreign incorporation certificates, and certify an intention to carry on business in Canada; (4) add program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RP (corporate income tax), RC (payroll); (5) if the entity is below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold but needs to recover GST on Canadian overheads, file the voluntary registration election under ETA s. 240(2). The BN-RT must be disclosed on all GST invoices (ETA s. 223).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation is a taxable corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income at federal 15% general effective rate plus Alberta 8% (2% small-business rate for a Canadian-controlled private corporation on up to CAD 500,000 of active business income). Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada are taxed on Canadian-source income only, plus a 25% branch tax under ITA s. 219, treaty-reduced (e.g., 15% for U.S. corporations). No tax-exempt zone applies in Alberta.

Available election forms

Canada uses no U.S.-style check-the-box regime; entity classification is statutory. Relevant elections: (a) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240(2) for small suppliers; (b) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations to treat intercompany freight, warehouse and back-office supplies as non-taxable (critical for DG forwarding groups); (c) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident property income; (d) ITA s. 219 branch tax election for foreign corporations operating a Canadian branch/permanent establishment; (e) hybrid-mismatch elections and dual-inclusion adjustments under ITA ss. 18.2-18.4 to deny timing mismatches on payments from foreign-related DG entities; (f) inability to check-the-box on a Canadian incorporated subsidiary, which remains a taxable Canadian corporation even if the foreign parent claims management control.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (ETA small-supplier threshold). A supplier carrying on business in Canada becomes a registrant once total consideration for taxable supplies made in Canada (including zero-rated export supplies) exceeds CAD 30,000 in either the current quarter or any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters. There is no separate Alberta registration threshold. Non-resident DG forwarders at any revenue level may register voluntarily to recover input tax credits on Canadian terminal, storage, and trucking costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (a) exported hazardous goods - a supply of tangible property by sale exported, or delivered to a common carrier for export before the end of the second calendar month following the month of supply (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, item 202(a)/(b)); (b) the export transportation service itself - carriage of goods from a Canadian point to a point outside Canada in a continuous unbroken journey, zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 6, including associated DG handling, loading, customs brokerage, and insurance supplied in conjunction with that export leg (s. 6.1); and (c) services rendered to non-resident recipients that do not relate to Canadian real property or to goods situated in Canada at the time of performance (s. 7). A freight forwarder acting as principal or disclosed agent must evidence the actual export movement; a purely domestic Alberta leg (e.g., Calgary-to-Edmonton linehaul) remains taxable at 5%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A via the Canadian Export Reporting System and carrier eManifest outward transaction)
  • Bill of Lading (multimodal sea/air/road negotiable or non-negotiable bill showing foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank/SWIFT MT103 or letter-of-credit advice matched to the sale)
  • Commercial Invoice with BN disclosure and Freight/DG margin schedules
  • Dangerous Goods Declaration under IATA DGR, IMDG Code, or Transport Canada TDG Regulations
  • Continuous Journey Evidence (master/house waybill, rail or truck eManifest transit records, terminal outturn)
  • Export-Authorization Retention Record (CRA documentation retention under ETA s. 286 and ITA s. 230, minimum 6 years)

Canada has no continuous transaction control regime (no digital stamping or mandatory government API clearance comparable to Brazil or the EU). GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (NETFILE) and remitted by EFT by registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1 million; registration and remittance may be required monthly for high-volume exporters. Bills of lading and commercial invoices must carry the supplier's BN and GST charged (ETA s. 223). For export clearance, the CBSA operates eManifest/ACI pre-arrival data interchange (EDIFACT and API) for air, marine, rail and highway modes, which functions as the de facto fiscal-customs clearance chain for outbound DG shipments; Transport Canada TDG data (e.g., dangerous goods declaration) is transmitted in parallel.

CRA audit scrutiny requires matching the export declaration, B/L, and inward remittance within 180 days of the B/L date; open-dollar receivables beyond 180 days trigger transfer-pricing review of intercompany export sales and an imputed interest adjustment under ITA ss. 80.4/12(1)(g). Export zero-rating denial on audit produces: (i) assessment of 5% GST on the full consideration; (ii) retroactive clawback of previously recovered input tax credits and non-resident s. 71 export rebates; (iii) gross-negligence penalty of up to 50% of net tax under ETA s. 285.1; (iv) s. 327 evasion prosecution with fines of 50%-200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; (v) a 4-year reassessment window, extended to 7 years for misrepresentation and unlimited for fraud; and (vi) separate CBSA licensing/export-privilege suspension and administrative penalties, alongside Transport Canada TDG penalties, which do not shield the ETA clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized foreign-exchange regime with no general exchange-control restrictions. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951. Profit repatriation via dividends, interest, royalties and management fees is permitted without BoC approval; however Part XIII withholding tax (Income Tax Act) applies at 25% domestic, treaty-reduced as applicable, and all cross-border intercompany payments must satisfy arm's length standards under s. 247 ITA. Foreign-currency invoicing (USD/EUR) is lawful, but the statutory books must be kept in CAD and translated at the Bank of Canada daily rate. No prior notifications to the BoC are required for remittances or hedging contracts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirement for export proceeds in Canada; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951. Exporters of regulated hazardous-goods freight services must file B13 export declarations with CBSA for shipments exceeding C$2,000 and report balance-of-payments data under the Statistics Act. The entity may retain export commissions in foreign currency, convert freely, or hold offshore accounts; the recommended treasury policy is conversion within five business days of receipt to minimize FX exposure. For income tax purposes, export revenue and related accounts receivable must be re-measured in CAD at the transaction-date exchange rate, with unrealized gains/losses recognized at year-end.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) is the monetary and financial stability authority; the Department of Finance Canada administers currency policy and the Currency Act; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) reporting; the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers export controls and trade data collection.

Feasibility: Medium-Low.
