Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act (Alberta), RSA 2000, c B-9 ('ABCA'), as amended; see in particular ss. 6 and 10 (articles and incorporation), s. 14 (corporate name restrictions), ss. 24-25 (share consideration), ss. 101-102 (registered office and notice), s. 106 (first directors), and s. 124 (directors' resolutions). Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (Alberta Form 01), together with Form 02 (Notice of Registered Office and First Directors) and a current NUANS name search report; a Certificate of Incorporation is issued upon approval.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the ABCA; the corporation may be formed with nominal capital. Shares must be issued for consideration in money, property, or past services fully performed before issuance (ABCA ss. 24-25). No escrow account is mandated for a standard business corporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Safety Codes Act (Alberta Fire Code) through the municipal fire department, including a fire safety plan; structural load inspection and building occupancy permit from the municipal development authority; occupational clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Alberta) and OHS Code, including hazard assessment, WHMIS/GHS communication, WCB-Alberta employer coverage, and, where applicable, Certificate of Recognition (COR) status; for GDP pharma freight, additional cold-chain temperature mapping records and racking/lifting equipment inspection certificates are required.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the ABCA; the corporation may be formed with nominal capital. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) as the national tax administrator for federal Part I corporate income tax, GST/HST, and payroll; self-service portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-registration-online.html and CRA 'My Business Account'. Alberta corporate income tax under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act (RSA 2000, c. A-15) is administered by CRA on Alberta's behalf through the integrated T2 filing; the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (ATRA) administers non-income levies (fuel tax, tourism levy). Export customs control for goods movement is administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign parent establishing an Alberta presence: (1) Incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act or federally with extra-provincial registration in Alberta in the name CA_CORP; (2) obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) and CRA program accounts (RC = corporate income tax, RT = GST/HST, RP = payroll) via BRO for locally incorporated entities, or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with CRA for a non-resident corporation without a Canadian incorporation; (3) where the non-resident 'carries on business in Canada' and makes taxable supplies in Canada exceeding CAD 30,000, register for GST/HST (RT program account) before the supply is made; (4) for a non-resident without a Canadian establishment, designate a GST/HST representative under ETA s.277 and provide proof of Alberta extra-provincial registration, certificate of corporate existence, and a Canadian address for service; (5) after registration, file T2 corporate income tax returns within 6 months of fiscal year-end and GST/HST returns per remittance cycle; (6) obtain the CBSA customs broker / freight forwarder licence for physical pharma cargo handling.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a 'Business Corporation' (CA_CORP) is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; there is no pass-through at the federal/provincial corporate level. A Canadian-resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident parent with an Alberta permanent establishment is taxed under Part I only on Canadian-source business income attributable to that PE (territorial nexus for the foreign entity) and remains subject to Part XIV branch tax of 25% (treaty-reduced) on after-tax earnings remitted to head office. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a 'Business Corporation' (CA_CORP) is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; there is no pass-through at the federal/provincial corporate level. A Canadian-resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident parent with an Alberta permanent establishment is taxed under Part I only on Canadian-source business income attributable to that PE (territorial nexus for the foreign entity) and remains subject to Part XIV branch tax of 25% (treaty-reduced) on after-tax earnings remitted to head office. Alberta applies its own 8% general corporate rate on taxable income allocated to the Alberta PE; no provincial tax on capital in Alberta.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a 'check-the-box' entity classification regime; available statutory elections include: (i) ITA s.216(1) election for a non-resident receiving rents/royalties; (ii) ITA s.219(4) election to increase Part XIV branch-tax taxable capital; (iii) ITA s.261 functional-currency election where the Canadian business records are kept in USD; (iv) ETA s.277 designation of a non-resident GST/HST representative; (v) voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier below the CAD 30,000 threshold to claim input tax credits on Canadian costs; (vi) annual GST/HST filing/remittance election where net tax is CAD 5,000 or less; (vii) for hybrid/mismatch arrangements, mandatory self-adjustments apply under ITA s.18.4 with no elective relief; Alberta piggybacks the federal T2 via the Tax Collection Agreement, so no separate provincial election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA s.148(1); a non-resident carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST once total taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s.240(1)); voluntary registration below the threshold is permitted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered GST/HST filer may claim the 0% export rate for: (i) a supply of a freight transportation service in respect of goods exported from Canada and services of arranging for such freight transportation, zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part I, ss.7 and 7.1; (ii) services rendered directly in respect of exported tangible personal property under s.5; (iii) ancillary GDP clinical-logistics services (cold-chain monitoring, customs clearance, warehousing in transit) supplied to a non-resident principal as part of the exported-goods service. The supplier must be GST/HST-registered, the service must be performed in Canada for the export of physical cargo, and contemporaneous evidence of the border crossing, the non-resident customer, and foreign consideration must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA B13A form or Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) electronic declaration for the underlying pharmaceutical cargo
  • Bill of Lading — master/house ocean bill or IATA Air Waybill naming the Canadian entity as consignor/agent and the overseas consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank credit advice or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of foreign-currency consideration from the non-resident principal
  • Executed freight-forwarding / GDP clinical logistics service contract and commercial invoice identifying the non-resident customer and CAD consideration
  • Proof of Delivery (POD) and temperature-monitoring GDP data-logger report (2–8°C excursion record) for the pharmaceutical consignment
  • CBSA customs broker/freight forwarder licence and any applicable Cargo/TIR carnets or bonded-carrier authorizations

No real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or API-authorization mandate exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing the supplier's Business Number, invoice date, recipient identification, tax rate, tax amount, and total consideration (ETA s.169(4)) and preserve the underlying records per ETA s.223. Mandatory electronic filing applies: GST/HST returns must be filed via GST/HST NETFILE (or electronic media) by registrants with net tax of CAD 5,000 or more, and large corporations with gross revenue above CAD 20 million must file T2 electronically. Structured e-invoicing (EDI/X12, UBL) is accepted as source documentation, but CRA does not operate a centralized invoicing clearance hub.

CRA audit focus is on export substantiation: absent the customs export declaration, transport document, and foreign payment trail, the 0% rate is reassessed at full 5% GST on the invoice value with concurrent denial of matching input tax credits. Reassessment window: 4 years under ETA s.298, unlimited where misrepresentation arises from neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. Late-remittance penalties under ETA s.280 (1% of the balance plus 25% of the amount times months late, capped at 12 months) plus daily compound interest; gross negligence penalty under ETA s.285 (greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax sought). Income-tax clawbacks: transfer-pricing penalty under ITA s.247(3) of 10% of the adjusted cross-border freight margin; false-statement penalty under ITA s.163.2 (greater of CAD 100 and 50% of understated tax); Part XIV branch tax recapture on disallowed earnings; criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ETA s.327 (fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment up to 5 years). Delayed FX repatriation beyond the 180-day norm shifts the evidentiary burden to the taxpayer to prove the service was actually rendered to the foreign principal.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized capital and current account system. The Currency Act and Investment Canada Act impose approvals only for certain foreign direct investments exceeding statutory thresholds, not for routine FX conversion. Profit remittance is unrestricted: dividends and branch profits may be denominated in CAD or foreign currency without central bank approval. The principal impediment is the Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding tax (base 25%, reduced to 5-15% under most income tax treaties), plus normal anti-money-laundering verification under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export proceeds surrender requirements in Canada. Exporters are not required to convert foreign currency into CAD within any statutory timeline, and no portion of export receipts must be sold to the Bank of Canada or any authorized government entity. Exporters may retain foreign currency earnings in Canadian or offshore bank accounts. However, reporting thresholds exist under the Statistics Act for balance-of-payments purposes (e.g., the Monthly Survey of International Trade in Services and Trade in Goods) and under CRA for foreign-source revenue, but these do not constitute surrender or conversion obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with significant constraints. Canadian-licensed fintechs and EMIs (e.g., certain FINTRAC-registered MSBs) can serve as payment service providers, but they are not banks and cannot access the Bank of Canada's Large Value Transfer System or the Canadian Depository for Securities. Offshore digital EMIs without a Canadian MSB license cannot clear CAD directly and face prohibitions under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and the Payment Card Networks Act. They may partner with a licensed Canadian payment processor or bank to achieve settlement, but the capital escrow and liquidity mandates of this stage require a direct bank relationship and cannot be fully outsourced to an unregulated offshore EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or an equivalent Fedwire/CHIPS credit to a Canadian dollar or USD correspondent account at one of the tier-1 Canadian banks. The bank will require the originator to include the Corporation's CRA business number or Alberta corporate number and a clearly stated purpose classification in field 70 of the SWIFT message, generally referencing 'Direct Investment – Share Capital Contribution' or 'Paid-up Capital Injection'. The bank will also verify the identity of the remitter and the ultimate source of funds under FINTRAC's large-transaction and EDD rules. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% (15% federal + 8% Alberta general corporate income tax rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on a dividend paid or credited by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident of Canada, subject to any applicable tax treaty reduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than six (6) months after the corporation’s taxation year-end. A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada files within the same six-month window. The balance of federal and Alberta income tax is due two (2) months after year-end for general corporations, or three (3) months after year-end for a CCPPC claiming the Small Business Deduction, subject to instalment rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA requires a T106 information return for each non-arm’s-length non-resident transaction class when the aggregate value of transactions of that type exceeds C$100,000; contemporaneous transfer-pricing records must be prepared and retained for all transactions governed by section 247, irrespective of transaction size. There is no official Canadian statutory distinction between Local File and Master File, but CRA expects the OECD-aligned local file, master file, and country-by-country report in respect of large multinational groups; country-by-country reporting applies to fiscal years beginning on or after January 1, 2016 for groups with consolidated group revenue of at least C$750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the kind used in UAE or BVI jurisdictions. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (subsection 245(2) ITA) denies tax benefits from arrangements lacking real economic substance and commercial purpose. For treaty access, a non-resident must be the ‘beneficial owner’ and satisfy the principal-purpose test under the Multilateral Instrument (MLI), and an Alberta permanent establishment must have genuine operational functions. A clinically inert shell or conduit with no physical presence, employees, or decision-making capacity in Alberta will not obtain provincial allocation benefits or treaty relief and may be disregarded by CRA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Under subsection 239(1) of the ITA, a person who evades tax is liable to a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, and imprisonment for up to five years, in addition to civil reassessments, gross-negligence penalties (section 163), and interest. CRA may publish taxpayer names under the Voluntary Disclosure Program limits and pursue seizure of business assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in fraudulent filings are personally liable under section 227.1 ITA for unremitted source deductions and may be subjected to civil restitution for the shortfall. In serious criminal prosecutions, a judge may impose a travel restraint order preventing departure from Canada, and a conviction may result in imprisonment of directors personally.
Non-Resident Withholding Non-Compliance (Part XIII)

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable for the full amount of the un-withheld tax, plus interest, and a 10% penalty under subsection 227(9) where the failure is due to a lack of reasonable care. If the failure is made knowingly and to evade tax, criminal penalties under section 239 apply.

Director personal liability: Directors of the payer corporation are jointly and severally liable with the corporation for the entire amount of unremitted withheld tax under subsection 227.1(1) ITA, without the need to prove fault. Personal assets can be seized, and CPAs, banks, or trade creditors can be garnished; flight-risk directors can face travel bans in court-ordered cases.
Transfer-Pricing Mispricing or Omission of T106

Statutory sanction: The CRA may reassess under section 247 using the arm’s-length amount, apply the 3-year or 10-year reassessment limitation window (10 years for misrepresentation), and assess a 10% transfer-pricing penalty (section 247(3)) where the net adjustment exceeds the greater of 10% of gross revenue and C$5 million. Failure to file T106 separately incurs a penalty of C$25 per day (minimum C$100, maximum C$2,500 per return).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP documentation or knowingly prepare inaccurate returns can be held personally liable for the resulting penalties under subsection 163.2(1) for false statements, and may face civil fraud claims on behalf of minority shareholders or creditors. Where TP transactions conceal a tax evasion scheme, director criminal liability under section 239 attaches with potential imprisonment.
Shell Company / GAAR / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR under section 245 ITA cancels the tax benefit and triggers reinstatement of tax plus interest and penalties; treaty benefits are denied under the principal-purpose test of the MLI. CRA may also use the ‘substance-over-form’ doctrine and section 17 of the ITA for foreign accrual. There is no separate ESR fine, but an artificial structure may lead to a 100% penalty on the tax benefit under a court finding of misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who create or operate an abusive conduit can be personally liable for the corporation’s unpaid taxes if the sham was used to mislead creditors or the tax authority; in egregious cases, directors have been subject to asset freezes, passport revocation, removal from office by shareholder action, and extradition or prosecution under money-laundering and tax-evasion statutes.
