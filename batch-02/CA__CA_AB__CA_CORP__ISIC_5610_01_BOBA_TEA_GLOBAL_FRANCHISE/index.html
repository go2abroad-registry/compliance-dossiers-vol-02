Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) - national self-assessment tax administration. Portal: My Business Account and Business Registration Online at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Alberta corporate income tax is imposed under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act and its administration is consolidated with the CRA under the federal-provincial collection agreement announced in Alberta Budget 2024, effective for the 2025 and later taxation years. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) operates the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for export documentation and customs control.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Nexus determination: a Canadian business corporation (and any foreign parent or foreign licensor) making taxable supplies in Canada must register for GST/HST once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act ss. 148 and 240). Step 2 - Application: obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (RT program code) through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal; non-resident entities may file Form RC1/RC1X through a Canadian representative and must provide a Canadian address of records. Step 3 - Issuance: CRA issues the BN and RT reference number (e.g., 123456789 RT0001), which functions as the statutory VAT ID for Canadian sales, imports, and export zero-rating. Step 4 - Income tax accounts: the corporation must also open an RC (corporate income tax) program account and file an annual T2 Corporation Income Tax Return, which reports both federal Part I tax and Alberta corporate tax (Alberta general rate 8%; Alberta small business rate 2% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income for a Canadian-controlled private corporation). Step 5 - Permanent establishment: a non-resident franchisor operating through an Alberta permanent establishment opens a PE program account and allocates Alberta-source taxable income on the T2, with treaty relief evaluated against the Canada-foreign tax treaty.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Income Tax Act s. 2(2) and does not qualify for US-style check-the-box or pass-through treatment. The Alberta-incorporated entity is resident in Canada by virtue of central management and control being located at its registered office, and it is taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credit relief. The boba tea global franchise entity may qualify as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) if Canadian residents own 50% or more of the voting shares or equity; otherwise it is classified as a non-resident-owned private corporation or another corporation for T2 purposes. Franchise royalties from foreign licensees remain taxable at the corporate level in Canada, subject to applicable treaty reductions. No tax-exempt zone or general tax holiday applies in Alberta for beverage franchise operations.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification regime; the following statutory elections and disclosures are relevant to an Alberta boba tea global franchise holding corporation: (i) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for closely related corporations, deeming intra-group supplies (including royalty and syrup transfer charges) to be made for nil consideration, eliminating GST/HST on inter-corporate flows; (ii) ETA s. 179 drop-shipment election for qualifying goods sold to non-resident registrants; (iii) Income Tax Act s. 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfer of intellectual property, franchise agreements, and syrup supply contracts into the Alberta corporation; (iv) ITA s. 85.1 share-for-share exchange election for restructuring the global franchise chain; (v) hybrid mismatch disclosure and deduction-denial rules under ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.5, which require structured arrangement reporting rather than an elective carve-out; (vi) CCPC and associated-corporation elections and disclosures filed with the T2 return, Schedules 9 and 12; and (vii) GST/HST annual filing election and quick method election where eligibility conditions are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1); registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Non-resident suppliers of specified digital services and e-commerce supplies to Canadian consumers are subject to a separate simplified GST/HST registration regime with no de minimis threshold, but the Alberta store-based franchise entity operates under the standard CAD 30,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment at 0% GST/HST applies under ETA Schedule VI: Part V, s. 21 for goods exported from Canada, covering boba tea syrups, tapioca pearls, flavor powders, and packaging shipped to foreign franchisees, provided the supplier maintains an export evidence trail demonstrating physical export within a reasonable time; and Part VI, s. 7 for services supplied to non-residents, covering franchise royalties, trademark licensing, training, and management services rendered to overseas franchisees, provided the recipient is a non-resident, the service is not performed for consultation? use or consumption in Canada, and the franchisee is not a GST/HST registrant. Tangible property supplied to a non-resident intermediary may qualify under the drop-shipment rule in ETA s. 179. The franchise entity may claim full input tax credits on inputs attributable to zero-rated exports, generating a refundable net GST/HST position, provided contemporaneous agreements, customs, transport, and payment records substantiate the export.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or G13 Export Declaration for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000
  • Commercial invoice with HS classification, FOB/CIF valuation, country of origin, and explicit zero-rated export notation
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), Air Waybill (air), or truck/cargo manifest evidencing physical departure from Canadian territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC), SWIFT MT103, or bank payment record evidencing settlement from the non-resident franchisee
  • Executed franchise or license agreement and royalty schedule demonstrating supply to a non-resident and use of intellectual property outside Canada
  • Packing list, delivery order, and shipping instructions substantiating the foreign destination and consignee

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate. GST/HST is self-assessed; registrants file returns (Form GST34) through CRA My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE, or approved EDI/API interfaces, and must retain electronic records in readable and machine-supportable format (ETA s. 286; ITA s. 230). The federal government is advancing a pan-Canadian PEPPOL baseline for voluntary B2B e-invoicing, but no federal or Alberta law compels pre-clearance of invoices before issuance. For exports, CBSA requires electronic export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for goods requiring permits, controlled goods, or goods valued over CAD 2,000 per shipment; CERS supports API/EDI filing but does not impose digital stamping.

CRA and CBSA jointly scrutinize zero-rated export claims and may reassess within the default four-year statutory period, which is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, willful neglect, or fraud is established (ETA s. 298). Unsupported export claims are reclassified as taxable at 5% GST, subject to arrears interest at prescribed rates and reversal of all claimed input tax credits, with penalty exposure under ETA s. 280 for failure to file returns, ETA s. 285 for false statements or omissions (the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the federal tax sought to be evaded), and ETA s. 286 for knowing evasion, including fines and imprisonment on summary conviction or indictment. Retroactive clawbacks also apply where franchise royalty zero-rating is found to involve a related Canadian-resident recipient or intellectual property used in Canada. Canada does not impose a formal statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day default reflects CRA administrative expectations for export evidence and cross-border payment settlement, and it is used as the internal compliance threshold for maintaining the export substantiation file.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; no exchange-control permits, repatriation quotas, or central-bank pre-approval for inward or outward remittances. Profit remittance is restricted in practice only by corporate-law solvency tests and by Part XIII withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents (default 25%; treaty-reduced, e.g., 5-15% for dividends and 10% for industrial royalties under the Canada-US Tax Convention). Compliance encumbrances are reporting-only: FINTRAC reporting for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more (including same-day aggregation), FATCA/CRS account disclosures, and Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys. The Alberta bubble-tea franchise platform is classified as a standard low-risk commercial treasury profile, not a restricted sector under the Bank Act or the Investment Canada Act service-sector rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no designated custodian requirement for franchise-royalty or syrup-export proceeds. Foreign-currency receipts (USD, CNY, TWD, MYR) may be held in foreign-currency accounts at a Schedule I bank or converted to CAD at commercial discretion. Only FINTRAC large-transaction reporting, FATCA/CRS classification, and Statistics Canada trade-survey filings attach to these flows; no central-bank approval or surrender order is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) (monetary authority and payment-systems oversight); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (prudential regulator for Schedule I-III banks); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (AML/ATF and large-transaction reporting); Payments Canada (operator of Lynx RTGS, ACSS, and Real-Time Rail); Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) (Investment Canada Act notification and review).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. Canada has no EU-style EMI passporting regime; non-bank payment service providers are subject to Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration (operational since 2024) and FINTRAC money-services-business registration. Offshore UK-FCA or EU-authorized EMIs are permissible only as ancillary rails: they cannot offer Lynx/ACSS clearing membership, CDIC deposit insurance, interac acquiring, or trade-finance facilities, and must clear through a Canadian Schedule I bank. Primary treasury and escrow functions for the franchise platform must therefore reside at Schedule I banks, with EMIs limited to small-ticket cross-border supplier disbursements and legacy POS concentration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit
