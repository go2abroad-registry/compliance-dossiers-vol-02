Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-registration-online.html). Provincial registration counterpart: Service Alberta, Corporate Registry, for certificates of incorporation under the Alberta Business Corporations Act or the Canada Business Corporations Act.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate federally or provincially through Corporations Canada or Service Alberta, obtaining a certificate of incorporation and a corporation number. Step 2: Register with the CRA via BRO (or paper Form RC1) to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN). Step 3: Enroll in program accounts: Corporate Income Tax (RC), GST/HST (RT), and Payroll (RP) if employees are hired. Step 4: For a non-resident controlling shareholder, appoint a Canadian resident director (if applicable under the CBCA/Alberta rules) and file Form RC1 together with any CRA-requested security under subsection 240(2) of the Excise Tax Act. Step 5: Commence filings — T2 corporate income tax return (within 6 months of fiscal year-end), GST/HST returns (usually quarterly), and Alberta Schedule 5 provincial allocation. Step 6: Register for the Alberta corporate income tax program with the CRA under the Canada–Alberta Tax Collection Agreement; no separate Alberta tax registration form is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada); no check-the-box or pass-through classification is available. A foreign-controlled corporation is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), so it is ineligible for the small business deduction. Income is taxable at the federal general rate (15%) plus the Alberta general corporate rate (8%), representing a combined 23% headline rate. Territorial scope: a corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident in Canada; if central management and control is exercised outside Canada, the CRA may treat it as a non-resident but it remains taxable on Canadian-source business income and, via Part I and Part XIV, the branch tax. Alberta does not levy a provincial retail sales tax, capital tax, or general payroll tax.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (1) subsection 85(1) rollover election to defer capital gains on transfers of eligible property to the corporation; (2) section 216 election for a non-resident to file a net-income return on Canadian rental property instead of gross-basis Part XIII withholding; (3) section 219 branch tax election governing Part XIV liability for a non-resident's Canadian branch; (4) subsection 156 election between closely related corporations to deem inter-corporate supplies as non-supplies for GST/HST; (5) section 22 election for the sale of accounts receivable; (6) subsection 150(2) fiscal-period election for non-calendar year-ends; (7) section 179 GST/HST election on acquisition of a business. Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification follows legal incorporation status, and hybrid mismatch rules apply under the Income Tax Act and the Multilateral Instrument.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, subsection 148(1) of the Excise Tax Act). A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada that is not a small supplier must register for GST/HST before making taxable supplies in Canada; registration is also mandatory for non-residents soliciting orders or performing services in Canada even below the threshold in specific circumstances where the Minister requires security.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control ledger is mandated by the CRA. Registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding C$1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE and remit electronically. Supplier self-billing and EDI are permitted. The federal government is adopting the Pan-Canadian Peppol BIS e-invoicing standard for suppliers to federal departments (phased implementation through 2025); on the Albertan fast-casual restaurant side, ordinary POS-generated receipts satisfying ETA invoice content rules remain acceptable. CRA audits rely on source records rather than tax authority pre-clearance of invoices.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial, current-account, and capital-account transactions. Canada generally permits foreign ownership, cross-border dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, loan repayments, and capital repatriation without prior central-bank approval. The corporation must satisfy corporate-law, tax, transfer-pricing, withholding-tax, AML, sanctions, beneficial-ownership, and bank-documentation requirements. Payments to non-residents may require Canadian withholding tax, generally subject to domestic exemptions or treaty reductions and appropriate residency documentation. Foreign investment, intercompany financing, and certain transactions may trigger reporting under the Income Tax Act, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, the Investment Canada Act, the Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Business Corporations Act, and applicable sanctions legislation. Canadian banks may delay or reject transactions involving opaque ownership, high-risk jurisdictions, unexplained third-party funding, unsupported royalty payments, or inconsistent business activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no compulsory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into Canadian dollars or deposited with a domestic bank. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the corporation's discretion. A restaurant or franchise operator must retain commercially reliable sales, invoice, settlement, customs, and payment records and report taxable income in Canadian dollars using applicable Canadian tax rules. Foreign-currency balances and receivables may create foreign-exchange gains or losses for tax and accounting purposes. Banks may request transaction-level evidence for unusual international receipts, and export-control, sanctions, customs, GST/HST, and income-tax obligations remain applicable where relevant.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration that imposes routine exchange controls. The Department of Finance Canada, the Canada Revenue Agency, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, the Canada Border Services Agency, and Global Affairs Canada regulate related tax, AML, prudential, customs, sanctions, and trade-compliance matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Foreign electronic-money institutions, payment institutions, and digital treasury providers may support CAD collection, foreign-currency balances, card processing, and cross-border payments through a Canadian bank or regulated payment-services structure. They cannot assume that an offshore licence authorizes deposit-taking, money transmission, retail payment activities, or safeguarding in Canada. Availability depends on the provider's Canadian licensing position, Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada registration where required, sponsoring-bank arrangements, Canadian payment-system access, sanctions screening, provincial commercial availability, settlement risk, and acceptance of a newly incorporated, foreign-owned restaurant. Client funds may be safeguarded or held through omnibus arrangements rather than as Canadian bank deposits. The primary operating account should remain with a Canadian deposit-taking institution, with fintech rails used only after legal, settlement, data, chargeback, and insolvency protections are reviewed.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder equity, paid-in capital, or documented shareholder loans from an account held in the legal funder's name directly to the corporation's Canadian bank account. Use a clear wire narrative such as 'share subscription,' 'shareholder loan,' or 'capital contribution,' supported by the subscription or loan agreement and board authorization. Canada generally does not prescribe universal foreign-exchange purpose codes; banks instead require a meaningful payment purpose, originator and beneficiary information, beneficial-owner data, and transaction-support documents. The receiving bank may request the corporation's articles, corporate profile, ownership chart, tax information, funding agreement, source-of-funds evidence, and expected-use schedule. Do not split payments to avoid review, route funds through personal accounts, or accept unexplained third-party remittances. Record the amount, currency, exchange rate, date, legal characterization, and accounting treatment. For non-Canadian lenders or shareholders, assess thin-capitalization rules, transfer-pricing rules, interest withholding tax, treaty relief, foreign-affiliate implications, and financial-statement disclosure. Maintain a reconciliation between the incoming wire, corporate ledger, issued shares or loan balance, and bank confirmation.Use Canadian EFT, wire, SWIFT MT103, or bank-supported commercial payment rails for ordinary suppliers, equipment, franchise fees, royalties, and international procurement. For material or first-time trade transactions, use a commercial letter of credit, documentary collection, or bank-approved open-account terms with purchase order, invoice, transport evidence, customs documentation, and proof of delivery. SWIFT MT103 is a payment message and not an independent legal payment guarantee; obtain confirmation of value date and beneficiary credit. A commercial LC should specify applicant, beneficiary, amount, currency, expiry, shipment or performance terms, documentary requirements, governing rules such as UCP 600 where selected, discrepancy procedures, and sanctions-screening responsibilities. Ensure invoices identify the true supplier, goods or services, origin, end use, and Canadian tax treatment.
