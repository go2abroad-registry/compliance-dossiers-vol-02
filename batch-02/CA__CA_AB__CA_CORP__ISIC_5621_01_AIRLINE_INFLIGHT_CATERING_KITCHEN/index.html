Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the ABCA; the board fixes share consideration and incorporation may proceed with nil paid-up capital. A CBSA customs bond or security may nevertheless be required if bonded airline-catering inventories are held in a customs bonded store; this is a commercial bond required under the Customs Act and is not an incorporation escrow.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA–ARC). Portals: My Business Account (canada.ca/my-business-account), Business Registration Online (canada.ca/business-registration-online), Corporations Canada (is.ic.gc.ca) for federal incorporation, and Alberta Corporate Registry (corporate-registry.gov.ab.ca) for provincial registration and annual returns.

E-government filing portal

The statutory tax identifier is the 15-character CRA Business Number (BN). For the Canadian subsidiary: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Business Corporations Act and obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number; (2) enrol via Business Registration Online, which issues the BN and GST/HST (RT), corporate income tax (RC), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM) program accounts in near real time; (3) complete Form RC1 if applying by mail (2–4 weeks) and provide a Canadian resident agent or registered office address; (4) file extraprovincial registration with the Alberta Corporate Registry if the direct operating entity is a non-resident corporation; and (5) obtain a non-resident GST/HST account only if a foreign airline branch, rather than a Canadian incorporated subsidiary, is chosen. Identity proof for foreign shareholders: CRA accepts the foreign parent's certificate of incorporation/registration and the Alberta attorney-for-service appointment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (as per pipeline; the Alberta-registered Business Corporation is a resident Canadian corporation liable on worldwide income; no pass-through election is possible; non-resident ownership up to 100% is permitted without losing entity-level classification).

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime is recognized in Canada. A Canadian corporation cannot be classified as a pass-through entity for federal or provincial income tax. Available stage-3 elections include: (a) GST/HST closely related corporation election under Excise Tax Act s. 156 for inter-affiliate pooling of taxable supplies; (b) voluntary early GST/HST registration under s. 148(3) before exceeding the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold to recover input tax credits; (c) GST/HST fiscal-year and reporting-frequency elections (annual/quarterly/monthly) subject to CRA approval thresholds; (d) hybrid-mismatch structures are not elective — sections 18.4 and 18.6 of the Income Tax Act operate to deny deductions on hybrid instruments and foreign-entity classification arbitrage, and disclosure is mandatory for larger groups.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. A supplier becomes a registrant once its worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated export supplies, exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four consecutive preceding calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act s. 148(1)). An inflight catering corporation with airline contracts necessarily exceeds this threshold and must register for GST/HST; physical delivery to the bonded ramp occurs in Canada, so the Canadian small-supplier exclusion does not apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The kitchen may zero-rate (0% GST) meals and beverages for (i) supplies made to non-resident airline operators in respect of zero-rated international air-transportation services, or (ii) food and beverages (excluding alcoholic beverages not qualifying as ships'/aircraft stores) physically loaded into an international aircraft or delivered to the customs-bonded aircraft stores area for consumption outside Canada, under Part IX/Schedule VI zero-rating rules. Zero-rating is available only where the airline flight operates a Canada–foreign international segment; meals loaded on purely domestic flights are taxable at 5% GST. Catering services themselves, such as meal preparation, galley service, and ramp logistics, are generally taxable unless bundled as a zero-rated supply of tangible property with provable exportation. Substantiation must show the aircraft operator is non-resident, the flight number, and the physical intake into bonded stores.

  • CBSA ACI eManifest / EDI cargo data and, if physical overseas exportation occurs, CBSA export declaration (Form B13) or proof of export
  • Master/House Air Waybill (AWB) and cargo manifest listing the international flight, routing, and carrier seal
  • Customs-bonded ramp delivery docket / aircraft catering intake receipt signed by the airline's ground handler or lead purser
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank FX settlement (SWIFT MT103 or equivalent) evidencing settlement by the non-resident airline, and matching IATA CASS statement
  • Catering order/contract and tax invoice retained with the non-resident status declaration of the airline customer

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, API tax stamping, or digital invoice clearance requirement exists under Canadian GST/HST or Alberta law. Vendors must issue invoices or simplified receipts containing the registrant's BN, date, vendor/buyer identity, description, and GST/HST payable (Excise Tax Act ss. 221–223), and invoices may be electronic (EDI, XML, PDF, PEPPOL BIS 3.0). The non-binding Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing initiative promotes PEPPOL interoperability. Operationally, airline catering requires CATSA air-cargo security data and IATA Cargo-IMP/XML EDI to align with the airline's ground-handling and customs-bonded store systems.

CRA (International/Large Business Directorate) and CBSA jointly audit zero-rated inflight-catering claims. If documented exports are deficient, CRA reverses zero-rating, assesses 5% GST, applies Excise Tax Act s. 280 interest, and imposes false-statement penalties of 5% (s. 285.1(1)) and, for gross negligence or knowing misrepresentation, 25% (s. 285.1(2)). Income tax assessments may be reopened beyond the ordinary four-year limitation period; Income Tax Act s. 163(2) adds a 50% gross-negligence penalty; and criminal tax evasion under s. 239 carries fines up to 200% of the tax and imprisonment. Customs Act ss. 160–162 expose the exporter or agent to administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 25,000 or amounts equal to five times the value of the goods, with repeat misconduct escalating to seizure of aircraft stores and loss of bonded-warehouse authorizations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD). Canada maintains no exchange control legislation requiring approval for inward or outward capital transfers. Profit remittances (dividends, interest, royalties, management fees) are freely permitted subject to Part XIII withholding tax obligations and arm's-length transfer pricing documentation. The Canadian dollar trades freely; there is no central bank currency rationing, surrender requirement, or prior-licensing framework for foreign exchange transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender rule, statutory conversion timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Canada. Exporters may retain the full amount of foreign currency receipts. Statutory obligations are limited to (i) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer/currency transaction reporting for transactions at or above CAD 10,000, (ii) CBSA export declarations for controlled goods, and (iii) retaining source documentation for bank compliance and corporate tax records.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money laundering/anti-terrorist financing reporting under FINTRAC

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) may be used as auxiliary collection/disbursement channels but cannot serve as primary banking infrastructure for a Canadian business corporation. They generally lack direct access to Payments Canada clearing, may not provide CAD settlement account numbers with Canadian clearing codes, are subject to strict OSFI/FINTRAC recognition, and face regulatory friction when processing aviation-related B2B wire settlements. Maintain a full-service domestic bank relationship as the designated treasury hub; treat any EMI rail as a sub-account with limited balances and immediate sweeps.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity or shareholder-loan funding should be executed via SWIFT MT103 with clear narrative 'Capital contribution / equity injection' in the instruction field. CAD is credited through the receiving bank's Payments Canada settlement account. The sending institution must be a recognized bank not subject to OFAC/OFI sanctions; provide a reference letter from the remitting bank, certified charter documents, board resolution approving the capital injection, and a source-of-funds declaration. For FINTRAC, the receiving Canadian bank will record all electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; include full sender/receiver identifiers and a unique transaction reference number. If foreign exchange conversion is required, the purpose code should be classified as 'investment in resident enterprise' or 'equity capital transfer' on the FX conversion form.Trade disbursements for catering raw materials, aviation fuel, airport concession fees, and supplier settlements should be made via SWIFT MT103 for high-value international wires; for major equipment or fuel procurement, use an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (UCP 600) with sight payment against presentation of air waybills, clean-on-board bills of lading, and commercial invoices. For Canadian domestic vendor payments, use Payments Canada ACH (via bank) or real-time CAD wire transfer. Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: Form T106 Information Return of Non-Arm's-Length Transactions with Non-Residents is mandatory where aggregate cross-border non-arm's-length transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000; CRA IC 87-2R permits simplified documentation for SME groups with Canadian gross revenues of CAD 50 million or less; Country-by-Country reporting, Master File, and Local File are substantively expected for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million; all documentation must be contemporaneous and available within 45 days of CRA request

Economic substance scope: No standalone UAE-style ESR in Canada; the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA) re-characterizes avoidance transactions lacking bona fide business purpose that misuse or abuse the Act; the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL, ss.18.2/18.21, effective 2024) restricts interest and financing deductions to 40% of EBITDA with a CAD 250,000 de minimis threshold; treaty-shopping and conduit arrangements are attacked under GAAR and Limitation on Benefits clauses; Alberta PE substance for the inflight catering kitchen requires actual physical kitchen premises, employed catering and ramp staff, board-level management in Canada, and invoicing/payment infrastructure tied to Canadian operations

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Willful Evasion of Part I Tax)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239 ITA: on summary conviction, fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, fine of 100% to 200% and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax; CRA may also apply 100% civil penalty for willful evasion and seize assets

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally prosecuted as principals or parties to the fraud; active arrest warrants trigger CBSA departure interdiction (travel ban); imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA publicly names convicted directors; corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent transfers and civil restitution of the unpaid tax plus interest
Gross Negligence / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Penalty under s.163(2) equal to 50% of the tax understated; late-filing penalty of 5% plus 1% per month (max 17%); prescribed-rate interest compounded daily; denial of loss carryforwards and reassessment beyond the normal 3-year period where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect or fraud

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns with knowledge face fraud charges under the Criminal Code; civil liability for misfeasance; CRA can name directors in public compliance lists and obtain court orders freezing personal assets; travel restrictions apply pending criminal trial
Failure to Withhold Part XIII Tax on Outbound Payments

Statutory sanction: The Canadian payer is liable for the full unwithheld 25% tax, plus arrears interest and penalties under s.215; failure to withhold penalty may reach 10% of the amount; the payor may be prohibited from deducting the expense; late-filed NR4/T5 information returns attract additional penalties

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 for unremitted withheld amounts, interest, and penalties; CRA may issue director liability assessments and register them in Federal Court; directors face asset seizure, garnishment of personal bank accounts, and travel bans where criminal charges for fraud are laid
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR re-characterization of the structure, full inclusion of income in Canada, disallowance of fee deductions, 50% gross negligence penalty, EIFEL limitation of interest expense to 40% EBITDA, loss of the Small Business Deduction, and denial of treaty benefits for conduit arrangements

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in sham arrangements are jointly liable for restitution of the diverted funds; courts may pierce the corporate veil in fraud or unjust-enrichment claims; directorships are disqualifiable under provincial corporate law; CRA can pursue personal judgments and compel examinations of personal travel and banking records
AML / Proceeds of Crime Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties assessed per violation; criminal liability under s.74 PCMLTFA for directors or employees: on indictment, imprisonment up to 5 years and significant fines; failure to maintain a compliant AML program triggers suspension or revocation of the airport catering license; asset freezing under sanctions enforcement

Director personal liability: Officers and directors are personally prosecutable as 'persons' under the PCMLTFA; civil restitution and disgorgement of illicit proceeds; travel bans and passport seizure under criminal proceedings; reputational debarment from government airport concessions and aviation procurement contracts
