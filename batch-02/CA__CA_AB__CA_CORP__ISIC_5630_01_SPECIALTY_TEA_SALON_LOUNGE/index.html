Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — GST/HST and federal income tax administration; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA for the Province of Alberta under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act and the Canada–Alberta Tax Collection Agreement. Primary portal: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services/business-account.html); registration via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO); GST/HST filing via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate federally via Corporations Canada (or provincially via Alberta Corporate Registry 'CORES' under the Alberta Business Corporations Act) — an incorporation number is issued; no minimum capital is required for Alberta ABCA corporations and 100% non-resident ownership is permissible. 2) The CRA automatically creates a 9-digit Business Number (BN) for the corporation upon corporate registry data exchange; the BN acts as the master tax identifier. 3) Through BRO or Form RC1, open the required CRA program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll) — providing registered office address, director details, fiscal year-end, and estimated annual worldwide taxable revenue. 4) GST/HST registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies (excluding certain zero-rated exports) exceed C$30,000 on a rolling four-consecutive-calendar-quarter basis (or a single calendar quarter for seasonal businesses); registration may be elected voluntarily before the threshold is met. 5) The corporation files a T2 corporate income tax return (which also computes Alberta tax under the collection agreement), and, if registrant, GST/HST returns (generally monthly/quarterly depending on revenue).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election; a Canadian Business Corporation is statutorily a resident corporation taxable on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Alberta general corporate rate 8.0%; CCPC small business rate 2.0% on the first C$500,000 of active business income (Alberta). Relevant elections: (1) voluntary GST/HST registration under ETA s. 221(1) even while below the small-supplier threshold; (2) closely-related corporation nil-consideration election under ETA s. 156 (Form GST25) to disregard inter-corporate supplies for GST/HST; (3) corporate rollover elections on tax-deferred transfers to a corporation under ITA s. 85; (4) associated-corporation elections/allocations under ITA s. 256; (5) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2–18.8) are self-executing with no elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — small-supplier threshold measured on total worldwide consideration for taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports), aggregated with associated persons, over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (or a single calendar quarter for seasonal operations); exceeding the threshold or anticipating its excess triggers mandatory GST registration with effect on the day small-supplier status ceases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI: (a) Goods — s. 20 zero-rates tangible personal property supplied by the registrant and delivered to a destination outside Canada (e.g., packaged loose-leaf tea sold online to a foreign customer and shipped abroad); proof of export must be retained by the vendor. (b) Services — s. 7 zero-rates a supply of a service made to a non-resident who is not in Canada, unless the service is performed in respect of real property in Canada, tangible personal property in Canada, personal services rendered while the individual is in Canada, or intangible personal property used in Canada. In-salon food and beverage service consumed at the Alberta premises is standard-rated at 5% GST and is not zero-rated. A packaged-goods export accompanies zero-rating; the supplier may also recover input tax credits attributable to the zero-rated supply via the GST/HST General Rebate/Refund system, subject to CRA audit substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration — filed electronically via the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), including exporter BN, description of goods, value (CAD), weight, and destination (required unless exempt under the Export Reporting Regulations)
  • Commercial Invoice (Canada Customs Invoice equivalent) evidencing the sale and identifying the foreign buyer and destination
  • Shipping documentation — Original Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) or Canada Post/USPS or courier waybill showing the goods departed Canada; for services, the written contract, statement of work, and invoice to the non-resident counterparty
  • FX receipt evidence — bank statement / SWIFT MT103 confirmation of payment received from the non-resident (Canada does not issue a formal Inward Remittance Certificate; such banking evidence is used as the analogous documentary proof)

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, digital stamping, or government pre-clearance API exists for Canadian GST/HST invoices. Requirements: (1) registrants must issue sales invoices containing prescribed information (vendor BN, date, description, tax amount, rate); (2) electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding C$1.5 million (GST/HST NETFILE), and T2 corporate returns must be e-filed for corporations with gross revenue over C$1 million; (3) CRA business APIs (e.g., BRO, GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account) are available but optional; (4) payment and remittance must be made electronically when remittances exceed prescribed CRA thresholds.

CRA has a four-year statutory reassessment window (ETA s. 298) for GST/HST assessments and refund reviews, but no limitation period applies where a return or claim contains a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud. Zero-rating may be retroactively clawed back if the prescribed export documentation is not maintained; the taxpayer bears the burden of proof and may lose input tax credits associated with the failed zero-rating. Penalties: failure to register under ETA s. 280 — the greater of C$250 and 5% of net tax; false statements or omissions under ETA s. 285(1) — the greater of C$250 and 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded; failure to file information returns — C$100 plus C$25/day. Criminal evasion under ETA s. 327.1: summary conviction fine up to 50% of evaded tax and up to 2 years less 1 day imprisonment; indictment fine up to 200% of evaded tax and up to 5 years imprisonment. Interest under the ETA is compounded daily at the prescribed annual rate on any reassessed tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully freely convertible currency under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Both current-account transactions (trade, services, royalties) and capital-account transactions (equity injection, shareholder loans, dividends, liquidation proceeds) are unrestricted and require no central-bank approval for conversion or repatriation. Profit remittance hurdles are tax-driven rather than FX-driven: 25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents (treaty-reduced, e.g., 5%–15% under the Canada-United States treaty), 15%–25% on certain interest and royalties, the anti-surplus-stripping provisions of ITA sections 84.1 and 212.1, and paid-up-capital (PUC) verification. No blocking or foreign-exchange licensing exists; conversion is available at commercial bank rates through the free interbank market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender rules, no statutory surrender timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in any convertible currency in domestic or foreign-based accounts. The statutory obligations are reporting and record-keeping: cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by financial institutions to FINTRAC; incoming/outgoing remittances substantiating capital contribution or trade settlement must be supported by the share-subscription ledger, commercial invoices, bills of lading/air waybills, and customs declarations (B3 form) for GST/HST zero-rating and statistical reporting. Border declaration of currency and monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more is mandatory for travellers under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with compliance administration distributed across the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential oversight, FINTRAC for AML/ATF and large cross-border EFT reporting, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for currency/negotiable-instrument declaration at the border. Canada does not operate a restrictive foreign-exchange administration or central-bank FX licensing regime for ordinary trade and investment flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as an auxiliary cross-border payment and low-value supplier settlement channel; LOW as a primary operational treasury account in Canada. Offshore digital EMI licences (e.g., UK FCA EMI or EU e-money institution) do not confer any Canadian banking or deposit-taking status. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), payment service providers operating for Canadians must be registered with the Bank of Canada; fund-holding activities are not CDIC-insured and generally expose the corporate to concentration risk. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital injections: use SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV for cover settlement) to the Canadian beneficiary bank's SWIFT BIC (e.g., ROYCCAT2 for RBC, TDOMCATTTOR for TD), quoting the corporate's account number, the corporate legal name and the payment reference 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION CAPITAL - ALBERTA CORPORATE ACCOUNT - [ENTITY NAME]'. For CAD wires from the United States, utilize the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent account (ABA) with Fedwire/CHIPS, followed by settlement over Canada's high-value Lynx system; for USD wires, instruct settlement into the USD operating sub-account. Preserve the share-subscription agreement, directors' resolution, share register, and receipts for each injection. FINTRAC EFT transaction reports are triggered at CAD 10,000 and above; all inbound injections must be reconcilable to the corporate's paid-up capital and disclosed on the annual return. For shareholder loans, use purpose reference 'INTERCOMPANY LOAN', execute a signed loan agreement, and apply the prescribed/proscribed interest rules under ITA section 15(2) and 17(1).Clearing rails for outbound trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers are the default rail for tea-leaf, ceramic-ware and packaging supplier payments — each MT103 must include supplier account details, invoice/PO numbers, and product description; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) are recommended for large seasonal procurement, typically sight or 30/60-day usance LCs issued by the Canadian bank in favour of the supplier's bank (especially in China, Japan, Taiwan and India); (3) Documentary collections (URC 522) on D/P or D/A terms are acceptable for mid-tier suppliers with established trade history; (4) Payments in USD, CNY, HKD or JPY should be pre-matched to FX forwards or swaps to lock margin; (5) SWIFT GPI tracking must be used for every cross-border disbursement; (6) all disbursements must be supported by commercial invoices, customs entry documentation, and supplier vetting records to satisfy the financial institution's ongoing transaction-monitoring obligations.Outright vanilla CAD/USD (and CAD/CNH where offered) FX forwards with tenors up to 12 months, executable under the bank's SME hedging facility, used to lock tea/ceramic import costs and export proceeds.Cross-currency swaps (CCIRS) converting USD-denominated import financing or supplier credit into CAD cash-flow exposure, used only where matched to a term loan or binding supply contract.Vanilla European-style CAD/USD FX options (puts and calls) purchased from a licensed Canadian bank dealer to protect against adverse Canadian-dollar appreciation or depreciation on contingent seasonal purchase volumes.

Wise (TransferWise) and Revolut Business may be used to pre-fund and pay tea/ceramics suppliers in CNY, HKD, JPY, or TWD, but Canadian-dollar settlement must run through the licensed Schedule I bank account to preserve treasury visibility and AML-compliant controls.
