Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada); primary online gateways: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA) via Canada.ca.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta business corporation first completes incorporation or extra-provincial registration with the Alberta Corporate Registry, then obtains a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (RT suffix) from CRA by filing Form RC1 (or online through BRO with an appointed Canadian representative and Form RC59). Non-resident suppliers of remote digital services (digital game downloads, subscriptions, in-app purchases) can use CRA's simplified electronic registration for non-resident digital service suppliers; CRA issues a 9-digit BN plus RT program account as the sole tax identifier. Canada does not operate a separate VAT ID system; the GST/HST account number is the mandatory identifier on invoices and returns.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (non-pass-through). A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxable person under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no check-the-box or pass-through classification election is available. Corporate income is taxed at entity level, and Canadian-resident shareholders receive integration only via the eligible dividend tax credit; the corporation is also a GST/HST registrant in its own right. — A foreign-owned Alberta business corporation first completes incorporation or extra-provincial registration with the Alberta Corporate Registry, then obtains a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (RT suffix) from CRA by filing Form RC1 (or online through BRO with an appointed Canadian representative and Form RC59). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (non-pass-through). A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxable person under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no check-the-box or pass-through classification election is available. Corporate income is taxed at entity level, and Canadian-resident shareholders receive integration only via the eligible dividend tax credit; the corporation is also a GST/HST registrant in its own right.

Available election forms

Canada does not recognise IRC 7701-style entity classification elections. Statutory elections available to a CA_CORP include: (i) Income Tax Act s. 85 transfer election for tax-deferred rollover of assets into the corporation; (ii) s. 89 eligible dividend designation to manage the dividend tax credit; (iii) Excise Tax Act s. 240 optional GST/HST registration for small suppliers and s. 148 Quick Method election to reduce net remittance; (iv) ETA s. 156.1 'single filer' election for related corporations; and (v) treaty certification (Form NR301) to obtain reduced Canadian withholding tax on cross-border payments. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are governed by domestic anti-avoidance and the OECD Pillar Two Income Inclusion Rule once the CAD 750M-equivalent group revenue threshold is crossed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the ETA s. 148(1) 'small supplier' threshold measured on total worldwide taxable supplies. Non-resident digital-economy suppliers (app-store game sales, subscriptions, virtual currencies) and platform operators must register once Canadian-source remote digital service supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar year (or on a rolling-quarter basis for newly commencing suppliers).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act to: (1) goods exported by the supplier (Part V); (2) intangible personal property, including digital game software, downloads, subscriptions and virtual items, supplied to a non-resident recipient for use wholly outside Canada (Part IX, places-of-supply rule in s. 142.1-142.2); and (3) services supplied to non-resident recipients for consumption outside Canada (other than services relating to Canadian real property or physically performed in Canada). An Alberta MMORPG publisher selling through Apple App Store and Google Play zero-rates supplies to non-resident end-users when platform territory and purchase data prove that the place of supply is outside Canada; supplies to Canadian resident players attract 5% GST.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13/AAD) - required for physical exports and retained for mixed digital/physical collector editions; not generated for pure digital downloads
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - evidence of shipment for physical media, servers or promotional hardware departing Canada
  • CRA zero-rating substantiation file - commercial invoices, Apple App Store / Google Play payout statements, territory purchase reports, and place-of-supply logs (IP address, storefront country, player residency) demonstrating use of the digital content outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / FX remittance evidence - bank credit advices, SWIFT MT103 records and foreign-exchange conversion slips confirming receipt of foreign-currency proceeds and repatriation to the Alberta corporate bank account
  • GST/HST period return and reconciliation workpaper claiming the zero-rated export amount on the RT program account

No real-time fiscal clearance, XML digital stamping, or API-based government invoice pre-authorisation exists in Canada. GST/HST invoices may be paper or electronic; voluntary e-invoicing follows the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (Peppol/OpenPeppol). Registrants must retain books and invoices for six years, file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account, and remit through Canadian financial institutions. CRA relies on risk-based e-audits and data mining against app-store payout and customs data instead of invoice pre-clearance.

CRA may reassess and claw back refunds within four years of the normal assessment date, extended to seven years for misrepresentation and unlimited for fraud. False zero-rating claims trigger ETA s. 280(1) 25% penalty on the tax that would have been payable, or s. 285 50% gross-negligence penalty where wilful blindness or deliberate false statements are shown, plus daily compound interest under s. 280.1. For income tax, s. 163(2) of the Income Tax Act imposes a 50% penalty on the understated Part I tax and ETA s. 327 allows criminal prosecution with fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment. Exporters must maintain the documentation bundle for six years and comply with CRA audit information requests within the statutory response period to avoid default assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no general requirement to obtain prior approval for foreign-currency purchases, dividend remittances, intercompany loans, royalty payments, service fees, capital contributions, loan repayments, or acquisition proceeds. Canadian-resident corporations may maintain foreign-currency accounts and remit funds offshore, subject to contractual bank controls, sanctions screening, beneficial-ownership verification, anti-money-laundering requirements, tax withholding, transfer-pricing rules, corporate-law solvency requirements, and financial-reporting obligations. Dividends, interest, royalties, and certain management or technical service fees paid to non-residents may require Canadian withholding tax unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable income-tax treaty. The Canada-United States treaty and other treaties may reduce withholding rates, subject to beneficial-ownership, limitation-on-benefits, residence, and anti-avoidance conditions. Related-party financing and service arrangements must be supported by arm's-length terms and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. Payments connected with restricted jurisdictions, sanctioned persons, unusual virtual-asset flows, or opaque payment intermediaries may be delayed or rejected.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed deadline for converting export receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may generally remain in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency accounts in Canada or abroad. The corporation must retain commercially reliable records supporting the underlying sale, customer, invoice, delivery, receipt, and tax treatment. Foreign-currency receipts must be translated for Canadian tax and financial-reporting purposes using an appropriate and consistently applied methodology. Cross-border payments must be screened for sanctions, anti-money-laundering concerns, applicable non-resident withholding tax, GST/HST treatment, transfer-pricing implications, and reporting requirements. Large international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, including related transactions aggregated within the applicable reporting period, may trigger reporting by the reporting entity to FINTRAC; this is not an export-conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing routine exchange controls. Relevant ancillary authorities include the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada for anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist-financing compliance, the Canada Revenue Agency for tax reporting and transfer-pricing administration, the Canada Border Services Agency for customs matters, and provincial securities regulators for derivatives and investment-dealer regulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary collection and payout functions; Low as the sole treasury bank. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment service provider may be used where its terms, licensing perimeter, correspondent arrangements, sanctions controls, and Canadian legal analysis permit the activity. It does not replace a Canadian operating bank for payroll, tax remittances, local supplier payments, statutory disbursements, or resilient treasury operations. A provider carrying on in-scope payment functions in Canada may be subject to registration and compliance obligations under the Retail Payment Activities Act administered by the Bank of Canada, in addition to applicable provincial, AML, sanctions, consumer-protection, privacy, and tax requirements. Apple App Store and Google Play proceeds should be paid to an account held in the legal name of the Alberta corporation, with platform contracts, payout reports, settlement statements, and reconciliation records retained. Offshore accounts and foreign assets may create Canadian tax, accounting, specified-foreign-property, transfer-pricing, and reporting considerations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined: Federal 15% + Alberta general corporate 8%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, subject to tax treaty reductions (e.g., 5% under the Canada-US DTA for a 10%+ corporate shareholder; 15% for portfolio shareholders).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No de minimis dollar threshold: Income Tax Act s.247(4) requires contemporaneous documentation for all controlled non-arm's-length cross-border transactions. Country-by-country reporting and Master File apply to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 1,000,000,000; documents must be produced within 3 months of CRA request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations; economic substance is tested through GAAR, sham doctrine, business purpose/factor tests, treaty-shopping limitations, and anti-hybrid/input-tax rules. An Alberta corporation must demonstrate actual Canadian substance before claiming DTA benefits for Canadian-sourced gaming revenue.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Gross Negligence

Statutory sanction: Indictment: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years (Income Tax Act s.239). Gross negligence: penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax (s.163.2); interest compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns can be convicted as parties to the offense, personally fined or imprisoned; civil restitution via alter-ego claim allows corporate veil piercing if the corporation is an artificial instrument of fraud.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment plus 10% penalty under s.247(3) where the aggregate adjustment exceeds the lesser of CA$50,000 or 10% of gross revenue; interest on reassessed tax; loss of treaty relief if beneficial ownership is absent.

Director personal liability: Directors authoring the offending transfer-pricing documentation may be personally assessed for failure to remit payroll or Part XIII deductions; knowingly understating income is an offence supporting personal criminal liability.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR denies the tax benefit and allows reassessment; foreign tax denial under treaty abuse; administrative penalties for false statements; corporate dissolution or loss of name availability in jurisdictional filings.

Director personal liability: If the corporate form is characterized as a sham or alter ego, courts may pierce the veil and hold directors personally liable for unpaid taxes, torts, and foreign entity liabilities; restrictive travel may be imposed by foreign competent authorities in extradition proceedings.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 per case, criminal fine up to CAD 500,000 and 5 years imprisonment for officers and employees; mandatory enterprise and jurisdictional risk assessments.

Director personal liability: Senior officers can be charged when failure results from consent, neglect, or wilful blindness; directors may be personally barred from reincorporating or managing a financial business under FINTRAC licensing regimes.
