Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; GST/HST and federal corporate income tax are administered centrally by CRA through the My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE portals. Alberta's provincial corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement, and Alberta does not operate a separate provincial sales tax authority for GST/HST purposes.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Business Corporations Act and obtain the certificate of incorporation. Step 2 - Submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' to the CRA, available online through My Business Account or by fax/mail, and request program accounts: RP (federal/Alberta corporation income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RC (payroll) if employees are hired. CRA will issue a 9-digit Business Number (BN). Step 3 - For foreign-controlled entities, file the applicable T2 corporate return schedules and maintain non-resident shareholder information. Step 4 - If the entity is a non-resident supplier of remote digital goods to Canadian consumers, use the CRA's simplified non-resident GST/HST registration process even without a Canadian permanent establishment, once the CAD 30,000 remote-supply threshold is exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or equivalent entity-classification election; a CBCA/ABCA business corporation is invariably taxed at entity level under both federal and Alberta income tax. Relevant statutory elections include: ITA s. 85(1) corporate rollover on incorporation/asset transfers; ITA s. 83(2) capital-dividend account election; GST/HST section 156 nil-consideration election for closely related registrants; and the foreign-affiliate dispositions and FAPI elections under ITA ss. 91-95. Hybrid mismatch rules are not elective: CRA applies ITA s. 18.4 and s. 20.2 to deny deductions where a payment is not included in the recipient's taxable income in its residence jurisdiction, aligning treatment by statutory rule rather than taxpayer classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (or current quarter for new registrants) for any person making taxable supplies in Canada; for non-resident remote digital suppliers, CAD 30,000 of remote supplies to Canadian consumers in a 12-month period triggers mandatory simplified GST/HST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of digital game content by electronic communication is zero-rated if made to a non-resident individual or non-resident business that is outside Canada at the time of the supply and the supply is not received, used, or enjoyed in Canada. For Steam/PlayStation downloads, the publisher must rely on platform end-customer data (IP geolocation, billing address, country code, and account profile) to segregate Canadian-resident sales from exported sales. Zero-rating is denied where the recipient is in Canada or where the digital license is used or exploited in Canada. The exporter must hold credible contemporaneous records proving the recipient's non-resident status for every zero-rated transaction, including marketplace sales routed through Valve or Sony.

  • CBSA Export Declaration Form B13A - required only for physical game media shipped out of Canada; not required for pure digital downloads
  • Digital invoice or sales contract specifying the non-resident customer, digital license grant, delivery method, and sale proceeds in CAD or USD
  • Steamworks payout report and/or PlayStation Store statement showing end-customer jurisdiction, platform fee, and net remittance to the publisher
  • Proof of inbound payment from outside Canada, such as SWIFT/remittance slips or Valve/Sony payout settlement records denominated in foreign currency
  • Customer geolocation evidence: IP address logs, billing country, and platform account residency declaration
  • Bill of lading or air waybill where physical console game media is exported to foreign warehouses or retail partners

Canada does not require real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE service. Suppliers must retain readable electronic or paper records, including detailed platform payout reports from Steamworks and PlayStation Store, for six years. Invoices must display the supplier's Business Number and GST/HST registration number, but no electronic invoicing protocol is mandatory.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period, with an unlimited reassessment window for fraud or misrepresentation. If a zero-rated digital supply is recharacterized as made to a Canadian resident without GST/HST, the tax becomes payable with interest from the original filing date, and associated input tax credits on related costs may be clawed back. False or misleading statements attract a gross-negligence penalty of not less than CAD 250 and up to 25% of the understated net tax; deliberate evasion can lead to fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. Exporters must preserve the full documentation bundle and FX repatriation evidence for six years; the 180-day remittance benchmark is a commonly applied audit tolerance and transfer-pricing proxy, although no explicit GST statute imposes a strict 180-day repatriation deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility exists on both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Canadian dollar is an internationally floating and freely deliverable currency, and no governmental authorization, exchange control permit, or conversion limit applies to USD-denominated revenues earned via Valve Steam and PlayStation digital store payouts. The only quantitative profit-remittance hurdle is the Part XIII non-resident withholding tax imposed by the Income Tax Act (Canada): the statutory rate is 25% on dividends and certain gross royalties paid by the Alberta corporation to foreign shareholders, reduced by applicable income tax treaties (Canada-US Convention: 5% for corporate shareholders owning at least 10% of voting stock, 15% otherwise; certain arm's-length royalty payments may be exempt or reduced under treaty provisions). Repatriation of equity capital is unrestricted. Procedural compliance requires filing CRA Forms NR4/NR4-Summary, obtaining a W-8BEN-E or NR301 treaty-residency certificate, and including the withholding certificate number in the remittance instructions to the clearing bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed holding period for export receipts in Canada. Digital video game sale proceeds received from Steam and PlayStation store payouts may be retained in USD, deposited into a non-resident or domestic USD operational account, converted into CAD at the corporation's discretion, or held offshore. The only applicable obligation is FINTRAC EFT reporting for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, plus regular CRA income reporting of the USD revenue at the Bank of Canada yearly average or transaction-date exchange rates. No Bank of Canada conversion certificate or export-validation document is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating alongside the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision of Schedule I banks and FINTRAC for enforcement of cross-border electronic fund transfer reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration because the Foreign Exchange Control Act and its subordinate regulations were repealed in December 1951; the foreign exchange market is liberalized and centrally supervised by the Bank of Canada only for monetary and financial stability purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from a foreign parent or non-resident shareholders must be routed via SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfer (or MT202 COV where a cover payment is needed) to the Alberta corporation's account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, with final settlement in CAD through Lynx, Canada's ISO 20022 high-value payment system, or in USD through the bank's US correspondent (e.g., Fedwire/CHIPS credit into the bank's USD head-office account). The remitter-to-receiver information line must state the legal entity name exactly as it appears on the Alberta corporate registry, the CRA Business Number (BN), and a purpose code corresponding to the IMF Balance of Payments direct-investment equity category (e.g., 'EQ - equity injection / capital contribution'), thereby avoiding the AML rejection risk arising from abbreviations or third-party beneficiary references. Inbound electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC within required statutory timelines. No Bank of Canada approval or exchange-control documentation is required; the bank may, however, request a board resolution, subscribed-share certificate, or investment agreement to evidence the non-repayment nature of the capital injection.Trade settlements for engine licensing fees, console platform SDK obligations, middleware royalties, localization services, and co-development retainers are cleared through four principal rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct remittance in USD or CAD with detailed remittance information referencing invoice numbers and the corporate creditor's bank details; (2) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit, payable at sight or term, issued by the Canadian bank for high-value hardware, middleware, or distribution contracts exceeding material monetary thresholds; (3) ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) or Lynx transfers for domestic Canadian-dollar disbursements; and (4) platform-level settlement where Steam and PlayStation store revenues are received directly from Valve and Sony into the treasury account, then fanned out to subcontractors. Optimal treasury architecture: use a Schedule I Canadian chartered bank as the primary liquidity and clearing hub, and retain at most one EMI rail as a contingency collection circuit for platform micro-payouts with a documented FINTRAC MSB registration and contractual fund-segregation opinion.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory dollar threshold triggers preparation of a mandatory Local File or Master File under Canadian legislation; CRA requires reasonable contemporaneous documentation for all controlled cross-border transactions. Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for multinational enterprise groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000. Form T106 reporting is required for reportable non-arm's-length transactions once the prescribed CRA reporting thresholds are exceeded, commonly CAD 2,000,000 in aggregate for transactions or loans/advances, and substantial intercompany transactions involving Steam platform royalties or publishing rights should be documented regardless of amount.

Economic substance scope: Alberta and Canada do not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations. Canada relies on the statutory general anti-avoidance rule (section 245 of the Income Tax Act), the transfer-pricing recharacterization rule (subsection 247(2)), and the OECD Multilateral Instrument principal purpose test to deny treaty or domestic benefits where an entity lacks real commercial substance. A Steam-publishing shell with no Canadian employees, no active decision-making, no IP ownership functions, and no operational risk may be disregarded as a sham, agency, or conduit, and its income may be reallocated or recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment and recomputation of tax; gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under subsection 163(2); failure-to-report penalties; daily compounded arrears interest; criminal prosecution under subsection 239(1) with fines from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 2 years on summary conviction or up to 5 years on indictment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorized false filings may be jointly charged and convicted; the corporate veil may be pierced for fraud; courts may order civil restitution, freeze assets, impose bail conditions including passport surrender and travel bans, and compel restitution of evaded amounts.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may recharacterize or adjust the transaction under subsection 247(2); transfer pricing penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where documentation is incomplete, unreliable, or not contemporaneous; reassessment interest and denial of loss/credit offsets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause false transfer-pricing records can incur gross-negligence penalties; where the arrangement is a sham, personal liability may attach for conspiracy or fraud; court orders may include restitution, asset freezing, and criminal travel restrictions.
Shell Company / Conduit / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the shell under common law sham doctrine, apply section 245 GAAR to deny the tax benefit, reallocate income to the true economic owner, impose gross negligence penalties, reassess Part XIII withholding tax on distributions, and refer the matter for criminal investigation.

Director personal liability: Where the shell is used to perpetrate fraud or evade tax, courts may pierce the corporate veil; directors and shadow directors are exposed to personal civil liability, disqualification orders, travel bans, and penalties for knowing assistance in a criminal tax scheme.
AML / FINTRAC Reporting Deficiency

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and up to CAD 1,000,000 for entities per violation; criminal liability for laundering the proceeds of crime under the Criminal Code may attract imprisonment and forfeiture of assets; the underlying corporate registration may also be suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be held personally liable for civil penalties where they failed to exercise due diligence to prevent the breach; prosecution may lead to individual fines, imprisonment, asset seizure, passport surrender, and travel restrictions while proceedings are pending.
