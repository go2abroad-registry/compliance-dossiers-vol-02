Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-portal.html). Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Canada–Alberta Tax Collection Agreement; Alberta has no separate corporate tax administration for registration purposes.

E-government filing portal

Resident Business Corporation (incorporated federally under CBCA or provincially under Alberta Business Corporations Act): CRA automatically issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) upon incorporation registration. The BN serves as the statutory taxpayer identifier; corporate income tax (RC program account), GST/HST (RT program account) and payroll (RP) accounts are opened through CRA Business Registration (Form RC1 or online via My Business Account). Foreign entity without Canadian incorporation: must file Form RC1 to obtain a BN and register RC/RT/RP program accounts if it carries on business in Canada; a non-resident digital-services supplier with no Canadian establishment must use the CRA simplified non-resident GST/HST registration portal where Canadian digital-supply turnover exceeds CAD 30,000 in a 12-month period.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian law. A Canadian Business Corporation is mandatorily classified as a separate taxpayer taxing worldwide income at the entity level. Hybrid mismatch rules apply under Income Tax Act s. 18.4 (hybrid financial instruments and hybrid transfer arrangements) and s. 212(1)(b) Part XIII withholding on hybrid surpluses. Functional elections include: CCPC-status designations under ss. 125(6)-(7) and s. 256(1.1) (non-resident-owned control prevention), s. 112 inter-corporate dividend deduction (automatic for taxable Canadian corporations; disallowed for hybrid surplus), safe-harbour elections under tax treaties for permanent-establishment attribution, and the foreign affiliate surplus/grifter computations under s. 5907 for outbound foreign subsidiaries. For ISIC 5820 social-casino publishers, no patent or IP box incentive exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small supplier rule: CAD 30,000 of taxable worldwide supplies in any trailing 12-month period (s. 148 ETA). Digital economy mandatory registration for non-resident electronic-commerce suppliers: taxable supplies of digital goods and services to Canadian-resident recipients exceeding CAD 30,000 in a 12-month period trigger mandatory GST/HST registration (effective July 1, 2021 framework). A Canadian-resident Business Corporation publishing simulated social casino and virtual slots software must register for GST/HST once its taxable supplies (including Apple IAP/Google Play Billing sales) exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold; registration is also mandatory where the person makes taxable supplies in Canada and is engaged in a commercial activity in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital games and virtual slots software delivered by wire/electronic transmission qualify for zero-rating under paragraph 23(2) (intangible personal property supplied to a non-resident) and section 25.1 (services provided to a non-resident) of Part V, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act, provided (i) the recipient is a non-resident at the time of the supply, (ii) delivery occurs electronically to a destination outside Canada, and (iii) the consideration does not relate to a supply consumed in Canada. Social casino in-app credits and simulated slots purchases routed through Apple IAP/Google Play Billing are zero-rated only where the storefront country code, IP geolocation logs and platform settlement reports confirm non-resident end users. Zero-rating is denied where a Canadian-resident user consumes the digital content while in Canada, where the service is connected to a Canadian permanent establishment or domestic server infrastructure, or where post-sale live-ops support is rendered in Canada. Physical exports of gaming servers or hardware qualify for zero-rating under s. 2, Part V, Schedule VI subject to customs documentation.

  • CBSA Canada Customs Export Declaration (G7/CAED via ARC or EDE portal) - required for physical goods export
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing cross-border movement of physical goods
  • Commercial invoice describing the digital supply and identifying the non-resident recipient (Apple App Store Connect / Google Play Console payout statements accepted for marketplace digital sales)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-exchange contract proving receipt of external consideration within the repatriation window
  • Non-resident status evidence pack: foreign tax identification number, residency declaration, IP-address routing logs, and platform storefront country code
  • GST/HST sales ledger segregating zero-rated export supplies (Schedule 5 code 0-5)
  • Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlement reports showing non-Canadian storefront sales and FX conversion
  • Server access or download logs indicating delivery to foreign IP addresses outside Canada

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance electronic invoicing mandates. However: (i) GST/HST registrants must maintain adequate books and records substantiating commercial activity and zero-rated exports (s. 286 ETA); (ii) electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants with gross annual revenue exceeding CAD 1,500,000 and for most corporations; (iii) T2 corporate income tax returns must be filed electronically for corporations with gross revenues exceeding CAD 1,000,000; (iv) CRA requires platform operators (Apple, Google) to report Canadian-resident sales through statutory information requests, and invoices must display the Business Number and GST/HST status; no specific API clearance or fiscal-stamping standard is prescribed.

CRA actively audits digital-publishing and casual-gaming companies under its E-Services and Digital Economy compliance program. Zero-rating claims lacking documented non-resident delivery are reassessed to add 5% GST plus arrears interest. Civil penalties: s. 280(1) ETA false-statement penalty equal to 25% of the tax understated (minimum CAD 500); s. 280(2) gross-negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 4,000 and 50% of the tax sought to be evaded. Criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under s. 327 ETA carries, on summary conviction, a fine of up to CAD 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years, and on indictment, a fine of up to CAD 200,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. The CRA may reassess beyond the normal 4-year limitation period where misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default is established, and indefinitely in cases of fraud. Refund set-off and deemed-trust rules (s. 222 ETA) authorize the CRA to withhold zero-rating refunds pending export-documentation compliance and to claw back refunds paid in error with accruing interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the Canadian dollar. There are no exchange-control laws, no foreign-exchange approval requirements, and no restrictions on profit remittances, dividend distributions, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders, provided that the underlying corporate filings, tax-cleared distribution documentation, and AML-driven source-of-funds verification are in order. For a non-resident-controlled Business Corporation carrying on digital software publishing in Alberta, all profits generated from international sales may be freely converted and remitted to the parent enterprise; the only practical hurdles are standard bank-level compliance screening for the virtual-gaming sector and the requirement to evidence beneficial ownership and source of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement. There is no mandatory timeline or minimum conversion ratio; export receipts earned in foreign currency may be retained in the original currency, converted at commercial rates, or held in assigned eligible deposit accounts. The Canadian reporting obligations are limited to statistical and AML reporting — specifically, certain incoming and outgoing international wire transfers and transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and, where applicable, to Statistics Canada under the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High with legal constraints. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) may be used only as paymentaggregator and foreign-currency clearing gateways, but they cannot maintain Canadian-dollar deposit-taking operations or provide banking services in Canada without a Canadian bank charter or a registered Money Services Business (MSB) authorization under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. An offshore EMI operating without Canadian licensing cannot legally open domestic clearing accounts or receive CDN settlement rails. For this cross-border social-casino publishing entity, the offshore EMI may serve as a supplementary collection vehicle for Apple IAP and Google Play billing remittances, provided all funds are swept to a licensed Canadian operational account and full transactional rail visibility is maintained for FINTRAC reporting.
