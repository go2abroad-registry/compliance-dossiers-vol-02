Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); federal income tax, GST/HST, and payroll administration via CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). Alberta Corporate Registry registers the provincial entity, but tax registration is exclusively with the CRA.

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian-incorporated Business Corporation, file Form RC1WW ('Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts') with the CRA immediately after incorporation. The CRA assigns a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts for corporate income tax (RT), GST/HST (RC), and payroll (RP). Foreign-owned Canadian-incorporated entities must also provide certain information regarding non-resident shareholders. Alternatively, incorporation through the Companies Office and tax integration via the Alberta Corporate Registry's online system permits automatic BN issuance upon filing initial corporate records; if not issued, the RC1WW must be filed by the corporation's representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Canadian-resident corporation is taxable on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act at both federal and Alberta provincial levels, with no eligibility for pass-through taxation and no territorial election. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Canadian-resident corporation is taxable on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act at both federal and Alberta provincial levels, with no eligibility for pass-through taxation and no territorial election. Alberta levies a single flat corporate tax rate separate from federal tax.

Available election forms

No check-the-box classification election exists under Canadian law; corporate personality is determinative for income tax. The CRA does not permit entity classification elections analogous to IRC 7701. Applicable statutory elections include: Canadian-controlled private corporation status declarations (for small business deduction), election under Income Tax Act section 256(2) for deeming a corporation not associated with another (loss consolidation), in certain circumstances, and the functional currency reporting election under section 261. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are subject to mandatory rules in Part VI.1 of Income Tax Act (sections 18.4 to 18.6, 20.2), which operate automatically and not by election. No elective domestic hybrid mismatch regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies generally triggers mandatory GST/HST registration, measured over any calendar quarter and the immediately preceding four calendar quarters for a person that is not already a small supplier. For non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada, the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies to taxable supplies made in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods and services made for export are generally zero-rated under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act if the goods are exported by the supplier, or the services are supplied to a non-resident and are not 'in connection with' Canadian real/immovable property or goods situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. For an esports community platform, digital services, tournament access, subscriptions, and downloadable virtual content provided to non-resident customers are treated as supplies made to a non-resident person and are zero-rated for GST/HST purposes. The supplier must have records establishing ordinary commercial export criteria; mere billing to a foreign address is insufficient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / Canada Customs Export Declaration, or equivalent electronic export reporting via Canadian Export Reporting System, if required by value/regulation)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export of the goods from Canada, or if no physical export occurs (digital services), objective service-recipient evidence such as the contract, subscription record, IP address of the non-resident user, and non-resident payment history
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / cross-border payment evidence (e.g., SWIFT, banking records, Stripe/PayPal payout reports) showing payment received from the non-resident customer in foreign currency or a foreign-source funding mechanism

Canada does not maintain a real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory e-invoicing/continuous transaction control system. The CRA does not require digitally stamped invoices; however, GST/HST registrants must maintain invoices containing prescribed information. Registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding prescribed thresholds and certain incorporated businesses are required to file GST/HST returns electronically. The Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework, based on Peppol BIS, is voluntary and recommended for interoperability, not legally mandatory.

The CRA thoroughly audits zero-rated export claims. A misregistered supply of a service that is found not actually to be exported or supplied to a non-resident will be reassessed for uncollected GST/HST at 5%, plus statutory interest and penalties, with retrospective clawback up to the statutory limitation period. Knowingly making false or deceptive statements or knowingly omitting material facts in a GST/HST return is a criminal offence under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act, subject to fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to two years. Repeat offences carry higher penalties. Directors can be held jointly and severally liable for corporate GST/HST unremitted amounts under the Excise Tax Act. Failure to maintain adequate export substantiation documentation shifts the burden to the taxpayer; CRA will deny zero-rating, assess GST/HST, and the taxpayer will lose refund claims for the same period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalised capital and current account with no exchange-control statute and no repatriation or profit-remittance hurdle; the Canadian dollar floats freely and is fully convertible for both residents and non-residents. Dividends, interest, and royalty remittances to a non-resident shareholder are permitted without prior regulatory approval, subject solely to Part XIII of the Income Tax Act withholding (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced where applicable) and to CRS/FATCA self-certification at the bank. No investment screening applies to equity injection in an ISIC 5820 software/esports platform except the non-Canadian-investor notification thresholds under the Investment Canada Act for controlling acquisitions (financing of a newly incorporated Alberta subsidiary below the WTO threshold is generally exempt).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no compulsory repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or services receipts; a Canadian exporter may hold any foreign-currency balance at Canadian or offshore banks without restriction. Cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC large-EFT reporting by the financial institution within 24 hours, and all incoming and outgoing wires require banks to record purpose codes and to verify the ultimate originator and beneficiary under the FATF travel-rule expectations; FX conversion of retained receipts is executed in the freely floating wholesale CAD market.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central monetary and payments authority under the Bank of Canada Act; prudential oversight of banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); the foreign-exchange and trade-sanctions legal framework is administered by the Department of Finance Canada and Global Affairs Canada, with anti-money-laundering supervision by FINTRAC pursuant to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints: Canada has no separate EMI licence, so an offshore digital EMI (e.g., UK/EEA-authorised) cannot take deposits or provide pan-Canadian account services; remittance and payment-facilitation activity into or out of Canada requires the service provider to be registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, once the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) framework is fully in force, licensed by the Bank of Canada as a retail payment service provider. A Canadian-domiciled PSP or a licensed local MSB partner, not the raw offshore EMI, should hold client funds; the esports platform may therefore use Stripe and PayPal as regulated payment facilitators for card-network and ACH/SWIFT settlement, but should avoid deploying any unlicensed EMI as a treasury cash-management depository.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident equity injections are wired by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank passes) to the Canadian bank's Nostro account, with remittance-information field 70 citing 'equity capital contribution / share subscription — no trade consideration'; the domestic CAD leg settles through Lynx, the ISO-20022 real-time gross settlement system operated by Payments Canada with settlement accounts at the Bank of Canada. The receiving bank records the transaction under the Canada balance-of-payments purpose category 'direct investment — equity capital' for the Statistics Canada International Transactions Reporting System, and any movement of CAD 10,000 or more triggers FINTRAC large-EFT reporting. The onboarding compliance gate for the escrow account comprises CRS/FATCA self-certification, the UBO attestation, the signed share-subscription agreement, a source-of-funds letter, and confirmation that the Investment Canada Act WTO-review threshold is not exceeded (acquisition-of-control review remains inapplicable to a newly incorporated Alberta subsidiary).Tournament prize pools and platform disbursements are split by rail: domestic CAD instant and standard payouts use ACSS batch clearing or Lynx high-value wires; cross-border USD, EUR, prize, royalty, and procurement payments clear by SWIFT MT103 (with ABA/Fedwire correspondent legs for USD and IBAN/SEPA legs for EUR); commercial letters of credit under UCP 600 serve hardware, licensing, and media-rights trade settlements where documentary checks are needed, while pure digital-platform revenue does not require LC.
