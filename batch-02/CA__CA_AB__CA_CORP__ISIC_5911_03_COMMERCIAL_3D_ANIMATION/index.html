Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); taxpayer portal: 'My Business Account' (Mon dossier d'entreprise). Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement, so Alberta does not operate a separate corporate income tax filing portal. The Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) handles residual provincial fiscal compliance only. Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers export declarations.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (ABCA) or under the CBCA with an Alberta extra-provincial registration at the Alberta Corporate Registry. 2) Obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) using the Alberta Corporate Access Number; non-resident foreign corporations apply through Form RC4601 with certified charter/registry extracts and a Canadian agent or registered office attestation. 3) Concurrently activate program accounts: RC (federal and Alberta corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll) if employees will be resident in Canada. 4) For zero-rated export activities, the RT account is used to file nil/net GST returns and reclaim input tax credits on Canadian production inputs. 5) Online BN issuance is immediate; paper applications take 1-10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Business Corporation is a separate legal person and statutory taxpayer resident in Canada under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA); it is taxed on worldwide income with no flow-through to shareholders. Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status enables the reduced federal small-business rate (9%) and Alberta's provincial small-business allowance (provincial small-business rate currently 0%); general federal rate 15% plus Alberta general rate 8% apply to non-CCPC income.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime; ABCA/CBCA incorporation is conclusive for entity classification. Elective mechanisms include ITA s. 85(1) rollover on qualifying incorporation transfers, ITA s. 86(1) share reorganizations, ITA s. 97(2) partnership rollovers, ITA s. 216 net-rental election for non-residents, ITA s. 218.1 dual-resident corporation election, hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2-18.3 (mandatory, with limited joint designation elections), and ETA s. 150/156 elections for closely related corporations to transact at nil consideration or combine compliance. No election can override the domestic classification of a Canadian corporation; foreign hybrid mismatches involving a US LLC parent are addressed through treaty/domestic-law analysis and advance tax rulings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 gross annual taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold). Once exceeded, GST/HST registration becomes mandatory and tax must be charged; zero-rated exporters of animation and VFX services normally register voluntarily at inception to recover input tax credits on Canadian production inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7 to services supplied to non-resident persons where the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada or to tangible personal property situated in Canada when performed; commercial 3D animation and CGI character animation delivered digitally to foreign studios qualifies. Part V s. 10 zero-rates supplies of intellectual-property rights to non-residents. The supplier must be a GST/HST registrant, charge 0% output GST, retain evidence of the purchaser's non-resident status, and document that the supply is not for consumption in Canada. No export licence is required for NAICS 512110. Canada imposes no statutory export-FX repatriation deadline; 180 days is used as the internal contractual compliance ceiling.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A/CAED) - mandatory only where physical render media (LTO tapes, portable drives, master discs) are shipped abroad; pure digital deliveries are evidenced by a signed digital-transfer manifest and server/cloud delivery logs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill - required for physical exports of animation assets; replaced by an electronic handover certificate for encrypted FTP, cloud, or streaming delivery of final animation files
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank MT103/SWIFT credit advice or Wise B2B transaction report) - evidencing receipt of foreign-currency consideration from the non-resident buyer within the 180-day repatriation benchmark

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory validation API comparable to Mexico (CFDI), Italy, or Indonesia. Mandatory electronic filing and electronic remittance apply once annual taxable supplies exceed approximately CAD 1 million. Invoices must meet ETA s. 223 content requirements, and books and records must be retained in readable electronic form under ETA s. 286. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (voluntary Peppol BIS 3.0) is available but not mandated.

CRA's normal GST/HST reassessment window is four years (ETA s. 298); gross negligence extends the window to seven years and fraud carries no limitation period. Late or deficient remittances attract a 5% penalty plus 1% per month with daily prescribed-rate interest (ETA ss. 280-280.1); misrepresentation penalties equal 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (ETA s. 281); false input tax credit claims and unsupported zero-rating are clawed back with statutory interest, retroactively converting zero-rated invoices to 5% taxable supplies. Criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 exposes directors and officers to fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. CRA audits zero-rating claims by verifying contemporaneous non-resident status, digital delivery evidence, and bank-level receipt records for each invoice.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada provides full current-account and capital-account convertibility; there are no exchange controls, currency surrender obligations, or quantitative limits on profit repatriation. Dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees paid to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory 25% rate, reduced by applicable tax treaties. Inbound non-resident equity investment requires nothing more than a permissive Notification under the Investment Canada Act for an entity below the prevailing review thresholds; this SME animation studio is below the monitoring threshold, so no prior review is necessary. The Canadian dollar floats freely and is fully tradable on global FX markets.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds under Canadian federal or Alberta law. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency accounts offshore or onshore, subject to CRA foreign-asset reporting (Form T1135 for specified foreign property over CAD 100,000; Form T1134/T106 for controlled foreign affiliates or reportable cross-border transactions) and FINTRAC large-value and electronic-funds-transfer reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000). For corporate tax purposes, foreign-currency receipts are translated to CAD at the Bank of Canada daily noon rate or an acceptable forward rate basis under section 261 of the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); currency and AML wire oversight is shared with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign exchange control commission.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium with conditions. Offshore digital EMI and cross-border payment providers (Wise Business, Airwallex, or similar) may serve as a settlement overlay for CAD, USD, and GBP receipts only if the provider holds a Bank of Canada Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration or operates through a Schedule I Canadian bank sponsor. Unregistered foreign EMIs cannot lawfully provide Canadian-dollar retail payment clearing and create downstream correspondent de-risking exposure. Given the low-risk ISIC 5911 commercial animation profile, a fintech rail is feasible for treasury receivables, but the primary operating and escrow accounts must remain with a federally regulated Canadian chartered bank.
