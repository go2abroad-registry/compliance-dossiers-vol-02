Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under ABCA; incorporation is valid with zero or nominal authorized/issued capital. No escrow, capital commitment, or deposit account requirement applies to Alberta business corporations. Share consideration may be money, property, or past/future services under s. 101(2).

Funding & unlock path: Commercial office occupancy permit and fire safety certification under the Alberta Fire Code and municipal fire-prevention bylaws (egress, occupancy load, and sprinkler/notification-system inspection for the leased premises); structural load verification under the Alberta Building Code for server/computer equipment rooms; and workplace clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Alberta) comprising hazard assessment, first-aid readiness, emergency-evacuation planning, and remote-work ergonomics compliance. No special civil-defence clearance applies to ISIC 6201.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under ABCA; incorporation is valid with zero or nominal authorized/issued capital. No escrow, capital commitment, or deposit account requirement applies to Alberta business corporations. Share consideration may be money, property, or past/future services under s. 101(2).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); registration and filing portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html)

E-government filing portal

A Canada Business Corporation (federal CBCA or Alberta ABCA) is automatically assigned a 9-digit Business Number (BN) upon incorporation by Corporations Canada or the Alberta Corporate Registry. The corporation must activate program accounts via BRO: RM (income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), and RC (R&D). Non-resident corporations registering extra-provincially in Alberta under the Alberta Business Corporations Act must obtain a BN through BRO or by filing Form RC1 with the CRA, providing legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, registered office, directors, and fiscal year-end. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a CBCA/ABCA corporation is a person and resident corporation under ITA s. 89(1) and s. 250(4)(a), taxed on worldwide income under Part I; no pass-through or partnership classification is available; Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) status under ITA s. 125 may apply if qualifying ownership conditions are met)

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or entity-classification election analogous to IRC s. 7701; corporate personality is fixed by lex incorporationis. Relevant statutory elections affecting tax posture: (i) joint election under ITA s. 256(9) to allocate the small business limit among associated CCPCs; (ii) election under ITA s. 216(4) to file on a net basis for Canadian-source rental real property (e.g., leased data-centre premises); (iii) GST/HST Quick Method and simplified accounting election under ETA s. 227; (iv) hybrid-mismatch deduction disallowance rules under ITA ss. 18.4 to 18.6 (in force for transactions after June 30, 2022) provide no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1)); registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports, exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four calendar quarters or in the current quarter; voluntary registration is available below the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 to a supply of a service made to a non-resident person, unless the service is rendered in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. B2B Enterprise SaaS subscriptions (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 511210) qualify where: the customer is a non-resident; the supply is not acquired for the customer's Canadian commercial activity; the service is consumed, accessed, or benefitted from outside Canada; no Canadian real or tangible property nexus exists; and the supplier maintains evidence of non-resident status, delivery, and remittance.

  • B13A Canadian Export Declaration (CBSA) - required for physical or hybrid goods; for pure SaaS cross-border service exports, substitute equivalent service-dispatch evidence comprising the executed Master Subscription Agreement, technical delivery records, and IP access logs proving offshore usage
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill - applicable where software is embedded in hardware; for pure cloud delivery, substitute electronic SaaS usage/access logs attributable to non-resident customers outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 or equivalent bank remittance advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in CAD or foreign currency within the settlement window

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous-transaction-control, digital stamping, or API-authorization mandate exists in Canada. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns (NETFILE/XML) is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,500,000. E-invoicing is voluntary; the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard based on Peppol is being phased in for public procurement but imposes no immediate private-sector clearance obligation.

CRA may reassess within the normal 4-year period under ETA s. 298(1), or without limitation where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established, reverse the zero-rating, assess 5% GST on export invoices, claw back previously claimed input tax credits, and charge arrears interest under ETA s. 280.1. False or grossly negligent statements attract penalties under ETA s. 285.1 (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated) and, for income tax matters, ITA s. 163(2) (50% of the understated tax); wilful evasion or fraud is subject to criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239. The 180-day receipt benchmark is monitored as a documentary-rebuttal indicator; failure to evidence receipt of export proceeds within this period materially increases audit risk of reassessment and penalty application.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully floating exchange-rate regime with unrestricted convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible through any Canadian chartered bank, Canadian branch of a foreign bank, or registered Money Services Business (MSB) without Bank of Canada authorization. Profit remittances by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders are lawful without central-bank or government approval; the only structural hurdles are fiscal rather than exchange-control-based: (i) Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) (ITA), at the 25% statutory standard or reduced to 5%–15% under an applicable tax treaty (e.g., the Canada–U.S. Tax Convention for U.S.-resident shareholders); (ii) earnings-stripping limitations under ITA s. 18(4.1) and the residual 1.5:1 debt-to-equity thin-capitalization rule under ITA s. 18(4) concerning shareholder debt from specified non-residents; and (iii) the Investment Canada Act (ICA) review thresholds, which for a B2B Enterprise SaaS company are not triggered absent an acquisition of control of a Canadian business with enterprise value substantially above the statutory thresholds for WTO investors. Dividends, branch profits, management fees, royalties, and repayment of intercompany debt can therefore be remitted freely in any currency through SWIFT or the Canadian high-value clearing system (Lynx) without prior approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory repatriation rule exists under Canadian law. Export receipts derived from B2B software subscriptions and enterprise SaaS license fees denominated in USD, EUR, GBP, or any other convertible currency may be retained in foreign-currency operating accounts indefinitely, or swept to a CAD base account per the corporate treasury's standing instructions. There are no statutory conversion ratios, no time limits for onshore repatriation, and no requirement to exchange export proceeds into Canadian dollars. The only compliance obligations are informational: (i) FINTRAC requires an International Electronic Funds Transfer Report (IETR) for any single or cumulative cross-border electronic transfer of CAD 10,000 or more executed within a 24-hour period; (ii) Form T1135 (Foreign Income Verification Statement) must be filed by the Alberta corporation if the aggregate cost amount of foreign property, including foreign bank balances, exceeds CAD 100,000 at any time during the taxation year; (iii) Form T1134 must be filed for any Controlled Foreign Affiliate used as a downstream billing entity; and (iv) GST/HST zero-rating of exported intangible supplies must be properly documented under section 3(c) of Part V, Schedule IX of the Excise Tax Act (Canada), supported by invoices and evidence that the recipient is a non-resident. None of these rules operates as an exchange-control restriction on export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation of chartered banks, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border wire reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The former Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and neither the Bank of Canada, OSFI, nor the Minister of Finance administers any form of exchange-licensing or exchange-approval regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility as a treasury overlay, but not as a replacement for a Tier-1 Canadian bank account. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Inc.) and Stripe (Stripe Processing Canada Inc.) are FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and are authorized to operate cross-border payment collection, local-currency receiving accounts, and mass-payout disbursement rails for a Canadian Business Corporation. For an Alberta enterprise SaaS company, Wise provides dedicated USD/GBP/EUR account details, and Stripe Billing provides integrated card and ACH/SWIFT receivable settlement. Legal constraints: (i) MSBs are not Canadian Schedule I banks and are not members of Payments Canada, so they cannot provide Lynx high-value CAD settlement or direct access to the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS); (ii) funds held with an MSB/EMI are not insured by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) and must be passed through to a chartered bank operating account for statutory cash management; (iii) the EMI cannot issue commercial letters of credit or act as a substitute for the corporate operating account; (iv) Canadian residency of the legal entity is required for FINTRAC MSB registration, and cross-border balance sheet accumulation should be monitored for ITA s. 93.1 and foreign-accrual-property implications. The optimum structure is a dual-rail treasury: Tier-1 Canadian bank for settlement, payroll, tax remittances, and FX hedging; Wise and Stripe for international collections and vendor disbursements up to a daily threshold.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

No Bank of Canada, OSFI, or FINTRAC pre-authorization is required for inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, or intercompany capital advances. The recommended wire protocol is SWIFT MT103 (or SWIFT ISO 20022 pacs.008) initiated by the remitting shareholder, with the remittance information (Field 70) coded 'EQUITY INJECTION', 'SHARE CAPITAL', or 'INTERCOMPANY LOAN', and with the exact legal name and Canadian operating account nubmer of the Alberta corporation in the beneficiary fields. The receiving Canadian bank will subject the transfer to standard sanctions screening and AML verification, and will record the transaction for FINTRAC reporting if the amount equals or exceeds CAD 10,000. Purpose coding under Canadian law is not required for an IMF Balance of Payments classification in the way China's SAFE system operates; however, the bank will ask the client to confirm the classification as a direct investment in equity or intercompany debt. Tax and regulatory considerations: (i) a bona fide equity subscription produces no Part XIII withholding tax and needs to be recorded as paid-up capital under the ABCA and as share capital for CRA purposes; (ii) intercompany loans must be documented at arm's length to avoid ITA s. 18(4) thin-capitalization and s. 18(4.1) earnings-stripping disallowance; (iii) interest payments on inbound loans are subject to Part XIII withholding of 25% unless treaty-reduced; (iv) the Investment Canada Act net-benefit review is not triggered for a B2B SaaS enterprise because the enterprise value of the Canadian business falls below the applicable WTO-investor thresholds; and (v) no national security review is expected because the SaaS ISIC 6201 activity does not involve critical minerals, defense, or dual-use goods.Settlement rails for outbound trade disbursements from a Canadian B2B SaaS corporate treasury are: 1) Domestic CAD disbursements (vendor payables, payroll, HST/GST remittances) use the Canadian Payments Association ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) EFT rails, with Lynx (Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement system) required for same-day large-value CAD settlements through a Tier-1 bank membership; 2) cross-border vendor payments, intercompany service fees, and dividend distributions are executed via SWIFT MT103 bilateral credit transfers, cleared domestically through Lynx on the Canadian side and through CHIPS/Fedwire (U.S.) or TARGET2 (EU) on the receiving side; 3) standby and commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600/URC 522 are permissible but not structurally necessary for SaaS intangible deliveries; they are available from RBC, TD, BMO, CIBC if the treasury or procurement counterparty demands unconditional bank payment undertakings, for example for major infrastructure or co-location purchases; 4) low-value cross-border payments (non-resident contractor payroll, international subscription payments, marketing vendor disbursements) can be routed through Wise's local clearing rails (ACH, SEPA CT, Faster Payments) as an overlay to the chartered bank account; 5) USD-denominated receivables from Stripe_Billing are collected into the Canadian bank's USD sub-account and converted to CAD through a standing conversion order at treasury-notified thresholds, minimizing bilateral FX spreads. Where payments are made to non-resident shareholders as dividends, the paying Canadian bank will require the corporate resolution and may request proof of CRS/FATCA self-certification before clearing the wire.Vanilla deliverable FX forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) quoted by Tier-1 Canadian chartered banks under ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a short-form FX confirmation; maturities from 1 day to 36 months; no OSFI approval required for non-financial corporate hedgers.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for CAD/CNY, CAD/BRL, CAD/INR and other restricted-currency exposures; permitted where ultimate collection currency is non-convertible; the Canadian bank books the NDF on its offshore books and the CAD leg settles domestically.Cross-currency swaps (XCS) to hedge principal and periodic interest flows on foreign-currency intercompany debt; subject to Board authorization, ISDA CSA margining where applicable, and CRA interest-stripping rules if the counterparty is a specified non-resident shareholder.FX options (plain-vanilla long calls/puts, zero-premium collars, and capped options) to protect budget conversion rates without requiring a firm deliverable obligation.FX swaps for managing value-date misalignments between receipt currencies and disbursement obligations.Composite forward structures (participating forwards, seagull and ratio strategies) for smoothing conversion of USD-denominated recurring MRR into CAD operating cash flow.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility as a treasury overlay, but not as a replacement for a Tier-1 Canadian bank account. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Inc.) and Stripe (Stripe Processing Canada Inc.) are FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and are authorized to operate cross-border payment collection, local-currency receiving accounts, and mass-payout disbursement rails for a Canadian Business Corporation. For an Alberta enterprise SaaS company, Wise provides dedicated USD/GBP/EUR account details, and Stripe Billing provides integrated card and ACH/SWIFT receivable settlement. Legal constraints: (i) MSBs are not Canadian Schedule I banks and are not members of Payments Canada, so they cannot provide Lynx high-value CAD settlement or direct access to the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS); (ii) funds held with an MSB/EMI are not insured by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) and must be passed through to a chartered bank operating account for statutory cash management; (iii) the EMI cannot issue commercial letters of credit or act as a substitute for the corporate operating account; (iv) Canadian residency of the legal entity is required for FINTRAC MSB registration, and cross-border balance sheet accumulation should be monitored for ITA s. 93.1 and foreign-accrual-property implications. The optimum structure is a dual-rail treasury: Tier-1 Canadian bank for settlement, payroll, tax remittances, and FX hedging; Wise and Stripe for international collections and vendor disbursements up to a daily threshold.
