Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), federal tax administration portal at canada.ca/cra-myba (My Business Account); Alberta corporate income tax is collected by the CRA on behalf of Alberta Treasury Board and Finance under the Canada–Alberta Tax Collection Agreement; corporate registry filings are with Service Alberta / Corporate Registry (Alberta).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing an Alberta-resident or non-resident business corporation apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus a GST/HST program account using Form RC1 (General Application for a CRA Program Account) or, for non-residents, Form RC4607 (GST/HST Registration for Non-Resident). Federal corporate income tax and Alberta corporate tax accounts are opened automatically upon Articles of Incorporation or extra-provincial registration through Service Alberta; no provincial sales tax registration exists in Alberta (Alberta imposes no PST), so only federal GST/HST registration applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope for non-resident corporations (a corporation that is a resident of Canada is taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation is taxable only on Canadian-source employment, business, and capital gains income; Alberta corporate tax follows the federal taxable-income base and is allocated using the Alberta permanent establishment formula). — Foreign entities establishing an Alberta-resident or non-resident business corporation apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus a GST/HST program account using Form RC1 (General Application for a CRA Program Account) or, for non-residents, Form RC4607 (GST/HST Registration for Non-Resident). Federal corporate income tax and Alberta corporate tax accounts are opened automatically upon Articles of Incorporation or extra-provincial registration through Service Alberta; no provincial sales tax registration exists in Alberta (Alberta imposes no PST), so only federal GST/HST registration applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope for non-resident corporations (a corporation that is a resident of Canada is taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation is taxable only on Canadian-source employment, business, and capital gains income; Alberta corporate tax follows the federal taxable-income base and is allocated using the Alberta permanent establishment formula).

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; incorporation creates a separate taxpayer by law. Available elections relevant to this entity include the functional-currency election under s. 261 of the Income Tax Act, the deemed-fiscal-year and s. 85 rollover elections on incorporation or reorganization, the non-resident s. 216(1) election for Canadian rental income, and controlled-foreign-affiliate/FAPI elections; however, none alter the entity-level Canadian corporate income tax treatment. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available because Canada has not enacted a comprehensive statutory hybrid-mismatch election regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable-supply threshold for a 'small supplier' (calculated over one calendar quarter and the immediately preceding three calendar quarters); registration becomes compulsory once the threshold is exceeded. Non-residents carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold and must register once any taxable supplies exceed it.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act applies to (i) tangible property exported by the supplier or recipient at the time of supply; (ii) services supplied to non-resident, unregistered persons where the service is performed wholly outside Canada; and (iii) intangible/computer-software or API platform licensing supplies made to non-resident customers who are not registered and who acquire the supply for use or consumption wholly outside Canada. The AI/LLM inference API token licensing and output sales to B2B non-resident customers, with no Canadian use or consumption, are zero-rated; domestic Canadian-use output is subject to 5% GST.

  • B13A/BAR Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency) or e-manifest filing through the Canadian Export Reporting System
  • Commercial invoice containing vendor and recipient identification, export classification, and platform licensing description
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement for exported goods (not applicable to digital services, but required when computational hardware is exported)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and payment-rail settlement records (SWIFT/MT103, bank transfer, or USDC blockchain ledger) evidencing payment from non-resident customers
  • Written non-resident status certificate or jurisdiction-of-residence declaration from the customer; IP address and server-access logs demonstrating offshore consumption of the AI/LLM API

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API clearance mandates. E-invoicing is permitted in PEPPOL-based formats under the voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (post-2024). GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account, or approved EDI/API channels; invoices must contain prescribed information under s. 169(1) of the Excise Tax Act, but there is no government-run real-time invoice approval system.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export claims within the normal four-year GST/HST reassessment period, extended indefinitely where there has been misrepresentation, fraud, or omission attributable to neglect. Exporters are liable to repay disallowed input tax credits and uncollected GST, plus daily compound interest and a gross-negligence penalty of up to 25% of the associated tax under s. 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act; criminal prosecution, fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount, and imprisonment may apply for wilful evasion or false export statements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally require prior approval for foreign investment, dividend remittances, shareholder-loan repayments, service-fee payments, royalty payments, or foreign-currency purchases by an Alberta corporation. Practical remittance hurdles arise from bank sanctions screening, source-of-funds verification, beneficial-ownership identification, anti-money-laundering controls, tax withholding, transfer-pricing support, thin-capitalization rules, non-resident reporting, and contractual restrictions in financing documents. Dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, and other amounts paid to non-residents may be subject to Canadian withholding tax, generally at 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty. Interest paid to arm's-length non-residents is generally exempt from Canadian withholding tax, subject to statutory exceptions. Related-party financing must be supported by commercially reasonable terms, debt-capacity analysis, transfer-pricing documentation, and compliance with Canadian thin-capitalization rules. The corporation should retain board approvals, contracts, invoices, tax certificates, treaty-relief documentation, and evidence of the underlying commercial purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed deadline for converting foreign-currency export receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the corporation's discretion. The corporation should maintain accounting records in Canadian dollars, recognize foreign-exchange gains and losses under applicable Canadian accounting and tax principles, and retain contracts, invoices, shipping or service-delivery evidence, payment confirmations, and reconciliation records. Financial institutions may request transaction-purpose information and may report suspicious or prescribed transactions under applicable AML legislation. Cash or monetary instruments physically imported into or exported from Canada in amounts of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency; this does not generally apply to ordinary electronic bank transfers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration comparable to a capital-controls authority. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions supervises federally regulated banks, while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada administers anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing compliance for reporting entities. The Canada Revenue Agency administers tax reporting, transfer-pricing, withholding-tax, and information-reporting obligations relevant to cross-border cash movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for collections, card acceptance, and payment orchestration; Low as a substitute for a primary Canadian corporate bank account. Canada does not operate a general electronic-money-institution licence equivalent to the European Union EMI regime. A provider offering payment services to Canadian users may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act and registration with the Bank of Canada, depending on its activities, although registration does not constitute deposit-taking authorization or unrestricted banking authority. Providers may also be subject to FINTRAC obligations, provincial securities or money-services rules, sanctions controls, consumer-protection requirements, and foreign-jurisdiction licensing. Offshore digital-wallet or EMI accounts may be used only where the provider legally services Canada, accepts the corporation's business model, supports Canadian corporate onboarding, and permits the intended source and destination of funds. Offshore accounts should not be represented as Canadian deposit accounts. Cryptocurrency or USDC settlement introduces additional counterparty, custody, blockchain-traceability, sanctions, tax, accounting, and AML risks; the corporation should use regulated counterparties, segregate customer and corporate funds where relevant, document wallet ownership, and reconcile all conversions and transfers.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26% combined (Federal general corporate rate 15% + Alberta general corporate rate 11%; Alberta small business rate of 2% applies to the first CAD 500,000 of eligible Canadian-controlled private corporation active business income, reducing the combined rate to 11% for qualifying small CCPCs).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Federal Canada Part XIII withholding tax rate of 25% on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; Alberta does not impose an additional provincial dividend withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed no later than six months after the end of the taxation year (e.g., December 31 year-end: June 30 following year); final payment of tax is due within two months of year-end, extended to three months for eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of cross-border transactions with non-resident related parties in a taxation year triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation under Income Tax Act subsection 247(4) and CRA Transfer Pricing Memoranda TPM-09/10/11; groups meeting the OECD Country-by-Country Reporting threshold of EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue are expected to maintain Master File and Local File documentation.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime equivalent to the offshore financial centres; however, the Income Tax Act's General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the transfer pricing substance rules require that the taxpayer's Canadian entity perform meaningful functions, assume real risks, and hold economically significant assets. Transactions structured through shell companies or tax-favourable no-substance affiliates are recharacterised by the CRA where the sole purpose is to obtain a tax benefit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 238/239: on summary conviction, a fine of up to CAD 25,000 or imprisonment up to six months, or both; on indictment, a fine not less than 50% and not exceeding 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to five years. Civil gross negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) is 50% of the understated tax; post-assessment compound daily interest accrues from the due date.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be jointly and severally liable under subsection 227.1 for unremitted withholdings; criminal proceedings can result in judicial travel bans, asset freezes, extradition, and imprisonment. In egregious cases, the corporate veil can be pierced where the director treated corporate funds or assets as personal property.
Shell Company / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Income Tax Act section 245 denies the tax benefit and imposes a 10% GAAR penalty on the federal portion of the tax benefit, plus provincial GAAR penalties where applicable. Transfer pricing reassessment under subsection 247(2) recharacterises the intercompany arrangement; subsection 247(3) imposes a 10% transfer pricing penalty on the net adjustment where the documentation threshold is met, with no reasonable-efforts defence where the taxpayer failed to maintain CDN 1M cross-border transaction records.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false certifications relating to transfer pricing documents or who direct transactions without economic substance can be held personally liable for tax adjustments and penalties if they knowingly participated in the fraud; directors may be subject to prosecution for conspiracy to evade tax, which can include travel restrictions, property seizure orders, and imprisonment.
AML / Virtual Currency Reporting Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for a first offence and up to CAD 2,000,000 for a subsequent offence; wilful evasion of Canadian tax on crypto proceeds triggers Income Tax Act section 162 repeated-filing penalty of up to 10% of omitted gross income plus gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax, and criminal charges under section 239 can result in fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who cause the corporation to fail to implement a FINTRAC-compliant AML programme or who knowingly conceal crypto revenues are personally exposed to administrative penalties of up to CAD 500,000; criminal AML violations and tax evasion can trigger personal travel bans, exclusion from flight, and imprisonment of up to five years.
