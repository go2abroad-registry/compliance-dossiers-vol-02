Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada); primary portal: CRA My Business Account, supplemented by the Government of Canada Business Registration Online portal.

E-government filing portal

Upon federal or Alberta incorporation, the registrar transmits corporate details to CRA, which issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN). GST/HST account registration is made through Business Registration Online, My Business Account, or Form RC1; non-resident corporations use Form RC171 to obtain a BN and may apply for GST/HST registration if they carry on business in Canada. The applicant must provide the legal entity name, registered office, directors, ISIC/NAICS business classification, and, for non-residents, a Canadian representative or address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian-incorporated Business Corporation is a resident taxpayer subject to Canadian federal and Alberta provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income; Alberta income is attributed under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act through a permanent establishment. No pass-through eligibility and no tax-exempt zone classification applies.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box regime. Corpus of applicable elections includes: income tax rollover elections (ITA s. 85), capital dividend elections (ITA s. 83(2)), associated-company designation elections, GST/HST closely related corporation election (Excise Tax Act s. 156), GST/HST Quick Method election, annual GST/HST filing election, and non-resident election under ITA s. 216 for Canadian-source rental income. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory and non-elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of total worldwide taxable supplies (before deductions) from Canadian commercial activity, determined in any calendar quarter or over the prior four consecutive calendar quarters; exceeding this gross turnover mandate requires GST/HST registration. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same small-supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Canada, and services or intangible personal property supplied to non-resident recipients, may be zero-rated for GST/HST where the supply qualifies under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act. For a mobile utility application sold through Apple App Store or Google Play, digitally delivered software supplied to non-residents who use and receive the app outside Canada is generally zero-rated; the supplier must evidence the non-resident status of the recipient, the foreign download/store account, foreign IP or device metadata, and absence of use or supply in Canada.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or equivalent electronic export declaration)
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (where physical media or goods are exported)
  • Contract, order, purchase records, and downloaded-app transactional records
  • Payment settlement evidence and foreign currency inward remittance certificate or bank confirmation

Canada has no government real-time fiscal clearance, continuous controls, or digital stamping mandate. GST/HST registrants must issue valid tax invoices containing the registrant's name, BN, invoice date, description of supply and tax amount. Large registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically. CRA requires books and records to be retained in machine-readable format where generated electronically; e-invoicing is accepted but not cleared by CRA in real time.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export claims within the normal four-year assessment window, extended to ten years in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful omission. Failure to retain the required documentary substantiation can result in denial of zero-rating, reassessment of GST/HST at the applicable rate, interest, and penalties under the Excise Tax Act. Gross negligence penalties may reach 50% of the tax understated or the overstatement of refund; deliberate falsification of export or non-residency evidence may attract criminal prosecution and retrospective clawback of refunded GST/HST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full capital and current account convertibility under a free-floating exchange rate regime. The Canadian dollar is a freely tradeable currency with no exchange-control legislation restricting conversion of CAD, acquisition of foreign currency, or repatriation of capital, dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees. Profit remittance is not subject to pre-clearance or central bank approval; however, outbound payments to non-residents may attract Part XIII withholding tax (generally 25% under the Income Tax Act, reduced to 5-15% under applicable tax treaties, including the Canada-US Treaty). No minimum duration for capital to remain in Canada and no forced conversion at prescribed rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline applies to export receipts or foreign-currency earnings. Canadian exporters and service providers may retain proceeds in foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I or II banks, may freely convert to CAD, and are not required to report routine receipts to the Bank of Canada. Only statistical reporting to Statistics Canada under the International Transactions Reporting System (threshold commonly CAD 1,000,000 for direct investment flows) may apply, and that is data reporting rather than an exchange-control permit requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Foreign exchange administration is not centralised in a single control authority comparable to other jurisdictions; relevant mandates are distributed among the Bank of Canada (monetary authority and Retail Payment Activities Act registration authority), Department of Finance Canada (policy), Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC, AML/CFT), and Statistics Canada (balance-of-payments and International Transactions Reporting System).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium as a standalone treasury rail. Canada does not recognise the EU/UK-style Electronic Money Institution licence with passporting rights. A non-resident EMI must either register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) directly with the Bank of Canada if it provides payment functions in Canada, or operate through an authorised Canadian payment service provider. The RPAA registration is activity-based and requires a Canadian establishment for full compliance with safeguarding, operational risk, and segregation obligations. A foreign EMI can therefore supplement, but not replace, a Schedule I bank account; using an offshore EMI for core treasury flows creates FINTRAC reporting complexity, sub-outsourcing risk, and FX inefficiency. Recommended hierarchy: Schedule I bank as anchor, licensed Canadian PSP (e.g., fintech subsidiaries of the Big Six banks) for alternative acquirer flows, and offshore EMI only for niche SaaS vendor payouts.
