Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (MyBA) portal; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement, with provincial policy oversight by Alberta Treasury Board and Finance, Tax and Revenue Administration.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation obtains its statutory tax identifier by applying for a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO), by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or through the integrated Newly Registered Business process at incorporation. Event-based enrolment adds program accounts (RT for GST/HST, RC for payroll deductions, RP for pension plan, etc.). The GST/HST registration effective date must precede the first taxable supply made in Canada; the applicant must provide non-resident proof (foreign certificate of incorporation, registered office address outside Canada, director information) and may use the simplified non-resident registration pathway for digital-economy supplies. The same BN serves as the corporate income tax account number for federal and Alberta tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under Canadian federal law; a Business Corporation is statutorily taxed at the entity level. Elections relevant to a cross-border DevOps/cloud structure include: Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 273 elections for closely related corporations and joint ventures, ETA joint venture nil-consideration elections, Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 216 election for non-resident rental income, ITA s. 217 election for non-resident employment income, and certain administrative designations for non-resident corporations under ITA s. 219. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory statutory provisions (no election) introduced in federal Budget implementation legislation, aligning with BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (or a single quarter); for non-resident digital-economy suppliers, the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies to taxable supplies made to Canadian recipients, with mandatory registration applicable since July 1, 2021 where the small-supplier limit is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Canada qualify for 0% GST/HST under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1, provided export is substantiated. Supplies of services to a non-resident qualify under s. 7 where the service is not rendered to an individual while present in Canada, is not in respect of Canadian real property, and is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. DevOps/cloud database, API monitoring, and infrastructure services supplied to non-resident customers generally qualify for zero-rating when non-resident status is documented and the services are not consumed in Canada.

  • CBSA Form B13A Export Declaration (for physical exports) or equivalent Canada Customs Invoice / automated export reporting
  • Commercial contract and detailed service invoice stating the non-resident recipient and the place of supply
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or consolidated shipping evidence (for exported goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/ACH payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Non-resident status documentation obtained at the time of supply (customer certificate of residence / foreign TIN)
  • API usage and service-delivery records evidencing offshore consumption and absence of Canadian use

No real-time fiscal clearance, invoice serialization, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. CRA mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns (NETFILE) and electronic remittance for large registrants (annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million). Canada announced a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing standard built on the Peppol network (2024); CRA offers web services and APIs for GST/HST registration, return filing, and data exchange, but taxpayers remain responsible for invoice issuance and record retention.

CRA may deny zero-rating on audit and reassess within the normal 4-year GST/HST reassessment period under ETA s. 298; the period is unlimited where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect exists. Substantiation is reviewed retrospectively, and recovered refunds attract statutory interest from the date the refund was paid. Penalties include the ETA s. 285.1 false-statement penalty (greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax sought to be avoided), gross negligence penalties, and, for egregious cases, criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 (evasion) exposing fines up to 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment. Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is the internal compliance benchmark, and failure to document export proceeds within that window materially increases the risk of zero-rating clawback and penalty assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Canada operates a free-floating CAD system with no exchange controls on current or capital accounts. Dividends, management fees, royalties and capital repatriation of profits may be remitted without prior approval, subject only to FINTRAC large-transaction reporting (C$10,000 threshold) and any required substantiation to the remitting bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements exist in Canada. Exporters may retain 100% of export receipts in any Currencies, with no prescribed remittance timeline or conversion ratio. All CAD clearing flows through the national settlement infrastructure automatically upon bank receipt.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank); foreign exchange and trade policy administered by the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Stripe Treasury) can support USD/GBP/EUR collection and ACH disbursements but cannot legally substitute for a Canadian financial institution for CAD-denominated clearing, payroll, or statutory remittances. They must be registered as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC or hold an equivalent exemption; otherwise their use in a Canadian treasury is limited to subsidiary cash flows and vendor payments in foreign currencies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for transactions with non-residents (triggering Form T7 filing). For Master File and Local File: MNE group consolidated revenue ≥ CAD 1 billion and Canadian entity transactions with non-residents exceeding CAD 5 million (services) or CAD 10 million (goods).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles. Treaty benefits may be denied under Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clauses if the entity lacks economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under s.227.1 if they fail to deduct, withhold, or remit amounts.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided, plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for negligence or fraud if they knowingly participate in the scheme.
Transfer Pricing Penalties

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net adjustment amount if the adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable if they knowingly participate in transfer pricing misstatements.
AML/Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 per violation for corporations.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for violations if they knowingly participate or fail to implement compliance measures.
