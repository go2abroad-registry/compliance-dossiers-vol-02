Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); primary portals are Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and Represent a Client. Alberta corporate registrations are administered separately by Alberta Corporate Registry.

E-government filing portal

A corporation carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and register applicable program accounts, including an RT GST/HST account and, where applicable, an RC corporate income tax account. The applicant submits incorporation or constitutional documents, registered office and principal business information, directors and ownership details, activities, expected taxable supplies, accounting periods, and authorized representative information through CRA Business Registration Online or the prescribed non-resident registration process. A non-resident entity without a Canadian permanent establishment may register for GST/HST where it makes taxable supplies in Canada, including supplies subject to the non-resident digital-economy rules. CRA may request identity, incorporation, banking, contracts, customer, platform, and business-activity evidence. Canada does not issue a separate national VAT number distinct from the BN; the GST/HST registration number is the BN followed by the RT program identifier. A Canadian corporation generally also receives or applies for an RC corporate income tax account. Alberta does not impose a provincial sales tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian corporations are generally taxed as separate legal entities and have no U.S.-style check-the-box election. A corporation may elect specific statutory treatments where available, such as eligible capital or accounting-period elections, but these do not convert the corporation into a flow-through entity. Partnerships are generally fiscally transparent, subject to partnership and anti-avoidance rules. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules can deny deductions or impose inclusion where arrangements exploit differences in the tax treatment of entities or instruments between Canada and another jurisdiction. A non-resident corporation remains subject to Canadian tax on income earned in Canada and may have withholding, filing, and permanent-establishment obligations under domestic law and an applicable tax treaty.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated supplies, generally measured over a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. Registration can be required earlier where the small-supplier threshold is exceeded or where special non-resident digital-economy rules apply. Certain public service bodies and financial institutions have different thresholds or rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated where the supplier exports or ships them outside Canada and satisfies the GST export conditions. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated only where the statutory exported-service requirements are met. For services, the supplier should establish that the recipient is a non-resident, that the service is supplied to and for the use of the non-resident, and that no exclusion applies. Important exclusions and limitations can apply to services relating to real property or tangible personal property in Canada, personal services physically performed in Canada, services connected with litigation or regulatory proceedings in Canada, and certain intermediary or financial services. Digital language-learning software or subscriptions require a specific place-of-supply and customer-status analysis; customer residence, billing address, IP or geolocation evidence, app-store contractual role, payment records, and usage facts should be retained. Zero-rating does not automatically eliminate GST registration, filing, evidence, or input-tax-credit requirements.

  • Canada Customs export declaration or applicable electronic export reporting record, where required by the goods-export rules
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, contract, and evidence identifying the non-resident customer
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier record, or other carrier documentation evidencing export from Canada, where goods are involved
  • Proof of delivery or receipt outside Canada, where relevant
  • For offshore services or digital supplies: customer residency evidence, contractual terms, billing and payment records, platform settlement statements, geolocation or account data, and evidence that statutory zero-rating conditions and exclusions were considered
  • Bank or payment processor records supporting receipt of consideration; an inward remittance certificate is not generally a Canadian statutory requirement
  • GST/HST returns, general ledger, tax invoices, credit notes, refund claims, and input-tax-credit support

Canada has no general real-time GST invoice-clearance, government e-invoicing, or mandatory digital tax-stamping regime for ordinary commercial supplies in Alberta. Registrants must issue compliant GST invoices or other documentary evidence, retain electronic books and records, and provide prescribed information when requested by CRA. Electronic records must remain reliable, complete, accessible, and auditable. Platform billing through Apple or Google does not by itself eliminate the supplier's GST obligations; the tax treatment depends on the supplier, platform, customer location, place-of-supply rules, and applicable digital-economy provisions. CRA's electronic filing and payment requirements may apply based on registrant status and filing period.

Canada generally has no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. CRA may reassess GST incorrectly zero-rated supplies, deny related input tax credits or refunds, and recover tax, applicable penalties, and compound daily interest. Failure to file or late filing can trigger statutory penalties; false statements or omissions may attract a penalty generally equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the understated tax or overstated credit, while gross-negligence penalties can generally equal 50% of the resulting tax or credit adjustment. Fraudulent claims may lead to criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. Customs non-compliance can result in seizure, monetary penalties, reassessment of duties and taxes, and penalties under the Customs Act and related legislation. Documentation must be retained for the applicable statutory retention period, commonly at least six years after the end of the relevant year, subject to exceptions and CRA requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted CAD convertibility on both current and capital accounts. No exchange-control licensing, quota, or approval regime exists under the Currency Act or Financial Administration Act. Profit remittance is free, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory; 15% treaty dividends / 5% for qualifying corporate shareholders) and transfer-pricing documentation. Capital repatriation and liquidation distributions require no regulatory clearance, conditional on solvency and creditor-protection compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has imposed no mandatory surrender/conversion ratio or statutory timeline on export proceeds since 1951; exporters retain receipts in any currency in domestic or offshore accounts. Only statistical/export-reporting obligations apply (via the Canadian Online Export Reporting portal) for shipments above C$2,000 under the Export and Import Permits Act; no monetary conversion or repatriation duty attaches.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); the Minister of Finance holds overall authority for official reserves (Exchange Fund Account), with OSFI supervising banks and FINTRAC administering the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23.0% combined (federal 15.0% + Alberta provincial 8.0%) for general business corporations; 11.0% combined (federal 9.0% + Alberta 2.0%) for Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII tax) on dividends paid or credited to a Canadian corporation's non-resident shareholders, unless reduced by an income tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. For a December 31 year-end, the filing due date is June 30 of the following year. The balance-due payment is due 2 months after year-end generally; CCPCs eligible for the SBD have an extended balance-due date of 3 months.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 200 - Corporation Identification Legal name, business number (BN), registered office address, jurisdiction of incorporation, provincial code (AB), and tax year start/end dates. For a Canadian corporation with non-resident shareholders, confirm the corporation's Canadian residency for tax purposes; a non-resident director is permissible but must authorize filings through the CRA's authorized representative process. Late filing penalty under s. 162(1): 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month for up to 12 months. If the corporation is assessed tax of more than CAD 5,000 and the return is late, additional penalty for repeated failure may reach 10%. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 annual aggregate value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions (purchases, sales, services, royalties, interest) triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (TP Memo 17); a consolidated group with global revenue exceeding CAD 750,000,000 for its Canadian parent or CAD 25,000,000 aggregate? Canada's Country-by-Country Report (Form RC4649) threshold is CAD 750,000,000 in consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the United Arab Emirates-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, the Canada Revenue Agency invokes the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245) and treaty anti-abuse rules to prevent artificial arrangements. An Alberta EdTech corporation must maintain real operational substance (staff, management, IP ownership decisions, contractual negotiations, QRTR? no, but physical oversight in Alberta) to avoid being treated as a conduit. The corporation must also satisfy the 'business carried on in Canada' test and, for treaty claims, may be denied benefits if there is no genuine commercial rationale or functions in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (s. 239)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s. 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for corporate withholding tax and GST/HST remittances under s. 227.1; where tax evasion is criminal, a court may order personal forfeiture, travel restrictions, and civil restitution against directors who participated.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 10% of the transfer pricing adjustment (net of any secondary adjustment) under s. 247(3), irrespective of fraud; no penalty if the taxpayer made 'reasonable efforts' to document the pricing at filing time.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed personally if they authorized the transactions and the corporation is insolvent; for gross negligence in TP reporting, directors may also be flagged in CRA's 'Name and Shame' program for egregious tax adjustments.
Failure to File / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax + 1% per full month (up to 12 months). Knowingly making a false statement: 50% gross negligence penalty of understated tax; access to justice under the Income Tax Act to seize unregistered assets.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for employer source deductions (payroll) and GST/HST net tax if the corporation fails to remit; CRA may issue notice of assessment to directors and obtain a travel ban if tax debt exceeds CAD 100,000 under s. 227.11.
Shell Company / Non-Economic Substance (Treaty Shopping)

Statutory sanction: CRA GAAR reassessment under s. 245; treaty benefits denied under OECD BEPS LOB provisions (where applicable); recharacterization of dividends/royalties as taxable Canadian-source income; 50% gross negligence penalty for misrepresentation of substance.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the corporation to issue misleading statutory declarations may be criminally charged for counseling corporate fraud; civil restitution orders may pierce the corporate veil, and directors can be prohibited from leaving Canada pending tax recovery.
AML / KYC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for failure to register or report; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment if wilful blindness.

Director personal liability: Directors of a non-registered MSB can be personally fined; directors can be jailed for up to 5 years for knowingly permitting transactions with sanctioned entities; personal travel bans may be imposed for failing to cooperate.
