Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal; Alberta provincial corporate income tax is collected federally by the CRA under a tax collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or by submitting Form RC1. The BN serves as the TIN; separate program accounts are created for corporate income tax (RC account), GST/HST (RT account), payroll, and import/export. Non-resident entities may apply by mail/fax to the CRA International and Non-Resident Division and must provide legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, directors, registered office, and an appointed agent for service.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Canadian-resident corporations pay federal and provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income, with a territorial carve-out for active foreign business income under certain treaty and exempt-surplus rules. — A foreign entity carrying on business or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or by submitting Form RC1. The BN serves as the TIN; separate program accounts are created for corporate income tax (RC account), GST/HST (RT account), payroll, and import/export. Non-resident entities may apply by mail/fax to the CRA International and Non-Resident Division and must provide legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, directors, registered office, and an appointed agent for service.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Canadian-resident corporations pay federal and provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income, with a territorial carve-out for active foreign business income under certain treaty and exempt-surplus rules.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Canada. Relevant elections include: GST/HST closely related corporation election under ETA s.156 (nil-valued taxable supplies), ITA s.216 election for non-residents renting or disposing of Canadian real property, and ITA s.85 rollover elections for transfer of property to a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies made in Canada (excluding zero-rated supplies) triggers mandatory GST/HST registration; the threshold applies to the permanent establishment or any resident/non-resident supplier except where supplies are made in the course of a commercial activity with a Canadian nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when exported by the supplier or via a common carrier under a contract for export, delivered outside Canada, and supported by documentary proof. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident if they qualify as exported services (performed wholly outside Canada) or relate to real property situated outside Canada; for SaaS/fintech tracking services supplied to non-residents, the place-of-supply rules and recipient non-residency determine eligibility.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A or Canada Customs Automated Export Declaration record)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing dispatch from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice and signed supply contract identifying the non-resident recipient
  • Freight/packing documentation or service performance records substantiating foreign destination

No national real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory XML clearance, or digital stamping regime is in effect. CRA accepts electronic invoicing and documents in common formats; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via NETFILE or EDI by most registrants, and all records must be kept in CRA-prescribed electronic format.

CRA may reassess to deny zero-rating and recapture uncollected GST/HST with interest if documentation is inadequate. Penalties include those for false statements or evasion under the Excise Tax Act (s.285: 25%–50% of net tax; s.286 civil penalties), plus criminal fines and imprisonment for fraudulent claims; clawback is retrospective without statutory limitation in cases of misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains IMF Article VIII status, ensuring unrestricted current account convertibility for trade, services, and profit remittances. Capital account transactions are liberalized; prior Bank of Canada approval is required only for direct investments and portfolio investments exceeding CAD 100 million. Profit remittances by Canadian corporations to foreign shareholders are permitted without quantitative limits, subject to Income Tax Act withholding tax obligations and beneficial ownership reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canadian exporters face no mandatory surrender regime or fixed conversion ratios. Foreign exchange proceeds must be converted to CAD at prevailing market rates through authorized dealers. Voluntary surrender is encouraged for statistical purposes only. All receipts require timely Canada Revenue Agency reporting and Bank of Canada notification if thresholds are met.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined federal-Alberta general corporate rate (federal 15% + Alberta 8%); 11% combined rate for a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) on active business income within the CAD 500,000 small business deduction limit (federal 9% + Alberta 2%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a resident Canadian corporation to non-residents, subject to reduction under an applicable income tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No absolute statutory exemption exists, but CRA administrative practice and s. 247(4) ITA require complete and accurate contemporaneous documentation for all non-arm's-length cross-border transactions; the 10% transfer pricing penalty is generally not applied where the aggregate annual value of such transactions does not exceed CAD 1,000,000 and reasonable efforts have been made. Corporations exceeding CAD 50 million in gross revenue or assets face heightened audit scrutiny and are expected to prepare Master File and Local File documentation substantially aligned with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE; substance is instead enforced through (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ss. 245(2)-(4) ITA), which denies tax benefits arising from avoidance transactions lacking a bona fide business purpose, (ii) the sham doctrine and substance-over-form principles, (iii) the transfer pricing recharacterization power under s. 247(2), which permits the CRA to substitute an arm's-length amount where the actual transaction would not have been entered into by arm's-length parties, and (iv) the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) individual-with-significant-control (ISC) register, which requires disclosure of ultimate beneficial ownership. A letter-box holding company with no Canadian employees, records, premises, or substantive board activity is vulnerable to GAAR reassessment, transfer pricing adjustment, and administrative dissolution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (s. 239 ITA)

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, fines up to double the summary amounts and imprisonment up to 5 years; the corporation also faces full reassessment, interest, and the s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the evasion face joint and several liability, civil restitution orders, and criminal prosecution; issuance of an arrest warrant can trigger Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) watchlists, passport refusal or revocation, and effective travel bans.
Gross Negligence and Deficient Transfer Pricing Documentation (ss. 163(2) and 247(3) ITA)

Statutory sanction: s. 163(2): civil penalty of 50% of the understated tax; s. 247(3): penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where documentation is absent and reasonable efforts are not demonstrated, plus interest and reassessment of both federal and Alberta tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may not be automatically assessed under s. 163(2) for the corporation's civil penalty, but they are exposed to personal liability where they were the directing mind of the misrepresentation, indemnity claims by the corporation, and joint liability under s. 227.1 for any unremitted withholdings that facilitated the scheme.
Shell Company Structure and GAAR Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under GAAR (s. 245) disallowing the tax benefit; transfer pricing recharacterization under s. 247; penalties up to 10% of the adjusted net amount; deregistration or administrative dissolution proceedings by Corporations Canada or Service Alberta; separate penalties for failure to maintain the CBCA ISC beneficial ownership register.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who sign false returns or sanction sham structures face joint liability for the resulting tax debt; CBCA s. 21.4 offences: on summary conviction a fine up to CAD 200,000 and/or imprisonment up to 6 months; on indictment a fine up to CAD 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; travel interdiction and extradition exposure may follow criminal indictment.
Unremitted Source Deductions and Non-Resident Withholding Tax (s. 227.1 ITA)

Statutory sanction: Withheld amounts are held in a deemed trust for the Crown; the corporation is liable for the unremitted amounts plus interest and penalties, with a 10% penalty for repeated failure to remit; CRA may issue a certificate of levy against bank accounts and receivables.

Director personal liability: Under s. 227.1 ITA, directors are personally and jointly and severally liable for the unremitted amounts unless they exercised the degree of care, diligence, and skill of a reasonably prudent person; the due diligence defense is unavailable where the director knowingly participated in the non-remittance, and civil restitution, garnishment, and travel restrictions may follow in cases of criminal fraud.
