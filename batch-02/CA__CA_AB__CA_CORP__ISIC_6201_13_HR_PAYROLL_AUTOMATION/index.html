Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Corporate Registry Division, Service Alberta (Alberta Corporate Registry), acting as registrar under the ABCA; accredited Registry Agents for electronic filings.

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act (Alberta), RSA 2000, c B-9 (ABCA), ss. 6–13, 15, 20–22, 101, 106, 127, and Part 17; supplementary rules under the Corporations Information Act and Regulation 119/2000 corporate registry practice.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1 – Articles of Incorporation (electronic filing through the Corporate Registry Portal or via an accredited Registry Agent), supported by a NUANS Name Search Report.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the ABCA; a non-offering corporation may incorporate with no escrowed initial capital. A nominal share subscription (e.g., C$1.00 per common share) is recommended only to establish solvency and facilitate corporate bank account opening; no escrow account rule applies.

Funding & unlock path: Under the Alberta Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) Act and the Safety Codes Act: obtain a municipal occupancy permit confirming the registered office meets structural and fire-code requirements; secure a fire safety inspection/clearance certificate from the local fire department where the leased premises hosts any staff; register as an employer with the Workers' Compensation Board (WCB-Alberta) within 7 days of first employee; for the payroll platform's server/co-location room, certify electrical load, emergency lighting, and fire suppression per the Alberta Fire Code; a business continuity plan consistent with PIPEDA security safeguards is recommended.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the ABCA; a non-offering corporation may incorporate with no escrowed initial capital. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — national tax administration and Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (www.canada.ca/business-registration-online) for T2 corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll source deductions, and excise acts; Alberta Ministry of Finance — Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) — competent authority for Alberta Corporate Income Tax (AT1 return), as Alberta is one of three provinces (AB, ON, QC) that assesses and collects its own provincial corporate income tax outside the CRA tax-collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta Business Corporation: (i) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Business Corporations Act, completing name search, articles of incorporation, and registered office in Alberta; (ii) obtain the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) via the BRO portal — the BN is the single commercial identifier for all federal tax accounts; (iii) activate program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll source deductions), RM (import/export reporting where physical deployment applies); (iv) register the Alberta corporate tax account with the Alberta TRA and file AT1 annually; (v) enroll in CRA 'My Business Account' and Alberta TRA e-services. Non-resident persons may own 100% of shares without a minimum capital requirement; director-residency rules under applicable corporate statute must be verified before filing. The CRA issues TIN-equivalent BN confirmation upon registration.

No pass-through or US-style Check-the-Box classification is available. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Canadian Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a resident taxpayer taxed on worldwide income. A foreign-controlled Canadian corporation is not a 'Canadian-controlled private corporation' and is therefore ineligible for the small business deduction; combined headline federal (15% general) and Alberta (8% general) corporate income tax rate is approximately 23%. No pass-through or US-style Check-the-Box classification is available. A non-resident corporation carrying on business through an Alberta permanent establishment is instead subject to Part XIV branch tax deemed-dividend regime on after-tax PE income.

Available election forms

No Check-the-Box election in Canada; classification follows the corporate statute. Elections with tax-planning relevance include: (i) GST/HST Quick Method (ETA s. 227) and annual/quarterly filing-frequency elections; (ii) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations to deem taxable supplies nil; (iii) ETA s. 167 joint election for business-transfer supplies; (iv) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident rental income and ITA s. 217 election for non-resident employment income; (v) hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4–18.6 (OECD BEPS Action 2) limiting deduction/non-inclusion mismatches through hybrid entities, hybrid instruments, and branch mismatches; (vi) Part XIV election concerning treaty-protected branch profits. All elections are time-sensitive and generally require prescription by CRA forms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148: a person becomes a GST/HST registrant when worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports, exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters. CA_CORP's payroll SaaS subscription revenue will exceed this threshold at first commercial invoicing, making registration mandatory; non-resident digital-economy suppliers of online services to Canadian consumers are captured by the same CAD 30,000 simplified-registration metric.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under ETA s. 6 (export of goods) and ETA s. 9 (supply of services to non-resident persons). The automated payroll and HR-compliance SaaS platform (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 541214) qualifies for 0% GST when: (a) the recipient is a non-resident of Canada; (b) the service does not relate to Canadian real property or to tangible personal property situated in Canada when performed; and (c) the software is delivered via the Internet and predominantly accessed/used outside Canada. The supplier must invoice 'GST/HST Zero-Rated Export — 0%', exclude GST from billings, and retain evidence of export; the supplier may claim all input tax credits attributable to the exported supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration via CERS (Canada: electronic export declaration for tangible goods above CAD 2,000; for pure SaaS payroll service exports, the equivalent document is a signed master services agreement/SOW identifying the non-resident recipient and offshore payroll function)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (for physical shipments; for digital payroll SaaS, the substitute is server access/usage logs with IP geolocation and a services delivery certificate evidencing platform activation and use outside Canada)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT-and-FX remittance evidence (Canada: CRA accepts SWIFT MT103 confirmations, bank statements, FX hedging/currency-conversion records, and intercompany settlement evidence that export consideration was received from the non-resident)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory CRA API pre-authorization exists in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary; a valid GST/HST invoice must show the registrant's BN, invoice date, description of the supply, tax status (5% GST, zero-rated, or exempt), and total amount (ETA s. 223). The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Interoperability Framework leverages PEPPOL, but PEPPOL exchange is a commercial/technical choice rather than a tax-validity condition; CRA audits accept electronic records in EDI, XML, PDF, or system-generated formats.

CRA audit scrutiny targets economic substance, place-of-supply analysis, excessive input tax credit claims, and transfer pricing of the SaaS subscription under ITA s. 247. Clawback mechanics: GST/HST assessments may be issued within 4 years of the return under ETA s. 298, and income tax reassessments within 3 years (4 years for a 'large corporation') under ITA s. 152(4); limitation periods are indefinitely extended where misrepresentation or fraud exists. Penalties: 50% gross-negligence penalty for false statements (ITA s. 163(2) and ETA s. 285); failure-to-register/file/remit penalties and interest (ETA ss. 280–284); GAAR (ITA s. 245 / ETA s. 274) may re-characterize circular or artificial export schemes. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day treasury deadline without an arm's-length explanation may cause reclassification as Canadian-source service revenue, imposition of Part XIII withholding, and retrospective denial of the export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current- and capital-account convertibility. Canada abolished exchange controls in 1951; the Currency Act (RSC 1985, c. C-52) imposes no restrictions on inflows, outflows, dividend remittances, or interest and fee repatriation. The only material hurdles are tax compliance: Part XIII withholding tax at 25% (5% to 15% under Canada-US treaty) on dividends and certain cross-border payments (NR4 return), the 1.5:1 debt-to-equity thin-capitalization limit on related-party interest deductibility, and s. 247 transfer-pricing documentation for all intercompany transactions. No central bank or governmental FX approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or minimum-holding-period rule for export proceeds; exporters may invoice and retain receipts in any freely convertible currency. Compliance is limited to CBSA export declaration filing (B13A/EUI), FINTRAC reporting of electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more, and six-year record retention under s. 230 of the Income Tax Act and s. 40 of the Customs Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada (exchange policy), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federal deposit-taker supervision, and FINTRAC for AML/ATF and cross-border EFT reporting. Since September 2024, the Bank of Canada also supervises retail payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA).

Legal constraints: Canadian payment service providers must register with the Bank of Canada under the RPAA, register with FINTRAC as an MSB under the PCMLTFA, and comply with PIPEDA for payroll data; payroll direct deposits must ultimately clear through ACSS via a Schedule I/II bank.
