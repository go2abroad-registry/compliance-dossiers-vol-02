Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — national tax administration for corporate income tax, GST/HST, and payroll; authorized portal is CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals-gst-hst.html); Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (RT account) from the CRA. The non-resident applies using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) submitted online through the CRA's Non-Resident GST/HST registration portal or by mail to the applicable CRA tax centre. The CRA validates the foreign incorporation certificate, Canadian agent for service, and place of business in Canada before issuing the BN. Registration is mandatory when the non-resident makes taxable (non-zero-rated) supplies exceeding the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold in a calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters, or when supplying specified digital services to Canadian consumers; voluntary registration is available for zero-rated exporters to claim input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Alberta Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Canada); no pass-through eligibility exists. It is subject to federal and Alberta provincial corporate income tax at the entity level on worldwide income; shareholders are taxed only on dividends. Territorial scope is limited by the Canada-US tax treaty for treaty-resident shareholders but the corporation remains fully resident if incorporated or centrally managed and controlled in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) non-resident corporation's section 216 election to file a return for Canadian rental income on a net basis; (ii) treaty-protected income election under subsection 115(1); (iii) GST/HST Quick Method (Form GST74) for suppliers with annual taxable supplies up to CAD 400,000; (iv) GST/HST Simplified Method for input tax credits; (v) joint election for closely related corporations (Form GST25) to treat certain supplies as being for nil consideration; and (vi) hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.2 and 18.21 that disallow deductions for hybrid mismatch outcomes, consistent with OECD BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold measured over a single calendar quarter or four consecutive calendar quarters. Non-resident suppliers of 'specified digital services' (including API access, data feeds, and software automation) are deemed to make supplies in Canada and must register with the CRA once gross revenue from Canadian consumers exceeds CAD 30,000 in a 12-month period; after registration, GST at 5% is chargeable on business-to-consumer supplies, while business-to-business supply chains may be zero-rated under the non-resident B2B rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V: (i) section 7 zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST, except services performed in Canada in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; (ii) section 21 zero-rates supplies of intangible personal property (data, software licenses, API subscriptions) to such non-residents. For the subject web-scraping/proxy API gateway, exports to foreign enterprise customers that are non-residents, unregistered for GST/HST, and consuming the data pipeline wholly outside Canada are zero-rated. Domestic Canadian sales or sales to GST/HST-registered non-residents are taxable at 5%.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) / Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) — applicable to physical goods, not digital services
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill with Customs cargo control number — used only where hardware or media is physically exported
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/ACH confirmation evidencing receipt of export consideration in the corporate account
  • Export contract, commercial invoice, and statement of work identifying the non-resident customer and offshore consumption
  • Digital delivery log and payment gateway settlement report (Stripe/USDC) evidencing the supply made and fee received

Canada does not require real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based government pre-clearance of invoices. The CRA mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE or EDI) for all registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,500,000, for all non-resident registrants, and for registrants using a third-party intermediary. The Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing framework is voluntary and currently limited to business-to-government transactions; commercial B2B export invoices may be issued electronically and stored digitally, but must be produced on request within CRA retention periods.

CRA may reassess zero-rated exports within the standard 4-year limitation period, extended to 10 years for misrepresentation or fraudulent omission. If the non-resident status or offshore consumption is unsupported, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5%. Penalties include: (i) failure to collect/remit GST — 5% of net tax plus 10% on repeat failures; (ii) false statements or omissions — 5% to 10% of the tax amount; (iii) gross negligence penalty under Excise Tax Act section 285 — up to 50% of the tax sought; (iv) daily compound interest at prescribed rates; (v) criminal prosecution for fraud with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. Exporters must retain all substantiation for at least six years from the end of the reporting period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There is no exchange-control licensing or profit-remittance approval. Dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees may be paid to non-residents without Bank of Canada authorization, but are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) at 25%, subject to treaty reduction to 5% or 15% in typical circumstances. The only practical hurdle is the legal and beneficial-ownership documentation required by the payor bank to substantiate treaty relief.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Canada. Exporters of software, data-pipeline, or API services are free to invoice in foreign currency, retain the receipts in CAD or foreign-currency accounts, or use proceeds to settle foreign payables. Record-keeping obligations under Income Tax Act subsection 230(4) remain applicable, and any wire or cash transaction of CAD 10,000 or more is reportable to FINTRAC as a large-value transaction record.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign exchange policy and prudential supervision administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money-laundering transaction reporting enforcement by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-Low as a primary liquidity rail. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital cross-border banking platforms (e.g., Mercury, Airwallex, Wise Business) are not Canadian Schedule I banks and cannot directly clear through Payments Canada. A foreign EMI is viable only as an auxiliary foreign-currency collection or disbursement vehicle if it holds a FINTRAC money-services-business registration or acts exclusively as a payment facilitator with settlement into a Canadian chartered bank account. An unlicensed foreign EMI that transmits funds or maintains a Canadian payment account on behalf of an Alberta corporation may itself require registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. The recommended structure is to use Stripe as a payment facilitator for card/USDC settlement into a Canadian operating account and to restrict EMI accounts to USD/EUR trade receipts where delivery into Canada is impractical.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder equity capital should be injected by SWIFT MT103 international wire or Fedwire-equivalent channel into the Alberta corporation's Schedule I bank account in CAD or USD. The SWIFT purpose code should be CFDI (Capital Financial Direct Investment), with the OECD BPM6 direct-investment equity category 510871 cited in the remittance narrative. Banks will require the receiving account name to match the corporate account exactly. The supporting verification package must include the board resolution authorizing the share issuance, executed share subscription agreement, certificate of corporate status, register of shareholders, and source-of-funds documentation from the remitting bank. If the capital originates in USDC or other cryptocurrency, conversion to CAD must occur through a licensed Canadian crypto-to-fiat gateway before the wire; direct blockchain settlement to a corporate bank account is not a compliant clearing rail.Operational disbursements use the following clearing rails: (i) domestic CAD payments via Payments Canada ACSS/EFT credits or Lynx real-time rail; (ii) international supplier and vendor settlements via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 COVER through the bank's correspondent network; (iii) commercial letters of credit via the issuing bank's trade desk for large software or data-infrastructure procurement contracts; and (iv) small-value global API labour or contractor payments via licensed money-services-business payout networks or Stripe Connect, provided those payouts are denominated in local currency and remitted from the CAD treasury account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined Alberta rate 23% (federal 15% after General Rate Reduction + Alberta general 8%). A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) claiming the Small Business Deduction pays 11% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (federal 9% + Alberta 2%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII dividend withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders (ITA s. 212(2)); DTA relief: Canada-US and Canada-UK treaties reduce the rate to 15% for portfolio holders and 5% where the recipient is a company owning at least 10% of the payer's voting shares; Canada-US treaty provides 0% for qualifying pension funds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory minimum threshold in the Income Tax Act; the CRA expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (local file and, where applicable, master file) where aggregate cross-border non-arm's-length transactions exceed approximately CAD 1,000,000 per taxation year; Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation comparable to UAE ESR; substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245, penalty of 25% of any tax benefit), the sham and agency-PE doctrines, treaty limitation-of-benefits provisions (Canada-US DTA Art. XXIX A), thin capitalization limits (1.5:1 debt-to-equity for non-resident shareholders under s. 18(4)), and the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL), which caps net interest deductions at 30% of tax EBITDA for large corporate groups.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Wilful)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239: fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment for up to 5 years; gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under s. 163(2); CRA asset seizure, bank account freezes, and criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized concealment face personal civil liability for the full deficiency, joint and several penalties, criminal fines, asset confiscation, imprisonment, and US inadmissibility or border flags restricting international travel.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at arm's-length prices under s. 247(2); 10% transfer pricing penalty under s. 247(3) where the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts; daily compound interest on reassessed amounts; risk of juridical double taxation and CRA audit referral.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false transfer pricing documentation may be pursued personally for the 10% penalty where they directed the mispricing; civil restitution follows corporate veil-piercing where funds were diverted.
Shell Company / Sham Structure

Statutory sanction: CRA may ignore the entity under the sham doctrine or GAAR, reassess the foreign parent on Canadian-source income, deny treaty benefits, impose the 25% GAAR penalty, and apply Part XIV branch tax of 25% on unrelieved Canadian-source income.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors are personally liable for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholding under s. 227.1; travel bans and extradition risk arise where laundering of mispriced revenue is proven; long-term disqualification from Alberta directorships is possible.
