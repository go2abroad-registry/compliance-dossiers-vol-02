Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), under the Minister of National Revenue; business registration through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and CRA My Business Account. Alberta's provincial corporate registry (Alberta Corporate Account Registry / CORES) administers incorporation and extra-provincial registration; Alberta corporate income tax is collected by the CRA for the Province of Alberta under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Alberta Business Corporations Act (or file an extra-provincial registration to carry on business in Alberta). Step 2: Register with CRA via Business Registration Online (BRO) or file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) together with the articles of incorporation, registered office address in Canada, director and officer details, fiscal year-end, and principal business activities (NAICS 541199 / ISIC 6201). Step 3: CRA issues the nine-digit Business Number and creates the statutory program accounts (RC for federal and provincial corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll where applicable). Step 4: A non-resident corporation must obtain an extra-provincial Alberta corporate registry account, designate a Canadian representative or agent for service, and file T2 returns using its Canadian permanent establishment allocation; non-resident GST/HST registration can be completed online through the simplified non-resident registration path.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A corporation incorporated in Canada is resident under ITA s. 250(4) and taxable in Canada on worldwide income (ITA Part I). The federal general corporate rate is 15% and the Alberta general corporate rate is 8%, giving a combined general rate of 23%; the small business deduction (federal 9% and Alberta 2% applied to the first CAD 500,000 of active business income of a non-associated Canadian-controlled private corporation) gives a combined small business rate of 11%. Non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source income attributable to a permanent establishment in Canada and are subject to a 25% Part XIV branch tax on repatriated earnings (reduced by applicable tax treaties). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A corporation incorporated in Canada is resident under ITA s. 250(4) and taxable in Canada on worldwide income (ITA Part I). The federal general corporate rate is 15% and the Alberta general corporate rate is 8%, giving a combined general rate of 23%; the small business deduction (federal 9% and Alberta 2% applied to the first CAD 500,000 of active business income of a non-associated Canadian-controlled private corporation) gives a combined small business rate of 11%. Non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source income attributable to a permanent establishment in Canada and are subject to a 25% Part XIV branch tax on repatriated earnings (reduced by applicable tax treaties). Territorial scope: residents are taxed on worldwide income; non-residents are taxed on Canadian-source income only.

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity-classification election exists in Canada. Available elections and compliance mechanics: (i) associated-corporation declaration and allocation of the federal and Alberta small business limits among associated CCPCs (Form T2SCH4); (ii) ITA s. 20(12) election to deduct foreign taxes instead of claiming foreign tax credits; (iii) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident to file a net-basis Part I return for Canadian rental and property income instead of Part XIII withholding; (iv) ITA s. 217 election for non-resident employment income; (v) voluntary GST/HST registration election for small suppliers (ETA s. 240(3)); (vi) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2-18.8, effective July 1, 2022) which disallow deduction benefits in hybrid instrument and hybrid branch arrangements and require the prescribed Hybrid Mismatch Arrangement Information Return (CRA Form RC4710); no curative check-the-box-style election is available for hybrid mismatches.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under a free-float regime; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and imposes no exchange controls on either current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance from an Alberta subsidiary is unrestricted and requires no regulatory approval; the sole burden is Canadian withholding tax on dividends (Part XIII of the Income Tax Act): 25% statutory, reduced to 15% generally, and 5% under the Canada-US Tax Treaty where the parent owns at least 10% of voting shares. FATCA/CRS reporting and Part X.2 capital rules may apply, but no convertibility rationing, repatriation permit, or end-use certification exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no export proceeds surrender regime: there is no statutory repatriation timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or FX liquidation requirement under the Bank of Canada Act, Currency Act, or Customs Act. Export receipts may be retained freely in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian banks. The only compliance obligations are statistical: Canadian exporters of goods must file CBSA export declarations (G13 Canadian Export Reporting) for shipments valued above CAD 2,000 or for controlled/dutiable goods, while selected services exporters must answer Statistics Canada quarterly International Transactions in Services surveys.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange policy co-administered by the Department of Finance Canada (Exchange Fund Account); prudential supervision of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF administration by FINTRAC; Payments Canada operates Lynx and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Nium) may serve an Alberta corporation as auxiliary settlement and cross-border disbursement rails, but they are not licensed Canadian banks and cannot legally accept Canadian deposits or provide CDIC-insured operational accounts under the Bank Act. As of November 2024, foreign payment service providers processing Canadian retail payments must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and comply with FINTRAC MSB obligations; an EMI engaged in deposit-taking without a Canadian banking licence would violate the Bank Act s.545. Accordingly, EMIs are viable only for transactional FX/correspondent flow, never as the primary treasury depository.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate non-arm’s-length international transactions with non-residents triggers mandatory filing of Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm’s Length Transactions). Canada has not adopted formal Local File / Master File thresholds, but contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is expected for all such transactions, especially those exceeding CAD 2,000,000; failure to produce documentation within three months on CRA request may result in penalty exposure.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have separate Economic Substance Regulations like OFC jurisdictions; however, CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) and common law substance-over-form and sham doctrines. Transactions lacking commercial economic substance and performed solely for tax avoidance may be recharacterized, and treaty benefits may be denied under beneficial ownership or anti-treaty-shopping principles.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment for up to 2 years on indictment. Civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; CRA may reassess beyond the normal statute period where misrepresentation is due to neglect or fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions under s.227.1; CRA may seek civil personal judgment, freeze personal assets, obtain travel bans through criminal proceedings, and extradite individuals involved in cross-border tax evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny related-party deductions, recharacterize payments as a sham distribution under s.245 (GAAR), assess a 50% penalty under s.163(2), impose Part XIII withholding on deemed dividends, and deny treaty benefits under substance-over-form or beneficial ownership rules.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil if the company is a mere facade; directors can be held personally liable for the restored tax, interest, and penalties, and may face disqualification and restraint orders over personal property.
AML / Money Laundering Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalty of up to CAD 2,500,000 for repeated violations; Criminal Code s.462.31 money laundering offense carries up to 10 years imprisonment plus asset forfeiture. Failure to implement a compliance program may also lead to regulatory penalty for each infraction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct or participate in laundering proceeds can be personally charged; possible travel bans, asset restraint orders, extradition, civil restitution, and ineligibility to continue serving as a director in Canadian corporations.
