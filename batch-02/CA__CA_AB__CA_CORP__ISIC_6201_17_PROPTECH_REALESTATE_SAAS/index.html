Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), federal authority for GST/HST under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act and for Part I corporate income tax. Operational portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Alberta corporate income tax (Alberta Corporate Tax Act) is administered and collected by the CRA on behalf of Alberta; Alberta corporate registration is administered through Service Alberta / Alberta Registries (CORES).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (or federal CBCA with Alberta extra-provincial registration) via the Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES); certificate issued within 1-3 business days. Step 2: CRA automatically issues the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) upon incorporation confirmation. Step 3: Add the GST/HST program account (RT suffix) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; registration is mandatory if taxable supplies made in Canada (including zero-rated supplies) exceed C$30,000 over any four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 240); voluntary registration is available for input tax credit recovery. Step 4: Register the corporate income tax program account (RC suffix) - automatic for resident corporations; open CRA My Business Account and authorize a representative via a RepID. Step 5: For non-resident directors or shareholders, maintain an Alberta agent for service of process when applicable. No minimum capital, stamp duty, or notarization is required; typical turnaround is 3-10 business days for BN issuance plus GST account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer for federal and Alberta corporate income tax, with no flow-through treatment. For indirect tax purposes, the corporation is an independent GST/HST registrant. Territorial_Scope nuance: Alberta is a non-participating province (non-HST, no provincial sales tax); the combined indirect tax headline rate in Alberta is the 5% federal GST only. No tax-exempt free-zone status applies to any Alberta location.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box classification exists domestically; a Canadian corporation is treated as a per se corporation for US federal tax purposes (Treas. Reg. sec. 301.7701-2(b)(8)(i)). Relevant elections and mechanisms: (i) ITA s. 85(1) rollover election on property transfers to the corporation; (ii) ITA s. 89(14) eligible dividend designation; (iii) ITA s. 45(2) election to defer capital gains on real estate change of use; (iv) ETA s. 156 election for nil consideration on closely related intra-group supplies; (v) ETA s. 273(1) joint election for closely related GST registrants; (vi) ITA s. 261 functional-currency election aligning USD/ACH revenue streams. Hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance: ITA s. 18.4 (excess interest) and ss. 94.1-94.4 apply; no elective hybrid-neutrality regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 (taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, made in Canada over any four consecutive calendar quarters; supplies made outside Canada - such as zero-rated services relating to foreign real estate supplied to non-resident customers - are excluded from the threshold test). Voluntary GST/HST registration is permitted at any time; the simplified non-resident digital-services registration uses the same C$30,000 de minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Services supplied to a non-resident person are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 ETA unless: (i) the non-resident is a GST registrant; (ii) the service relates to real property situated in Canada; or (iii) the service relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. For this PropTech SaaS (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 531390 - property management and rental screening cloud), 0% GST applies to subscription services supplied to non-resident customers for non-Canadian real estate. If the platform supports Canadian real property, even for non-resident owners, the supply is taxable at 5% GST and zero-rating is unavailable. Digital/cloud supplies are further governed by Part 2 of Schedule IX place-of-supply rules: supplies of intangibles to Canadian-resident individuals are deemed made in Canada, and B2B supplies are generally taxed where the Canadian recipient's normal place of consumption is situated in Canada. Substantiation follows ETA s. 180 and CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17-1 (Proof of Export / secondary evidence policy).

  • CBSA B13A export declaration and bill of lading or air waybill (required only for tangible goods export; not applicable to pure SaaS delivery)
  • SaaS master subscription agreement / SOW identifying the non-resident customer, Canadian GST status, and the real property locations covered
  • Commercial invoice marked 'Zero-rated - Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 ETA' and bearing the corporation's GST/HST RT program account number
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice, ACH settlement report, or Stripe Billing payout record evidencing inbound FX payment within a prudential 180-day benchmark (Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline)
  • Service delivery evidence: usage logs, API access records, tenant-screening report delivery confirmations, and IP-geolocation data for the non-resident customer
  • Non-resident self-certification / beneficial ownership questionnaire for B2B customers
  • Contemporaneous export dossier retained for six years under ETA s. 286 books-and-records requirements

No continuous transaction control (CTC) or real-time fiscal clearance / digital stamping regime is in force in Canada. The CRA does not pre-clear or pre-validate invoices. GST/HST returns must generally be filed electronically (NETFILE/TELEFILE/XML via My Business Account API); large registrants use EDI/XML interfaces. Invoices must contain prescribed content under the ETA Information Returns Regulations: seller identity, BN and RT program account, invoice date, description, consideration, GST rate and amount. A voluntary PEPPOL-based national e-invoicing framework is under phased rollout but is not a statutory clearance requirement.

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is a documentary-evidence benchmark adopted for diligence. The CRA may reassess a zero-rating claim within four years of the filing date (ETA s. 298(2)), extended to eight years where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. If export evidence is deficient, the CRA reverses the 0% treatment, reassesses at 5% GST, charges compound daily interest at CRA prescribed rates, and applies the ETA s. 285.1 civil false-statement penalty equal to the greater of C$250 and 50% of the net tax understated. Knowingly false export claims are prosecuted under ETA s. 327 with fines of 50%-200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years on indictment; directors and officers are jointly and severally liable under ETA s. 323 for unremitted amounts. CRA Memorandum 17-1 secondary-evidence policy is applied strictly: secondary evidence is accepted only where original documentation was lost and all reasonable efforts to obtain replacements are demonstrated. Claimed input tax credits and refunds are subject to offset and clawback under ETA s. 292; repeated failures trigger the exporter review-flag protocol and mandatory full-scope GST audits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible pursuant to Article VIII, Sections 2-4 of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Both current-account and capital-account transactions (foreign direct investment, share subscription, intercompany financing, principal and interest servicing, and profit/dividend repatriation) are unrestricted and require no central-bank approval, license, or repatriation computation. The only fiscal friction on outbound profit remittance is non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act: 25% statutory, usually reduced to 5-15% under Canada's tax treaties (e.g., under Article X of the Canada-US Treaty, 5% on dividends where the US beneficial owner is a corporation holding at least 10% of voting shares; 15% generally; 0% on certain interest under the parent-subsidiary exemption). For CA_CORP, an Alberta Business Corporation with a PropTech SaaS revenue model, no surplus-capital test, no dividend-blocking statute, and no 'capital account registration' obligation (as encountered in EMEA/LATAM regimes) applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished statutory foreign-exchange controls in 1951 and has never re-imposed surrender or repatriation obligations. Exporters of services and digital goods, including cross-border PropertyTech subscription revenue flowing through Stripe Billing and ACH Direct rails, may retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds indefinitely in either CAD or foreign-currency operating accounts. There is no mandated timeline for conversion, no minimum conversion ratio, and no obligation to channel export receipts through an authorized dealer. The only administrative overlay is the Bank of Canada / Statistics Canada International Transactions Reporting requirement: resident corporations must report cross-border receipts and payments above prescribed thresholds to Statistics Canada for balance-of-payments statistical purposes, and Canadian banks apply standardized purpose-of-payment codes on cross-border wires; these are statistical and prudential recording measures, not exchange-control measures.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation of banks and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF supervision. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control department; external monetary policy is discharged via the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act, and banking market entry/foreign bank operations are licensed under the Bank Act (Schedule I, II, and III status).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High viability as a secondary clearing overlay, but not as the principal treasury bank for capital escrow or long-term liquidity. Wise (Wise Payments Canada), Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury (via Stripe Payments Canada), and Mercury (US-based) are operational in Canada; however, under the Bank Act a non-bank entity cannot accept Canadian 'deposits' in the legal sense, cannot be a member of the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC), and must settle each electronic payment through a settlement account held at a Canadian Payments Act member bank. The Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23), with regulations phased in commencing 2024, requires all non-bank payment service providers to register with the Bank of Canada, safeguard funds in trust/escrow, maintain operational incident plans, and comply with FINTRAC registration as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA. Recommended architecture for ISIC_6201 (PropTech SaaS): Stripe Billing and ACH Direct rails collect USD/CAD subscription payments, funds settle through the fintech's Canadian settlement bank, and daily treasury sweeps move balances into the CA_CORP Schedule I bank operating and escrow accounts.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% composed of 15% federal Part I rate and 8% Alberta general corporate rate; reduced to 11% (9% federal + 2% Alberta) for a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) eligible for the small business deduction on active business income up to CAD 500,000 (federal allocation) and Alberta's CAD 500,000 threshold

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or credited to a non-resident of Canada, unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty (e.g., Canada-US Convention: 15% general, 5% if the shareholder is a corporation that owns at least 10% of the voting stock directly and is the beneficial owner)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous documentation thresholds under Canadian Income Tax Act s.247(4): the taxpayer's gross revenue for the taxation year exceeds CAD 5 million, and the aggregate fair market value of all hard-to-value intangibles, interest, royalties, management fees, and purchase/sale transactions with each non-arm's length non-resident exceeds CAD 1 million in the year; penalties for failure to prepare adequate documentation are waived only if complete and contemporaneous records exist and are provided to the CRA within 30 days of a written request

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the CRA applies a robust 'economic reality' assessment via the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) and the statutory transfer-pricing rule (s.247). A transaction without commercial economic substance and lacking business purpose will be disregarded; intercompany fees, management charges, or royalty streams that do not align with actual services rendered, personnel, and assets are vulnerable to reallocation as amounts that would have been agreed upon at arm's length; for a PropTech SaaS, the relevant economic activities (software development, data hosting, customer contract management, and risk control) must be conducted by Canadian employees and supported by payroll, technical infrastructure, and management records to avoid substance-based reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Willful Understatement of Digital Commerce Revenue)

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty of 50% of the amount of tax understated under s.163(2), plus daily compound interest; summary conviction under s.238 of the Income Tax Act carries a fine not exceeding CAD 10,000 and/or six months imprisonment; indictment under Criminal Code s.380 (fraud) may expose the corporation to fines of up to CAD 100,000, and responsible individuals to imprisonment up to 14 years

Director personal liability: Corporate directors who knowingly participate in or direct evasion are jointly and severally liable under s.160(5) for amounts that the CRA cannot otherwise collect; civil restitution orders may attach personal assets, travel bans are imposed under the Criminal Code, and imprisonment up to 14 years may be ordered; for repeated evader directors, the CRA may request director residency restrictions and overseas pass revocation.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse / GAAR

Statutory sanction: CRA will recompute income under s.247 and s.245 denying the deduction or benefit; a penalty equal to 50% of the tax benefit from GAAR avoidance transaction (s.245(4)) plus interest; gross negligence penalties of 50% under s.163(2) may also apply; the classification as a 'foreign-controlled private corporation' or 'non-resident-owned corporation' may trigger additional reporting requirements such as T1146, T1134, and Form T106 penalties of CAD 2,500 per form

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the sham transactions can be held personally liable for corporate tax under s.227.1 (Part XIII withholding) if the shell entity becomes insolvent; for participation in fraudulent transactions, civil liability for the tax shortfall plus punitive damages; the CRA may seek regional court injunctions preventing the sale of personal assets and, in egregious cases, a director may be subject to a travel restriction while the tax investigation is pending.
AML / Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under PCMLTFA up to CAD 2 million for corporations (and CAD 1 million for individuals) for each violation; criminal penalties for laundering proceeds under Criminal Code s.462.31 include imprisonment for up to 10 years; seizure, restraint, and forfeiture of the SaaS accounts, bank balances, and connected real-estate holdings; the stock exchange or payment services (Stripe, ACH network) may suspend or terminate services, causing contractual liability

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and compliance officers may be exposed to police prosecution if they knowingly or negligently conduct a prohibited financial transaction; criminal sanctions include imprisonment up to 5 years for failing to report a suspicious transaction, loss of professional credentialing, personal fines up to CAD 500,000, and lifetime disqualification from serving as a director of any Canadian corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act if convicted of an indictable offence.
