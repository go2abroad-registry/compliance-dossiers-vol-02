Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign corporation carrying on business in Alberta must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by registering online via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1, attaching certified corporate registration extracts from the Alberta Corporate Registry and, if federally incorporated, from Corporations Canada. The BN is the master statutory TIN. A distinct GST/HST program account (RT) must be opened at the same time under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act, unless the corporation qualifies as a small supplier with worldwide taxable supplies of C$30,000 or less in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters. No separate Alberta provincial sales tax registration exists because Alberta levies no provincial retail sales tax and no HST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election system. Statutory election mechanisms include: (1) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for closely related corporations to treat intra-group supplies as not being made, eliminating GST/HST on intercompany email infrastructure services; (2) Excise Tax Act s. 238 election to file combined GST/HST returns and rebates for related registrants; (3) Income Tax Act s. 85 rollover election for transferring property into a Canadian corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (4) Income Tax Act s. 249.1 election to adopt a non-calendar taxation year; and (5) mandatory hybrid-mismatch disallowance rules under Income Tax Act ss. 18.4 to 18.6 which operate non-electively to deny deductions where foreign jurisdictions treat payments inconsistently for tax purposes. A non-resident corporation may also elect under Income Tax Act s. 219 to compute Canadian tax on a branch-pooled basis, affecting the interaction between federal and Alberta corporate income tax on Canadian-source business income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 (CAD) of worldwide taxable supplies measured in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters under ETA s. 240(1); non-resident suppliers of digital services and intangibles such as SaaS email infrastructure may be deemed to carry on business in Canada under ETA s. 143(1.1) and related sourcing rules, creating registration exposure for supplies made to Canadian recipients even where gross revenue is below the general threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, tangible goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under section 22. Email infrastructure and newsletter-hosting platform services supplied to non-resident customers are zero-rated under section 7, Part V, Schedule VI, which applies to a supply of a service made to a non-resident unless the service is performed wholly in Canada or is in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. For ISIC 6201 email newsletter infrastructure, subscription and transactional email API fees charged to foreign recipients are zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident, the contract is negotiated outside Canada, the payment originates offshore, and the service such as software access and data hosting is not supplied for use in Canada. Incidentally, any supply made to a Canadian-resident customer is taxable at 5% GST.

  • CBSA Export Declaration record (Canadian Export Reporting System or eManifest) for tangible media or incidental physical exports; not required for pure digital service exports
  • Commercial invoice describing the zero-rated supply and identifying the non-resident recipient
  • Master SaaS agreement, order form, or terms of service evidencing offshore recipient and prohibited use in Canada
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier receipt for tangible media or hardware supporting the service delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent banking evidence (SWIFT MT103, credit-card settlement record) proving receipt of export consideration from the non-resident
  • Non-resident status documentation, such as a foreign business registry extract, foreign VAT registration certificate, or statutory declaration of non-residency
  • Internal GST/HST technical memorandum documenting the Schedule VI zero-rating position and related CRA policy interpretation

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary and implemented through the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing pilot based on the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standard. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding C$1M must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, and mandatory electronic remittance thresholds apply for payments over C$10,000. CRA uses cross-border data matching, banking information analytics, and risk-based audit targeting to verify zero-rated export claims for digital service providers.

Canada has no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export receivables; the 180-day benchmark represents the documentary best-practice evidentiary window for demonstrating receipt and settlement of export consideration. On audit, CRA may recharacterize a zero-rated export as a taxable Canadian supply if the non-resident status of the recipient, the offshore delivery, or the actual receipt of funds is not substantiated. The normal reassessment window is 4 years under ETA s. 298, extended to 7 years for misrepresentation and without limitation for fraud. Penalties include false-statement penalties under ETA s. 280 (C$250 to C$25,000 or up to 200% of net tax), gross-negligence penalties under Income Tax Act s. 163(2) equal to 50% of understated tax, compounded arrears interest, denial of input tax credits and rebates, and criminal prosecution for wilful tax evasion and refund fraud under ETA ss. 326 and 327 and Criminal Code s. 380.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating CAD with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no general exchange-control laws, no central-bank approval for profit repatriation, and no requirement to route remittances through a licensed dealer. Cross-border dividends, management fees, and software subscription revenue may be remitted freely to non-residents, subject only to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (statutory 25%, generally treaty-reduced to 5%–15% depending on ownership) and applicable GST/HST on Canadian taxable supplies. The email-infrastructure SaaS revenue model is treated as an export of services and is not subject to GST/HST when the recipient is a non-resident or the service is supplied to a recipient outside Canada, provided the conditions in section 7 of Part V of Schedule IX of the Excise Tax Act are met.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canadian law imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no conversion ratio on receipts earned by Canadian exporters. The Alberta-based corporation may retain USD/EUR receivables indefinitely in domestic or offshore settlement accounts, and may manage FX exposure without Bank of Canada notification. Only statistical reporting obligations exist, such as the Survey of International Merchandise Trade and quarterly balance-of-payments surveys administered by Statistics Canada for entities with large export positions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central monetary authority, with currency and exchange-control policy administered jointly by the Department of Finance Canada and prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT enforcement is vested in FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for regulated payment service providers and electronic money institutions registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC, including Wise (formerly TransferWise), Payoneer, and Flywire, which may offer Canadian-dollar settlement and USD, EUR, GBP, and CAD multi-currency balances to the Alberta entity. Foreign digital EMIs not registered as MSBs in Canada have Low viability: under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), any foreign EMI providing payment processing, FX conversion, or cross-border fund transfer services to Canadian customers must register with FINTRAC as an MSB within their first business day, comply with compliance-program and reporting obligations, and may not rely solely on an offshore license. For the Stripe_Billing / Credit_Card revenue model, Stripe operates as a Payment Facilitator (PayFac) rather than an EMI; funds settle via Stripe's Canadian acquiring bank to the Alberta corporate bank account, which is the recommended primary rail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined (Federal general corporate rate 15% + Alberta general corporate rate 8%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on gross dividends paid or credited by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder; Alberta imposes no separate withholding tax on dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 must be filed no later than six months after the end of the taxation year; a calendar-year corporation must file by June 30 of the following year. Balance-due day is two months after year-end for a non-CCPC and generally three months after year-end for a CCPC meeting instalment thresholds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of cross-border non-arm's length transactions with a single non-resident, triggering Form T106 filing and requiring contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File / Master File comparable to OECD standards) to avoid the CRA secondary penalty.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain UAE-style economic substance regulations. However, substance requirements apply through the general anti-avoidance rule, domestic anti-conduit rules, permanent establishment sourcing, and DTA beneficial ownership / limitation-on-benefits tests. An Alberta-registered entity with no local staff, board control, or operational risk cannot sustain treaty or corporate attribution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the understated balance plus cumulative compound interest; upon criminal conviction under Income Tax Act s.239(1), a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be made personally liable for unremitted source deductions and can face criminal prosecution if they directed, acquiesced in, or profited from the evasion; courts may pierce the corporate veil to enforce restitution, asset forfeiture, and travel restrictions pending trial.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment denying the corporate attribution, reclassifying the arrangement as a sham; assessment at the 23% combined rate, 25% Part XIII withholding on deemed shareholder distributions, and penalties up to CAD 100,000 under false-statement rules.

Director personal liability: Directors of a nominee shell face civil claims for breach of fiduciary duty, joint and several liability for tax debts, disgorgement of fees, and criminal fraud charges if the entity is used to launder profits or mislead tax administrators.
Money Laundering and FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and, if criminalized, up to ten years' imprisonment under the Criminal Code for laundering proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or knowingly failed to prevent the pass-through scheme are personally exposed to forfeiture orders, fines, or imprisonment; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed pending criminal investigation.
Transfer Pricing / Contemporaneous Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA transfer pricing reassessment under s.247(2), a secondary penalty of 10% of the total pricing adjustment under s.247(3), interest from the original filing date, and potential criminal penalties for intentionally false documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign misleading transfer pricing documentation, approve inflated intercompany deductions, or hide overseas affiliate relationships may be assessed personally for false statements, ordered to pay civil restitution, and subjected to passport denial or surrender for serious tax fraud.
