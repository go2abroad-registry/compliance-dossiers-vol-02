Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Primary portals are Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and Represent a Client. Alberta corporate income tax is administered through the CRA under federal-provincial tax administration arrangements; Alberta Treasury Board and Finance legislates Alberta corporate tax rates. Alberta has no general provincial sales tax.

E-government filing portal

Apply to the CRA for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online, Form RC1, or the applicable non-resident registration process. A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada generally requires a BN and a corporate income tax program account. 3. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Canadian corporation is generally taxed as a separate legal person and has no United States-style check-the-box election to elect partnership or disregarded-entity treatment. Canadian-resident corporations are generally taxable on worldwide income, subject to foreign tax relief and applicable treaty rules. Partnerships are ordinarily flow-through structures, but a corporation cannot unilaterally elect partnership treatment. Certain statutory elections can affect transactions or tax attributes, including corporate rollover elections under section 85 of the Income Tax Act, but these are not entity-classification elections. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose income inclusions for specified deduction or inclusion mismatches; they do not provide a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, determined under the small-supplier rules, generally measured over the relevant single-quarter and four-consecutive-quarter periods. The threshold is generally CAD 50,000 for public service bodies. Registration may be voluntary below the threshold. A corporation making taxable supplies in Canada, including taxable software or support services, must assess whether it is carrying on business in Canada and whether the small-supplier threshold is exceeded. Special simplified GST/HST rules may apply to non-resident vendors supplying digital products or services to Canadian consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating is transaction-specific and is not available merely because a supplier receives foreign currency or because software is delivered online. Exported goods may generally be zero-rated when they are supplied for export and the statutory export, delivery, and documentary conditions are satisfied. Services supplied to a non-resident may generally be zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not excluded by the Excise Tax Act, including exclusions for certain services connected with Canadian real property, tangible personal property, or litigation and other Canadian activities. Certain supplies of intangible personal property, software rights, electronically supplied services, and remote support services may qualify where the statutory non-resident and use or connection conditions are met. The supplier should verify the customer’s non-resident status, GST/HST registration status where relevant, contractual recipient, place of use, service performance, and applicable exclusions. Services supplied to a Canadian customer, or services effectively consumed or used in Canada, are generally taxable at the applicable GST/HST rate. A registrant claiming zero-rating must retain contracts, invoices, customer representations, delivery or access records, payment evidence, and records supporting the statutory place-of-supply analysis.

  • For exported physical goods where reporting is required: CBSA export declaration filed through CERS or the applicable permitted reporting method
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, purchaser, description, quantity, value, currency, and delivery terms
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier record, or other transportation document evidencing export from Canada
  • Proof of delivery or importation in the destination jurisdiction where relevant
  • Sales contract, purchase order, customer non-resident declaration, and evidence that the recipient is outside Canada or is otherwise eligible under the applicable zero-rating rule
  • Payment and accounting records, including bank settlement evidence where relevant; an inward remittance certificate is not a general Canadian statutory requirement
  • For remote software, cloud access, telemetry, or support services without physical goods: service agreement, customer location and status evidence, access or delivery logs, invoices, usage records, and evidence supporting the statutory zero-rating provision; a customs export declaration is generally not required for the electronic service itself

Canada has no general real-time GST invoice-clearance, mandatory continuous transaction control, or universal digital-stamping requirement for ordinary commercial supplies. GST registrants must issue prescribed documentation when required, retain electronic or paper books and records, and maintain records that permit verification of tax charged, input tax credits, place of supply, customer status, and zero-rating. Electronic invoices are generally acceptable if they preserve authenticity, integrity, readability, auditability, and required invoice particulars. CRA may require electronic filing for specified returns or information filings, but this is not equivalent to real-time invoice clearance. Import and export customs data may be filed electronically through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), including the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) where applicable.

Canada has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days or within another fixed period solely to preserve GST zero-rating. CRA may reassess GST where zero-rating was unsupported, the recipient was not eligible, the supply was consumed in Canada, required export evidence was absent, or the transaction was mischaracterized. The reassessment can include GST collectible, denial or recovery of related input tax credits or refunds, arrears interest, and applicable administrative penalties. False statements or omissions may attract a penalty based on the applicable GST/HST penalty provisions, including gross-negligence penalties where the statutory conditions are met; deliberate fraud can result in prosecution under tax legislation. Physical-goods export reporting failures may also trigger CBSA administrative monetary penalties, seizure or other customs remedies. A refund or input tax credit obtained on the basis of an invalid zero-rating claim is recoverable through reassessment, interest, and penalties, subject to statutory limitation periods and extended periods for misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada adheres fully to IMF Article VIII obligations, granting unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar floats freely on international markets, and profit remittances of dividends, branch profits, interest, and royalties to non-residents require no central bank approval. The sole fiscal friction is Part XIII of the Income Tax Act: a 25% statutory withholding tax on passive payments, reduced to 5%-15% under Canada's tax treaties, and, for certain surplus-stripping scenarios, the application of thin-capitalization and earnings-stripping rules. Foreign-currency accounts may be held domestically and funded without convertibility quotas, exchange licenses, or prior authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory conversion timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for foreign-currency receivables. Software subscription receipts from remote-support and telemetry services may be retained in USD, EUR, or any convertible currency and either repatriated or held offshore at the corporation's discretion pending treasury policy. The only compulsory compliance duty is FINTRAC's electronic funds transfer report (ETR) for any cross-border wire transfer of CAD 10,000 or more in a single transaction day, together with standard record-keeping and client-identification obligations under the PCMLTFR.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and systemic authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises banks, while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers cross-border fund reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control administration comparable to a SAFE-type authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for ancillary treasury functions, Low for a primary operating account. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise, Revolut Business) may serve a Canadian corporation only as FINTRAC-registered money service businesses (MSBs) or unlicensed payment facilitators; they cannot lawfully take deposits in Canada under section 545 of the Bank Act, and their balances are not CDIC-insured. A compliant architecture uses a Tier-1 chartered bank as the settlement spine, with an offshore EMI wallet permitted solely for low-value FX isolation and contractor disbursement, swept daily to the domestic operating account to avoid regulatory and counterparty concentration risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the subscribing shareholder's foreign bank to the Alberta corporation's Tier-1 settlement account; the sender must populate the purpose narrative with 'Share capital contribution - Board Resolution No. [X]' and use the receiving bank's designated capital-subscription payment code (commonly 'CAP' or 'INV'). The receiving bank screens the transfer against OFAC and OSFI sanctions lists and files a FINTRAC ETR for amounts of CAD 10,000 or more. Verification collateral includes the subscription agreement, the directors' capital-contribution resolution, the UBO attestation, and a statement of account reflecting the funds as unencumbered contributed surplus. Structuring the payment into multiple sub-threshold tranches to evade reporting is a criminal offence and will trigger immediate account suspension.Operational disbursements for licensing, subcontractor payments, and cloud-infrastructure procurement settle by SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary BIC, account number, and USD ABA or EUR IBAN, using charge code OUR or SHA under the corporate banking fee template. Domestic CAD clearing rides the Lynx RTGS system (successor to LVTS) and the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for instant low-value claims. High-value cross-border physical or software trade settlements may use a commercial letter of credit confirmed by a Tier-1 Canadian bank, although MT103 is preferred for recurring telemetry-software subscriptions.
