Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; one-stop registration via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/business-number.html. Alberta corporate income tax is administered federally by the CRA under the Alberta Tax Collection Agreement through the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (ATRA); Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax, so no separate provincial indirect-tax registration exists.

E-government filing portal

Procedure for the foreign parent to establish the Alberta ODC (CA_CORP) and obtain the statutory tax identity: (1) Incorporate under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) or federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act followed by Alberta extra-provincial registration; obtain the corporate registry number. (2) Apply through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and simultaneously open program accounts RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll if employees are hired). (3) Provide the CRA with the articles of incorporation, registered office address in Alberta, full names and residential addresses of directors (non-resident directors are permitted), and the foreign ultimate parent's identification; no minimum capital is required and unrestricted non-resident ownership is allowed. (4) Alberta corporate income tax registration is automatic through the federal T2 return; separate ATRA registration is not required. (5) The foreign parent itself does not require a Canadian BN unless it separately carries on business in Canada, makes taxable supplies in Canada, or has Canadian-source income subject to Part XIII withholding; dividend payments to the parent are reported on NR4 returns and treaty relief is applied through CRA Form NR301. (6) GST/HST registration is mandatory when annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; a small supplier may voluntarily register earlier to claim input tax credits.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. An Alberta business corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate Part I taxpayer under the Income Tax Act: federal general rate 15% plus Alberta general rate 8% = 23% combined. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An Alberta business corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate Part I taxpayer under the Income Tax Act: federal general rate 15% plus Alberta general rate 8% = 23% combined. Territorial_Scope: a resident corporation (incorporated in Canada under ITA s. 250(1)(a)) is taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation is taxed only on Canadian-source business income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment, Canadian-source capital gains, and Part XIII withholdings. No pass-through eligibility exists for corporations, Canada has no tax-exempt zones, and the Alberta ODC is not eligible for any special economic-zone exemption.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Hybrid mismatch and reverse-hybrid rules (ITA ss. 12.3, 18.2, 18.3, 20.2) apply automatically, and no domestic election can disapply them. GST/HST election available: the ETA s. 156(2) close-connection election permits nil-consideration treatment for taxable supplies between closely related registrants, which may simplify intercompany shared-service billing while preserving full input tax credit recovery. For Part XIII withholding on dividends paid to the foreign parent, the parent files CRA Form NR301 for treaty-based rate reduction; this is an annual administrative application, not an entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. The ODC ceases to be a small supplier when its annual worldwide taxable supplies, including supplies of associated persons, exceed CAD 30,000 (Excise Tax Act s. 240); registration is mandatory and the application must be filed within 30 days after the small-supplier status ceases. Voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits before the threshold is crossed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The ODC's software engineering, development, maintenance, and outsourcing services qualify for the 0% GST rate as services supplied to a non-resident under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7, provided: (i) the foreign client is not a GST/HST registrant; (ii) the services are not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; and (iii) no part of the service is rendered to an individual who is present in Canada. Exported tangible deliverables qualify under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 1 and 2 if exported as of the time of payment or delivered outside Canada. If the foreign client is a GST/HST registrant, the supply is generally taxable at 5% unless the service is delivered for use entirely outside Canada; the ODC should obtain and keep evidence of the client's non-resident status, including a certificate of residence, incorporation documents, and confirmation of no Canadian establishment.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) - required only for tangible hardware or equipment physically exported; not filed for purely electronic software development services
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier evidencing shipment of any exported goods delivered with the service
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank statement of the ODC evidencing receipt of export consideration in CAD or convertible foreign currency
  • Executed master services agreement, purchase order, and zero-rated commercial invoices identifying the non-resident client and stating the 0% GST treatment under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7
  • Corporate records proving the client's non-resident status, such as certificate of incorporation, registered office address, tax residence certificate, and declaration that the client is not a GST/HST registrant

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Peppol framework, with a phased mandate for suppliers to the Government of Canada to transmit e-invoices through the federal e-invoice portal. Invoices must be issued and records retained for six years under ETA s. 286; GST/HST returns are filed electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE, and corporations with annual gross revenues exceeding CAD 1,000,000 must file the T2 corporate return and related schedules electronically. No API-based government pre-clearance of individual invoices is required.

Canada does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline; however, CRA audit scrutiny will test whether intercompany export receivables are collected on arm's length terms under ITA s. 247, and a stale receivable may give rise to transfer pricing adjustments, imputed interest, or shareholder-benefit treatment. If export zero-rating is denied for inadequate documentation, the CRA reassesses 5% GST on the full consideration plus compounded quarterly interest; the normal reassessment window is 4 years, extended to 10 years for misrepresentation or negligence, and unlimited for fraud. Civil penalties include ETA s. 280 failure-to-file penalties, ETA s. 285 false-statement penalties of CAD 250 or 25% of net tax, ETA s. 273 wilful-evasion penalties of 50% of tax sought to be evaded, ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalties of 50% of understated income tax, and ITA s. 247(3) transfer-pricing penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000). Criminal prosecution is possible under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239; the ODC must retain all export documentation for at least six years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII status with no restrictions on capital movements or profit remittances from Canadian subsidiaries to foreign parents. Current account transactions, including dividends, management fees, and service payments, are fully convertible without prior approval or convertibility hurdles, subject only to standard tax filings, beneficial ownership reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and FINTRAC compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from services (software engineering and maintenance) are classified as current account items. No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratios to CAD apply. Funds may be retained in foreign currency accounts or repatriated freely, provided all foreign exchange transactions comply with FINTRAC reporting thresholds (e.g., CAD 10,000 cash transactions) and anti-money laundering obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
