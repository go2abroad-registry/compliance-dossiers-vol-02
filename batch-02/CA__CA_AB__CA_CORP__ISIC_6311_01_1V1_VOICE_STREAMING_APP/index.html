Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) is the competent national tax authority for federal income tax and GST/HST administration. Alberta's provincial corporate income tax is also administered by the CRA under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement with Alberta Treasury Board and Finance. Principal portals: CRA 'My Business Account' (MyBA), GST/HST NETFILE, Corporation Internet Filing (CIF), and the Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES) for provincial incorporation and business number issuance.

E-government filing portal

For an Alberta corporation: (1) Incorporate under the federal Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Business Corporations Act via the Alberta Corporate Registry; the corporation receives a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA. (2) Register program accounts with the CRA online through 'Register with CRA' or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts', opening the mandatory GST/HST account (RT program), corporate income tax program account (RC), payroll account (RP) if employees exist, and import/export account (RM/RE) if physical merchandise is moved. (3) Non-resident foreign corporations register through the CRA International Tax Services Office using Form RC1 or the online portal, and may need a Canadian representative or power of attorney. (4) The CRA issues the BN immediately upon electronic registration; statutory evidence comprises the Certificate of Incorporation, the BN (e.g., BN 123456789 RT0001), and the GST/HST registration confirmation letter.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian/Alberta business corporation is a 'corporation' under the Income Tax Act, taxed at the entity level on worldwide income (federal Part I tax plus Alberta provincial corporate tax). It is not a pass-through entity; shareholders are taxed only on dividends or capital gains. The classification label is Entity_Level_Taxation (with a worldwide, non-territorial sourcing regime for resident Canadian corporations). If the corporation is Canadian-controlled and private, it qualifies as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) eligible for the small business deduction and lower Alberta small business rate, subject to the statutory income threshold. — For an Alberta corporation: (1) Incorporate under the federal Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Business Corporations Act via the Alberta Corporate Registry; the corporation receives a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian/Alberta business corporation is a 'corporation' under the Income Tax Act, taxed at the entity level on worldwide income (federal Part I tax plus Alberta provincial corporate tax). It is not a pass-through entity; shareholders are taxed only on dividends or capital gains. The classification label is Entity_Level_Taxation (with a worldwide, non-territorial sourcing regime for resident Canadian corporations). If the corporation is Canadian-controlled and private, it qualifies as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) eligible for the small business deduction and lower Alberta small business rate, subject to the statutory income threshold.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or US-style entity classification election; tax classification is dictated by legal incorporation and CRA rules. Available statutory elections include: ITA s. 216 (non-resident election to file a net-income Canadian return for rental income), ITA s. 217 (non-resident election respecting Canadian employment income), ITA s. 85 and s. 86 (rollover elections for corporate reorganizations/transfers), ITA s. 250(2.1) (deemed Canadian residency status is not elective), and Excise Tax Act s. 273 (election to have the recipient self-assess GST/HST for certain supplies). Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 12(2.3), 18.2, 18.3) apply compulsorily and cannot be elected away. Provincial authorities offer no tax classification election; a federal corporation may elect to 'continue' into Alberta under the ABCA, but this is a corporate law continuation, not a tax classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the general 'small supplier' registration threshold for GST/HST, measured against the value of all worldwide taxable supplies (including supplies of associated persons) in any single calendar quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters. Once exceeded, registration is mandatory within 30 days. Special E-commerce rules effective July 1, 2021 remove the small-supplier threshold for non-resident digital-platform operators and non-resident suppliers of digital services (including voice-streaming apps), requiring registration at CAD 0 in Canadian-source digital commerce. A Canadian-resident developer of a globally monetized voice-streaming app will in practice cross the CAD 30,000 threshold and must register for GST/HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of services and intangible personal property to a non-resident are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, Section 7 and Part III, Section 10/12 of the Excise Tax Act, provided the recipient is not registered under the GST/HST and the supply is not performed in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada. For a voice-streaming social app, the place of supply is determined by the subscriber's usual place of residence: if the billing address, IP geolocation, and App Store territory indicate residence outside Canada, the digital supply is zero-rated; if the subscriber is in Canada, 5% GST applies (Alberta). Zero-rating is substantiated through app-store payout statements, Apple/Google transaction and tax reports, customer location evidence, and bank remittance records showing proceeds received from foreign subscribers.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) for physical goods or merchandise exports; not required for pure digital voice-streaming services
  • Commercial invoice or app-store transaction report identifying the recipient, territory, and consideration
  • Bill of lading or air waybill showing the Canadian port of exit (for physical shipments)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days
  • Apple IAP and Google Play payout statements, VAT/GST transaction reports, and developer-of-record tax receipts
  • Customer geolocation and billing documentation (IP address, postal code, App Store country) proving non-resident recipient status

Canada does not mandate a national real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API pre-authorization for e-invoices. CRA is progressively adopting a Pan-Canadian e-invoice standard based on the PEPPOL framework, but participation is voluntary. Mandatory obligations instead require GST/HST registrants to file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE/GSTNET and to retain complete records in accessible Canadian format. Digital-platform operators such as Apple (App Store) and Google (Play Store) may act as statutory collection intermediaries for Canadian GST/HST where they control the essential transaction elements, and are obliged to maintain records for CRA audit. The merchant-of-record payment rails (Apple IAP and Google Play Billing) impose contractual tax-invoicing and remittance obligations that must be reconciled by the developer against its own GST/HST return.

CRA may reassess an export zero-rating claim within the ordinary 4-year assessment period, extended to 7 or 8 years for misrepresentation, neglect, or negligence. Reassessment retroactively claws back the zero-rated status and assesses the 5% GST/(HST) payable, plus arrears interest under Excise Tax Act s. 280. Penalties include the gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 285 (the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax understated), false-statement penalties under ETA s. 327, and record-keeping penalties under ETA s. 286. Fraudulent zero-rating schemes may trigger criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327, Criminal Code s. 380, and Income Tax Act s. 239. CRA cross-references Apple/Google merchant-of-record remittances with the developer's GST/HST returns, so discrepancies in digital-export zero-rating claims attract elevated audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full current-account and capital-account convertibility. It is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction and maintains no pegs, surrender taxes, or exchange licensing for ordinary corporate treasury flows. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, shareholder loan repayment, and capital repatriation from an Alberta corporation are not FX-controlled. The effective hurdles are tax and regulatory: non-resident withholding under Income Tax Act Part XIII (e.g., dividends, interest, royalties) with possible treaty relief; bank-level anti-money-laundering source-of-funds due diligence; FINTRAC EFT reporting for cross-border transfers; and Investment Canada Act notification or review thresholds only if a non-Canadian establishes a new Canadian business or acquires control of a Canadian business, not for routine equity subscription into a newly incorporated active operating company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada does not impose export-proceeds surrender, repatriation timelines, or mandatory conversion ratios, including for digital service export receipts from app stores. Export receipts, including settlement received from Apple and Google, may remain in USD or be swapped into CAD at the entity's discretion. For tax accounting, foreign-currency-denominated receipts must be translated into the functional currency using the Bank of Canada spot or annual average exchange rate as appropriate, and any realized or unrealized FX gain or loss must be booked consistently and recognized under the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). No separate Foreign Exchange Administration exists in Canada; the financial intelligence unit FINTRAC, prudential supervisor OSFI, and Payments Canada together operate the relevant regulatory rails for foreign exchange and clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as the primary treasury spine; Medium only as an ancillary payout concentration rail. An offshore bank or fintech EMI that is not a Schedule I bank cannot access Payments Canada settlement systems directly, cannot offer CDIC-insured deposits, and does not have a branch license under the Bank Act or applicable Alberta provincial requirements. It must be registered as a Canadian money services business with FINTRAC if it performs transfer or payout services in Canada, and may need provincial money-transfer licensing. The corporate treasury should maintain its main operating account at a Schedule I or II bank and use EMIs only for niche disbursement routes (e.g., small developer payouts or localized card funding), never for shareholder capital, tax payments, or large supplier settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial shareholder loan or equity contribution, use an inbound SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the chosen Schedule I bank in Canada. The beneficiary must be identified by institution, branch or transit number, and account number; Canada does not use IBAN. In the MT103 Field 70 remittance details, include corporate reference 'CA_CORP Capital Injection' plus the share subscription or intercompany loan identifier. The bank will normally require the purpose code to be classified as 'Direct Investment Equity Subscription' or 'Intercompany Shareholder Loan' rather than a trade-in-goods payment. Verification bundle: board resolutions, executed subscription or promissory note, source-of-funds statements, and a corporate authorization resolution for the receiving signatory. The transaction does not require Bank of Canada or government approval, but the bank will file or rely on FINTRAC reporting for cross-border funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or above; the company must ensure remitter, intermediary, and beneficiary names are exact and no sanctioned entity appears in the chain.Use SWIFT MT103 for cross-border trade settlements; for CAD-denominated domestic payments use Payments Canada LVTS for high-value disbursements and ACSS plus pre-authorized debit for operating payments. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23.0% combined general corporate income tax (15% federal Part I + 8% Alberta provincial); 11.0% combined rate for a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) exercising the Small Business Deduction on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (9% federal + 2% Alberta).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on all dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident shareholders (ITA s. 212(2)); reduced to 5%-15% under applicable tax treaties (e.g., Canada-US DTA: 5% for a corporate beneficial owner holding at least 10% of voting shares, otherwise 15%; Canada-HK DTA: 5%/15%; Canada-China DTA: 10%/15%).
