Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Federal indirect tax administration and corporate income tax administration for Alberta under the Tax Administration Agreement with the Government of Alberta. Portals: GST/HST NETFILE via My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO).

E-government filing portal

A federal or Alberta-incorporated business corporation must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA. Foreign entities may register via postal application on Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number', by telephone through the Business Window, or online through Business Registration Online. The GST/HST account is opened concurrently; a non-resident corporation supplying digital services in Canada is also required to complete the simplified non-resident registration under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act. Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax, and all Alberta corporate tax filings are administered through CRA, eliminating separate provincial registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian business corporation is resident in Canada under common law (central management and control) and taxable under Income Tax Act s. 2(1) on worldwide income; it is not a pass-through entity under any federal or Alberta statute. Closed or closely held corporations may elect the Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) status if the parent is a qualifying Canadian resident; a foreign parent of a video app corporation will typically render the subsidiary a non-CCPC, losing the small business deduction. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian business corporation is resident in Canada under common law (central management and control) and taxable under Income Tax Act s. 2(1) on worldwide income; it is not a pass-through entity under any federal or Alberta statute. Closed or closely held corporations may elect the Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) status if the parent is a qualifying Canadian resident; a foreign parent of a video app corporation will typically render the subsidiary a non-CCPC, losing the small business deduction. No tax-consolidated group filing is permitted except the lifetime capital gains exemption transfer and related entity elections.

Available election forms

Canada does not employ the US check-the-box regime; a corporation is always a separate taxable person. Relevant elections include: (i) s. 156 Excise Tax Act election to treat a closely related group of registrants as a single GST/HST registrant; (ii) ETA s. 238(2) election to file GST/HST annually when all supplies are zero-rated (including cross-border video matching services); (iii) Income Tax Act s. 18.4/18.6 and s. 95(5.1) hybrid mismatch elections for controlled foreign affiliates to neutralize double non-taxation; and (iv) s. 152(4) designation election for the computation of foreign accrual property income. For Alberta, no separate provincial S elections exist given the single-administration regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – GST registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies in Canada (excluding zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in a series of four consecutive quarters (s. 148 ETA small supplier threshold; for non-resident digital service providers, the same CAD 30,000 quarterly threshold applies to specified supplies under the regime at ss. 211.1–211.18 ETA).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registrant operating a real-time video matching and cross-border social telephony application may apply the zero-rate (GST/HST 0%) to supplies of services made to non-resident recipients where the supply is deemed to be made outside Canada under s. 142.1(1)(d) ETA, or where the service qualifies under Part V, Schedule VI (services supplied to a non-resident, other than services in respect of real property in Canada or physical goods in Canada). The zero-rating applies irrespective of the commercial payment model (subscription, virtual gifts, advertisement sponsorship) provided the recipient is a non-resident and the performance of the service does not occur at a place in Canada at the time of supply. Supplies to users physically present in Canada are standard-rated at 5% and require a Canadian GST/HST charge; corporate income tax on export profits is otherwise shielded by the federal 'supply of services to non-residents' rules only for GST purposes, not for income tax, so transfer pricing documentation for a foreign parent or associated platforms is essential.

  • Customs Export Declaration for any ancillary physical exports (SD on CAED) or proof that export is of services where no customs declaration is applicable; for digital services the equivalent is the service contract or subscription agreement
  • GST/HST zero-rated commercial invoice showing BN, date, description of the video matching service, and a legend 'Zero-rated supply to non-resident — Part V, Schedule VI, ETA'
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill (only where incidental hardware/media is shipped) plus service performance logs demonstrating the app server's Canadian IP origin
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT/MT103 records evidencing foreign currency settlement received from Apple IAP and Paymentwall payment rails
  • Non-residence evidence of the end user: billing country attribute, geolocation IP logs, or payment card country code retained in the BSS/OSS system
  • RC4640 or Form T1134 related-party disclosure where the foreign parent is the contracting counterparty, to support arm's length consideration

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or API-based government preclearance exists for GST/HST. The CRA requires prescribed disclosure on invoices (seller's BN, date, supplier and recipient names, description, consideration, tax status of the supply; s. 221 ETA and Reg. 242). Electronic invoicing is permitted without prior authorization under the pan-Canadian Peppol BIS 3.0 standard, which remains voluntary for B2B exchanges. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via NETFILE, and for non-resident simplified registrants, through the dedicated web portal. Records must be retained electronically for six years and made available for CRA audit in a readable format.

CRA subjects zero-rated export claims to audit under s. 296 ETA, with a four-year normal reassessment window (s. 298(1)) that is extended indefinitely where a taxpayer has made any misrepresentation attributable to fraud, neglect, or wilful default. Penalties: s. 280 failure-to-register/collect penalty of 5% of net GST, increased by 1% per month up to 12 months; a penalty equal to the amount of improperly claimed input tax credits under s. 285.2; and a 10% 'civil penalty for false statements' under s. 285.1. Criminal tax evasion under s. 327 ETA is punishable on summary conviction by a fine up to CAD 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to one year, and on indictment by a fine up to 200% of the evaded GST and/or imprisonment up to five years, for export zero-rating fraud such as synthetic non-resident users, circular invoicing through a shell foreign intermediary, or deliberate destruction of geolocation evidence. Where export proceeds are not repatriated to Canada within the 180-day window, the outstanding balance is treated as a shareholder or intercompany loan subject to s. 15(2)/90(2) income inclusion, foreign accrual property income attribution under s. 91, and Canadian-dollar translation gains under s. 261 are taxed; CRA exchange rate revaluation can also trigger reassessments of the GST refund base.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full Article VIII IMF convertibility with no exchange-control licensing for either current or capital account transactions. Capital inflows, repatriations, dividends, and profit remittances are unrestricted in mechanism; practical hurdles are tax-based: (i) Part XIII withholding tax of 25% on dividends/royalties payable to non-residents unless reduced by an applicable treaty (e.g., US/UK treaty rates 5% or 15%); (ii) Investment Canada Act (ICA) review if a foreign investor later acquires control of a Canadian business above statutory financial thresholds, with a separate national security review for sensitive digital media businesses; (iii) for the Alberta entity, the Income Tax Act requires conversion of all foreign-currency revenue into CAD for reporting unless a section 261 functional-currency election (generally USD) is made.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada repealed mandatory foreign-exchange surrender with the abolition of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951; there is no statutory repatriation window, minimum holding period, or forced conversion ratio for export service receipts, including digital net settlements from Apple IAP or Paymentwall. The Alberta company may lawfully retain USD/EUR in Canadian-chartered foreign-currency accounts. De facto obligations arise solely from tax reporting, transfer-pricing arm's-length documentation, and CRA functional-currency rules, not from any surrender regime.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) exercises monetary policy and currency issuance; exchange-control competency is residual under the Minister of Finance pursuant to the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bretton Woods and Related Agreements Act. FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administers cross-border currency monitoring and AML/CTF reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). OSFI supervises deposit-taking bank conduct.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM, supplementary-only (never as primary treasury). Legal constraints: the Bank Act (s. 972.2) prohibits unauthorized deposit-taking in Canada, so an offshore EMI such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, or Mercury may provide Canadian-dollar custodial sub-accounts only if it is a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB) under PCMLTFA or partners with a Schedule I bank as an authorized agent. For this specific video-match app, the company is properly classified as a merchant (not an MSB) because all end-user payments are captured by Apple IAP and Paymentwall; the firm merely receives net settlements. Permitted structure: maintain one FINTRAC-registered EMI account (e.g., Wise Business) for low-cost USD/EUR corridor liquidity and small creator payouts, capped at under 5% of monthly treasury flow, with the main balance ledgered at a Tier 1 chartered bank to avoid unlicensed deposit risk and to satisfy banking due diligence on the entity's principal account.

Foreign shareholders wire via SWIFT MT103 directly to the company's designated USD or CAD account at the Canadian chartered bank (e.g., RBC SWIFT ROYCCAT2, TD SWIFT TDOMCATTTOR). Remittance field 70 should state 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' and field 72 should reference the board resolution date. Canada requires no regulatory pre-approval and no purpose-code permit; the receiving bank records the inward credit under standard corporate account treatment. For amounts above CAD 100,000 the bank performs EDD verification linking the remitter to the corporate registry and UBO schedule. An Investment Canada Act notification is not required for greenfield incorporation or organic share subscription of the Alberta operating entity, but if a foreign investor subsequently acquires control of an existing Canadian business, ICA net-benefit or national-security review may apply. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM, supplementary-only (never as primary treasury). Legal constraints: the Bank Act (s. 972.2) prohibits unauthorized deposit-taking in Canada, so an offshore EMI such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, or Mercury may provide Canadian-dollar custodial sub-accounts only if it is a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB) under PCMLTFA or partners with a Schedule I bank as an authorized agent. Permitted structure: maintain one FINTRAC-registered EMI account (e.g., Wise Business) for low-cost USD/EUR corridor liquidity and small creator payouts, capped at under 5% of monthly treasury flow, with the main balance ledgered at a Tier 1 chartered bank to avoid unlicensed deposit risk and to satisfy banking due diligence on the entity's principal account.
