Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); corporate registry portal: Alberta Corporate Registry; electronic services: CRA My Business Account portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity with Canadian activities must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or applying through CRA online. If the entity is incorporated outside Canada, it must also register extraprovincially with Alberta Corporate Registry before carrying on business in Alberta. For GST/HST purposes, the entity must register for a GST/HST account under the Excise Tax Act when it is not a small supplier and makes taxable supplies in Canada. The CRA will issue a distinct GST/HST program account number under the same BN. For corporate income tax, a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must file a T2 Corporation Income Tax Return and may need to elect or be deemed resident if incorporated federally or provincially.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian federal and Alberta provincial tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Legal personality governs classification: a Business Corporation is a separate taxable entity. Statutory elections available include: section 85 asset rollover election, section 256 amalgamation election, and GST/HST group registration election under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act for affiliated persons. No hybrid mismatch election is available; Canada applies mandatory hybrid mismatch rules in sections 18.4 to 18.8 of the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters in worldwide taxable supplies unless the non-resident is registered voluntarily; Alberta does not impose a provincial sales tax, so the effective registration threshold for GST/HST remains CAD 30,000 at the federal level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated supplies include goods exported from Canada, commercial goods shipped to a non-resident, and services performed directly for a non-resident if the services are performed wholly outside Canada or relate to exported goods and are supplied to a non-resident outside Canada. For financial newswire API services to non-residents, the supply is zero-rated where the service is intangible and delivered to the non-resident for use outside Canada. The supplier must retain sufficient contemporaneous evidence connecting the supply to an export, including contracts, billing addresses, IP geolocation, delivery logs, and foreign payment trails.

  • Canada Border Services Agency B13A Export Declaration or Automated Export Declaration record
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Contract or service agreement evidencing the foreign recipient and offshore use
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/payment confirmation proving foreign-source payment
  • Freight forwarder receipts and insurance certificates

Canada currently imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance API, or digital stamping for standard invoices. CRA accepts electronic invoices provided they are in an accessible, readable format and contain required disclosure elements. B2B e-invoicing through the Pan-Canadian PDF standard is voluntary. Mandatory record retention requires books and records to be kept at the entity's Canadian place of business or made electronically accessible to the CRA.

CRA may reassess zero-rated treatment for up to four years, or indefinitely where misrepresentation is attributable to negligence, fraud, or willful default. False export zero-rating claims attract penalties under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act, including 25% gross negligence penalties on the amount of tax avoided, compound arrears interest, and denial of input tax credits. Knowingly making false statements can lead to criminal prosecution with fines up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to five years, plus mandatory repayment of wrongly refunded GST/HST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada is an IMF Article VIII member: dividends, interest, royalties, and syndicated-news subscription proceeds are remittable without regulatory pre-clearance. Profit-repatriation restraint is fiscal only — Part XIII Canadian non-resident withholding tax at 25% on dividends (treaty-reduced, e.g., 5–15% under the Canada-U.S. Treaty for qualifying corporate shareholders). Inbound capital injection requires no Bank of Canada approval; an Investment Canada Act filing is triggered only by acquisitions of control, not greenfield treasury operations. The Alberta newswire entity may hold escrow in CAD, USD, or EUR without conversion mandate, and no minimum holding-period or negative-interest constraints apply to non-resident shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada has abolished statutory exchange controls: there is no surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export, service, or licensing receipts. The entity may retain 100% of USD/EUR API-subscription proceeds in foreign-currency accounts and may elect FX conversion timing at its sole commercial discretion. GST/HST zero-rating for exported financial-news services (ETA Section 7, non-resident recipient) applies but does not impose any FX compliance condition.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) — central monetary authority and RTGS settlement overseer; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) — prudential bank supervisor licensing Schedule I banks under the Bank Act; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre (FINTRAC) — AML/CFT surveillance; Foreign exchange administration flows from the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act. Canada maintains a free-floating CAD and has no separate exchange-control agency, capital-control office, or pre-approval mechanism for cross-border fund flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium (role-specific). Canada issues no standalone EMI banking licence; Wise, Stripe, and Payoneer operate as payment service providers (PSPs) subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA, in force November 2024) with mandatory registration at the Bank of Canada. Viability is High for API-based low-value payout and subscription collection rails, but Low as a primary treasury or escrow holder: PSPs are not deposit-taking banks, lack Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation protection, and must settle into a Schedule I account. Holding syndicated-finance escrow capital in a PSP only would contravene the Bank Act. Recommended structure: Schedule I bank as the treasury anchor with PSPs layered solely for disbursement efficiency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity subscription proceeds must route via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign corporate shareholder to the Alberta entity's USD or CAD account at a Schedule I bank. The remittance narrative must include the escrow agreement number and ISO purpose code L900 (corporate equity injection), with supplementary code 70 'CRED'. CAD clearing occurs through Payments Canada's Lynx RTGS (same-day final settlement) or ACSS (ACH) for amounts below CAD 25 million; USD clearing is effected via the Canadian bank's New York correspondent using Fedwire/CHIPS. No Bank Act or OSFI approval is required for the injection. Escrow segregation is mandatory: the Capital Escrow Account and Operational Account must be distinct ledger accounts, and release to operations requires a board drawdown resolution.
