Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada via CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO); Alberta corporate income tax is collected and administered by CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act, with Alberta Treasury Board and Finance as the policy authority.

E-government filing portal

After incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Business Corporations Act (Alberta), register the corporation with CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO). CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts, including the RC corporate income tax account and the RT GST/HST account. Non-resident corporations without a Canadian incorporation file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) and provide director information, registered office/agent details, and evidence of corporate existence; CRA may require a Canadian representative for service. No separate Alberta TIN is issued; the CRA BN is the statutory tax identification number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer; a corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident under ITA s. 250(4) and is taxable on worldwide income. A pure equity-holding SPV is not pass-through and cannot be disregarded for Canadian tax purposes. — After incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Business Corporations Act (Alberta), register the corporation with CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer; a corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident under ITA s. 250(4) and is taxable on worldwide income. A pure equity-holding SPV is not pass-through and cannot be disregarded for Canadian tax purposes.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian domestic law. Relevant statutory elections for a HoldCo include: Income Tax Act s. 85(1) tax-deferred rollover of property into a taxable Canadian corporation; s. 86 share-for-share exchange; s. 93(1) dividend election for distributions out of a foreign affiliate's exempt/ taxable surplus; s. 88(2) amalgamation; and Excise Tax Act s. 156 election between closely related corporations to treat intra-group supplies as made for nil consideration. No hybrid-mismatch election is available in Canada.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold of worldwide taxable supplies arising in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters; for a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada, the threshold applies to taxable supplies made in Canada. Passive dividends and share dispositions are exempt financial supplies under Schedule V of the Excise Tax Act and do not count toward the registration threshold for a pure equity-holding SPV.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act if they are delivered to a destination outside Canada and supported by documentary proof of export. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated under ETA s. 23 where the service is performed for a non-resident and is not excluded (e.g., Canadian real property or goods). For a pure equity-holding SPV, dividends and share sales are exempt financial supplies, not zero-rated; input tax credits are recoverable only on inputs attributable to taxable supplies.

  • CBSA export declaration / Canada Customs Coding Form B13A or electronic export reporting through the Canada Border Services Agency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, rail, or truck) or equivalent transportation document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer documentation evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Written proof of export or delivery memorandum, including destination and date of export

CRA does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-linked clearance gateway. E-invoicing is accepted if invoices satisfy the prescribed content requirements under the Excise Tax Act and are retained in English or French for six years. Large registrants are subject to mandatory electronic GST/HST filing through CRA-approved software; electronic records must be retained in an accessible machine-readable format.

CRA may audit export substantiation and deny zero-rating where documentary evidence does not establish export or delivery outside Canada. A zero-rating denial triggers reassessment, repayment of net GST/HST, recapture of claimed input tax credits, prescribed interest, and penalty provisions under the Excise Tax Act for false statements or omissions, including gross negligence penalties that can be up to 25% of the tax amount. Fraudulent export schemes can also attract criminal penalties, fines, and imprisonment for tax evasion, and the normal four-year reassessment period is extended in cases of misrepresentation or wilful default.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts under a free-floating exchange-rate regime; no Canadian restriction exists on dividend remittances, shareholder-loan repayments, or cross-border acquisition consideration. Non-resident dividend payments are subject to Part XIII withholding tax — statutory 25%, generally reduced to 5% under applicable tax treaties where the recipient owns ≥10% of the voting shares (e.g., Canada-US treaty Article 10(3)) — with NR4 information returns and Treasury remittance to the Receiver General. The Investment Canada Act (ISED review) is the sole material foreign-investment gateway and is not an exchange-control measure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada repealed its Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951; there is no statutory timeline, compulsory surrender ratio, minimum conversion percentage, or repatriation quota for export receipts or treasury inflows. Relevant compliance obligations are limited to FINTRAC cross-border currency reports for physical import/export of CAD 10,000 or more, large international EFT reports (EFTR) triggered at the CAD 10,000 threshold, and CRA reporting of foreign-source property (T1134/T1135) where applicable to the HoldCo.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) exercises monetary authority and supervision of payment, clearing and settlement systems; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated banks; FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no discretionary foreign-exchange control administration under the Currency Act or the Financial Administration Act, and no central-bank or government approval is required for capital-account or current-account transfers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate viability. Canada does not issue an EU-style e-money institution (EMI) licence; payment service providers are being brought into the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) regime, supervised by the Bank of Canada, with mandatory registration phased through 2024-2026. Offshore EMIs (e.g., FCA- or ECB-authorised) can provide rapid multi-currency onboarding and low-cost FX conversion but are not authorised Canadian deposit-taking institutions, cannot access Lynx high-value settlement, and attract elevated FINTRAC/PCMLTFA scrutiny. An offshore EMI is acceptable only as a secondary disbursement rail for small operational payments; it must never hold the SPV's primary treasury balance or capital-escrow funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan funding is routed by SWIFT MT103 from the overseas investor or group treasury to the HoldCo's Canadian Schedule I bank account. Recommended payment narrative: 'Capital Contribution — [Legal Entity Name] — [Board Resolution Ref]' with a bank-recognised purpose code (e.g., EQUI/CORP/CFIN or 'Capital Injection'). CAD-denominated transfers above ACSS thresholds settle through Lynx, Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement system (successor to LVTS). Pre-arrival steps include activating the receiving account, pre-validating the SWIFT BIC and account number, and issuing a spot FX instruction if the remittance currency is non-CAD. Both sending and receiving banks file FINTRAC EFTRs for transfers at or above CAD 10,000; the treasury archive must retain the covering board resolution, source-of-funds statement, MT103 confirmation, and bank credit advice.Outbound disbursements of a pure equity-holding SPV comprise (i) downstream intercompany subordinated loans, (ii) operating expenditures, and (iii) upstream dividends. Dividend remittances are executed through SWIFT MT103 wires in CAD/USD/EUR; prior to release, the SPV must remit Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced as applicable) and file NR4 information returns. For acquisition consideration, the recommended rail is SWIFT MT103 with a board-approved settlement schedule and, where needed, a conditional escrow instruction. A documentary commercial letter of credit (UCP 600) is permissible only where the target transaction expressly requires a trade-finance instrument — atypical for an ISIC 6420 HoldCo. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general rate (Federal Part I 15% + Alberta provincial 8%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, including a foreign parent SPV, unless a treaty reduction is successfully invoked.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the end of the taxation year (fiscal period end), irrespective of the accounting year end; no discretionary extension of the filing deadline is granted by the CRA. Balance of corporate tax payable is due within 2 months for non-CCPCs and within 3 months for CCPCs after the year-end; Alberta's provincial portion follows the federal due dates. Late filing accrues prescribed daily compound interest plus the penalty described below.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada imposes no monetary de minimis threshold for transfer-pricing documentation. Contemporaneous records documenting arm's-length terms of every non-arm's length cross-border transaction are conditionally required to defeat the s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of any net adjustment exceeding the lesser of 10% of gross revenue and C$3,000,000 in a given year. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) is mandatory only for MNE groups with annual consolidated revenue of €750 million (approximately C$1.1 billion); otherwise the SPV must still maintain full Master File/Local File-equivalent documentation to satisfy CRA's IC 87-2 'reasonable efforts' requirement.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime; substance is instead enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245), the foreign-affiliate 'active business' test under s. 95(1), and treaty Limitation-on-Benefits provisions. A zero-employee, zero-premise SPV interposed solely to extract dividends, generate deductible interest, or procure treaty rates will be recharacterized by CRA as a conduit: GAAR may eliminate the entire benefit and impose a 25% penalty; CRA will deny DTA relief under Article XXIX-A for failure of the ownership/base-erosion test; the SPV's income becomes fully taxable Part I at 23% and future dividends are trapped by Part XIII without credit. The Alberta 'economic substance' proxy is fixed by several factors, including the location of board meetings, presence of a treasury function, and evidence of commercial decisions; an SPV with no employees, no premises and no independent business purpose is the single strongest red flag for a CRA transfer-pricing audit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False T2 Declarations

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 239(1): fine between 50% and 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to 2 years per count; Criminal Code s. 380(1) fraud over C$5,000 attracts imprisonment up to 14 years. Civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the misstated amount under s. 163(2), plus prescribed compound interest. Voluntary disclosure does not protect willful or impossible-to-correct evasion, and the CRA audit may extend to the look-back period of 4-10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the SPV's unpaid Part I and Part XIII amounts under s. 227.1; CRA may register certificates and freeze directors' personal assets through exigible notification. Directors who signed the false T2 face personal criminal prosecution for evasion, travel restrictions at Canadian border crossings, loss of director eligibility under the Alberta Business Corporations Act, and civil actions by minority shareholders under the oppression remedy (ABC A s. 242).
Shell SPV / GAAR / Economic-Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245(3)) retroactively recharacterizes the transaction, disallowing all tax benefits; 25% additional penalty of the disallowed benefit is imposed under s. 245(4); a failure on LOB/PPT treaty tests restores full 25% Part XIII withholding. CRA's offshore investment bulletin conducts a separate anti-conduit investigation, and the SPV may be subject to substantive penalties for unsupported management-fee invoices.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who orchestrated the conduit will be treated as jointly liable for all re-assessed corporate tax, penalties, and interest; the Alberta courts may pierce the corporate veil to the directors' personal assets where the SPV is a façade, and the Crown may use the 'directing mind' doctrine to ground criminal liability on the shareholders rather than only the entity.
Unremitted Part XIII Withholding and Information Returns

Statutory sanction: The unwithheld amount is an absolute liability and a debt to the Crown under s. 227(9); CRA assesses the SPV for the gross amount, adds daily compound prescribed interest from the due date, and imposes a 10% civil penalty for failure to withhold in addition to the interest; the failure to file NR4 information returns adds a C$1,000 summary conviction fine per return.

Director personal liability: Under s. 227.1, directors are jointly and severally liable with the corporation for every unremitted Part XIII amount if the SPV defaults and the director knew or ought to have known of the failure; the due-diligence defense requires proof of an active and functioning supervision system, not nominal delegation; directors who resign after the arrears accrued remain liable; CRA can seek a departure embargo and asset-preservation order preventing the director from leaving Canada until the withholding debt is settled.
Transfer-Pricing Documentation Failure / Incorrect Allocation

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment for the year (under s. 247(3)) where reasonable arm's-length efforts are absent; CRA may make secondary adjustments that deem excess capital to be a shareholder loan, causing a 25% Part XIII withholding tax; loss carrybacks cannot be used to extinguish the adjusted income; Form T106 late filing is a separate penalty of C$100 to C$7,500 per month.

Director personal liability: Directors can be assessed personally for the TP penalty under the general directors' liability provision where the penalty constitutes a debt of the corporation and the SPV is insolvent; in Alberta insolvency courts will also entertain wrongful-trading claims against officers who authorized intercompany pricing without a proper functional analysis, potentially voiding director indemnity insurance.
