Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under Alberta law; a corporation may be incorporated with nil issued capital and shares may be issued for money, property or past services (s. 25 ABCA). Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal Part I corporate income tax, GST/HST, Part XIII withholding and, under the 2008 Alberta Tax Administration Agreement, Alberta provincial corporate income tax is assessed, collected and administered by CRA. Registration and filing portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/canada-revenue-agency-digital-services-businesses.html); Alberta corporate registry: Alberta Corporate Registration System (ACRS), Service Alberta.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate in Alberta under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) via ACRS, or federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act followed by extra-provincial registration in Alberta; the registration generates a Corporate Access Number. Step 2: CRA normally issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN) automatically upon incorporation through the Newly Incorporated Business stream; if not, file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or register online. Step 3: A non-resident corporation must provide proof of continued existence (foreign certificate of good standing), designate a Canadian representative/address for service, and, if applying for a GST/HST account, an authorized Canadian agent for records purposes under ETA subsection 286(2.1). Step 4: Activate relevant program accounts under the BN: GST/HST (RT) only when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 (four-quarter small-supplier test) or voluntarily to support zero-rating of exports; payroll (RP) if employees are engaged; income tax (RC) for Part XIII withholding on non-resident payments. For a Single Family Office earning primarily exempt financial supplies, GST registration is generally not required and is usually detrimental because ITCs are unavailable. Step 5: No Alberta corporate tax ID or separate provincial sales tax registration is required; Alberta has no PST and Alberta corporate income tax is reported through the federal T2 return using the same BN.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation with residence-based worldwide taxation; no pass-through eligibility for a Canadian business corporation. A Canadian-resident corporation is taxable under ITA subsection 2(2) and section 248(1) on worldwide income. Alberta general corporate rate: 8%; federal general rate: 15%; combined active business rate: 23%. For a Canadian-controlled private corporation earning aggregate investment income (the typical SFO HoldCo), the notional combined rate is approximately 46.7% (federal 38.67%, inclusive of 10% provincial abatement and 10.67% refundable top-up, plus Alberta 8%), of which approximately 30.7% is refundable through the Refundable Dividend Tax On Hand mechanism upon taxable dividend distributions, leaving an approximate net non-refundable burden of 16%. For a foreign-controlled (non-CCPC) holdco, the combined rate is 23%. Capital gains are subject to a one-half inclusion rate; eligible dividends are grossed up with a dividend tax credit at the shareholder level. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with residence-based worldwide taxation; no pass-through eligibility for a Canadian business corporation. A Canadian-resident corporation is taxable under ITA subsection 2(2) and section 248(1) on worldwide income. Alberta general corporate rate: 8%; federal general rate: 15%; combined active business rate: 23%. For a Canadian-controlled private corporation earning aggregate investment income (the typical SFO HoldCo), the notional combined rate is approximately 46.7% (federal 38.67%, inclusive of 10% provincial abatement and 10.67% refundable top-up, plus Alberta 8%), of which approximately 30.7% is refundable through the Refundable Dividend Tax On Hand mechanism upon taxable dividend distributions, leaving an approximate net non-refundable burden of 16%. For a foreign-controlled (non-CCPC) holdco, the combined rate is 23%. Capital gains are subject to a one-half inclusion rate; eligible dividends are grossed up with a dividend tax credit at the shareholder level. No tax-exempt zone regime exists in Alberta; deferral for foreign subsidiaries is governed by the foreign accrual property income (FAPI) rules under ITA sections 91-95.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; entity classification flows from corporate law, with CRA's Foreign Entity Classification rules (TIB-244R) governing hybrid foreign entities such as US LLCs. Available elections relevant to an SFO: (a) ITA section 85 rollover to transfer capital property into the corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (b) ITA section 83(2) capital dividend election to distribute tax-free Capital Dividend Account balances; (c) ITA section 89(1) paid-up capital elections; (d) ITA section 111(5.1) loss continuance election and section 111 loss carryover elections; (e) ETA section 150 election to treat closely related corporations as a single person for GST/HST (HoldCo + MgmtCo structures); (f) ETA section 156 election to deem supplies between closely related corporations to be for no consideration; (g) ITA section 216(4) election for non-resident owners of Canadian rental real estate held by the SFO; (h) ITA section 95(2)/(3) FAPI grouping and recharacterization elections for foreign affiliate structures; (i) ETA Part IX.1 election/designation as a selected listed financial institution where the SFO has permanent establishments in more than one province.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada abolished all exchange control restrictions in 1952 (repeal of the Exchange Control Act) and operates a free-floating currency regime. There are no Central Bank approvals, registration requirements, or quotas for inbound or outbound capital remittances. Profit repatriation (dividends, interest, royalties, liquidation proceeds) is unrestricted, subject solely to federal Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally 25% domestic rate, reduced by applicable treaty, e.g., 5% under the Canada–US treaty on arm's-length dividends and 15% on portfolio dividends; subpart F/CBC thin-cap rules apply separately).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada has no export proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no timing constraints on retaining foreign currency. Exporters and holding entities may maintain foreign currency accounts domestically or offshore without restriction; only general reporting obligations apply, namely FINTRAC reports for cross-border physical currency and monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more and Statistics Canada international trade declarations for statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC); prudential supervisions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); cross-border transaction surveillance and AML/CTF reporting administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), which oversees compliance with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating for the SFO treasury function: Low. Canadian federal banking law (Bank Act) requires a Schedule I or II bank charter to accept deposits; most offshore/European Fintech EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Wise, BlackBanx) are neither chartered in Canada nor prohibitively restricted for cross-border remittances but cannot offer insured deposits (CDIC coverage), prime brokerage, or custody-grade clearing. Their utility is limited to low-value spot conversion and correspondent settlement enhancements, with mandatory FINTRAC registration as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) where they operate in Canada. They are structurally unsuitable as the primary clearing and custody rail for a medium-risk SFO under Alberta securities custody expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocol: (1) foreign entity remits CAD or foreign currency via SWIFT MT103 to the SFO's operational account at a Big Five clearing bank; (2) domestic settlement occurs through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) in real-time; (3) ISO 20022 / SWIFT purpose codes must be populated (e.g., EQUI for equity contributions, INVE for general investment flows, or TRAD for trading settlement) and must exactly match the declared source-of-funds rationale in the bank's onboarding file; (4) supporting documentation (share subscription agreement, Director's Resolution, statement of source of funds, and for shareholder loans, a signed promissory note) must be retained for FATCA/CRS and bank surveillance audits; (5) no exchange control approval is required; funds credited in CAD are freely usable, but amounts over CAD 10,000 will generate FINTRAC reports if moved in physical form, and all electronic wire payments are recorded by FINTRAC's SWIFTR data capture for national threat assessment.Primary clearing rails: SWIFT MT103/MT202 for cross-border wires; LVTS for high-value Canadian-dollar domestic transfers; ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System, via EFT) for low-value recurring disbursements; and the emerging Real-Time Rail (RTR) for instant domestic retail payments. Trade-related disbursements are executed by bank-guaranteed Commercial Letters of Credit under ICC UCP 600 rules (advising/confirming bank in the seller's jurisdiction) and by standby LCs (ISP98) for performance guarantees. For listed-securities purchases, settlement flows through CDS (CDS Clearing and Depository Services Inc., part of TMX Group) via an approved Canadian custodian; cross-currency settlement of USD/CAD is cleared through the bank's U.S. correspondent network (e.g., RBC's U.S. entity, TD Bank, N.A.).
