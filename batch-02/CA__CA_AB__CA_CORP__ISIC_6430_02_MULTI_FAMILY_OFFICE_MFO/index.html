Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Number (BN) registration and GST/HST portal via My Business Account; Alberta Treasury Board and Finance (ATBF) for provincial Corporate Income Tax, administered and collected by CRA under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with CRA or apply online through CRA 'My Business Account' to obtain a 9-digit BN and associated GST/HST, corporate income tax, and payroll program accounts. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada must register for a BN; no separate Alberta provincial registration is required as Alberta's corporate income tax is collected by CRA. Supporting documentation includes the certificate of incorporation, registered office address, and proof of Canadian resident director or applicable provincial licence.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with CRA or apply online through CRA 'My Business Account' to obtain a 9-digit BN and associated GST/HST, corporate income tax, and payroll program accounts. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada must register for a BN; no separate Alberta provincial registration is required as Alberta's corporate income tax is collected by CRA. Supporting documentation includes the certificate of incorporation, registered office address, and proof of Canadian resident director or applicable provincial licence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian law; corporations are taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. Available elections include: (i) Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status for the small business deduction; (ii) GST/HST group registration under s.156 ETA; (iii) hybrid mismatch adjustments under ss.18.2/18.3 ITA; (iv) functional currency election under s.261 ITA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies for the current calendar quarter and the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident suppliers of digital or remote services are also required to register if they exceed the same CAD 30,000 threshold of Canadian-source supplies in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported or services rendered to non-residents who are not in Canada, but financial services are generally exempt unless supplied to non-residents and not related to the administration of a Canadian investment plan. MFO services (fee-based portfolio advisory and discretionary investment management) may qualify as zero-rated exports if the recipient is a non-resident throughout the supply, no goods or real property in Canada are involved, and the service is not characterized as an exempt 'financial service' under ETA Schedule VI Part IX.

  • CBSA Canada Customs Export Declaration (G7 / Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED))
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing export from Canada
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Evidence of non-resident status of recipient (e.g., overseas address, passport/ID, certificate of residence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire payment record from the non-resident client
  • Executed service agreement and detailed service scope

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime is in effect. Registered suppliers must issue tax invoices containing prescribed disclosures (supplier name and GST/HST number, transaction date, taxable supply description, consideration, and tax payable); electronic invoices are permitted. CRA may require electronic access to books and records. Platform operators may be deemed to make underlying supplies in the marketplace context.

CRA may reassess GST/HST for up to four years (or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect), deny zero-rating on the basis of insufficient or contemporaneous documentation, charge arrears interest, and impose penalties of up to 25% of the tax amount under s.285.1 ETA, plus an additional 50% of the benefit for gross negligence; criminal prosecution under s.327 ETA carries fines up to CAD 100,000 and/or imprisonment. Retroactive clawbacks are standard when export documentation is incomplete or inconsistent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible, and Canada generally imposes no controls on capital-account transfers, foreign investment, dividends, interest, or repatriation of investment proceeds. Transfers remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, tax law, corporate-law restrictions, contractual covenants, and bank due diligence. An Alberta corporation may remit dividends only when authorized under the applicable corporate statute and when statutory solvency tests are met. Payments to non-residents may trigger Canadian withholding tax, subject to domestic exemptions and applicable tax treaties. A multi-family office providing discretionary portfolio advice may require registration under provincial securities laws; custody, dealing, investment-fund, or portfolio-management activities can create additional registration and compliance obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds or convert a prescribed portion of foreign-currency receipts into Canadian dollars. Export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted commercially. Exporters must comply with applicable customs, sanctions, tax, contractual, and bank documentation requirements; banks may request invoices, shipping evidence, client instructions, and the payment purpose.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing exchange controls. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions supervises federally regulated financial institutions; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada administers applicable anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing reporting requirements. Alberta securities regulation is administered by the Alberta Securities Commission.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for an offshore electronic-money institution as the primary Canadian banking or client-funds solution. Canada does not generally recognize a foreign EMI account as a substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking institution or for legally compliant custody or client-money arrangements. Use may be possible for the corporation's own operating funds, subject to the provider's geographic eligibility, Canadian payment access, sanctions screening, source-of-funds checks, and contractual terms. Do not route client assets through an operating account or payment intermediary unless the arrangement is expressly permitted by securities, trust, custody, and payment-services requirements. Verify the provider's Canadian regulatory status and any applicable Retail Payment Activities Act registration obligations.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Alberta Securities Commission registration analysis; registration certificates or documented exemption basis; service descriptions and client agreements; organizational and supervisory charts; custody and client-asset flow diagrams; compliance policies; and evidence that banking accounts are not being used to commingle client assets.

Unsubstantiated business substance, inconsistent stated revenue, or inability to explain the economic purpose of cross-border transfers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Receive capital contributions and shareholder loans into a corporate account held in the corporation's legal name at a Canadian financial institution.
