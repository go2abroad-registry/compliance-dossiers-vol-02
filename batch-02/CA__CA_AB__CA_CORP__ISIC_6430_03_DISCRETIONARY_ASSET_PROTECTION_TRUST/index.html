Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), operating under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement for Alberta; statutory portals: CRA My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html), Business Registration Online (BRO), and GST/HST NETFILE. Alberta does not administer a separate corporate income tax, so all federal and Alberta corporate and GST/HST filings are made solely through the CRA.

E-government filing portal

A foreign or domestic entity becomes a taxpayer by obtaining a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN). Procedure: (1) file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) through Business Registration Online, or by mail, attaching proof of existence (articles of incorporation, certificate of amalgamation, or extra-provincial registration); (2) open each required CRA program account: RC (Corporate Income Tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (Payroll), and RM (Import/Export); (3) for non-resident corporations, appoint an agent for service in Canada and, where no permanent establishment exists, provide security at CRA's discretion; (4) for Alberta, complete the extra-provincial registration with the Alberta Corporate Registry, which updates the CRA registry; and (5) where the corporation acts as trustee of an irrevocable offshore spendthrift trust, deliver the trust deed and beneficiary ownership schedule under the Trust Reporting Rules (ITA s. 150(0.1)) and AML/KYC documentation to satisfy CRA beneficial-ownership and FINTRAC requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; classification is statutory and mandatory. A Business Corporation is always a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Relevant elections for this asset-protection-trust structure include: (a) ITA s. 85 rollover election on transfers of property to the corporation or to a trust; (b) ITA s. 93(1) election on dividends from foreign affiliates; (c) ITA s. 104(2) election under which a trust's income retains estate-tax treatment for specified beneficiaries; (d) ITA s. 104(5.3) election post-1971 spousal/alter-ego trust treatment; (e) ITA s. 216(1) election for non-residents to pay net Part XIII tax instead of Part I tax on Canadian-source rental or timber income; and (f) hybrid mismatch rules under Part VI.1 contain no elective carve-outs, so CRA requires statutory characterisation of hybrid instruments to prevent double non-taxation. The 21-year deemed disposition rule for trusts (ITA s. 104(4)) is non-elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the GST/HST small-supplier threshold, measured over a single calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters based on worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies. A non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada must register when the threshold is exceeded and may voluntarily register below it. For a corporation whose business is fiduciary administration of offshore spendthrift trusts, registration is mandatory irrespective of the threshold if the corporation is a selected financial institution or has made the ETA s. 243(1) election.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% GST/HST rate applies to: (i) goods exported by a carrier or by the recipient per ETA s. 21(1)(a), provided the export is completed within the statutory time frame and evidenced by prescribed records; and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident per ETA s. 23(1), provided the services are not performed on real or tangible personal property situated in Canada and do not constitute a financial service. Fiduciary administration of an irrevocable offshore spendthrift trust is generally a financial service under ETA s. 123(1), so the management fee is typically exempt under Schedule V Part VII rather than zero-rated; however, where the administration is performed wholly offshore, the recipient is a non-resident trust with no Canadian-resident beneficiaries or settlor, and no Canadian property is administered, CRA accepts zero-rating upon a complete documentary trail establishing non-resident status and the absence of Canadian nexus.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canada Export Reporting System (CERS) for goods exceeding CAD 2,000 or controlled/prohibited goods
  • Commercial invoice or pro forma invoice referencing the GST/HST registration number and export sale identifier
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), Air Waybill (air), or CERS ITN receipt for rail/truck movement proving physical departure from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 wire confirmation, or equivalent payment evidence establishing receipt of export proceeds
  • Non-resident status proof for the offshore trust: certified trust deed, passport or FATCA W-8BEN-E self-certification of settlor and beneficiaries, and CRA Form RC517 security election where applicable
  • GST/HST information return (Form GST191) or Schedule D input tax credit calculation reflecting zero-rated supply reporting

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance API validation like SIL or VAT-servers. Separate obligations apply: (1) GST/HST registrants with annual gross revenue above CAD 1.5 million must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or an approved API gateway; (2) every supplier must issue invoices bearing the supplier name, GST/HST registration number, date, description, and tax amount; (3) books and records must be retained in readable electronic form under ETA s. 286(1); (4) the Pan-Canadian e-invoice standard (PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0) is voluntary and not legally mandatory; and (5) a fiduciary company providing financial services must comply with CRA's selected financial institution rules, including filing of annual information returns under ETA s. 239.

CRA audits zero-rated export claims strictly against the prescribed evidence. Missing or inconsistent documentation results in denial of zero-rating, reassessment at 5% GST, disallowance of input tax credits, and arrears interest. A false statement or omission made knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence triggers a penalty equal to 25% of the tax payable (ETA s. 280(3) and s. 285.1), an unlimited reassessment window (ETA s. 298(1.1)), and, for misconduct, prosecution under Criminal Code s. 380 with fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years on indictment. Additionally, ITA s. 163(2) imposes a 50% penalty on understated income tax, and CRA retains the right to offset GST/HST refunds, seize non-resident security, and refer significant wrongdoing to FINTRAC for AML enforcement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII status. The Canadian dollar is freely floating; there are no exchange controls, currency surrender requirements, escalator approval regimes, or repatriation constraints affecting cross-border fund movements. Profit and dividend remittances from a Canadian business corporation are generally unrestricted, subject to corporate law solvency tests, applicable withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act (e.g., Part XIII for non-residents), and normal AML/ATF monitoring. Discretionary asset protection trusts may remit income and capital abroad freely, provided the trustee holds adequate records and meets filing/reporting obligations under the Income Tax Act and the Criminal Code proceeds-of-crime provisions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, forced conversion, or repatriation timeline on export receipts. Exporters and commercial entities may hold foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts, convert it at market rates, or use it for trade settlements without central bank approval. For corporations operating in Alberta, there is likewise no provincial foreign-exchange surrender requirement; only standard reporting under tax law and FINTRAC applies to large cross-border EFTs at CAD 10,000 or above.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; prudential supervision of banks and trust companies by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); no dedicated foreign exchange control administration exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Canadian law does not confer bank or trust-company status on offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs); an offshore EMI may only operate in Canada as a Money Services Business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC, and cannot lawfully hold fiduciary trust funds, escrow balances, or deposit accounts for a Canadian asset protection trust. EMIs can serve as ancillary cross-border payment facilitators and FX conversion providers, but the primary trust and escrow clearing functions must sit at a Canadian regulated bank or licensed trust company.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are cleared through SWIFT MT103 wires routed to the trust's segregated Capital Escrow Account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. CAD-dominated payments settle through the LYNX large-value payment system; USD-denominated wires are cleared via SWIFT with correspondent banking through CHIPS or Fedwire as applicable. The instruction must show the original settlor/trustor as the remitting account holder and include the narrative reference: 'Capital contribution to irrevocable trust — [Trust legal name], [date of trust deed]'. Canadian law does not mandate a specific purpose code, but the bank's compliance program requires source-of-funds documentation, a fully executed trust deed excerpt, and a trustee resolution instructing the injection prior to booking. No foreign exchange license or central bank pre-approval is required.Outbound trust asset disbursements and trade settlements are executed via SWIFT MT103 for value transfer; commercial letters of credit are issued under UCP600 through the Canadian bank's trade finance desk using MT700 (issue), MT707 (amendment), and MT752 (authorization) message types. For low-value domestic movement, CAD Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) and Real-Time Rail (RTR) provide conditional finality; for cross-border USD, the bank's US clearing reflection and correspondent network ensure same-day or next-day value.
