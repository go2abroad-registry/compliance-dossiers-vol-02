Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; My Business Account portal and GST/HST NETFILE (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html)

E-government filing portal

Canadian resident business corporations obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA following federal or Alberta incorporation; GST/HST registration is effected through Form RC1 or online via CRA's Registering for a Business Number service. Foreign non-resident entities carrying on business in Canada, or otherwise supplying taxable supplies in Canada, must apply for a BN and relevant program accounts (e.g., GST/HST) using the same form, designating a Canadian agent/representative and maintaining statutory records in Canada. CRA issues the 9-digit BN integrated with program accounts such as RD (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act, elections include the s.216 election for non-residents receiving Canadian services/employment income, s.219 election for non-resident corporations, and ss.18.2/18.3 hybrid mismatch elections. Under the Excise Tax Act, GST/HST s.156 election permits closely related corporations to zero-rate internal supplies. No US-style 'check-the-box' transparency election exists; entity classification is statutory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in worldwide annual taxable supplies (including remote supplies); registrants exceeding the small-supplier threshold must register. Non-residents supplying exempt financial services such as factoring are generally not required to register unless carrying on business through a permanent establishment in Canada; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits/rebates.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) supplies of goods exported by the supplier or delivered to a foreign destination in accordance with Schedule VI, Part V; (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered under the Excise Tax Act and not a resident of Canada, provided the service is not performed principally in Canada and is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property; and (c) financial services, including factoring, discounting receivables, and reverse-factoring arrangements, supplied to a non-resident recipient not registered, provided the supply is not in respect of Canadian-situs property, securities, or entitlements. Claims must be supported by contemporaneous documentation and reported at 0% on the GST/HST return.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CAES / G13)
  • Commercial Invoice / Statement of Account
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Cargo Control Document / Carrier Receipt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or bank wire payment confirmation
  • Factoring, Assignment, or Reverse Factoring Agreement establishing the cross-border supply and consideration

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable revenues over C$1M; e-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (Peppol / EN 16931).

CRA may reassess beyond the normal 3-year limitation period where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, or wilful default is established. GST/HST penalties include 5% of net tax owing plus 1% per month (capped at 10%), and the gross-negligence penalty of 25% of net GST/HST (or 50% of income tax payable) for false statements or participation in false statements. Improper export zero-rating claims result in full restoration of tax, interest, denial of refunds, cancellation of input-tax-credit carryforwards, and civil or criminal prosecution with fines up to C$100,000 and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account under Canada's OECD commitments. Profit remittances in the form of dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees are freely transferable abroad without central bank approval; however, payments to non-residents may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act. Cross-border funds transfers are reported through FINTRAC for AML purposes but do not require Bank of Canada authorization. Capital account operations, including equity injections and shareholder loans, are likewise unrestricted for non-resident investors.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a central bank or to any Canadian authority. Exporters may retain receipts in foreign currency, deposit them in offshore or domestic foreign-currency bank accounts, and repatriate or convert them at any time. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no prescribed surrender timeline. The only compliance duties relate to ordinary tax record-keeping, FINTRAC large transaction and EFT reporting, and customs/trade document retention under the Customs Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada and Department of Finance Canada; no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration exists because Canada operates under a liberalized legal framework with no general exchange control legislation

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms have medium-low viability as primary banking rails. They are not licensed banks in Canada and cannot provide deposit-taking accounts, Bank of Canada settlement access, or CDIC-insured CAD custody. Non-Canadian EMIs may support FX conversion, card issuance, and payment initiation, but any payment service provider carrying on retail payment activity in Canada must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act and have a Canadian legal presence. For a trade-supply-chain factoring entity, an offshore EMI can only serve as a supplementary FX/payment gateway, never as the principal escrow or liquidity clearing bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit
