Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal administration of the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST). Primary portal: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html), supplemented by Business Registration Online (BRO) and GST/HST NETFILE for electronic return filing. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Alberta Tax Collection Agreement; the Alberta Corporate Tax and Revenue Administration sets provincial policy only.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Business Corporation either federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (online through Corporations Canada) or provincially under the Alberta Business Corporations Act using the Alberta Corporate Registry; no minimum capital requirement applies. Step 2: Obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts'); a foreign-incorporated non-resident entity may also obtain a BN through the CRA non-resident account registration path. Step 3: Simultaneously open program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RC (corporation income tax), and RP (payroll) if human resources exist; registration is compulsory where worldwide taxable supplies exceed C$30,000 over four consecutive quarters, but a buyout SPV should register voluntarily to sustain input tax credit (ITC) claims on acquisition, financing, legal, and due diligence costs. Step 4: Where investors or lender counterparties are non-residents, file Form NR301 or a treaty-based waiver under Part XIII to reduce or exempt Canadian withholding tax on interest and dividends; issue NR4 or T4A-NR information slips. Step 5: Activate CRA My Business Account for electronic notices of assessment, direct deposit of refunds, GST/HST elections, and audit correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an incorporated SPV is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act and is deemed resident in Canada under ITA s.250(1); resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income, while non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source income under ITA s.2(3). The SPV is not eligible for pass-through classification, and the small business deduction is denied to specified investment businesses or holding vehicles; combined headline corporate rate in Alberta for a non-SBD entity is 23% (15% federal general rate plus 8% Alberta general rate), absent deemed taxable Canadian corporation treaty planning.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; a corporation is irrebuttably subject to entity-level taxation. However, the following statutory elections are available for a PE buyout group: (1) Excise Tax Act s.156(2) 'closely related corporation' election deeming inter-corporate supplies of taxable services to be made for nil consideration, eliminating self-supply GST leakage; (2) ETA s.150 financial-service election allowing a member of a closely related group to elect that exempt financial services supplied to another member be treated as a separate supply for ITC preservation; (3) ETA s.173 joint venture election designating a single participant as operator for GST/HST compliance; (4) hybrid mismatch rules codified at ITA ss.222.1–222.4 (BEPS Action 2) operate as mandatory primary and imported-mismatch responses, not elections, and will deny deductions where an expense or loss recognized in Canada is also deductible in a foreign jurisdiction (e.g., through a US LLC, limited partnership, or hybrid instrument); no elective relief exists against those rules, so capital structure documentation must demonstrate economic substance and non-mismatched treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold is based on total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies, but excluding exempt financial services and capital property dispositions) in the current calendar quarter and the preceding four calendar quarters. A holding SPV earning only exempt interest and dividends may remain unregistered, but voluntary registration is customary to claim ITCs on professional fees and debt-issuance costs attributable to the enterprise.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the ETA where the registrant supplies goods and exports them directly, or delivers them to a common carrier or consignee for export, provided the goods leave Canada within the prescribed time and export proof is secured. Services: zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 where supplied to a non-resident and either (a) the service is wholly performed outside Canada, or (b) the service is performed in Canada but in relation to foreign real property or tangible personal property situated outside Canada at the time of performance; services rendered to a non-resident while that person is physically present in Canada are excluded. For a PE buyout SPV: management, structuring, and monitoring fees charged to offshore portfolio companies qualify for zero-rating only if rendered outside Canada and not attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment; interest, dividend, and switched-fund returns are exempt financial supplies, not zero-rated, so ITCs allocated to them are restricted unless the closely related election and proper fair-value allocation methods are deployed.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) system or via a licensed customs broker (Form B13A/G12 equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods from Canada to the foreign destination, with carrier certification or customs stamp
  • Commercial Invoice stating the exporter, foreign consignee, value in Canadian dollars, and 10-digit HS classification

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or mandatory digital-stamping regime. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records where they retain the prescribed information content under ETA s.223, and e-invoicing is mandatory only for suppliers to federal government procurement through the Pan-European Public Procurement OnLine (Peppol) network. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE on a monthly, quarterly, or annual cycle; CRA may require the electronic production of books and records in a prescribed machine-readable format under ETA s.286, enabling data-mining audit techniques but without pre-clearance approval.

CRA audit teams verify contemporaneous export documentation; where proof of export is absent, incomplete, or obtained later than two years after the supply, or where goods are diverted to domestic use, the 0% rate is denied and 5% GST is assessed on the full consideration, with daily compound interest under ETA s.280. Gross negligence penalty of 50% of the net tax understatement applies under ETA s.285(1); failure to register triggers penalties of up to C$10,000; false statements attract C$100-per-incident penalties escalating to C$25,000; and criminal tax evasion under ETA s.97 exposes fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. For an SPV, retrospective clawback extends to ITCs previously recovered on acquisition and financing costs where the supporting zero-rating conditions are not substantiated, producing both uncollectible GST and loss of input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has unrestricted capital and current account convertibility for residents and non-residents. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely under a market-determined exchange rate. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, interest payments, and repatriation of contributed capital are not subject to exchange approvals or quantitative limits. The only structural frictions are withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (e.g., 25% on dividends/interest to non-residents, subject to treaty reduction under the Canada-US Tax Treaty and other applicable DTAAs). Additional reporting obligations exist under section 233.3 of the Income Tax Act (Foreign Income Verification) and FINTRAC transaction reporting thresholds. No convertibility permit or central bank authorization is required for an Alberta private-equity buyout SPV.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no statutory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters, including SPVs, may retain foreign currency accounts at any Schedule I Canadian bank or authorized foreign bank branch. Receipts in USD or other currencies may be held, converted, or used directly for cross-border disbursement at the holder's discretion; only standard FINTRAC record-keeping and large transaction reporting obligations apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally chartered banks, while foreign-exchange and monetary matters fall under the Bank of Canada and the Currency Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as the primary treasury bank for a Canada/ Alberta PE buyout SPV; Medium as a supplementary payment gateway for disbursement efficiency. Legal Constraints: Canada has no domestic EMI licence equivalent to the EU regime. Fintech entities providing payment services must meet the registration requirements under the Retail Payment Activities Act, administered by the Bank of Canada. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK/HK/FCA-licensed entities) may not accept deposits, issue lending, or hold client funds as principal treasury accounts in Canada. Use of a foreign EMI for outbound trade or investor capital calls is permissible as an ancillary rail, but account balances are not CDIC-insured and Canadian banks retain exclusive deposit-taking privileges for CAD operational liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity and shareholder loan injections must be executed by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer using the SPV's exact legal name on field 59, with the contributing entity as originator on field 50K. Recommended purpose-of-payment wording: 'Equity capital contribution — LBO acquisition SPV' or 'Shareholder loan — cross-border funding'. CAD same-day settlement is effected through Lynx, the Bank of Canada's high-value payment system (successor to LVTS); USD funds are settled in the Canadian bank's Federal Reserve, CHIPS, or Fedwire correspondent account. The transfer must be preceded by an account-opening cash-flow forecast to avoid automatic wire hold. Under Canada's Currency Act, there is no exchange control permit, currency conversion certification, or central bank notification for inbound investment capital. The paying bank is obligated to ensure the underlying corporate registry filings and Part XIII withholding exposure (for interest-only tranches) are disclosed. FINTRAC and the bank's AML program must be satisfied with the source of the injected funds; pre-identified investor schedule is mandatory.Outbound trade settlements for the buyout SPV must use SWIFT MT103 for high-value cleared payments and, where seller or lender security is required, a commercial letter of credit under UCP600 (SWIFT MT700) issued by the Canadian relationship bank.The SPV's disbursement drawer is to be restricted to: (1) purchase price payments to sellers via escrow release; (2) repayment of bridging acquisition debt to the LBO lender syndicate; (3) distribution of management fees or transaction expenses to law firms, financial advisors, and tax advisors; (4) LBO cash-pool sweeps to treasury accounts. CAD transfers are cleared in Lynx, while USD disbursements run through Fedwire/CHIPS via the bank's correspondent. For cross-currency disbursements, settlement-in-FX is concluded under ISDA FX definitions, and all payments to a paying agent must include ISIN or legal-entity code of the target. Payments Canada's retail rails (e.g., Automated Clearing Settlement System or Real-Time Rail) are unsuitable for LBO funding and must not be used for closing funds. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low as the primary treasury bank for a Canada/ Alberta PE buyout SPV; Medium as a supplementary payment gateway for disbursement efficiency. Legal Constraints: Canada has no domestic EMI licence equivalent to the EU regime. Fintech entities providing payment services must meet the registration requirements under the Retail Payment Activities Act, administered by the Bank of Canada. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK/HK/FCA-licensed entities) may not accept deposits, issue lending, or hold client funds as principal treasury accounts in Canada. Use of a foreign EMI for outbound trade or investor capital calls is permissible as an ancillary rail, but account balances are not CDIC-insured and Canadian banks retain exclusive deposit-taking privileges for CAD operational liquidity.
