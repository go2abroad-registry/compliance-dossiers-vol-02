Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account portal (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals/business-account.html); Alberta Corporate Registry for provincial incorporation; Corporations Canada for federal incorporation

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain a Canadian federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or using CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). The BN is the umbrella identifier for all tax accounts, including the GST/HST account, corporate income tax account, and payroll deduction account. If the entity is incorporated in Alberta, it must register with the Alberta Corporate Registry; if federally incorporated and carrying on business in Alberta, it must complete extra-provincial registration. The non-resident corporation may register directly online or appoint an authorized agent with written authorization. The CRA may require proof of a Canadian address or third-party service address for service of notices. Upon registration, the CRA issues a BN plus a two-letter program account suffix (e.g., RC for corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box regime exists. Under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and Excise Tax Act, available elections include: section 216 election for non-resident corporations deriving rental income or timber royalties; section 219 branch tax election; section 273 of the Excise Tax Act for GST/HST election between closely related corporations to simplify reporting; election for eligible intercompany supplies; and hybrid mismatch adjustments under the Income Tax Act (including s. 163.2 penalties) implementing BEPS Action 2. Provincial tax elections follow federal T2-based calculations; Alberta does not impose a separate capital tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) calculated over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents making no taxable supplies to recipients in Canada other than zero-rated exports are generally not required to register, but may voluntarily register under the Excise Tax Act for input tax credit recovery; once registered, the entity remains required to file GST/HST returns even if supplies fall below threshold, until revocation is approved.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act applies to supplies of goods exported by the supplier (s. 1 of Part V of Schedule VI), supplies of services performed wholly outside Canada (s. 7), and supplies of services provided to a non-resident person that are not in connection with real property in Canada or a taxable supply in Canada (s. 1 of Part V, Schedule VII). For an equity crowdfunding portal, platform service fees charged to non-resident issuers are typically zero-rated if the service is supplied to a non-resident and the service does not relate to making a taxable supply in Canada. The portal must obtain a written declaration, business registration evidence, and residential or place-of-business proof from each non-resident recipient. For goods exported, the exporter must demonstrate physical export within a reasonable time; for services, contemporaneous documentation must show where the service was performed and for whom.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration (B13A or approved electronic equivalent)
  • Commercial invoice with zero-rating notation and HST exemption clause
  • Bill of lading, waybill, or freight forwarder's cargo receipt
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank transfer confirmation evidencing foreign proceeds
  • Signed service agreement with the non-resident issuer or counterparty
  • Foreign exchange buy/sell contract and payment ledger
  • Proof of non-resident status (certificate of incorporation, tax ID, or residency declaration)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or API-based digital stamping regime applies. Electronic invoicing is permitted, but every GST/HST invoice must contain prescribed information under section 3 of the Excise Tax Act Regulations, including supplier BN, recipient business name, transaction date, description, tax amount, and tax rate. Filing is electronic via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI for registrants. Digital record-keeping and CRA audit access to electronic books are mandatory. Certain industries operate under CRA-issued electronic filing specifications, but the crowdfunding portal is not subject to real-time invoice clearance.

CRA routinely scrutinizes zero-rating claims through matching customs export data, banking inflows, and service agreements. A denied zero-rating results in reassessment of GST/HST plus interest (prescribed rate compounded daily), reinstatement of penalties under Excise Tax Act section 280 (failure to file), section 281.1 (false or misleading statements), and gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the tax avoided. Companies face interest under the Income Tax Act for unreported taxable amounts, prosecution for wilful evasion, levy on unremitted taxes, and director liability under the Excise Tax Act. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are retroactively clawed back for the entire audit period and may trigger criminal referral.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has a fully convertible currency under a floating exchange-rate regime. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are free from exchange-control restrictions, including profit remittances, dividend distributions, and foreign direct investment returns. No prior regulatory approval is required for repatriation. Financial institutions must report electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC, and issuers must comply with withholding tax and securities-law disclosure obligations on distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export receipts. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency accounts with domestic banks without restriction, subject to FINTRAC reporting thresholds and anti-money-laundering recordkeeping.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration shared with the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for transaction monitoring and reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate to high for licensed Canadian money services businesses (MSBs) and non-bank payment service providers that are registered with FINTRAC and, where applicable, hold a provincial money lender or payment-services licence. Offshore digital EMIs without Canadian registration may provide cross-border payout services only on a passive cross-border basis, but active solicitation or settlement of Canadian retail crowdfunding investors requires a domestic MSB registration and compliance with National Instrument 45-110 (NI 45-110) and applicable Alberta securities laws. Use of an offshore EMI as the sole settlement rail is legally constrained and operationally risky; a Canadian-registered MSB or a Schedule I bank with escrow-capable trust accounts is preferred.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign-currency or CAD capital contributions from investors must be remitted by wire to a segregated escrow account in Canada held by a licensed bank or trust company, with the investor name, offering name, and subscription reference included in the SWIFT MT103 beneficiary information. The bank account title must clearly indicate escrow/trust status. FINTRAC registers all electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. Fund origin, beneficial owner, and source-of-wealth documentation must be verified before acceptance. For non-resident investors, no exchange-control approval is needed, but the reverse side of the offering documents must confirm compliance with Canada's foreign-investor tax and securities exemptions.Outbound settlement payments to vendors, service providers, and refunded investors use SWIFT MT103 payment messages for fiat transfers via correspondent banking corridors, and SWIFT MT202 for interbank or nostro account transfers. Commercial letters of credit are used for trade-linked disbursements with the issuing bank receiving compliant presentation documentation. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal period-end. The balance-due day for taxes is generally the last day of the 3rd month after fiscal year-end for a CCPC, and the 2nd month after fiscal year-end for non-CCPCs. Quarterly instalments are required for large corporations.

Line 431 (Alberta Tax) Alberta provincial corporate income tax calculated on the Alberta schedule: 8% general rate, or 2% for the first CAD 500,000 of eligible CCPC active business income. Allocate Alberta taxable income based on the prescribed permanent-establishment formula (Schedule A5) if the non-resident has a qualifying permanent establishment in Alberta. Late filing penalty of 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month, up to 12 months; repeated failure to file an additional 10% federal penalty; late-payment interest compounded daily at the CRA prescribed rate; gross negligence penalties up to 50% of unreported income; additional penalties under s.162(2) and s.162(7) for non-compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million aggregated non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents in the fiscal year triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) and Form T106 filing; Master File documentation becomes mandatory for taxpayers whose consolidated group gross revenue exceeds CAD 20 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute as in low-tax jurisdictions, but the CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule, treaty-benefit denial, and recharacterisation principles where a corporation lacks commercial substance. An equity crowdfunding portal must demonstrate real Alberta presence: leased office, resident directors/managers, active board meetings, executed vendor agreements, payroll, and the required securities licences and controls.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act: on indictment, penalties of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years (summary conviction: up to 2 years); civil penalty under s.163(2) of the greater of 50% of evaded tax and CAD 4,000; interest accrues daily on arrears; the CRA may re-audit and issue levy against assets. The Alberta Securities Commission may simultaneously revoke the portal registration and issue administrative fines up to CAD 2 million per contravention.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST pursuant to s.227.1 of the Income Tax Act. Where fraud or fraudulent preference is proven, the Alberta Business Corporations Act allows a court to pierce the corporate veil, rendering directors personally liable for civil damages, restitution, and award costs. Criminal directors face imprisonment, asset-seizure and formal travel bans while under investigation.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny expense deductions, recharacterise revenue under GAAR, reallocate income under Canada's transfer-pricing rules (s.247), and assess default penalties of 10% of gross transfer-pricing adjustment. Alberta’s securities regulator may suspend or cancel the exempt-market dealer or crowdfunding licence for failure to maintain a head office and essential staff; corporate registration may be cancelled for failure to return a statutory declaration or file annual returns.

Director personal liability: Directors may be named in CRA director-liability assessments for taxes, interest, and penalties arising from unremitted withheld amounts. Courts will disregard the corporate separation if the corporation is a façade for personal liability or if directors personally controlled the route of funds; civil judgments may include disgorgement, freezing orders, and, in cases of fraud, imprisonment of up to 14 years under s.380 of the Criminal Code for fraud over CAD 1 million.
AML Deficiency / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 10 million per violation for corporations (and up to CAD 2 million for individuals) per occurrence; further CRA penalties for unregistered MSB activity; the Alberta Securities Commission may impose cease-trade orders and license revocation; FINTRAC may publish the violation and impose cross-border sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable only when they knowingly directed or authorised the AML breach. For active participation in money laundering or terrorist financing, the Criminal Code imposes imprisonment up to 10 years (money laundering). Courts may issue travel bans pending the criminal case, order restitution, and in serious cases permit the forfeiture of personal and corporate assets traceable to the unlawful activity.
