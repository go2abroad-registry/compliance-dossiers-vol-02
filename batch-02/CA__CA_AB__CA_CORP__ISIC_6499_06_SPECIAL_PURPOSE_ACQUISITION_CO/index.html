Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (MyBA); https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident SPAC incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Alberta Business Corporations Act must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA. File Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) electronically via BRO or by mail/courier to the CRA's Business Accounts Registration desk, providing the Articles of Incorporation, Alberta Extra-Provincial Registration Certificate (if federally incorporated), registered office address in Alberta, and the name and address of the authorized Canadian representative for service. The CRA issues the 15-character BN (9-digit base plus the 2-letter program account suffix) upon approval; the RC1 program account is the corporate income tax account (administered by CRA for both federal and Alberta provincial corporate income tax under the collection agreement), and the RT program account is the GST/HST account. Registration is required within 30 days of making the first taxable supply in Canada; GST/HST registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 over any period of four consecutive calendar quarters or a single calendar quarter. A SPAC may also voluntarily register for GST/HST to claim input tax credits on IPO and due-diligence costs, subject to the exempt financial services restrictions. The same BN serves as the statutory TIN for corporate income tax and as the VAT ID for GST/HST purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a US-style check-the-box election; every corporation is a statutory taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act. Elections relevant to a foreign-owned SPAC include: (i) the subsection 211(1) GST/HST election for closely related corporations to treat certain intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration, which is valuable when an acquisition target trust or escrow structure is used; (ii) a substitutional tax year end election under section 142.2(1)(a) where applicable, subject to CRA acceptance; (iii) a Part XIII treaty-based withholding relief election via Form NR301 for payments to the foreign parent; and (iv) a designation of the SPAC's representative under section 142.1 of the Excise Tax Act for non-resident registrants. Hybrid mismatch rules (sections 18.2, 18.3, 20.2 of the Income Tax Act) and the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) rules (sections 18.21-18.23) are mandatory and do not permit an elective opt-out. The small business deduction rate reduction is unavailable to a SPAC because it does not carry on an active business.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold, computed on the SPAC's worldwide taxable supplies together with associated entities, over the four preceding calendar quarters). A non-resident SPAC making taxable supplies in Canada must register for GST/HST irrespective of the threshold if it is engaged in an adventure or concern in the nature of trade involving Canadian real property or makes a prescribed supply of real property in Canada; a SPAC holding an escrow/trust in Canada may have Canadian reporting obligations even without regular supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under section 16 and Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, and services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated where the service is performed wholly or substantially outside Canada (Schedule VI, Part V, section 7) or where the supply is of an intangible to a non-resident and not for use in Canada. For a SPAC, custodial, trust, underwriting, and share-issuance services are financial services under Schedule VI, Part VII and are EXEMPT, not zero-rated; accordingly the SPAC generally cannot claim 0% GST on its service fee streams and its input tax credit eligibility is restricted under the exempt-supply de minimis rules. To claim zero-rating on any qualifying corporate export transaction, the SPAC must be a GST/HST registrant, hold contemporaneous evidence of physical export or offshore performance, and demonstrate that the supply was made to a resident of a foreign country in the ordinary course of its commercial activities.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency ACI/eManifest export report with unique transaction reference)
  • Bill of Lading (negotiable or straight, showing the SPAC as shipper and the foreign recipient as consignee)
  • Commercial Invoice with Incoterms, description, quantity, and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank statement evidencing foreign currency proceeds credited to the SPAC's Canadian bank account
  • Export Evidence Worksheet (CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17.4 exhibit) signed by the SPAC officer
  • Freight Forwarder's Certificate of Export or carrier airway bill confirmation when a third-party logistics provider is used

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API invoice-approval regime. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants whose total annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1,000,000 (returns filed via CRA Netfile), and to all T2 corporate income tax filers with gross revenue over CAD 1,000,000. Standard GST/HST invoice content requirements (BN of the supplier, date, description, and tax amount) are enforceable, but there is no pre-clearance portal. Registrants must retain auditable electronic records, including the underlying export documentation, EDI messages, and bank wire proofs, for six years.

CRA large-business audit programs focus on SPAC structures because net tax refunds and ITC claims on pre-IPO and acquisition costs are frequently traced. Where zero-rating is claimed without the statutory export bundle, CRA will reassess the supply at 5% GST, deny the associated ITC refund, and apply interest compounded daily at the prescribed rate. A gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the falsely claimed refund (minimum CAD 250) applies under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act; the late-filing/reporting penalty under section 280 adds 1% of the net tax plus 0.25% per month up to 12 months. Fraudulent evasion is prosecuted under section 327, with summary conviction fines up to CAD 200,000 and/or five years' imprisonment and, on indictment, fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and/or five years. The normal four-year reassessment window is extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation made fraudulently or with gross negligence. For a SPAC, clawback risk is heightened where trust, escrow, and broker fees are characterized as exempt financial services rather than zero-rated exports, rendering any 0% claim ineligible ab initio.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the International Monetary Fund Article VIII status. The Canadian dollar floats freely; there are no general restrictions on the conversion of CAD to foreign currency for trade or investment. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is legally permitted via dividends, interest, royalties, or management fees, subject to Canadian federal withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and applicable double taxation treaty relief. For a SPAC vehicle, repatriation of escrowed IPO proceeds and M&A target funding are treated as capital transactions and are not subject to exchange control licensing, though Canada's anti-money laundering and sanctions reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and the Criminal Code apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirements for export proceeds. There is no statutory timeline requiring exporters to bring foreign currency receipts into Canada, and entities are free to maintain foreign currency accounts domestically or abroad. Reporting obligations arise only under the Income Tax Act for foreign-source income, the FATCA/CRS framework for financial accounts, and the Currency and Foreign Transactions Reporting Act counterpart rules for cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs). Canada does not recognize the EMI licence; an entity seeking to offer payment accounts or issue e-money must be a bank under the Bank Act, a credit union, or a registered payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) once fully in force. Offshore EMIs generally cannot obtain a Canadian banking licence without substantial physical presence and prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). An unregulated offshore EMI may apply for registration as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC, but such registration does not confer deposit-taking authority. For a SPAC treasury, relying on offshore EMIs for escrow or capital preservation is not viable; they may be used only for auxiliary cross-border payment aggregation subject to FINTRAC MSB compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital routing for SPAC injections should use SWIFT MT103/MT202 with the full legal name and Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) of the sending and receiving entities. The Canadian receiving bank must be notified in advance with the account number, bank branch, and purpose code. In Canada, inbound cross-border wire payments are classified under Statistics Canada's Cross-Border Wire Reporting framework; the purpose code for securities transactions or capital investments should be used where applicable (e.g., 'Securities Purchase' or 'Investment in Issuer'). The sending financial institution should include a clear narrative in the wire such as 'SPAC IPO escrow contribution' or 'Share subscription proceeds', and the receiving bank will verify against sanctions lists (OSFI/DFATD), FATCA/CRS, and the Bank of Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) rules. Where the amount exceeds CAD 10,000, the receiving bank must file an International Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC and may request the underlying subscription agreement.Outbound disbursements for target acquisitions should be executed via SWIFT MT103 for low-value or non-LC transactions, and MT202 COV for interbank cover payments. For large-value CAD payments, use the Lynx payment system (formerly LVTS) to settle in central-bank money. For cross-border USD, the Canadian bank will typically clear through CHIPS (Clearing House Interbank Payments System) or correspondent banking via SWIFT. Commercial letters of credit (LC) are permissible for trade-related disbursements, particularly when acquiring cross-border operating assets; standby LCs may back the performance obligations of the SPAC. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for goods, CAD 500,000 for services/interest/royalties (threshold for filing T106); Master File and Local File required if total revenue exceeds CAD 5 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions apply. SPACs must demonstrate commercial substance to avoid being deemed a conduit or sham.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and interest on unpaid amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act if they failed to withhold or remit.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Reassessment, penalties under GAAR, and potential denial of treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the scheme; civil restitution may be ordered.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million per violation for corporations, imprisonment for individuals, and suspension of operations.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML penalties if they consented or were negligent.
Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties up to 10% of net adjustment, and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they participated in the pricing decision; travel bans may be imposed for egregious cases.
