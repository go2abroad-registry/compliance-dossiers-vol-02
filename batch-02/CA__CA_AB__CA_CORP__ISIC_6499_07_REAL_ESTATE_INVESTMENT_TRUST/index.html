Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); primary portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); GST/HST filings via GST/HST NETFILE.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) by registering with CRA. Registration is effected electronically through the Business Registration Online (BRO) system or by submitting Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number', available on the CRA website. Required supporting documentation generally includes certificate of incorporation (or analogous foreign registration), full particulars of directors/officers, foreign tax identification number or business registry number, proof of identity, statement of activities to be carried on in Canada, and appointment of an Authorized Representative (Form RC59 or T1013). After the BN is issued, a separate GST/HST account is created for Canadian supplies exceeding CAD 30,000. For import/export activities, the entity must also enroll with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for a business number import/export (RM) account and complete the CBSA client profile.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident corporation is taxed at the federal Part I rate plus applicable Alberta corporate tax; no pass-through attribution from the corporation); Territorial_Scope: a resident corporation is subject to Canadian tax on worldwide income, while a non-resident corporation is taxable only on Canadian-source income. A foreign corporation that is managed in Canada or is incorporated federally/provincially will be deemed resident. — A foreign entity must first obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) by registering with CRA. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident corporation is taxed at the federal Part I rate plus applicable Alberta corporate tax; no pass-through attribution from the corporation); Territorial_Scope: a resident corporation is subject to Canadian tax on worldwide income, while a non-resident corporation is taxable only on Canadian-source income. A foreign corporation that is managed in Canada or is incorporated federally/provincially will be deemed resident.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a 'check-the-box' regime. Entity classification is driven by legal form and the Income Tax Act. Available elections include: functional currency election under s. 261; accelerated capital cost allowance election under s. 1100(2) of the Regulations; deemed-disposition election on emigration (s. 128.1); and for Canadian REIT structures constituted as mutual fund trusts, an election under s. 122.1 to obtain mutual fund trust status. Hybrid hybrid-mismatch rules in ss. 18.4 and 18.5 deny deductions for certain payments regarded inconsistently by Canada and another jurisdiction. If this 'CA_CORP' entity is a corporation, it cannot itself be a REIT for Canadian tax purposes; the REIT tax election is available to a trust, not a corporation. Where an interposed trust is used, the trust may select a December 31 taxation year.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 gross annual worldwide taxable supplies in Canada (carrying-on-business threshold). For a non-resident without a permanent establishment, registration is required when the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada above the small-supplier threshold of CAD 30,000. For certain non-resident vendors of specified digital services and other prescribed supplies, registration may be required regardless of the threshold once the supply is taxable in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada qualify for zero-rating under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the supplier is a GST/HST registrant and the goods are shipped to a non-resident buyer outside Canada pursuant to a contract of carriage. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident and performed wholly outside Canada, or where the supply is directly in connection with real property situated outside Canada, or in respect of intellectual property licensed to a non-resident. For a REIT in the real-estate investment scope, zero-rating may apply to services rendered in relation to foreign real property or to certain commercial leasing services performed outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A) or approved electronic export declaration
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical departure of goods from Canada with consignee outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank advice demonstrating receipt of foreign-currency consideration from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial Invoice, packing list, and export container control details (cargo control number) where applicable

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval of invoices, or digital stamping. CRA requires registrants to maintain complete accounting records and electronically file GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE where the tax liability exceeds zero. Voluntary electronic invoicing through the PEPPOL network is recognized, and CRA has adopted a national electronic invoicing framework applicable to government procurement. No API-based fiscal stamp or clearance authorization is required before issuing invoices.

The CRA applies a strict documentary-substantiation test for export zero-rating. If a zero-rating claim is not supported by contemporaneous export and remittance evidence, the supply is reassessed at 5% GST plus arrears interest from the filing deadline. Penalties are imposed under Excise Tax Act s. 285 (false statements or omissions) of up to 25% of the tax otherwise assessed, and under s. 280.1 for failing to provide information. Fraudulent export zero-rating schemes attract criminal liability under s. 231.7 of the Excise Tax Act and s. 239 of the Income Tax Act, with fines and imprisonment. The normal reassessment period is four years from the end of the relevant reporting period; the period is unlimited where there has been misrepresentation or fraud attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under a free-floating exchange-rate regime. The Bank of Canada does not impose exchange controls or restrictions on foreign-currency transactions, cross-border fund transfers, or profit remittances. A Canadian REIT may freely repatriate dividends, interest, and capital gains to non-resident unitholders, subject only to the withholding tax obligations set out in Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), the Canada-US enhanced exchange of information framework, and standard AML/KYC verification under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Foreign direct investment in Alberta real estate is permitted without prior central-bank approval, but transactions above CAD 10,000 and wire transfers must be reported to FINTRAC. Non-resident vendors of Canadian real property remain subject to the Section 116 clearance certificate withholding regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline for export receipts. A REIT also has no export business in its typical operational scope. Any cross-border receipts may be retained in the currency received and must be reported on the REIT's books in Canadian dollars for income tax and financial-statement purposes under IFRS/IAS 21.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; prudential oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF regulation by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate, with material constraints. Canada does not have a domestic EMI licence category. Cross-border fintech platforms operating as electronic-money institutions must either partner with a Canadian Schedule I bank to hold settlement balances or register as a foreign money-service business (MSB) with FINTRAC. A Canadian REIT can use an offshore EMI for supplementary collection corridors and cross-border payment efficiency, but it cannot use an unregistered EMI as its primary disbursement or concentration platform because such entities lack direct access to the Canadian clearing infrastructure (Payments Canada / Lynx and the Automated Clearing Settlement System). Using unregistered offshore EMIs for real-property treasury flows could also trigger enhanced scrutiny from FINTRAC, OSFI, and the bank's correspondent relationships. Recommended: use a Schedule I bank as the primary treasury account and a licensed EU/UK EMI as a secondary collection-only conduit for international tenant rent, with daily sweeps via SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian concentration account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Complex trust/partnership chain where the REIT is a stapled entity or where the unitholder register is opaque.

Root cause: Complex trust/partnership chain where the REIT is a stapled entity or where the unitholder register is opaque.

Defense evidence: Updated organizational chart; certified copy of the trust declaration/indenture; full register of unitholders (or a notarized attestation if unitholder count is excessive); identification documents for all trustees and any substantial unitholder holding 25% or more; audited financial statements; and an opinion letter from Canadian counsel describing control, tax classification, and economic substance.
Foreign directors or unitholders resident in FATF grey-listed, sanctioned, or high-risk jurisdictions.

Root cause: Foreign directors or unitholders resident in FATF grey-listed, sanctioned, or high-risk jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Passport, tax-residence certificates, source-of-wealth and source-of-funds documentation, reference letters from qualifying financial institutions in the home country, negative sanctions/PEP screening results, sworn declarations regarding the absence of illicit-wealth nexus, and a Canadian legal opinion confirming no Prohibited Money Laundering Trigger under the PCMLTFA.
High-volume cash-cycle or third-party payment patterns under large-cash and wire reporting thresholds.

Root cause: High-volume cash-cycle or third-party payment patterns under large-cash and wire reporting thresholds.

Defense evidence: Independent audit of tenant rent roll; executed lease agreements; economic-substance evidence; an enterprise-wide AML risk assessment; FINTRAC compliance program with a designated compliance officer; and documented cash-flow narratives showing that each international payment relates to a genuine real-property or financing contract.
Inbound capital from an affiliated offshore real-estate group or private-equity box in a tax-favored jurisdiction.

Root cause: Inbound capital from an affiliated offshore real-estate group or private-equity box in a tax-favored jurisdiction.

Defense evidence: Fully executed subscription, loan, or trust-unit agreements; transfer-pricing documentation; group audited financial statements; corporate registry certificates from each involved jurisdiction; commercial substance proof; and a legal opinion from both sending- and receiving-country counsel confirming that the investor's funds are sourced from legitimate operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the REIT's Canadian concentration account at a Schedule I bank. The wire must clearly state the purpose of payment using the appropriate IMF BPM6/Statistics Canada purpose code: for direct investment into real estate, use 'Direct Investment – Real Estate' (Code 2.2 or the bank's prescribed code for 'International direct investment'); for trust-unit subscriptions or shareholder loans, use a clear descriptive text such as 'RETURNED CAPITAL / TRUST UNIT SUBSCRIPTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN' to satisfy the receiving bank's compliance and FINTRAC reporting. Beneficiary and remitter legal names must match the KYC file, including the CRA business number (BN). For inbound wires exceeding CAD 50,000–100,000, pre-notification and supporting uploads (subscription agreement, wire instruction letter, source-of-funds evidence) are generally required. Do not fragment large injections into sub-CAD 10,000 transfers to avoid structuring detection.Outbound payments from the REIT treasury should be cleared via: (1) domestic Canadian payments using Lynx for high-value same-day CAD settlements (typically >CAD 100,000) or the Retail Batch Settlement System / ACH for rent, utility, and vendor payments; (2) cross-border vendor and professional-service payments via SWIFT MT103 with appropriate purpose codes such as 'Management and administrative services fees' (code 228), 'Construction, engineering, and architectural services' (code 231), or 'Real estate services' (code 242); (3) cross-border distribution payments to unitholders via SWIFT MT103/MT202 with dividend code 'D' and the relevant Canada Revenue Agency NR4/T3 filing reference; and (4) when required, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit under UCP600/ISP98 issued via SWIFT MT700/MT710 for construction equipment, supply contracts, or real-estate acquisition deposits, with settlement on presentation through the advising bank.Deliverable FX forwards with a Canadian Schedule I bank (usual tenor T+1 to 12 months) to lock CAD/USD or CAD/EUR conversion rates on committed cross-border capital contributions, rent receipts, or acquisition proceeds; no central-bank approval is required.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are legally permissible in Canada but are not market-standard for CAD, because the CAD has a deep deliverable interbank market; NDFs are best restricted to hedges of sub-exposures in emerging-market currencies that the REIT might hold via international real-estate funds.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and CSA, executed with an OSFI-regulated bank or licensed dealer, to convert USD-denominated property debt into CAD synthetic fixed/floating liabilities.Exchange-traded CAD/USD futures and options, traded on CME or the Montréal Exchange through a registered investment dealer, for rolling short-tenor treasury hedges within board-approved limits.Plain vanilla FX spot contracts for immediate trade settlement and treasury transformation; these are fully permitted and subject only to standard credit-limit and conduct-risk policies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$1 million (reportable non-arm's-length non-resident transaction threshold for Form T106); country-by-country reporting is mandatory where consolidated group revenue is at least CA$750 million. No separate CA$ threshold exists for a statutory Local File or Master File, but taxpayers must prepare contemporaneous documentation sufficient to support an arm's length outcome when performing any reportable cross-border transaction.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime; however, GAAR (section 245), the beneficial-ownership doctrine, and the MLI Principal Purpose Test will deny treaty benefits to mailbox corporations or entities lacking commercial substance. A corporate REIT must display substantive Canadian real estate holdings, employee headcount, record-keeping, and trading activity; pure intermediary or conduit structures are audited by CRA under the off-shoot of the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting initiative.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax (subsection 163(2)); criminal tax evasion conviction attracts imprisonment of up to 5 years and criminal fines up to 200% of the attempted tax evaded; the corporation's charter may be administratively dissolved for repeated failure to file.

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in or authorized the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporate penalty (section 227.1 if withholding amounts are involved); courts will pierce the veil for fraud; director travel bans can issue under collection enforcement, and officers may face custodial sentences.
Withholding Tax / Part XIII Failure

Statutory sanction: Payer must remit the full unpaid amount to CRA; penalty of 10% of the amount withheld at source (increased to 20% if the failure was made knowingly); non-deductible interest; the payer loses the right to deduct the underlying royalty or interest expense until payment is rectified.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under subsection 227.1(1) for unremitted withheld taxes; civil collection against personal assets; travel bans and officer director delinquency notices are issued under CRA collection policies.
Transfer Pricing / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA will reattribute income at fair market value; transfer pricing penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment where reasonable documentation has not been provided within three months of a request; denial of treaty withholding reductions; and disallowance of deductions for payments made to non-residents without arm's length substance.

Director personal liability: Directors who were the architects of artificial intermediary chains face personal civil liability for the tax benefit obtained, and in egregious cases, gross negligence penalties have been assessed jointly and severally. Protection of the corporate veil is unavailable to officers who intentionally disguise foreign control over Canadian property assets.
GAAR / Treaty Benefit Abuse

Statutory sanction: An avoidance transaction identified under section 245 is void for tax purposes; all tax benefits are denied; a 25% GAAR penalty is imposed on the tax benefit (from the current statutory iteration); interest is calculated from the originally filing date; CRA may also reassess beyond the normal three-year limitation period if oversight or false documentation is established.

Director personal liability: Director liability follows the false-declaration regime: directors who sign incomplete or misleading GAAR-related disclosure in T106 or NR4 forms are personally liable for penalties and interest; in severe abuse, criminal tax evasion prosecution is possible, and civil asset freezes may be imposed.
