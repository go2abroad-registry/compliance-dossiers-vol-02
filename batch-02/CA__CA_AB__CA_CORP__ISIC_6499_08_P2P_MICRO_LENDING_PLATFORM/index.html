Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html; Alberta corporate registry on service Alberta Corporate Registry (APEX)

E-government filing portal

Foreign-incorporated entities must (1) obtain a Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number') or via CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) both for federal income tax and GST/HST; (2) where the entity carries on business in Alberta through a permanent establishment, it must also register under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act (Form RC1 with Alberta account) — note Alberta levies an 8% corporate income tax on allocated taxable income; (3) obtain an Alberta Corporate Access Number from Service Alberta/Alberta Corporate Registry when extra-provincially registered under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta); (4) open a payroll account (RP0001) and GST/HST account (RT0001) if applicable; (5) non-resident lenders that are 'specified financial institutions' may have special GST/HST registration obligations under Division II of Part IX of the Excise Tax Act. The BN is the single central identifier for all CRA program accounts (income tax, GST/HST, payroll, import/export).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxable person under s. 2 of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and is generally not eligible for pass-through or 'check-the-box' classification; all taxable income is computed at the corporate level and then subject to dividend withholding tax on distribution to non-resident shareholders under Part XIII of the ITA. Territorial scope: resident corporations (incorporated in Canada) are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada are taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source income under Part I and on certain Canadian-source payments under Part XIII. Alberta, as a province with a federal-provincial tax collection agreement, applies an 8% provincial corporate rate on treaty-protected business income allocable to Alberta through a permanent establishment.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a U.S.-style check-the-box regime. Relevant elections include: (i) Part IX GST/HST election under s. 156 of the Excise Tax Act to treat closely related corporations (90% or more ownership) as a single person for GST/HST on internal supplies; (ii) election under s. 21 of the ITA regarding Canadian exploration expenses; (iii) functional currency reporting election under s. 261 ITA (Canadian dollar or U.S. dollar reporting); (iv) hybrid mismatch rules are addressed by s. 18.2/18.3 ITA (dishonoured and excessive interest matching rules) and s. 16.1 / 17.1 ITA — no elective safe harbour exists for offshore hybrid instruments; (v) Ontario/Alberta foreign tax credit and 'treaty-exempt business income' surplus election under s. 5908 of the Income Tax Regulations. For GST/HST zero-rating, the election in paragraph 12.1 of Schedule IX (partner-directed supplies) is not relevant to lending; the relevant provision is s. 23 of Part V of Schedule VI (financial services to non-residents).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Financial services (including lending, loan administration, and payment processing by a P2P lending platform) are generally exempt under s. 123 and s. 2 of Schedule V, not zero-rated. However, under s. 23 of Part V of Schedule VI (Excise Tax Act), a supply of a financial service made to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and who is not a specified financial institution is zero-rated (0% GST), provided the service is not made in the course of the resident's business in Canada for the purpose of the non-resident's use in Canada. For a P2P micro-lending platform: (i) loan origination fees charged to foreign borrowers are zero-rated if the borrower is a non-resident non-registered person and the loan proceeds are not used in Canada; (ii) platform service fees charged to foreign lenders for administrative/arrangement services are zero-rated under Part V ss. 23 and Sch. Claim of zero-rating must be supported by evidence of the non-resident status (e.g., an export questionnaire or Section 23 certificate signed by the non-resident counterparty).

Canada has no mandatory real-time electronic invoicing/clearance regime for B2B or B2C supplies as of the knowledge cutoff; businesses may issue invoices in paper or electronic form as prescribed by the Excise Tax Act (Regulation 211) and CRA Information Circular IC 4022. There is no API-based fiscal clearance or digital stamping. However, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and CRA enforce the GST/HST 'import tax clearance' on commercial goods at the border via the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) system, and businesses must keep machine-readable records (digital books and records rules — IC 2007-07). For a lending platform, no e-invoicing stamping applies, but the platform must maintain complete digital ledgers, issue GST/HST invoices if registered, and comply with CRA electronic funds transfer threshold reporting under s. 273.2 ETA for large electronic payments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. No exchange-control approval, FX allocation, or profit-remittance license is required for the repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, or branch profits. Outbound remittances are subject only to (i) statutory withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) (25% statutory base, generally reduced by treaty), and (ii) FINTRAC large-value electronic funds transfer reporting for CAD $10,000 or more in any 24-hour period. Non-resident capital injections into a Canadian Business Corporation are lawful without exchange-control consent; however, acquisitions of control may trigger Investment Canada Act review above statutory thresholds, and non-arm's length transactions with non-residents must be reported on Form T106. P2P micro-lending activity is additionally constrained by provincial consumer-lending licensing and the criminal rate of interest ceiling under s. 347 of the Criminal Code (Canada).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory export-receipt surrender, no forced conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. Exporters and lenders may hold export proceeds or loan repayments in any freely convertible currency. FINTRAC reporting obligations apply only when an electronic funds transfer of CAD $10,000 or more is transmitted in a 24-hour period, or when a large cash transaction of CAD $10,000 or more occurs, regardless of currency denomination.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange administration is shared with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); there is no dedicated FX-control agency because Canada operates a free-floating, fully liberalized exchange-rate regime under the Currency Act and the Bretton Woods Agreements Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a direct banking or payment clearing provider. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act (Canada), cannot accept deposits, and cannot obtain direct membership in Canada's core payment and clearing systems (Lynx, Retail Batch ACH, or Real-Time Rail). An offshore EMI may operate only as an uncredited payment facilitator through a sponsorship or white-label partnership with a Schedule I Canadian bank, in which case the bank retains regulatory responsibility and the EMI must register with FINTRAC as a money services business (MSB) if it engages in remittance activities. Provincial MSB and consumer-lending registrations, along with a Canadian physical presence for KYC, are additional legal constraints. Viability rating improves to MEDIUM solely under a licensed Canadian bank sponsorship arrangement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the foreign shareholder remittance via SWIFT MT103 (where no correspondent carve-out is required) with a clear payment reference quoting 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'INTERCOMPANY LOAN', the Canadian Business Number (BN), and the corporate legal name. For value dates above CAD $10,000, the receiving Canadian bank must file a FINTRAC electronic funds transfer report. The capital must be received in a designated Capital Escrow or operational account segregated from client money. Corporate records must include the authorizing board resolution, share subscription agreement or loan agreement, source-of-funds documentation, and a closing binder holding the foreign exchange confirmation if converted. A Form T106 must be filed for non-arm's length transactions with non-resident related parties, and an Investment Canada Act notification/review may apply if the foreign investor acquires control or if the asset value exceeds statutory thresholds. All funds must be free of any sanctions-related parties and traceable to a FINTRAC-compliant originator.Cross-border and domestic trade settlements should clear through SWIFT MT103 credit transfers (with MT202 COV for intermediary cover), or through SWIFT MT101 for bulk payment initiation. Commercial letters of credit are issued via MT700 under UCP600 with the Canadian bank acting as advising, confirming, or issuing bank. USD-denominated settlements traverse US correspondent banking relationships through Canadian banks' New York and Chicago clearing agents. High-value CAD payments clear through Lynx (successor to LVTS); retail consumer disbursements are executed via the Canadian Payments Association Retail Batch ACH and Direct Debit rules, or through local wallet payout APIs. Viability rating improves to MEDIUM solely under a licensed Canadian bank sponsorship arrangement.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined federal-provincial general corporate income tax for a corporation with a permanent establishment in Alberta (federal general rate 15% + Alberta general rate 8%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents is the statutory rate. Under the Canada-US Treaty the rate is reduced to 5% where the beneficial owner is a US corporation owning at least 10% of the voting shares of the Canadian corporation and to 15% in all other cases; most other Canadian DTAs provide 5%/15% rates.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the fiscal year-end (e.g., a December 31 year-end is due June 30 next year). The balance-due day is two months after the fiscal year-end for Canadian-controlled private corporations and three months for other corporations; instalment deadlines follow monthly/quarterly prescribed dates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada imposes no CAD-denominated safe harbor for contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation, but CRA Form T106 (Information Return on Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed where the aggregate fair market value of all reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions in the year exceeds CAD 100,000. Contemporaneous documentation must demonstrate reasonable arm's-length efforts to avoid the s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment (minimum CAD 1,000), which is triggered when the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue. Country-by-Country reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations; substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA), the judicial sham doctrine, the Canada-US Treaty Limitation-on-Benefits clause, and the Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test for applicable treaties. An Alberta micro-lending platform must demonstrate real business premises, locally engaged employees, board-level risk and credit decisions, and substantive financial records; a letterbox entity with no operational substance risks GAAR reassessment and denial of treaty withholding relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Return / Willful Omission of Revenue

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years; s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax; CRA may additionally assess late-filing penalties, interest, and criminal restitution orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted payroll and Part XIII withholdings and under s. 160 for non-arm's-length transfers made to defeat tax; the Alberta Business Corporations Act allows veil-piercing in cases of fraud or dishonesty; responsible directors face criminal indictment, imprisonment, disqualification from serving as directors, and civil restitution.
Shell Company / Artificial Corporate Vehicle

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under s. 245 ITA denying the tax benefit, with a 25% GAAR penalty on the benefit; denial of treaty benefits under the Limitation-on-Benefits provision; CRA may disallow the Small Business Deduction, reclassify income as property income, and reassess unaffected limitation periods.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed personally under s. 160 on transferred corporate funds; courts will pierce the corporate veil to hold directors liable for fraud, oppression, or reckless insolvency; travel restrictions may be imposed as a bail or probation condition in criminal tax proceedings.
