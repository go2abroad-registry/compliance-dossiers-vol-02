Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, with portal access via Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency. Alberta's 8% provincial corporate income tax is administered federally by the CRA under the Canada–Alberta Tax Collection Agreement; there is no separate Alberta corporate tax registration portal and no Alberta retail sales tax.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the foreign corporation 'carries on business in Canada' under section 253 of the Income Tax Act (ITA) through a permanent establishment (PE) in Alberta; passive cross-border lending to foreign borrowers generally does not create a PE. Step 2: If registration is required, the entity applies for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 - Request for a Business Number, filed electronically through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by mail to the Jonquière Tax Centre. Step 3: The applicant selects the required program accounts: GST/HST ('RT'), payroll ('RP') and corporate income tax ('RC'). Step 4: CRA issues the BN immediately online and sends a notice confirming the GST/HST registration effective date, which is the date the entity ceased to be a small supplier or the voluntary registration date. Step 5: A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must file a T2 corporate income tax return and, if registered, GST/HST returns even if it has no Alberta PE; extra-provincial registration under the Alberta Business Corporations Act is a corporate-law prerequisite but not a tax registration precondition. Step 6: For the fund's revenue-based financing activity, the GST/HST 'RT' account is mandatory only once taxable (including zero-rated export financing) supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; no minimum share capital or statutory licence is required for tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian Business Corporation is a taxpayer separate from its shareholders; it is taxed at the federal basic rate of 15% (reduced by the provincial abatement to 9% net federal for Alberta-allocated income) plus Alberta corporate income tax of 8%, for a combined Alberta general rate of 23% (subject to the global minimum top-up rules for in-scope MNE groups). Canada taxes Canadian-resident corporations on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source business income, dispositions of taxable Canadian property, and are subject to Part XIII withholding on Canadian-source payments. The entity is a 'financial institution' under section 149 of the Excise Tax Act because its principal business is lending and non-dilutive revenue-based financing; domestic supplies of these financial services are exempt under Part VII of Schedule V to the ETA, while financial services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under Part VII of Schedule VI. Non-resident shareholders face Part XIII withholding tax of 25% (treaty-reduced, e.g., 5%–15% under Article X of the Canada–US Treaty) on dividends and treaty-reduced rates on interest and royalties.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' regime; classification follows the ITA and ETA statutory definitions. Available elections include: (i) section 216 ITA net-income election for non-residents with Canadian rental or timber royalties (not applicable to financing interest); (ii) section 115 ITA election for non-residents disposing of taxable Canadian property; (iii) treaty elections under Article XIII of the Canada–US Income Tax Convention for share dispositions and Article IV residence tie-break filings; (iv) section 156 ETA election for closely related corporations to treat otherwise taxable intercorporate supplies for nil consideration; (v) Quick Method election under section 227 ETA (unavailable for financial services); (vi) special attribution method election under section 225.2 ETA available to a selected listed financial institution (SLFI) for allocating provincial HST, and an SLFI status election under subsection 225.2(1) ETA even where the CAD 10 million gross-revenue threshold is not met; and (vii) foreign tax credit and Article XXIV Canada–US Treaty foreign tax credit elections under subsection 126(1) ITA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (the small-supplier threshold under section 148(1) ETA, measured on the value of worldwide taxable supplies during the four preceding calendar quarters and the current calendar quarter). Zero-rated export financing supplies to non-residents count toward the threshold; exempt domestic financing supplies do not. Once total taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, GST/HST registration is mandatory; a non-resident that does not carry on business in Canada is not required to register for GST/HST and generally cannot claim input tax credits unless it elects to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A financial service supplied to a non-resident person is zero-rated under section 1 of Part VII of Schedule VI to the ETA unless it is a prescribed financial service under section 2 of the Financial Services (GST/HST) Regulations, which except services in respect of debt securities issued by Canadian residents, shares of Canadian resident corporations, mortgages on Canadian real property, Canadian registered pension/RRSP arrangements, and certain life insurance policies. The Revenue-Based Financing Fund may zero-rate its interest and revenue-share income from cross-border loans to non-resident digital-brand borrowers provided: (i) the borrower is a non-resident person and not registered under the ETA for the relevant supply; (ii) the supply is not made through the fund's permanent establishment in Canada in a manner that causes the service to be consumed in Canada; (iii) the underlying receivable is not in respect of Canadian-resident debt securities, Canadian real property, or a Canadian registered plan; and (iv) the fund retains contemporaneous evidence of the non-resident status of the borrower and the foreign location of the funded brand's operations. Supplies to Canadian-resident borrowers remain exempt (not zero-rated) and generate no input tax credits. No Customs Export Declaration is required for exported financial services; the goods-export zero-rating regimes under Parts I–VI of Schedule VI ETA (which require customs documentary proof) do not apply to services, and the documentation bundle below substitutes for the goods-export customs file.

  • Executed revenue-based financing / loan agreement identifying the non-resident borrower by legal name, foreign tax identification number, jurisdiction of incorporation, and principal place of business outside Canada
  • Zero-rated export invoice or funding schedule annotated 'Exported financial service — ZR pursuant to s.1, Part VII, Schedule VI, ETA', reflecting the full consideration and the absence of Canadian consumption
  • Non-resident status evidence: foreign certificate of incorporation, tax residence certificate issued by the borrower's home jurisdiction, or IRS Form W-8BEN-E / W-8IMY for US-based digital-brand borrowers
  • Inbound wire transfer records and bank statements evidencing receipt of principal repayment and revenue-share payments from foreign funding sources, matched to the 180-day repatriation benchmark
  • Internal underwriting and fund diligence files demonstrating that the funded brand is a non-resident enterprise, the receivable is not secured by Canadian real property or Canadian-resident debt securities, and no part of the service is consumed or performed for the benefit of a Canadian-resident establishment

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance API regime. GST/HST registrants with net tax greater than CAD 10,000 must file electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or approved EDI; monthly remittances are required by EFT where average monthly net tax exceeds CAD 10,000. The CRA is implementing a voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework aligned with the OECD Peppol standard (announced in Budget 2023); participation is mandatory only for suppliers engaged in certain federal government procurement streams and remains optional for B2B transactions, including the fund's financing and revenue-share invoicing.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year period from the date of the original assessment; the period extends to 10 years in cases of misrepresentation, neglect or certain carryback adjustments, and is unlimited in cases of fraud or wilful default. Where zero-rating is denied, the supply is recharacterized as an exempt financial service, the fund loses any input tax credit entitlement, and the previously zero-rated consideration may be assessed as taxable at 5% together with interest compounded daily under section 280 ETA. Gross negligence or wilful participation in a false zero-rating claim triggers a penalty of 25%–50% of the tax sought to be evaded under section 285(1) ETA, and the parallel false-statement penalty under section 163.2 ITA may apply where the claim affects income tax. Criminal prosecution under section 327 ETA or section 239 ITA can result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. Clawback exposure is retrospective from the date the zero-rate was first claimed, and Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline for GST/HST purposes; the 180-day metric is the fund's internal treasury and documentation benchmark used to evidence commercial reality and to align with foreign tax credit and transfer-pricing documentation standards.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar floats freely; no exchange-control approval, surrender requirement, or central-bank pre-authorization applies to profit remittance, royalty distributions, or capital repatriation. Practical hurdles are limited to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, generally treaty-reduced), CRA Part XIII reporting filings, and bank-level FINTRAC documentary screening for payments lacking evidentiary economic substance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters, trade-finance beneficiaries, and financing vehicles retain unrestricted discretion over foreign-currency receipts, subject only to general record-keeping obligations and FINTRAC transaction-reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF compliance; the Department of Finance Canada administers exchange policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: Limited. Canada does not issue standalone electronic-money-institution (EMI) licenses; deposit-taking requires authorization under the Bank Act (Schedule I or II bank charter). FINTRAC-registered money-services businesses (e.g., Wise Canada, Payoneer) may act as approved payment intermediaries but cannot lawfully hold escrowed capital or extend credit. Such platforms are viable only for low-value disbursement and collection convenience, not as primary treasury liquidity or clearing rails for a revenue-based financing fund.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and Alberta general corporate income tax rate of 23% (15% federal statutory rate plus 8% Alberta provincial corporate income tax). A fully reduced combined rate of 11% applies to a Canadian-controlled private corporation on active business income up to C$500,000 (9% federal small-business rate plus 2% Alberta small-business rate).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

A dividend paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident is subject to Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory rate of 25% of the gross dividend.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian transfer-pricing rules require contemporaneous documentation for each non-arm's-length cross-border transaction unless the aggregate value of all cross-border dealings with a single related party is C$1 million or less in the fiscal year. A Transfer Pricing Memorandum (Local File in T106 format) and CRA-compliant master file are compulsory when transactions exceed this threshold or when the taxpayer is in a high-risk audit environment.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) analogous to certain offshore jurisdictions, but CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), statutory residency tests, source rules, and the 'substance over form' doctrine. A royalty financing fund must maintain real head office functions, business decision-making personnel, and substantive Canadian activities; otherwise CRA may recharacterize or deny treaty benefits and impose transfer-pricing penalties for transactions lacking economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Blindness

Statutory sanction: Under Section 239 and 327 of the Income Tax Act, a penalty of 50% to 200% of the tax intentionally evaded is applied, plus recovery of unreported tax, interest, and Part XIII penalties. A third-party tax preparer or promoter may also be assessed for civil penalties and the transaction may be subject to GAAR deconstruction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly, or had reasonable grounds to know, failed to remit withholding tax or made a contribution to deliberate evasion receive civil liabilities up to the full unremitted tax plus interest; criminal conviction can result in imprisonment up to 5 years, travel bans, cross-border asset freezing, and a permanent ban from acting as a director or holding a BN.
Transfer-Pricing and Permanent Establishment Concealment

Statutory sanction: CRA may reallocate profits upward, deny deductions, and impose under Section 247 a penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment where total adjustments exceed the lesser of C$5 million or 10% of gross revenues; the penalty is the greater of the 10% adjustment and C$5,000, plus Part XIII withholding on any deemed dividend or royalty.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to stamping of contingent liability in an involuntary bankruptcy if the transfer-pricing relocation makes the corporation insolvent; courts may also pierce the corporate veil where profit stripping amounts to a fraudulent conveyance, making directors personally answerable for the shortfall and interest.
Shell Company and False Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the entity for tax purposes under the deemed-purpose exception, reallocate all revenue to the foreign principal, deny treaty benefits, and impose assessing penalties under both the ITA and the Excise Tax Act on uncollected GST; Alberta corporate registry can dissolve the entity for non-compliance with filings, causing deemed taxable dispositions of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid GST/HST and source deductions made by the corporation through CRA's individual director liability rules, and in a sham-plus-fraud scenario, directors may face criminal prosecution for conspiracy, tax evasion, and money laundering contrary to the Criminal Code.
Anti-Money Laundering and Unregistered Financial Facilitation

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act imposes monetary penalties up to C$1 million per administrative violation for unregistered financing activity; capital-gain-driven funds may be reviewed as securities-dealers by the Alberta Securities Act as well, with additional enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally exposed under the PCMLTFA for knowingly carrying out unregistered activities; prosecution can lead to imprisonment in addition to fines, counsel may be barred from indemnification by corporate articles, and CRA will treat unpaid Part XIII amounts as a deemed trust obligation held personally by the directors.
