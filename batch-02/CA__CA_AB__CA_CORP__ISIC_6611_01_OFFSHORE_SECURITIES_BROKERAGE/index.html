Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html)

E-government filing portal

Incorporate under either the Canada Business Corporations Act (federal) or the Alberta Business Corporations Act (provincial). Upon incorporation, register with the CRA using Form RC1 or through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service. The Canadian Business Number (BN) is the statutory tax identifier, with program accounts for corporate income tax (RP), GST/HST (RT), and, if applicable, payroll (RP) and import/export (RM) accounts. Non-resident corporations with Canadian-source income must additionally file an income tax return under section 150 of the Income Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Canadian-resident corporations are separate taxable entities subject to Canadian income tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act; there is no pass-through election for a Business Corporation. The corporation is subject to federal and Alberta income tax, and is taxed on worldwide income if resident in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada has no general check-the-box election. Entity classification is determined by the incorporation statute. Available elections include: the functional currency election (Income Tax Act s.261); the preferred-election to treat a dividend as a capital dividend (s.83(2)); and, for hybrid mismatch, the election under subsection 18.4(8) to exclude certain hybrid instrument amounts from the limitation rules. These elections do not alter the corporation's per se taxable status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — a person (including a non-resident) making taxable supplies in Canada must register for GST/HST if their worldwide taxable supplies exceed this small-supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of financial services (including offshore securities brokerage) to a non-resident may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part VI, subsection 6 of the Excise Tax Act, provided the service is not for use, enjoyment, or benefit in Canada and is not supplied to an individual in Canada at the time of performance. For goods exports, zero-rating applies when goods are exported by the supplier or on their behalf and documentary evidence of export is retained. Canada does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is a common due-diligence convention for maintaining proof-of-export documentation.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (CBSA) or CBSA A8/A6 account export report
  • Commercial invoice / bill of sale
  • Bill of lading or airway waybill
  • Proof of export / delivery confirmation (e.g., signed proof of delivery, customs release documents)
  • Service contract and confirmatory non-resident status certificate (for zero-rated financial services)

No statutory real-time clearance or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. E-invoices are permitted if they satisfy the information requirements under subsections 223(1) and 224(1) of the Excise Tax Act. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically in certain cases; however, there is no API-based pre-authorization mandate.

Where zero-rating is claimed incorrectly, the CRA can reassess within four years (or longer in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default), claw back denied input tax credits, and apply penalties. The penalty for making a false statement in a return, knowingly or under gross negligence, is the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax that would have been payable may be assessed under s.285.1, plus applicable interest. Criminal tax evasion charges may be pursued under s.381 of the Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with fully convertible current and capital accounts. The Canadian dollar floats freely, and no exchange-control permits, quantitative limits, or conversion authorization requirements are imposed on resident or non-resident parties. Profit remittances and dividends are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax and applicable tax treaty rate reductions, but there is no exchange-control clearance or foreign investment repatriation approval requirement. All cross-border wires remain subject to sanctions screening and to FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose a statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in CAD or convertible foreign currency at the exporter's discretion, subject only to applicable record-keeping, financial-crime compliance, and tax reporting requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for ancillary operating treasury and unacceptable for client money segregation. Canadian law requires segregated client monies to be held with a licensed Canadian depository institution and/or a CIRO-approved custodian. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut) may operate in Canada only as money service businesses, not as banks; they can support low-value multi-currency float accounts after FINTRAC MSB registration, but all client-money, margin, and regulatory settlement accounts must be held with a Tier-1 Canadian bank or an approved trust company.
