Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada), administered through the Business Registration Online portal and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/services-businesses.html). Customs export and import accounts are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) single window. Alberta provincial corporate income tax is collected federally by the CRA pursuant to the Canada-Alberta Tax Collection Agreement.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the entity under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (or federal CBCA with Alberta extra-provincial registration) through the Alberta Corporate Registry, obtaining a Corporate Access Number, registered office address and service address. Step 2: Apply for the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts (RC for Part I corporate income tax; RT for GST/HST; RP for payroll; customs account for prepaid card stock movements) via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1 submitted to the CRA tax centre. Step 3: Where the ultimate parent is foreign, appoint a Canadian resident director, file certified constating documents and a power of attorney for service; if the foreign parent itself carries on business in Canada, register the parent through the CRA International Tax Services Office under the non-resident corporate tax regime. Step 4: Register with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) because an Electronic Money Institution is not a distinct statutory license category in Canada; the FINTRAC registration number must be referenced when opening the CRA financial institution account. Step 5: Where applicable, complete Bank of Canada registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act to support the tax residency and substantive activity file. Step 6: File the first T2 corporate income tax return within six months after the fiscal year-end; no separate Alberta corporate tax registration is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An Alberta business corporation is a separate legal taxpayer and cannot elect pass-through treatment. A Canadian-incorporated corporation is resident in Canada (ITA s.250(4) deemed residence) and taxable on worldwide income under ITA Part I; the Alberta provincial corporate tax (8%) is collected via the federal T2 return through the Canada-Alberta collection agreement. In the GST/HST regime, an entity whose principal business is money transmission, lending, payment card issuance or digital wallet reserve management is a financial institution (ETA s.149), meaning its financial services (virtual IBAN issuance, wallet fee income and reserve earnings) are exempt supplies, input tax credits are generally denied on inputs consumed for exempt supplies, and special net tax calculation and attribution rules apply.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election; classification follows the Income Tax Act definition of corporation (ITA s.248(1)) and corporate law. Available elections for this fintech structure: (i) ETA s.156 nil-consideration election for qualifying closely related corporations; (ii) ETA s.238.1 GST/HST group registration election for Canadian financial institution groups; (iii) special net-tax and input tax credit attribution method elections for financial institutions (ETA s.225.2 and associated regulations, including Schedule VI Part VIII zero-rating elections); (iv) ITA s.126 election to claim foreign tax credits for foreign branch income taxes paid by the Canadian corporation; (v) ITA s.216(1) election for a non-resident corporation receiving Canadian rental income on a net basis, which may apply to Canadian real estate held by a foreign parent; and (vi) treaty non-resident withholding elections in respect of Part XIII payments. Hybrid mismatch elections under the OECD BEPS Action 2 framework are not separately codified in the Income Tax Act, but the foreign affiliate rules (ITA s.95) govern any offshore wallet treasury vehicles.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized cross-border capital account. No exchange control legislation or central bank approval is required for current account transactions or profit remittances. The Canadian dollar is a freely convertible currency under IMF Article VIII. However, a licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) must respect OSFI's supervisory expectations regarding prudential liquidity and client fund safeguarding; there is no regulatory barrier to repatriating capital or dividends, subject to compliance with corporate solvency requirements and tax clearance procedures for non-resident directors or shareholders where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to Canada. There are no statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds. A registered Canadian exporter may retain foreign currency indefinitely. For an EMI, the relevant obligation is to ensure customer funds held in foreign currency are appropriately segregated and reported for regulatory capital purposes under OSFI guidelines. FINTRAC reporting thresholds apply to cross-border currency transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, but there is no surrender or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) and Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange oversight is decentralized, with FINTRAC administering monitoring and reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High with significant legal constraints. A foreign digital EMI (e.g., London-based with e-money license) may partner with Canadian Tier-1 banks as a white-label program manager, but must not hold Canadian client funds without a valid money services business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC and, if operating in Alberta, a money remittance license in the province under the Money Services Business Act and applicable consumer protection legislation. Direct issuance of Canadian 'virtual IBANs' requires a local clearing member bank or a Payments Canada participant; principal contact with the Canadian payment ecosystem cannot be done solely through an unregulated offshore EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions should be routed via SWIFT MT103 or wire under Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) using the recipient bank's CAD clearing identifier. Purpose must be stated as 'Equity capital injection — Business Corporation Act incorporated entity' to avoid remittance classification. Beneficiary account name must exactly match the licensed EMI corporate legal name. Supporting verification includes board resolution, issued share certificate, and attestation by the bank's compliance officer that the funds represent internal company equity rather than customer deposits.
