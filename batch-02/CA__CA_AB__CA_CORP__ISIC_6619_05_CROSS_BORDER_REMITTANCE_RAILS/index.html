Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC); official administrative portals are Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA) accessible via canada.ca/revenue-agency; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement, while the provincial corporate registry (Alberta Corporate Registration System) handles incorporation filings.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the business corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Business Corporations Act; the registrant may request a CRA Business Number (BN) during incorporation through the Alberta Corporate Registry. 2) Alternatively, apply via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1 and obtain the 9-digit BN together with program accounts, including RT for GST/HST, RC for corporate income tax, and RP for payroll. 3) For foreign-owned or non-resident applicants, provide certified corporate documents, a registered agent for service in Alberta, and identify the Canadian permanent establishment; CRA will open the non-resident account streams and assess whether GST/HST registration is mandatory or voluntary. 4) CRA issues written confirmation letters; the BN/GST/HST account reference must appear on invoices, customs documentation, and periodic GST/HST returns (Forms GST34 and GST62 or online filing).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxable person and taxpayer for federal and Alberta corporate income tax and for GST/HST purposes; the corporation is not fiscally transparent, shareholders do not directly report corporate income, and, for GST/HST, the registered corporation accounts separately from its shareholders. Canada taxes on a residence-based worldwide basis; Alberta imposes a flat provincial corporate income tax administered by the CRA.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or multi-entity pass-through election analogous to the US regime exists in Canadian federal income tax law. The relevant statutory elections include: section 216 election for non-residents to compute Canadian net rental income; association and deemed-control elections under section 256(2) of the Income Tax Act affecting Alberta corporate tax attribute pools; GST/HST elections under Excise Tax Act s.156 for nil consideration supplies between closely related registered corporations; s.150 elections on solicitor-client disbursements; and the s.178.6 joint election for a non-resident registrant's Canadian branch. For hybrid mismatch arrangements, Canada applies deductibility denial rules (including measures on hybrid financial instruments and import mismatch branch mismatches) with no affirmative elective opt-out, except for the specific carve-outs for specified financial institutions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - under ETA s.247, mandatory GST/HST registration applies when the total consideration for worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies but excluding exempt financial services) exceeds CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Because domestic money-transfer fees are usually exempt financial services under ETA s.123(1) and Sch. V Pt VII, only the zero-rated cross-border remittance/financial-service supplies to non-residents count toward the threshold; voluntary registration may still be advisable to recover GST-paid operating and compliance costs attributable to zero-rated export activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For exported goods, zero-rating under ETA Sch. VI Pt V requires that the goods be physically exported from Canada in accordance with the Customs Act by a non-resident recipient. For cross-border remittance and payment rails, a financial service (money transfer, payment clearing, foreign-exchange spread) supplied to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated under ETA s.136.1 where: (i) the supply is not in connection with the non-resident recipient's business carried on in Canada, and (ii) if the recipient is a financial institution, the service is not attributable to the recipient's Canadian permanent establishment. If the service is supplied to a Canadian resident consumer (for example, an expatriate worker in Alberta remitting funds abroad), the supply is exempt under Sch. V Pt VII and is not zero-rated; the supplier must apportion input tax credits and cannot claim ITCs attributable to exempt domestic supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed via the Canada Border Services Agency Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), including the exporter's BN and the CBSA cargo control number (A6/D10) where physical goods are involved
  • Commercial invoice, Bill of Lading, and/or air waybill evidencing export of goods from Canada to a non-resident consignee and the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103/MT202 message, or bank credit statement providing contemporaneous evidence of foreign-currency settlement and repatriation of proceeds

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no single connectivity unit (SCU) regime exists in Canada. GST/HST invoices must meet prescribed content requirements under ETA Sch. VI Pt V (supplier name and BN, invoice date/period, total consideration, tax payable, and specified statements) but may be issued on paper or electronically, including PDF, EDI, or interoperable Peppol BIS 3.0 format. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoice Pilot using the Peppol network is voluntary and has been closed to new entrants; CRA does not pre-clear invoices, but it obtains transaction-level evidence through bank, FINTRAC, and SWIFT-related information flows to verify zero-rating claims.

CRA may reassess a zero-rating claim within four years of the filing date under ETA s.298(1), but the limitation period does not apply where the claim arises from misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default attributable to the taxpayer. Where zero-rating is disallowed, the refund is clawed back, the supply is reassessed as taxable or exempt, and the full tax amount plus prescribed daily compound interest is recovered. A knowingly or grossly negligent false statement on a return or zero-rating claim triggers a penalty under ETA s.285(1) equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax or refund at issue; fraud and wilful evasion are prosecutable under ETA ss.327 and 333 with fines and imprisonment. Directors and officers may face personal liability under ETA s.323 for unremitted GST/HST. While Canada has no exchange-control repatriation statute, the finance department and CRA expect settlement and repatriation evidence within a 180-day commercial cycle; unexplained delays support transfer-pricing and diversion-of-corporate-opportunity reassessments. CRA also cross-references FINTRAC large-value virtual-currency/EFT transaction reports and SWIFT records to reconstruct the foreign receipts backing every zero-rated remittance claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains fully convertible current and capital accounts and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; there are no surrender requirements, licensing restrictions, or prior approvals for repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, or equity. Profit remittance from an Alberta subsidiary is subject only to Part XIII withholding tax at 25 percent statutory (typically reduced by treaty, e.g., to 5-15 percent for dividends), and the Investment Canada Act applies only to acquisitions of control, not to greenfield remittance agency establishments. The Canadian Dollar is a free-floating currency with no central bank exchange-rate intervention for commercial settlement purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion deadline, or mandatory repatriation ratio exists for Canadian export receipts; the Foreign Exchange Control Board regime was abolished in 1951. A cross-border remittance operator must, however, report all electronic fund transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC within 24 hours, maintain dual-currency treasury accounts to avoid open FX exposure, and ensure that settlement obligations to foreign payout partners are met under provincial consumer-protection and money-services licensing rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with prudential supervision delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); transaction oversight under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act is exercised by FINTRAC, while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre handles cross-border EFT reporting; no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration exists due to the liberalized regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (ancillary rail only). A pure offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) cannot serve as the primary treasury bank because receiving client or corporate funds into an unlicensed foreign EMI for a Canadian MSB violates the PCMLTFA and the Bank Act. A foreign EMI may register with FINTRAC as a foreign Money Services Business and route small-value corridors, but the master liquidity pool must be held at an OSFI-regulated Canadian bank. Practical structure: deploy licensed platform solutions such as Wise Platform or Remitly for consumer-facing payouts, with daily net settlement through the Schedule I bank via SWIFT MT103.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined (15% federal Part I general corporate rate + 8% Alberta general corporate rate); 11% combined for a qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation (9% federal small business rate + 2% Alberta small business rate applicable to first C$500,000 of active business income); federal rate is reduced under the small business deduction only for CCPC status.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax under ITA s.212(2) on dividends paid by the Alberta corporation to a non-resident shareholder, reduced by an applicable tax treaty; under the Canada-US DTA, 5% where the beneficial owner is a corporation holding at least 10% voting power and 15% for portfolio shareholders; under the Canada-UK DTA, 5% for substantial corporate holdings and 15% otherwise; most other Canadian DTAs reduce the rate to 5% or 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 filing deadline is six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end; balance of tax payable is due ordinarily two months after year-end (three months for an eligible CCPC claiming the small business deduction on the C$500,000 business limit); instalment obligations apply where estimated tax payable exceeds prescribed thresholds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions in a taxation year triggers the mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation requirement under ITA s.247(4) (the Canadian 'Local File'); a broader group Master File must be produced to CRA on demand for multinational groups above prescribed revenue thresholds; Country-by-Country reports are required where the ultimate parent (or Canadian surrogate parent) reports consolidated group revenue of at least C$750 million; failure to maintain documentation shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer and exposes penalties on adjustments.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) denies tax benefits from transactions lacking genuine commercial substance and business purpose; OECD/G20 Pillar 2 GloBE rules (IIR, UTPR and domestic minimum top-up tax) will apply to in-scope multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750 million; the expatriate remittance agency must maintain a real operational presence in Alberta — licensed MSB premises, substantive IT and AML compliance staff, board activity and risk-management functions — to support its Canadian tax residence, PE allocation and treaty claims; the CRA can recharacterize intercompany remittance commissions, settlement fees or software royalties as disguised equity distributions or payments lacking substance; FINTRAC MSB registration, CBCA/ABCA corporate records and Canada's beneficial ownership transparency framework corroborate economic substance and must be preserved.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False or Deceptive T2 Return (ITA s.239)

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence: on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the understatement, plus daily prescribed-rate interest, full reassessment and potentially permanent CRA audit scrutiny.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid Part I and Part XIII taxes, interest and penalties under ITA s.227.1; Canadian courts may pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a façade for tax evasion; directing minds face asset seizure, restitution orders, travel restrictions, and imprisonment; CRA criminal referrals may trigger extradition requests for senior managers resident overseas.
Transfer Pricing / Non-Arm's Length Mispricing (ITA s.247)

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under ITA s.247(2) applying the arm's length principle, plus a s.247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the net upward adjustment when the taxpayer has not made reasonable efforts to establish arm's length terms or has failed to produce required documentation; interest accrues daily at prescribed rates; denial of deductions for undocumented intercompany balances.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fabricated T2 declarations face personal gross-negligence penalties under s.163(2) as 'persons' who participated in the misstatement; personal liability is extended to payroll and withholding failures caused by artificial cash flows; directors may be investigated under CRA's Voluntary Disclosures and criminal referral programs; professional advisers and directors who instruct false TP documentation are exposed to prosecution for counselling tax evasion.
Unlicensed or Non-Compliant Money Services Business (PCMLTFA; Criminal Code)

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA offences: operating an unregistered MSB is punishable in a summary conviction proceeding by a fine of up to C$500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) can be imposed per violation at statutory maximums in the multi-million-dollar range for repeat or systemic failures; Criminal Code money-laundering offences under s.462.31 carry imprisonment up to 10 years; proceeds of unlawful activity and related property are subject to forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners who direct, authorize or acquiesce in AML non-compliance are personally liable under PCMLTFA and Criminal Code provisions; personal exposure includes fines, imprisonment, asset freezes, travel bans and extradition; the corporate veil is pierced where the remittance agency operates as a conduit for illicit fund movement rather than a genuine business.
Unremitted Source Deductions / Payroll Trust Compliance

Statutory sanction: ITA s.227(1) and s.227(8): the unremitted amounts are held in trust for the Crown; CRA assesses the full principal plus refundable interest and penalties; s.227(9.1) penalties for failure to remit as required; repeated failure adds further penalties; CRA may garnish bank accounts, seize property and invalidate payroll programs.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable under ITA s.227.1 for 100% of unremitted source deductions, interest and penalties with no cap; personal liability notices are issued before corporate dissolution; director liability survives bankruptcy of the corporation; fraudulent diversion of source deductions constitutes criminal conduct and may result in personal imprisonment and restitution orders.
Shell Company / GAAR Treaty and Substance Abuse (ITA s.245)

Statutory sanction: GAAR under s.245(2) denies the tax benefit and recharacterizes the abusive transaction; treaty shopping is struck under limitation-on-benefits provisions and the principal-purpose test; CRS/FATCA reporting failures attract penalties; CRA may impose gross-negligence penalties for undisclosed conduit arrangements; the corporation faces reassessment and loss of treaty-based refund claims.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who establish or maintain the shell are treated as directing minds; personal civil liability attaches under fraudulent preference and wrongful trading principles recognized through the corporate veil; tax evasion charges may lead to imprisonment, travel bans and extradition; PBSA/ABCA dissolution does not extinguish director liability for false oaths or unlawful distributions.
