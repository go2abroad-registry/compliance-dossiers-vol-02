Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html; Alberta corporate income tax is assessed and collected federally under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act administration agreement, so the CRA is the single competent statutory authority for GST/HST and federal/Alberta corporate tax filings.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain an incorporation certificate from the Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) for an Alberta Business Corporation, or from Corporations Canada for a federal corporation registering extraprovincially in Alberta. Step 2: File Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or register online through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) service; no fee is chargeable for the initial BN. Step 3: Upon approval, the CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN); the GST/HST program account is created by selecting the 'RT' program account suffix, or by later filing Form RC1 or through My Business Account. Step 4: A non-resident corporation without a Canadian permanent establishment must nonetheless register for GST/HST where it makes taxable supplies in Canada, provides services in Canada, solicits orders in Canada, or offers property for supply in Canada; supporting identity documentation (foreign certificate of good standing, director identification, proof of foreign tax registration) must accompany the RC1 filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; a Business Corporation is per se an entity-level taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Canada). Available mechanisms include: (i) the s.216 ITA election allowing non-residents to report Canadian property income on a net taxable basis instead of Part XIII gross withholding; (ii) the s.156 ITA associated-corporation designation and the s.156 Excise Tax Act ('ETA') closely-correlated corporations joint election permitting related registrants to file a single GST/HST return; (iii) the s.167 ETA election treating a transfer of a business as a single taxable supply; (iv) the hybrid-mismatch restriction framework under s.18.4 ITA, which disallows deduction/non-inclusion outcomes where a foreign entity is fiscally transparent in its home jurisdiction but entity-level in Canada; and (v) CRA administrative 'fiscally transparent entity' positions for foreign LLCs and partnerships applying the OECD BEPS Action 2 commentary rather than a unilateral check-the-box filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — annual worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies (small-supplier threshold under s.148 ETA). A non-resident without a Canadian permanent establishment generally has no CAD 30,000 small-supplier exemption once it carries on commercial activity in Canada or makes taxable supplies through a fixed establishment; registration is triggered from the first taxable supply in Canada even where subsequent export zero-rating would apply.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate applies in Canada. Federal procurement invoicing is transitioning to the PEPPOL e-invoicing framework (voluntary for business-to-business; mandatory for certain federal suppliers), and the CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns for registrants above prescribed thresholds, but each invoice is not pre-cleared through an API-based tax authority system. Records must be preserved in readable electronic format for audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility with a free-floating Canadian dollar (CAD). No exchange-control approvals, central-bank permits, quotas, or prior-notification requirements apply to inbound capital injection, outbound disbursements, intercompany loans, dividends, or arbitration settlement payments. The only remittance frictions are fiscal and informational: (i) 25% statutory Part XIII withholding on dividends, interest, rents, or management fees paid to non-residents under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)), generally reduced by tax treaty (e.g., 5-15% on dividends, 0-15% on interest); (ii) Canadian information returns for non-resident-owned and controlled corporations (forms T106, T1134); (iii) Canadian FATCA/CRS financial-account reporting; and (iv) FINTRAC reporting of each electronic funds transfer (EFT) of CAD 10,000 or more. Profit remittance is therefore fully convertible, subject to tax-withholding compliance rather than exchange-control permission.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canadian law imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export proceeds or litigation recovery payments. A resident Alberta corporation may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in CAD or in foreign-currency accounts at a Schedule I bank, provided that (a) books and records are preserved under the Income Tax Act and the Business Corporations Act (Alberta), and (b) each inbound or outbound EFT of CAD 10,000 or more is reported to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); monetary and financial-stability authority. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration: exchange controls were effectively abolished in 1951 and the Foreign Exchange Control Act was finally repealed in 1990. Cross-border funds reporting and AML/ATF supervision are exercised by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium (restricted). Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, or European-licensed electronic-money institutions) may lawfully provide foreign-exchange, expense-payment, and low-value disbursement services in Canada only as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA, with mandatory transaction reporting and a compliant AML/ATF program. They are not authorized to take deposits or hold client trust funds. Under the Law Society of Alberta Rules Part 6, trust monies must be held at a chartered bank, credit union, or licensed trust corporation authorized in Alberta. Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Sanctions exposure through adverse parties, counterparties, or settlement flows involving restricted jurisdictions

Root cause: Sanctions exposure through adverse parties, counterparties, or settlement flows involving restricted jurisdictions

Defense evidence: (1) institutional sanctions-screening report of all counterparties against the Special Economic Measures Act (Canada), the United Nations Act (Canada), the Criminal Code (Canada), and OFAC SDN lists; (2) legal opinion on the territorial and jurisdictional application of sanctions; (3) contractual clauses blocking sanctioned payment instructions; (4) enhanced due-diligence file with senior compliance-officer sign-off; (5) transaction-screening logs for the full account relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for an inbound equity or shareholder-loan injection from a foreign parent: (1) originate SWIFT MT103 (with MT202 COV cover) to the Alberta entity's CAD account at a Tier 1 bank, quoting the beneficiary account number and narrative 'Equity Contribution' or 'Intercompany Loan', referencing the Alberta corporate registry number and business activity (ISIC 6910 / NAICS 541110); (2) execute a shareholder resolution subscribing treasury shares or a loan agreement prior to credit; (3) record the share subscription or loan in the corporate securities register without delay and before the next board meeting; (4) apply treaty-based Part XIII withholding relief where the payment is interest or dividend and file forms T106/T1134 for the non-resident relationship; (5) screen the remitter and intermediary banks against UN, Canadian SEMA, and OFAC sanctions lists and verify the origin for PEP and FINTRAC purposes; (6) report the inbound EFT to FINTRAC if CAD 10,000 or more; (7) preserve the audit trail of the corresponding-bank reference and the FX conversion rate if the wire is converted to CAD. No Bank of Canada approval, license, or currency-surrender requirement applies.Clearing rails for cross-border trade settlements and litigation disbursements: (i) counsel fees, arbitrator fees, expert invoices, court costs, and adverse-party settlement payments — SWIFT MT103 via Lynx (CAD), CHIPS/Fedwire (USD), and TARGET2 (EUR), using a correspondent Nostro bank for USD; (ii) commercial letters of credit — SWIFT MT700 issued by the Alberta bank against cash collateral or performance security for countersecurity obligations; (iii) IOLTA disbursements — trust cheque or wire executed under Law Society of Alberta Rules Part 6 with dual-signature authorization, sub-ledger posting, and monthly reconciliation; (iv) all external EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more reported to FINTRAC; (v) every payment instruction screened against UN, Canadian SEMA, US OFAC, and UK sanctions lists, with beneficiary bank verification (IBAN/ABA and tax identifiers) to avoid return-wire fees and sanctions friction; (vi) beneficiary-bank KYC completed in advance to ensure clearing acceptance of settlement proceeds.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Schedule I dealer bank, cash-flow-matched to known settlement and fee obligations.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted currencies (e.g., offshore CNY, INR) permitted only as a hedge of a genuine underlying litigation-cost or settlement exposure in a non-convertible currency, settled in CAD on published reference rates.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps) for intercompany loan principal and interest conversions, executed under a bank's dealer-trading authority and subject to internal margin and credit-valuation-adjustment requirements.Over-the-counter vanilla FX options (puts and calls) on major currencies for settlement-contingency hedging, requiring dealer credit approval and an ISDA Credit Support Annex.Amortizing forward swaps matched to milestone-based litigation win-fee recoveries, permissible where the bank confirms hedging intent and no speculative leverage is created.
