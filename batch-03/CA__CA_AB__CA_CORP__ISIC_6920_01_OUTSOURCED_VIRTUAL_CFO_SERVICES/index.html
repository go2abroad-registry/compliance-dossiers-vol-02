Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official digital portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement, CRA administers both federal Part I corporate income tax and Alberta provincial corporate income tax; Alberta Treasury Board and Finance is the provincial revenue authority responsible for Alberta-specific taxes (fuel, tobacco, etc.) but not corporate income tax.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Alberta-incorporated Business Corporation: (1) file Articles of Incorporation under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) or the Canada Business Corporations Act, maintaining a registered office in Alberta; (2) apply online or by Form RC1 for the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN), which is the single statutory TIN for all federal and provincial tax programs; (3) activate the required program accounts during BN registration: RT (corporate income tax), GST/HST, RP (payroll if employees are hired), and RM (import/export if applicable); (4) disclose the full particulars of directors, officers, and the ultimate non-resident parent entity (if any) to CRA; (5) CRA issues the BN confirmation and creates a separately referenced GST/HST account number. A foreign entity with no Canadian presence that is required to register as a non-resident supplier must file RC1 under the non-resident GST/HST registrant stream, appoint a Canadian address for service/agent, and evidence its foreign registered address; (6) verify the GST/HST registration status of counterparties through CRA's public GST/HST Registry before issuing invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box pass-through election for corporations. Available statutory elections include: (i) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election among closely related corporations to deem intercompany taxable supplies made for nil consideration; (ii) s. 228(5)/(6) GST/HST return-period and annual-filing elections; (iii) CRA streamlined accounting credit election for non-resident registrants; (iv) Income Tax Act s. 111(4) election to waive the carry-back of taxable losses; (v) Part XIV branch tax elections applicable only to non-resident corporations. Hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.2 and 18.3) apply automatically to deduction/non-inclusion and dual-inclusion mismatches and do not require an affirmative election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold), measured on worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies in the current calendar quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters. A non-resident supplier making taxable supplies in Canada must register for GST/HST once worldwide taxable revenues exceed CAD 30,000, unless all its Canadian supplies are zero-rated; a person may nevertheless register voluntarily. Registration is not required solely for exports or other zero-rated supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 6 (Excise Tax Act) when supplied for export and exported by the supplier or delivered to a common carrier/consignee for export before release from the supplier's custody; proof of actual removal from Canada is mandatory. Services (virtual CFO advisory, multi-currency ledger bookkeeping, outsourced financial management): zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 9 where the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST, the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or intangible personal property intended for use principally in Canada. The supplier should obtain and retain a signed non-resident declaration evidencing the client's jurisdiction of organisation, foreign address, and non-registrant status, together with records showing the service was contracted by and delivered to the foreign person from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Canada Export Report (AES/CAED or carrier-filed export report for goods exceeding CAD 2,000; retained as supporting evidence and not applicable to pure service exports)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / delivery receipt (for goods); for virtual CFO services, a signed services agreement, statement of work, and time-and-attendance log evidencing cross-border performance and delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident purchaser in the agreed foreign currency or Canadian dollars
  • Non-resident status evidence pack: client certificate of good standing, foreign tax residency proof, and GST/HST non-registrant declaration

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, CRA pre-approval of invoices, or digital stamping exists. CRA supports voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing built on the PEPPOL interoperability framework. GST/HST returns and files must be transmitted electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or approved CRA application-to-application (API/SDK) channels. Registrants must retain electronic books and records for at least six years and make them available on CRA demand; invoices must contain the information prescribed by the GST/HST Input Tax Credit Information Regulations but require neither a QR code nor a government-issued fiscal stamp.

CRA export zero-rating verification reviews require reconstruction of the export evidence trail and reconciliation with bank remittances. On failure to substantiate zero-rating, CRA reverses the 0% rate, assesses uncollected GST output tax, disallows related input tax credits, and may impose: s. 280 failure-to-file penalties (1% of net tax plus 0.25% per full month up to 12 months); s. 285 gross negligence penalties of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax evaded; and prescribed interest. The normal GST/HST reassessment period is four years, extended to seven years for misrepresentation or neglect, and is unlimited in cases of fraud. Criminal prosecution under s. 327 of the Excise Tax Act may result in fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Export receivables remaining unpaid beyond 180 days from invoicing trigger CRA audit scrutiny of revenue recognition and collection documentation, and may lead to adjustment of revenues and ITCs if the export is determined not to have been delivered to or paid by the non-resident purchaser.
