Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Principal portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MBA), and Represent a Client. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act using the same federal Business Number; no separate Alberta corporate TIN exists.

E-government filing portal

The federal Business Number (BN, 9 digits) is the statutory Canadian TIN for corporations. A foreign entity obtains the BN by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' through the BRO portal or by paper submission; Alberta incorporated entities may have the BN auto-created via the Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES) data transfer. The GST/HST program account is a 15-character identifier formed by appending an 'RM' prefix and 4-digit reference to the BN (e.g., BN RM0001) and is selected on the same Form RC1. Registration is compulsory once the entity is no longer a small supplier (worldwide taxable supplies exceeding CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters) or, for non-residents carrying on business in Canada, once taxable supplies in Canada exceed the same threshold; voluntary registration is permitted to recover input tax credits and to issue zero-rated invoices. Supporting documents: certificate of incorporation or foreign registration extract, registered office address, director and officer identification, and Alberta extra-provincial registration where applicable.

It has no flow-through status; it computes taxable income and pays federal and provincial corporate tax at entity level. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Canadian federal or Alberta business corporation is a separate legal person and taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (RSC 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.)). It has no flow-through status; it computes taxable income and pays federal and provincial corporate tax at entity level. A resident corporation is subject to Canadian tax on worldwide income, with indirect foreign tax credits for foreign source taxes; non-resident-owned investment corporations and foreign affiliates remain taxable at the entity level with specific exempt-surplus ordering rules.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification follows incorporation law and the Income Tax Act. Relevant elections and anti-hybrid constraints include: (i) GST/HST s. 211 election for closely related corporations to treat separate persons as a single registrant; (ii) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for nil consideration between closely related persons; (iii) Income Tax Act s. 93(1) election to designate a capital gain on disposition of foreign affiliate shares as exempt surplus; (iv) ITA s. 219(1) Part XIV election for non-resident international shipping corporations; (v) ITA s. 216 election for non-residents to report Canadian rental income on a net basis; (vi) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s. 18(4), s. 18(5) and s. 94.2 which disallow deductions where the same payment is deductible in Canada and exempt in the recipient jurisdiction; (vii) ITA s. 256(2) associated-company designations have no tax election effect for GST/HST grouping.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148(1)); calculated on the value of worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, made in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters by the entity and its associates. For a non-resident provider, the threshold applies to taxable supplies made in Canada; once exceeded, mandatory registration is required as of the day the threshold is crossed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory services are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 when the supply is made to a non-resident who is not registered under Part IX and the service is not performed in connection with (i) real property situated in Canada, (ii) tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of the supply, or (iii) intangible property attributable to a permanent establishment in Canada. The non-resident recipient must not be present in Canada at the time the service is performed. Where the supply includes tangible deliverables, zero-rating requires exportation of the goods within two years of the supply and evidence of export to the recipient's foreign address.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A Export Declaration form; required only where tangible goods are exported, not for pure consulting deliverables)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (mandatory for exported goods; retained where hybrid consulting-and-goods engagements include physical deliverables)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103, bank payment advice, or credit note evidencing payment received from the non-resident's bank account)
  • Signed Engagement Letter and Cross-Border Tax Structuring Services Agreement identifying the non-resident client, service scope, fee schedule, and governing currency
  • GST/HST Invoice bearing the supplier's BN, the recipient's foreign name and address, the fee, and the statement 'Zero-rated supply — ETA Schedule VI, s. 7'
  • Declaration of Non-Residency and Confirmation that the Recipient was Not Present in Canada at the Time of Service Performance
  • Working papers, time records, and deliverable logs substantiating offshore performance of the advisory service

None. Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based invoice stamping, or digital stamps for GST/HST invoices. E-invoicing is voluntary; the Canadian market is aligning with PEPPOL and GSTC standards, and the CRA accepts paper, PDF, XML, and EDI invoices provided legible originals are retained for six years. The CRA supports e-filing via GST/HST NETFILE and bulk EDI transmission, but no transactional pre-approval or fiscal validation is required before an invoice is issued.

CRA may reassess within four years of the filing period, without restriction in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default, and may re-characterize a zero-rated supply as taxable, assessing the 5% GST on the full consideration plus prescribed-rate interest. Failure to register carries a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 1% of the consideration for taxable supplies (ETA s. 280). Net tax deficiencies attract a 6% penalty on the assessed amount (ETA s. 285). False statements or omissions made knowingly or under gross negligence are subject to a penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax involved (ETA s. 297.1); fraudulent evasion or wilful misrepresentation in zero-rating claims exposes the entity to a 50% gross-negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2) as well as criminal prosecution under ETA s. 239 with fines and imprisonment. If export zero-rating is claimed without genuine offshore performance or without the required documentary bundle, CRA will deny input tax credits, claw back refunds, apply net tax reassessments, and may invoke the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245) for artificial cross-border structuring loops. Note: Canadian GST/HST law imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is the pipeline's documented substantiation standard for cross-border receivables, supported by CRA guidance requiring evidence of consideration and payment traceability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange control laws or registration requirements for foreign direct investment, dividends, interest, or royalty remittances. Profit remittances to non-residents are subject solely to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (25% treaty-reduced), not to any FX approval or conversion mandate. Banking operations require connection to the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for CAD clearing and SWIFT for cross-border flows, with no settlement currency restrictions other than sanctioned-party screening under robust AML/ATF frameworks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency accounts with authorized financial institutions. Cross-border currency transactions above CAD 10,000 must be reported to the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. There is no timeline or conversion ratio requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMI incumbents are not recognized as banks in Canada and cannot accept deposits or provide account guarantees unless registered under the Bank Act (Part XII) or as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC. Legal constraints include mandatory FINTRAC registration, AML/ATF program compliance, and a prohibition on in-market deposit-taking without a bank license. EMIs may be used only as supplementary payment gateways, not as primary liquidity repositories.
