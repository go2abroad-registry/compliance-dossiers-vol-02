Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portal: CRA My Business Account (MyBA) at https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-taxes/portal.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) and program accounts. Apply via CRA form RC3911 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' (non-resident applicant), or online through the CRA's 'Non-Resident Business Registration' service if eligible. Required supporting documents typically include: certified articles of incorporation, proof of jurisdiction of incorporation, full list of directors, Canadian resident agent for service (if required under Alberta Business Corporations Act), and details of Canadian business activity. CRA issues a 9-digit BN plus a 2-letter program account suffix (e.g., RM for regular income tax, RT for GST/HST). Registration for GST/HST is mandatory once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a trailing 12-month quarter, or immediately for non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada unless the supplies are zero-rated. From 2022, a foreign entity not carrying on business in Canada may still require a 'non-resident tax account' for Part XIII withholding tax. Compliance contact: CRA International and Non-Resident Division.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada); no transparent treatment unless the entity is an unlimited liability company incorporated under Alberta law and validly elects under the Canada-US tax treaty concept (rare and elective). For the standard 'CA_CORP' Business Corporation, default is full CRA entity-level taxation and residence is determined by place of central management and control (de jure and de facto). Territorial scope: Canada taxes worldwide income for resident corporations; non-resident corporations with a Canadian permanent establishment are taxable on Canadian-source business income. Alberta has a provincial corporate income tax (8% Alberta rate) administered by CRA under a single administration agreement; the Canada Revenue Agency collects both federal and Alberta corporate taxes.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. Entities are classified by statutory rules. Relevant statutory elections include: (1) election under section 216 (Return for Non-Resident of Canada) to report Canadian rental or timber royalties income on a net basis; (2) election under subsection 115(1) or 115A for non-residents carrying on business in Canada; (3) concurrent Part I/Part XIII withholding tax election for certain non-arm's length cross-border services (Reg. 105 waiver/concurrent); (4) GST/HST 'qualifying collateral' and 'section 156(2)' election between closely related corporations to streamline GST/HST accounting; (5) joint venture election under GST/HST section 273 for co-venturers; (6) corporate amalgamation winding-up continuation elections (s. 87 / 88); (7) treaty-based hybrid entity remedial election for fiscally transparent entities (if applicable under a specific income tax treaty). No general hybrid mismatch election beyond those expressly listed; CRA applies domestic anti-avoidance rules and the OECD BEPS hybrid mismatch provisions via statutory amendments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (gross revenue from worldwide taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated, in a single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters). A non-resident not carrying on business in Canada may elect to register voluntarily; however, registration for GST/HST is mandatory for a non-resident that makes taxable supplies in Canada unless all such supplies are zero-rated. For 'commercial activity' a foreign corporation may become a 'registrant' once the threshold is crossed, and must file a GST/HST return even if operating at a loss.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: Supply of goods exported from Canada is zero-rated under section 9 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the supplier retains evidence of export within prescribed timeframes (usually within 60 days if the purchaser is not a registrant, or within one year under normal commercial terms). Services: A supply of a service made to a non-resident is zero-rated under section 7(1), Part V Sch. VI if the service is performed wholly or mainly outside Canada or is an 'advisory, consultancy, or professional service' performed for a non-resident even if partly performed in Canada, unless the service is 'in connection with' real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada (other than the goods being exported), or a service rendered to a person present in Canada with respect to Canadian-source employment. For market-entry consultancy for a foreign client expanding overseas, the service is generally zero-rated as an 'advisory service supplied to a non-resident' provided there is a written contract and the service does not relate to Canadian real estate. If the consultant also provides Canadian regulatory licensing agency services (i.e., representing the client before Canadian authorities), that component may be subject to GST at 5% because it is a service performed in Canada and not a zero-rated export service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form A64/ACI eManifest for goods; may require a CBSA 'Export Declaration' or 'Goods Available for Export to be Reported' (GAR) through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for goods over CAD 2,000)
  • Bill of Lading / waybill (for goods exported by air, sea, rail, or truck; proof of export must name the supplier as shipper or the shipper consigned to non-resident)
  • Commercial invoice and proof of contract (for services, a signed engagement letter, detailed narrative of service, processing/reporting deliverables, and invoice addressed to the non-resident client)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank wire, SWIFT, or letter of credit) evidencing payment in foreign currency to a Canadian bank in the exporter's name; for services, the supplier must retain bank confirmation of receipt from the non-resident
  • For services: completed Form GST66 'Application for Refund by Non-Resident' or GST189 'General Application for GST/HST Rebate' when applicable, plus CRA's 'GST/HST Export Evidence Requirements' (RC4020) stated in commercial books and records.

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing, or digital stamping for GST/HST. Electronically generated invoices are acceptable if they comply with section 3 of the ETA (GST/HST information requirements). GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE, GST/HST TELEFILE, or through a registered service provider; the CRA strongly recommends electronic payments. Since 2022, CRA has adopted the Peppol framework on a voluntary basis for B2B e-invoicing (test/pilot). No pre-approval or fiscal stamp is required before issuing invoices. Record retention: all electronic records must be retained in readable format and made available to CRA on request, and GST/HST invoices must show premium, dealer, or patented medicine status when applicable (not relevant to consultancy).

Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; GST/HST zero-rating does not require repatriation but does require evidence that payment was received or reasonably expected within a period consistent with commercial practice, and CRA may treat a claim as invalid if the supplier does not demonstrate that the service actually supplied to the non-resident. Priorities of audit scrutiny include: (1) false or overstated zero-rating claims by a non-resident or export intermediary; (2) artificially arranging a service to appear non-resident when the recipient is a Canadian resident; (3) missing or insufficient documentation under s. 286 of the Excise Tax Act. Penalties: under s. 280(1) ETA, knowingly or under circumstances of negligence making a false statement in a return can result in a penalty of 25% of the net tax stated to be payable or, if no net tax, a minimum CAD 250 (for voluntary disclosure). Where the claim is fraudulent, criminal penalties under s. 380 of the Criminal Code (Canada) may apply: up to 5 years imprisonment and/or fine in the prosecutor's discretion. Retrospective clawback: CRA may reassess within 4 years for ordinary negligence; 7 years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default; and indefinitely in cases of fraud (s. 298 ETA). Interest (compound daily) at prescribed rates and 'failure to comply' penalties under s. 284 can also apply. Consultancy firms claiming 0% GST on all cross-border services must maintain a transfer-pricing-compliant engagement file to avoid recharacterization of the export as a Canadian taxable supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full CAD convertibility on current and capital account; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and no exchange-control rationing, currency approval, or repatriation license applies; non-residents may freely remit dividends, management fees, royalties, and loan repayments from an Alberta corporation subject only to Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding and treaty relief, standard AML wire verification, and Investment Canada Act notification/review rules that do not impair convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or statutory realization period exists in Canada for export receipts, service fees, or consultancy proceeds; exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency in unrestricted CAD or USD accounts; financial institutions must report all cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC as EFTRs and all cash transactions above CAD 10,000 as LCTRs; supporting commercial documentation must be preserved for five years under the PCMLTFA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with prudential banking supervision by OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) and AML/CTF administration by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada); Canada operates without a dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority on the model of a SAFE-type agency.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 related-party transaction reporting threshold is CAD 1,000,000 annually; CRA contemporaneous documentation expectations apply to all controlled transactions exceeding a materiality threshold and to CbC-reporting groups with consolidated revenue over EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations; however, CRA uses the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), sham doctrine and substance-over-form principles to disregard artifical transactions. A market-entry consultancy must demonstrate real personnel, contractual functions and decision-making in Alberta to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, up to 5 years imprisonment and a fine of up to 200% of evaded tax; civil penalty under ITA s.163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted amounts; travel bans and foreign-asset freezes may be imposed through mutual assistance requests.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial of tax benefits, reassessment, interest, and a 50% gross-negligence penalty; treaty abuse under OECD MLI may also result in denial of treaty withholding rate.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for corporate tax shortfalls if they signed false certifications or directed an artificial arrangement, and may face criminal prosecution for fraudulent conveyances.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment restating taxable income; s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (CND 5,000 if understatement exceeds CND 5,000); plus interest and potential gross-negligence penalty if deliberate.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly instruct artificial TP schemes can receive director penalty assessments for unremitted non-resident withholding taxes; extradition risk for bilateral tax treaty offences.
Part XIII Refund / Dividend Strip Abuse

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax assessed at 25% on gross distributions plus s.227.1 penalties equal to the amount withheld; anti-treaty-shopping rules under the revised Canada-US tax treaty and MLI prevent treaty benefit claims for pure conduit entities.

Director personal liability: Board members may be personally liable for withheld amounts plus interest and may face orders prohibiting foreign business travel where assets are transferred abroad to avoid collection.
