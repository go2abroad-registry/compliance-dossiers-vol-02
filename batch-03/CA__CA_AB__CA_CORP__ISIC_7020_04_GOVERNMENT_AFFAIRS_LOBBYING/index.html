Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/business-number-registration.html; Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) for extra-provincial corporate registration under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Alberta business corporation must: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially in Alberta, or extra-provincially register the foreign corporation with the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain an Alberta entity registration number; (2) obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or using the BRO online wizard, explicitly disclosing non-resident status where applicable; (3) simultaneously register for a GST/HST account (RT program) and a corporate income tax account (RC program); non-residents must provide a Canadian registered office address or resident agent and may be required to post security for GST/HST under s. 240 of the Excise Tax Act (ETA); (4) register for payroll deductions (RP program) if employees are engaged in Alberta; (5) open an import/export account (RM program) and obtain any required customs security or carrier bond if goods cross borders; (6) obtain the mandatory lobbying authorization: registration under the federal Lobbying Act with the Office of the Commissioner of Lobbying of Canada and/or registration under the Alberta Lobbyists Act with the Alberta Ethics Commissioner; proof of lobbying registration is a compliance prerequisite for government-facing billings and is separately auditable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — an Alberta-incorporated or extra-provincially registered business corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation (or a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Alberta). It is not a pass-through entity: it must file a T2 corporate income tax return and pay federal Part I tax (15% general rate, or 9% small-business rate on the first CAD $500,000 of active business income for a Canadian-controlled private corporation) plus Alberta corporate income tax (8% general rate, or 11% small-business rate on the first CAD $500,000 of taxable income for a qualifying CCPC); the combined rates are 23% general and 20% small-business. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Alberta-incorporated or extra-provincially registered business corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation (or a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Alberta). It is not a pass-through entity: it must file a T2 corporate income tax return and pay federal Part I tax (15% general rate, or 9% small-business rate on the first CAD $500,000 of active business income for a Canadian-controlled private corporation) plus Alberta corporate income tax (8% general rate, or 11% small-business rate on the first CAD $500,000 of taxable income for a qualifying CCPC); the combined rates are 23% general and 20% small-business. A non-resident corporation is taxed on Canadian-source business income under Part I, is subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reduced) on passive distributions, and may be liable for Part XIV branch tax (25%, treaty-reduced) on after-tax Canadian branch profits. The ISIC 7020 / NAICS 541820 government-relations advisory scope does not attract any tax-exempt-zone or territorial-classification benefit; worldwide income taxation applies to a resident corporation.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime and no election is available to treat a corporation as a pass-through for tax purposes. The elections available to this entity include: (1) Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 216 election — a non-resident with Canadian rental or royalty income may file a net-basis Canadian return; (2) ITA s. 219 election — a non-resident corporation may elect deemed residence in Canada for certain asset-disposition and branch-exit planning; (3) ETA s. 156 election — election between closely related corporations to treat intercorporate taxable supplies as non-taxable/non-consideration supplies; (4) ETA s. 211.6 and the simplified GST/HST registration election for non-residents — streamlines registration, return filing, and rebate procedures; (5) hybrid-mismatch anti-avoidance rules under ITA ss. 14, 23, 94 to 95, 96, 111, and 112 — Canada enforces BEPS Action 2 deeming provisions automatically, with no elective carve-out, to disallow deductions for hybrid mismatch arrangements; (6) reduced treaty-based withholding elections under s. 216.1 ITA and Regulation 105 where the Canada-US or other applicable treaty provides a lower rate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 — the small-supplier threshold under s. 148 ETA. For a non-resident, the threshold is measured by worldwide revenues from taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory for any non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada in the course of a business. Registration below the threshold is voluntary and permits recovery of input tax credits through the ordinary GST/HST return; CRA may require security under s. 240 ETA from non-residents without a permanent establishment in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V ETA where goods are exported by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf and are exported within 60 days of delivery to a common carrier (or within a longer period accepted by CRA as reasonable), with contemporaneous documentary evidence. Services (lobbying and government-relations advisory, ISIC 7020 / NAICS 541820): a supply of a service to a non-resident person is zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V, s. 9 ETA unless the service is a personal service rendered in Canada to an individual present in Canada, or the service is performed wholly in Canada and relates solely to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Cross-border trade-policy analysis and foreign-stakeholder lobbying supplied to a foreign government or foreign principal therefore qualify for 0% GST where the recipient is a non-resident and the service relates to intangible property or policy outcomes outside Canada; a domestic lobbying retainer for a Canadian-resident client is taxable at 5% GST. Mixed domestic and export supplies must be apportioned on a fair-value basis and supported by contemporaneous time and activity records.

Registrants must retain sales and purchase invoices, including export substantiation records, for a minimum of six years in readable format and produce them on CRA audit. CRA may reassess a zero-rated export claim within the normal four-year reassessment period, or indefinitely where fraud, wilful misrepresentation, or failure to file is established. If zero-rating is denied, the supplier is assessed 5% GST on the consideration, plus prescribed interest on unpaid tax under s. 280 ETA. Civil penalties include: (a) s. 280 ETA gross-negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD $250 and 25% of the net tax understated; (b) s. 285 ETA failure to register or failure to collect tax — 10% of the tax plus the full tax amount; (c) s. 327 ETA tax evasion — fines up to CAD $25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; (d) s. 328 ETA false statements or conspiracy — fines up to double the amount of tax sought to be evaded. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, surrender requirements, or restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, or royalty payments. The only substantive hurdles are regulatory and tax-based, namely withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (typically 25% on dividends, interest, and royalties absent treaty relief) and compliance with the Investment Canada Act for non-resident acquisitions of control. No central-bank approval is required for repatriation of capital or earnings; the free-floating CAD and robust interbank market ensure unrestricted access to foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Under the Export and Import Permits Act and Bank of Canada regulations, exporters may retain foreign currency earnings in any licensed financial institution or convert them at market rates. No surrender obligation exists; all export receipts may be held, used, or exchanged freely. For supervisory purposes, banks report currency transactions in excess of CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC, but this does not affect convertibility or surrender.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acting in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for currency issuance, banking supervision, and anti-money-laundering/terrorist-financing compliance respectively. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange control ministry; the Bank of Canada and OSFI jointly administer the liberal capital-flow regime under the IMF Article VIII obligation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW for primary corporate banking. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut, or Payoneer are permitted as payment service providers but are not licensed to accept deposits in Canada. They cannot maintain a capital escrow account or issue cheques from a Canadian clearing code. A foreign EMI may be used only as an ancillary operational satellite for expenses in limited jurisdictions, but it will not satisfy OSFI Tier-1 banking expectations or the enhanced due diligence required for lobbying/foreign-stakeholder business. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for foreign-shareholder capital injections into the Alberta corporation uses a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 message from the remitting bank to the receiving Canadian Schedule I bank, referencing the purpose 'Share Capital Subscription' or 'Paid-in Capital' in the narrative field. For amounts above CAD 10,000, FINTRAC's Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) is triggered and the bank must record the remitting account, beneficiary account, and beneficial owners. A board resolution approving the injection, a certified share certificate/ledger, and a source-of-funds declaration must be presented at or before settlement. No investment approval is required if the contribution does not exceed the ownership thresholds under the Investment Canada Act, but a post-closing notice may be necessary for larger acquisitions. All funds must be remitted through the banking system; cash or third-party prepaid instruments are not acceptable.Outbound trade settlements are executed primarily by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers, documentary letters of credit (commercial L/C) via MT700, MT710 or MT760 for guarantee instruments, and, for smaller payments, through the Electronic Data Interchange for Finance (EDIFACT) used by Canadian clearing and settlement. Payments in USD, EUR, or other convertible currencies may be routed through correspondent banking or the Clearing House Interbank Payments System (CHIPS) for USD, with fintech-side rails limited to non-deposit payment facilitators. The bank will require underlying export/consultancy invoices, copies of contracts, and customs documentation to support each outbound disbursement, ensuring full auditability and compliance with Canada’s trade sanctions and export control legislation.Outright Foreign Exchange Forwards up to 12 months in traded CAD pairs (USD/CAD, EUR/CAD, GBP/CAD) with a licensed Canadian dealerNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for currencies without full convertibility, executed offshore and cash-settled in CAD without physical deliveryCross-Currency Swaps (CCS) for exchanging principal and fixed/floating interest rate payment obligations on underlying commercial debtVanilla FX Options (puts/calls) on major tradable currencies for treasury hedging of trade-policy retainer flowsCanadian Dollar Overnight Indexed Swaps and Basis Swaps to manage liquidity and interest-rate mismatch on foreign-currency borrowings

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: LOW for primary corporate banking. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut, or Payoneer are permitted as payment service providers but are not licensed to accept deposits in Canada. They cannot maintain a capital escrow account or issue cheques from a Canadian clearing code. A foreign EMI may be used only as an ancillary operational satellite for expenses in limited jurisdictions, but it will not satisfy OSFI Tier-1 banking expectations or the enhanced due diligence required for lobbying/foreign-stakeholder business. Canadian incorporation and physical banking relationships remain mandatory for substantive treasury operations.
