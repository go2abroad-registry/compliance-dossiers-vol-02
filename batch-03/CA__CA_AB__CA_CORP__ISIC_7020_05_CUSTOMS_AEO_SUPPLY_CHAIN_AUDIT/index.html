Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta); a non-offering corporation may incorporate with nominal authorized capital. No escrow account or capital deposit rule applies; consideration for issued shares may be cash, property, or past services (s. 24 ABCA).

Funding & unlock path: Alberta Fire Code compliance for office premises (fire extinguishers, alarm systems, and unobstructed egress); municipal building occupancy permit confirming structural load suitability for the office use; and Workers' Compensation Board of Alberta clearance letter (or exemption confirmation) where employees are engaged, together with an Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) workplace hazard assessment per the Alberta OHS Code.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta); a non-offering corporation may incorporate with nominal authorized capital. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration via Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA); Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) — provincial corporate income tax administration (AT1 return); Alberta Corporate Registry — extra-provincial registration and Corporate Access Number (CAN); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) — customs/export reporting (CARM portal) and AEO administration (PIP/CSA programs).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the business corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (federal) or the Alberta Business Corporations Act; if federally incorporated, register extra-provincially with the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain the Corporate Access Number (CAN). Step 2 — Obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, selecting program accounts: RT (corporate income tax), RC (GST/HST), RP (payroll) and RZ (revenue). Step 3 — A foreign-owned corporation must designate a Canadian resident representative for service of notices, register for GST/HST where taxable supplies in Canada exceed the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold or are expected to, and file the T2 return within six months of its fiscal year-end. Step 4 — Register the same BN with Alberta TRA for provincial corporate income tax and file the AT1 return where a permanent establishment exists in Alberta.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the CA_CORP is a resident taxpayer subject to worldwide income taxation under Part I of the Income Tax Act and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; no pass-through or disregarded-entity treatment is available for Canadian business corporations. Headline rates: federal general corporate rate 15%; Alberta general corporate rate 8%; combined general rate 23%. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the CA_CORP is a resident taxpayer subject to worldwide income taxation under Part I of the Income Tax Act and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; no pass-through or disregarded-entity treatment is available for Canadian business corporations. Headline rates: federal general corporate rate 15%; Alberta general corporate rate 8%; combined general rate 23%. The federal small business deduction (9% federal / 2% Alberta) is denied because a foreign-owned corporation is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC).

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regulation; corporations are per se entity-level taxpayers. Relevant elections: (i) ETA s.156 — election by closely related registrant corporations to treat taxable intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration, reducing GST/HST cash-flow frictions; (ii) ETA s.240(1) — voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier to claim input tax credits; (iii) GST/HST filing-period election (annual versus quarterly or monthly); (iv) hybrid-mismatch exposure is constrained by ITA ss.18.2–18.4 and the Canada-US Treaty Art. IV(7) fiscally transparent entity ordering rule where interposed flow-through vehicles are used.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — GST/HST small-supplier threshold (ETA s.240); registration is mandatory where taxable supplies made in Canada exceed C$30,000 in any single calendar quarter or the aggregate of four consecutive calendar quarters; zero-rated exports count as taxable supplies for threshold purposes but attract no actual tax; non-resident B2B audit service providers must register once this threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V (ETA ss.20–22) where the supplier ships or delivers the goods to a non-resident recipient and the goods are exported as soon as reasonably possible, evidenced by transport documents and export declarations. Services: an AEO supply-chain audit, consulting or professional service supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, para 23(b), provided the service does not relate to (i) real property situated in Canada, (ii) tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or (iii) an individual present in Canada; that portion of the service physically performed against Canadian-sited inventory or premises does not qualify.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA export reporting (CARM/CAED electronic filing) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier evidencing exit of goods from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing FX repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and executed service contract (zero-rated AEO audit services)
  • Foreign recipient's non-resident status evidence (foreign business registry extract or foreign VAT/GST number)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or mandatory e-invoicing regime exists at the federal or Alberta level. Canada is implementing an optional pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (Peppol PINT standard, announced in Budget 2023; voluntary participation as of 2025). Mandatory electronic filing: GST/HST registrants with annual gross revenue above C$1 million must file and remit electronically (GST/HST NETFILE plus electronic payment). For customs trade flows, the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal is now the mandatory digital channel for import/export accounting and duty/GST collection; AEO (PIP/CSA) participants must maintain EDI/API connectivity with CBSA.

CRA and CBSA may conduct joint verification of exported quantities, valuations and service-recipient status. A zero-rating claim must be substantiated within the 6-year records retention period (ETA s.286). Denial or overstatement triggers clawback of unremitted GST at 5% plus prescribed daily interest (ETA s.280) and civil penalties: ETA s.285 false-statement penalty — the greater of C$100 and 25% of the net tax misstatement; ITA s.163(2) gross-negligence penalty — 50% of additional tax payable for wilful blindness or negligence; criminal exposure under ETA s.327 and Criminal Code s.380(1) where intent is established. The CRA reassessment window is four years, unlimited where a return contains misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default (ETA s.298(2)). CBSA may also levy administrative monetary penalties (AMPS) of up to C$25,000 per export-reporting contravention and suspend or revoke AEO (PIP/CSA) certification for systemic non-compliance.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. No central-bank permit or exchange-control approval is required for equity injections, loan repatriation, dividend remittance, or trade settlements. Profit remittance is unrestricted in law, but Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced) applies to dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents, and FATCA/CRS reporting and a valid CRA non-resident tax number may be required for streamlined remittance. CAD/USD and other major-currency conversions are freely transacted by the banking system at commercial rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation time limit. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign currency in Canadian or offshore foreign-currency accounts. Cross-border currency or monetary instrument movements in excess of CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC, but that reporting is not a conversion or surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) — OSFI supervises banks but has no foreign-exchange-control mandate; Canada does not operate a central exchange-control approval agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a global payment pipeline extension, Low as a primary transactional banking solution. Canada does not recognise the European EMI/e-money passport. A non-resident EMI can only take Canadian-dollar deposits or issue e-money to the corporate client if it obtains a Canadian bank licence under the Bank Act or an order of the Minister of Finance; in ordinary practice, foreign EMIs such as Wise operate as FINTRAC-registered money services businesses (MSBs) and use a Canadian Schedule I bank for actual CAD clearing, custodial balances, and settlement. Using an unlicensed offshore EMI for primary corporate deposits is legally unavailable, exposes the operating entity to uninsured-funding risk, and may trigger enhanced scrutiny from OSFI and FINTRAC.

Defense evidence: Executed client service agreement; detailed invoice ledger; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Partners in Protection (PIP) certificate; AEO-like security audit report; customs broker engagement letter; and a list of completed audit projects with reference numbers.

Defense evidence: Denied-party/sanction screening logs for all counterparties; bills of lading and Customs Entry documents; Incoterms and contractual chain; independent international trade compliance opinion; and enhanced due-diligence file demonstrating no sanctioned country predicate.

Defense evidence: Directors' resolution authorising share issuance; Canada Revenue Agency corporate filings; shareholders' register update; share certificate specimens; and bank reference letter confirming the remitter's source of funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital is routed by international wire transfer using the SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire/CHIPS for USD funding) to the corporate CAD or USD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The payment narrative should state the purpose as ‘share capital contribution’, ‘equity contribution’, or ‘shareholder loan advance’ and include the corporate legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, and Canadian bank account instructions (institution number, transit number, and SWIFT BIC). Canada does not operate a fixed purpose-code taxonomy; the bank records the narrative and may assign an internal transaction code for FINTRAC reporting. For CAD clearing, the settlement leg uses Payments Canada Rails, including Lynx for high-value real-time gross settlement and ACSS for batch automated clearing. The bank must verify the share register, directors' resolution, and source of funds before releasing the full balance for operational disbursement; no Bank of Canada approval or exchange-control permit is required.Outbound trade payments for supply-chain audit services, customs compliance work, and operational consulting are cleared through (i) SWIFT MT103 for standard single customer credit transfers, (ii) SWIFT MT700/MT701 for documentary Commercial Letters of Credit issued or confirmed by the Canadian bank, and (iii) SWIFT MT202 for bank-to-bank cover when a separate cover payment is required. Domestic disbursements in Canadian dollars use Payments Canada Rails: Lynx for urgent high-value payments, Real-Time Rail for immediate retail/API payments, and ACSS for batch electronic funds transfer. Trade settlements require supporting invoice packs, service certificates, CBSA or customs documentation, and, where applicable, a letter of credit or documentary collection instruction. Payment clearing is unrestricted; no Bank of Canada permission is required.
