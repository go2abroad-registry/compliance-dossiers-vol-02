Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) is the competent federal tax authority, operating the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register.html. In Alberta, provincial corporate income tax is administered on behalf of Alberta by the CRA under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement; the provincial fiscal authority is Alberta Finance – Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) (https://www.alberta.ca/tax-and-revenue-administration). Alberta levies no provincial sales tax and no harmonized sales tax.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Alberta business corporation obtains a statutory tax identification number through the following procedure: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) via Corporations Canada Online Filing Centre or Alberta Corporate Registry, obtaining a nine-digit Business Number (BN) upon initial CRA registration; (2) where the BN is not obtained at incorporation, apply online through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO), by paper Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number (BN)', or by telephone; (3) open CRA Program Accounts necessary for the corporation's operations: Corporate Income Tax (RC account ending in C), Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) (RT), Payroll (RP) where employees are engaged, and Import/Export (RM/RD) where physical goods are cleared; (4) a non-resident foreign corporation making taxable supplies in Canada must separately apply for GST/HST registration using Form RC4155 'Application for Registration of a Non-Resident Organization for GST/HST (Simplified)' or BRO, and may optionally register for a BN program account without incorporating; (5) the CRA issues the BN in writing (Business Number confirmation / GST/HST account notice); the BN serves as the single TIN for both corporate income tax and GST/HST, and Alberta corporate tax is assessed under the same BN through the federal collection agreement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election; a corporation is statutorily taxable at the entity level, and the CRA does not permit transparent treatment. Cross-border and group elections relevant to an offshore BIM/CAD export structure include: (a) Excise Tax Act (ETA) subsection 273(1) election for closely related corporations to treat certain supplies as made for nil consideration, eliminating GST/HST compliance friction between related GST registrants; (b) ETA subsection 167(1) joint election to use the 'tax charged in error' recovery procedure; (c) Income Tax Act (ITA) subsections 216(1) and 217 elections for non-resident taxpayers to compute Canadian-source rental/employment income on a net basis; (d) ITA subsection 219(4) election in respect of non-resident-owned investment corporation dispositions; (e) ITA subsection 250(1) deemed residence and branch-loss streaming allowances; (f) hybrid mismatch rules: Canada enacted targeted OECD BEPS Action 2 rules (ITA sections 12.6, 18.2, 18.3, 20(1)(n.1), 163.1 and 212.3) disallowing deductions in hybrid mismatch arrangements, but there is no elective regime—compliance is mandatory and fact-based. The Canadian corporation may also elect, under ITA subsection 249.1, an alternative corporate taxation year-end (other than December 31) with CRA acceptance, and a foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada may elect under ETA section 266 to be deemed registered in limited supply situations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold under ETA subsection 148(1): a person, including a non-resident corporation, must register for GST/HST if its total annual gross revenue from taxable supplies exceeds CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the four consecutive calendar quarters; below this threshold registration is voluntary. A non-resident making no taxable supplies in Canada that only zero-exports services may apply for a GST/HST rebate of input tax credits via Form GST66 and may be registered on a voluntary basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7, a supply of a service is zero-rated (0% GST) if the supply is made to a non-resident person and the service is not performed on tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, is not related to the non-resident's Canadian business or Canadian invitation/inducement, and the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST. For offshore BIM/CAD and structural drafting services supplied by an Alberta corporation to non-resident construction/EPC clients, the services will qualify for zero-rating where the deliverables (CAD files, BIM models, structural calculations) are produced for projects physically located outside Canada and the foreign client has no Canadian GST registry. Strictly domestic-to-domestic B2B supplies in the EPC chain (e.g., supply by the Canadian corporation to a Canadian prime contractor that later exports construction services) remain taxable at 5% GST. Services supplied to a non-resident that involve Canadian real property or the non-resident's Canadian establishment are taxable; therefore the Canadian supplier must obtain and document the client's non-resident status.

  • No Canadian Customs export declaration is required for exported intangible/zero-rated BIM/CAD services; Canada does not treat engineering services as goods subject to export declarations under the Customs Act or the Export of Goods Regulations
  • Signed supply/services agreement with the non-resident client containing a non-Canadian project situs clause, delivery terms and payment currency
  • Tax invoice/commercial invoice referencing the GST/HST registration number and showing zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI Part V section 7
  • Inward remittance evidence: SWIFT MT103/MT202 wire transfer confirmation or Wise transaction receipt showing foreign-funder remittance to the Canadian corporate account
  • BIM/CAD deliverable handover records (electronic transmittal logs, issue sheets, PDF/IFC model download records) proving services are not connected with real or tangible property in Canada
  • Certificate of non-resident status of the client (foreign tax registration, registered office address, or client representation letter)
  • CRA Form GST66 (where applicable) claiming a GST/HST rebate of Canada-input tax paid by a non-resident, together with supporting invoices for Canadian input tax credits

Canada does not presently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based continuous transaction controls (CTC), or digital stamping/e-invoicing for GST. CRA requires taxpayers to maintain adequate books and records in electronic or paper form (ETA section 286); electronic invoices are acceptable, but there is no mandatory e-invoice issuance or exchange platform. The Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework is nascent and voluntary, with federal, provincial and municipal governments progressively adopting Peppol/PINT standards for B2G invoicing; mandatory B2B clearance is not legislated as of the knowledge cutoff. For an export-service business, invoices should nevertheless conform to ETA section 169 input tax credit disclosure requirements (e.g., supplier name, BN/RT, amount of tax, description, date) and the customer's document retention rules.

CRA audits zero-rated export claims under the ETA with heightened scrutiny on non-resident status, place-of-supply situs and the absence of Canadian real-property linkage. If zero-rating is denied, the supplier is assessed for 5% GST on the gross consideration together with daily compound interest under ETA section 280. Penalties include: (a) failure to register (ETA section 280(1)(a)); (b) knowingly making a false statement — minimum CAD 250 and up to 10% of the net tax amount (ETA section 285); (c) gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated net tax (ETA section 285.1); and (d) under the Income Tax Act, a false-statement penalty of 50% of the tax avoided for knowingly participating in a misstatement (ITA subsection 163(2)). Criminal prosecution under ITA section 239 and ETA section 328 carries, upon indictment, a fine up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years, with additional civil assessments and potential revocation of corporate registrations. Under audit, repayment of claimed input tax credits attributed to zero-rated export supplies plus clawback interest is a standard consequence where the mandatory substantiation package cannot be produced.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions under the OECD Codes of Liberalisation. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, unpaid loan servicing, and capital repatriation are not subject to prior exchange-control approval, provided all applicable corporate, tax, and AML/KYC documentation is in place. No Canadian foreign-exchange restriction or central bank monetization requirement applies. Banking counterparties will independently apply FINTRAC wire-reporting thresholds and OFSI/OSFI prudential checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio in Canada. Contractors and corporate groups may retain export receipts in any freely convertible currency. For balance-of-payments statistics, Statistics Canada requires periodic reporting of cross-border payments, but no timeline or percentage-based conversion obligation exists. For the BIM/CAD engineering services exporter, invoicing in CAD or USD is purely commercial and unrestricted.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary policy); foreign exchange oversight exercised exclusively under the authority of the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada maintains no designated central bank foreign exchange control body under a fully liberalized OECD capital movement regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for regulated financial technology and authorized electronic money institutions such as Wise and similar cross-border neo-banks. Legal constraints: such firms must be registered as money services businesses (MSB) with FINTRAC, maintain telecom authorization or international money transfer authority, and are prohibited from offering deposit-taking products or issuing commercial credit. The primary treasury and operating account should remain with a Schedule I Canadian bank to minimize settlement and regulatory risk, while EMI rails can serve as collateral payment channels for smaller vendor payments and efficiency-oriented disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant injection from the non-resident parent requires a direct SWIFT MT103 wire from the parent's authorized bank to the Alberta entity's designated CAD operational account. The wire must reference purpose code 'FDI injection — paid-in share capital' under the Canadian Department of Finance balance-of-payments classification (category 'Direct Investment — Capital injected into a Canadian enterprise'); alternate bank credit notes must explicitly state 'corporate capital contribution' to prevent misclassification as factoring or commercial credit. Final verification includes matching the bank advice against a shareholder capital-contribution ledger, the board minutes approving the contribution, and confirmation of exemption or notification under the Investment Canada Act if the entity's asset value remains below the review threshold. For RMB or USD injections, the converting bank handles CAD conversion without need for central-bank approval.Operational payments such as local payroll, office lease, and Canadian software subscriptions use the domestic CCD (Canadian Credit Clearment) EFT rail or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) with CAD settlement in less than one business day. Cross-border settlements for engineering procurement, royalties, dividends, and parent-company subcontracting are cleared via the SWIFT network using MT103 for direct beneficiary transfers and MT202 COVER for correspondent-bank bypass structures. Commercial documentary credits (letters of credit) are reserved for high-value supply contracts of BIM software platforms or specialty graphic workstations; these LCs must conform to UCP600 and SWIFT MT700/MT750 with full transport, invoicing, insurance, and acceptance certificate compliance. Turnover: No statutory audit is automatically required for a private, non-distributing Alberta business corporation; the ABCA permits shareholders to waive auditor appointment by unanimous shareholder resolution. A compulsory audit applies where the corporation is a distributing or reporting issuer, where a unanimous shareholder agreement or the articles require an auditor, where the corporation has issued securities to the public, or where an order of the Alberta Court of King's Bench or a regulator requires an audit. Prudent lenders or clients may also require audited statements through private contract, but such a requirement is not a statutory corporate threshold.
