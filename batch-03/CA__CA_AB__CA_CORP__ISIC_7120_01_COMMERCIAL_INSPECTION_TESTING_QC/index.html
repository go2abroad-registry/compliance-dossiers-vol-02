Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); GST/HST and corporate tax administration portal: CRA My Business Account via https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; provincial corporate registry counterpart: Alberta Corporate Registry (https://www.albertaca.com).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta Business Corporation is incorporated under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) and receives an Alberta Corporate Access (ACA) number; it must then obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and program accounts from the CRA by filing Form RC1 or registering online through Business Registration Online (BRO). The BN plus the RT suffix constitutes the GST/HST account; separate RP (payroll), RM (import/export), and RC (corporation income tax) program accounts are opened where applicable. Non-resident corporations must also file Form RC1JA (Request for a Business Number and Registration of Non-Resident) and, if carrying on business in Alberta, an extra-provincial registration with Alberta Corporate Registry. Mandatory attachments include: certificate of incorporation or amalgamation, registered office address, summary of intended Canadian commercial activity (quality-control inspection and testing for export goods), anticipated first taxable supply date, expected Canadian annual gross revenue, and identification of directors and Canadian agent for service of process. CRA issues the BN instantly online and by mail within 10 business days; GST/HST registration is effective on the date chosen or the date of first supply, whichever is earlier.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian-incorporated business corporation is deemed resident in Canada under Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 250(4)(a) and taxed at the entity level under Part I on worldwide income; no pass-through or partnership look-through treatment is available for a corporation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian-incorporated business corporation is deemed resident in Canada under Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 250(4)(a) and taxed at the entity level under Part I on worldwide income; no pass-through or partnership look-through treatment is available for a corporation. Alberta provincial corporate income tax at 8% general rate; federal rate 15% (combined 23% general); a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) is eligible for the federal small business deduction (9%) and Alberta small business rate (2%) on the first C$500,000 of active business income, resulting in combined 11% small-business rate. Non-resident shareholders of the Alberta corporation are subject to Part XIII withholding tax on dividends (generally 25%, reduced to 5-15% under tax treaties). The foreign-parent corporation itself is generally not taxable in Canada on the active export-service income of its Canadian subsidiary except under the treaty permanent-establishment threshold.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or classification election exists under Canadian tax law; a Business Corporation is automatically a separate taxable entity. Applicable elections for this corporation include: (i) ITA s. 85 rollover election on the transfer of eligible property into the corporation at agreed tax values; (ii) ITA s. 87 amalgamation continuity election if the corporation is continued in an amalgamation; (iii) ITA s. 249 election for a non-calendar taxation year; (iv) GST/HST annual or 2-filing election options under ETA ss. 243-247; (v) the hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6) are mandatory and provide no elective relief. CCPC status is statutory, not elective, and is terminated automatically if the entity fails the qualifying test.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (other than zero-rated supplies) made by the entity in any single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once a non-resident or domestic corporation exceeds C$30,000, GST/HST registration and the 5% GST collection obligation become mandatory. Cross-border services to non-residents that are zero-rated do not count toward the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported tangible goods are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 1 where delivered to a non-resident and removed from Canada within 60 days. Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated under s. 7 of Part V unless performed in Canada in connection with real property in Canada or with goods situated in Canada at the time of performance. For an on-site factory pre-shipment QC and audit agency in Alberta, inspection and testing performed on goods physically present on an Alberta factory floor at the time of testing are excluded from zero-rating and attract 5% GST even when billed to a foreign buyer. Zero-rating is available where the goods are in Canada solely for the purpose of the QC or testing and are then exported (goods-in-transit or CRA policy), or where the service is performed at a foreign plant. The Canadian exporter of the inspected goods can zero-rate its goods sale and claim input tax credits for the QC service fee, provided the export documents are retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canadian Export Declaration, B13A format, or CADEX/EDI record) filed by the exporter or licensed customs broker
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing removal of the goods from Canada to a foreign consignee
  • Commercial Invoice and Certificate of Inspection endorsed by the QC agency and signed by the foreign buyer or principal
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or Wise transaction record evidencing settlement by the non-resident principal in foreign currency
  • Written inspection contract between the non-resident principal and the QC agency stating that the service is performed for the non-resident outside its Canadian business activity

No Canadian federal real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate exists; Alberta does not impose any e-invoicing obligation. The CRA mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE or CRA Business Gateway API) for registrants with annual taxable supplies above C$1,000,000; e-invoices are permissible, and the CRA Business Gateway API supports machine-to-machine submission. Standard tax invoices must contain the vendor's BN, invoice date, recipient name, description, consideration, and GST payable (ETA s. 224(3)). Quebec's B2B e-invoicing and QR-code mandate (effective September 2026) does not apply in Alberta.

Canada does not impose a specific 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation statute; the 180-day benchmark is retained by the compliance pipeline as an internal evidentiary cutoff for export substantiation. CRA audit practice requires documentary retention for at least six years from the end of the taxation year. Where zero-rating is improperly claimed and export proof is missing, CRA will reassess the supply as taxable at 5% and charge interest at the prescribed rate plus the late-remittance penalty (ETA s. 280: 5% of the unremitted amount plus 1% per month up to 12 months); false or deceptive statements triggering gross negligence attract an additional 50% penalty under ETA s. 285 and ITA s. 163(2). Fraudulent export schemes are criminal offences under ETA s. 330 and ITA s. 239, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years; civil clawbacks run retrospectively to the date of the original zero-rating claim, and input tax credits are correspondingly denied where the QC agency fails to substantiate its own commercial activity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no statutory exchange controls, conversion queues, approval requirements, or profit repatriation hurdles for dividends, service fees, capital returns, or liquidation proceeds. All cross-border transactions remain subject only to general AML, sanction screening, FATCA/CRS reporting, and OSFI/FINTRAC transaction-reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain revenue in any permissible currency, hold foreign-currency bank accounts at Canadian financial institutions, and settle invoices in Canadian dollars without restriction. Receipts at or above CAD 10,000, and any wire transfer forming part of a reportable cross-border transaction, must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary and systemic stability authority); financial-sector AML supervision by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada); banking conduct, solvency, and prudential regulation by OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium viability with material legal constraints. Wise (Wise Canada Inc.) and similar firms operate in Canada as FINTRAC-registered money services businesses, not as electronic money institutions as recognised in the EU/UK. Such fintechs cannot accept deposits, issue e-money, provide Canadian Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protection, or extend regulated bank credit. They are usable only for low-value cross-border supplier disbursements, minor FX conversion, and expense payments. Treasury-grade escrow, trade finance, and liquidity clearing must be held through a Schedule I bank.
