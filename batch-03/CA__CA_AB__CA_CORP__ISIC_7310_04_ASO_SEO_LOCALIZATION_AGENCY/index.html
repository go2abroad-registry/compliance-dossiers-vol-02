Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses.html). Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the Canada-Alberta Tax Collection Agreement; there is no separate Alberta provincial tax authority.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the business corporation federally (Corporations Canada) or provincially (Alberta Corporate Registry), generating an Alberta Corporate Access Number (CAN). Step 2: Obtain the CRA Business Number (BN) automatically at incorporation, or by filing Form RC1 for extraprovincial/non-resident entities. Step 3: Add the GST/HST program account (RC) and corporate income tax account (RT); payroll (RP) should be added if Canadian employees are hired. Step 4: Non-resident corporations without a permanent establishment may use CRA's Non-Resident Business Registration path, sign the required authorization via Represent a Client (RepID), and appoint an authorized agent with a Canadian service address for provincial service.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity-Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer under Income Tax Act s. 89(1): resident corporations are taxable on worldwide income at 15% federal Part I plus 8% Alberta corporate income tax, reduced to 2% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income for Canadian-controlled private corporations eligible for the small business deduction. A foreign-controlled subsidiary generally pays the combined 23% headline rate. Dividends paid by a taxable Canadian corporation are generally tax-deductible to corporate recipients; non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax of 25% (treaty-reduced), and a Canadian branch of a non-resident would be exposed to Part XIV branch tax of 25% (treaty-reduced). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity-Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer under Income Tax Act s. 89(1): resident corporations are taxable on worldwide income at 15% federal Part I plus 8% Alberta corporate income tax, reduced to 2% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income for Canadian-controlled private corporations eligible for the small business deduction. A foreign-controlled subsidiary generally pays the combined 23% headline rate. Dividends paid by a taxable Canadian corporation are generally tax-deductible to corporate recipients; non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax of 25% (treaty-reduced), and a Canadian branch of a non-resident would be exposed to Part XIV branch tax of 25% (treaty-reduced). No pass-through eligibility applies to a Business Corporation.

Available election forms

N/A - U.S.-style check-the-box elections do not exist in Canadian tax law; an incorporated entity is per se a distinct taxpayer. Relevant elections include: (i) s. 216 ITA net-basis election for non-resident rental income; (ii) s. 156 Excise Tax Act election to treat supplies between closely related corporations as made for nil consideration; (iii) GST/HST Quick Method election for smaller suppliers; (iv) s. 22 ITA election on transfers of accounts receivable; and (v) associated-corporation elections where applicable under s. 256 ITA. Hybrid mismatch assessments follow static rules in s. 18.4 ITA rather than elective mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small-supplier threshold tested over four consecutive calendar quarters (or a single calendar quarter for non-resident suppliers of specified digital/e-services under the July 2021 simplified GST/HST framework). Registration in Alberta is required once taxable global supplies to Canadian recipients exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Canada by the supplier qualify for 0% GST under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act. Services (including ASO/SEO and localization services) supplied to a non-resident customer qualify for zero-rating if the recipient is not a resident of Canada, the service is not performed in respect of real or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and the benefit is consumed wholly outside Canada. Zero-rated exporters claim full Input Tax Credits and must retain contemporaneous evidence that the customer is non-resident and that payment is received from outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canada International Trade Single Window) for goods; CBSA export report or record of export
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air waybill) evidencing physical export movement from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (wire transfer/remittance proof in foreign currency from the non-resident customer)
  • Commercial invoice and executed service agreement specifying zero-rated supply and foreign-use clause (for services supply)
  • Non-resident status evidence (foreign corporate registration, address, and/or tax residency certificate)

No continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate, no real-time fiscal clearance, and no digital stamping requirement in Canada or Alberta. GST/HST returns must be transmitted electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE (mandatory once annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1 million); e-invoicing remains voluntary/contract-driven. CRA risk-based audits cross-match GST/HST returns with CBSA export declaration data, corporate filings, and banking information.

CRA scrutinizes high-volume zero-rated billings to related non-residents, prolonged foreign receivables, and missing CBSA export evidence. Reassessment window is 4 years from the filing date, but no limitation period applies in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud (Excise Tax Act s. 298 limitation exceptions). Civil penalty for false statements: greater of CAD 250 and 25% of net tax misstatement (ETA s. 280(1)); refund clawback plus interest accrues from the original return date. Criminal evasion is a hybrid offence punishable by a fine of up to 250% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years on indictment. While no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline exists in Canadian law, the 180-day benchmark is the prudent evidentiary standard to preserve export zero-rating and ITC claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. There are no foreign-exchange licensing requirements, quantitative limits, or mandatory repatriation restrictions. Profit remittance is operationally unrestricted but triggers Part XIII withholding tax on dividends at the statutory rate of 25%, ordinarily reduced to 5%-15% under an applicable tax treaty. Compliance barriers are not exchange-control; they arise from FINTRAC EFT reporting at CAD 10,000 or above, T1135 foreign-property reporting for amounts above CAD 100,000, and bank-source-of-wealth requirements for large capital injections.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for repatriating or converting foreign currency receipts. A corporate exporter may retain, invest, or disburse export earnings freely in any currency. However, the exporter must maintain full traceability of contractual documents, customs declarations, invoices, and proof of service delivery for CBSA and CRA audit purposes, and all cross-border electronic funds transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC reporting by the financial intermediary.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority; there is no dedicated Canadian foreign exchange administration. Cross-border financial intelligence, AML/CTF supervision, and transaction report collection are vested in FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. OSFI oversees prudential bank conduct, while the Canada Revenue Agency enforces tax-related reporting and withholding for remittances.

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: High for disbursement-only treasury purposes and low-value operational flows; Medium for use as the primary corporate operating account. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound investor capital must be sent via SWIFT MT103 to the corporate operating or escrow account at a Canadian tier-1 bank. USD-denominated injections are cleared through the sending bank's New York correspondent (Fedwire or CHIPS) and then converted onshore at the receiving bank's spot rate; CAD-denominated injections settle real-time in Lynx. The beneficiary account must reference the full legal corporate name and Alberta registration number. Remittance information should state 'Shareholder Loan or Equity Capital Contribution under Canada Business Corporations Act'. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23.0% combined (Federal 15.0% + Alberta 8.0%). For qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) active business income within the federal small business limit (C$500,000), the combined rate is 11.0% (Federal 9.0% + Alberta 2.0%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by Canadian-resident corporations to non-residents under subsection 212(2) of the Income Tax Act. Treaty reductions apply only where the shareholder is a resident and beneficial owner under an applicable Canada double tax agreement; for example, the Canada-U.S. DTA provides 5% for qualifying corporate shareholders holding at least 10% of voting shares and 15% for other portfolio holdings.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 is due six months after the corporation's taxation year-end (e.g., December 31 year-end is due June 30). The balance-due date for tax liability is generally two months after year-end (three months for a qualifying CCPC with the small business limit in the prior taxation year).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary de minimis threshold for contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation; all material cross-border non-arm's-length transactions require support. A Form T106 information return becomes mandatory when the aggregate value of non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents in a taxation year is C$1,000,000 or more. The section 247(3) penalty bright-line is generally the lesser of C$5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue, while a transfer-pricing adjustment above that amount triggers a 10% penalty on the upward adjustment.

Economic substance scope: Alberta does not maintain a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing. Canada addresses synthetic substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (subsection 245(1) of the Income Tax Act), treaty residence rules (central management and control / place of effective management), controlled foreign corporation attribution, and CRA recharacterization of sham, agency, or conduit arrangements. For the ASO/SEO localization agency, genuine economic substance must be demonstrated through Canadian employees, contracts, performance monitoring, board decisions, and an active office; shell functions and mailbox corporates invite GAAR and treaty-benefit denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Late or Non-Filing of T2 / CRA Assessments

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalty of 5% of tax plus 1% per complete month up to 12 months; additional 10% penalty after a CRA demand; arbitrary assessment on estimated income under subsection 152(7); interest compounding daily; possible strike-off or cancellation of corporate registration under the Alberta Business Corporations Act for prolonged non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for corporate income tax, but subsection 227.1 of the Income Tax Act makes directors jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholding. A director who approves or acquiesces in a false T2 may face civil liability for losses suffered by the corporation, oppression claims under the Alberta Business Corporations Act, and in fraud cases the corporate veil can be pierced.
Tax Evasion / Gross Negligence / False Return

Statutory sanction: Civil gross negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) of the Income Tax Act of 50% of the understated tax; criminal tax evasion under subsection 239(1) punishable by a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to two years; CRA may also seize bank accounts and garnish trade receivables.

Director personal liability: Directs sign officers and controlling shareholders who knowingly sign false returns are personally prosecutable for tax evasion; courts will lift the corporate veil where fraud or sham is established; personal assets, car and real estate can be subject to civil execution, and an active criminal prosecution can trigger travel restrictions or surrender of passports.
Transfer-Pricing Mismatch / Offshore Cost-Shifting

Statutory sanction: CRA may recharacterize under subsection 247(2) and impose the 10% transfer-pricing penalty under subsection 247(3) when the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; interest and gross-up apply; separate penalties may be assessed for failure to file T106 information returns.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly create and approve documentation that masks a non-arm's-length price may be assessed for participating in false statements. If CRA disregards the foreign entity as a sham agent rather than a true service provider, the Canadian entity's profits may be attributed to the foreign parent or vice versa, collapsing the corporate shield and exposing controlling directors to collection.
Unreported Non-Resident Withholding (Dividend / Interest / Royalty)

Statutory sanction: The Canadian payer remains liable for the entire unwithheld Part XIII tax, plus a penalty of 10% of the amount not withheld or remitted and daily compound interest. The CRA may file a lien on corporate property and notify the corporate shareholders of the enforcement action.

Director personal liability: Subsection 227.1 creates full personal joint and several liability of directors and trustees for unremitted Part XIII withholding, even after the corporation is dissolved. The CRA can assess directors directly, seize personal assets, and in egregious deliberate cases pursue criminal charges for failure to remit.
Shell Corporation / Economic-Substance Deficit / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The CRA will invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to deny any tax benefit and may recharacterize the entity as an agent or conduit. Treaty relief is denied if the claimant is not a bona fide resident or beneficial owner; corporate registration may be revoked under Alberta legislation; and the foreign parent may be reassessed as the actual Canadian business operator.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham corporation that merely executes instructions from the foreign parent can be personally assessed for taxes of the actual trader where agency or sham finding is made; deliberate creation of an artificial transactional structure can support criminal liability under section 239, travel bans in pending prosecutions, and seizure of personal assets through civil recovery.
