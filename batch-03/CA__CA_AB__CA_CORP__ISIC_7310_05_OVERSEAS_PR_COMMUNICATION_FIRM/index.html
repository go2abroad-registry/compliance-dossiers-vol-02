Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is mandated under the ABCA for a business corporation; shares may be issued for nominal or non-par consideration without an escrow or capital deposit requirement. A corporation must maintain records of issued share capital but no statutory escrow account is required for a non-licenced public relations services company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required under the Alberta Fire Code, including a municipal inspection for means of egress, extinguishers, and emergency lighting; a building occupancy permit or leasehold improvement clearance may be required by the municipality; workplace health and safety compliance is mandated under the Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) Act and Code, including hazard assessments and training; Workers' Compensation Board – Alberta registration is mandatory once employee wages are paid; structural load inspection is only required for signage, mezzanines, or substantial office alterations.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is mandated under the ABCA for a business corporation; shares may be issued for nominal or non-par consideration without an escrow or capital deposit requirement. A corporation must maintain records of issued share capital but no statutory escrow account is required for a non-licenced public relations services company.

Category: No provincial operating or manufacturing permit is required; a 'Municipal General Business Licence' may be required for any physical office, production studio, or event-support premises within Alberta.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; the CRA is also the authority responsible for administering Alberta's collection of federal and provincial corporate income tax under the Alberta Corporate Tax Collection Agreement. Primary registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), and taxpayer self-service portal: CRA My Business Account.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta business corporation: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) through Corporations Canada or the Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES), obtaining a certificate of incorporation; (2) register with the CRA for the statutory Business Number (BN) and applicable program accounts using Form RC1, either online through Business Registration Online or by mail; the CRA will issue a 15-digit BN, with the RT account identifier used for GST/HST obligations; (3) where the corporation carries on business in Alberta, complete any applicable extra-provincial registration obligations and appoint a registered office and service address under Alberta law; (4) for non-resident shareholders, no minimum local ownership is required, but directors, residence and annual return requirements must be verified under the applicable corporate statute. A BN and GST/HST account are usually issued immediately for online registration, with the effective GST/HST registration date set according to the first taxable supply.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the corporation is a separate legal and taxable taxpayer under the Income Tax Act, is not eligible for pass-through treatment, and there is no check-the-box election for Canadian business corporations. Resident Canadian corporations are taxable on worldwide income, while a non-resident corporation is taxable only on Canadian-source business income, employment income, certain Canadian-source investment income, and gains from dispositions of taxable Canadian property. Alberta has no general tax-exempt zone for this commercial scope, and the federal GST/HST applies at the entity level. — For a foreign-owned Alberta business corporation: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) through Corporations Canada or the Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES), obtaining a certificate of incorporation; (2) register with the CRA for the statutory Business Number (BN) and applicable program accounts using Form RC1, either online through Business Registration Online or by mail; the CRA will issue a 15-digit BN, with the RT account identifier used for GST/HST obligations; (3) where the corporation carries on business in Alberta, complete any applicable extra-provincial registration obligations and appoint a registered office and service address under Alberta law; (4) for non-resident shareholders, no minimum local ownership is required, but directors, residence and annual return requirements must be verified under the applicable corporate statute. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the corporation is a separate legal and taxable taxpayer under the Income Tax Act, is not eligible for pass-through treatment, and there is no check-the-box election for Canadian business corporations. Resident Canadian corporations are taxable on worldwide income, while a non-resident corporation is taxable only on Canadian-source business income, employment income, certain Canadian-source investment income, and gains from dispositions of taxable Canadian property. Alberta has no general tax-exempt zone for this commercial scope, and the federal GST/HST applies at the entity level.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) a functional currency election under subsection 261(1) permitting eligible corporations to compute Canadian tax liabilities in a qualifying foreign currency; (ii) the section 216 election permitting a non-resident corporation to compute tax on Canadian-source rental income on a net basis as though a resident; (iii) the GST/HST election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for closely related registrants to treat certain inter-corporate supplies as made for no consideration and outside the scope of GST/HST; and (iv) hybrid mismatch and anti-avoidance rules in sections 18.4 and 18.5 of the Income Tax Act, which constrain deductions in cross-border arrangements involving inconsistent entity classification between Canada and another jurisdiction such as the United States.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD — a person generally becomes required to register for GST/HST when taxable supplies, including worldwide taxable supplies, exceed $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident corporation carrying on a taxable supply business in Canada should assess its Canadian GST/HST nexus separately and may be required to register despite an absence of provincial sales tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply may be zero-rated under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if it qualifies as an export of tangible personal property or as a service supplied to a non-resident person. For an overseas public relations and communications firm, services supplied to non-resident clients, such as international media relations and crisis communications consulting, are generally eligible for zero-rating where the supply is made to a person who is not resident in Canada, the service is not supplied to an individual present in Canada at the time the service is performed, and the service is not performed in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The registrant must retain contemporaneous evidence demonstrating the non-resident status of the customer and the actual provision of the service, including the contract, service scope, power point of performance, and delivery of media or communications deliverables outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) for goods exported from Canada, or the underlying service contract, engagement letter, and detailed final invoice evidencing the supply to a non-resident client for the PR and communications services
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill for goods, or equivalent electronic delivery records and proof of service performance outside Canada for services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT payment confirmation, bank credit advice, or ACH/Wire transfer evidence demonstrating actual receipt of payment from the non-resident client

Canada has no mandatory real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance regime. The CRA does not operate a digital stamping platform, and invoices may be issued in paper, PDF, or standard electronic formats, provided they contain the prescribed GST/HST invoice information: supplier name, Business Number or GST/HST registration number, date of invoice, description of supply, consideration, and the amount of GST/HST charged or a clear statement that the supply is tax-inclusive. Registrants with annual taxable revenue exceeding $1 million CAD are required to file GST/HST returns electronically using CRA GST/HST NETFILE or a registered EDI service.

The CRA applies heightened audit scrutiny to zero-rated export transactions under GST/HST Memorandum 4-4-13. If the statutory documentary evidence is not maintained or the non-resident supply characterization is not substantiated at the time of audit, the CRA may reassess the supply as taxable at 5%, reverse input tax credits, impose arrears interest, and apply penalties. False statements or omission of material information attract penalties under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act, generally equal to the greater of C$250 and 25% of the net tax assessed or the refund claimed. Wilful evasion or gross negligence may lead to prosecution, additional fines of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, and criminal sanctions. Retrospective clawbbacks are possible because the CRA may reassess beyond the normal four-year limitation period where a registrant has made a misrepresentation, contrived a false export transaction, or failed to maintain adequate books and records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII obligations. No Foreign Exchange Control Act, no exchange licensing regime, and no prior regulatory approval applies to inbound capital injections, profit repatriation (dividends, interest, royalties, management fees), or loan servicing. Dividends and other outbound payments are subject solely to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (default 25%, treaty-reduced to 5%/15% under the Canada-US and other tax treaties) and to applicable transfer-pricing/arm's-length documentation. No minimum holding period, no repatriation permit, and no net open position requirement is imposed on corporations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. A resident exporter may retain foreign currency receipts in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency bank accounts, convert them freely, or use them for offshore operating disbursements. The only quantitative reporting obligation is statistical: direct investment and balance-of-payments transactions above threshold must be reported to Statistics Canada (via the International Transactions Reporting System, BERD, and Direct Investment forms), but these reports entail no surrender, conversion, or prior-approval duty.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); payments infrastructure operated by Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low for offshore digital EMIs. A foreign electronic money institution (EMI) may lawfully service a Canadian corporate client only if it registers as a money service business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) framework, registers as a retail payment service provider with the Bank of Canada. In practice, EMIs such as Wise or Airwallex can be used as supplementary low-value collection or disbursement conduits, but they commonly rely on Canadian chartered-bank sponsorship and may impose material balance caps, velocity limits, and narrower transaction-purpose tolerance. For a capital escrow and FX execution mandate, a Schedule I chartered-bank primary account remains the only fully reliable clearing and treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital injections are routed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 via the remitting bank's correspondent into the Alberta operating account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. No Bank of Canada or OSFI approval is required; the remittance information field (MT103 /70) must clearly state 'capital contribution' or 'shareholder loan' for audit trail integrity. For equity contributions the bank may request a board resolution and the escrow ledger reconciliation; for shareholder loans, a signed loan agreement and interest terms are expected to satisfy transfer-pricing substance. If the injection constitutes inward foreign direct investment exceeding the Statistics Canada reporting threshold, a Direct Investment Report or BERD return must be filed; otherwise the transaction proceeds without prior authorization or conversion requirement.Canadian-dollar trade settlements and retainer fees clear via Payments Canada retail and high-value rails: the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for EFT/ACH batches and the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for high-value real-time gross settlement. Cross-border USD, EUR, and GBP disbursements execute via SWIFT MT103 messages through the sponsoring bank's correspondent network (Fedwire/CHIPS for USD, T2 for EUR, CHAPS for GBP). Larger procurement or production settlements may be structured with commercial letters of credit issued in accordance with ICC UCP 600 and settled against documentary compliance; bank payment obligations and supply-chain finance facilities further optimize working capital. Real-time rail migration to the Real-Time Rail (RTR) is monitored but does not alter SWIFT/MT103 trade-clearing requirements.Deliverable FX forwards between CAD and USD, EUR, GBP, and other freely convertible currencies, executed ISDA/CSA-documented with Schedule I bank counterparties (typically 1–12 month tenors).Vanilla FX options, call/put spreads, and zero-cost collars determined by the treasury hedging policy, with board-approved delegation and no speculative uncovered positions.Cross-currency swaps (CAD/USD and CAD/GBP) under ISDA Master Agreements, used to convert fixed or floating interest liabilities without disturbing the underlying trade payables.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market or restricted currencies where CAD deliverability is unavailable; NDFs are not used for CAD/USD or other G7 pairs because full deliverability exists.Currency swaps with settlement against trade-clearing banks under collateralized CSA arrangements, limited to underlying retainer and production-cycle exposures verified by forecast cash-flow statements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% on the first $500,000 of active business income (small business rate); 38% on passive income and active business income exceeding $500,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, subject to reduction under applicable income tax treaties

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: $500,000 CAD for local file documentation; $50 million CAD for master file documentation

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations do not apply in Canada; however, General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and transfer pricing rules require transactions to have economic substance and arm's length pricing

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction; fines up to $1,000,000; imprisonment up to 5 years; restitution orders

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for willful evasion; potential criminal charges, fines, and disqualification from office
Failure to File or Late Filing

Statutory sanction: Penalty of $100 per day, capped at $2,500; additional penalties for continued default

Director personal liability: No direct personal liability for filing, but directors may face personal liability for unpaid source deductions or other taxes
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10-50% of the transfer pricing adjustment; possible reassessment and interest

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable if they directed or acquiesced in the non-compliance
Dividend Withholding Tax Non-Remittance

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to the amount of tax not remitted; interest on unpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and penalties if they failed to comply with remittance obligations
