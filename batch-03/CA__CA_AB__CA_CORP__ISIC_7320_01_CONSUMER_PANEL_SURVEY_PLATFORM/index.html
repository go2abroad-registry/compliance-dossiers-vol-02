Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no mandatory escrow deposit under the ABCA. Incorporators may issue common shares for nominal consideration (e.g., CAD $1.00). Directors must fix the consideration for shares without par value and ensure shares are fully paid before issuance (s. 25(3)); par-value shares cannot be issued for less than par value. No escrow account or capital bank confirmation is required.

Funding & unlock path: No fire-safety or structural certification applies to a remote-first digital company. If a physical office is occupied, the building must hold an occupancy permit under the Alberta Fire Code (1997) with acceptable fire inspections, the leasehold must meet structural load limits of the Alberta Building Code, and the employer must complete a hazard identification and risk assessment under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (SA 2020, c O-2.2), provide first-aid equipment per the OHS Code, and ensure electrical safety of IT equipment. No dangerous-goods permit applies to office use.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no mandatory escrow deposit under the ABCA. Incorporators may issue common shares for nominal consideration (e.g., CAD $1.00). Directors must fix the consideration for shares without par value and ensure shares are fully paid before issuance (s. 25(3)); par-value shares cannot be issued for less than par value. No escrow account or capital bank confirmation is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; national portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Alberta corporate income tax and federal GST/HST are both administered by the CRA under a federal-provincial tax administration agreement. Registration and account administration are performed through CRA's My Business Account portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian business corporation first incorporates under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Alberta Business Corporations Act and obtains a federal incorporation number or Alberta corporate access number. The corporation then applies to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or through the CRA online registration service, selecting the Corporation Income Tax program account and the GST/HST program account. The BN is the statutory tax identification number; the GST/HST registration number is the BN followed by the RT program-account suffix (e.g., BN/RR0001). Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada may apply directly to the CRA for a BN and GST/HST registration without domestic incorporation; foreign entities must provide prescribed corporate authorization documents and a Canadian representative where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Canadian business corporation first incorporates under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Alberta Business Corporations Act and obtains a federal incorporation number or Alberta corporate access number. The corporation then applies to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or through the CRA online registration service, selecting the Corporation Income Tax program account and the GST/HST program account. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box or entity classification election for income tax purposes. Business corporations are automatically taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada). For GST/HST purposes, closely related corporations may file an election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act to treat otherwise taxable supplies between them as not being a supply, which can reduce cash-flow GST/HST burden. For income tax purposes, associated Canadian-controlled private corporations may file an agreement under subsection 256(2) of the Income Tax Act to allocate the business limit for the small business deduction. No elective hybrid-mismatch or consolidated-fiscal-unity regime exists; Canadian rules follow the OECD hybrid mismatch recommendations and the CRA may apply domestic de minimis or anti-hybrid provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada during the current calendar quarter and the preceding four calendar quarters; below this threshold an unregistered person may be a small supplier, except that non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies may be required to register if the threshold is exceeded. Voluntary registration is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for supplies of goods exported from Canada if the supplier retains evidence of physical export to a foreign destination. For a consumer panel and market research platform, supplies of advisory, consulting, and research services provided to non-resident persons may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 of the Excise Tax Act, provided the non-resident recipient is not registered for GST/HST and the service is not supplied for consumption or use in Canada. Personal/virtual payment platform services and subscriptions must be separated from any taxable Canadian supply to maintain zero-rating eligibility.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or Canada Export Reporting System declaration) for goods physically exported
  • Commercial invoice describing the exported goods or exported service
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment out of Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or equivalent bank payment record showing receipt of funds in foreign currency from the non-resident customer
  • Contractual service agreement and delivery logs documenting the offshore supply for market research services

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping for e-invoices. The CRA requires that GST/HST registrants issue invoices containing prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act, retain electronic and paper records, and make records available on demand. B2B e-invoicing may be transmitted via EDI, XML, or PDF through commercial networks, but there is no government pre-clearance API or e-invoice validation stamping requirement.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST returns within the normal four-year limitation period, and may exceed that period where there is misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect. If export zero-rating is claimed without sufficient documentary proof, the CRA will disallow the zero-rating, assess GST/HST at 5% on the full value of the supply, charge compound daily interest, and apply penalties. Gross negligence penalties may be imposed at 50% of the tax owing under the Excise Tax Act, and criminal prosecution can result in fines and imprisonment for wilful evasion or fraudulent export claims. Retrospective clawbacks can reach the entire audited period plus penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally require approval to receive or remit foreign currency, repatriate profits, make intercompany payments, or return capital. Banks may require transaction-purpose information, beneficial-owner identification, source-of-funds evidence, sanctions screening, and tax documentation. Profit distributions remain subject to corporate-law solvency requirements, applicable Canadian withholding tax, treaty eligibility, transfer-pricing rules, thin-capitalization rules, interest-limitation rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, and any contractual restrictions. Dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents are generally subject to Canadian withholding tax, commonly reduced under an applicable tax treaty. Interest, royalties, management fees, and other cross-border charges require separate withholding-tax and transfer-pricing analysis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general Canadian statutory requirement exists to surrender export proceeds, convert them into Canadian dollars, or deposit them within a prescribed period. Export receipts may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the corporation's discretion, subject to the account agreement and applicable bank controls. Ordinary market-research and SaaS exports do not have a mandatory conversion ratio or universal export-receipt deadline. The corporation must retain commercial records, invoices, contracts, and payment evidence and satisfy applicable tax, customs, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, and statistical-reporting obligations. Banks may impose internal documentation or monitoring requirements that are contractual rather than statutory.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no separate foreign-exchange administration imposing general exchange controls. Prudential and payment-system oversight may also involve the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, the Department of Finance Canada, and the Canada Revenue Agency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Maintain a subscription agreement, shareholder or director resolution, capitalization table, investor identification, source-of-funds evidence, and wire confirmation. Do not describe a loan as equity or an operating receipt as capital. Reconcile the bank statement to the general ledger and preserve records for tax, audit, AML, and transfer-pricing purposes. Canadian anti-money-laundering registration, licensing, or registration-exemption analysis must be confirmed for the relevant service and provider.
