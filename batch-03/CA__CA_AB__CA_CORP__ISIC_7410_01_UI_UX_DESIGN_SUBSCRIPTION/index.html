Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration via My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Alberta Corporate Registry and Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) handle provincial registration, with corporate income tax administered by CRA under a federal-provincial collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Canadian Business Corporation carrying on business in Alberta: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the Alberta Business Corporations Act; (2) obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA, which functions as the corporate TIN and GST/HST registration identifier; (3) register online via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or submit Form RC-1 Request for a Business Number and CRA Program Accounts; (4) open a GDPA/Corporate Income Tax program account (corporation is automatically registered upon incorporation for federal corporate taxes); (5) open a GST/HST program account if annual Canadian taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, or voluntarily if earlier; (6) if the corporation is a non-resident or has offshore directors/control, file Form RC-4649 (Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts for a Non-Resident) and appoint a Canadian representative for service of documents if required; (7) register with the Alberta Corporate Registry for the provincial corporate registry number and confirm the CRA account covers Alberta corporate income tax remittance under the collection agreement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxable person under Part I of the Income Tax Act. It is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income if resident in Canada (central management and control test under common law, deemed residence under s. 250). It has no pass-through eligibility; no S-corp or check-the-box equivalent exists. Territorial scope: residents taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations taxed on Canadian-source business income via Part I and Part XIV branch tax.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity election. Key statutory elections relevant to a design services corporation: (i) GST/HST Quick Method election for qualifying small suppliers (s. 227 ETA); (ii) s. 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of property on incorporation; (iii) for non-resident corporations providing services in Canada, s. 216 election (rental income) or s. 219 election (branch tax calculation) may apply; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under ss. 18.4 and 18.5 of the Income Tax Act apply automatically with no election, preventing deduction/inclusion mismatches. No statutory election is available to change the entity-level tax classification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

UI/UX design subscription services supplied to a non-resident recipient are zero-rated under s. 7(1) of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (service supplied to a non-resident) provided the supply is not 'in connection with' real property in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or a service to be performed in whole or in part in Canada. For digital intangible supplies, s. 142.2(3)(b) deems a supply made outside Canada if the recipient is a non-resident and is not registered for GST/HST, and the supply is not received in Canada. Eligibility criteria: (i) recipient is non-resident and not a Canadian resident; (ii) recipient is not registered for GST/HST; (iii) the design subscription is delivered remotely via digital channels and is not attributable to Canadian situs; (iv) contractual terms expressly provide for supply outside Canada; (v) payment is received via cross-border rails (e.g., Stripe Billing or Wise) but this is not a statutory condition — zero-rating is substantiated by documentary evidence.

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance regime. GST/HST invoices issued by a registrant must meet the information requirements of s. 3 of the Information Requirements Regulations (ETA) (e.g., supplier name, BN/GST registration number, date of invoice, total amount of tax charged, description of supply) for taxable supplies over CAD 30. There is no CRA API stamping or digital clearance requirement. Voluntary pan-Canadian e-invoicing based on the PEPPOL standard is supported by the CRA for B2B exchanges. For exports of physical goods, customs clearance is handled through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and the Canadian International Trade Single Window, but no such clearance applies to digital services.

CRA may reassess GST/HST within a 4-year normal period under s. 298(8) ETA (and income tax within 3 years under s. 152(4) ITA); both periods extend to 10 years in cases of misrepresentation or wilful default. If zero-rating is denied on audit, CRA assesses the taxpayer for uncollected 5% GST plus compound interest under s. 280 ETA, and imposes a gross-negligence penalty under s. 327.1(2) of 25% of the amount by which the tax self-assessed is understated, or 50% under s. 327.1(1) for knowingly making a false statement (i.e., fraudulent zero-rating). Input tax credits claimed on related expenses may be clawed back. Criminal prosecution is possible under s. 326 ETA and s. 239 ITA. Clawback also triggers a requirement to file amended returns and remit the full amount of forgone export tax, including on a retroactive basis, subject to the statutory reassessment windows.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency with no exchange control regime under the Currency Act, Bank of Canada Act, or the Foreign Exchange Control Act (proclaimed inoperative since 1951). Current-account transactions (including cross-border subscription fees, dividends, and service fees) are freely remittable without Bank of Canada approval. Capital-account transfers are likewise unrestricted, subject to Canada's Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), Canadian Economic Sanctions Regulations, and the United Nations Act implementing UN Security Council resolutions. Profit remittance hurdles under the Income Tax Act are limited to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax obligations (typically 25% of dividends unless reduced by treaty, e.g., under the Canada-US treaty to 15% or 5% for significant shareholdings).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio applies to export proceeds under Canadian law. Exporters, including digital service providers and design-subscription entities, may maintain foreign-currency bank accounts either in Canada or abroad. Reporting obligations arise solely under the PCMLTFA: an entity deemed a reporting entity under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Regulations (PCMLTFR) must report a large virtual currency transaction (CAD 10,000 or more) and suspicious transactions, and FINTRAC EFT reports apply to international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. The CAD/CAD functional currency rules under the Income Tax Act (ITA section 261) permit Canadian corporations to compute income in Canadian dollars, with foreign-currency exchange gains and losses triggered upon settlement through the ITA section 39(1) rules; no requirement exists to convert export proceeds to CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High viability for treasury optimization, but only as a supplementary non-bank payment rail, not as the primary settlement bank. Wise (formerly TransferWise) holds appropriate licenses in multiple jurisdictions and operates in Canada through regulated money-services business channels; legal constraints require an entity using Wise to remain compliant with Canadian AML/KYC obligations and to ensure that the EMI does not hold the corporate account in a manner that constitutes deposit-taking without a Canadian banking license. For an Alberta corporation, onboarding with foreign digital banks (e.g., Mercury, Airwallex, or Payoneer) is legally permissible if the foreign financial institution is not soliciting deposits from Canadians; the entity must maintain a genuine Canadian domestic bank account for tax remittance (CRA payroll, GST/HST, corporate tax) and for FINTRAC reporting. The blue-sky assessment is: (1) Wise as inbound/outbound USD/CAD clearing layer is strongly viable; (2) foreign EMIs cannot issue payment accounts denominated in CAD as a licensed bank in Canada; (3) the CA_CORP should structure the treasury stack as Alberta-chartered entity bank account (primary) plus Wise business account (cross-border USD/GBP/EUR) and Stripe as merchant acquiring rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an Alberta business corporation, the preferred loan/traffic route for non-resident shareholder capital injection is a direct SWIFT credit transfer in USD or CAD to the corporation's Canadian-dollar operating account maintained with a Schedule I bank. The wire/transfer instructions must include: (1) the corporate legal name exactly matching the bank account and CRA records; (2) the account number and the bank's SWIFT/BIC code; (3) a proper purpose/remittance code. Canada has no prescribed statutory purpose-code list akin to China's State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), but the bank will record the incoming EFT as either 'Capital Contribution' or 'Director's Loan/Shareholder Subordinated Loan'. RBC/TD/BMO input forms typically accept 12-character purpose descriptors. To avoid mandatory FINTRAC international EFT reporting thresholds, inbound wires over CAD $10,000 trigger an EFT report; the entity should not split transactions to avoid reporting. The shareholder contribution should be documented with a board resolution referencing ABCA; if structured as a shareholder loan, the loan agreement must contain market-rate interest (currently CRA prescribed interest rates in effect) and written terms to avoid a taxable benefit/deemed dividend. Inbound FDI capital does not require prior approval from Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) unless the transaction is an acquisition of control of a Canadian business above the reviewed threshold (not applicable to capital injection into a newly incorporated Alberta design company). All capital must be recorded via the corporation's general ledger and disclosed in its Canadian Financial Statements. Wire purpose codes to use: 'Capital Investment', 'Injection of Equity', or 'Shareholder Loan Financing'. If the capital injection exceeds CAD $2,000,000 in a 12-month period, the entity should evaluate the need to register with Statistics Canada for a Canadian Direct Investment (CDI) survey; no exchange-control permit is required.For payment of overseas subcontractors, tool subscriptions, or disbursement of trade settlements, the clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) from the Canadian corporate bank account to a foreign beneficiary bank; (2) SWIFT MT940/MT950 as the account statement to reconcile settlements; (3) commercial letters of credit are generally not required for design-subscription service payments because there is no shipment of merchandise; however, a standby letter of credit (SBLC) may be used in the context of a large client services contract as a payment-security instrument; (4) low-value cross-border operational payments may instead use Wise/Stripe Payouts/ACH wire aggregation, but the underlying economic substance remains an MT103-equivalent rail. The recipient must be security-screened against consolidated sanctions lists (OSFI/DFATD, OFAC) by the issuing bank before payout. The entity must ensure that the outbound payment purpose field clearly reflects a services payment (e.g., 'UI/UX design subcontracting services', 'software licensing fee', 'royalty payment') and is supported by an invoice citing the applicable subcontract agreement. Canadian withholding tax on cross-border service fees to non-residents: fees for services rendered entirely outside Canada are generally not subject to Part XIII; fees for services rendered in Canada may trigger Part XIII deemed payments or Part I branch circumstances; an Alberta entity must obtain non-resident vendor IDs and apply to CRA for relief where appropriate. On card-based disbursements, Stripe Billing (acting as Merchant of Record) is the primary rail for consumer subscriptions; Wise is the primary rail for direct-to-foreign-object bank account settlement; the corporate bank account remains the termination point for all CRA remitances.Vanilla CAD/USD FX Forward contract (executed through a Schedule I Canadian bank or swap dealer; hedges revenue repatriation on a fixed-date basis; requires an underlying real exposure and no speculation-only rule, but the bank will request the entity to sign an ISDA Master Agreement or a Canadian FX Master Agreement for forward contracts).Non-deliverable forward (NDF) referencing an inconvertible counterparty currency (e.g., CAD/NGN or CAD/ARS) for contract settlements against high-risk emerging-market clients; permitted if the Canadian corporate bank's counterparty credit department can operate an NDF via a non-resident nexus; the payout is settled in CAD net of differential).Cross-currency swap (CAD/USD) to manage long-dated multi-year subscription revenue in USD with CAD operating expenses; permissible under the Bank Act and OSFI's Derivatives Sound Practices if the counterparty is a regulated Canadian financial institution or licensed derivatives organization; the mandatory clearing obligation under IIROC (now CIRO) rules may apply to standardized interest-rate swaps, but FX cross-currency swaps are exempt from central clearing if not entered via a multilateral trading facility).Plain vanilla FX swap (swap one currency for another outright on a spot/forward basis) permissible for cash-flow timing; must be economically linked to underlying commercial payments.Currency options (European-style CAD/USD put/call) permitted with CRA hedge-accounting treatment; requires IFRS hedge accounting under IAS 39 to defer mark-to-market P&L volatility, supported by formal hedge designation documentation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High viability for treasury optimization, but only as a supplementary non-bank payment rail, not as the primary settlement bank. Wise (formerly TransferWise) holds appropriate licenses in multiple jurisdictions and operates in Canada through regulated money-services business channels; legal constraints require an entity using Wise to remain compliant with Canadian AML/KYC obligations and to ensure that the EMI does not hold the corporate account in a manner that constitutes deposit-taking without a Canadian banking license. For an Alberta corporation, onboarding with foreign digital banks (e.g., Mercury, Airwallex, or Payoneer) is legally permissible if the foreign financial institution is not soliciting deposits from Canadians; the entity must maintain a genuine Canadian domestic bank account for tax remittance (CRA payroll, GST/HST, corporate tax) and for FINTRAC reporting. The blue-sky assessment is: (1) Wise as inbound/outbound USD/CAD clearing layer is strongly viable; (2) foreign EMIs cannot issue payment accounts denominated in CAD as a licensed bank in Canada; (3) the CA_CORP should structure the treasury stack as Alberta-chartered entity bank account (primary) plus Wise business account (cross-border USD/GBP/EUR) and Stripe as merchant acquiring rail.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined general Canada/Alberta corporate income tax rate of 23% (Federal 15% + Alberta 8%); combined reduced rate of 11% applies to active business income up to the C$500,000 federal and Alberta small business deduction thresholds for Canadian-controlled private corporations (9% federal + 2% Alberta).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act, unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the corporation's taxation year-end. Final balance of tax payable is generally due within 2 months after the year-end for non-CCPCs and within 3 months after the year-end for CCPCs eligible for the federal small business deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation where the aggregate annual fair market value of transactions involving non-resident related persons exceeds C$1,000,000. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue is at least C$750,000,000. The documentation must include an accurate economic analysis and support the arm's-length outcome.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulation styled on Gulf-cooperation special-purpose regimes, but the Income Tax Act contains a general anti-avoidance rule (section 245) and CRA uses `benefits test`, `business purpose`, `sham`, and `artificial transactions` doctrines. A Canadian corporation engaged in subscription design services must show real employees, local management, physical presence in Alberta, and actual business decision-making. A shell without Canadian substance may be denied treaty benefits under the OECD BEPS principal-purpose test and be recognized as resident in a foreign jurisdiction, exposing directors to liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful False Statements

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction, imprisonment of up to 2 years plus fines; on indictment, imprisonment of up to 5 years; CFA-style penalties under Income Tax Act section 162/163: false statements or omissions penalty equal to the greater of C$100 and 50% of the understated tax; additional gross negligence penalties can reach 100% of unpaid tax. Interest compounds daily.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or authorize tax evasion may be criminally prosecuted personally. Civil restitution orders, disclosure orders, freezing orders, and travel bans are obtainable. Court-approved piercings of the corporate veil allow CRA and civil claimants to recover from personal assets of directors and officers where the corporation is a facade or was operated in disregard of revenue obligations.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment adding unreported income or deductible disallowance plus interest; transfer-pricing penalty under section 247(3) equal to 10% of the net adjustment, subject to a minimum penalty of C$5,000 in certain cases. Offensive to arm's-length standard triggers CRA full audit and publication under anti-avoidance rulings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed for unremitted withholding tax and may be named in penalty assessments for knowingly participating in the deficiency. Where agreements are found to be shams, courts may lift the corporate veil to impose personal liability for adjusted tax, penalties, and legal costs.
Shell Company / Artificial Residency / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRAA re-sources income under GAAR (section 245) and the OECD principal-purpose test; technical amendments can deny treaty relief and apply full 25% Part XIII WHT to distributions; civil penalties and interest apply to all understated tax. CRA may also strike the corporation from the register for failure to file or misrepresenting residency.

Director personal liability: Directors of an artificial-residency shop may be personally taxed where they are the real beneficiaries or where the corporate form is used to evade taxes; Canadian courts may impose unjust-enrichment restitution, contempt travel bans, and imprisonment for organized tax evasion.
Withholding Tax / Remittance Failure / AML Interface

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax liability is imposed on the Canadian payer, not the non-resident recipient; penalties under subsection 227(1) and 227(2) equal 10% of the unremitted amount plus daily interest. Failure to report FINTRAC transactions carries penalties of C$500 and additional sanctions under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Repeated failures trigger criminal prosecution and gross-negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of the non-resident-owned corporation are jointly and severally liable under Income Tax Act section 227.1 for unremitted employee deductions and Part XIII tax up to the amounts withheld. Travel bans, personal fines, and imprisonment are possible when the director knowingly allowed a scheme to strip profits offshore without tax compliance.
