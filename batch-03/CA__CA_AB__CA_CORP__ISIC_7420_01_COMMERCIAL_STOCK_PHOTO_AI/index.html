Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online portal and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign (non-resident) corporation carrying on business in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with an 'RT' GST/HST program account: (1) determine whether taxable supplies in Canada exceed the C$30,000 registration threshold in the trailing four calendar quarters; (2) if the threshold is exceeded, register mandatorily via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number); (3) for income tax purposes, a non-resident corporation deriving Canadian-source business income or disposing of taxable Canadian property must also file a T2 corporation income tax return using its BN; (4) CRA assigns a single BN used concurrently for GST/HST, corporate income tax, and Alberta corporate income tax (Alberta administers its provincial corporate tax through the same T2 return via the CRA); (5) if the non-resident carries on business in Alberta, no separate Alberta BN is required, but the corporation must notify the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration through the T2 provincial schedule. Service-only digital businesses may voluntarily register for GST/HST before the threshold to recover input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a 'check-the-box' regime; a Business Corporation (CA_CORP) is a separate legal person taxed at entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act (federal) and Part 1 of the Alberta Corporate Tax Act (provincial). Available statutory elections affecting the tax identity include: (a) section 216 election to file on a net rental basis for Canadian real property; (b) section 217 election on non-resident employment income; (c) section 219 Part XIV election for non-resident corporation investment income; (d) treaty-based 'tie-breaker' determinations under the applicable income tax treaty; (e) GST/HST section 156 election for closely related corporations treating intra-group supplies as nil; (f) the CRA administrative approach to foreign entity classification, which generally follows the entity's legal form under its jurisdiction of incorporation, subject to hybrid mismatch rules in sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act applying to interest deductibility where a foreign entity is treated as fiscally transparent for foreign tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, per ETA section 240; non-residents carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold, but must also register if they solicit orders in Canada from a place of business in Canada

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Commercial stock photography and generative visual asset sales qualify for 0% GST under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supply is made to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and the supply is not a supply of tangible personal property delivered in Canada. The zero-rating condition requires that the service be supplied to a non-resident customer and that the intellectual property right (license to download, use, or redistribute the digital image) is for use outside Canada. Supporting evidence must objectively demonstrate both the non-resident status of the recipient and the foreign use entitlement; where the supply is digital in nature (delivered electronically), the place of supply rules in Schedule IX, sections 3 and 9 assign the place of supply to the consumer's usual place of residence. To rely on zero-rating, the vendor should obtain the recipient's foreign address, non-resident status declaration, foreign tax identifier, and should exclude any supply to Canadian-based customers from zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration — not applicable for pure digital service exports (no physical goods crossing the border; CBSA B13 export declaration is not required for intellectual property licences or cloud-delivered media)
  • Bill of Lading — only applicable if physical media is shipped; for digital stock photo delivery, the controlling document is the electronic delivery record and download log
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — for deposits over C$10,000 through CBIC/FinTRAC reporting or banking records confirming cross-border payment
  • Executed Stock Media License Agreement or Terms of Service evidencing grant of use to a non-resident licensee and foreign-use clause
  • Commercial Invoice or sales receipt stating 'Zero-rated supply under Part V, Schedule VI, ETA — section 7'
  • Non-Resident Status Evidence: foreign billing address, foreign IP/geo-location data, W-8, or foreign tax ID, plus customer's non-resident declaration
  • Bank remittance records and Stripe/PayPal settlement reports linking the transaction to the non-resident counterparty

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, pre-approval digital stamping, or PEPPOL-based continuous transaction controls for GST/HST purposes. Registered businesses must issue invoices containing prescribed ETA information (section 223 and Information Circular IC 4031), retain electronic records under the Electronic Records and Books of Original Entry requirements (RC4140), and file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or through a service provider. The CRA is exploring optional e-invoicing interoperability, but no statutory clearance or real-time reporting obligation currently applies.

The CRA aggressively audits zero-rated digital exports through its 'Place of Supply' verification mandate. Where zero-rating is claimed without adequate non-resident substantiation, the CRA reassesses GST at 5% on gross consideration, recovers input tax credits previously allocated to the zero-rated supplies, and applies statutory interest (compounded daily) plus penalties: (a) under ETA section 280, a false statement or omission penalty of C$100 per GST/HST return plus 10% of the net tax understated for a first incidence; (b) under ETA section 285.1 and ITA section 163.2, a gross-negligence or false-statement penalty of 50% of the understated net tax; (c) under ITA section 239, conviction for wilful tax evasion attracts fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus to five years imprisonment; (d) clawback of input tax refunds under ETA section 296 for cascading fraudulent refund claims. The CRA also requires exporters to retain non-resident status documentation and foreign-remittance proof for at least six years after the year of the supply.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada maintains no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. Dividend and profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is lawful without a blocked-border or repatriation permit; however, Part XIII non-resident withholding tax applies unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty. Transfer-pricing documentation must support intercompany royalty or service payments. Investment Canada Act notification thresholds remain relevant for non-resident capital injections but do not impose FX conversion restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no statutory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency with a Canadian bank or a foreign bank account; amounts are reportable for Canadian tax on an accrual or realization basis as applicable and must be reflected in the Canadian-dollar functional-currency tax calculation. There is no central-bank approval for holding foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential authority: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF regulator: Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); sanctions administration: Global Affairs Canada and Public Safety Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licences, such as FCA EMIs in the UK or EEA-authorised EMIs, are not passportable into Canada and are not recognised as banks by OSFI. An offshore EMI may service the Alberta entity only if its activities in Canada do not trigger FINTRAC MSB registration; otherwise it faces enforcement risk. EMI rails may usefully support low-value payment collection from foreign customers, but are not a lawful substitute for a Canadian Schedule I bank account for treasury, clearing, or capital-escrow purposes. Overall viability: Medium as a supplemental collection rail; Low as a primary banking solution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders should remit capital via SWIFT MT103 from their own account to the Alberta corporation's Canadian bank account. Obtaining instructions should include beneficiary name, account number, bank BIC, and bank address; payment details should state 'contribution to share capital' or 'director loan' to create an audit trail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate non-arm's length transactions per coverage type triggers the mandatory contemporaneous Local File; Master File required on CRA demand for the same transaction universe; Country-by-Country Report filing at consolidated group revenue exceeding EUR 750 million (approximately CAD 1.1 billion).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute equivalent to DIFC/Abu Dhabi ESR laws. Anti-avoidance substance is enforced via GAAR under s. 245, FAPI attribution (s. 91-95) for passive income, the sham doctrine at common law, and the principal-purpose test under the MLI. Alberta supplements through the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) requiring a registered office, documented board meetings, and share records. For a commercial stock-photo and generative AI marketplace, substance is evidenced by Alberta-based editorial and engineering employees, physical server assets, substantive Canadian decision-making, and domestic trade-book and bank activity; nominee-only service arrangements will be disregarded by the CRA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Concealment of Canadian-Source Digital Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s. 239 Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% under s. 163(2); arrears interest at the prescribed rate plus 4%; CRA may extend the reassessment window by 3 years and obtain third-party banking and PayPal/Stripe records.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the concealment are personally exposed under s. 227.1 for unremitted Part XIII or payroll amounts and under s. 160 for transfers of property to avoid tax; severe cases attract travel bans, seizure of passport via criminal indictment, and extradition under existing tax treaty instruments with the US and other DTA partners.
Shell / Conduit Company Without Economic Substance (Treaty Shopping)

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under s. 245(2) disregarding the conduit; full 25% Part XIII withholding re-imposed; denial of treaty benefit under MLI Article 7 / principal-purpose test; Part IV tax on excessive dividends; misrepresentation penalties up to 50%; interest accruing quarterly compounded on the reassessed amount.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for Part XIII amounts deemed unremitted; shareholder loans created by distributions in excess of paid-up capital are assessed personally under s. 15(2); a 'de facto director' doctrine applies where a foreign parent directs distributions without formal resolution; CRA may freeze personal bank accounts and cross-communicate under the OECD multilateral information-exchange framework.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty under s. 247(4) of 10% of the net upward adjustment where the gross adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; s. 162(10) penalty of CAD 100 per day for non-compliance with documentation demand; reassessment of prior statute-barred years extended by 3 years; the CRA may contract an external economist and shift the burden of proof onto the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Where documentation default is combined with knowledge of systematic under-reporting of Canadian income, the CRA applies s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty at 50% personally to signing officers and prepares a referral to Public Prosecution Service of Canada; directors' personal credit and cross-border banking records are subject to lifestyle analysis, and international travel is restricted once a criminal charge is laid.
Money Laundering / Unregistered MSB and Beneficial Ownership Opacity

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for corporations; criminal indictment under PCMLTFA carrying imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; seizure of the payment settlement account under the Criminal Code proceeds-of-crime provisions; Alberta Registry administrative penalties and orders disqualifying directors for the beneficial-ownership non-filing.

Director personal liability: Directors operating the marketplace account and controlling payout instructions are personally chargeable; fines up to CAD 500,000 per violation and imprisonment; directors who knowingly transfer assets out of the Canadian entity without fair consideration are assessed personally under s. 160 of the Income Tax Act; nominee or undisclosed beneficial owner arrangements render the actual owner liable alongside the nominee, and travel bans are routinely issued pending criminal disposition.
