Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), administered via the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html; Alberta provincial corporate income tax is also collected by the CRA under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement, with the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration responsible only for non-income provincial levies.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by applying online through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), using Form RC1W for non-resident registrants. The application must specify the GST/HST, corporate income tax, and payroll program accounts required; non-residents carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must register for GST/HST (Form RC1, Part II) and may be assigned a 15-character BN with an 'RT' program account. Supporting evidence includes certificate of incorporation or registration, proof of Canadian registered address or address for service, and a non-resident information notice; where no permanent establishment exists in Alberta, no separate Alberta corporate account is opened and the corporate tax return remains the federal T2 return, with Alberta corporate tax assessed by the CRA on that return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) authorized under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Business Corporations Act is a separate taxable person subject to federal Part I corporate income tax (15% general rate) and Alberta corporate income tax (8% general rate, effective January 1, 2020); it is not a flow-through vehicle, has no S-corporation equivalent for non-residents, and its Canadian fiscal residence is determined by the location of central management and control for non-resident corporations with Canadian permanent establishments.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. Entities rely instead on statutory classification and CRA administrative look-through guidance. Relevant elections include: the Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for closely related corporations to treat supplies as made for no consideration, the s. 273 election to treat a joint venture as a person for GST/HST purposes, the Income Tax Act s. 216 election for non-residents receiving rental or timber royalties, single-tier corporate characterization of foreign hybrid entities under the Canada-U.S. tax treaty and ITA s. 94.3 hybrid mismatch rules, and the 'associated corporation' filings with CRA which allocate the small business deduction among related CCPCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s. 148); non-resident services, including consulting services rendered by a foreign regulatory-compliance firm to a Canadian client, may require GST/HST registration once the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded, and voluntary registration is available below the threshold to recover input tax credits on exports and zero-rated supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act applies to (i) supplies of goods exported from Canada where possession transfers in Canada and the goods are exported within a reasonable time, and (ii) supplies of services made to a non-resident person that are not supplied to any person in Canada (Schedule VI Part V, s. 7 for goods and s. 9 for services). For MedTech regulatory consulting (FDA 510(k) filing and CE Mark compliance), the services qualify as zero-rated offshore services if the recipient is a non-resident, the service consists of consulting, filing preparation, or compliance-attestation work performed for the foreign principal, and the benefit of the service is not rendered to a person in Canada. The provider must retain evidence of the non-resident status of the recipient and the service agreement to support the 0% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration / CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (G7 or CERS) record for exported goods
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Inward remittance or bank credit evidence, documentary letter of credit, or equivalent bank transfer proof demonstrating receipt of payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Zero-rated supply invoice issued with the Canadian Business Number and explicit identification as 'zero-rated export' or 'supply to a non-resident'
  • Service agreement, FDA/CE engagement letter, and non-resident status evidence (foreign incorporation certificate, tax residence certificate) supporting the offshore services characterization

Canada has no mandatory real-time electronic fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based invoice pre-authorization regime. E-invoicing is voluntary and interoperates through the Pan-Canadian Peppol network; GST/HST returns and non-resident GST/HST returns must be filed electronically once annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1 million (and all registrants must use GST/HST NETFILE or e-services for rebate claims); CRA audit verification relies on invoice data, portal records, bank corroboration, and underlying export documentation rather than government-stamped fiscal documents.

Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline under the Excise Tax Act; the 180-day benchmark is applied by CRA auditors as a documentary-substantiation and commercial-reality convention, and non-repatriation within normal commercial terms may trigger transfer-pricing recharacterization under Income Tax Act s. 247. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract Excise Tax Act civil penalties of 25% of the tax sought (s. 280), gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the understated tax (s. 285), interest, retroactive denial of the 0% rate and reassessment for up to four years (unlimited in the case of misrepresentation or fraud), and criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s. 239 with fines of up to 200% of the tax avoided and imprisonment of up to five years; CRA also routinely issues penalty assessments where the exporter cannot produce actual export documentation or contemporaneous non-resident payment evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD) on both the current account and the capital account in compliance with IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no exchange-control restrictions on foreign direct investment, dividend remittances, interest payments, or capital repatriation. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is subject to the Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Canadian Income Tax Act (typically 25%, reduced to 5-15% under an applicable tax treaty, such as the Canada-US Treaty), and must be reported through Form NR4 information returns and the T7/NR-T remittance mechanism. Administrative due diligence requires clear documentation of the underlying consulting invoices and corporate resolutions to satisfy Canadian banks and the Canada Revenue Agency that payments are commercially genuine.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no statutory conversion timeline or fixed conversion ratio. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency proceeds in Canadian or offshore bank accounts, invoice and settle in any currency, and convert CAD to foreign currency, or vice versa, at will. The only obligations are statistical reporting to Statistics Canada under the balance-of-payments framework and certain anti-money-laundering disclosures under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act if transactions meet the reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Moderate. Canada does not issue electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences equivalent to the United Kingdom's or the European Union's EMI regime. Offshore digital EMIs cannot independently hold client funds, accept deposits, or provide deposit-like payment services in Canada, as deposit-taking is reserved to regulated Canadian financial institutions. An offshore EMI may only operate in Canada through a registered domestic Money Services Business (MSB) or Retail Payment Activity Provider (RPAP) under FINTRAC and the Retail Payment Activities Act, or by partnering with a Canadian chartered bank for custody and settlement. For the purpose of holding corporate treasury funds or receiving international wire fees, a physical commercial bank account at a Tier 1 Canadian bank remains the mandatory primary clearing hub.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital is performed through SWIFT MT103/MT202 messages from the foreign parent to the Canadian corporate bank account, with the correspondent bank identified and the correspondent fee pre-arranged. The remittance information must clearly reference a capital contribution for equivalence with the share register. The recommended purpose code follows the BPM6/IMF standard: 'Direct investment - Equity capital, other than reinvestment of earnings'. The Canadian receiving bank must be given: the company's Business Number (BN), the sole-purpose 'Capital Escrow Account' designation, the director's resolution, and a certified copy of the shareholder ledger. For F/X conversion, the receiving bank will use the spot rate agreed between the corporate treasury and the bank; no exchange-control approval is required, but all inward transfers above CAD 10,000 are automatically recorded and subject to FINTRAC's large-value reporting requirements.Outbound settlements for cross-border professional services and procurement are executed through SWIFT MT103 single-payment wires, with the IBAN/SWIFT code of the beneficiary and a clear purpose code reflecting 'contract-based services fee'. For physical medical-device logistic purchases, Canadian banks offer commercial letters of credit (documentary credits) under UCP600, typically using MT700/MT707 for issuance and amendment, as well as CAD and USD clearing via Canada's high-value clearing system, Lynx. For recurring smaller fees to international vendors, Canadian ACH/EFT (via Payments Canada) is acceptable. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined headline (15% federal general + 8% Alberta general); 11% combined on active business income of a qualifying Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) up to the CAD 500,000 small-business limit (9% federal + 2% Alberta).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents (ITA s. 212(2)); under the Canada–US treaty, reduced to 5% where the beneficial owner is a corporation controlling 10% or more of the payer's voting rights, and 15% for portfolio dividends; other DTAs generally provide 5%/15% or 10%/15% rate tiers.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal year-end (ITA s. 150(1)); the balance-due day for corporate income tax is 2 months after fiscal year-end (3 months for eligible CCPCs) under ITA s. 157(1); quarterly instalments are required where Part I tax payable for the current or prior year exceeds CAD 3,000.
