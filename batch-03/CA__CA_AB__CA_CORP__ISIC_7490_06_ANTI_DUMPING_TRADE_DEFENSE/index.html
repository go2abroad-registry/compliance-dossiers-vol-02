Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election or pass-through classification for business corporations; a CA_CORP is always entity-level taxable. The available identity-relevant elections are: (i) GST/HST closely-related corporation account consolidation election under s. 156 Excise Tax Act; (ii) GST/HST joint venture election under s. 273 Excise Tax Act where the trade-defense practice is carried through a Canadian JV; (iii) s. 216(1) Income Tax Act net-basis election for non-resident rental income (inapplicable to trading services); and (iv) Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status, which provides the small-business deduction, is a statutory attribute rather than an elective one and is unavailable where a non-resident controls the company; hybrid mismatch rules of the Income Tax Act apply to deny benefits where offshore classification diverges from Canadian entity-level treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible. Canada has accepted Article VIII, Sections 2, 3, and 4 of the IMF Articles of Agreement and maintains no exchange restrictions on current international transactions; capital-account transfers (equity injections, shareholder loans, M&A consideration) are likewise unrestricted. Profit remittances by a non-resident shareholder are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, treaty-reducible) but not to any foreign-exchange approval, convertibility, or repatriation permit. There is no central-bank licensing for FX dealings and no requirement to channel transactions through any onshore market; cross-border settlements may be made freely in CAD or any foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, conversion quota, or repatriation timeline exists in Canada. Export and service receipts (including fees for trade-defense legal and consulting services under ISIC 7490) may be retained indefinitely in foreign-denominated accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks, Canadian branches of authorized foreign banks, or offshore banks. The only compliance overlay is FINTRAC's reportable-transaction regime for amounts at or above CAD 10,000, including large-cash-transaction reporting and cross-border EFT record-keeping as in force; none of these obligations mandates conversion into CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acting under the Bank of Canada Act, in conjunction with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of deposit-taking institutions, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/CFT supervisor, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and Canadian International Trade Tribunal (CITT) as the authorities administering anti-dumping trade-defense measures under the Special Import Measures Act (SIMA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for non-core treasury functions, Low as a sole banking relationship. FINTRAC-registered money services businesses (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) may be used lawfully by an Alberta corporation for low-value cross-border collections, contractor disbursements, and FX conversion; however, for the anti-dumping trade-defense mandate, only a Canadian Schedule I bank or a Canadian branch of an authorized foreign bank can provide (a) CBSA/CITT acceptable financial security instruments, (b) SWIFT MT103 client deposits with beneficiary-bank verification, and (c) credit-supported FX hedging capacity. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate income tax rate for a Business Corporation operating in Alberta: federal basic rate of 38% reduced by the 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction to 15%, plus the Alberta provincial general corporate rate of 8%. This rate applies to taxable income from ISIC 7490 anti-dumping trade-defense consulting activities where the corporation is not eligible for a preferential deduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, specifically section 212(2), on all dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident of Canada. This includes cash dividends, stock dividends, deemed dividends on winding-up, and appropriations to shareholders. Withholding must be remitted to the CRA by the 15th day of the month following the month in which the dividend is paid.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold exists in Canada for transfer-pricing documentation. Every reportable cross-border transaction between the Alberta consulting corporation and a non-resident related party is subject to section 247 of the Income Tax Act. CRA considers contemporaneous documentation mandatory support: the taxpayer must make reasonable efforts to determine and use arm's-length transfer prices and must maintain all records needed to verify income, including a Canadian Local File and, when applicable, a Master File. The documentation must be available within a reasonable time upon notice and must show the selection of the best transfer-pricing method, the comparability analysis, and the arm's-length range. For this activity, a prudent threshold of any intercompany transaction, including cost allocations, is CAD 10,000 or below; all related-party agreements should be reduced to writing regardless of amount.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime modeled on the Caribbean six-activity standard. However, economic substance is a governing compliance principle enforced through the Income Tax Act's general anti-avoidance rule in section 245, the treaty beneficial-ownership requirement, section 247 transfer-pricing rules, and the judicial 'sham' and 'agency' doctrines. For an anti-dumping trade-defense consulting corporation operating in Alberta, the following substance indicators will be demanded by the CRA: actual physical office presence, local management and employees, board meetings held in Canada, genuine risk-bearing capacity, arm's-length intercompany contracts, and revenues that align with the 'effective management and control' exercised in Alberta.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Under section 239(1) of the Income Tax Act, conviction is punishable by a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. In addition, section 163(2) imposes a civil penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax attributable to gross negligence, plus unpaid tax and compound interest at the prescribed rate.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false returns with knowledge or criminal negligence can be prosecuted personally, and courts will pierce the corporate veil where the entity is a mere facade. Directors may also be made jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under section 227.1 and related provisions. Criminal tax evasion can lead to travel bans through passport registration restrictions and extradition under Canada's Mutual Legal Assistance Treaties.
Transfer-Pricing Penalty and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under section 247(3), a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment is imposed when the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts to use arm's-length transfer prices or fails to provide required documentation. The CRA may reattribute the full adjusted income to the Canadian corporation, deny deductions, and charge interest on the resulting tax liability.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct or approve the preparation of false intercompany invoices may be assessed as having 'participated in the misrepresentation' and can be subject to the 50% gross-negligence penalty on their own account. Personal liability can extend to restitution orders, director disqualification, and, in flagrant cases, criminal counselling charges under section 239.
Repatriation and Non-Resident Withholding Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The payer remains liable for the amount required to be withheld, plus compound daily interest and any penalty for failure to deduct or remit. Such failure can render the expense non-deductible for tax purposes and may constitute an offence subject to fines and imprisonment if done wilfully.

Director personal liability: Under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act, directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted non-resident withholding tax and related penalties. The CRA may assess directors directly and enforce collection against personal assets, including bank accounts, before any discharge of the corporation from the Alberta registry.
Shell Company / Economic-Substance Avoidance

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule under section 245 can deny the tax benefit and cause the income to be reattributed to the Canadian corporation. The CRA may also treat the corporation as a sham or as an agent for the foreign parent, resulting in the full inclusion of revenue in the Canadian taxable income plus penalties and interest. Alberta corporate authorities may dissolve a non-active shell for failure to comply with registered-office and annual-return obligations.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate form is recognized as a sham, courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for the avoided tax. Directors can also face claims of fraudulent or wrongful conduct, travel restrictions while under criminal investigation, and orders to disgorge funds transferred to related parties.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act imposes administrative monetary penalties that may reach CAD 500,000 for a corporation and CAD 100,000 for an individual, depending on the contravention. Criminal prosecution can also result in imprisonment for up to 5 years and forfeiture of property associated with the offence.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize deficient compliance programs or who knowingly avoid reporting can be prosecuted personally. Conviction can trigger director disqualification, travel bans, and seizure orders against personal and corporate assets. The low-risk standard classification for this scope is not a statutory exemption; it only lowers the ordinary due-diligence burden but does not excuse record-keeping for wire transfers.
