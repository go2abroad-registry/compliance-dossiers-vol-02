Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A corporation must maintain a registered office in Alberta and must maintain corporate records and a records office as required by the Alberta Business Corporations Act. A commercial virtual office may be acceptable as a registered office if it is a genuine Alberta address, receives and promptly forwards legal documents, maintains required records or identifies the records office, and satisfies the registry agent and financial institution. A mailbox-only, accommodation-only, or non-operational address is high risk. For a global employer-of-record and cross-border payroll business, a virtual office does not establish operational substance, payroll capability, employment-law compliance, privacy controls, client support, or tax-residency management. The business may require payroll-account registration with the Canada Revenue Agency, workers' compensation registration where applicable, employment-standards compliance, privacy compliance, and additional licensing or registration in the provinces where employees or employment-agency activities occur. Canadian immigration-recruitment services may require use of an authorized immigration representative and provincial recruiter compliance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible. Canada has assumed Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement, so current-account payments, capital-account transfers, dividend remittances and intercompany funding are not subject to exchange-control approvals or quantitative limits. The exchange rate floats freely under the Bank of Canada's inflation-control mandate. The only substantial remittance hurdle is tax-driven, not currency-driven: non-resident profit and dividend remittances carry Part XIII withholding tax of 25% statutory, typically reduced to 15% or 5% under the Canada-US Convention (5% for a US parent holding at least 10% of voting power) and comparable treaties; interest paid to non-resident shareholders or creditors is subject to a 25% base withholding and to 10% or 15% treaty rates. Such taxes are withholding obligations, not repatriation duties, and do not restrict convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio and no repatriation deadline for export or services proceeds; a Canadian EOR entity may retain 100% of foreign-currency receivables in CAD or in any convertible currency. Operative compliance constraints are limited to: (a) FINTRAC international EFT reporting for each inbound or outbound wire of CAD 10,000 or more (due within five business days); (b) sanctions and export-control screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act (the Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List and Public Safety Canada's sanctions database); and (c) Income Tax Act section 247 transfer-pricing documentation for intercompany service revenue. There is no legal obligation to convert export receipts to CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - the monetary authority and foreign-exchange agent for the Government of Canada; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of Schedule I chartered banks under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF supervisor under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); and Global Affairs Canada as administrator of economic sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act. Canada's exchange liberalization derives from the Currency Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-52) and the repeal of the former Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech rails are rated High for disbursement-side treasury (Wise, certified as a foreign MSB registered with FINTRAC, and Payoneer) and Medium-to-Low for legal-entity cash concentration. Canadian-dollar clearing requires a FINTRAC-registered MSB, and the incoming Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), supervised by the Bank of Canada, will require payment service providers to obtain authorization and to safeguard client funds; an offshore digital EMI without MSB or PSP registration cannot lawfully clear payroll in Canada. Wise balances are not CDIC-insured and are not deposits in a Schedule I bank, so a prudent structure uses a Schedule I bank for capital escrow and operational concentration, with Wise or Payoneer batch APIs only for small-value, high-frequency contractor payouts.
