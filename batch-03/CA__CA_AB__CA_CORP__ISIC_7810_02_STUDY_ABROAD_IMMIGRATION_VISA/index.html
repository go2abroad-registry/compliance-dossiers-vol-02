Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-registration-online.html) and CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services/business-account.html). Alberta corporate income tax is administered and collected by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Income Tax Act via the single federal T2 return; Alberta's Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) handles provincial programs. Alberta has no provincial sales tax, so no PST/HST registration is required.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the Canadian subsidiary under the Canada Business Corporations Act (Corporations Canada) or Alberta Business Corporations Act (Alberta Corporate Registry), obtaining the corporate access number. 2) Register for the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) with program accounts RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST) and RP (payroll) via BRO, by telephone (1-800-959-5525), or by mailing Form RC1; the BN is the functional TIN/VAT ID. 3) Provide a Canadian address and appoint a Canadian-resident agent for service before the BN is activated. 4) Elect a fiscal year-end (first period cannot exceed 53 weeks) and register for CRA direct deposit to receive GST/HST input tax refunds. 5) Include the BN plus RT0001 program suffix on all GST/HST invoices. 6) File an annual T2 corporate return even for nil amounts; non-resident shareholders need no Canadian TIN for entity operation. Online BN issuance is immediate; RC1 processing takes 10–15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Alberta-incorporated subsidiary is resident in Canada by incorporation (ITA s. 250(4)) and taxed on worldwide income (ITA s. 2(1)). It is a non-CCPC (foreign parent control) and therefore ineligible for the small-business deduction; combined general rate 15% federal + 8% Alberta = 23%. Foreign-source income is subject to foreign tax credits; dividends to the foreign parent are subject to Part XIII 25% withholding, treaty-reduced to 5% at ≥10% voting ownership (15% otherwise) under most OECD treaties. Territorial scope: worldwide taxation for residents; no tax-exempt-zone regime applies.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; all corporations are entity-level taxpayers. Available elections: (i) GST/HST closely-related corporation election (ETA ss. 150/156; Form GST25) to treat inter-affiliate taxable supplies as nil-consideration; (ii) functional-currency election (ITA s. 261; Form RC4596) to compute Canadian tax in USD; (iii) non-resident branch election (ITA s. 219) if the foreign parent operates through a Canadian permanent establishment instead of the subsidiary; (iv) hybrid-mismatch relief elections under ITA ss. 18.2(3)/18.2(4) (deduction/non-inclusion relief after a 12-month inclusion window) and ITA s. 18.21 for imported mismatches; (v) the s. 216 net-rental election is available only for real-property rental income and is irrelevant to fee-for-service income. CCPC small-business-limit allocations (ITA s. 256) are unavailable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold — registration becomes mandatory at the end of the quarter in which the registrant's (plus associated persons') worldwide taxable supplies in the four preceding calendar quarters exceed CAD 30,000 (ETA ss. 148, 240, 156.1). Both taxable and zero-rated study-abroad/immigration-consulting fees count toward the threshold. Voluntary early registration is advisable to recover input tax credits on Canadian inputs. Because the subsidiary carries on business in Alberta, reverse-charge rules (ETA ss. 217.1/218.01) apply only to unregistered non-resident suppliers, not to this entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0% GST) applies to services supplied to a non-resident recipient (ETA Sch. VI, Part V, s. 7(1)) unless the service is rendered to an individual present in Canada at the time of supply, or relates to real property or tangible goods situated in Canada. Study-abroad placement and immigration/visa consultancy to a client physically outside Canada qualifies: a visa/study-permit application is a personal right, not an interest in Canadian real property or goods, consistent with CRA technical bulletin B-080 and settled interpretive practice. Services to a client already in Canada (e.g., in-Canada study-permit extension) are taxable at 5%. The registrant must retain contemporaneous proof of the client's non-resident location and must bifurcate mixed Canadian/international fees on invoices under a written allocation; otherwise the entire fee is taxable at 5%. Full input tax credits are available for GST paid on inputs attributable to zero-rated exports.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (Form B13/B13A) — only if physical promotional materials ship abroad; for pure consultancy, the signed engagement agreement is the operative export document
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill — only for accompanying physical shipments; otherwise the scope-of-work and service-milestone records
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 wire confirmation — evidencing FX payment from the non-resident client's foreign bank account into the Canadian corporate account for each zero-rated invoice
  • Non-resident status certification — Form RC-3 or notarized attestation confirming foreign residency and presence outside Canada at each service milestone
  • Visa/immigration file records — study-permit or visa applications, consular receipts and embassy correspondence evidencing the service was performed for Canadian immigration purposes and not in relation to Canadian real property or situated goods

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping is in force in Canada. Mandates: (1) GST/HST returns must be e-filed via GST/HST NETFILE where annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1,500,000 (ETA s. 273.1); (2) books and records must be retained for six years (ETA s. 286); (3) every taxable/zero-rated invoice over CAD 30 must show the BN (RT account), date, recipient, description, consideration, and rate/tax (ETA ss. 169, 223); simplified invoices allowed under CAD 150. The Pan-Canadian Peppol 'PINT Canada' e-invoicing standard is voluntary as of 2026; no CRA API clearance or stamping.

CRA audits zero-rating claims through GST/HST risk analytics and cross-border financial data (CRS/FATCA and SWIFT payment traces). Where 0% is wrongly applied, the CRA reassesses the full consideration at 5% GST with compound daily arrears interest at the prescribed quarterly rate plus 4 percentage points for corporations (ETA s. 280.1). Penalties: (1) 5% failure-to-remit penalty plus 1% per full month, up to 12 months (ETA s. 280); (2) 5% accuracy penalty plus 50% gross-negligence penalty on the 'false statement amount' for knowing or grossly negligent misstatements (ETA s. 285.01); (3) criminal prosecution for wilful evasion (ETA s. 327): up to CAD 100,000 fine and/or 5 years' imprisonment on indictment; (4) professional-discipline revocation by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants. Reassessment windows: 4 years normally, extended to 10 years for misrepresentation/neglect/wilful default (ETA s. 298(4)); the GST/HST general anti-avoidance rule (ETA s. 274) can reopen linked transactions contrived to obtain ITCs or zero-rating benefits. ITCs tied to the mischaracterized supply are clawed back with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible with no exchange-control statute, no capital-account licensing, no restriction on capital repatriation, and no distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility. Profit-remittance hurdles are tax- and corporate-law centered rather than FX-centered: (i) dividends and interest paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax of 25% statutory, with treaty-reduced rates applicable (e.g., Canada-China DTA may reduce dividends and interest to 10%; Canada-India DTA may reduce certain dividends to 15%); (ii) the Alberta business corporation must satisfy the solvency test in sections 42-43 of the Alberta Business Corporations Act before any distribution, and must be current on CRA filings and Part XIII return obligations; (iii) funds may be freely wired to any jurisdiction through a bank in Canada, subject only to ordinary CDD/EDD screening, sanctions screening under Canada's Special Economic Measures Act, the Criminal Code, and the United Nations Act, and to FINTRAC reporting thresholds; (iv) for inbound equity subscriptions, corporate records must be updated in the Alberta Corporate Registry, and the bank will require board resolutions and share-register evidence; (v) currency conversion is unrestricted at market rates via any authorized bank, foreign-exchange broker, or registered MSB, provided the underlying transaction documentation is retained for at least five years.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline. An Alberta corporation may hold export receipts in CAD or foreign currency in any chartered bank or authorized foreign bank branch. Remaining obligations are informational and AML-related only: (i) international electronic funds transfers (EFT) of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC by the financial institution, and the corporate exporter must supply the underlying commercial documentation upon request; (ii) exports of professional services such as study-abroad placement and immigration-visa consulting are captured by Statistics Canada and the Canada Border Services Agency through balance-of-payments trade-in-services reporting, not by the central bank; (iii) foreign-exchange gains and losses on export proceeds must be tracked for CRA corporate income tax purposes under section 39 of the Income Tax Act; (iv) all foreign-currency transactions remain subject to sanctions screening and the enterprise's own AML/ATF compliance program, including record-keeping of the underlying service contracts and tuition/visa-file evidence.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central monetary authority and fiscal agent; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of deposit-taking institutions and authorized foreign banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF supervisor and Money Service Business (MSB) registrar; Department of Finance Canada as sanctions, international financial policy, and payments-system authority. Canada operates no dedicated Foreign Exchange Control Administration; exchange matters are managed through the market, prudential bank supervision, and statutory AML reporting rather than through ex-ante FX licensing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (supplementary rail only; not permissible as the primary escrow depository). Canada does not issue an EMI licence; therefore an offshore digital bank or fintech EMI holding Canadian client funds must either be an authorized foreign bank branch (Schedule III) under the Bank Act or operate as a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, with additional obligations under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) supervisory framework phased in by OSFI during 2024-2025. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer may serve as secondary collections and supplier-payment rails for EUR, GBP, CNH, INR, and other cross-currency operating flows, but they must not hold client tuition and visa fees in lieu of a chartered-bank client trust escrow. The Alberta entity must itself avoid functioning as an unregistered MSB when transmitting funds on behalf of clients; fee settlement should be structured as principal-to-principal payment for the corporation's own professional services, with escrow balances remaining exclusively at a Canadian Schedule I bank or licensed trust company. Passing client funds through an unregulated offshore EMI is a KYC and regulatory blocker.

The Alberta entity must itself avoid functioning as an unregistered MSB when transmitting funds on behalf of clients; fee settlement should be structured as principal-to-principal payment for the corporation's own professional services, with escrow balances remaining exclusively at a Canadian Schedule I bank or licensed trust company. Passing client funds through an unregulated offshore EMI is a KYC and regulatory blocker.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transactions with non-residents exceeding CAD 1 million (for services) or CAD 5 million (for goods, intangibles, or financing) trigger mandatory Local File and Master File documentation. Smaller transactions may still require contemporaneous documentation if they are not at arm's length.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions. A corporation must demonstrate real economic activity (e.g., employees, premises, decision-making) to claim treaty benefits and avoid recharacterization of transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and full restitution of taxes, interest, and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s. 227.1) if they failed to withhold or remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the entity under GAAR, deny treaty benefits, reassess taxes, and impose penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit (s. 163.2).

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly and severally liable for penalties and taxes if they knowingly participated in the sham; potential criminal charges for conspiracy.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal fines and imprisonment if they knowingly fail to implement AML compliance programs; civil liability for damages to clients.
