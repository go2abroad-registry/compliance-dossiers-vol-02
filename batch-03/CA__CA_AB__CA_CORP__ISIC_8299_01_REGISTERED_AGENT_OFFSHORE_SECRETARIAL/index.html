Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the ABCA; the Corporation may incorporate with zero issued capital or a nominal subscription of one or more shares. The authorized share structure is set by the board. No escrow or capital verification account is mandated for incorporation. If shares are issued for non-cash consideration (property, past services), a contemporaneous directors' resolution and capital maintenance statement should be documented for tax and GST/HST records. NUANS name search and corporate extract fees are paid separately to the registry.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Alberta Fire Code and municipal fire inspection, including NFPA 13-compliant sprinkler system where required by occupant load; structural load and occupancy clearance issued by a professional engineer under the Alberta Building Code; preliminary workplace hazard assessment and OHS site clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, RSA 2000, c O-2 (Alberta OHS Code Part 22), covering first-aid equipment, fire evacuation routes, emergency lighting, and accessibility compliance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal and Alberta corporate income tax, GST/HST, and payroll; CRA 'My Business Account' portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-business/business-account.html). Alberta Registrar of Companies handles corporate incorporation; Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) administers Alberta non-income levies while the CRA administers Alberta corporate income tax pursuant to the federal-provincial administration agreement.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-controlled Alberta business corporation: (1) complete incorporation/extra-provincial registration in Alberta; (2) obtain the federally issued Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA, online via Business Registration Online (BRO), or by mail with certified copies of articles of incorporation and officer/director identification; (3) activate the GST/HST program account (RT0001) and register for any required import/export (RM0001) or payroll (RP0001) accounts; the 15-character BN is the statutory tax identifier replacing any foreign TIN for Canadian filings. Non-resident shareholders/directors must provide a power of attorney, certificate of status, and signed CRA forms for third-party representation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a Canadian-resident business corporation is a separate taxable person under subsection 89(1) and section 250 of the Income Tax Act; no fiscal transparency, no territorial remittance election; the corporation is liable for Part I tax on world-wide income and, where applicable, Alberta corporate income tax administered by CRA.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box and no elective pass-through for Business Corporations. Relevant elections include: (a) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election between closely related corporations to treat inter-corporate taxable supplies as made for nil consideration for GST/HST; (b) GST/HST annual filing or accelerated fiscal-year elections where authorized; and (c) hybrid-mismatch carve-out elections under s. 18.4–18.8 of the Income Tax Act (where an entity is fiscally transparent outside Canada) together with prescribed disclosure in CRA Form RC2108. All elections require prompt filing with CRA; late elections require administrative relief under s. 220(2.1)/ETA s. 32.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – a person ceases to be a small supplier and must register for GST/HST under Excise Tax Act s. 240(1) if total taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports and assessed at the worldwide level in any calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters) exceeds CAD 30,000; voluntary registration is permitted under s. 240(2).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (s. 7), a supply of a service made to a non-resident person is zero-rated if the service is not rendered in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada and is not otherwise to be received in Canada. Registered-agent and offshore corporate secretarial services supplied to non-resident clients (payment from abroad, board resolutions from outside Canada, files and records maintained offshore) qualify as zero-rated supplies. The supplier must obtain and preserve in one bundle: a signed engagement letter or service agreement, the non-resident customer's certificate/self-declaration of residence or underlying board resolution, invoices and remittance proofs; the claim is then reflected in the provider's GST/HST return as a zero-rated export, allowing recovery of related input tax credits (net tax refund).

  • Customs Export Declaration (for goods, a B13/CAED or CBSA declaration; for services, the written service agreement and SOC are the evidentiary stand-in)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill (for freight; for offshore secretarial services, the delivery evidence may consist of electronic transmission logs, registered-office instruction records and counterparty acknowledgement)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or bank wire transfer confirmation showing payment received in foreign currency from the non-resident client or its authorized agent

No CRA real-time invoice clearance, no national electronic-invoicing mandate, no digital stamping, and no B2B/B2G clearance API. Registrants must issue an invoice before the due invoice date containing prescribed ETA s. 223(1) particulars. Filing obligations are administered through electronic channels: GST/HST NETFILE, XML/EDI for specified large filers, and CRA My Business Account. Records and e-invoices must be retained in a readable, accessible electronic format for audit purposes.

CRA examines export zero-rating claims through income/FX matching. Where the zero-rating is reversed, the supplier is reassessed to remit 5% GST on the full consideration, disgorge any input tax credits taken, and pay arrears interest (ETA s. 280). If a false or misleading statement was made knowingly or under gross negligence, ETA s. 285.1 imposes a civil penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the under-assessed net tax. Reassessment remains open for four years, or indefinitely for misrepresentation/fraud (ETA s. 296); criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 can attract fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. CRA also applies transfer-pricing documentation standards where the export is between non-resident related parties.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no statutory exchange controls under the Currency Act, no registration requirement for ordinary cross-border capital flows, and profit repatriation by non-residents is permitted without currency conversion restrictions. Ministerial approvals may be required for larger FDI under the Investment Canada Act solely on national-security or net-benefit review, but this does not restrict convertibility. Dividends and branch remittances are subject only to withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (parts XIII and XIV), not to FX convertibility hurdles. Non-resident accounts may be held in any convertible currency and freely remitted once Canadian-resident withholding and income tax obligations are discharged.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no ratio for export receipts under the Currency Act or the Bank of Canada regulations. Canadian exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency, may hold such funds in resident or non-resident foreign-currency bank accounts, and may convert to CAD at any time at a licenced dealer's market rate. Reporting obligations are limited to FINTRAC's cross-border currency reporting (CAMRA) for physical currency movements exceeding CAD 10,000 and electronic funds transfer reports; these do not trigger conversion or surrender.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control administration; policy coordination falls under the Department of Finance Canada, with FX-related clearing and settlement oversight by Payments Canada and prudential supervision by OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for non-Canadian offshore digital EMI platforms. A foreign EMI that is not incorporated and licenced in Canada cannot accept deposit liabilities or operate as a payment service provider without registration as a Money Services Business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and any foreign entity providing payment services to Canadians must either partner with a CDIC-insured Schedule I bank or obtain a provincial money-services licence. In addition, the Bank Act requires any foreign bank acting as a ‘bank’ in Canada (including receiving deposits) to be authorized via the OSFI foreign-bank branch entry regime. For an ISIC 8299 registered-agent / offshore-secretarial service, using a fully regulated Canadian Schedule I bank is significantly safer; EMIs can be used only for non-deposit transactional FX flows after local AML obligations are satisfied.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to an Alberta Business Corporation should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the overseas investor's bank to the Canadian account with a clear purpose statement. Standard purpose codes for debt and equity investment remittances: ‘N301’ (trade in equity capital and shares), ‘N302’ (capital in cash form), or BOP codes as supplied by the receiving Canadian bank. For investments exceeding the statutory thresholds (CAD 5.0M enterprise value for non-controlled transactions), the Investment Canada Act may impose a notification or review obligation independent of banking. The receiving bank must perform a name-and-principal-operator sweep against OFSI/COLC/SAN and require a board resolution confirming the purpose of funds and legitimate corporate treasury activity. Funds relating to registered-agent and offshore-secretarial services are legal but will require detailed EDD to identify the underlying commercial purpose, contract context, and the ultimate beneficiary; prudent practice is to use either a Capital Escrow Account or a distinct Operational Account to avoid commingling.For outbound trade settlements and operational expenses, the standard rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct remittances (with mandate and reference clauses), Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits) under UCP600 for goods movement, and SWIFT gpi (Global Payments Innovation) to reduce intermediary latency. For recurring secretarial-service fees destined to affiliated offshore entities, the optimum route is a single cross-border MT103 batch with a pre-mapped beneficiary bank code, invoice copy, and ACH fallback for CAD-denominated payments within Canada. All outbound disbursements require FINTRAC wire-transfer records for transactions of CAD 10,000+ and may need supporting invoices for contract-based services.
