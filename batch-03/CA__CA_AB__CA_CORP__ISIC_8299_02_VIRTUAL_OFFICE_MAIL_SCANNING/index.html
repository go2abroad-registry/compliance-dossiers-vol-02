Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; electronic access via Business Registration Online and My Business Account; Alberta corporate income tax and GST/HST are administered by the CRA under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement; customs and export controls are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Alberta Business Corporations Act; the corporate registry data exchange automatically assigns the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and the corporate income tax program account (RC). Step 2 - A foreign entity without a federal charter obtains a BN by filing Form RC1 through Business Registration Online or by calling the CRA Business Window (Canada/US: 1-855-432-4230; overseas: 1-416-973-5776) with certified foreign registration documents, proof of authorized representation in Canada, and Form RC59 where a tax representative acts. Step 3 - Register the GST/HST program account (suffix RT) on the same BN when total consideration for taxable supplies made in Canada, including zero-rated exports, exceeds CAD 30,000 over the current calendar quarter and the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or voluntarily under ETA s. 240 to claim input tax credits on Canadian costs. Step 4 - Activate a payroll account (RP) and Alberta workers' compensation account only if Canadian employees are engaged. Step 5 - File through CRA My Business Account or certified software/API; the BN is the statutory TIN for federal and Alberta corporate tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian-incorporated Business Corporation is a resident taxpayer on worldwide income (ITA s. 2(1) and s. 250(4)(a)); no pass-through treatment applies. Alberta corporate income tax is 8% general / 2% small-business on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income eligible for the federal small-business deduction (federal rates 15% general / 9% small-business); no consolidated group filing is available. A non-resident parent is not directly taxed on the subsidiary income, but dividends are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% unless treaty-reduced. For GST/HST, supplies made in Canada fall within the Alberta GST nexus at the federal 5% rate only; Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax and does not participate in the HST.

Available election forms

Canada does not administer Check-the-Box entity-classification elections; corporate and partnership status is statutory. Available mechanisms relevant to this structure: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration and filing-frequency elections under ETA ss. 240 and 243-245 (annual filing where annual net tax does not exceed CAD 3,000; monthly filing where net tax exceeds CAD 3,000,000); (ii) non-calendar fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) aligned with the GST/HST fiscal year; (iii) associated-CCPC allocation election under ITA s. 125(6.1) to share the CAD 500,000 small-business limit among related corporations; (iv) ITA s. 85 rollover election if a proprietorship is converted into the CA_CORP shell; (v) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2-18.4, enacted by Bill C-59 on June 20, 2024, applying to payments made on or after July 1, 2024) apply automatically without elective opt-out; recharacterisation of the Canadian entity as a flow-through is not permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - total worldwide consideration for taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports of administrative/mail-scanning services) made by the person in Canada in the current quarter and the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier rule, ETA s. 148). Zero-rated supplies are taxable supplies and count toward the threshold; once exceeded, registration is mandatory and all future taxable supplies carry 5% GST unless zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Mail-scanning and virtual-office administrative services supplied to a non-resident corporate client are zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI, s. 7(a) of the Excise Tax Act where: (i) the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST (or registered but not entitled to input tax credits); (ii) the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada - the CMRA agreement must be structured as a service contract, not a lease of the Alberta address; and (iii) the service is not rendered to an individual while physically present in Canada. Physical mail forwarding from the Alberta CMRA to foreign destinations is separately zero-rated under s. 6 as goods exported as soon as is reasonably practicable after the supply. The supplier must retain contemporaneous commercial records proving non-resident identity and export movement for six years (ETA s. 286(1)); a voluntary registrant with only zero-rated export revenue files nil-net-tax returns and obtains cash refunds of input tax credits on Canadian rent, software, courier, and payment-processing inputs.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CBSA Form B13A or Canadian Export Reporting System (e-manifest) record for each physical mail/parcel shipment exported from Canada, showing the Alberta supplier as exporter and the foreign client as consignee
  • Bill of Lading or House Air Waybill issued by the international carrier, linking the shipment to the sales invoice and confirming departure from a Canadian port or courier hub
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 banking credit advice demonstrating receipt of the non-resident client's subscription payment in foreign currency into the Canadian corporation's account

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing regime exists in Canada. The CRA operates a trust-based periodic filing model: GST/HST returns (Form GST34) must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE or E-FILE via certified software/API) by registrants with annual net tax over CAD 1.5 million; other registrants may file online or by paper. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoice Standard (Peppol BIS 3.0, UBL 2.1) is voluntarily adopted for federal procurement and large integrated platforms but is not mandatory for a CMRA. Invoices to non-residents must satisfy ETA s. 223(1): supplier BN, date, description, consideration, and a statement that the supply is zero-rated; CRA clearance is not required before issuance.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST within four years of a return (ETA s. 298(1)) and income tax within four years for a non-CCPC such as a foreign-controlled corporation (ITA s. 152(3.1)(b)); no limitation period applies where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is shown. If export zero-rating is denied, the net GST and refunded input tax credits are clawed back with compounded daily arrears interest at the prescribed rate (90-day Government of Canada T-bill benchmark plus 2 percentage points), plus a 25% civil gross-negligence penalty on the net tax under ETA s. 280(1), increased to 50% for a second violation within five years under ETA s. 280(1.1). Knowingly claiming a false export zero-rating or refund constitutes tax evasion under ETA s. 327, punishable by a fine of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years on indictment. The CBSA/CRA cross-border data suite - export declarations, courier manifests, and SWIFT payment records - forms the standard audit evidence package; additionally, ITA s. 17 imputes interest income on interest-free receivables owed by non-arm's-length non-residents outstanding beyond the 180-day benchmark.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member; the CAD is fully convertible, freely floating, and fully deliverable. Current-account and capital-account transactions (equity injection, intercompany loans, dividends, liquidation proceeds, service export revenue) require no Bank of Canada approval and carry no swap, placement, or repatriation-timing obligations. The practical gates are: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) on outbound dividends/interest (general 25%, treaty-reduced to 5%-15% for OECD residents); (ii) GST/HST zero-rating of exported mail-scanning services under the Excise Tax Act if supply is made to non-residents; (iii) Investment Canada Act 'New Business' notification for non-Canadian investors when the new Canadian enterprise falls below the C$1.23 billion WTO threshold (2024/2025 adjustment) - a notification obligation, not an approval gate; and (iv) FINTRAC/sanctions screening as the operational convertibility constraint. Profit remittance is unrestricted once withholding tax is settled; there is no exchange-control moratorium or capital-registration instrument.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist in Canada. A CMRA/mail-scanning service exporter (NAICS 561431) may retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds indefinitely, with zero statutory conversion ratio, zero surrender deadline, and no required sale of export receipts to an authorized dealer. Operational constraints are bank-level only: (i) the financial institution must report International Electronic Fund Transfers (IETRs) to FINTRAC when single cross-border wire transactions exceed C$10,000 in a 24-hour window; (ii) transaction messages must carry accurate beneficiary and remitter information; (iii) if the corporation later collects and forwards third-party funds (money-transfer activity), it would require FINTRAC MSB registration and the Retail Payment Activities Act PSP regime, which this treasury structure should expressly avoid. Export revenue invoiced in USD may be held in a USD demand deposit account and converted solely at corporate discretion; foreign-exchange gains/losses are recognized under section 39 of the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); exercises monetary authority but no administrative foreign-exchange control. The functional FX/treasury regulators and operators for an Alberta corporate are: (i) the Minister of Finance (Canada) under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) for legal tender; (ii) the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for authorized Schedule I banks and their FX dealings; (iii) FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for AML/CTF supervision of reporting entities; (iv) Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) under the Investment Canada Act for inbound capital notification; and (v) Payments Canada for clearing system access. No exchange-control licence, quota, FX allocation, or central-bank trade settlement filing applies to the CAD or to inbound/outbound wire flows.

Stripe Fit: High

Conditional Low-Medium. Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Registered office and operational address presented to the bank is a virtual-office/CMRA suite; FINTRAC and bank policy treat a commercial mail-receiving address as the sole corporate location to be a shell-company indicator.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder funding: route via SWIFT MT103 (or USD/EUR clearing through correspondent banks) to the entity's CAD DDA or USD DDA held at the Alberta branch; beneficiary line must show the registered corporate legal name and the 9-digit CRA Business Number; SWIFT field 70 (Remittance Information) must read 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION / ISSUANCE OF COMMON SHARES'. Purpose classification for the paying bank is 'equity injection / contributed capital'; the receiving bank may call for the executed share-subscription agreement (price per share, series, share count) and the board resolution authorizing issuance. If the shareholder is non-Canadian, file the Investment Canada Act 'New Business' notification with ISED within 30 days of commencement where the enterprise value is below the C$1.23 billion WTO threshold; no approval waiting period applies below that threshold. If the infusion is instead a shareholder loan, execute a written loan agreement with arm's-length interest to avoid deemed-benefit rules (ITA s. 15(2)) and instruct the bank to classify the credit as 'intercompany loan'. Equity contributions are GST/HST-exempt financial services and non-taxable receipts; credit share capital in the corporate ledger and, where foreign currency is converted, retain the bank FX ticket for income-tax and IFRS 9/ASC 815 hedge-designation documentation.Domestic CAD disbursements of C$100,000 and above settle with finality through Lynx (Payments Canada high-value system) using institution/transit/account routing; smaller amounts use the ACSS batch EFT rails or the Real-Time Rail where enabled. Cross-border outbound payments use SWIFT MT103 for values up to approximately C$500,000 equivalent and MT103 with MT202 COV cover above that level; beneficiary details must be complete and accurate (Canadian beneficiaries use institution/transit/account because IBAN is not used in Canada; U.S. and European beneficiaries require account, ABA or IBAN, and BIC). For documentary trade settlement, the Alberta bank's trade desk issues MT700 under UCP600 and ISBP 821 for import or export letters of credit; however, an asset-light ISIC 8299 service model makes commercial LCs inefficient, and a Bank Payment Obligation (BPO) or open account with credit insurance is the recommended substitute. Card rails: Stripe Recurring collects client card payments over Visa/Mastercard/Amex networks and settles in CAD through Stripe's Canadian acquiring bank; treasury should sweep Stripe balances to the corporate DDA daily by EFT to prevent the float from being characterized as third-party funds. Cheques are disallowed for cross-border treasury policy; pre-authorized debit (PAD) via ACSS is the acceptable alternative for recurring Canadian client billing.Deliverable USD/CAD FX forwards (fixed-date and rolling contracts) executed with the Alberta bank's FX desk under ISDA/CSA bilateral terms.FX swaps (USD/CAD) and cross-currency interest-rate swaps used for treasury maturity-matching of capital inflow, dividend outflow, and USD receivables.Vanilla OTC FX options on USD/CAD, including zero-cost collars and participating forwards, offered by Schedule I banks without exchange-execution mandate.Cross-currency swaps (MTM) eligible for IFRS 9 hedge accounting when documented in advance through a hedge designation file.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for counterparty currencies lacking deliverable markets (e.g., BRL, KRW); the CAD is fully deliverable, so USD/CAD NDFs are uncommon and may attract additional OSFI scrutiny.Margin requirements for non-centrally cleared derivatives apply under the Canadian implementation of the BCBS/IOSCO framework (National Instrument 93-101) where the corporation's derivatives exposure crosses the applicable threshold; treasury policy should adopt variation-margin compliance in advance of the threshold.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Conditional Low-Medium. Canada has no EU-style EMI licence class; payment service providers fall under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA, administered by the Bank of Canada, in force November 2024, with PSP registration phased in) and FINTRAC MSB registration when performing money transfer or foreign exchange. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currencycloud) may lawfully serve as an inbound billing corridor for foreign clients, but must not hold CAD settlement accounts inside Lynx/ACSS or act as the Alberta entity's primary treasury account. For an ISIC 8299 'Medium_Risk_Enhanced' profile, Schedule I banks treat sustained offshore-EMI balances as indicia of undisclosed third-party funds; acceptable use is limited to (i) USD receivables from foreign marketplaces, converted and swept same-day via MT103 into the Canadian DDA, and (ii) FX price benchmarking. Stripe Payments is itself a registered PSP/acquirer in Canada; it settles recurring card transactions through its Canadian acquiring bank and does not replace the corporate DDA or disqualify the treasury structure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No absolute statutory dollar threshold; CRA expects a complete Local File and contemporaneous documentation to be prepared for all non-arm's length cross-border transactions where the aggregate annual value exceeds CAD 500,000. A Master File must be prepared if the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000 under the OECD Country-by-Country Reporting rules. Transactions below this threshold may still be challenged if documentation is absent or inadequate.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not enact Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to the UAE. However, corporate residence and permanent establishment determinations under the Income Tax Act and relevant tax treaties require real commercial substance, such as physical premises, employees, sales contracts, and bank accounts. The Canadian GAAR (section 245) may deny treaty benefits where an arrangement is undertaken primarily to obtain a tax benefit without a bona fide business purpose. CRA also applies dividend stripping and attribution rules to personal services corporations. A shell CMRA/virtual office with no Canadian employment activity or decision-making presence will likely be disregarded for treaty purposes and may be subject to full Part XIII tax on deemed dividends.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting (Income Tax Act, Criminal Code)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for up to five years. Civil penalties under section 163(2) (gross negligence) equal to 50% of the understated tax; CRA may also impose regular late-filing penalties and compound interest on unpaid amounts and reassess beyond the standard three-year limitation period due to misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable under section 227.1 for unremitted taxes and withholding amounts; may be subjected to criminal fines and imprisonment; subject to asset freezes, travel bans by the CRA, and extradition from treaty countries if fraudulent conduct is proven.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under section 245 including a 20% penalty on the tax benefit; denial of treaty benefits; deemed dividend treatment for distributions from a foreign affiliate; interest and penalties of up to 50% for gross negligence; CRA may collapse the arrangement and treat all income as Canadian-source business income.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for taxes evaded where the corporate form is considered a sham or a bare façade; courts may pierce the corporate veil; directors face prohibition from serving as officers of any Canadian corporation and travel restrictions for non-payment of assessed amounts.
AML / Proceeds of Crime (CMRA Abuse)

Statutory sanction: Under the Criminal Code (sections 462.31, 462.33) and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), criminal penalties for laundering proceeds of crime include imprisonment up to ten years and fines up to CAD 2 million; FINTRAC administrative penalties may reach CAD 1 million per violation for non-compliance with AML obligations; business licence may be revoked under the Alberta Personal Information Protection Act and other consumer protection statutes.

Director personal liability: Directors can be found personally liable for criminal fines if they authorized, directed, or had custody of the funds; personal restitution orders may be issued to disgorge proceeds; travel bans and seizure orders may be imposed; director disqualification under Canada Business Corporations Act section 118 or Alberta Business Corporations Act for complicity in unlawful conduct.
Unremitted Non-Resident Withholding Taxes

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable for the full amount of unwithheld tax plus daily interest; late-payment penalties up to 20% of the amount not withheld; failure to file information returns (NR4, T4B-NR) results in penalties of CAD 100 to CAD 2,500 per form and up to CAD 25,000 in certain cases; CRA may also impose a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the difference.

Director personal liability: Directors are strictly and personally liable under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act for the entire amount of unremitted withholding taxes plus interest and penalties; resignation after the dividend declaration does not terminate liability; CRA may issue director assessment notices and impose travel bans under the Federal Debt Collection guidelines to block departure from Canada until the debt is settled.
