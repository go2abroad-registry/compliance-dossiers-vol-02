Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Corporations — Corporate Registry Division, Service Alberta; online filing through the Corporate Registry System (CORES) portal.

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act (Alberta), RSA 2000, c B-9 (ABCA) — ss 6 (incorporation), 8 (articles of incorporation), 9 (certificate of incorporation), 10 (commencement of existence), 21 (share consideration), 99 (by-laws), 103 (registered office), 110/111 (corporate records); supplemented by Corporations Information Regulation, Alta Reg 32/2001 and the form requirements under Alta Reg 119/2000.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1 — Articles of Incorporation, together with the initial Notice of Registered Office and initial Notice of Directors forms filed under the ABCA and Alta Reg 119/2000; the Certificate of Incorporation is issued by the Registrar.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital for an Alberta business corporation; a nominal consideration of CAD $1.00 per issued share is acceptable. Shares may be issued for money, property, or past services under ABCA s 21, subject to director valuation documented in the corporate records. No escrow account deposit or paid-up capital block is required for private incorporations.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a municipal building occupancy permit confirming structural load capacity and life-safety code compliance under the Safety Codes Act, RSA 2000, c S-1; satisfy fire safety certification under the Alberta Fire Code (Alta Reg 123/2007), including an approved fire evacuation plan and inspection by the local fire marshal; pass occupational health clearance under the OHS Act, RSA 2000, c O-2 covering hazard assessment, ergonomic workstations, first-aid equipment, and emergency preparedness for employees.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up share capital for an Alberta business corporation; a nominal consideration of CAD $1.00 per issued share is acceptable. Shares may be issued for money, property, or past services under ABCA s 21, subject to director valuation documented in the corporate records. No escrow account deposit or paid-up capital block is required for private incorporations.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED that the initial subscription for shares of the Corporation be approved, the consideration received be accepted, and management be authorized to issue the corresponding share certificates. RESOLVED that the registered office of the Corporation within the Province of Alberta, as described in the Articles of Incorporation, be adopted, and that the registered office record holder be appointed to accept service of process. RESOLVED that the following persons are appointed as officers of the Corporation, to hold office until the next annual meeting or until their successors are chosen: Chief Executive Officer, Chief Financial Officer, and Corporate Secretary. RESOLVED that the by-laws of the Corporation be ratified, and that the officers be authorized to open corporate bank accounts and execute contracts.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — no Alberta industrial licence applies to a digital web-novel platform (ISIC 9000 / NAICS 511130). General workplace authority: Alberta Ministry of Jobs, Economy and Northern Development — Occupational Health and Safety; municipal planning and business licensing division of the local city; CRTC exempt under applicable digital content policies.

Category: No provincial operating or manufacturing permit applies to the subscription web-novel platform; the only permit is the municipal Business Licence issued under the Municipal Government Act, RSA 2000, c M-26, together with the building occupancy permit for the office.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Federal tax authority with exclusive administration of corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and excise levies. In Alberta there is no provincial sales tax administration relevant to this entity (no PST, no provincial corporate tax collection agency; Alberta Corporate Tax is collected by CRA under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement). Primary portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) for BN/program accounts, GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account at www.canada.ca, and the CRA's GST/HST E-Services API for electronic filings.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian-resident Business Corporation obtains a single 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Procedure: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) via the Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES); (2) apply for the BN via Business Registration Online (BRO) or by CRA Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts, within 30 days of the date that a GST/HST account is required or payroll is started; (3) the CRA activates program accounts attached to the BN: 'RC' for corporate income tax, 'RT' for GST/HST, 'RP' for payroll source deductions, and 'RZ' where a single program account is sufficient; (4) the corporation must provide its registered office address, directors' names, and incorporation certificate; a non-resident director is permitted but a resident agent may be required in Alberta; (5) the BN is quoted on all returns, invoices, customs documents, and CRA correspondence. A foreign-owned Canadian corporation files an annual corporate income tax return (Form T2) once its taxation year ends and must disclose cross-border related-party transactions. There is no paid registration fee for the BN; provincial incorporation filing fees apply. The GST/HST account must be opened before the entity makes a taxable supply in Canada in excess of the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold in a single calendar quarter, or before it has remained above CAD 30,000 in any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Business Corporation is a Canadian-resident taxpayer subject to Canadian corporate income tax on worldwide income at the federal corporate rate (15% basic, after provincial abatement) plus Alberta corporate income tax (8% general rate; 2% for Canadian-controlled private corporations eligible for the small business deduction on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income, if applicable). The corporation is a separate legal person: no flow-through to shareholders, no check-the-box election, and no US-style pass-through classification. It may be designated a CCPC based on statutory facts (Canadian-controlled, private, no public shares). Dividends received from taxable Canadian corporations are generally deductible; interest and royalties are subject to withholding tax. Alberta applies a single-rate territorial corporate income tax for general corporations but does not itself impose a goods and services tax sales tax. The entity is outside any federal tax-exempt zone; no offshore 'tax-exempt zone' status is available.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification regime; entity status is determined by the incorporating statute, and hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4, 18.6, and s. 19.8/94) apply mechanically without elective relief. Available statutory elections relevant to a Canadian web-novel platform corporation: (1) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240(3), allowing a small supplier to register and charge GST/HST and recover input tax credits; (2) ETA s. 156 election to make certain intercompany taxable supplies (e.g., between closely related corporations) nil for GST/HST purposes; (3) ETA s. 273 election to file a consolidated single-member GST/HST return for closely related corporations (GST/HST group election); (4) ITA s. 256(2) election to deem a corporation not associated with another corporation to preserve the CCPC small business deduction; (5) ITA s. 89(1.2) or s. 110(3) elections for excluding taxable dividends from a subsidiary; (6) ITA s. 219 election by a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada (not needed for a Canadian-resident issuer); (7) election for a subsidiary-controlled private corporation to treat a taxable dividend as a capital dividend under ITA s. 83(2) if the capital dividend account balance permits. There is no form of 'hybrid mismatch election'; reverse hybrid and imported mismatch rules are self-executing and require careful MNL disclosures in the T2 return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 gross taxable supplies per calendar quarter and over any four consecutive calendar quarters (ETS s. 240(1) small-supplier threshold). Registration for GST/HST is mandatory once the entity exceeds CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or in the trailing four quarters, unless the supplies are exclusively zero-rated exports (in which case registration is not required but is recommended to recover ITCs). A single taxable supply of real property exceeding CAD 30,000 also triggers registration. The platform may voluntarily register even while below the threshold to claim ITCs and to zero-rate its export revenues cleanly.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA s. 23 (supplies of services, intangible personal property) and s. 24 (supplies of goods), a supply of digital web novel content (intangible personal property in the nature of copyright and serialized fiction) made by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident person who is not registered under Part IX of the ETA and who is outside Canada at the time of supply is zero-rated (0% GST). Additional eligibility conditions: (i) the supply is not a 'service rendered to a person in Canada' and no degree of ordinary commercial use occurs in Canada; (ii) the digital content must not be delivered or made available to a person in Canada as part of the same supply; (iii) the supplier must maintain contemporaneous documentation proving the recipient's non-resident status (foreign address, IP-geolocation evidence, app-store territory data, payment settlement from foreign bank cards); (iv) for supplies made through Apple App Store and Google Play Billing, the platform may act as a distribution platform/merchant of record; the Canadian developer must confirm whether the platform is deemed to be the GST supplier for Canadian consumers, in which case the developer's supply to the platform may be zero-rated to the extent the platform is not registered and is non-resident, or subject to GST if the platform is registered and the supply is for consumption in Canada. Zero-rating does not apply to supplies made to Canadian residents, including subscriptions billed to Canadian Apple ID or Google Play accounts; those supplies attract 5% Alberta GST. An election to file for a rebate of GST paid on business inputs (ITCs) can be claimed within 4 years after the end of the tax year in which the input tax credit arose.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / Canadian Export Declaration) - applicable only to physical goods; for pure digital fiction exports transmitted over the internet, no B13A is required, and a written explanation of the intangible nature of the supply should be retained in the export file.
  • Bill of Lading (or electronic equivalent) - for goods-only supply chain; not applicable here because no tangible freight exists; app-store settlement reports and digital delivery server logs replace the bill of lading.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Settlement Record - bank statements and SWIFT payment records showing receipt of funds from non-resident payors in foreign currency, including Apple IAP and Google Play Billing payouts, together with the merchant settlement statements and the platform's tax invoices or withholding documents.

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no pre-clearance e-invoicing regime for GST/HST as of the current year. The CRA accepts electronic invoices and records if they are complete, accurate, and readable; invoices must separately state the GST amount charged (or clearly indicate that GST is included). There is no CRA API mandate for invoice clearance; however, GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE or via EDI if filing above a threshold). Distributors of digital content remain responsible for complete digital books and records, including app-store settlement statements, Apple IAP and Google Play Billing reports, and user geolocation evidence, which must be retained for at least six years on a useable electronic format. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement and maintains no exchange-control law: both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible, and the Canadian dollar floats without parity bands or surrender duties. Profit remittance from an Alberta web-fiction entity is unrestricted at the FX level, but legally material remittance hurdles exist in tax and reporting form: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties (statutory default 25%, reduced by treaty to e.g. 15%/5% for US/UK/Singapore residents and 10% for PRC residents where applicable); (ii) FATCA/CRS due diligence and CRA registration of the entity as a reporting financial institution; (iii) FINTRAC international electronic-fund-transfer reports triggered at CAD 10,000; and (iv) transfer-pricing and anti-avoidance documentation for outbound royalties paid to related foreign authors under section 247 of the Income Tax Act. No central-bank permit, capital-export license, or convertibility declaration is required for dividends, service fees, or equity repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no conversion ratio for export receipts, including digital-content export proceeds from Apple App Store and Google Play. A Canadian exporter may retain USD and other foreign currency in foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks, convert at the account holder's instruction, and reinvest abroad without approval. For income-tax purposes, revenue and inventory must be translated at the Bank of Canada published exchange rates; taxpayers holding specified foreign property above the CAD 100,000 aggregate cost threshold must file Form T1135, and controlled foreign affiliates trigger Form T1134.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Statutory head of foreign-exchange policy and emergency exchange-control authority is the Minister of Finance (Government of Canada) under the Currency Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52, which is exercised through the Bank of Canada as fiscal agent. Banking and settlement supervision is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); cross-border transfers and currency-monetary-instrument reporting are administered by FINTRAC pursuant to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business) are licensed as e-money/payment institutions in their home states, but Canada does not license a domestic EMI class, and such providers are not banks under the Bank Act (Canada); balances are therefore not CDIC-insured. Legal constraints are provider-side: an EMI soliciting Canadian customers or settling CAD liabilities must be FINTRAC-registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) unless a foreign-MSB or bank-partner exemption applies. For this low-risk web-novel treasury, the prudent blueprint is a primary OSFI-regulated bank account with the EMI as a sidecar rail for USD payout conversion, batch micro-royalties to overseas authors, and low-value FX; all FINTRAC reporting, CRA disclosure, and Alberta corporate-record integrity must remain anchored in the Canadian bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate rate (federal 15% + Alberta 8%); 11% combined rate on the first CA$500,000 of active business income for Canadian-Controlled Private Corporations (CCPCs), composed of federal 9% + Alberta 2%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on all dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; the Canadian payer must withhold, remit, and report the tax on NR4 information returns.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) is mandatory where the aggregate of controlled cross-border transactions with a non-resident counterparty exceeds CA$1,000,000 in a fiscal year; the CRA concurrently requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation of a Local File / Master File character under IC 87-2R as a reasonable-efforts defense, and a 10% penalty under subsection 247(3) applies to net upward adjustments where documentation is absent or insufficient.

Economic substance scope: Canada is not an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) jurisdiction in the Caribbean/offshore sense; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act authorizes the CRA to re-characterize any transaction lacking genuine business purpose or economic substance. A web-novel serial platform that licenses content IP to an offshore affiliate without employees, real functions, and decision-making in that affiliate is exposed to GAAR reassessment, treaty-shopping denial, and PE-attribution challenges. The Canadian corporation must maintain substantive operations in Alberta, including development staff, server and data infrastructure, user-operations functions, and board-level strategic control over the core digital business.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting (Income Tax Act, s. 239)

Statutory sanction: Fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded; imprisonment up to five years; gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; CRA is empowered to assess on a net-basis and to place holds on bank accounts and trade receivables.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or direct evasion are jointly and severally liable under civil assessments, may be personally named in criminal proceedings, are subject to departure orders prohibiting exit from Canada while taxes are unpaid, and face asset freezes and possible imprisonment upon conviction.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Income Tax Act, s. 247)

Statutory sanction: CRA re-determination of the arm's-length price; 10% penalty on the net upward transfer-pricing adjustment; daily compound interest on the additional tax; potential GAAR application if the pricing arrangement is a sham or lacks substance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for withholding and non-remitted amounts where T106 documentation is fraudulently withheld; willful participation in a false pricing scheme activates the same civil and criminal exposure as tax evasion, including restitution orders and departure bans.
Shell Company and Artificial IP Migration (GAAR and Treaty-Shopping)

Statutory sanction: GAAR immediate reassessment nullifying the tax benefit, an additional 25% penalty on the tax benefit for abusive transactions that occur after 2019, and denial of treaty reduced withholding rates under the principal-purpose test of tax treaties; CRA may also apply the foreign affiliate rules to impute active business income.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize a sham offshore structure can be held personally liable for the corporation's unpaid Part XIII and Part I taxes where the corporate entity is a mere façade, in addition to fiduciary breach claims and potential fraud convictions with custodial sentences.
AML and Payment-Channel Integrity (PCMLTFA / FINTRAC)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA of up to CA$1,000,000 per violation for a corporation; cease-and-desist orders; suspension or revocation of payment-processing arrangements; referral to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada for criminal money-laundering charges.

Director personal liability: A responsible corporate officer can be separately fined up to CA$500,000 and may be subject to restrictions on acting as a director; directors who authorize or ignore suspicious settlement patterns face personal disqualification orders, asset seizure, and imprisonment of up to five years.
