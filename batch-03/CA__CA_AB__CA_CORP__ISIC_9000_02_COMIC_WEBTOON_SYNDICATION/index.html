Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A corporation must maintain a registered office in Alberta and a records office as required by the Business Corporations Act. A commercial virtual office may be acceptable for statutory mail if it provides a genuine Alberta address, reliable document receipt, authorized access, and records-office functionality. It is not sufficient where it is merely a mail drop, does not permit access to corporate records, or cannot support regulatory, banking, tax, employment, or service-of-process requirements. For digital webtoon publishing and chapter-unlocking activities, no general Alberta industry licence is ordinarily required solely because the business publishes digital comics. The company should nevertheless maintain commercially credible substance, including contractual control of intellectual property, books and records, tax records, decision-making evidence, payment-processor documentation, privacy compliance, and appropriately documented Canadian or foreign operating functions.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Central portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). Provincial counterpart for Alberta: Ministry of Treasury Board and Finance, Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) — Corporate Income Tax administered federally under the Tax Collection Agreement; Provincial payroll and health contribution levies administered separately.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, which simultaneously registers for GST/HST, payroll deductions, corporate income tax, and import/export accounts. Step 2 — For a non-resident corporation, complete and submit Form RC457 (Request for a Business Number (BN) and Certain Related Program Accounts) to the CRA's Non-Resident Corporation registry, attaching certified proof of incorporation, the entity's constating documents, and a completed Form RC447 (Non-Resident Corporation Information Return) for the first fiscal year. Step 3 — Upon BN issuance, the entity automatically holds a corporate income tax account and a GST/HST registrant status if the registration threshold is met. Step 4 — Register with Alberta's Corporate Registry (via the Alberta Corporate Access System, ACAS) for extra-provincial registration, and obtain an Alberta Corporate Tax Number if deemed to have a permanent establishment in Alberta for provincial income allocation. Step 5 — For Malaysian (non-resident) ultimate beneficial owners, file an NR4 information return and withhold Part XIII non-resident tax on any Canadian-source passive income or dividends.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxable entity subject to federal and provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income (if resident) or Canadian-source income (if non-resident). No check-the-box or fiscal transparency election exists under the Canadian Income Tax Act. An Alberta-registered corporation is not a pass-through. Taxable Canadian corporation status triggers full Canadian reporting and transfer pricing obligations. Territorial scope: for a non-resident corporation, Canadian tax applies to taxable Canadian property, Canadian business income, and any capital gains on the disposition of Canadian-situs property under section 115 of the Income Tax Act. Tax-exempt zones: none at federal or provincial level; no free trade zone privilege for GST/HST.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election under Canadian law. Available statutory elections: (a) Section 216 election — a non-resident corporation may elect to file a full Canadian tax return on its Canadian-source property rental income to avoid flat 25% gross-basis Part XIII withholding; (b) Section 216.1 election — a non-resident service-provider corporation may elect to file a Part I return to claim treaty benefits and business expense deductions; (c) Section 104(21) election generally not applicable to corporations; (d) Ontario/Alberta resource-specific elections do not apply absent mining; (e) Hybrid mismatch adjustments under sections 12.2(1) and 18.2/18.3 apply automatically and disallow deductions on hybrid instruments in cross-border arrangements; no elective mechanism exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including taxable e-commerce sales, excluding zero-rated or GST/HST-exempt supplies) over any consecutive four-calendar-quarter period, per subsection 240(1) of the Excise Tax Act. For a non-resident person selling digital products or services to Canadian consumers, the threshold is measured on worldwide sales; if exceeded, mandatory registration for the GST/HST applies. For the Alberta-based digital publishing entity, registration is effectively compulsory effective from the first taxable supply, because its commercial activity in the province exceeds the small-supplier threshold upon the first licensing/royalty invoice.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The Alberta corporation may claim 0% GST/HST on: (1) export of intangible property that is intellectual property rights or trademarks (Schedule VI, Part V, section 12); (2) export of digital goods (e-books, webtoons, digital subscription codes) where the supply is made to a non-resident consumer and the supply is an 'intangible personal property that is not financial services' per section 22 of Schedule VI; (3) services supplied to a non-resident that are performed entirely outside Canada, provided the performance is verifiable — see subsection 7(1) of Schedule VII; (4) translation and syndication services supplied to a non-resident (foreign distributor), if the Canadian corporation does not itself act as an intermediary for a supply to a Canadian consumer. For the webtoon syndication model, the corporation must distinguish: (a) B2B sales of license/royalty rights to a foreign publisher — zero-rated under section 12, Schedule VI; (b) B2C digital chapter unlocking to foreign readers — zero-rated under section 22, Schedule VI provided the recipient is a non-resident individual; (c) B2C sales to Canadian readers through Apple/Google IAP — subject to applicable GST/HST at the standard rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (form B13A — may be electronically filed in the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) for tangible goods; for pure digital services, no customs declaration is required, but an export record is mandatory)
  • Bill of Lading (not applicable to digital supply; substitute: digital delivery records, IP address log repository, download logs, or platform transaction file from Apple/Google showing foreign-country destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of receipt of payment from a non-resident — obtainable from the receiving Canadian bank; CRA accepts bank settlement statements from payment processors or Apple/Google payout confirmation as equivalent evidence)
  • Executed commercial agreement with the non-resident counterparty (license agreement, syndication contract) stating the non-resident's legal address
  • Copy of the issued zero-rated GST/HST invoice with the Schedule VI/Schedule VII exemption reference.

No real-time fiscal clearance, government-issued digital stamp, or continuous-control (CTC) validation regime is in effect for GST/HST in Canada. The CRA permits electronic invoices (e-invoices) and electronic records, but does not mandate API-based clearance or the use of a specific e-invoicing network. For Canadian digital platform sales of webtoon content, invoicing must be issued within 15 days of the end of the month in which the supply occurs, but electronic issuance in PDF or XML is accepted. Relevant fiscal Intermediary System: no foreign tax representative is statutorily mandated, but the CRA may require a non-resident registrant to post security up to CAD 50,000 for a fiscal representative under subsection 240(1.1). For Alberta residents, no security requirement is imposed. Digital service providers that use Apple IAP or Google Play Billing are subject to the CRA's 'gross payment' treatment: the platform (Apple/Google) is deemed to be the supplier, but the operator of the platform may nevertheless be a deemed supplier if it appoints the platform — in the present case, the Canadian entity is the true supplier and must reveal the BN on the platform seller page.

CRA audit scrutiny of export zero-rating claims focuses on evidentiary sufficiency under section 3 of the Excise Tax Act and the 'ordinary course' requirements of the Inward Remittance Certificate. Failure to substantiate a zero-rated supply for which the vendor did not collect GST/HST results in reassessment of uncollected 5% federal tax and 8% Alberta provincial tax (if any), or 13% HST for Canadian destination supplies, plus (a) interest charges under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act at CRA-set rates (approximately 5-7% annually, compounding daily); (b) a negligence penalty of 25% of the tax not collected under section 285(2); (c) a 10% penalty for failure to remit trust funds if made knowingly; (d) for fraudulent export claims (e.g., fabricated foreign-resident documentation), culpable conduct under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act — fines up to 100% of the tax evaded and up to 5 years' imprisonment under subsection 327(2); (e) clawback of all input tax credits that related to the export period for which evidence was not retained, plus a 6% compounding daily interest. Where the exporter failed to repatriate the foreign proceeds within 180 calendar days from the date of the supply, the CRA may reclassify the export as a domestic supply for the foreign entity, impose Part XIII withholding of 25% on the deemed Canadian-source royalty income, and reverse any foreign tax credit claimed in the foreign jurisdiction, subject to the mutual assistance provisions in the Canada-US Tax Treaty and UN Multilateral Convention.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a liberalized foreign exchange regime under the Bank of Canada Act. Current account transactions, including profit remittances, dividend distributions, and trade settlements, are freely convertible at market rates with no prior approval required. Capital account transactions, such as direct equity investments or portfolio flows exceeding CAD 100,000, may trigger review by the Department of Finance or Investment Canada under the Investment Canada Act. For a Canadian-resident corporation, repatriation of earnings is permitted under current account rules without surrender obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canadian exporters are not subject to mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted at prevailing market rates. All export transactions exceeding CAD 2,000 require registration with Export and Import Canada (EIC). FX transactions by residents must comply with Bank of Canada reporting thresholds and FINTRAC AML obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) facilitating cross-border digital payments in Canada are subject to licensing and oversight by the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) and Bank of Canada. Established platforms such as Stripe, PayPal, or Apple/Google payment rails demonstrate medium viability for webtoon syndication merchants provided full compliance with Payment Services Regulation, AML/KYC, and merchant category restrictions. Non-compliant fintechs face license denial or operational suspension.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate rate (15% federal Part I rate + 8% Alberta general corporate rate); 11% combined rate for Canadian-controlled private corporations on first C$500,000 of active business income under the small business deduction

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-residents under ITA s.212; treaty rates may reduce the rate to 15% for portfolio dividends and often 5% where the beneficial owner is a corporation holding at least 10% of the voting shares, subject to the relevant DTA

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate non-arm's length cross-border transactions with each non-resident triggers T106 information return filing; CRA may require contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation for all material intercompany transactions (typically above C$1,000,000) and reasonable TP records supporting the arm's length nature of any cross-border payment

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime like UAE-style jurisdictions, but GAAR under ITA s.245 serves as the economic substance doctrine: an avoidance transaction lacking real business, corporate, or economic substance may be recharacterized and the tax benefit denied. Courts also apply the sham doctrine to disregard artificial corporate arrangements, and DTA benefits are subject to limitation of benefits and the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test where applicable

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239(1): summary conviction fine of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or up to 2 years imprisonment; on indictment, fine of 100%-200% and/or up to 5 years imprisonment; gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; taxes reassessed with compound daily arrears interest

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be jointly and severally liable for unremitted corporate withholdings under ITA s.227.1; courts will pierce the corporate veil in fraud or sham circumstances; principals may face civil restitution, travel bans as bail conditions, and imprisonment upon criminal conviction
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where a gross valuation misstatement occurs and the taxpayer failed to make reasonable efforts to use arm's length pricing; CRA may reassess income upward, deny deductions, and charge arrears interest; T106 late or incomplete filings are subject to additional information-return penalties

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly sign false TP documentation or participate in inflated intercompany pricing risk personal criminal exposure under s.239; if corporate assets are stripped prior to assessment, CRA may pursue shareholders or directors under transaction-based liability rules and veil-piercing equitable remedies
