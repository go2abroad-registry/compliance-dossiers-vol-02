Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a U.S.-style check-the-box election or an entity-classification regime comparable to the U.S. Treasury Regulations. An Alberta limited partnership is, by statute, fiscally transparent for income tax purposes: the LP does not file an entity-level tax return, and Canadian-source income or losses are allocated and taxed to the partners under the Income Tax Act. The partnership may use its partnership agreement to allocate income/remittance amounts, but allocations are subject to the anti-avoidance reallocation rules in section 103 of the Income Tax Act if they artificially reduce tax. There is no Canadian election to treat the LP as a taxable corporation. Any foreign check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election that treats the LP as opaque for foreign tax purposes is subject to Canadian hybrid mismatch rules, including sections 18.7 and 18.8 of the Income Tax Act, and may be overridden to prevent double non-taxation. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is a separate 'person' and may elect to register as a GST/HST registrant; the partnership may also make available elections to have a general partner act as agent in respect of the collection, remittance, and reporting of GST/HST.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. A person other than a small supplier must register for GST/HST if it makes taxable supplies in Canada in the course of a commercial activity. The small-supplier threshold is exceeded when the value of total taxable supplies, including zero-rated export supplies, made in Canada in the current calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters is greater than CAD 30,000. Because the freeze-dried pet food is manufactured in Alberta for export, export revenue counts as taxable supply revenue for this threshold. Voluntary registration is available before the threshold is reached and is advisable for an export-only LP that wishes to claim input tax credits and invoice zero-rated exports at 0% GST/HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property, including freeze-dried raw meat pet food, is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the property is exported by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf as part of a continuous movement to a destination outside Canada. The supplier must be registered for GST/HST and must invoice the export supply at 0% GST/HST. The criteria are: (i) the goods are exported by the supplier 'as soon as may reasonably be practicable' after delivery; (ii) legal and beneficial ownership passes to a non-resident purchaser or foreign customer; (iii) the goods are not consumed, used, processed, or resold in Canada before export; (iv) the completed export shipment, destination, and export declaration are consistent with the commercial invoice; and (v) sufficient documentary evidence is retained for CRA audit. Domestic pet treats are generally taxable supplies in Canada because they are not food for human consumption, but this does not prevent zero-rating of an actual export. If the LP is a small supplier and does not register for GST/HST, it is not required to collect GST on exports, but it is also not entitled to claim input tax credits.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed with the Canada Border Services Agency through the Canadian International Trade Single Window or CBSA-approved Form B13A/electronic export declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing foreign destination, foreign consignee, and matching exported quantities
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds from the overseas purchaser within the commercial payment period

Canada does not operate a national continuous transaction control framework, real-time tax clearance regime, or mandatory digital stamping system for B2B invoices. CRA does not require pre-approved electronic invoices, but all GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing the prescribed particulars under section 223 of the Excise Tax Act and must retain supporting books and records for six years (unless CRA has authorized otherwise). Electronic invoicing in a standard machine-readable format is acceptable. For exported goods, the supplier must report the export to the Canada Border Services Agency using an export declaration filed through the Canadian International Trade Single Window or the CBSA-approved export reporting mechanism; there is no CRA fiscal stamp affixed on the export declaration.

CRA and CBSA jointly audit export zero-rating claims. If the registered exporter cannot substantiate physical export or fails to meet the 'as soon as practicable' or continuous-shipment requirements, CRA will reclassify the supply as taxable in Canada at 5% GST/HST and reassess unremitted GST/HST, with daily compound interest under the Excise Tax Act. Penalties include the 25% gross-negligence or false-statement penalty under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act, repeat-offence penalties, and, in cases of knowing evasion, prosecution that may result in substantial fines and imprisonment. CRA may also claw back input tax credits previously allowed in respect of the export-related inputs and apply the six-year reassessment window for negligence or misrepresentation. Although Canada has no general statutory FX-repatriation deadline, failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days of shipment may be treated by CRA as indicia that no genuine export sale occurred, and exporters should be prepared to produce banking and remittance records to rebut such an inference.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. Canada imposes no general exchange controls, no advance foreign-exchange approval requirements, and no quantitative restrictions on capital outflows or profit remittances. The material regulatory hurdles for an Alberta LP are fiscal rather than exchange-rate based: (i) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposes a 25% withholding tax on certain passive payments, such as dividends, interest, royalties, rent, or management fees, owed to non-residents, subject to treaty relief under an applicable tax treaty; (ii) non-resident partners of the LP earning Canadian business income are required to file Canadian Part I tax returns, and the LP must issue T5013 partnership information returns and allocate income to partners accurately; (iii) all international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to automatic FINTRAC reporting; and (iv) non-resident investments into the LP may trigger registrations or notifications under the Investment Canada Act if the transaction meets the applicable control-in-fact tests or prescribed asset thresholds. Profit remittance remains fully permissible after payment of applicable Part XIII withholding and compliance with source-of-funds documentation requests.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds in Canada. The Canadian dollar is a free-floating currency, and exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in domestic or offshore bank accounts without any obligation to convert them into CAD or repatriate them within a specified period. The only mandatory requirements relate to reporting and anti-money-laundering: exporters must file accurate export declarations with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) where applicable, and any incoming or outgoing international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more is automatically reported by the financial institution to FINTRAC. There are no surrender rules equivalent to those found in emerging-market exchange-control jurisdictions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the central monetary authority responsible for Canada's monetary policy, currency issuance, and foreign exchange reserve management; no separate foreign exchange administration agency exists. Financial conduct and AML supervision are carried out by OSFI and FINTRAC respectively.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer are acceptable for ancillary vendor payments, multi-currency collections, and reduced-spread FX conversions. They are not viable as the primary treasury account because they cannot provide an Alberta-based CAD clearing account, a recognized capital-escrow deposit-taking framework, or documentary letter-of-credit issuance. Legal constraints include the risk that such an EMI may constitute a money services business carrying on activities in Canada, triggering FINTRAC registration and compliance obligations. Additionally, many Canadian chartered banks will not rely on EMI-provided bank references for enhanced due diligence, and EMI funds are generally separated through their own banking partners rather than direct CLS-eligible rails. The recommended architecture is a tier-1 Canadian bank account as the sole liquidity anchor, with an EMI used only for cross-border expense collections and conversion efficiency on non-core flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from foreign partners are routed by SWIFT MT103 from the partner's home bank to the LP's Canadian operating account at a Tier 1 bank. The wire's field 70 or purpose code should reference 'Capital Contribution — Limited Partnership Agreement dated [date]' to avoid any misclassification as revenue. The bank requires the LP's institution, branch/transit, and account number, and it may request a source-of-funds questionnaire and a copy of the capital call notice whenever the amount is CAD 10,000 or more. Compliance verification includes a jointly signed F401 International Electronic Funds Transfer declaration, where applicable, and the bank's internal FINTRAC reporting. The LP must post a journal entry to each partner's capital account and file the required T5013 series. If the incoming funds represent a controlling investment as defined by the Investment Canada Act, the transaction may require notification or review by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada before closing.Outbound trade settlements are executed through the Tier 1 Canadian bank's SWIFT network. For open-account supplier payments, use a SWIFT MT103 wire with the applicable ISO payment-purpose code (e.g., goods purchase) and SWIFT gpi tracking for real-time object-level visibility. For documentary trade, the Canadian bank may issue an irrevocable commercial letter of credit via SWIFT MT700, with payments made against compliant presentation documents; MT710 transfer/assignment is available where the credit is divisible. Same-day CAD settlement uses the Canadian High-Value Payment System (HVPS) under Payments Canada, while cross-currency payments in CAD, USD, and EUR may settle via CLS for the major interbank currencies. All outgoing international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC reporting, and the LP must maintain a documented trade-compliance file with purchase orders, invoices, shipping documentation, customs declarations, and CFIA certificates. Additionally, many Canadian chartered banks will not rely on EMI-provided bank references for enhanced due diligence, and EMI funds are generally separated through their own banking partners rather than direct CLS-eligible rails. The recommended architecture is a tier-1 Canadian bank account as the sole liquidity anchor, with an EMI used only for cross-border expense collections and conversion efficiency on non-core flows.
