Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online system, My Business Account, and Represent a Client portals. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers export declarations and customs compliance. Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration administers certain Alberta provincial tax programs; Alberta has no general provincial sales tax.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian or foreign partnership carrying on business in Canada should obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and the applicable program accounts. The LP should first be legally formed or registered in Alberta, identify its general and limited partners, registered office, Canadian business activities, authorized representative, fiscal period, and non-resident status, and then apply to CRA through Business Registration Online, RC1, or the applicable non-resident registration process. The LP should obtain a GST/HST program account before making taxable supplies when registration is required or elected. A non-resident entity must provide legal formation documents, ownership and control information, foreign tax identification details, Canadian business contacts, and evidence of Canadian commercial activity. CRA may require additional identity verification or documentary review. A partnership generally files a T5013 Partnership Information Return if filing requirements apply; the return does not itself create a separate federal income-tax liability for an ordinary partnership. Each partner reports its allocated share of partnership income, loss, deductions, and credits. A BN and GST/HST account are not equivalent to a personal or corporate income-tax identification number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no general United States-style check-the-box election for an Alberta limited partnership. An ordinary Canadian LP is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes, subject to partnership-status requirements, anti-avoidance rules, specified investment flow-through partnership rules, and special regimes such as the specified investment flow-through trust rules where applicable. The LP may make or participate in specific statutory elections, including elections concerning partnership property, capital cost allowance, resource expenditures, rollover transactions, and GST/HST joint or election-based treatments where legally available. A foreign partner may require treaty residence, entity-classification, hybrid-entity, and beneficial-ownership analysis. Canadian anti-hybrid mismatch rules can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences where foreign treatment creates a deduction/non-inclusion or duplicate deduction outcome. No election converts an ordinary LP into a corporation for all Canadian tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated supplies, measured under the small-supplier rules generally by reference to a single calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. The threshold applies to the person and associated persons under the applicable rules. Registration is generally required once the small-supplier threshold is exceeded and may be elected voluntarily below the threshold. A non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada may be required to register even without an ordinary Canadian establishment, subject to the applicable GST/HST rules and non-resident simplified-registration regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods manufactured in Alberta and exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated at 0% GST when the supplier exports the goods or ships them to a recipient outside Canada in accordance with the Excise Tax Act and its documentary conditions. The supplier must retain evidence that the goods left Canada and that the transaction satisfies the applicable export rules. Goods delivered in Canada are generally taxable even if the purchaser is non-resident, unless a specific export or other zero-rating provision applies. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated only where the statutory exported-service rules are met. The analysis depends on the service type, recipient status, place of performance, connection with real property or tangible personal property, and exclusions for services relating to goods in Canada, litigation, personal services, and other specified categories. GST paid on eligible inputs may be recovered through input tax credits if the LP is registered, the inputs are acquired, imported, or brought into a participating province for commercial activities, and prescribed documentary requirements are met. A non-resident may use the GST/HST rebate mechanisms only where the relevant statutory conditions are satisfied; there is no automatic export refund merely because the supplier is foreign-owned.

  • CBSA export declaration or CERS filing where required by destination, goods classification, value, and applicable exemption
  • Commercial invoice identifying exporter, consignee, buyer, description, quantity, value, currency, origin, tariff classification where relevant, and delivery terms
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including bill of lading, airway bill, or carrier receipt
  • Proof that the goods were exported from Canada, such as carrier confirmation, customs export record, delivery confirmation, or foreign import documentation
  • Purchase order, sales contract, customer correspondence, and accounting records linking the exported goods to the zero-rated invoice
  • Evidence supporting GST/HST treatment and any claimed input tax credits, including tax invoices, import accounting documents, and payment records
  • Inward remittance certificate: not a generally mandatory Canadian tax or customs document. Bank payment records may support commercial substantiation, but Canada does not generally require an inward-remittance certificate as a condition of export zero-rating

Canada has no general nationwide real-time GST invoice-clearance mandate, mandatory government e-invoice network, or universal digital tax stamp for ordinary commercial supplies. Registered suppliers must issue compliant invoices or receipts containing prescribed information, including supplier identity and GST/HST registration number where applicable, description of supplies, consideration, tax amount or applicable tax statement, and invoice date. Electronic invoices are generally acceptable if integrity, authenticity, readability, and record-retention requirements are satisfied. GST/HST registrants must retain books and records, normally for six years from the end of the relevant tax year, subject to extended periods for unresolved objections, appeals, or statutory requests. Commercial exports may require electronic export reporting through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), depending on destination, goods, value, and applicable exemptions.

Canada has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days, and no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline applies to this Alberta LP. CRA may reassess GST where zero-rating is unsupported, goods did not qualify as exported, services failed the exported-service rules, invoices were inaccurate, or input tax credits were overstated. Reassessed tax may include applicable interest and administrative penalties. Gross-negligence penalties can generally be imposed at the greater of CAD 500 and 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit or refund attributable to the conduct. False statements, fraudulent claims, obstruction, or deliberate tax evasion may result in additional penalties and criminal prosecution. Export declarations and customs records may be subject to CBSA verification, administrative monetary penalties under the Administrative Monetary Penalty System, seizure, forfeiture, reassessment of duties and taxes, and other enforcement measures. A GST refund or input tax credit obtained on an invalid zero-rated claim can be recovered retrospectively, with interest and penalties. Records should normally be retained for at least six years, and longer where an audit, objection, appeal, investigation, or statutory requirement remains outstanding.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with fully convertible current and capital accounts. No exchange-control licence, central-bank pre-approval, or onward-repatriation authorization is required for an Alberta limited partnership to receive foreign capital, service imports, or distribute profits to non-resident partners. The operative constraints are fiscal: (i) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, RSC 1985, c 1 (5th Supp.), imposes 25% withholding (treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% or 5% under the Canada-US treaty) on the Canadian-source income allocation of non-resident LP partners, enforced via remittance under subsection 215(1); (ii) the section 216 elective return regime applies where a non-resident partner seeks to reduce withholding on rental or timber income, though manufacturing business income generally remains taxed on the partner's share basis; (iii) the Investment Canada Act, RSC 1985, c 28 (1st Supp.), applies only to acquisitions of control above net-benefit-review thresholds, not to ordinary equity capital contributions from passive limited partners. Cash movements of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC large-currency-transaction and electronic-funds-transfer reporting, and a CA-LP must register with the Alberta Corporate Registry under the Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c P-3 before opening banking facilities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or fixed repatriation timeline exists in Canada. The Alberta LP may lawfully retain USD, EUR, or CNH proceeds in foreign-denominated accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank or through its correspondent network. Mandatory compliance is confined to: (i) FINTRAC large currency transaction reports for cash deposits/withdrawals of CAD 10,000+; (ii) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports for SWIFT/telegraphic transfers of CAD 10,000+ in or out of Canada; (iii) Canada Border Services Agency declaration for physical cross-border transport of currency or monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000. Export documents (B13A export declarations, commercial invoices, bills of lading) must be retained to substantiate the trade-based nature of flows for both FINTRAC and OSFI-regulated bank KYC reviews.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as central bank and monetary authority. Canada operates no dedicated foreign-exchange approval administration; cross-border currency, EFT, and virtual-currency reporting is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, SC 2000, c 17. Prudential supervision of Schedule I chartered banks rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury; Medium only as a supplementary settlement layer for small supplier payouts. Canada does not recognise the EU/UK electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence. An offshore EMI cannot hold CAD deposit balances, issue Canadian payment accounts, or provide escrow/clearing services without a Canadian Schedule I bank licence. A foreign fintech may operate only after: (i) registration as a money services business with FINTRAC; and (ii) registration as a payment service provider with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act, SC 2021, c 23. Registration does not confer deposit-taking powers, therefore the EMI cannot replace a bank for the LP's capital escrow, payroll, tax remittances, or trade-finance obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 for direct partner-to-LP wires in USD or CAD, or MT202/MT210 for CAD cover via a Canadian clearer. Field 50K must identify the ordering partner and ordering bank; field 59 must name the LP exactly as registered with the Alberta Corporate Registry, using the bank account at a Schedule I bank; field 70 must state 'Capital contribution to Alberta limited partnership' with the partnership agreement and subscription schedule reference. Recommended ISO 20022 purpose codes: 'ENTR' (equity investment) or 'CAPH' (capital increase); for intercompany shareholder loans use 'INTC' or 'TLAN'. The receiving bank will verify that the depositor is a registered partner and that the purpose matches the LP's stated business. Verification bundle: executed LP agreement and any amendments; general partner certificate; subscription list and capital account ledger; bank reference from the investing entity; source-of-wealth evidence where amounts exceed CAD 100,000 equivalent.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 for supplier invoices and royalties below USD 500,000 per transaction. Documentary collections (DP/DA) serve as a secondary fallback for repeat suppliers with demonstrated delivery history. A corporate partner allocated Alberta active business income pays 15% federal general corporate tax plus 8% Alberta corporate tax = 23% combined; Canadian-controlled private corporations eligible for the small business deduction pay 9% federal plus 2% Alberta = 11% combined on the first C$500,000 of active business income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate value of reportable cross-border non-arm's-length transactions in a taxation year triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation and the filing of Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, the sham transaction doctrine, and substance-over-form principles. An Alberta LP formed solely to divert e-bike manufacturing profits without real employees, premises, business purpose, or commercial risk will be disregarded or re-characterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Revenue Concealment

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years under Income Tax Act section 239; fines up to 200% of the evaded tax; civil gross-negligence penalty up to 50% of the understated tax plus compound daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct or knowingly participate in evasion are jointly liable for civil restitution, may have the corporate veil pierced, face asset seizures, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment at the arm's length price; penalty under section 247(3) equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5 million and 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue; prescribed interest accrues until paid.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause false transfer pricing statements may be liable under section 163.2 for civil penalties, with veil-piercing exposure to civil restitution and potential travel restrictions.
Part XIII Withholding Evasion

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable to remit the full amount required to be withheld, plus a 10% penalty (20% for gross negligence), compound daily interest, and criminal prosecution in egregious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding under section 227.1; CRA may pursue personal assets, impose travel bans, and obtain civil judgments without the protection of the corporate veil.
AML / Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Obscuring

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under PCMLTFA can reach C$2,000,000 for corporations; criminal money laundering under the Criminal Code carries imprisonment up to 10 years and seizure of proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize laundering, false filings, or structured transactions face criminal liability, travel bans, personal asset forfeiture, and loss of limited-liability protections through shareholder veil-piercing.
