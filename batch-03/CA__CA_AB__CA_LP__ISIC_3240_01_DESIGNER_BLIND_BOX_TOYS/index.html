Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online and My Business Account portal (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services.html); provincial contact is Alberta Treasury Board and Finance (Tax and Revenue Administration) for Alberta corporate tax matters; CBSA for import/export accounts.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Alberta LP must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' (or Business Registration Online). A GST/HST program account (RT) is mandatory once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or the trailing four quarters; voluntary registration is allowed for non-residents carrying on commercial activity in Canada. An Import/Export account (RM) must be opened with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). The LP then files an annual T5013 Partnership Information Return with CRA and issues T5013 slips to resident and non-resident partners. Alberta does not levy a provincial sales tax and imposes no separate partnership income tax return; partners report Alberta-allocated income directly on their own returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Alberta Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act; the LP itself pays no federal or Alberta income tax, and each partner includes its allocated share of income. For GST/HST, however, the LP is a separate 'person' registrant under the Excise Tax Act. Territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source partnership income and are subject to Part XIII withholding on Canadian business income allocations under subsection 212(2).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in Canada. Available elections include: (i) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST74) for small suppliers; (ii) nil-consideration election among closely related registrants (Form GST25); (iii) partnership non-calendar fiscal-period election under subsection 249.1(4) of the Income Tax Act; (iv) simplified GST registration for non-resident e-commerce vendors under the 2021 marketplace rules; (v) OECD hybrid-mismatch attestations and US check-the-box counterpart elections must be aligned to prevent entity-characterization mismatches under Canadian hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or over the trailing four calendar quarters of worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada; the threshold applies to the LP as a person carrying on commercial activity in Canada, and non-resident marketplace sellers with Canadian fulfillment inventory must register regardless of revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Designer blind-box toys (tangible personal property) are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the LP ships or mails the goods to a destination outside Canada, or transfers possession to a common carrier with delivery outside Canada; supplies to a non-resident buyer who removes the goods from Canada as part of the sale also qualify. Design, rotocasting, and airbrush-finishing services are zero-rated when performed wholly outside Canada or supplied to a non-resident not registered for GST/HST where the service relates to goods situated outside Canada. Contemporaneous proof-of-export documentation must satisfy section 10 of the Proof of Export Regulations.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A or electronic equivalent in the Canadian Export Reporting System) filed for each outbound shipment
  • Commercial invoice plus the original Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing the foreign destination and non-resident consignee
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT payment evidence from the non-resident buyer supporting the foreign consideration

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API digital stamping, or government e-invoicing clearance exists in Canada. The CRA operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-based e-invoicing framework for B2B/B2G transactions, with federal procurement e-invoicing being phased in. GST/HST invoices must contain the supplier's legal name and Business Number, invoice date, description, consideration, and the tax rate/amount; zero-rated exports must be clearly identified on the invoice.

CRA may reassess improperly documented zero-rated exports as taxable at 5% GST with arrears interest and penalties. False or omitted statements attract a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 15% of the tax sought to be evaded under Excise Tax Act section 280 (doubled for repeat failures); gross negligence carries a 25% penalty under section 285(1); knowing evasion is an indictable offence under section 281 with fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment for up to five years. Partner-level exposure includes the Income Tax Act section 163(2) gross negligence penalty of 50% of any understated income. Though Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline, adhering to the 180-day settlement benchmark strengthens export documentation and reduces CRA audit challenge risk.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions, adhering to IMF Article VIII obligations. Profit remittance and partnership distributions to non-residents are not subject to exchange controls or withholding on the partnership level for qualifying limited partners, but cross-border remittances equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, and any suspicious transactions, must be reported to FINTRAC. Banks will also require documentary proof of underlying economic substance and beneficial ownership to prevent reverse money laundering risks in connection with outbound profit remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters and trading entities may retain foreign currency in Canadian or offshore bank accounts; however, all export proceeds exceeding CAD 10,000 entering the financial system must be supported by ledger entries, commercial invoices, bills of lading, and B13A export declarations, and corresponding Large Value Transaction Reports must be filed by the financial institution.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (jurisdictional monetary authority) with foreign exchange and AML/CFT oversight jointly implemented through FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for regulated fintech and electronic money institutions that are either registered as money services businesses (MSBs) in Canada with FINTRAC or that operate as partner programs of a Canadian Schedule I bank. Direct onboarding with an offshore EMI lacking Canadian MSB registration is legally constrained under PCMLTFA; the EMI must implement Canadian client identity verification, record-keeping, and suspicious transaction reporting obligations to serve the Albertan limited partnership.
