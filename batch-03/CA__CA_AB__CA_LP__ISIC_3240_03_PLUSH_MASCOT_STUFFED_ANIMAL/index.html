Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at cra.gc.ca; Alberta corporate and provincial tax is administered by CRA under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement (Alberta has no provincial sales tax)

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST account if it makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier; registration is available online through BRO, by mail using Form RC1/RC165, or by remote non-resident registration; separate import/export program (RM) account is required for customs clearance; no Alberta provincial tax ID is required

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes, with each partner taxed on their share; however, the partnership is a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes and is required to register for GST/HST when carrying on a commercial activity in Canada unless the small-supplier threshold applies); Territorial_Scope: federal GST and Alberta corporate income tax apply; no Alberta retail sales tax — A foreign limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST account if it makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier; registration is available online through BRO, by mail using Form RC1/RC165, or by remote non-resident registration; separate import/export program (RM) account is required for customs clearance; no Alberta provincial tax ID is required

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes, with each partner taxed on their share; however, the partnership is a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes and is required to register for GST/HST when carrying on a commercial activity in Canada unless the small-supplier threshold applies); Territorial_Scope: federal GST and Alberta corporate income tax apply; no Alberta retail sales tax

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box; under the Income Tax Act (Canada) a limited partnership can make elections on fiscal period (s. 249.1), Canadian partnership status (s. 102), and partner allocation; under Excise Tax Act the partnership may file an election under s. 156 with closely related corporations to treat supplies as nil where applicable; hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss. 95 and 247 may disallow unintended deductions

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – the small-supplier threshold based on gross worldwide taxable supplies (including associated entities) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents carrying on business in Canada also register when exceeding this threshold unless making only zero-rated supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Taxable supplies of goods are zero-rated when exported from Canada in accordance with Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act (delivered outside Canada or transferred to a common carrier for direct export); services to non-residents may be zero-rated when they are performed in Canada in relation to exported goods or for the benefit of a non-resident who is not registered and the service relates to real property outside Canada

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A/G13, filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System, or EDI confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading or waybill showing final destination outside Canada and actual shipment date
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer record or foreign buyer payment evidence evidencing settlement in foreign currency

No federal real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; invoices must contain prescribed GST/HST information (CRA GST/HST Info Sheet 10-11); CRA is progressively adopting Peppol-compliant e-invoicing on a voluntary basis; returns and payments must be filed electronically for annual registrants above CAD 1.5M threshold

CRA may deny zero-rating and claw back input tax credits if substantiation is insufficient; false or misleading statements attract gross-negligence penalties of up to 25% of the net tax amount, daily interest charges, and the exporter may be liable under ETA s. 280; deliberate evasion is subject to criminal prosecution, fines up to CAD 100,000, and imprisonment

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. There are no general exchange controls, no repatriation permits, and no prior central bank approval for profit remittance, dividend distributions, management fees, or loan repayments. Non-resident limited partners are subject to Canadian withholding tax on certain partnership allocations and distributions under the Income Tax Act, particularly where the partnership derives Canadian-source income that is not attributable to a permanent establishment; ERPA and partnership reporting obligations apply. Treaty relief may reduce withholding rates. No significant impediments exist for a plush toy OEM structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within any timeline. Export receipts may be maintained in foreign currency accounts. FINTRAC reporting obligations apply to electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and the CRA requires all export revenue to be recognized on a functional currency basis under Canadian GAAP. No mandatory conversion ratio exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) is the designated AML/ATF regulator and receives cross-border electronic fund transfer reports. Canada does not operate a centralized Foreign Exchange Administration; the regime is liberalized under IMF Article VIII obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as an ancillary disbursement and FX conversion rail, Low as the primary operating treasury account. Offshore digital EMIs are not authorized to provide full CAD clearing or Bank of Canada settlement services unless they hold a Canadian money services business (MSB) license and are registered with FINTRAC as a money service business and, where applicable, comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act. Unlicensed EMIs cannot issue Canadian payment accounts, transfer funds through the Canadian clearing system, or support commercial letters of credit. Use a Schedule I Canadian bank for the primary settlement account and limit fintech EMIs to vendor payout facilitation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and loan injections to the Alberta LP are executed via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident parent company's bank. The wire must clearly reference the beneficiary Alberta LP bank account, include the full legal entity name, and provide a purpose code matching the BPM6 direct investment classification ('Equity Capital' or 'Loan/Intercompany Debt' as applicable). The Canadian bank will require supporting board resolutions, copies of the parent remittance order, and a Beneficial Ownership Report. All incoming electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC through the EFT reporting system. Physical gold or commodity escrow structures are neither necessary nor recommended in Canada.Trade settlements with overseas fabric and accessory suppliers are handled through SWIFT MT103 direct remittances and commercial letters of credit issued by the Canadian commercial bank under UCP600. Outbound EFTs over CAD 10,000 must also be recorded and transmitted through FINTRAC reporting systems. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services transactions; CAD 5 million for other transactions (e.g., goods, intangibles) – if exceeded, the partnership must prepare a Local File; Master File required if total revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the 'substance over form' doctrine apply; partnerships must demonstrate real economic activity (e.g., employees, premises, functions) to avoid recharacterization of transactions and to qualify for treaty benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in the evasion; travel bans and seizure of personal assets may be imposed
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reassessment of income, and penalties under GAAR (s. 245): 25% penalty on the tax benefit; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the tax benefit denied if they were the architects of the arrangement; possible injunctions and asset freezes
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals

Director personal liability: Directors and partners can be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly failed to implement compliance programs; travel bans and extradition may apply
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income, penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation incomplete), and interest; double taxation if not corrected

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they signed false or misleading documents; potential criminal charges for tax evasion
