Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement exists. Contributions to the limited partnership must be money or property; services are not a permitted contribution under s. 8 of the Partnership Act (Alberta). Contributions are governed by the executed Limited Partnership Agreement and should be credited to a dedicated partnership escrow/operating account maintained at a Canadian Schedule I bank; at law, a limited partner may withdraw capital only to the extent the withdrawal does not reduce that partner's share below the total of all prior agreed contributions and profits not yet distributed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); central portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/business-registration-online; non-resident applications are handled by the CRA International Tax Services Office, Ottawa; Alberta provincial tax compliance for the LP is also administered by the CRA under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement, while the LP's legal formation records are filed separately with the Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the Alberta LP is required to register for GST/HST — registration is mandatory for a partnership carrying on business in Canada with taxable supplies exceeding C$30,000 in the current and preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a foreign-owned LP at or below the threshold may voluntarily register to obtain input tax credit recovery. Step 2: Apply for a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) with the GST/HST program account code RT using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), submitted online via BRO, by mail, or by fax; non-resident partners filing on behalf of the LP should use the CRA International Tax Services Office intake channel. Step 3: The partnership registers under its own legal name as a distinct 'person' for GST/HST purposes; a designated representative partner must be identified, and all general partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST obligations under ETA s. 272.1. Step 4: After issuance of the BN, the LP files GST/HST returns under the RT account, issues invoices showing the BN, and maintains partner details for the T5013 Partnership Information Return, including each partner's foreign tax identification number for CRS reporting. Step 5: For partners that separately carry on Canadian activities, each such foreign entity must obtain its own BN following the same RC1 procedure; passive limited partners without Canadian business activity generally need no BN but must provide their home-country TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For Income Tax Act purposes the LP is fiscally transparent: it is not a taxable entity, and each partner, whether resident or non-resident, reports its allocated share of the partnership's Canadian-source income in Canada. Non-resident partners are taxable only on the income allocated from the LP's Canadian business under ITA s. 2(3), s. 96 and s. 115, subject to treaty relief. For GST/HST the LP is a separate 'person' that must register and remit net tax, but the partners remain jointly and severally liable for unpaid amounts. Territorial scope: residents are taxed on worldwide income while non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source income; no entity-level corporate income tax applies to the LP itself. Alberta has no tax-exempt zone for this activity.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; the pass-through classification of an Alberta LP is mandatory. Available statutory elections and designations relevant to the LP include: (1) a partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) permitting a non-calendar taxation year in limited circumstances; (2) the GST/HST closely-related election under ETA s. 156 to treat taxable supplies between the LP and a closely related registrant corporation as made for nil consideration; (3) the GST/HST joint-venture election under ETA s. 273(2) designating one participant as operator; and (4) the designation of a representative member under ETA s. 273.1 to file and remit GST/HST on behalf of the partnership. Canada applies mandatory OECD hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4 to 18.6, s. 12(2.5), s. 20(1)(p.1)) effective for 2022/2023 with no elective opt-out; a hybrid entity partner such as a US LLC may trigger deduction denial or reverse-hybrid deemed-dividend treatment under ITA s. 94, and restructuring is the only mitigation. A US Form 8832 election may alter US characterization of the LP but has no effect on Canadian tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 small-supplier threshold: a person, including a partnership, whose total world-wide taxable supplies, inclusive of zero-rated supplies, in the current calendar quarter and the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters do not exceed C$30,000 is exempt from mandatory registration; a non-resident is measured only on taxable supplies made in Canada while carrying on business in Canada. Once the threshold is exceeded, the LP must register within 29 days of ceasing to be a small supplier; a foreign-owned LP with no Canadian business presence may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: sales of tangible personal property (printed collectible trading cards) are zero-rated where (a) the supplier exports the goods as part of the supply before they are used or enjoyed in Canada, with acceptable evidence retained (ETA s. 23 and Schedule VI, Part V, item 1); or (b) the supply is made to a non-resident recipient who takes delivery in Canada and exports the goods as soon as practical afterwards, generally interpreted administratively as within 60 days of delivery (Schedule VI, Part V, item 2). Services: printing, holographic foil stamping, and finishing services supplied to a non-resident principal for goods delivered outside Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, item 7 as a supply of service to a non-resident, provided the service does not relate to Canadian real property and is not a personal service rendered in Canada; the wholly-outside-Canada rule does not apply because production occurs in Alberta. The LP must hold a complete export evidence package for each shipment; without substantiation the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST. Supplies to Canadian residents, Canadian GST registrants, and sales delivered to foreign fulfillment centres without valid export documentation do not qualify.

  • Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) export declaration fiscal transaction and CBSA control code, which replaced the former B13A Customs Export Declaration for electronic filing of export data through CERS-certified service providers
  • Commercial invoice and packing list showing the supplier BN, invoice date, full product description, HS classification of the trading cards (typically HS 4911.10 printed collectables or HS 9504.40 playing cards), quantity, weight, declared value, terms of sale, and the identity of the non-resident buyer
  • Transport document — original bill of lading (ocean), airway bill (air), or rail/highway cargo control document evidencing physical movement from Canada to the foreign destination and naming the LP as exporter or shipper
  • CBSA eManifest cargo control number and customs release confirmation demonstrating that the goods left Canada and were not diverted to the domestic market
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 / credit advice issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency consideration from the non-resident buyer, converted at the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate; this is documentary best practice because the ETA conditions zero-rating on proof of export rather than on repatriation

Canada does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or tax-authority invoice validation system comparable to Latin American CTC regimes. Mandatory requirements are: (1) every GST/HST registrant must issue a sales invoice containing the supplier's name, BN, date of supply, description of goods/services, and tax charged (ETA s. 221(1) and the Information Respecting GST/HST Registrants Regulations) sufficient to support the recipient's input tax credit; export invoices should state that the supply is zero-rated under Schedule VI of the ETA. (2) GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding C$1.5 million must file returns electronically via CRA-certified GST/HST NETFILE or EDI software; CRA performs automated assessment in near real time, but this is not a pre-clearance of invoices. (3) The CRA participates in the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoice framework on a voluntary basis only; no API clearance or digital stamp is required. (4) Paper and electronic records are equally valid for audit, but electronic records must be readable and convertible to CRA-accessible formats on request.

The CRA audits export zero-rating claims by cross-matching CERS customs data with GST/HST returns, examining the timeliness of export against the 60-day 'as soon as practical' administrative rule, verifying the non-resident status of the buyer, and inspecting the supporting transport and remittance records. An unsubstantiated or fraudulent zero-rating claim is reassessed at 5% GST with compound daily arrears interest at the quarterly prescribed rate (approximately 9-10% per annum in 2025). Civil penalties for false statements or omissions equal the greater of C$250 and 25% of the net tax understated (ETA s. 285), extend the normal four-year reassessment window to seven years if gross negligence is established, and carry no limitation period for fraud. On conviction for wilful evasion, the ETA s. 327.1 penalties include a fine of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. The CRA may also demand security, impose a 10% failure-to-remit penalty, and assert joint and several liability against the general and limited partners, in proportion to their partnership interests and within the limits of ETA s. 272.1, for any unremitted GST/HST of the LP. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds; the 180-day figure in this dossier is a recommended record-retention benchmark for remittance evidence because GST zero-rating rests on proof of physical export rather than on the timing of repatriation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally require approval for foreign investment, repatriation of contributed capital, dividend distributions, partnership allocations, loan proceeds, or liquidation proceeds. A Canadian limited partnership may receive and remit foreign currency through regulated financial institutions, subject to bank due diligence, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering requirements, tax withholding, transfer-pricing support, partnership agreement restrictions, and documentary verification of the commercial purpose. Payments to non-residents may require Canadian withholding tax analysis, including potential withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act where the payment is interest, royalties, management fees, certain services, or other prescribed income. Treaty relief generally requires valid residency and beneficial-ownership documentation. Cross-border related-party financing must be reviewed for thin-capitalization, transfer-pricing, interest-deductibility, withholding-tax, and debt-versus-equity issues. An Alberta limited partnership should maintain separate records for partner contributions, distributions, foreign-exchange gains and losses, and partner-level tax allocations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no general statutory deadline for converting export receipts into Canadian dollars and no mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign-currency receipts in Canadian or offshore accounts, subject to ordinary banking controls, tax reporting, customs documentation, sanctions compliance, and contractual obligations. Foreign-currency accounts may be subject to Canadian income-tax and accounting treatment for exchange gains and losses. Large or unusual international transfers may trigger enhanced due diligence and transaction-reporting obligations. Cross-border monetary instruments or currency movements may also be subject to Canada Border Services Agency reporting rules, including declaration requirements for currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more when physically imported into or exported from Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing exchange controls, mandatory currency conversion, or capital-account approval requirements. Relevant parallel authorities include the Department of Finance Canada, the Canada Border Services Agency, the Canada Revenue Agency, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection and routine commercial payments, subject to provider eligibility, Canadian and foreign regulatory perimeter analysis, settlement geography, and merchant underwriting. Canada does not treat every payment service provider as a bank, and an offshore electronic-money institution cannot assume that its services are available to a Canadian Alberta limited partnership without regulatory analysis. A provider carrying on payment activities in Canada may be required to register under the Retail Payment Activities Act and comply with Bank of Canada operational-risk and safeguarding requirements when the applicable regime is in force. Foreign providers may also be subject to foreign licensing, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, consumer-protection, data-transfer, and account-closure risks. Stripe and Authorize.Net availability depends on the legal contracting entity, Canadian merchant eligibility, beneficial-owner screening, settlement-account location, prohibited-business policies, chargeback profile, and card-network rules. The LP should retain a Canadian bank account as primary treasury infrastructure and use fintech providers as collection or payment sub-accounts rather than as the sole liquidity rail.

Use an accurate wire purpose description such as partner capital contribution, limited-partner loan, general-partner loan, or intercompany financing; Canada does not impose a universal statutory purpose-code system for ordinary commercial wires, but the sending or receiving bank may require internal purpose codes and supporting documents. For related-party loans, document principal, currency, maturity, interest, repayment terms, arm's-length support, withholding-tax treatment, and transfer-pricing rationale. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Alberta Limited Partnership entity level (full flow-through; the partnership is not a taxpayer). Corporate general/commercial partners resident in Canada bear combined federal + Alberta corporate income tax at 23% headline (15% federal Part I general rate + 8% Alberta general rate); qualified Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) benefit from an 11% combined small business rate (9% federal + 2% Alberta) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income per annum (subject to the CAD 15,000,000 taxable capital limit). Individual partners bear personal income tax on allocated income; non-resident corporate partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP are taxable under Part I on Canadian-source business income attributable to the Alberta permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on any dividend paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Note: distributions from an Alberta Limited Partnership are not dividends - partnership income allocations flow through and are taxable in the hands of partners under Part I, with no additional Part XIII withholding at distribution. If profits are subsequently interposed through a Canadian corporation that pays an actual dividend to a non-resident parent, the statutory 25% Part XIII rate applies, subject to treaty reduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed for any taxation year in which the aggregate value of reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000. Canada has no safe-harbour dollar threshold for contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation - the CRA requires 'reasonable efforts' documentation (local file / master file equivalent) for every non-arm's-length cross-border transaction. The s.247 documentation penalty (10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment) is only applied where the aggregate adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; a Country-by-Country (CbC) report is mandatory for multinational enterprise groups with consolidated annual revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, s.245 of the Income Tax Act), the sham doctrine, and substance-over-form jurisprudence require that the Alberta LP carry on genuine commercial activity with real employees, physical premises, capital at risk, and active management decisions. An Alberta limited partner may not participate in the control or management of the partnership without forfeiting limited-liability protection under the Alberta Partnership Act; a corporate general partner must hold substantive assets. A conduit arrangement using a mailbox Alberta LP to route trading-card royalties or printing profits to a no-tax jurisdiction, without economic substance in Alberta, will be recharacterized by the CRA - the tax benefit is denied, the arrangement is reassessed under GAAR, and penalties for gross negligence (50% of the recaptured tax) can apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act s.239)

Statutory sanction: On indictment, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years; on summary conviction, a fine up to CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months. Civil gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) is CAD 100 or 50% of the understated tax, whichever is greater, plus arrears interest at the prescribed rate + 4% compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and senior managers who direct or participate in the evasion are personally assessed under s.227.1 (for unremitted payroll/GST withholdings) and s.160 (transfers at undervalue to non-arm's-length persons); the corporate veil is pierced for fraud - personal restitution orders, CRA travel bans under the Income Tax Act enforcement regime, and imprisonment up to 2 years on indictment.
Sham Partnership / Conduit Entity (GAAR section 245)

Statutory sanction: GAAR disallows the entire tax benefit; the LP may be recharacterized as a deemed corporation, causing Part XIII dividend withholding at 25% on deemed distributions, reassessment of the full Canadian-source income against the foreign parent, and a 50% gross negligence penalty; a promotion penalty of CAD 500,000 applies to persons who promote a GAAR avoidance plan.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner and the foreign promoters are jointly and severally liable for the reassessed tax and penalties; the CRA may issue a compliance order, seize personal assets, and impose travel bans pending payment; criminal prosecution as accessory to tax evasion is available where the conduit was knowingly used to conceal a true principal.
Transfer Pricing / Non-Arm's Length Mispricing (Income Tax Act s.247)

Statutory sanction: Full reassessment of the transfer pricing adjustment plus a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (where the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue and no reasonable-efforts documentation exists); T106 failure penalty of CAD 100 per month of non-filing, maximum CAD 2,500; associated interest from the reassessed year.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the T2/T5013 with knowledge of the mispricing are jointly and severally liable for the 10% penalty under the directors' liability provisions and can be pursued for the unremitted assessed tax; in a fraudulent scheme, personal civil restitution to the CRA and criminal prosecution under s.239 are available with imprisonment up to 2 years.
Unremitted Withholdings - Part XIII and Source Deductions (s.215, s.227.1)

Statutory sanction: An unwithheld amount is immediately assessable against the partnership or payer (s.215); failure to deduct/remit triggers a 10% penalty on the amount (20% where the failure was knowing or grossly negligent) plus arrears interest at the prescribed rate + 4% corporate markup; the withheld amount is held in a deemed trust for the Crown (s.227(4)) with super-priority over all other creditors.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the full unremitted amounts (s.227.1) where they knew or should have known of the failure and the amounts are uncollectible from the entity; the CRA may assess directors personally, file liens against personal assets, obtain travel bans, and pursue criminal liability where deliberate suppression of withholdings is proven.
Anti-Money Laundering / FINTRAC Compliance (PCMLTFA and Criminal Code s.462.31)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for corporations (higher for aggravated repeat violations) imposed by FINTRAC; criminal prosecution for laundering proceeds of crime under Criminal Code s.462.31 is punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years for individuals and an unlimited corporate fine; goods-involved in the offence are subject to forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors who knowingly or recklessly permit AML program failures are personally liable - FINTRAC penalties are assessed against the entity but the CRA and FINTRAC pursue directors of a directing mind where wilful blindness is shown; criminal convictions trigger automatic travel bans under Canadian criminal law, asset freezing orders, and personal confiscation orders; the corporate veil does not protect a directing mind in a laundering offence.
