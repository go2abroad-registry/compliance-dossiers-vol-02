Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). Alberta corporate and personal income tax is administered by the CRA on behalf of Alberta Treasury Board and Finance under the federal tax collection agreement. Alberta has no provincial sales tax (no PST/HST provincial component), so only the federal GST program applies.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Alberta Limited Partnership with the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or CRA's business registration online service, quoting the Alberta partnership registration number; (2) open the GST/HST program account ('RT') at the same time, mandatory once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters or voluntarily to claim ITCs and zero-rate exports; (3) file the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return and issue T5013 slips to all partners, including non-resident partners; (4) non-resident partners with a Canadian permanent establishment must obtain their own BN/assessment reference and file Canadian Part I income tax returns; (5) register as an exporter with the Canada Border Services Agency through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) to transmit export declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - the LP is fiscally transparent for income tax (ITA s. 96): no entity-level Part I tax; each partner is allocated income/loss per the partnership agreement and taxed on that share, including non-resident partners on Canadian-source business income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment (which the Alberta keyboard-cap manufacturing plant will normally constitute). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the LP is fiscally transparent for income tax (ITA s. 96): no entity-level Part I tax; each partner is allocated income/loss per the partnership agreement and taxed on that share, including non-resident partners on Canadian-source business income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment (which the Alberta keyboard-cap manufacturing plant will normally constitute). For GST/HST purposes the partnership is a separate legal 'person' (ETA s. 123(1)) and registers in its own name; no Canadian tax-exempt zone applies.

Available election forms

(a) Partnership fiscal-period election (Form T1139, ITA s. 249.1) to adopt a non-calendar year-end with CRA concurrence; (b) non-resident partner optional net-basis election for Canadian-source rental/timber income (ITA s. 216(1)); (c) GST/HST joint election (ETA s. 156) for supplies between a qualifying partnership and a closely related corporation to be deemed made for nil consideration, preserving ITC eligibility; (d) Quick Method of GST/HST accounting election (Form GST74, ETA s. 227) for eligible small businesses with revenue under CAD 400,000; (e) Canada does not recognize the US check-the-box regime; hybrid mismatch arrangements are neutralized by ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3, which deny or defer deductions where the same payment is deductible in Canada and not included in the payee's foreign jurisdiction, with no elective out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in Canada over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold, ETA s. 148). Registration is mandatory within 29 days of exceeding the threshold (ETA s. 240(2)); voluntary registration is available to small suppliers to claim ITCs and zero-rate exports. A non-resident LP with a Canadian permanent establishment uses the standard registration regime; the simplified non-resident GST/HST regime is limited to digital/intangible supplies and does not cover physical goods such as keycaps.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported tangible personal property under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1: the Alberta LP must transfer possession of the custom mechanical keycaps to the customer or a common carrier, ship the goods to a destination outside Canada as soon as is reasonable in the ordinary course of business, and retain prescribed proof of export (ETA s. 221) plus a zero-rated commercial invoice. A zero-rated supply of goods is also available where the goods are delivered outside Canada by the vendor or at the vendor's risk. Services relating to tangible personal property performed for a non-resident (Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7) are zero-rated only where the property is not in Canada during the service; this is not the primary route for finished keycap inventory. Domestic Canadian sales remain subject to 5% GST.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) / Form B13A transmission (mandatory for goods departing Canada by land, air or water where the value exceeds CAD 2,000 or the goods are controlled; courier low-value shipments may rely on the courier's electronic manifest).
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - through-carrier document evidencing transportation from Canada to a named foreign destination.
  • Commercial Invoice - itemized keycap description, foreign purchaser identification, unit values, destination, and express statement 'zero-rated supply - exported goods under ETA Schedule VI Part V'.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or Shopify-Payments/Stripe settlement statement - evidencing receipt of payment from the foreign buyer.
  • Packing List and Freight Forwarder's Certificate of Export - supplementary proof satisfying CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17-5-4 ('Exports - Proof of Export') requirements.

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory XML-to-government e-invoicing, API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. CRA supports voluntary Peppol-compliant e-invoicing through 'the Exchange' network; electronic invoices are accepted without pre-authorization. Mandatory obligations are: issue and retain valid GST/HST invoices (ETA s. 223) showing supplier BN/RT account, recipient, invoice date, description, applicable rate and tax, or for zero-rated exports an explicit zero-rating statement; mandatory e-filing of GST/HST returns for registrants above prescribed revenue thresholds (generally CAD 1.5M) and mandatory e-filing of T5013 partnership returns for larger partnerships; records retained for six years.

If the export evidence is not produced within a reasonable time (CRA administratively benchmarks receipt and export within 180 days of the supply; Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline), the CRA will deny zero-rating and reassess 5% GST on gross sales, plus compound daily prescribed-rate interest from the original filing due date. Civil penalties include ETA s. 280(1) ($100 per false statement or failure) and ETA s. 285(1) gross negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax; under the Income Tax Act, s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty is 50% of the understated tax (minimum CAD 100). Criminal prosecution for evasion carries fines of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to two years on indictment (ETA s. 327; ITA s. 239); general partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's net tax and penalties under ETA s. 272. Reassessment limitation periods are four years from the filing due date (ETA s. 298(1); ITA s. 152(4) three years normal/four years for unreported income), with no time limit where the taxpayer made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free convertibility regime for both current and capital accounts under the Bank of Canada Act. Capital account transactions including profit remittances, equity injections, and cross-border investments are permitted without prior regulatory approval for residents and non-residents. Profit repatriation faces no material hurdles beyond standard Canadian tax withholding at source and reporting obligations under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act. All FX conversions must comply with FINTRAC AML/CTF requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory surrender to the Bank of Canada. Exporters may freely retain, hold, or convert foreign exchange earnings for operational use or repatriation. No statutory timeline mandates conversion of export receipts. All export transactions require accurate commercial documentation, Incoterms compliance, and FINTRAC reporting. Funds may remain in foreign currency accounts without forced conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Generally not applicable at the Alberta limited partnership level because a Canadian limited partnership is ordinarily fiscally transparent. Allocated business income is taxed to partners. For an Alberta-controlled private corporation, the combined federal and Alberta general corporate rate is 23% (15% federal plus 8% Alberta); the combined small-business rate is generally 11% (9% federal plus 2% Alberta) on qualifying active business income within the applicable limit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for taxable dividends paid or credited to a non-resident, subject to treaty reduction. A limited partnership generally distributes partnership draws or allocations rather than corporate dividends. Payments by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder may qualify for treaty rates, commonly 5% where the beneficial owner is a qualifying corporate shareholder meeting the ownership threshold under the applicable treaty, and 15% in other treaty cases; the Canada-U.S. treaty commonly provides 5% for a qualifying 10% voting-interest corporate shareholder and 15% otherwise.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form T5013 is generally due within 5 months after the partnership's fiscal period end. Partnership information slips, including T5013 slips, are generally due to partners and the CRA by the same statutory deadline. The partnership's fiscal period generally cannot exceed 12 months and must comply with Canadian fiscal-period rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with non-residents. A generally applicable exemption may apply where the total value of reportable non-arm's-length transactions, excluding certain transactions such as specified compensation, does not exceed CAD 1,000,000 for the year. Form T106 reporting has a separate CAD 1,000,000 threshold framework. Canada does not operate a simple universal local-file/master-file threshold expressed solely in Canadian dollars. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational enterprise groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million in the preceding fiscal year. A Local File and Master File may be required under OECD-aligned rules where applicable to the Canadian reporting entity and group.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone general Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the UAE ESR rules. Canadian tax outcomes are tested through the arm's-length principle, the general anti-avoidance rule, specific anti-avoidance provisions, beneficial-ownership and treaty-entitlement principles, thin-capitalization rules, foreign-affiliate rules, interest-limitation rules, and judicial substance-over-form analysis. A partnership structure must have genuine commercial activity, credible personnel and decision-making, appropriate assets and risks, accurate books, and commercially supportable allocations. Treaty benefits may be denied under the principal-purpose test or other treaty anti-abuse provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties may include gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax or improperly claimed credit, interest, reassessment, and limitation-period extension. Tax evasion may result in prosecution, fines generally ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, and imprisonment of up to 5 years under the Income Tax Act.

Director personal liability: A director may face criminal prosecution where personally involved. A general partner may be personally liable for partnership debts and taxes under applicable partnership law. Limited-partner protection may be lost or weakened through management conduct, guarantees, fraud, or statutory liability. Directors may also face derivative civil claims and director-liability assessments for certain unremitted source deductions and GST/HST.
Failure to Remit Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: The CRA may assess the unremitted amount, interest, penalties, and in serious cases prosecution. Part XIII and source-deduction remittance penalties can be material and may increase for repeated or intentional failures.

Director personal liability: Directors can be assessed personally for certain corporate source deductions and GST/HST under director-liability provisions where statutory conditions are met. General partners may be directly exposed for partnership obligations. Resignation does not automatically eliminate liability for periods during which the individual was a director or responsible person.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: The CRA may adjust income under the arm's-length rules, impose transfer-pricing penalties generally equal to 10% of the adjustment where the statutory conditions are met, deny deductions, assess withholding tax, and charge interest. Repeated non-compliance may trigger audit expansion and anti-avoidance analysis.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may face civil claims for breach of duty, negligent tax governance, or knowing participation in improper transactions. Criminal liability requires proof of the relevant offence and personal participation, knowledge, or authorization.
Shell Company and Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: The CRA may deny deductions, recharacterize transactions, apply the general anti-avoidance rule, assess taxes and penalties, deny treaty benefits, and refer suspected fraud or money laundering for prosecution. Corporate registration does not establish tax residence, beneficial ownership, or commercial substance.

Director personal liability: The general partner and individuals controlling the arrangement may face civil restitution, fraud-related claims, criminal fines, forfeiture, and imprisonment. Limited liability is not a shield for fraud, personal guarantees, unlawful distributions, or direct participation in misconduct.
Beneficial Ownership and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The payer may be liable for the full 25% domestic withholding rate, arrears, interest, penalties, and reassessment. Treaty benefits may be denied under beneficial-ownership principles, limitation-on-benefits rules, or the principal-purpose test.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers, directors, and general partners may face assessments, penalties, civil claims, and criminal exposure if they knowingly submitted false treaty certificates or engineered abusive arrangements.
AML, Sanctions, and Proceeds-of-Crime Exposure

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include compliance enforcement, administrative monetary penalties, seizure or forfeiture, criminal charges, substantial fines, and imprisonment under Canadian proceeds-of-crime and sanctions legislation.

Director personal liability: Individuals who direct, authorize, facilitate, or knowingly benefit from prohibited transactions may be prosecuted personally. Directors and general partners may face civil recovery, asset restraint, travel restrictions imposed by court or government process, and imprisonment where statutory elements are proven.
Corporate and Partnership Record Failure

Statutory sanction: The CRA may impose information-return penalties, deny unsupported deductions, estimate income, extend audit periods, and apply gross-negligence penalties. Alberta corporate or partnership-law non-compliance may cause registration, filing, or administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: General partners may be personally liable for partnership debts and regulatory obligations. Directors may face statutory liability for corporate remittances and civil claims for failure to exercise reasonable care, oversight, and recordkeeping controls.
