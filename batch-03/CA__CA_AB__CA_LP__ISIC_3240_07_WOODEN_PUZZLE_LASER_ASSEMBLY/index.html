Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and Business Number registration via My Business Account portal; Alberta Ministry of Finance for provincial tax matters (Alberta has no provincial direct tax on LPs).

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 with the CRA. If the LP is required to register for GST/HST, an RT account is added. The application may be completed online, by mail, or through the CRA's international services. Supporting documentation includes the foreign certificate of registration, constituent partnership agreement, and proof of a Canadian contact or agent. If carrying on business in Alberta, the LP must also file an extra-provincial registration with Alberta Corporate Registry, but the tax identifier remains the CRA BN.

Canada does not have 'check-the-box' elections under federal tax law. The LP is fiscally transparent: partners compute their respective shares of income. For GST/HST, an election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act may simplify net-tax calculation for qualifying partnerships. Treaty-based declarations may be made by non-resident partners. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (small supplier threshold). A non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada may be required to register regardless of threshold if it makes taxable supplies in Canada unless considered a small supplier. The threshold is generally based on worldwide revenues for non-resident registrants.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export supply applies to wooden puzzles supplied to a non-resident customer and delivered by the supplier to a destination outside Canada. Tangible personal property is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act. The Canadian supplier must retain documentary evidence of export within the required period, including the carriage contract and customs documentation. Facilitative services directly connected to the export may also be zero-rated.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (via Canada's United States Export Reporting or GCA if applicable)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment settlement proof
  • Sales contract, invoices, and purchase order matching shipment terms

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, API vouchering, or digital stamping mandate currently applies for GST/HST in Alberta. E-invoices (PDF, EDI, XML) are accepted if the CRA record-retention requirements are satisfied. Issuers must retain all transaction data and provide access on demand. A future phased e-invoicing framework has been announced, but no legislative mandate is in force for this scope.

CRA may audit export substantiation records for up to four years (or longer in cases of misrepresentation). If zero-rating is denied, the supplier becomes liable for uncollected GST plus interest. Knowingly making false statements can trigger penalties under Excise Tax Act section 285.1 (up to 25% of the net tax), an additional penalty for wilful false statements of up to CAD 25,000 (summary conviction), and potential criminal prosecution. Retrospective clawbacks are unlimitable where there is fraud or wilful negligence.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating exchange rate regime with full current and capital account convertibility under the International Monetary Fund's Article VIII obligations. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible into all major currencies for trade, investment, and remittance purposes. Non-resident investors, including Alberta limited partners, may remit dividends, partnership distributions, interest, and royalty payments abroad subject to applicable Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada). Treaty relief may reduce such withholding under the Convention Between Canada and the applicable taxing jurisdiction. There are no foreign exchange controls requiring central bank approval for inbound or outbound funds. Nevertheless, all cross-border wire transfers and cash transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC pursuant to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Financial institutions may also be required to verify the economic substance and ultimate beneficial ownership of the recipient for deposits from non-resident limited partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or any foreign exchange authority. Exporters may retain export earnings in CAD or foreign currency accounts, subject to prudential and anti-money laundering requirements. However, all receipts from foreign sources, including proceeds from the export of wooden puzzles and related laser-cut educational hobby toys, must be supported by commercial invoices and customs declarations (Canada Border Services Agency) that align with prescribed export codes and the Harmonized System (HS) tariff classification. For banking and clearing purposes, the underlying transfer must include accurate narrative referencing the commercial contract, invoice number, and export purpose code. FINTRAC reporting thresholds and the Equivalent-to-Currency Reporting Obligation apply to all export-related inbound remittances of CAD 10,000 or more, requiring banks to maintain a due diligence file that establishes the lawful origin and purpose of the funds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with material legal constraints. Canadian federal law does not provide a specialized electronic money institution (EMI) license analogous to the EU's EMI framework. Digital cross-border payment providers such as Stripe, Wise, and Payoneer operate under money services business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC and, in some provinces, as remittance or currency exchange businesses under provincial legislation. For an Alberta limited partnership engaged in ISIC 3240 wooden puzzle laser assembly, a Canadian-licensed MSB or payment fintech may be used solely for clearing and settlement of low-value commercial receipts and invoicing, subject to compliance obligations under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) if classified as a payment service provider. Offshore non-Canadian EMIs may not hold clearing accounts in Canada unless they maintain a FINTRAC-registered MSB branch or use a sponsored arrangement with a Canadian Schedule I bank. The primary constraint is that an unrecognized offshore EMI cannot provide settlement finality in CAD or act as a correspondent for the limited partnership's treasury operations. For larger capital injections and trade disbursements, a Schedule I bank clearing account remains mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident limited partners into the Alberta LP must be routed through a Canadian Schedule I bank's clearing account using SWIFT wire transfer with a clear purpose narrative: 'Capital Contribution to Alberta Limited Partnership' or 'Equity Injection - ISIC 3240 - Wooden Puzzle Assembly'. The receiving bank must be pre-notified through a funds-readiness advice and provided with the partnership's banking authorization, the subscription resolution, and the capital account instructions. For amounts exceeding CAD 50,000, the transferor bank may require the completion of a FINTRAC-required large virtual currency transaction report (if applicable) and a beneficial ownership declaration. The wire must include ISO 20022 standard fields: Beneficiary account number (CAD clearing account), Beneficiary legal name (exact registered partnership name), Beneficiary address (Alberta registered office), and the business identifier code (BIC) of the receiving bank. Purpose code should reflect the capital account transaction under the Canadian Balance of Payments classification, typically code 'F' for financial account transactions, cross-border equity investment, or the equivalent bank-specific code. The bank will verify the inbound funding against the limited partners' capital commitment schedule and may hold funds for a pending-source review before crediting the operating account.For payments to foreign suppliers of raw timber, MDF, adhesives, laser tube consumables, or packaging, the Alberta LP should utilise SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for direct settlements where the supplier provides bank account and IBAN. For larger or documentary trade transactions, a commercial letter of credit (LC) issued by the Toronto branch of a Schedule I bank is appropriate, using UCP 600 rules. The LC should be irrevocable, confirmed by a foreign advising bank if required, and payable at sight.
