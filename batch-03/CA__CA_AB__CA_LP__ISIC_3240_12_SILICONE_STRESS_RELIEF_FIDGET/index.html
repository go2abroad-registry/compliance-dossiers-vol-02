Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible without exchange controls. Canada has accepted the IMF Article VIII obligation; both current account and capital account transactions are unrestricted. Profit remittances and partnership distributions to non-resident partners are not subject to FX approval, but are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, reduced to 15% under the Canada-US Tax Treaty for US limited partners where applicable, and other treaties) administered by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Non-resident limited partners must obtain a CRA tax identification number and file Section 216 or treaty-exemption forms as required; CRA forms T5013 information returns must be issued annually to record allocable income. There are no legal barriers to the repatriation of contributed capital, identified income allocations, or liquidation proceeds, provided the LP has satisfied BC/CRA filing and remittance obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Government of Canada does not impose any surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirement on export proceeds. Exporters and trading entities may retain earnings in freely usable foreign currencies in any domestic or offshore account. Financial institutions are required to report inbound and outbound international wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC under the Retail Payment Activities Act framework and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The LP's obligations are limited to maintaining auditable trade documentation, filing GST/HST returns on export sales (zero-rated), and responding to Statistics Canada Balance of Payments/International Trade surveys when requested.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) as prudential regulator of domestic banks; Department of Finance Canada as the federal economic and financial policy authority; FINTRAC as the national AML/ATF supervisory body. Canada conducts foreign exchange operations through the Bank of Canada and the Minister of Finance; no separate foreign exchange administration agency exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as a supplementary collection/payout rail; Low as a primary treasury depository. Canada does not recognize the European-style 'EMI' licence; foreign fintechs providing payment services in Canada must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where they perform payment clearing, finalization, or settlement functions, must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. A foreign EMI (e.g., an FCA-regulated UK EMI) may not legally maintain a 'deposit account' for the LP in a manner constituting deposit-taking; instead, the LP should use the EMI solely as a payment initiation and merchant-of-record/Payout platform, with settlement ultimately routed by SWIFT MT103 to the LP's Schedule I operating bank. Platform Pay rails (PayPal, Stripe, Wise Business) are viable for e-commerce B2C receipts, but must be configured to settle directly into the primary Canada-based operating account to avoid treasury fragmentation and to maintain a clear audit trail for the General Partner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partners execute inbound wire transfers via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's Canadian settlement account using the ISO 20022 purpose code CAPC (capital-related) or INVEST (investment/equity) as directed by the receiving bank. The transfer must include the LP's CRA Business Number (BN), the partner's legal name, and a narrative field stating 'Capital Contribution to [LP Name]'. The Canadian bank files FINTRAC international wire reports for any transaction equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 and the Department of Finance 'report of incoming intangibles' only when in-kind contributions of licenses occur. No Exchange Control consent is needed; however, the bank may require the constituent LP Agreement or a board/GP resolution confirming the capital call, and will apply 'origination and destination risk' screening. The LP must record the contribution in its capital accounts and issue a T5013 (Schedule 100) allocation only when income is generated; the contribution itself is non-taxable. The LP must not rely on same-day value for complex chains; the bank will provide tracer/reference comments and an SWIFT acknowledgment.The LP settles overseas raw-material purchases (food-grade silicone resin, packaging) using: (1) commercial import letters of credit (documentary credits transmitted via SWIFT MT700/MT707) for large production batches, with bills of lading, commercial invoices, and packing lists as presenting documents; (2) standard SWIFT MT103/MT202 corporate credit transfers for supplier invoices below USD 250,000, with SWIFT purpose codes for goods trade (e.g., 'GDS' Goods and Services / 'TRAE' Trade-Related Payment) and the supplier's full details; (3) CAD domestic high-value payments via Lynx (Payments Canada) for all settlements above CAD 50,000, and EFT/ACH (Interac) for lower-value domestic disbursements; (4) USD clearing through the Canadian bank's US correspondent network (e.g., RBC via RBC Bank Georgia and JPMorgan New York, or TD via TD Bank N.A.) for USD-denominated settlements; and (5) Platform Pay mass payouts (PayPal MassPay, Stripe Payouts/Connect) for final-mile supplier or refund disbursements, routed ultimately to the same Schedule I operating account for reconciliation. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% (15% federal general corporate rate + 8% Alberta general corporate income tax rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident beneficial owner. An Alberta LP is fiscally transparent: profit distributions to limited partners are not dividends; non-resident partners are instead taxed under Part I on their share of Canadian-source business income only where the partnership has a Canadian permanent establishment or the partner is otherwise carrying on business in Canada.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate for non-arm's length cross-border transactions subject to s.247(4); the documentation must be contemporaneous and produced within 90 days of a written CRA request. Canada does not prescribe separate Local File/Master File labels; CRA follows OECD-style contents requiring business overview, controlled transactions, comparability/functional analysis and arm's-length pricing.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the common law substance-over-form doctrine, GAAR (s.245) and the OECD PE concept perform the same anti-avoidance function. An Alberta LP whose fidget-toy manufacturing activities, storage, employees and decision-making are not genuinely located in Canada is exposed to denial of treaty benefits and reallocation of income to the real economic owner.
