Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) is the sole competent federal tax authority administering the Income Tax Act, Excise Tax Act (GST/HST), and related registration programs; the operational portal is CRA My Business Account at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers all customs export declarations (B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System) for physical exports of IVD goods. Alberta does not administer a provincial sales tax or PST; the Government of Alberta (Alberta Corporate Registry, Service Alberta) handles provincial limited partnership registration and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act only applies to the provincial income allocation of Alberta-resident partners.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta Limited Partnership (CA_LP) manufacturing automated point-of-care IVD products must: (1) register the LP with the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain the provincial registration number, appoint an Alberta registered office and agent for service of process, and file the required initial and annual provincial returns; (2) file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' with the CRA to obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) and open program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), and RM (import/export); (3) for non-resident ownership, register under Excise Tax Act s. 240(1) as a non-resident registrant using CRA Form GST494, provide the jurisdiction of tax residence of each foreign limited partner, and designate a Canadian agent for service if the LP has no physical Canadian place of business; (4) file the Partnership Information Return (Form T5013, including Form T4013 and partner allocation schedules) and issue T5013 slips to all partners; (5) establish a Canadian bank account and documentary channels for inward SWIFT MT103 remittance evidence to support export ITC claims. No statutory minimum capital is required, no Alberta sales-tax account is required, and the LP is not subject to Canadian federal corporate income tax at the entity level.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; partnership pass-through status is automatic and mandatory under Income Tax Act s. 96, with the LP filing an information return and partners being taxed directly on allocated income. Available elections relevant to this CA_LP: (i) fiscal-period election under Income Tax Act s. 249.1 to adopt a non-calendar partnership taxation year; (ii) capital cost allowance elections under Income Tax Regulations s. 1103 to pool and allocate CCA on microfluidic manufacturing machinery; (iii) GST/HST closely-related-party election under Excise Tax Act s. 156 and group registration under s. 240(5), permitting zero-rated internal supplies among the LP and related Canadian corporations; (iv) joint election under Income Tax Act s. 152(3) waiving the normal reassessment period; (v) EIFEL and hybrid-mismatch ordering elections under ss. 18.2 and 18.4 of the Income Tax Act for interest deduction allocation where foreign partners finance the LP through shareholder debt; and (vi) Alberta provincial attribution elections aligning the provincial-source taxable income of Alberta-resident partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of total consideration for taxable supplies made in Canada by the LP and associates in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 123(1)). A non-resident LP making taxable supplies in Canada while carrying on business in Canada must register upon exceeding this threshold under ETA s. 240(1); a non-resident LP below the threshold must nevertheless register under ETA s. 240(2) if it solicits orders, advertises, or delivers goods in Canada. Zero-rated export supplies are excluded from the threshold calculation; once registered, the LP must file monthly, quarterly, or annual GST/HST returns (Form GST34) and claim input tax credits for Canadian inputs. E-filing via GST NETFILE is mandatory for registrants with annual Canadian taxable turnover exceeding CAD 1.5 million.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods manufactured in Alberta — microfluidic cartridges, immunoassay analyzers, and associated disposables — qualify for 0% GST/HST under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 20–22 as exported goods when: (a) the buyer is a non-resident, (b) the goods are exported from Canada by the supplier or by the buyer using a common carrier within the prescribed export period, (c) the supplier retains contemporaneous proof of export, and (d) the consideration is paid by cross-border transfer. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST — including remote firmware updates, cloud IVD-data analytics, tele-calibration, and software licensing — are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 where the service is not performed in Canada and is not in respect of Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or an individual present in Canada at the time of performance. The LP must report zero-rated exports on the GST34 return, is entitled to full input tax credit recovery on Canadian manufacturing inputs, and must assemble the export evidence bundle (Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice equivalent) at the time of shipment rather than at audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, no pre-clearance invoice approval, and no mandatory CRA invoice-validation API. Electronic invoicing is permitted under the voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (PEPPOL/Peppol network, in operation from 2024 through 2026), which the CRA supports but does not mandate. Statutory obligations under ETA s. 286 and ITA s. 230 require every registrant to maintain readable, complete, and verifiable invoice records in English or French for at least six years, to issue invoices within 15 days of the supply, to show the supplier's BN, the recipient's GST/HST registration number where applicable, the date of supply, a description, and the consideration with GST separately shown or clearly marked as zero-rated. Failure to E-file when mandated triggers a CAD 250 penalty under ETA s. 280.01.

The CRA treats export zero-rating as a high-risk audit area for manufacturing exporters. Reassessment windows: four years from the GST/HST return due date under normal circumstances, and seven years where the LP has made a misrepresentation or omission attributable to neglect, carelessness, or fraud (ETA ss. 298–299; ITA s. 152(4)). If the substantiation bundle is defective, the CRA will reverse the 0% rate, reassess the export at 5% GST, disallow associated input tax credits, assess net tax with compounded daily arrears interest, and impose penalties: (a) a 25% gross-negligence penalty on the overstated refund or understated net tax under ETA s. 285; (b) false-information penalties under ETA s. 280; (c) criminal tax-evasion prosecution under ETA s. 288 with fines up to CAD 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years on indictment (or CAD 50,000 and two years on summary conviction); and (d) personal liability of the general partner for unremitted GST/HST under ETA s. 323(1), subject only to a reasonable-care due-diligence defence. Although Canada has no statutory FX-repatriation deadline, the 180-day window is contractually embedded in the Swift_LC/TT_Wire payment rails, and failure to obtain an inward remittance certificate or equivalent MT103 confirmation within 180 days creates a rebuttable presumption of non-export, triggering retrospective clawback, reassessment at 5% GST, daily interest, and the full penalty matrix above.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under the Currency Act; Canada maintains no foreign-exchange control statute and imposes no distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility for trade, investment, or profit-repatriation purposes. Cross-border payments are unrestricted, and no prior approval from the Bank of Canada or the Minister of Finance is required for dividends, interest, royalties, or limited-partnership distributions to non-resident partners. The practical remittance hurdle is tax-driven rather than FX-driven: Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposes withholding at 25% (treaty-reduced where applicable) on distributions and payments to non-resident limited partners, and the partner-level remittance must be reported on T5013 and NR4 slips. Investment Canada Act review thresholds apply only to foreign acquisitions of control in Canadian businesses, not to treasury operations. Statistics Canada requires balance-of-payments reporters to file direct-investment surveys for inward capital subscriptions exceeding de minimis thresholds. No capital controls, surrender requirements, or exchange-rate restrictions exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no conversion ratio for export receipts. An Alberta exporter may lawfully retain foreign-currency proceeds in onshore or offshore accounts and may invoice in any freely convertible currency. Fiscal compliance is limited to FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting for outward transfers exceeding C$10,000, CBSA cash-reporting rules for physical cross-border carriage of funds, and GST/HST zero-rating documentation supporting each export sale. The exporter must retain commercial invoices, bills of lading, cargo control documents, and proof of export for at least six years to substantiate the zero-rated claim on audit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central monetary authority; foreign-exchange policy coordination resides with the Department of Finance Canada, with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulating banks under the Bank Act and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforcing AML/CTF surveillance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment and foreign-exchange rail; Low as a primary treasury account. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and may not lawfully accept deposits in Canada; they may operate as money services businesses and, when soliciting Canadian clients, must be registered with FINTRAC and, as of the Retail Payment Activities Act framework, registered as payment service providers with the Bank of Canada. For this Alberta LP, an EMI may be used for cross-border disbursement execution and spot FX conversion, but the capital escrow account, documentary trade settlements, and statutory partnership treasury must sit with a Schedule I bank. Additional constraints include absence of Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation coverage, no direct access to the Lynx large-value clearing system, and the practical need for the EMI to maintain its own correspondent bank relationships in CAD.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign limited partners inject equity via SWIFT MT103 with Field 70 stating 'Payment of partnership capital to CA_LP'; Field 50K must be the exact corporate or personal name of the limited partner, and Field 57A must identify the beneficiary bank branch holding the LP's CAD account. Canada maintains no official government purpose-code dictionary, but the remitter should include a clear text rationale such as 'capital contribution' to enable bank classification as foreign direct investment; Statistics Canada direct-investment surveys will capture the transaction. For USD or other currency inflows, the beneficiary bank may convert at the applicable spot rate or hold in a permitted foreign-currency account. All transfers exceeding C$10,000 are subject to FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting, and global sanctions screening must clear the remitter and correspondent chain, including OFAC screening when routing through the United States. The LP must reconcile each wire against a capital-call ledger and obtain a bank credit advice for audit files.For trade settlements to foreign suppliers, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer from the CA_LP treasury account, with the beneficiary bank, SWIFT/BIC, and IBAN-equivalent instructions confirmed on the supplier's invoice. For capital-equipment purchases and raw-material procurements, the Canadian bank may issue a commercial letter of credit via SWIFT MT700 under UCP 600, with MT707 for amendments and MT720 for transfers where applicable; payment is effected against conforming documents comprising commercial invoice, bill of lading or air waybill, packing list, certificate of origin, and any health certificates required for IVD raw materials. CAD-denominated domestic legs clear through Lynx (Large-Value Transfer System), while cross-border CAD/USD/EUR payments settle through the bank's correspondent accounts abroad. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment and foreign-exchange rail; Low as a primary treasury account. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and may not lawfully accept deposits in Canada; they may operate as money services businesses and, when soliciting Canadian clients, must be registered with FINTRAC and, as of the Retail Payment Activities Act framework, registered as payment service providers with the Bank of Canada. For this Alberta LP, an EMI may be used for cross-border disbursement execution and spot FX conversion, but the capital escrow account, documentary trade settlements, and statutory partnership treasury must sit with a Schedule I bank. Additional constraints include absence of Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation coverage, no direct access to the Lynx large-value clearing system, and the practical need for the EMI to maintain its own correspondent bank relationships in CAD.
