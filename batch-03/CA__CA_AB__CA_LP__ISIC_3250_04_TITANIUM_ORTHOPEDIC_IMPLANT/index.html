Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Alberta limited partnership. The Declaration must state the initial capital contribution agreed by the partners. A limited partner's liability is limited to its agreed contribution provided it does not take part in the control of the partnership. For bank counterparty due diligence, contributed funds may be held in an escrow/trust account at a Schedule I Canadian chartered bank pending issuance of the Certificate, although this escrow is not statutorily mandatory.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada via the 'My Business Account' portal at Canada.ca; Alberta corporate and partnership allocation administered jointly with Alberta Treasury Board and Finance (Tax & Revenue Administration), although GST/HST is exclusively federally administered by CRA.

E-government filing portal

Register the limited partnership in Alberta at Corporate Registry (Alberta Registries) including NUANS name reservation to obtain a Corporate Access Number; then apply in one step for the CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account through Business Registration Online (BRO) at Canada.ca or on paper Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts'. A non-resident partnership must designate a Canadian representative, provide a Canadian service address, and submit the partnership agreement and non-resident ownership declarations; the BN is issued immediately for online filers and the GST/HST account activation date is aligned to the later of incorporation or first taxable supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Alberta LP is transparent: income and loss are computed at partnership level under Income Tax Act (ITA) s.96, with no federal or Alberta entity-level tax on the partnership itself; each general and limited partner, resident or non-resident, reports an allocable share via the T5013 information return, subject to limited-partner at-risk limitations (ITA s.96(2.2)) and Canadian-source business income attribution (ITA s.115).

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box elective regime; available statutory elections include the partnership fiscal-period election (ITA s.96(1)(b)(i)), the deemed non-dissolution election on admission or retirement of partners (ITA s.98(5)), elections to characterize a vehicle as a 'Canadian partnership' for withholding purposes, hybrid-mismatch ordering elections under ITA ss.18.4 and 94.2 to align deduction timing when a non-resident partner is fiscally opaque in its home jurisdiction, and the GST/HST small-supplier revocation election under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s.240(5).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the GST/HST 'small supplier' threshold measured over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and the current calendar quarter; registration becomes mandatory upon exceeding CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter, effective the first day of the following month, and voluntary registration is always available to make input tax credit claims.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a GST/HST registrant exporting tangible goods under ETA Schedule VI s.1 when the goods are delivered to a common carrier, mailed, or transferred for export and physically leave Canada with documented evidence of export. Offshore services, including electron-beam melting and finishing of titanium orthopedic components, qualify under Schedule VI s.7 strictly where the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST and the service is not performed on tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply; zero-rated claims require connection to the direct export transaction and full export documentation reconciliation.

  • CBSA export evidence: B13A Export Declaration or Automated Export Declaration (ACI eManifest) with carrier confirmation of physical departure from Canada
  • Commercial invoice, bill of lading or air waybill, and packing list identifying the titanium implant components, destination, and consignee
  • Bank-issued Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103 advice evidencing settlement of export proceeds in foreign currency

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping regime. CRA operates a voluntary national e-invoicing framework aligned with the Peppol network (Connect 2021, live access points 2024-2025). All registrants must retain electronic invoices and export evidence for six years; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE in My Business Account where annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million, but no API-based invoice clearance or fiscal authorization code is required.

CRA may disallow zero-rating and claw back corresponding input tax credits where export evidence is incomplete or inconsistent; reassessment is permitted within four years under ETA s.298, with no time limit where misrepresentation or negligence is shown. Civil penalties apply up to 25% of the net tax avoided under ETA s.280(2), plus compound daily interest; criminal exposure under ETA s.297 includes fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment for knowingly making false statements or forging export documentation. All export dossiers, customs declarations, and banking records must be retained for six years and reconciled to the GST/HST return and audited financial statements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely without exchange-control licensing or Central Bank approval. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible. Non-resident limited partners may inject capital and repatriate profits without administrative restriction; the sole fiscal friction is Part XIII withholding tax on income distributions (statutory 25%, treaty-reducible, e.g., 15% or 0% under the Canada-US tax treaty where applicable), while returns of capital are non-taxable and free of withholding. No repatriation quotas, advance approvals, or conversion fees apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export proceeds. An Alberta Limited Partnership may retain foreign-currency receipts in USD or other currencies at any authorized Canadian financial institution. Cross-border payments are reported on a statistical basis under the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS), but no approval is required. The only regulatory gate is the Export Control List (ECL): titanium orthopedic implants are not controlled goods; however, a Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) and US FDA registration are mandatory for lawful export.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and retail payments supervisor under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of banks and deposit-taking institutions; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre (FINTRAC) as AML/CFT supervisor; Payments Canada as operator of the national clearing and settlement systems. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with strict legal constraints. Foreign offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act; they may not accept deposits in the Canadian legal sense and operate only as payment service providers subject to RPAA registration (transitional compliance window through 2025). They may be used as secondary settlement conduits for vendor payments denominated in USD or EUR, but all primary treasury flows — capital calls, escrow deposits, debt drawdowns, and export collections — must route through Tier-1 chartered banks. Incoming wires from unregistered foreign EMIs into the LP's Canadian account will trigger enhanced due diligence (EDD), source-of-funds requests, and possible hold times; the receiving bank's compliance team must pre-approve the EMI before first use.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partner contributions are routed via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's Tier-1 Alberta bank account using purpose-of-transfer narrative 'LP Capital Contribution — ISIC 3250 Medical Device Manufacturing'. The bank classifies the credit as a direct-investment transaction under the ITRS; wires above CAD 10,000 are automatically AML-monitored, and wires above CAD 100,000 require a pre-advice memo and source-of-funds documentation. The receiving account must be designated as 'Capital Escrow Account' to prevent misrouting into operating balances. USD-denominated subscriptions should be credited to a segregated USD clearing account to avoid forced conversion; CAD conversions are executed only upon investor instruction via the bank's FX desk.
