Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including Business Registration Online, My Business Account, and Represent a Client. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs and the CARM Client Portal. Alberta has no provincial value-added tax and no separate provincial GST registration.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian or foreign partnership carrying on business in Canada generally obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA through Business Registration Online or the CRA non-resident registration process. The applicant must provide the legal name, partnership agreement or formation evidence, principal and mailing addresses, formation jurisdiction, business activities, fiscal period, responsible individuals, partners or authorized representatives, and expected Canadian taxable supplies. A GST/HST program account is added as an RT account. Non-residents without a permanent establishment may register voluntarily or may be required to register when carrying on business in Canada and exceeding the small-supplier threshold. A non-resident importer or exporter should separately obtain a CBSA RM import-export account through the CARM Client Portal and complete customs registration, importer-of-record, broker, tariff-classification, valuation, and origin procedures. A BN and GST/HST account do not themselves create Canadian corporate income-tax residency; partnership filing and partner-level obligations must be separately assessed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes: the partnership computes its income and allocates it to partners under the Income Tax Act, while partners generally bear the income-tax liability. A limited partnership is not automatically a Canadian corporation and does not generally pay income tax as a separate corporate taxpayer. The general partner ordinarily has unlimited liability under applicable partnership law; limited partners must preserve limited-liability protection by avoiding conduct that constitutes management or control under applicable law. A non-resident partner may have Canadian filing, withholding, treaty, permanent-establishment, and branch or business-income exposure. The partnership may have information-reporting obligations, including Form T5013 where applicable. GST/HST treatment is assessed at the partnership level as the person making supplies.

Available election forms

Canada has no general US-style check-the-box election for an Alberta limited partnership. Classification is determined under Canadian law by the legal characteristics and operation of the arrangement. A foreign entity may be characterized independently for Canadian tax purposes based on its legal attributes, liability regime, ownership, continuity, and income rights. Available elections are transaction-specific and do not convert the partnership into a corporation, including GST/HST elections for qualifying joint ventures, closely related corporate groups, real-property arrangements, or other prescribed situations; section 216 elections for certain non-resident dispositions or rental income; partnership rollover provisions such as section 97(2), subject to statutory conditions; and treaty-based positions where relevant. Hybrid or entity-classification positions must be documented consistently with Canadian domestic law, applicable treaty provisions, and anti-hybrid or anti-avoidance rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, measured under the small-supplier rules generally over a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. A person exceeding the threshold must register within the applicable statutory period and begin charging GST from the effective registration date. Registration can be voluntary below the threshold. Certain special rules apply to non-residents, associated persons, taxicab and ride-sharing operators, charities, public service bodies, and supplies through digital platforms. The threshold does not eliminate import GST, customs obligations, or Canadian income-tax nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods manufactured in Alberta and exported from Canada are generally zero-rated supplies under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the goods are exported in accordance with the statutory conditions and are not consumed, used, or supplied in Canada before export, subject to prescribed exceptions. The supplier must retain evidence linking the supply to the export, such as commercial contracts, purchase orders, commercial invoices, shipping records, carrier confirmations, export declarations, customs records, and proof of delivery outside Canada. Exported medical imaging equipment may also require Health Canada, Canadian Medical Devices Regulations, export-control, sanctions, product-classification, and destination-country regulatory review; those regulatory requirements do not themselves establish GST zero-rating. Certain services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI where the statutory recipient, use, performance, and service-category conditions are met. Services relating to real property in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, services performed in Canada, and excluded financial, personal, or specified services may remain taxable or may be subject to special rules. Zero-rating is not available merely because the customer is non-resident or payment is received in foreign currency.

Canada has no general nationwide real-time GST invoice-clearance, mandatory electronic-invoicing, or government digital-stamping regime for ordinary private-sector transactions. Electronic invoices are generally acceptable if they contain the prescribed GST/HST information, permit reliable verification, and are retained in an accessible and readable form. A registrant must generally issue compliant invoices showing the supplier identity and GST/HST registration number, date, description, consideration, tax rate, tax amount or required tax indication, and applicable recipient information. GST/HST records must generally be retained for six years from the end of the relevant year, subject to longer periods required by law or dispute status. Commercial export and import declarations are filed through the CBSA CARM environment; export reporting may require the Canadian Export Reporting System or an applicable exemption, and import accounting requires CARM procedures. Customs electronic filing is not the same as GST invoice clearance.

Canada has no general statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds to Canada within 180 days, and no general Canadian foreign-exchange certificate is required solely to support GST zero-rating. The registrant must nevertheless substantiate that the supply met the statutory export conditions and must report GST correctly. If zero-rating is unsupported or incorrectly claimed, the CRA may reassess GST, deny input tax credits or GST refunds, assess arrears interest, and impose penalties for late filing, failure to remit, false statements, gross negligence, or repeated non-compliance under the Excise Tax Act and Tax Administration rules. Fraudulent or deliberately false claims may attract gross-negligence or criminal penalties, including fines and imprisonment, depending on the conduct. A refund or input-tax-credit claim can be clawed back through reassessment, with interest and applicable penalties. Records must be retained and produced for audit; unsupported export evidence, inconsistent customs data, related-party pricing anomalies, unusual payment routes, and transactions involving high-risk jurisdictions are likely to receive heightened scrutiny. Customs misclassification, undervaluation, false origin declarations, or export-control breaches may generate separate CBSA penalties, seizure, forfeiture, administrative monetary penalties, prosecution, and reassessment of duties and taxes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a floating-exchange-rate regime with unrestricted convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange-control approvals, no capital-account licensing, no profit-remittance ceilings, and no general withholding tax on limited-partner distributions where the Alberta LP itself is a fiscal pass-through entity and the payment is a distribution of partnership profits rather than a management fee or interest. Non-resident limited partners remain subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on Canadian-source fees, rents, royalties, interest, and deemed benefits; their allocable share of LP income is reportable on Canadian T5013 information returns and may be taxed under Part I on a Canadian-source basis. Profit repatriation is therefore blocked only by ordinary tax-compliance filing and by the need to demonstrate that the recipient is the true beneficial owner under the Canada-foreign tax treaty applicable to the jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory repatriation timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in CAD, USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency in domestic or foreign bank accounts. The only binding compliance duties are (i) filing of a Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration under the Customs Act for controlled/high-value outbound shipments, (ii) reporting of incoming and outgoing wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC under PCMLTFA electronic funds transfer reporting rules, (iii) filing of the annual Canadian income information return on foreign income-earning property (Form T1135) in respect of funds held outside Canada, and (iv) maintenance of books and records sufficient to support the arm's-length pricing of cross-border medical-device sales under section 247 of the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign-exchange and AML/ATF supervisory authority shared by the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

MEDIUM-LOW: Foreign digital EMI platforms (for example Airwallex, Currenxie, Wise Platform, or Mercury) are viable only as ancillary collection/payment gateways, never as the primary treasury bank. Canadian law does not recognize the 'EMI' licence category as a deposit-taking institution; the Bank Act prohibits any unlicensed entity from accepting deposits in Canada. A foreign digital bank may lawfully operate solely as a money services business (MSB) if registered with FINTRAC, and cannot provide CDIC deposit insurance or a regulated Canadian clearing relationship. The compliant blueprint is therefore a Schedule I Canadian bank as the sole operational settlement account, with an optional FINTRAC-registered MSB used exclusively for low-value cross-border receivable collection and daily sweeping of balances to the bank account. Counterparty payment instructions should expressly prohibit settlement to unregulated offshore EMI accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol is as follows: (i) the LP issues a dated, board-approved capital-call notice referencing the exact account style and contribution amount; (ii) each limited partner remits via SWIFT MT103 using the Canadian bank's 3-digit institution number, 5-digit branch transit number, and 9-digit account number, with the payment narrative 'Capital contribution — Alberta Limited Partnership — [legal name]'; (iii) the payment must match the capital-call schedule and the remitter must be exactly the named limited partner on the partnership register; (iv) the Canadian bank verifies the inbound wire against the account's UBO list and the LP's registered ownership schedule, and may request the capital-call notice and partnership agreement before posting value; (v) same-day Canadian-dollar value is cleared through Payments Canada Large Value Transfer System (LVTS), while cross-border United States dollar contributions clear via Fedwire or CHIPS using the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent; (vi) every incoming or outgoing wire of CAD 10,000 or more triggers a FINTRAC electronic funds transfer report; and (vii) all inbound remittances must derive from lawful funds in the remitter's own account with a well-established banking relationship, and no third-party or cash-based contributions are permitted.Clearing-rail selection matrix: (a) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer for urgent supplier payments and low-value settlements, executed through the Canadian bank's FX and global-payments desk; (b) irrevocable documentary Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Canadian bank via SWIFT MT700 (with MT707 amendments), payable at sight, where the supplier's bank acts as advising bank and the underlying export is structured under Incoterms 2020 (typically FCA, CPT, or CIP, depending on insurance and freight obligations); (c) each outbound disbursement must be supported by a complete documentary package comprising a signed purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, pre-shipment inspection certificate, health and safety compliance certificate, and the CBSA export declaration; (d) United States dollar settlements should be routed through a dedicated USD clearing account at a U.S.based correspondent (RBC Bank (Georgia), TD Bank US, or BMO Harris) to reduce correspondent charges and FX spread; and (e) Canadian-dollar payments to domestic vendors clear through Payments Canada ACSS or Real-Time Rail, while LVTS is reserved for time-critical, high-value CAD disbursements.Deliverable CAD/USD spot and outright forward contracts quoted by a Schedule I Canadian bank under an ISDA 2002 Master AgreementShort-dated foreign-exchange swaps (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/CNH) used to match inter-trade liquidity mismatches between the USD capital escrow and CAD operating expensesCross-currency interest-rate swaps executing fixed-rate USD debt conversion into CAD floating-rate liabilities where the LP carries a capital-escrow-linked USD asset against a CAD operating cost basePlain-vanilla OTC currency call or put options with fully stated premiums, deployed to protect minimum export margins on competitive medical-device tendersNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited strictly to hedging the emerging-market settlement leg, for example CNY/CNH invoices payable to Chinese component suppliers, because CAD/USD is fully deliverable and an NDF is administratively inferior for the core currency pairAll hedging instruments require a formal general-partner hedging policy, designation of an Alberta-licensed FX relationship manager, and an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with collateral annex (CSA) and credit-support annex executed prior to the first trade

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: MEDIUM-LOW: Foreign digital EMI platforms (for example Airwallex, Currenxie, Wise Platform, or Mercury) are viable only as ancillary collection/payment gateways, never as the primary treasury bank. Canadian law does not recognize the 'EMI' licence category as a deposit-taking institution; the Bank Act prohibits any unlicensed entity from accepting deposits in Canada. A foreign digital bank may lawfully operate solely as a money services business (MSB) if registered with FINTRAC, and cannot provide CDIC deposit insurance or a regulated Canadian clearing relationship. The compliant blueprint is therefore a Schedule I Canadian bank as the sole operational settlement account, with an optional FINTRAC-registered MSB used exclusively for low-value cross-border receivable collection and daily sweeping of balances to the bank account. Counterparty payment instructions should expressly prohibit settlement to unregulated offshore EMI accounts.
