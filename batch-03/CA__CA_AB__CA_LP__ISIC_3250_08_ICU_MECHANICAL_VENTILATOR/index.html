Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – via the 'My Business Account' portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity that carries on business in Canada must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and, where relevant, a GST/HST registration number. The application is filed using CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). Non-resident limited partnerships may be required to file additional information such as Form RC171e (GST/HST Return for Non-Registrant) or appoint a Canadian resident representative under Form RC59. The BN is issued upon CRA validation of the legal entity particulars, non-resident address, and Canadian place of business; the GST/HST registration can be requested on the same application and is effective from the date specified on the approved registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident entity that carries on business in Canada must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and, where relevant, a GST/HST registration number. The application is filed using CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). Non-resident limited partnerships may be required to file additional information such as Form RC171e (GST/HST Return for Non-Registrant) or appoint a Canadian resident representative under Form RC59. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act, a Canadian limited partnership is ordinarily a fiscally transparent flow-through entity, with income or loss computed at partnership level and allocated to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. No direct 'check-the-box' election exists under Canadian domestic law. However, the SIFT (Specified Investment Flow-Through) rules can convert certain publicly traded trusts and partnerships into deemed corporations, and non-residents may make elections under subsection 216(1) or section 217 of the Income Tax Act in respect of specified Canadian-source income. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed through the federal general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and the hybrid mismatch provisions in Part XXVI.1 of the Income Tax Act, including elections consistent with the OECD BEPS Action Item 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 on total worldwide taxable supplies (including exports) for the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a person who exceeds this threshold must register for GST/HST, and a voluntary registration may be made regardless of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for supplies of goods exported from Canada, provided the supplier keeps evidence of the export within the prescribed time frame, and for certain services supplied to non-residents if the supply is not 'made in Canada' or if it qualifies as a zero-rated supply under Section 23 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Medical devices, including critical care ventilators, may also be zero-rated when the supply satisfies the specific description in Part VI of Schedule VI; for exports, the general export zero-rating rule applies to property delivered outside Canada to a non-resident recipient.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Declaration (B13A / CADEX or similar electronic export reporting), equivalent to the Custom Export Declaration
  • Commercial Invoice or Customs Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (e.g., waybill, air waybill, ocean bill of lading)
  • Proof of export such as carrier's confirmation, warehouse receipt, or forwarder documentation
  • Foreign exchange inward remittance certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds from a non-resident buyer

Canada does not currently impose a mandatory continuous transaction control / real-time clearance e-invoicing regime. However, GST/HST-registered suppliers must issue valid invoices containing prescribed information (supplier name, BN, date, description, consideration, GST/HST rate, and amount). Electronic invoices are accepted if they contain the same information and are accessible in a readable format. CRA is implementing mandatory GST/HST electronic filing and electronic payment for certain registrants; as of the current legislation, annual GST/HST returns for most registrants using prescribed threshold criteria must be filed electronically on GST/HST NETFILE. No fiscal stamping or government clearance is required on each invoice.

Under sections 295 and 297 of the Excise Tax Act, failing to maintain documentary proof of export may result in the reassessment of GST/HST as unremitted output tax, plus interest and penalties. Knowingly making or participating in a false statement or omission in a return or supporting document is an offence punishable, on summary conviction, by a fine of not less than 50% of the tax that would have been evaded and not more than 200% of that tax, or imprisonment of up to two years, or both. On indictment, the penalties increase up to five times the evaded tax and imprisonment not exceeding five years. Retrospective clawbacks may be issued where CRA establishes that the export zero-rating was inapplicable in fact or in law, and the vendor and any signatory are jointly and severally liable for the net assessed amount, including both GST and penalties, without limitation except as set out in the Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized foreign exchange regime. The Canadian dollar is a free-floating currency with unrestricted convertibility for both current account transactions (trade, services, royalties) and capital account transactions (equity subscriptions, debt placements, portfolio transfers, and profit repatriation). No ministerial approval or exchange control permit is required for remittances into or out of Canada. For an Alberta limited partnership, profit distributions to non-resident partners are subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty; this creates a tax-related treasury reconciliation requirement, but not a currency convertibility restriction. Because the entity is tax-transparent for Canadian tax purposes, the LP itself does not pay entity-level withholding tax; each non-resident partner's share is assessed on the partner.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no mandatory surrender quotas, forced conversion timelines, or minimum conversion ratios for export proceeds in Canada. Export earnings may be retained in any permitted foreign currency, held in offshore banks, or used directly to settle trade obligations. The only relevant statutory obligations are reporting thresholds imposed by the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): international electronic funds transfers of CAD $10,000 or more, and large cash transactions of CAD $10,000 or more, must be reported to FINTRAC. These are recordkeeping and verification duties, not surrender or conversion obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange regulation is administered through the Currency Act, the Bank Act, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for transaction monitoring and AML/CFT compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low – Not a compliant primary banking channel. Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) accounts cannot replicate a tier-1 Canadian chartered bank account for an Alberta LP, particularly where government procurement letters of credit or domestic tax filings require a regulated deposit-taking institution. An EMI may be used as an ancillary collection vehicle for pre-contract feasibility funds or non-regulated working capital, but it is legally constrained by Canadian AML/CFT rules and the Bank Act's restriction that only chartered banks, trust companies, or provincial credit unions may hold 'deposits' in Canada. For a medium-risk medical manufacturing entity, reliance on offshore EMI rails for settlement would likely trigger enhanced due diligence and potential restriction by tax authorities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital contributions to the Alberta LP should be routed via SWIFT MT103 with the field 50K stating 'Capital subscription for CA_LP: [Name]' and a clear purpose code (e.g., 'Equity' or 'Capital Injection'). The receiving Canadian bank will require the following verification chain: executed subscription agreement, GP-approved capital call resolution, source-of-wealth statement for the non-resident partner, and confirmation that the funding institution is a regulated bank in its own jurisdiction. Payments of CAD $10,000 or more will trigger FINTRAC electronic funds transfer report. No exchange control permit is necessary; however, banks may apply internal 'non-qualifying funds' screening to ensure the contribution is not derived from illegal proceeds.
