Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary portal is CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration-online. Alberta corporate income tax is administered on behalf of Alberta by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act through the federal Tax Collection Agreement; Alberta has no provincial sales tax and no separate tax registration for limited partnerships. The Alberta LP must also be registered with the Alberta Corporate Registry under the Partnerships Act, and municipal business licensing may apply at the place of operations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine GST/HST registration duty under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act (ETA). A non-resident Alberta LP carrying on business in Canada must register once its worldwide taxable supplies (exclusive of zero-rated supplies) exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and the current calendar quarter; non-resident fulfillment-warehouse vendors and digital platform sellers delivering goods located in Canada to Canadian recipients must register without any threshold. Step 2: Obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) for the LP via CRA's Business Registration Online, by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) by mail or fax to the Sudbury Tax Centre, or by calling the CRA Business Window; the BN is the statutory TIN and is supplemented by program accounts: RT for GST/HST and RM for import/export accounts. Step 3: File the T5013 Partnership Information Return for the LP's fiscal period where the LP is a Canadian-resident partnership, carries on business in Canada, or has a Canadian-resident partner or a partner required to file a Canadian return; each partner separately reports its allocable share under section 96 of the Income Tax Act. Step 4: Register for an RP payroll account if the LP employs individuals in Canada and obtain a customs RM account for the export declaration obligations. Step 5: Foreign partners with Canadian-source income should obtain their own BN and program accounts when filing Canadian returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — An Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian tax purposes: income and losses are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners under section 96 of the Income Tax Act; the LP itself is not subject to federal or Alberta income tax and files only an information return. Non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on their allocable share of Canadian-source income, subject to treaty relief. The LP is not a 'per se corporation' for US check-the-box purposes and may be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership as elected by its US partners.

Available election forms

The LP is tax-transparent and no entity-level corporate election exists under Canadian law. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(9) permitting a non-calendar year-end in limited circumstances; (ii) GST/HST 'designated member' election pursuant to ETA s. 273 as applied to partnerships, allowing one general partner to account for and remit GST/HST on behalf of the partnership; (iii) election under ETA s. 156 for closely related corporations, relevant only if corporate partners establish a GST/HST group; (iv) election under ITA s. 216 by a non-resident partner to file on a net basis for Canadian rental income; (v) for US partners, reliance on Article IV(6) of the Canada-US Tax Convention to claim treaty benefits through the fiscally transparent LP; and (vi) voluntary GST/HST registration under ETA s. 240(2) when the small-supplier threshold is not exceeded, to claim input tax credits.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and the current quarter (the small-supplier threshold); non-resident fulfillment-warehouse vendors and platform sellers supplying goods located in Canada to Canadian recipients are subject to mandatory GST/HST registration with no threshold; non-residents making taxable supplies of real property in Canada are also required to register regardless of revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of sterile examination nitrile gloves qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate under Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA (section 1 for goods exported as soon as is reasonable after sale; section 2 for goods delivered to a common carrier for export; section 3 for goods supplied to a consignee located outside Canada). The exporter must maintain documentary proof of export, generally showing the goods left Canada within 60 days of the sale for direct shipments or within one year for goods moved through bonded or consignment processes. Offshore services rendered to non-residents may be zero-rated under section 7 of Part V if the services relate to goods outside Canada or are otherwise performed for a non-resident and not in connection with Canadian real property. Domestic supplies of qualifying surgical or examination gloves may additionally be zero-rated under Part II of Schedule VI (medical devices) where the goods meet the prescribed description; an advance ruling from the CRA is recommended. Zero-rated exports are taxable supplies at 0%, preserving full input tax credit recovery.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed via the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) or the CBSA Export Declaration (G16/G17), or the Carrier Export Declaration (CED) for goods shipped by air or sea
  • Transport documentation: ocean Bill of Lading or air waybill, including through/inland bills of lading covering movement from Alberta to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying buyer, seller, Incoterms, quantity, value, and HS classification (e.g., HS 4015.12 for rubber medical gloves), marked as an export sale
  • CBSA cargo control documents, customs entry/export summary, container tracking receipts, terminal or warehouse receipts, and insurance certificates evidencing physical removal from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or TT wire credit advice evidencing settlement by the foreign buyer; bank statements showing receipt of foreign currency
  • Transactional correspondence: purchase order, sales contract, letter of credit (Swift LC) documentation, and any broker's export report demonstrating the foreign destination

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoice clearance, or digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary; the Pan-Canadian Peppol eDelivery Network (PINT/EN 16931) is supported for B2B exchange, and federal government procurement via CanadaBuys expects Peppol-compliant e-invoices. GST/HST tax invoices must be issued on request and contain supplier name, BN, date, description, consideration, and tax amount. Export declarations to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) must be transmitted electronically via the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) or a CBSA-approved service provider/API, and all GST/HST records must be retained for six years.

The CRA audits zero-rating evidence under GST/HST Memorandum 4.4.2 and may reassess within four years of the reporting period, or indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect is established. Deficient export proof causes the supply to be re-characterized as taxable at 5%, resulting in assessment of unpaid GST, interest, and clawback of related input tax credits. Civil penalties under ETA s. 280 apply for false statements or omissions: 5% of the net tax understated, increasing to 25% for willful default, gross negligence, or knowing participation; subsequent violations attract a penalty of CAD 100 or 25% of the claim, whichever is greater. Willful evasion is a criminal offense under ETA s. 327 punishable by fine up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to five years on summary conviction, or up to 200% of the evaded amount on indictment; partners may be liable under ETA ss. 323 and 324, and the federal general anti-avoidance rule may apply to artificial transactions designed to manufacture export refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime under the Bank of Canada Act and foreign exchange regulations enforced by the Department of Finance. Both capital and current accounts are freely convertible with no prior approval requirements for Alberta Limited Partnerships. Profit remittances to foreign partners are permitted without surrender or repatriation mandates, subject only to tax filing obligations and beneficial ownership reporting to the Canada Revenue Agency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists for surrender of export proceeds. Canadian exporters may retain foreign currency receipts and convert them at prevailing market rates. All export transactions must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency and Canada Revenue Agency for statistical and tax purposes. No mandatory conversion timeline or ratio applies; proceeds may be held in foreign currency accounts or converted as needed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms face significant regulatory constraints under Canadian AML and FATF compliance standards. Corporate entities must route cross-border flows through traditional correspondent banking networks; fintech EMI services are generally not viable for medical PPE supply chain transactions without full bank partnership.
