Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (ATRA). CRA portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). ATRA portal: https://www.alberta.ca/alberta-tax-and-revenue-administration.

E-government filing portal

The Limited Partnership (LP), acting through its general partner or a resident Canadian representative, must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1. The application includes the LP's legal name, business address, principal activities, and required tax accounts. GST/HST registration is completed on the same form when the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier or when voluntary registration is beneficial. The non-resident LP must also consider withholding obligations on Canadian-source payments and may be required to file an annual T5013 partnership information return. A separate ATRA corporate tax registration is generally not required at the LP level because Alberta corporate income tax applies to partners, not the partnership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election. A limited partnership is statutorily treated as a transparent pass-through entity for federal and provincial income tax; it files an information return (T5013) but does not pay income tax. Available elections include certain partnership elections under subsection 96(3) of the Income Tax Act (ITA) for allocating income, and limited exceptions under the GST/HST regime where partners may elect special treatment for capital assets. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA Part VI.3 follow OECD recommendations and do not offer elective opt-outs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies (including zero-rated and export supplies), as defined under the Excise Tax Act for the small supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating on exports applies to goods if the supplier ships the goods directly out of Canada by means of common carrier or owns the goods at the time of removal and exports them as soon as reasonably possible. The supply must be documented with commercial evidence showing the goods left Canada. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated unless the service is performed in Canada or relates to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property located in Canada, or services referred to in Schedule VI Part V exceptions. The LP must maintain sufficient export documentation to substantiate the zero-rated treatment on its GST/HST return.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / Electronic Export Reporting)
  • Commercial Invoice (finished surgical suture needle product invoice)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing carriage to destination outside Canada
  • Packing List
  • Cargo Control Document (CCD) or carrier manifest
  • Bank inward remittance advice / SWIFT payment credit evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based transaction approval is mandatory for GST/HST. Canada has adopted the Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing Framework based on the Peppol standard, but e-invoicing is currently voluntary for B2B transactions. Registered persons must issue invoices containing prescribed information (supplier name, address, Business Number, invoice date, description, GST payable). GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through CRA e-filing channels once registered, with penalties for non-compliance of electronic filing.

CRA closely audits zero-rated export claims. A false or unsupported zero-rating claim triggers reassessment, disallowance of input tax credits, and interest on unremitted GST. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the unpaid tax amount (s. 280), failure-to-file penalties on net tax (1% plus 0.25% per month up to 12 months), and, for deliberate tax evasion, fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment under s. 327. The ordinary limitation period is four years from the assessment date, but it is extended indefinitely where there is misrepresentation attributable to negligence, carelessness, or wilful default. CRA may also retrospectively claw back refunds and input tax credits where export documentation is deficient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; no licensing, exchange-control permit, or repatriation approval is required under the Currency Act, Bank of Canada Act, or Investment Canada Act for capital inflows, trade settlements, or profit remittances. Non-resident limited partners may freely subscribe capital to an Alberta LP, but CRA imposes tax at stake: active business income attributable to the Alberta permanent establishment is taxable under Part I on each partner's share; passive payments (interest, royalties, deemed distributions) are subject to Part XIII withholding at 25% (treaty-reduced); and Part XIII.1 Section 194 withholding applies to distributions of non-PE income to non-resident partners who do not file a Part I return. The LP must maintain a documented capital-account ledger to distinguish equity from debt, because debt-like injections above 1.5:1 debt-to-equity can trigger the excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds; an Alberta LP may hold USD/EUR receivables indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts. The only obligations are (i) FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting for each transaction of CAD 10,000 or more, (ii) CRA taxation of foreign-exchange gains under Sections 39 and 95 of the Income Tax Act, and (iii) compliance with the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA) and the United Nations Act, which prohibit settlement with sanctioned jurisdictions regardless of currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole monetary and currency authority; there is no separate Foreign Exchange Administration in Canada. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises Schedule I banks under the Bank Act, the Department of Finance Canada coordinates monetary-policy and sanctions law, and FINTRAC enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for all cross-border electronic-funds-transfer reporting. Canada abolished exchange-control boards in 1951 and operates a free-floating CAD regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low. Under the federal Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), effective November 1, 2024, all payment service providers operating in Canada must be registered with the Bank of Canada by the November 2025 transitional deadline, and offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise or Airwallex cannot accept deposits or lend without Bank Act authorization and are not CDIC-insured. An offshore EMI is feasible only as a supplementary virtual-account concentration rail for low-value inbound customer payments; it is not acceptable as the sole treasury or capital-escrow platform for an Alberta LP, and all partner capital must be held with a Schedule I CDIC-insured bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound partner capital is wired via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's CAD capital-escrow account at the Alberta branch of a Tier 1 bank, with Field 72 remittance information reading 'CONTRA-ENTRY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — [LP legal name] — SUBSCRIPTION AGREEMENT [date]'. Canada prescribes no statutory wire purpose codes, but the bank's internal classification must record 'capital contribution to partnership equity' (non-debt) and FINTRAC EFT reporting must be filed automatically for any transfer of CAD 10,000 or more. For USD contributions, settlement is executed through CLS Bank's USD/CAD continuous linked settlement window; for EUR or GBP contributions, correspondent accounts are used and Field 50K (ordering customer) must include the partner's legal name and address to prevent OFAC holds. The LP shall maintain a GP resolution, subscription schedule, and capital-account ledger; any injection documented as a subordinated partner loan must include an executed promissory note and be tested against the 1.5:1 debt-to-equity safe harbour.Export disbursements for surgical sutures and needle assemblies settle via (i) SWIFT MT103 direct wires for distributor prepayments and open-account terms, with Incoterms 2020 and UCP600-aligned commercial invoices; (ii) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) issued by the Canadian bank and settled through MT754 and MT756 advices; and (iii) standby LCs (SWIFT MT760) as performance bonds. CAD/USD legs clear through the Lynx real-time gross settlement system (successor to LVTS, operating 06:00-18:30 ET) with cross-currency legs through CLS; all non-CAD/USD disbursements clear via correspondent balances. Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods; CAD 500,000 for services; CAD 200,000 for other transactions

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) apply. Limited partnerships must demonstrate real economic activity (e.g., employees, premises, operational functions) to avoid recharacterization of transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Income Tax Act: fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; civil restitution, travel bans, and asset seizure possible.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Reassessment, penalties, and potential criminal charges under GAAR; fines up to 50% of tax avoided

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax evasion if they knowingly participated in the scheme.
AML

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million per corporation, imprisonment for individuals up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined and imprisoned for willful non-compliance.
