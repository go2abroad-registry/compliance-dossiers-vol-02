Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Gateway and My Business Account portal; for Alberta provincial statutory registration, Alberta Corporate Registry through the Corporate Online Application System (CORES).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned or non-resident-owned Alberta Limited Partnership that carries on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST account from CRA through Business Registration Online or Form RC1. The BN is the universal tax identifier. Where the LP is an extra-provincial limited partnership, it must also register under Alberta's Partnership Act via Alberta Corporate Registry. When applying as a non-resident, a Canadian representative or agent must be appointed, and CRA may require a signed statutory declaration attesting to the nature of the Canadian business. The LP's partners report their allocated income directly on their own returns, while the LP files an information return (T5013) if it is a registered partnership. For the purpose of claiming refunds on zero-rated exports, the LP must maintain a full export evidence file, including customs declarations and payment records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not use a US-style check-the-box regime; classification is determined by domestic law. Pertinent elections for an Alberta LP include: (i) under subsection 102(2) of the Income Tax Act, a limited partnership that would cease to be a Canadian partnership because limited partners are non-residents may elect to be deemed a Canadian partnership; (ii) under subsection 177(2.1) of the Excise Tax Act, an eligible person and a partnership may jointly elect using Form GST31 to deem the partnership a registrant; and (iii) hybrid mismatch adjustment rules under sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act apply automatically but may be mitigated by certain elections or documentation filed with the CRA.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property, including dental clear aligner sheets, is zero-rated as an export under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier ships the goods directly to a destination outside Canada, or the non-resident recipient exports the goods within a reasonable period, generally 60 days, and the supplier retains documentary evidence of the export. The supply need not be in a physically exported customs format if the goods are sent by mail or courier, but CRA requires sufficient contemporaneous evidence showing the goods left Canada and were purchased by a non-resident. Zero-rating is also available where the supply is made to a non-resident in Canada if the goods are subsequently exported and no use, consumption, or entertainment occurs in Canada before export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency A8/A6 or Canada Export Reporting)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill showing foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank payment evidence for foreign currency settlement

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices is mandated in Canada for GST/HST. Registrants must issue prescribed GST/HST invoices showing consideration, tax charged, vendor name, BN and GST/HST registration number. CRA requires mandatory electronic filing (GST/HST NETFILE) for registrants with total annual revenues above $10,000,000 and mandates electronic remittance of GST/HST amounts over $10,000. For Alberta, there are no provincial e-invoicing or fiscal clearance rules because Alberta does not impose a provincial retail sales tax.

CRA may reassess GST/HST returns within the usual four-year limitation period, but the period is unlimited where there is misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud in the export documents. If export zero-rating is rejected, the supply becomes taxable at 5% and the resulting net tax, plus interest from the date it should have been remitted, is assessed. Gross negligence penalties under the Excise Tax Act equal the greater of $100 and 25% of the federal tax sought. Deliberate falsification of export evidence can lead to prosecution, fines equal to up to twice the amount of tax sought, and imprisonment, in addition to CRA's authority to claw back refunds and input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and operates no exchange-restricted import/export regime, no dual-rate system, and no capital import licensing requirement. Profit remittance by a non-resident limited partner is subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% unless treaty-reduced (e.g., Canada-US Convention) where a reduced rate applies to partnership distributions of Canadian-source income; this is a tax checkpoint, not an FX-control restriction. The Investment Canada Act requires notification of acquisitions of control of a Canadian business, but does not restrict ordinary cross-border capital flows to an operating manufacturing LP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no centralized repatriation account for export receipts. Export proceeds from the thermoforming trade may be retained freely in CAD, USD or EUR. Practical compliance: (i) a Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) under the Food and Drugs Act and Medical Devices Regulations (SOR/98-282) is mandatory for exporting dental clear aligners, and (ii) exporters must file a B13A Export Declaration via the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for any shipment valued above CAD 2,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary and settlement authority; foreign-exchange administration is dispersed under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52), the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2) and the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46). AML/KYC enforcement for financial institutions is conducted by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17) and by OSFI for prudential supervision; payment-system oversight is governed by the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23, s. 174).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High for operational payment rails, Low for capital escrow and fiduciary cash concentration. Canada does not issue a standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence; fintech treasury providers (Stripe, Wise, Payoneer) are registered as Money Service Businesses with FINTRAC and will transition to the Retail Payment Activities Act regime. Stripe is fully viable for the Platform_Credit_Card rail and receivables concentration, but holds no CDIC-insured deposit protection and cannot originate documentary letters of credit; therefore the LP must keep its capital escrow and all outbound FX/LC flows on a Schedule I bank balance sheet.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route via SWIFT MT103 only, not MT202, for the foreign parent contribution. Beneficiary account name must match the CORES registration exactly (e.g., X Dental Clear Aligner Manufacturing Limited Partnership). Field 70 (Remittance Information) must read 'CA-LP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / LPA SCHEDULE X' to ensure the receiving bank classifies the credit as equity capital, not taxable revenue. For USD wires from the US parent, use beneficiary bank's U.S. clearing correspondent (e.g., RBC's New York branch, BMO Harris, or Scotiabank N.A.) with FF (Further Credit) to the Alberta LP account; include the bank's SWIFT BIC and the Alberta branch transit. Because the credit exceeds CAD 100,000, the bank is obligated to record an EFT report; the LP must supply the underlying LPA extract, board resolution and proof of source-of-funds at the credit-processing desk. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23.0% combined federal and Alberta headline rate (15.0% federal Part I general corporate rate plus 8.0% Alberta general corporate income tax rate).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 – contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is mandatory for a taxation year in which non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with non-residents, in aggregate, exceed CAD 1,000,000; Master File documentation is prepared where the multinational group's gross revenue exceeds CAD 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000 in the preceding fiscal year; penalty relief under ITA s.247(4) requires documentation to be prepared before the T2 filing deadline.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply Gulf-Council style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the Alberta Limited Partnership must demonstrate real economic activity in Alberta, including physical premises for thermoforming, payroll, production assets, and substantive management functions, to be respected for tax purposes. Where substance is absent, the CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA s.245 to disregard or recharacterize the structure, deny treaty benefits under the Multilateral Instrument's Principal Purpose Test, and attribute income to Canadian controlling parties under CFC rules in ITA s.91.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil gross negligence penalty of up to 50.0% of understated tax under ITA s.163(2); on indictment under ITA s.239(1), a fine of 100.0% to 200.0% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who authorized the filing are jointly and severally liable for the assessment; Criminal Code s.380 fraud prosecution applies where the evasion exceeds CAD 5,000, carrying imprisonment of up to 14 years; the CRA may certify unpaid assessments exceeding CAD 50,000 to trigger passport suspension, departure-prevention measures, and international asset seizure.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Net transfer pricing adjustment under ITA s.247(2); a 10.0% penalty on the net adjustment where transaction value exceeds CAD 1,000,000 and documentation is inadequate; additional gross negligence penalty of 50.0% of understated tax; daily compound prescribed interest on reassessed balances.

Director personal liability: Directors approving the mispricing are exposed to liability for unremitted amounts under director liability provisions, and general partners of the LP face unlimited liability for the full adjustment; repeated schemes may trigger CRA criminal referral for fraud and the issuance of severe-recalcitrant tax penalties.
