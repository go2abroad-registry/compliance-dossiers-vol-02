Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and CRA My Business Account (MyBA); provincial registry counterpart (non-tax) is the Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) for formation under the Partnership Act (Alberta). GST/HST and payroll accounts are administered solely by CRA.

E-government filing portal

1) Form the Alberta limited partnership under the Partnership Act (Alberta) at Alberta Corporate Registry — no minimum capital requirement; obtain the Alberta registration number. 2) Apply for the CRA 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online, Form RC1, or by telephone, within 21 days of commencing business; select the GST/HST (RT) program account — the LP is a separate 'person' for GST/HST even though fiscally transparent for income tax. 3) File annual T5013 partnership information return with schedules allocating income to partners; non-resident partners must provide their foreign TIN, Canadian treaty residence particulars, and, where applicable, appoint a Canadian agent for service (s. 103 ITA / s. 221.2 ETA). 4) Add RP payroll account if the LP hires employees; an RC corporate account is not required because the LP itself does not file a T2. 5) If the LP acts as agent for a non-resident principal, the principal's identity and GST/HST registration status must be documented on file to prevent deemed supplier exposure under ETA s. 177(1).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — An Alberta LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ss. 96–103); neither federal nor Alberta income tax is imposed on the partnership itself, and each partner is taxed on allocated Canadian-source income (Part I for residents; Canadian-source business income for non-residents, subject to treaty). Territorial scope: residents taxed on worldwide income; non-residents only on Canadian-source income. For GST/HST, the LP is a separate registrant person (ETA s. 123). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — An Alberta LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ss. 96–103); neither federal nor Alberta income tax is imposed on the partnership itself, and each partner is taxed on allocated Canadian-source income (Part I for residents; Canadian-source business income for non-residents, subject to treaty). Territorial scope: residents taxed on worldwide income; non-residents only on Canadian-source income. For GST/HST, the LP is a separate registrant person (ETA s. 123). Alberta levies no provincial sales tax; only federal GST at 5% applies.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Canada; transparency is statutory. Relevant elections and designations: (i) designation of the LP as a 'Canadian partnership' under Part I of the ITA (filed with the T5013) to confirm Canadian situs for cross-border withholding and treaty purposes; (ii) ETA s. 156 closely-related election to treat the LP and a Canadian corporate partner as a single person for GST/HST simplification; (iii) ETA s. 177(1) agency deeming — the LP must document the non-resident principal relationship to avoid deemed supplier status; (iv) s. 249.1(1) ITA fiscal-period election for a calendar year-end to align T5013 filings; and (v) hybrid-mismatch control: non-resident partners (e.g., US LLC members) may elect opaque treatment for the CA LP in their home jurisdiction, triggering Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (s. 212.3 ITA) and BEPS Action 2 responses — allocations may be recharacterized as deemed dividends subject to Part XIII withholding unless the partnership deed and election documentation satisfy corresponding-inclusion tests. Election documentation should be finalized in the partnership agreement before the T5013 due date.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters (ETA s. 148 small-supplier threshold); non-residents carrying on business in Canada are not eligible for the small-supplier exemption and must register from the first taxable supply; the Alberta LP should register immediately (voluntary registration) to recover input tax credits on brokerage, freight, storage and grain-procurement inputs, and to avoid deemed-agent GST collection under ETA s. 177(1) when acting for non-resident suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating eligibility arises under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act: (a) Part V, s. 20 — goods (soybeans, yellow corn) supplied to a non-resident and exported by or on behalf of the supplier to a destination outside Canada, evidenced by CBSA export documentation; (b) Part IV, s. 7 — services (vessel brokerage, chartering commissions) supplied to non-residents are 0% except where the service is performed in respect of real property in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed — a brokerage service relating to grain still in Canadian elevators or port terminals will ordinarily be taxable at 5%, so the LP must carefully structure and document which commission services are supplied to non-residents in respect of offshore goods; (c) Part III — basic-grocery zero-rating may cover grain sold domestically for human consumption, but feed-grade and industrial-grade corn and soybeans remain taxable at 5% absent export. CRA requires the LP to hold documentary evidence establishing the recipient's non-resident status, the physical export movement, and the payment or repatriation of proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A filed electronically with CBSA via the Canadian Export Reporting System / eExport, including HS 1201 (soybeans) and HS 1005 (corn) commodity codes)
  • Bill of Lading / Ocean Waybill (clean on-board, endorsed to order, evidencing loading at a Canadian port and discharge at a foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) — or equivalent SWIFT MT103 / wire transfer proof evidencing FX repatriation of the export proceeds within the 180-day mandate

No real-time continuous-transaction-control clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory fiscal API exists in Canada. CRA promotes voluntary Peppol-based e-invoicing (Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing initiative); GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1,000,000 must file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE or EDI), and all registrants must preserve complete audit-trail records of e-invoices and transaction logs. CBSA mandates electronic B13A export declarations (eExport / Canadian Export Reporting System) for commodity exports; CRA may require ITC substantiation via electronic supplier invoices. No CRA digital stamp is affixed to invoices.

CRA may reassess beyond the standard 4-year period — extended to 7 years for neglect and indefinitely for misrepresentation or fraud under ETA ss. 296–298 — reversing the 0% rate with interest from the original supply date where export evidence is deficient. Penalties include: 50% gross-negligence penalty on understated net tax (ETA s. 285); failure-to-remit penalties and compound interest under ETA s. 280; criminal evasion fines of up to 200% of evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 5 years (ETA s. 327; ITA s. 239); and CBSA penalties up to CAD 25,000 per false export declaration and forfeiture of goods under the Customs Act. Where exported grain is diverted back into the Canadian market, or a brokerage service is recharacterized as relating to Canadian-situated grain, the full 5% GST is clawed back from the LP, not merely from the foreign principal.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. The Currency Act, Bank of Canada Act, and the federal deregulation framework allow free repatriation of profits, dividends, interest, and liquidation proceeds without prior regulatory approval. For a non-resident LP engaged in bulk commodity trading, there is no conversion restriction, no central bank permission requirement, and no requirement for a beneficial foreign investment certificate. Canadian bank accounts may be denominated in CAD or foreign currencies; CAD earnings can be freely converted via the interbank FX market. The only practical hurdles are FINTRAC reporting thresholds, sanctions screening, and documentary verification of the underlying trade for anti-evasion purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Bulk grain and soybean exporters may retain all foreign currency proceeds in resident or non-resident accounts. FINTRAC requires Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) reports for international transactions of CAD 10,000 or more within prescribed timelines, but this is an informational reporting obligation, not a surrender or conversion mandate. For tax purposes, a Canada-based partnership must compute income in CAD using the Bank of Canada exchange rate at the relevant transaction date; unrealized FX gains/losses are recognized in accordance with normal accounting principles.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; administered in coordination with Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/sanctions enforcement. Canada imposes no standalone foreign exchange control authority or exchange permit regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Canada does not license 'electronic money institutions' as a distinct category. Non-resident digital EMIs may access the Canadian market only if they are registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and maintain a physical or agency presence for compliance. For a bulk agricultural commodity LP, the better path is a direct account with a Schedule I Canadian chartered bank, using the bank's own cross-border clearing network, rather than a fintech EMI, to avoid dual-entity AML friction, ring-fencing of escrowed capital, and uncertain settlement finality on LC operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident investors may inject capital via standard SWIFT MT103 wire to the LP's Canadian operational account. No exchange control approval is required. The payment must include clear narrative referencing the limited partnership name, contribution purpose ('LP Capital Contribution'), and applicable grain purchase contract references. For any single inward transfer ≥ CAD 10,000, the receiving Canadian financial institution must file an EFT report with FINTRAC within prescribed timelines. If the incoming wire originates from a foreign corporate account, banks will request the underlying beneficial ownership declaration and a board resolution verifying the payor's authority. For soybean and corn price-linked funding, the routing can include an advance under an irrevocable commercial LC issued by the buyer's bank, confined to a commodity trade finance desk in Toronto or Vancouver.Outbound settlement rails for bulk grain and corn purchases include (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct correspondent bank transfers; (ii) Irrevocable, transferable documentary letters of credit issued under ICC UCP600, advised through Canadian banks, with payments executed either by sight draft or deferred payment undertaking; (iii) domestic prefunded transfers via Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for CAD and near-real-time Rails for USD (using a Canadian bank's US clearing correspondent); and (iv) documentary collections for low-value counterparties. Grain commodity disbursements should not be drawn against open-account credit unless a bank-approved FX forward covers the CAD/USD conversion risk.
