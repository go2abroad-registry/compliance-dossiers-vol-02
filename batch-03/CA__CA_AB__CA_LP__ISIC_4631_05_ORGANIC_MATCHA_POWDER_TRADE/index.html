Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the CRA Business Registration Online service and CRA My Business Account portal. The CRA administers federal income tax and the federal Goods and Services Tax (GST). Alberta has no provincial sales tax; applicable Alberta corporate and partnership tax administration is generally integrated with the CRA system.

E-government filing portal

An Alberta limited partnership carrying on business in Canada should obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and the relevant program accounts. The standard sequence is: (1) confirm the partnership agreement, Alberta registration, registered office, partners, business activities, fiscal year, and authorized representative; (2) apply for a BN through CRA Business Registration Online where eligible, or by the CRA telephone or prescribed registration process; (3) register for a GST/HST account using the BN if the partnership exceeds the small-supplier threshold, voluntarily registers, or is required to register as a non-resident; (4) obtain an Import-Export Program account (RM) where the entity imports or exports commercial goods, with export declarations and customs activity managed through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) CARM system; (5) register for payroll, information-return, or other CRA program accounts only if applicable. A foreign partner or non-resident partnership should maintain evidence of Canadian business activities, Canadian tax nexus, partnership authority, identity, ownership, and authorized-signatory status. Canada does not issue a VAT identification number separate from the GST/HST account number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no United States-style check-the-box election for an Alberta limited partnership. A valid partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes, with partnership income and losses allocated to partners under the partnership agreement and applicable Canadian tax rules. The partnership may be required to file a T5013 Partnership Information Return, and non-resident partners may have Canadian filing, withholding, treaty-relief, or section 116 obligations depending on the activities and assets. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences where differences in entity or instrument characterization produce deduction/non-inclusion or duplicate-deduction outcomes. No general elective hybrid-mismatch classification is available. Treaty positions, fiscal-year choices where permitted, and GST/HST registration elections must satisfy their specific statutory conditions and are not entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters for the small-supplier test. The threshold generally applies to worldwide taxable supplies of the person and associated persons, subject to statutory exclusions and special rules. A non-resident commercial business may be required to register based on carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies in Canada even where the ordinary small-supplier analysis does not eliminate the obligation. Voluntary registration is generally available subject to CRA conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are generally zero-rated for GST where the statutory export conditions are met, including delivery or making the goods available outside Canada, shipment by the supplier or another qualifying person to a destination outside Canada, or other prescribed export circumstances. The supplier must retain objective evidence that the goods left Canada and must correctly identify the purchaser, goods, destination, shipping terms, and export transaction. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated only under specific place-of-supply and service-category rules; non-resident status alone is insufficient. Services directly connected with goods for export, certain financial or intermediary services, and other prescribed services require separate statutory analysis. Input tax credits or GST rebates may be available for GST incurred on Canadian inputs, but eligibility depends on registration status, commercial activity, documentation, and the applicable rebate or ITC rules. Organic or ceremonial matcha classification does not itself create a special GST zero-rating rule; export status and documentary substantiation control.

  • Canadian export declaration or electronic export reporting through the applicable CBSA process, including the required export permit or declaration data where applicable
  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, importer or consignee, complete goods description, quantity, value, currency, Incoterms, country of origin, and destination
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier record, or equivalent carrier evidence showing shipment from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Packing list and inventory or lot records sufficient to reconcile the exported matcha powder with the commercial invoice
  • Proof of delivery, customs import record, delivery confirmation, or equivalent evidence that the goods arrived outside Canada
  • Certificate of origin or preferential-origin documentation where required by the destination country or a trade agreement
  • Export permits, food, plant, organic, or other regulatory certificates where required by the goods, destination country, or applicable Canadian and foreign law
  • Customer purchase order, sales contract, payment record, and correspondence supporting the foreign sale and export destination
  • GST/HST invoice and accounting records linking the zero-rated sale to the export evidence
  • Inward remittance or foreign-exchange evidence where relevant to commercial substantiation or banking controls; Canada does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a condition for GST zero-rating

Canada has no general real-time GST clearance, mandatory government e-invoicing network, or universal digital invoice-stamping requirement for ordinary commercial supplies. GST invoices must contain prescribed information appropriate to the transaction and the customer may require sufficient documentation to support input tax credits. Electronic records and invoices are generally acceptable if they preserve authenticity, integrity, readability, and audit accessibility. GST/HST returns and payments are filed electronically where mandatory or where the registrant elects electronic filing. Customs declarations and related trade data are submitted through CBSA systems, including CARM where applicable; this is separate from real-time GST invoice clearance. Large corporations and certain specified registrants may have mandatory electronic filing obligations.

Canada has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export proceeds may remain offshore, subject to normal tax reporting, transfer-pricing, anti-avoidance, anti-money-laundering, customs, and financial-reporting requirements. CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess GST at the applicable rate if export conditions or documentary evidence are not established. The registrant may then owe reassessed GST, arrears interest, and applicable administrative penalties, including gross-negligence or false-statement penalties where the statutory conditions are met. Improperly claimed input tax credits or rebates may be reassessed with interest and penalties. Deliberate fraud, false invoices, concealment, or fabricated export evidence may result in criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, denial of benefits, and extended reassessment periods. CBSA may impose Administrative Monetary Penalty System sanctions, seize or detain goods, reassess duties or taxes, and pursue enforcement for inaccurate export declarations, prohibited goods, or documentary non-compliance. A later CRA audit can claw back historical zero-rated treatment for all unsupported transactions within the applicable reassessment period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible with no exchange-control statute or capital-account licensing regime. Current-account and capital-account transactions are equally unrestricted for residents and non-residents. Profit and capital remittances by an Alberta limited partnership are subject to normal tax compliance rather than FX approval; non-resident limited partners face Part XIII withholding tax under the Canadian Income Tax Act (25% statutory, potentially treaty-reduced), which is a tax-clearing obligation, not a convertibility hurdle. Banks may require source-of-funds evidence for large inbound contributions but cannot impose convertibility limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement under Canadian law to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or to mandatorily convert foreign-currency receipts into CAD. The Alberta LP may retain export and resale proceeds in USD, JPY, or other convertible currencies in domestic or foreign-currency bank accounts. FINTRAC large-transaction reporting obligations apply to cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; physical currency movements exceeding CAD 10,000 must be declared to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) on Form E677.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC); prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); there is no dedicated foreign-exchange administration in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a supplemental rail, low feasibility as a primary treasury anchor. Canada does not license standalone e-money institutions; the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) regime applies to payment service providers and FX/EMI-style fintechs, while only federally chartered Schedule I banks can hold insured Canadian deposits. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may lawfully process flows for the LP, but their Canadian operation is subject to RPAA registration, they are not CDIC-insured, and their accounts cannot replicate a domestic banking escrow or documentary-credit capability. The LP should hold cash and letters of credit at a Schedule I bank and use fintech platforms only for expedited low-value supplier collections.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for goods transactions; CAD 500k for services; CAD 100k for intangibles (per non-resident non-arm's length party)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires genuine economic substance to prevent tax avoidance. Partnerships must demonstrate real business activity, including physical presence, employees, and decision-making in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion
