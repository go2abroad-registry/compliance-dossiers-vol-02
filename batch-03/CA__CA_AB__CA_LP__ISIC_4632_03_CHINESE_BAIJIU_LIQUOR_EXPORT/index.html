Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office is generally impermissible for the registered office of a limited partnership where day-to-day management is conducted from the address. A registered office in Alberta must be at a physical street address staffed during business hours and capable of receiving service of process and regulatory notices. For a limited partnership engaged in spirits wholesale export, the AGLC license agreement requires a physical commercial office or licensed warehouse address; a mailbox or virtual-office room without physical presence is not acceptable. Virtual office may be accepted only as an ancillary communications contact, but the registered office must have substantial physical presence.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership. The partnership agreement should specify each partner's capital commitment, contribution timing, profit allocation, withdrawal restrictions, and liability limitations. Alberta does not require a statutory escrow account for formation capital. Any banking, anti-money-laundering, lender, liquor-license, or contractual funding requirement remains separate.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal development, building, occupancy, fire inspection, and fire-code approvals for the premises. Maintain emergency exits, extinguishers, spill response, flammable-liquid controls, storage separation, ventilation, and emergency plans as applicable. Complete structural and floor-load reviews for racking, pallets, bulk containers, and warehouse equipment. Comply with Alberta Occupational Health and Safety legislation, including worker training, hazard assessments, personal protective equipment, forklift controls, incident reporting, and workplace inspection requirements.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized, market-based foreign exchange regime with no statutory exchange controls since the abolition of the Foreign Exchange Control Board in 1951; it accepts IMF Article VIII obligations, permitting full current- and capital-account convertibility without prior governmental approval. Inbound capital contributions by non-resident limited partners and outbound profit remittances are unrestricted; principal compliance obligations are FINTRAC EFT reports (CAD 10,000+ aggregated over 24 hours) and income tax collection mechanics. Non-resident partners are taxable on Canadian-source partnership allocations, and distributions may attract Part XIII withholding tax at the 25% statutory rate, subject to relief under the Canada-China Double Taxation Agreement (CA-PRC DTA) and applicable PE thresholds; no capital account convertibility frictions exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirements for export proceeds in any currency. The Alberta limited partnership may freely retain USD, CNH, or other foreign currency balances in Canadian or offshore accounts. However, all cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be monitored and reported by the reporting entity (the bank) to FINTRAC; China-origin remittances remain subject to PRC SAFE rules on the remitter side, and Canadian export of spirits is documented through CBSA export declarations and AGLC (Alberta Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis) permits rather than any FX conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central bank for monetary policy and currency issuance; Department of Finance Canada — exchange-rate policy and federal financial-sector legislation; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) — prudential regulation of chartered banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) — cross-border electronic funds transfer (EFT) reporting and AML/ATF supervision under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as the primary or sole banking relationship. Canada does not charter foreign fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs); such entities operate as registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC or through agency agreements with Schedule I banks. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) may provide auxiliary USD/CNH payout rails and virtual account details, but they cannot hold Canadian deposits as a regulated bank, do not satisfy OSFI-prudential expectations for capital escrow of a limited partnership, and are generally unacceptable for AGLC liquor-import compliance or documentary LC issuance. Recommended use: supplementary collection and small-ticket supplier payments only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partners remit equity contributions by SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the Alberta LP's segregated CAD or USD account at a Schedule I bank. Each remittance must reference the specific capital call notice and carry purpose code 'CAPT' (capital contribution) or the bank's equivalent purpose-of-payment code in MT103 Field 70. Banks will apply FINTRAC large-EFT monitoring automatically for every transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, potentially requesting the underlying governing documents; amounts above USD 100,000 sourced from mainland China should preferably be routed through a Hong Kong CNH account to minimize PRC SAFE approval friction and documentary delay. Upon credit, the bank verifies the ownership match between the remitter and the executed LP ownership schedule; any mismatch triggers a redirect to a lawyer's trust account pending re-papering.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 for open-account settlement of confirmed purchase orders against Chinese baijiu distilleries; for consignments exceeding USD 50,000, a documentary Commercial Letter of Credit (at-sight or usance) is recommended, issued by the LP's Canadian bank in favour of the Chinese advising bank (e.g., Bank of China) with conforming documents comprising commercial invoice, bill of lading, CIQ health certificate, AGLC import/export permit, and certificate of origin. For onward deliveries to US distributors utilizing bonded warehouses, disbursements may settle via MT103 with a CUSMA (Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement) certificate of origin and the US customs bond in place; all outbound flows are screened for OFAC/Canadian sanctions alignment and reported at CAD 10,000+ thresholds as required.Vanilla deliverable forward foreign-exchange contracts (CAD/USD and CAD/CNH) with a Schedule I Canadian bank or licensed derivatives dealer for firm purchase ordersNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing onshore CNY to hedge Chinese supplier cost exposure without requiring offshore CNH fundingCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) between CAD/USD for longer-tenor structured trade-finance facility tranchesOver-the-counter European-style currency options (CAD call / USD put and USD call / CNH put) to protect against adverse currency moves on US-bound baijiu receivables
