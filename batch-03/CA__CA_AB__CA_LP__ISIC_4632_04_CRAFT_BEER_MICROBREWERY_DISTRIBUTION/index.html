Governing statute

Alberta Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c P-3, including the limited-partnership provisions in Part 3 (ss. 51–68), and the regulations and prescribed forms under that Act. An Alberta limited partnership is registered, not incorporated, and does not have a corporate board.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow requirement applies to an Alberta limited partnership. The limited partnership agreement should specify each partner’s contribution and the limited partner’s liability is generally limited to the amount contributed or agreed to be contributed, subject to statutory exceptions. At least one general partner is required and bears general-partner liability. A general partner that is a corporation or other entity must be validly formed and maintained.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); principal portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO) via the Canada Business Portal.

E-government filing portal

Foreign partnerships doing business in Canada must (i) obtain a standardized 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) filed through Business Registration Online, by fax, or by mail; (ii) append the GST/HST program account (suffix 'RT') at registration; (iii) complete Alberta extra-provincial LP registration with the Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta / myAlberta eBiz) prior to commencing distribution in Alberta; (iv) if a non-resident partnership, file Form RC4635 (or apply for GST/HST registration under ETA subsection 240(1)) and make a 'Part XIII extension' application to avoid gross withholding on partnership income; and (v) obtain an AGLC liquor wholesale/manufacturer authorization (Alberta Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis) for the keg wholesale activity, since the excise and liquor regime is administered separately from CRA income tax registrations.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: the Alberta Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA) — the LP files Form T5013 (Partnership Information Return) but pays no federal income tax; each partner includes its allocated share of income/loss on a calendar-year basis under ITA subsection 96(1). For GST/HST purposes the LP is treated as a separate 'person' (ETA section 272.1) and is the registered supplier for all taxable supplies made in Canada. Non-resident partners are subject to Canadian withholding tax (Part I, not Part XIII) on the Canadian-source partnership income at progressive rates, and the partnership may be liable for the related remittance obligations. Territorial scope: Canada taxes worldwide income of partnerships with Canadian-resident partners; a non-resident partnership is only taxable on Canadian-source income. Default tax classification

Alberta imposes no provincial retail sales tax; only 5% federal GST applies at the point of wholesale supply in Alberta (Alberta exempt from HST). No tax-exempt zone applies to the LP.

Available election forms

Under the ITA, (i) an LP may make a subsection 97(2) election to transfer assets to the partnership on a tax-deferred rollover basis; (ii) the partnership can elect under ITA subsection 96(3) for a trailing year-end; (iii) non-resident partners may jointly elect under ITA subsection 216(1) (or for partnership income, ordering rules under sections 115 and 115.1) to file a Part I return on a taxable Canadian income basis rather than be subject to flat 25% withholding; (iv) for GST/HST, an import or export zero-rating election can apply under sections 180 or 209 of the Excise Tax Act for the supply of goods exported to non-residents; (v) the LP may elect under ETA section 191 for items; (vi) Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' regime — CRA will look through the legal form of the foreign entity; a foreign limited liability partnership providing corporate attributes may be recharacterized as a corporation under the 'association/business trust' rules or the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR, ITA s. 245) if hybrid features would create a mismatch.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters (the 'small supplier' threshold); non-resident suppliers carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies above this threshold must register (ETA s. 240), and CRA may require a refundable security deposit (ETA s. 240.1) to cover future GST/HST liabilities, usually equal to 50% of the highest annual net tax assessment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Sales of craft beer that the supplier exports from Canada are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V (s. 20-24) when the supplier establishes that the goods were shipped directly to the non-resident purchaser as part of the supply agreement and documentary evidence of export is retained. Wholesale supplies to licensed export houses (e.g., a bonded warehouse) are also zero-rated under s. 22 at the time of transfer to the export warehouse. Zero-rating is denied when beer is delivered in Canada to a resident customer (intra-Alberta keg distribution is taxable at 5% GST). Services relating to the exported kegs (e.g., cold-chain logistics, customer-specific labelling prep) are zero-rated as 'services in respect of personal property exported' or as supplies to a non-resident under Schedule VI, Part V ss. 7 and 8.

Since 1 January 2024, GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1 million must file returns and information electronically (ETA s. 276.1; CAD 500,000 threshold from 2029). E-invoicing follows the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (PEPPOL) on a voluntary B2B basis. There is no API-based tax authority pre-clearance; however, prescribed records for exported beer must be kept electronically in a CRA-compliant format (including the GST/HST export documentation under ETA s. 286 and Schedule VI Part V), and the brewer-distributor must maintain Form GST11 and export evidence to support zero-rating.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully floating currency regime with no exchange controls. Both current account transactions (trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, royalties) and capital account transactions (direct investment, portfolio investment, loans, capital contributions) are freely convertible. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is unrestricted; however, payments made by a Canadian resident partnership to non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty. Craft beer wholesale distribution is a standard current-account activity and does not require central bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists in Canada to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or to convert foreign currency earnings into Canadian dollars. An Alberta exporter may retain all export receipts in foreign bank accounts or convert them through commercial banks at market rates. There is no mandatory conversion ratio or filing deadline for the repatriation of export proceeds, although cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange matters are also overseen by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a principal banking channel. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs), including UK/FCA and EU/EMI-licensed entities, are not authorized to provide Canadian-dollar clearing or deposit-taking services in Canada. An offshore EMI may be used only as a supplementary payments tool for small-value foreign supplier disbursements, provided the EMI holds a Canadian money services business (MSB) registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and complies with FINTRAC reporting. The primary operating account must remain at a Canadian Schedule I bank or a provincial credit union.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

An Alberta limited partnership is generally not itself subject to Canadian income tax. Taxable income and losses flow through to the partners under the Income Tax Act. Corporate partners generally face a 15% federal general rate plus an 8% Alberta general corporate rate, producing a combined 23% general rate; qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations may access lower federal and Alberta small-business rates, subject to eligibility and allocation rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding generally applies to taxable dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident. A limited partnership distribution is generally not a dividend; its treatment depends on the character of the underlying income, partnership status, Canadian-source rules, and any applicable withholding regime. Amounts allocated to a non-resident partner may require Canadian filing, withholding, or remittance analysis rather than dividend withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 return is generally due within five months after the end of the partnership fiscal period. A partnership with a December 31 year-end generally files by May 31 of the following year. Partnership fiscal periods and year-end changes are subject to Income Tax Act restrictions. A non-resident corporate or individual partner generally files its applicable Canadian return by its own statutory deadline, commonly six months after year-end for a corporation and April 30 or June 15 for individuals, subject to balance-payment rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For Canadian non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents, contemporaneous documentation is required under section 247 of the Income Tax Act. The T106 reporting threshold is generally CAD 1,000,000 of aggregate reportable non-arm's-length non-resident transactions, calculated under the applicable rules. Canada does not impose a universal statutory Local File or Master File threshold identical to the OECD three-tier terminology. Country-by-Country reporting generally applies to a multinational enterprise group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million and a Canadian reporting entity or constituent-entity connection. CRA may request contemporaneous documentation within the statutory response period.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate general Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to some offshore jurisdictions. Substance is evaluated through the Income Tax Act, transfer-pricing arm's-length rules, the general anti-avoidance rule, beneficial-ownership principles, treaty anti-abuse provisions, partnership provisions, and judicial tests concerning sham, purpose, control, and commercial reality. The LP should maintain Canadian commercial substance proportionate to its activities, including genuine distribution functions, inventory and cold-chain controls, personnel or outsourced operational oversight, decision-making records, customer and supplier contracts, banking controls, books and records, liquor and excise licences, and documented risk assumption. A partnership cannot convert Canadian-source operating profit into exempt foreign income solely through contractual labels or offshore ownership.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil reassessment, arrears interest, gross-negligence penalties, repeated-failure penalties, and possible criminal prosecution. Tax evasion under the Income Tax Act may result in a fine between 50% and 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Related GST/HST and excise offences may carry additional fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: An LP has general and limited partners rather than directors, although a corporate general partner may have directors and officers. Individuals who authorize, participate in, assent to, or acquiesce in contraventions may face personal prosecution, fines, restitution, and imprisonment. Corporate separateness is not a defence to personal criminal conduct. Travel restrictions are not automatic, but bail conditions, passport surrender, arrest warrants, or border consequences may arise in criminal proceedings.
Unlawful Liquor, Excise, Customs, or Distribution Operations

Statutory sanction: Licence suspension or cancellation, seizure and forfeiture of beer, equipment and vehicles, assessment of unpaid duty, GST/HST, provincial markup and penalties, administrative monetary penalties, prosecution, fines, and imprisonment under applicable federal excise, customs, and provincial liquor legislation.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly operate, direct, permit, or assist unlicensed liquor or excise activity may be personally prosecuted. A corporate general partner's directors and officers may face personal exposure for their own conduct or statutory director-liability provisions. There is no automatic civil veil piercing solely because the entity is an LP.
Transfer Pricing, Profit Diversion, and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing reassessment under section 247, income adjustment, withholding-tax assessment, interest, contemporaneous-documentation penalties, T106 penalties, gross-negligence penalties, and potential application of the general anti-avoidance rule or treaty anti-abuse provisions.

Director personal liability: Partners and corporate officers may be personally liable for knowingly false records, deliberate tax evasion, or participation in fraudulent arrangements. Commercial transfer-pricing disagreement alone ordinarily does not create personal criminal liability or automatic veil piercing.
AML, Proceeds of Crime, and Beneficial-Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties, compliance orders, disclosure to law-enforcement authorities, seizure and forfeiture, proceeds-of-crime prosecution, and criminal penalties. Money-laundering offences may carry imprisonment of up to 10 years for indictable offences, with additional sanctions for terrorist-financing conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, partners, compliance officers, and other individuals may be personally prosecuted for knowing participation, counselling, direction, or wilful blindness. Conviction may result in fines, restitution, imprisonment, asset forfeiture, and court-imposed release or travel conditions. Entity-level compliance does not protect an individual who intentionally conceals ownership or criminal proceeds.
Payroll, GST/HST, and Trust-Fund Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: Trust assessments, interest, civil penalties, director or responsible-person liability, garnishment, liens, seizure, collection enforcement, and prosecution. A director may be assessed personally for unremitted source deductions and certain indirect taxes where statutory due-diligence defences are unavailable.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner and persons responsible for remittances may face personal statutory assessments. The due-diligence defence requires documented reasonable steps to prevent the failure. Imprisonment is possible for wilful tax offences, but ordinary late payment does not automatically create imprisonment or a travel ban.
