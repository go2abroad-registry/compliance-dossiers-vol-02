Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - federal tax administration portal: My Business Account and Business Registration Online at canada.ca/revenue-agency. Customs counterpart: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) CARM/Assessment and Revenue Management portal. Provincial counterpart: Service Alberta / Alberta Corporate Registry (limited partnership registration); Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under a Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement, and Alberta levies no provincial sales tax or separate VAT, so no additional provincial indirect-tax registration is required.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Alberta limited partnership: (1) file the initial limited partnership declaration under the Alberta Partnership Act (RSA 2000 c. P-3, Part 2) with the Alberta Corporate Registry / Service Alberta to obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number; (2) after partnership registration, apply to the CRA via Business Registration Online, or for a non-resident partnership with no Canadian-resident partner or administrator, file Form RC148 'Application for Registration for Non-Resident Businesses', to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN); the application must list all partners with their foreign tax identification numbers and provide a Canadian address for service or an authorized representative; CRA may require security from a non-resident registrant under ETA s. 241; (3) open program accounts on the BN: RT (GST/HST) - mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter plus the four preceding quarters, and recommended for a pure exporter to claim input tax credits; RM (import/export) for customs transactions, and RC for payroll if staff are hired; (4) no separate Alberta corporate TIN or provincial sales-tax account is issued; Alberta corporate income tax is reported on the federal T2 return with AT1 provincial schedules; (5) because the LP is a flow-through, each partner must also obtain a CRA BN or SIN.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. Under Income Tax Act s. 96 the Alberta limited partnership is not a taxable entity; income, losses, and tax credits flow through to the partners, and non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on their share of Canadian-source partnership business income (with Part XIII withholding applicable only to non-resident allocations paid or credited to the partnership income of a non-resident). For GST/HST, the LP itself is a 'person' and the registrant under the Excise Tax Act. Alberta is a non-participating province, so only the 5% federal GST applies; the LP is eligible for partnership elections and flow-through treatment, and is outside any tax-exempt zone. — For a non-resident-owned Alberta limited partnership: (1) file the initial limited partnership declaration under the Alberta Partnership Act (RSA 2000 c. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under Income Tax Act s. 96 the Alberta limited partnership is not a taxable entity; income, losses, and tax credits flow through to the partners, and non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on their share of Canadian-source partnership business income (with Part XIII withholding applicable only to non-resident allocations paid or credited to the partnership income of a non-resident). For GST/HST, the LP itself is a 'person' and the registrant under the Excise Tax Act. Alberta is a non-participating province, so only the 5% federal GST applies; the LP is eligible for partnership elections and flow-through treatment, and is outside any tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; flow-through status is statutory. Available elections include: (1) ITA s. 97(2) election to transfer property to the partnership at tax cost on formation; (2) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) to use a non-calendar year end; (3) ETA s. 156 election (Form GST25) to treat certain supplies between closely related corporations/partnerships as not being a supply for GST/HST; (4) ETA quick-method election (Form GST74) for small registrants; and (5) hybrid-mismatch and EIFEL interest-limitation rules (enacted 2022) which can disallow deductions on double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes involving hybrid entities - there is no elective opt-out, but a US LLC corporate partner should be documented under a Section 97(2)/transfer-pricing-compliant structure to minimize hybrid outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the Excise Tax Act small-supplier threshold (s. 148), measured on total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) for the current and preceding four calendar quarters; voluntary registration is permitted below that threshold and is virtually always advisable for a zero-rated exporter because it permits recovery of input tax credits and removes the need to prove the threshold is exceeded month-by-month.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI Part I (s. 1), the LP may zero-rate the exported dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper where: (1) the buyer is a non-resident; (2) possession is transferred to the buyer, carrier, or freight forwarder in Canada and the goods are shipped directly to a destination outside Canada; (3) export occurs as soon as a reasonable time after the sale (CRA administrative guidance generally accepts up to one year, with 180 days as the recommended internal benchmark); (4) the LP holds contemporaneous proof-of-export documentation; and (5) the supply is not caught by any exception (e.g., goods leased or licensed for use in Canada, or exported for further processing by a related party under a specified false-export arrangement). Ancillary services performed outside Canada for a non-resident (freight, inspection, storage) are zero-rated as offshore services under Schedule VI Part V. The export invoice must state 'zero-rated' and be excluded from net tax; full input tax credits are claimable on inputs attributable to zero-rated exports. Intercompany export invoices must satisfy ITA s. 247 arm's-length pricing.

  • CBSA/CERS Customs Export Declaration (B13A electronic export report filed only when a declaration is triggered; retained with the export file when filed)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, rail, or truck) or Cargo Waybill naming the non-resident consignee and showing the outbound port of exit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 wire log / export letter of credit records showing receipt of funds from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract with INCOTERMS (FOB/CIF/CFR foreign port) allocating export responsibility
  • Carrier or freight forwarder certificate of export plus CBSA marine cargo report (Customs Act s. 12) corroborating physical exit
  • Certificate of Origin (where an FTA preference is claimed) and CFIA or destination-country phytosanitary/food-safety certificates (where required)

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance API for invoices. All GST/HST registrants must issue ordinary tax invoices or simplified invoices showing the supplier BN, name, and address; no government validation is required. However, registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically and remit by EFT. In the customs corridor, Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) operates mandatory electronic gateways: the eManifest portal for carrier cargo reporting and the CARM system for customs accounting; Canada Export Reporting System (CERS) is used for B13A export declarations where required, though bulk dehydrated garlic and crushed chili pepper (ISIC 4638 / NAICS 424490) are non-controlled commodity exports and ordinarily require no affirmative export declaration apart from carrier eManifest data.

CRA audit practice for zero-rated exporters requires a complete and contemporaneous documentary chain; absent that evidence, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST and the CRA reassesses gross proceeds, with compound daily interest at prescribed rates, plus civil and criminal penalties: (1) gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the net tax under ETA s. 285.1; (2) knowingly false statements or evasion - ETA s. 285 criminal fines and imprisonment; (3) income-tax gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated income under ITA s. 163(2) for related-party mispricing; (4) denial or clawback of input tax credits traced to undocumented export inventory; (5) six-year record-retention duty under ETA s. 286 and ITA s. 230, with penalties for destruction of records; and (6) extended reassessment windows - seven years for misrepresentation, unlimited for fraud. CRA may also audit the exporter's carried-forward ITC pool and recover refunds previously paid.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a floating exchange rate regime with full current-account convertibility under the Foreign Exchange Act. Capital-account transactions require voluntary repatriation reporting to the Bank of Canada. Profit remittances to non-resident limited partners are permitted without prior approval, subject only to tax filing and FINTRAC compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the Bank of Canada. Receipts must be repatriated and converted to CAD within 90 days or per contractual terms. All export transactions require prior declaration to the Canada Border Services Agency via the Automated Export Reporting System (AERS).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not viable for corporate treasury operations due to strict FINTRAC regulations prohibiting unlicensed money services businesses and restrictions on crypto-asset dealings for Canadian entities.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% (15% federal + 8% Alberta provincial; levied on corporate partners of the pass-through LP; the limited partnership itself has no separate corporate income tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-resident shareholders. Limited partnership profit allocations/distributions are not dividends and are generally exempt from Part XIII WHT; non-resident partners remain taxable under Part I on allocated Alberta business income. Treaty-reduced dividend rates commonly 5% (≥10% corporate ownership) or 15% (portfolio) under the Canada-US treaty; 10% for qualifying corporate shareholders under the Canada-China DTA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within five months after the partnership fiscal period-end; e.g., a December 31 year-end LP must file by May 31 of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the corporate partner's taxation year-end; non-resident corporations with a Canadian permanent establishment via the LP must file and pay Part I tax by the same deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: contemporaneous documentation expected where the taxpayer's gross revenue exceeds CAD 5 million and controlled cross-border transactions exceed CAD 50,000; Master File and Country-by-Country Report at CAD 750 million consolidated group revenue (CRA administrative thresholds; no separate CAD threshold exemption under ITA s.247).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) applicable to Alberta LPs; CRA applies the GAAR in ITA s.245, the sham/substance-over-form doctrine, and treaty limitation-on-benefits/beneficial-ownership analysis. An Alberta LP must have real business activity, Canadian-resident general-partner management, and a valid commercial purpose for exporting dehydrated garlic/chili. Limited partners who participate in management forfeit limited liability; loss deductions are restricted to the at-risk amount under ITA s.96(2.2).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s.239: fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment for up to five years; civil gross-negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax, plus daily compound interest on unpaid amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors/general partners who participated in the evasion may be personally assessed and prosecuted; CRA can impose a court-ordered departure ban under ITA s.179.1 for serious tax debt, freeze personal assets, and obtain seizure orders.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess the LP's income, impose transfer-pricing penalty under ITA s.247(3) equal to 10% of the net adjustment, add interest, and make a consequential adjustment to any related Canadian corporate partner's return.

Director personal liability: Not automatic, but directors who knowingly sign false T2/T5013 schedules or direct the mispricing may face gross-negligence penalties and criminal liability; treaty APAs/MAP procedures do not waive domestic penalties.
Artificial Shell / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR can disallow the entire tax benefit under ITA s.245; reassessments with interest; CRA may also deny treaty benefits on the ground that the foreign partner is not the beneficial owner; penalty under ITA s.163(2) if the misrepresentation is knowing.

Director personal liability: A shell LP may be disregarded, exposing the general partner to unlimited liability for partnership debts; directors of a corporate general partner can be held personally liable for CRA withholding and for fraudulent conveyance where assets have been stripped.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC penalties for money-laundering violations under PCMLTFA: up to CAD 1 million per administrative violation for entities; criminal money-laundering under Criminal Code s.462.31 carries imprisonment up to 10 years; proceeds are subject to confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors/officers who approve or knowingly implement laundering transactions face personal criminal prosecution, confiscation, and extradition; insurance or corporate indemnity generally will not cover deliberate criminality.
