Applicable AML statute: Alberta limited partnerships are governed principally by the Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c P-3, and the Limited Partnerships Act, RSA 2000, c L-7, together with applicable regulations. Alberta has no generally applicable beneficial-ownership register for limited partnerships equivalent to the corporate transparency register applicable to Alberta corporations. The federal Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, SC 2000, c 17, and regulations impose AML duties on reporting entities, not generally on an ordinary wholesale limited partnership solely by reason of its formation. Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

An Alberta limited partnership must provide the prescribed Alberta address information for its registered office and records or service contacts as applicable to the filing. A commercial virtual-office address may be acceptable if it is a genuine, authorized address at which required records, notices, and service can be handled. A mailbox-only address may fail registry, banking, tax, or operational due-diligence requirements. Wholesale distribution does not ordinarily require a dedicated retail or warehouse premises at formation, but actual inventory, employees, and logistics arrangements should be accurately documented.

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow requirement for an Alberta limited partnership. Capital contributions may be in cash or property as agreed in the limited partnership agreement, and need not be fully advanced at registration unless the agreement so requires.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal authority for GST/HST, income tax, partnership information returns and Business Number (BN) issuance; portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) via https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Provincial counterpart: Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) at https://www.alberta.ca/business-registration for limited partnership constitutive registration under the Partnership Act (RSA 2000, c P-3). Export/customs counterpart: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for B13A export declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Alberta Limited Partnership with the Alberta Corporate Registry (OneStop) - no minimum capital, no Canadian residency requirement for partners. Step 2: Obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) from CRA via BRO; open a GST/HST program account (RT) (compulsory once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 p.a., or elect voluntarily for export zero-rating and input tax credit recovery). Step 3: Open CRA Import/Export (RW) program account for CBSA clearance records. Step 4: File Form T5013 partnership information return annually with Schedule 50 (non-resident partners). Step 5: Where the LP is a non-resident registrant with no Canadian presence, appoint an agent for service; the CRA may accept the foreign partner's tax ID as associated identifier. Step 6: Maintain partner-level T1135/T1134 foreign reporting if applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - an Alberta LP is a fiscally transparent partnership under ITA s. 96 (not an entity subject to entity-level income tax); each partner reports its share of income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source partnership income under Part I or Part XIII as applicable. For GST/HST, however, the LP itself is a separate 'person' (registrant) under ETA s. 123(1). Canada does not permit a check-the-box election to convert the LP to corporation; classification follows the Partnership Act and ITA.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; the LP status is statutory. Available elections and mechanisms: (i) Voluntary GST/HST registration election (ETA s. 240(2)) to activate zero-ratings and input tax credits; (ii) ETA s. 156 election for closely related entities (corporate general partner and LP) to treat intra-group supplies as non-taxable; (iii) ITA s. 216(1) election for a non-resident partner to be taxed on a Canadian return only upon partnership income; (iv) Hybrid-mismatch notifications and de minimis exclusions under ITA ss. 18.2-18.4 for cross-border partnership-fiscally-transparent conflicts; (v) ETA s. 243(1) election to align fiscal year for GST/HST returns; (vi) Alberta does not offer a provincial check-the-box, and the LP is exempt from Alberta corporate income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1)(c): a person (including an LP) making taxable supplies in Canada below CAD 30,000 during the immediately preceding four calendar quarters is not required to register; once exceeded, registration is compulsory. Non-resident LPs may voluntarily register regardless of threshold to zero-rate exports and recover input tax credits. ISIC 4646 (wholesale ophthalmic eyewear frames) has no special threshold or statutory licence gate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available under ETA s. 22(1) and Schedule VI, Part V for tangible personal property (acetate eyeglass frames): the LP must (a) deliver the goods to a common carrier or freight forwarder for export to a destination outside Canada, or (b) export the goods in accordance with the Proof of Export Regulations (SOR/91-45), which require documentary evidence of export to be obtained within the prescribed period (CRA administratively expects proof of export within 60 days after the date of supply). The buyer must be a non-resident and title must pass outside Canada; sales to Canadian brokers for later export do not qualify. Associated transport, freight, customs-brokerage, and insurance services supplied to the non-resident buyer in respect of the exported goods are also zero-rated where the underlying supply is zero-rated (ETA s. 20 and Schedule VI, Part V); business-support services (offshore logistics and marketing) supplied to non-resident optical retailers not registered for GST/HST may qualify as zero-rated export services under Schedule VI, Part VII.

  • CBSA B13A Canada Export Declaration (electronic) identifying the harmonised-system code for acetate optical frames and the foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill naming the non-resident buyer or its agent and evidencing movement from Canada
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Alberta LP to the foreign wholesale optical customer
  • Proof-of-export certificate from the freight forwarder or customs broker (delivery confirmation, terminal receipt)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice confirming collection of export proceeds in the LP's Canadian account
  • Executed supply contract, purchase order, destination address evidence, and correspondence with the foreign customer

Canada has no real-time government fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no mandatory API-based invoice approval regime for B2B invoices. Electronic invoices are accepted provided they meet ETA s. 223 record-keeping and the prescribed content (seller name and BN, date, description, consideration, GST rate). CRA directs electronic records retention for six years. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing initiative (Peppol BIS 3.0) is voluntary adoption by federal/provincial governments, not a compliance mandate. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE or GST/TELEFILE) and to large partnerships' T5013 returns (Internet File Transfer); no invoice-level clearance or stamping is required for wholesale export transactions.

Export zero-rating claims are priority audit targets for the CRA. The registrant must retain the complete export evidence bundle for six years (ETA s. 286). Where proof-of-export documentation is missing, late, or internally inconsistent, CRA will reassess the supply as taxable at 5%, claw back input-tax credits or refunds, and charge compound daily arrears interest (ETA s. 280(1)). Gross negligence penalties can reach 50% of the understated net tax (ETA s. 285); wilful evasion or forged export documents constitute a criminal offence under ETA s. 327(1) with fines up to CAD 200,000 and/or imprisonment for up to two years. The normal four-year reassessment period extends to ten years where misrepresentation is proven. Canada sets no statutory FX-repatriation deadline, but the 180-day benchmark applied in this dossier reflects the CRA's documentary consistency expectations; failure to repatriate export proceeds within that period, without a commercial explanation, invites sham recharacterisation under ITA s. 245 GAAR, denial of the zero-rating, and transfer-pricing penalties under ITA s. 247(3).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains IMF Article VIII current-account convertibility; the Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both capital and current accounts with no statutory approvals, quotas, or licensing for foreign direct investment, capital calls, profit remittances, or loan servicing. The wartime Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1952; the Bank of Canada does not require notification for LP capital subscriptions or distributions. Functional hurdles are tax-driven, not FX-driven: a non-resident LP partner is taxable in Canada under Part I on its share of Canadian-business income (via the partnership's permanent establishment), and passive flows (interest, royalties, management fees) attract Part XIII withholding subject to treaty relief (e.g., under the Canada-US Tax Treaty). Neither issue constitutes a foreign-exchange restriction. Administrative friction arises through bank KYC, corporate substance expectations, and CRS/FATCA self-certification obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or conversion regime exists. Canada does not require export proceeds to be repatriated, converted, or liquidated within any statutory timeline, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio. The LP may hold export receivables in USD/EUR indefinitely in a foreign-currency account at a Schedule I bank, or convert them at market rate. FINTRAC only imposes record-keeping and cross-border EFT reporting (threshold CAD 10,000) plus standard sanctions screening, which the bank executes on its own account.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — Canada's central bank with monetary authority but no exchange-control administration; OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) supervises the Schedule I chartered banks; FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) enforces the PCMLTFA and MSB/EFT reporting; the Minister of Finance administers the Exchange Fund Account under the Financial Administration Act; Payments Canada operates the Lynx high-value CAD settlement system and the ACSS batch retail clearing system. No Canadian authority grants FX permits or approves capital flows.
