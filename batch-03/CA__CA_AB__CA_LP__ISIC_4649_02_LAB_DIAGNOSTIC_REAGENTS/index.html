Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada qualify for zero-rated GST treatment provided the supplier demonstrates export through customs documentation and the goods do not enter the Canadian market.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

All GST/HST registrants must submit returns and payments electronically through the CRA's My Business Account portal. Real-time fiscal clearance and digital stamping are not required; however, advanced digital reporting may apply to large registrants under CRA's modernization efforts.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims result in penalties of up to 200% of the evaded tax, plus interest and potential criminal sanctions. CRA conducts rigorous audits with retrospective adjustments and clawback provisions for non-compliant taxpayers.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general Canadian corporate headline rate (15% federal Part I rate plus 8% Alberta provincial corporate tax rate). The Alberta Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes and is not itself subject to Part I corporate income tax; each partner is taxed on its allocated share of partnership income or loss.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to taxable dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders. Ordinary profit allocations paid by an Alberta Limited Partnership to its partners are not dividends; they are allocations of partnership income taxable under Part I at the partner level. If a corporate partner pays a dividend out of Canadian partnership earnings, the 25% statutory withholding rate applies unless reduced by treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with any single non-resident related party triggers Form T106 reporting. Country-by-country reporting (Form RC4649) is triggered where the multinational group has consolidated revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared and retained for all reportable transactions; there is no general exemption for small wholesale distributors.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulation similar to certain offshore regimes. However, the Canada Revenue Agency applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under ITA s.245, the sham doctrine, and the substance-over-form principle. An Alberta Limited Partnership used solely as a tax conduit, without real local employees, warehouse inventory, business premises, or risk control over its IVD reagent distribution, will be disregarded or recharacterized. Non-resident partners may then be deemed to carry on business in Canada through a permanent establishment, and treaty benefits may be denied under limitation-on-benefits or beneficial-ownership provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Failure to File

Statutory sanction: ITA s.238(1): failure to file an income tax return is an offence punishable by a fine between C$1,000 and C$25,000 plus imprisonment. ITA s.239(1): tax evasion is punishable on summary conviction by a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years, or on indictment by imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil reassessment is unlimited where misrepresentation was fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of a corporate general partner who direct or participate in the evasion are personally exposed to prosecution, fines, imprisonment, restitution orders, and asset freezes. Canadian courts may pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a mere shield for the fraud, and travel bans may be imposed as a bail or sentencing condition.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable income under ITA s.247(2). A penalty of 10% of the gross downward transfer pricing adjustment applies under s.247(4) where reasonable efforts were not made. Administrative penalties for late or incomplete T106 reporting attach in addition to interest; gross negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax may also apply under s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Officers who sign false T106 or T2 filings can be charged under the Criminal Code and ITA as principals or accessories. Directors may be personally liable in civil fraud where they orchestrate the mispricing; corporate veil defenses are weak where intercompany invoices and tax filings knowingly misrepresent the true commercial arrangement.
Shell Company / Sham Limited Partnership

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the LP as a sham, apply GAAR under s.245, recharacterize the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, and assess tax on the Canadian permanent establishment as if the non-resident partners carried on business in Canada through the Alberta entity. Reassessments beyond the normal three-year period apply for misrepresentation; penalties and interest run from the original due date.

Director personal liability: A corporate general partner is jointly and severally liable on all partnership obligations under the Alberta Partnership Act. Directors who knowingly execute a sham structure can be held personally liable for corporate and partnership taxes as the directing mind, and limited partners who take part in control may forfeit their limited liability shield.
