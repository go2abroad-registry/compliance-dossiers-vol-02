Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under Alberta law. Each limited partner must make or agree to make a capital contribution in cash, property, or other consideration as recorded in the Limited Partnership Agreement and the partnership register. Contribution funds may be held in escrow pending closing conditions; withdrawals of contributed capital are restricted to preserve solvency.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement. Limited partnership formation is registered with the Alberta Corporate Registry (Alberta Corporate Access).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the limited partnership with Alberta Corporate Registry (file declaration and obtain Registration Certificate). Step 2: Apply to CRA via BRO or Form RC1 for a 9-digit Business Number and the applicable program accounts (GST/HST and partnership income tax/T5013). Step 3: If no Canadian-resident partner, appoint a Canadian agent for service. Step 4: Non-resident partners obtain CRA individual/corporate account numbers. Step 5: Enroll in GST/HST NETFILE and EFT remittance if annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Alberta Limited Partnership has no separate tax personality under Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 96; income/loss is computed at partnership level and allocated to partners. Limited partners are subject to at-risk rules. Non-resident partners are taxed only on income attributable to the Canadian business (Territorial_Scope); ITA s. 197 recharacterizes publicly traded 'SIFT' partnerships as corporations. Alberta does not levy PST or capital tax on the partnership.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal tax law. Applicable elections: (i) non-calendar-year partnership fiscal period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) where all partners are non-residents and the business is carried on outside Canada; (ii) GST/HST s. 156 election for nil consideration among closely related entities; (iii) s. 167 election for GST/HST consequences on partnership wind-up; (iv) foreign hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3, 20.1 and 94 apply automatically and cannot be overridden by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (excluding financial services, capital property and goodwill) made in Canada during the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident soliciting orders or offering goods for sale in Canada is deemed to carry on business in Canada (ETA s. 143) and must register even below CAD 30,000. A non-resident making only zero-rated exports is not required but may elect to register under ETA s. 240 to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods (SSD and CPU chips) may be zero-rated under ETA s. 23 and Schedule VI, Part V if exported in the course of the supply or by the non-resident recipient as part of the supply; documentary proof of export, normally with shipment within 60 days, is required. Brokerage or arranging services supplied to a non-resident principal in respect of goods located outside Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, item 21. Input tax credits are fully recoverable on inputs attributable to zero-rated exports; unused credits are refunded.

None. Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, no invoice stamping, and no mandatory e-invoicing regime. CRA requires electronic filing for GST/HST registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million and electronic T2 filing for corporations with gross revenue over CAD 1 million. Voluntary e-invoicing follows the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (Peppol BIS 3.0); CRA data exchange uses NETFILE, EDI and REST/JSON APIs.

CRA applies intensive scrutiny to zero-rating refund claims; missing or inconsistent export documents result in reassessment at 5% GST, denial of input tax credits, disallowance of T5013 losses/allocations, and clawback of refunds with interest. Ordinary reassessment window is four years (ETA s. 298), but fraud or misrepresentation extends the period indefinitely. Civil penalties: ETA s. 280(1) of CAD 250 to CAD 25,000 plus 10% of net tax evaded; ITA s. 163(2) penalty of 50% of understated tax. Criminal evasion is punishable under ITA s. 239 and ETA s. 328 with fines of 50%-200% of tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and does not impose exchange controls, capital repatriation permits, or profit remittance licensing. An Alberta limited partnership may freely receive foreign capital contributions and repatriate profits, subject to standard withholding-tax obligations under the Income Tax Act for non-residents receiving distributions characterized as interest, deemed royalties, or management fees. All conversions are executed on the floating interbank market at negotiated ADI rates. There is no central-bank pre-approval for equity injections, trade settlements, or dividend distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed timeline for export receipts. An Alberta-limited-partnership exporter of SSD and CPU wholesale inventory may hold proceeds in CAD, retain USD or other foreign currency in a licensed foreign-currency account, or convert at its own risk. The only relevant obligations are statistical reporting to Statistics Canada for balance-of-payments surveys and CRS/FATCA disclosure forms for financial accounts held by non-residents.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada and Department of Finance; Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange administration authority. Domestic banking supervision sits with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, while AML/ATF supervision rests with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with strict legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI solutions can provide useful ancillary FX and disbursement channels, but they are not licensed Canadian banks and cannot act as a substitute for Schedule I banking infrastructure in an escrow-inspection-wire model. A Canadian controlled goods and semiconductor wholesale structure requires a Canadian-regulated bank to hold the escrow account, issue commercial letters of credit, and clear MT103 settlement wires. Offshore EMIs may require FINTRAC registration if serving Canadian customers, and they generally cannot support the documentary-inspection or third-party-escrow mechanics required for SSD/CPU open-market transactions. Viable for low-value operational payments after full compliance validation, but not for principal clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or working-capital injections should be structured as SWIFT MT103 transfers from the investor's bank to the Alberta LP's Canadian operating account using the LP's registered legal name and Canadian account number. The bank credit narrative should state clearly that the funds are an equity capital contribution for the acquisition of SSD/CPU inventory and related treasury requirements. Correspondent banks will require a FATCA/CRS classification and source-of-funds evidence, so the sending bank should attach the executed capital subscription agreement, the LP's Canadian bank account confirmation, and a copy of the Alberta LP registration. For high-value injections exceeding $100,000 CAD, Canadian banks will generally invoke enhanced due diligence and may request the underlying purchase commitment, identity of end-sellers, and a certificate of incumbency. Use the country and beneficiary fields precisely; do not split funds to avoid monitoring thresholds. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Not applicable to the Alberta Limited Partnership itself (fiscally transparent pass-through); corporate partners are subject to the combined federal-Alberta general corporate income tax rate of 23% (15% federal + 8% Alberta). For Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) eligible for the Small Business Deduction, the combined rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income is 17% (9% federal + 8% Alberta).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act. For an Alberta limited partnership, allocations paid or credited to a non-resident limited partner are treated as deemed dividends under s.212(1.1), attracting 25% withholding at source unless a tax treaty reduces the rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than 5 months after the partnership's fiscal period end. For a calendar-year fiscal period ending December 31, T5013 slips must be furnished to partners by March 31 and the T5013 return must be filed with CRA by May 31 of the following calendar year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For an Alberta limited partnership, the T106 return is due by the same date as the T5013 Partnership Information Return: no later than 5 months after the partnership's fiscal period end.
