Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and Represent a Client portal. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers import and export customs processes through the Canada Border Services Agency Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) system. Alberta corporate and partnership registrations are administered separately by Alberta Registries.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian limited partnership or foreign entity carrying on business in Canada should obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online, by telephone, or by submitting the applicable RC1 registration form. The partnership must provide its legal name, Alberta registration details, principal business activity, business address, responsible-person information, formation documents, and representative authorization where applicable. A GST/HST program account is added to the BN when registration is required or voluntarily elected. A non-resident entity generally appoints an authorized representative, provides evidence of Canadian business activities and supplies, and may be required to register for customs, payroll, corporate income tax, or non-resident withholding accounts depending on its activities. The BN and program-account registration do not replace Alberta partnership registration, customs enrolment, import-export account requirements, or applicable municipal and sector registrations. For a Canadian limited partnership, income tax is generally assessed at the partner level rather than by filing a corporate income tax return for the partnership; the partnership may nevertheless have information-reporting obligations, including a T5013 Partnership Information Return where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no general United States-style check-the-box election for Canadian limited partnerships. A Canadian limited partnership is generally treated as a partnership for Canadian income tax purposes, with income and losses allocated to partners under the partnership agreement and applicable tax rules. Available elections are transaction-specific and may include a subsection 97(2) tax-deferred rollover for qualifying transfers of property to a Canadian partnership, section 85 elections where the statutory conditions are satisfied, and elections relevant to partnership allocations, depreciable property, or resource expenditures. These mechanisms do not convert the partnership into a corporation or create a general entity-classification election. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules apply by operation of law where relevant and do not provide a general elective classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, over the relevant preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter, for the small-supplier test applicable to most businesses. Registration may be voluntary before the threshold is reached. The threshold is assessed under the Excise Tax Act and must be analyzed with associated-person and special-supplier rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods may generally be zero-rated where the goods are supplied for export and exported in circumstances prescribed by the Excise Tax Act and its regulations, including where the supplier delivers or ships the goods outside Canada or obtains and retains satisfactory evidence that the goods were exported and not consumed, used, or supplied in Canada. A sale to a non-resident alone is insufficient if the goods remain in Canada or are acquired for use in Canada. Exported services may be zero-rated only where the statutory service-export conditions are met; the analysis depends on the nature of the service, the recipient's residence, Canadian use or benefit, and specific exclusions. A registrant claiming zero-rating must retain commercial, transport, customs, delivery, and contractual evidence sufficient to establish the statutory conditions and may claim eligible GST input tax credits through its GST return, subject to normal documentation and limitation rules.

  • CBSA export declaration or other applicable export reporting record, including electronic export data where required
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, purchaser, goods, value, terms, and destination
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier record, freight record, or equivalent transport evidence
  • Packing list and proof of delivery or destination outside Canada
  • Purchase order, sales contract, and customer correspondence establishing the export transaction
  • Import or customs entry evidence in the destination jurisdiction where available
  • Evidence of payment or settlement where relevant to corroborate the transaction
  • No general Canadian statutory inward-remittance certificate is required for GST zero-rating, and no universal Canadian export-proceeds repatriation certificate applies

Canada has no general real-time GST fiscal-clearance, electronic tax-invoice stamping, or mandatory nationwide B2B e-invoicing requirement. GST registrants must issue invoices or other prescribed documentation containing required information, retain books and records, and support input tax credit claims. Electronic records and invoices are generally acceptable if they preserve integrity, accessibility, and auditability. CRA electronic filing, online account, and payment channels may be mandatory for specified registrants or filing circumstances, but they do not constitute real-time invoice clearance. Customs declarations and accounting are processed through CBSA systems, including CARM, where applicable.

Canada has no general foreign-exchange-control rule requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. CRA may reassess GST where zero-rating was unsupported, goods were not exported, services failed the export conditions, or records were inadequate. The reassessment may include GST collectible, denial or repayment of related input tax credits, arrears interest, and applicable administrative penalties. False statements or omissions may attract a penalty of up to 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit, subject to statutory rules; gross-negligence penalties may also apply. Repeated or deliberate non-compliance can result in prosecution and fines. Customs misdeclarations may trigger duties, GST on importation, administrative monetary penalties under the CBSA compliance framework, seizure, and other customs remedies. Any reassessment is subject to the statutory objection, appeal, limitation, record-retention, and interest rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada maintains no general foreign exchange control laws under the Currency Act or Exchange Control regulations; both current-account (trade in goods and services) and capital-account (direct investment, portfolio flows, loans) transactions are freely permitted without central bank approval. Profit repatriation by non-resident partners of an Alberta limited partnership is unrestricted, subject only to standard corporate tax withholdings under the Income Tax Act Part XIII for non-resident allocations and transfer-pricing documentation where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export proceeds in Canada. Exporters may retain revenue in foreign currency, convert freely at negotiated commercial rates, or hold offshore accounts. Reporting to FINTRAC is required only for wire transfers and large cash transactions under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act thresholds; no surrender or monetization obligation applies to electronic component inventory export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF monitoring; no standalone foreign exchange administration exists in Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is MEDIUM with legal constraints. A fully-operational non-resident electronic money institution (EMI) cannot serve as the primary operating bank for an Alberta-limited-partnership distribution business unless it is registered as a money-services business (MSB) with FINTRAC. Registered Canadian fintech platforms and digital banks are viable for payroll and FX conversion but may lack commercial credit lines and physical cheque clearing. Foreign EMIs regulated under the E.U. or U.K. regimes are not directly licensed in Canada; they may provide auxiliary payment gateway services but cannot issue Canadian settlement accounts or clear CAD wires as a primary bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections to the Alberta LP treasury must be routed through a licensed Schedule I Canadian bank account using SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire for USD inflows. For CAD remittances, use the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) clearing platform. Wire payment must label the purpose code as: 'Treasury capital injection / partnership contribution' (Bank of Canada Payment Clearing code 26 under Canadian Payments Association Payment Type Code 'CO' where applicable). Verification includes matching the remitter name to the LP's general partner or declared investors, confirming beneficial ownership with FINTRAC-compliant origins, and applying enhanced due-diligence for any inflow exceeding C$100,000. No central-bank pre-approval is required, but embargo and sanctions screening against Global Affairs Canada and the United Nations sanctions list is mandatory.Trade settlements for outbound component distribution should be executed via SWIFT MT103 irrevocable payments for high-value individual transactions, and via commercial standby letters of credit (SBLC) or documentary letters of credit (L/C) for secured receivables. For direct CAD payments to domestic Alberta suppliers, use LVTS or Payments Canada Real-Time Rail (RTR) in the near clear. USD-denominated supplier payments are settled through international clearing in New York (CHIPS or Fedwire) via the Canadian bank's nostro accounts. Outbound disbursements over C$10,000 must include FINTRAC-compliant EFT reporting; for exports, invoice number, terms, and Incoterms must be embedded in the MT103 field 72 'Remittance Information'.
