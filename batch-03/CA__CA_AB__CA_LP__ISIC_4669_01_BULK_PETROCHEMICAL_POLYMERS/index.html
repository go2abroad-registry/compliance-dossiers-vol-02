Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital or paid-up capital applies to an Alberta limited partnership. Capital contributions and partnership interests must be specified in the limited partnership agreement and the filed declaration as applicable. No statutory escrow account is required; contributed funds should be paid to the partnership account under the partnership agreement.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal fire-authority review and occupancy approval, including emergency access, extinguishers, fire separations, storage configuration, and emergency response planning. Building and structural approvals must comply with Alberta's Safety Codes Act and applicable Alberta Building and Fire Codes, including floor-load verification for bulk polymer storage. Workplace compliance requires registration and procedures under the Alberta Occupational Health and Safety Act, Regulation, and Code, including hazard assessment, worker training, incident reporting, and WHMIS/SDS controls. Transportation, storage, and handling procedures must address combustible dust, ignition sources, spills, and classified additives where applicable.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital or paid-up capital applies to an Alberta limited partnership. Capital contributions and partnership interests must be specified in the limited partnership agreement and the filed declaration as applicable. No statutory escrow account is required; contributed funds should be paid to the partnership account under the partnership agreement.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The General Partner resolves that the limited partnership be established under the Alberta Partnership Act and that the filed Declaration of Limited Partnership be approved. The partnership interests shall be subscribed and funded in accordance with the limited partnership agreement, with no statutory minimum capital applicable. The registered office and records office stated in the declaration are adopted. The General Partner is authorized to open bank accounts, execute the partnership agreement, obtain tax and business registrations, and appoint the initial officers, authorized signing officers, compliance contact, and service providers. All prior preparatory acts are ratified.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No general Alberta industrial operating licence is required solely for wholesale distribution of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules. Conditional approvals may be required from Alberta Environment and Protected Areas, the Alberta Energy Regulator, municipal authorities, and federal transport authorities depending on storage, processing, imports, releases, and facility design.

Category: Not applicable for ordinary wholesale trading without processing or regulated bulk-storage infrastructure. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); principal federal tax portal is canada.ca/en/revenue-agency with the CRA 'My Business Account' (MyBA) and GST/HST NETFILE/e-file channels. Provincial counterpart for Alberta is the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) (tra.alberta.ca), which administers the Alberta corporate income tax under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement; Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax or provincial VAT.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership (LP) must procure a single CRA Business Number (BN) acting as the statutory TIN. The BN is obtained by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), either online through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by mail/fax to the CRA non-resident taxation division. The LP opens a GST/HST program account with suffix 'RT' on the same BN. GST/HST registration is mandatory where the LP's taxable supplies made in Canada (including zero-rated export supplies) exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter or in any preceding four consecutive calendar quarters under ETA section 148; where the threshold is not met, voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits and to support zero-rating of exported petrochemical granules. The registered identifier takes the format NNNNNNNNN RT0001. For Alberta income tax purposes no separate provincial registration is required (the CRA administers Alberta corporate income tax under the Tax Collection Agreement); the extra-provincial registration of the Alberta LP with Service Alberta Corporate Registry is a commercial law requirement, not a tax registration. The LP must also file T5013 Partnership Information Return and related T5013 slips with the CRA and, where a non-resident partner is involved, maintain a representative in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. An Alberta LP that is not a 'specified investment flow-through' is fiscally transparent under ITA subsection 96(1); the partnership computes income at the entity level and allocates to partners. Available statutory elections include: (i) Excise Tax Act subsection 273(1) closely related partner/partnership election, permitting supplies between a qualifying partner and the LP to be deemed made for nil consideration for GST/HST; (ii) Income Tax Act subsection 216(1) election for a non-resident partner deriving Canadian-source rents or royalties to file a Part I net-basis return rather than suffer gross-basis Part XIII withholding; (iii) Income Tax Act subsection 96(3) functional currency election, allowing the LP to compute Canadian tax results in a permitted currency (e.g., USD) where books of account are maintained in that currency; (iv) ITA subsection 96(11)/section 98.1 elections limiting the withdrawal/dissolution recapture in respect of the deemed disposition of the LP's Canadian business assets. Hybrid mismatch rules contained in draft legislation implementing BEPS Action 2 are not elective and operate automatically; no 'hybrid mismatch election' is currently available under Canadian federal law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 per calendar quarter (or in the aggregate over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters) of worldwide taxable supplies sourced to Canada, per ETA subsection 148(1). The small-supplier threshold includes zero-rated export supplies, since zero-rated supplies remain 'taxable supplies' for threshold calculation; however, exempt and financial supplies are excluded. A non-resident LP selling virgin polyethylene/polypropylene granules from Alberta storage into Canada is registrant-mandatory once the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded, and may voluntarily register before the threshold is crossed in order to zero-rate exports and recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST is available on the export sale of the bulk polyethylene/polypropylene granules under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, sections 1, 2, and 4 for tangible personal property (TPP) that is (a) exported by the supplier as soon after the supply as is reasonable having regard to the circumstances, or (b) delivered to a common carrier for direct export, or (c) shipped by the supplier to the non-resident buyer's address outside Canada, provided no further processing, assembly, or use occurs in Canada. Offshore services sold to non-residents qualify under section 7 of Part V if the service is not rendered in respect of real property situated in Canada nor in respect of TPP situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. The LP must retain contemporaneous written evidence of export, including a CBSA B13A export declaration (or automated CAED/CCS record), a negotiable bill of lading or waybill naming a foreign consignee, the commercial invoice (FOB/Incoterms 2020), the packing list, and proof of settlement of foreign currency through the banking system (SWIFT MT103 or inward remittance credit advice). Where the LP acts as an intermediary and sells to a Canadian trader who later exports, the domestic first sale is not zero-rated; only the transaction completed by the registered exporter with the overseas buyer qualifies.

  • CBSA Canada Export Declaration (Electronic B13A through CAED/CCS-online or CARM) showing the export transaction and statistical value for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000
  • Original negotiable Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) endorsed to the overseas consignee, plus delivery order / cargo manifest evidence
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the Canadian LP in USD or other permitted foreign currency, stating Incoterms 2020 (e.g., FOB Alberta plant / CIF destination port)
  • Packing List detailing product description, HS codes (e.g., HS 3901 polyethylene; HS 3902 polypropylene), net/gross weights, and container/equipment numbers

Canada has no statutory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate; no CRA clearance server pre-authorizes invoices. Electronic invoices in PDF, XML, or EDI formats are legally recognized, provided the record satisfies the ETA section 286 substantiation and six-year retention rules. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns where annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million; all other registrants using a tax preparer must also file electronically via certified GST/HST NETFILE/e-file software. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing standard (Peppol-based) is being adopted voluntarily by the federal government and is not yet mandatory for B2B petrochemical wholesale exporters. The CBSA operates the older Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) and the newer Canada Border Services Agency Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) platform for electronic export declarations, which serve as the functional export clearance record.

The CRA audits export zero-rating claims under GST/HST Memorandum 7-2 (Exports) and 12-10 (Records). Where the LP cannot produce the B13A/B/L/remittance bundle within the CRA's information request period, the CRA reassesses the supply at 5% GST on the gross invoice amount and imposes compound daily arrears interest at the prescribed rate (quoted quarterly). Statutory penalties include: ETA section 285 — greater of CAD 250 and 10% of the net tax understated for failure to report or misreporting; ETA section 280 — 25% of the net tax for false statements or omissions knowingly made; ETA section 327.01 — criminal tax evasion subject to fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment. Retrospective clawback includes reversal of input tax credits, denial of the 0% rate, reassessment within four years of the filing date (or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default), and consequential adjustments under the Income Tax Act (Part I and potentially Part XIII withholding for non-resident partners) together with interest and penalties. The CBSA may also impose administrative penalties under the Customs Act for inaccurate export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada observes full current-account and capital-account convertibility without exchange-control licensing or approval requirements. The Canadian dollar floats freely and is fully tradable onshore and offshore through Schedule I banks. Profit and capital repatriation by non-resident limited partners is not blocked or pre-authorized; the only hurdle is fiscal: partnership distributions to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% under the Income Tax Act (Canada), reducible to the applicable treaty rate upon submission of an NR301/NR303 form or tax clearance evidence. No FX quotas, repatriation sequencing or central-bank permission apply to the LP's USD, EUR or CNH trade flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canadian law imposes no surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio or statutory deadline for export proceeds earned by an Alberta limited partnership. Export receipts may be retained in any convertible currency in foreign-denominated accounts at Canadian or foreign banks. For tax administration, the partnership may elect a functional currency (CAD or USD) under s.261 of the Income Tax Act (Canada) provided the records are auditable in that currency; no FINTRAC or Bank of Canada approval is necessary beyond standard transaction reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and systemic-risk authority; exchange-rate policy co-managed with the Department of Finance Canada; prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision and enforcement by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration and no exchange-control statute of general application.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Low as the primary banking rail; Medium as a supplementary disbursement channel. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex and similar) are not chartered banks under the Bank Act (Canada) and cannot issue or confirm letters of credit or process SWIFT MT700/LC operations. Such entities must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada for payment services, their deposit balances are not CDIC-insured, and their sanctions and FATCA/CRS compliance may be weaker. The LP must therefore maintain its main operating and escrow accounts at a Schedule I bank and may engage an EMI solely for small-value supplier payments after an independent legal and sanctions review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partners subscribe by executing the LP's subscription agreement and remitting capital via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire for US-based partners) to the LP's named CAD or USD account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The remittance information must state 'Capital Contribution - [LP legal name]' and match precisely the KYC-recorded depositor identity. No Bank of Canada approval is required; the bank will internally file large EFT transaction reports where thresholds are met. The compliance file must retain the subscription agreement, source-of-funds declaration, CRS self-certification and partner register to support the credit if requested within the bank's review window.Supplier settlements for polymer procurement are executed through SWIFT MT103/202 for open-account terms using the bank's correspondent network (JPMorgan, Citibank, HSBC, BNY Mellon) with SWIFT GPI tracking; for documentary trade the bank issues an Irrevocable Letter of Credit governed by UCP600 via SWIFT MT700 to the supplier's banker, typically confirmed by a US or EU bank to eliminate country and issuing-bank risk. The standard document package comprises the commercial invoice, full set of clean-on-board bills of lading, certificate of analysis, packing list and inspection certificate. Settlement legs are cleared in CAD, USD, EUR or CNH; USD legs settle via Fedwire/CHIPS, FX legs via CLS, and Canadian-dollar legs via the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the new Real-Time Rail.Permissible outright FX forward contracts on CAD/USD, CAD/EUR and CAD/CNH executed under an ISDA Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex collateral with a Schedule I dealer;Permissible FX swaps (spot/forward combinations) for short-dated liquidity alignment between polymer payables and receivable settlement dates;Permissible cross-currency swaps for long-dated hedging of USD/EUR working-capital and refinancing exposures, plus flexibly structured forwards; NDFs are permissible only where CAD is not the underlying currency (e.g., CNH/USD) and only when booked with a licensed Canadian Schedule I institution; speculative derivative positions are prohibited under the LP's investment policy and would compromise the tax pass-through election if used outside genuine trade hedges.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Low as the primary banking rail; Medium as a supplementary disbursement channel. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex and similar) are not chartered banks under the Bank Act (Canada) and cannot issue or confirm letters of credit or process SWIFT MT700/LC operations. Such entities must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada for payment services, their deposit balances are not CDIC-insured, and their sanctions and FATCA/CRS compliance may be weaker. The LP must therefore maintain its main operating and escrow accounts at a Schedule I bank and may engage an EMI solely for small-value supplier payments after an independent legal and sanctions review.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate rate (15% federal + 8% Alberta, effective 1 January 2025); the Alberta Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent and is not itself assessed to income tax; each partner is taxed on its allocable share at partner-level rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII rate on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident. For an Alberta Limited Partnership, the equivalent statutory rate is 25% of the non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source business income under s. 194 ITA, applied at the partnership level when the income is allocated, not at the time of cash distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed when the total value of reportable transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents exceeds C$1,000,000 in the fiscal year. A Country-by-Country (CbC) report is required when the multinational enterprise group's consolidated revenue is C$950,000,000 or more. Contemporaneous Local File / Master File-style documentation is recommended for all significant cross-border intercompany transactions, without a fixed statutory threshold, to satisfy the 'reasonable efforts' requirement against the s. 247 penalty.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as applied in zero-tax jurisdictions; however, substantive presence is required under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA), the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test for treaty benefits, and the Alberta Partnership Act. The LP distributing petrochemical polymers must maintain genuine decision-making, purchasing, logistics, inventory risk, invoicing, and treasury functions in Alberta. A passive letterbox arrangement or a general partner with no real operations will be disregarded, treaty benefits denied, and Part XIII withholding reapplied at 25%. The LP must also be properly extra-provincially registered if its general partner is outside Alberta.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under s. 239 ITA: fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil reassessment of the full 10-year statutory period, daily compounding interest, and a 50% gross-negligence penalty under s. 163.2 ITA.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII tax under s. 227.1 ITA. Upon criminal prosecution, directors can face restrictive bail conditions including travel bans, asset freezes, extradition requests, and court-ordered civil restitution in favour of the CRA.
Transfer Pricing and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing reassessment under s. 247 ITA with a penalty equal to 10% of the net upward adjustment (minimum C$5,000); denial of deductions for the excessive portion; interest accrues daily on reassessed amounts; repeated failure can escalate to gross-negligence penalties and criminal review.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve distorted intercompany pricing can be assessed the 50% gross-negligence penalty personally under s. 163.2; in the event of corporate insolvency, CRA may institute director negligence claims for corporate tax and penalty liabilities.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245 ITA) applies to deny the tax benefit; treaty benefits are denied under the Principal Purpose Test; the allocation to the non-resident partner is re-sourced as Canadian income subject to 25% Part XIII; a GAAR penalty of 50% of the lopped-off tax benefit may also apply; the Alberta Registrar may cancel or dissolve the LP for failure to maintain a genuine registered address.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who sign statutory declarations without substance can personally face fraud or negligent-misrepresentation claims, CRA penalties under s. 163.2, and professional disciplinary action if licensed; travel restrictions and asset prohibitions can attend criminal fraud investigations.
AML, Sanctions, and Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), FINTRAC may issue administrative monetary penalties up to C$1,000,000 per contravention; violations of the United Nations Act or the Special Economic Measures Act carry fines and imprisonment up to 5 years; assets used in the offence are subject to freezing, seizure, and forfeiture; banks will terminate correspondent relationships.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize or knowingly facilitate AML/CFT violations are personally liable under the PCMLTFA; individual fines, travel bans, arrest warrants, and extradition proceedings may result from RCMP or international enforcement action; directors may also face civil claims and clawbacks in bankruptcy.
