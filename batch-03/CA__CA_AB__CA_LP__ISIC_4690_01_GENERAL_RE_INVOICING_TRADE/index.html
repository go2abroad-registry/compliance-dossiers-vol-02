Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); My Business Account portal at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that carries on business in Canada or is required to file a Canadian income tax return must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA by registering online via Business Registration Online (BRO) or by mailing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). Registration for GST/HST, payroll, import/export, and corporate income tax accounts is consolidated under a single BN. A non-resident without a permanent establishment in Canada may still need a GST/HST account for zero-rated export activities or if it makes taxable supplies in Canada. Foreign partnerships are assigned a BN for each tax account and may appoint a non-resident representative for correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian domestic tax law does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election; partnerships are generally fiscally transparent under the Income Act unless electing under section 216 for investment income or making a joint election under subsection 96(1) to compute income on a fiscal-period basis. Non-resident partners may use the Part XIII withholding regime or, if treaty-protected, file a waiver under section 216. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules in sections 18.2 and 18.3 may recharacterize certain payments if there is a deduction/non-inclusion mismatch arising from a foreign entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada during the preceding four calendar quarters (and crossing the threshold in the current quarter); voluntary registration is permitted for persons making zero-rated exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, exports of goods are generally zero-rated (GST at 0%) if the goods are exported by the supplier, delivered outside Canada within reasonable time, and retained exclusively for use outside Canada. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated if the service is performed wholly outside Canada, or the supply relates to real property outside Canada, or is ancillary to exported goods. Re-invoicing transactions must meet the documentary evidence rules in ETA s.169 and associated SOR/91-45 requirements; the supplier must retain evidence of export (e.g., customs export declaration, shipping documents, proof of receipt outside Canada) within the prescribed books and records retention period.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or CBSA Export Report generated through Canada's Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) when the value of goods exceeds CAD 2,000 or controlled/goods require a permit
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the Canadian vendor / re-invoicing hub showing zero-rated GST
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill indicating Canadian point of origin and foreign destination
  • Proof of export (CBSA stamped or electronic proof, cargo control document and destination terminal receipt)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire advice evidencing foreign currency settlement from the overseas buyer

No federal real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory API e-invoicing regime exists in Alberta or for federal GST. Invoices must meet Income Tax Act records requirements; CRA may require electronic records in a readable format upon audit. Ontario and Quebec have separate e-invoicing/billing requirements only for specified sectors; a re-invoicing hub in Alberta without a physical presence in those provinces is not subject to provincial digital stamping. GST/HST returns and rebate claims must be filed electronically if the business has annual taxable supplies above CAD 1,500,000.

CRA may deny zero-rating and assess uncollected GST plus penalties under ETA s.280(1) if export documentation is insufficient or false. Knowingly making false statements in GST returns attracts penalties of not less than CAD 5,000 and not more than CAD 50,000 (or 50% of the net GST evaded) and criminal prosecution under the Excise Act, 2001 with fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Failure to report foreign property or taxable supplies may result in additional civil penalties and interest. A zero-rating claim can be retroactively clawed back for up to four years from the assessment date, and indefinitely where misrepresentation is attributable to fraud or neglect.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely, and there are no general exchange control restrictions on cross-border capital movements, dividend distributions, or profit remittances by non-resident partners. However, payments must comply with anti-money laundering (AML), sanctions (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions and Global Affairs Canada sanctions administration), and tax reporting obligations. Re-invoicing trade transactions routed through an Alberta limited partnership are generally permissible, but the transactions must be structured with economic substance to satisfy transfer pricing rules under Income Tax Act section 247 and avoid a deemed disposition or withholding tax characterization. Profit remittances to non-resident partners of an Alberta limited partnership are subject to Part XIII withholding tax on certain amounts deemed to be dividends, royalties, or interest, but the partnership itself may be treated as a flow-through entity, requiring careful allocation of income and withholding obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose statutory surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. Exporters and trading entities may retain foreign currency proceeds in offshore or domestic accounts and are not required to convert CAD within any prescribed timeline. Payment settlement for commercial exports must be reported to Statistics Canada under the Canadian International Merchandise Trade statistical program when thresholds are met, and the underlying transactions must be documented for tax and customs purposes. For re-invoicing hubs, no CAD conversion is required on the intercompany settlement leg if the transaction is booked in a foreign currency; nonetheless, the functional currency rules of the Income Tax Act (section 261) apply and the entity must track foreign exchange gains and losses for tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); foreign exchange administration is decentralized, with no comprehensive exchange control law administered by a dedicated FX authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium, subject to legal constraints. Offshor-free digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) accounts with non-Canadian licensees are generally not accepted as primary operating accounts for an Alberta registered limited partnership because the partnership must hold a Canadian bank account for GST/HST registration, tax remittance, and payroll obligations, and banks regulated by OSFI require a Canadian nexus. Foreign fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, or Mercury-style platforms) can be used only as auxiliary settlement rails for low-value trade disbursements and must not be the sole treasury account. The legal constraints include: (i) FINTRAC reporting obligations for the LP as a money services business if it engages in foreign exchange dealing or funds transfer; (ii) the need to establish that the EMI provides pass-through FDIC/IIROC equivalent protection only if applicable; (iii) restrictions under Canadian AML/KYC rules requiring ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) disclosure of all partners; and (iv) the re-invoicing hub’s principal banking relationship must be with a Schedule I bank to maintain clearing access for CAD-denominated payments and to support commercial letters of credit.

Defense evidence: Present the EMI’s regulatory license (e.g., FCA, FinCEN MSB registration, or equivalent), a copy of the executed terms of service, a written explanation of the ancillary use of the wallet, and the LP’s reconciliation process to ensure all funds passing through the wallet are traceable to the operating bank account.
Unexpected sanctions exposure due to counterparty in high-risk jurisdiction

Root cause: Unexpected sanctions exposure due to counterparty in high-risk jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Provide Global Affairs Canada and OFAC sanctions screening reports for all counterparties, a compliance policy including an exclusion list, the partnership’s beneficial ownership declaration, and an opinion from a Canadian trade counsel confirming the goods are not controlled under the Export Control List or the sanctions regimes administered by the United Nations Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections to the Alberta limited partnership must be made through a Canadian chartered bank account using SWIFT wire transfer (MT103/MT202) or internal transfer from the partners. The payment purpose code should align with the Bank of Canada reporting and Statistics Canada International Transactions system; for capital contributions from non-resident partners, use the classification for direct investment or equity injection. The documentation must include: (i) a capital call notice issued by the general partner; (ii) a counterparty remittance instruction referencing the partnership’s full legal name, Alberta registration number, and bank account number; (iii) a copy of the executed partnership agreement evidencing the capital commitment; and (iv) an undertaking to file the prescribed information return under the Income Tax Act for transfers of property to a partnership (Form T5013 and applicable schedule). Funds may not be routed through virtual asset intermediaries or unlicensed money service businesses due to enhanced due diligence requirements under the PCMLTFA.Primary clearing rails for outbound trade disbursements are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct TT wire transfers to suppliers and logistics providers; (ii) commercial letters of credit (L/C) issued by the Alberta LP’s bank on behalf of the entity, typically governed by UCP 600, with the issuing bank as a Canadian Schedule I bank; (iii) documentary collections (DP/DA) using SWIFT MT400 series where appropriate; and (iv) XT Transfer or domestic ACH-style electronic funds transfer via Payments Canada’s Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for CAD-denominated settlements. For re-invoicing trade, the preferred rail is MT103 for high-value supplier payments and commercial L/C where the supplier requires assurance. The entity must include proper transaction narrative referencing the invoice number and underlying sale contract to ensure traceability and to mitigate bank anti-money laundering review.
