Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at canada.ca and CRA My Business Account portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Alberta must obtain a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' online via Business Registration Online (BRO), by mail, or by phone to the CRA; the BN Program Accounts added are RT (GST/HST), RP (Payroll), RC (Corporate Income Tax), and RM (Return Filings). Non-resident limited partners and general partners without Canadian residency must also obtain an individual/corporate BN if required and appoint a Canadian agent for service as applicable; the Alberta Corporate Registry must be satisfied via the LP registration filed under the Partnership Act (Alberta), obtaining a Registry Number correlated to the federal BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; for income tax, the Alberta Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent (income/loss flows to partners under the Income Tax Act, subsection 96(1)); for GST/HST, the partnership is treated as a 'person' for registration, but the general partner may be deemed the registrant in certain statutory valuations and joint election scenarios

Available election forms

Elections available include: (i) partnership is not a taxpayer and allocations are made via the partnership agreement on a split of partnership income; (ii) election under subsection 97(1) for transfers of property to the partnership at agreed amounts; (iii) GST/HST joint election for a general partner to be treated as the registrant for the partnership under GST/HST Memorandum 8-3; (iv) for hybrid entities with US nexus, no elective 'check-the-box' exists in Canadian law, but the Canada-US Treaty Article IV(7) tie-breaker and entity transparency analysis applies; (v) SIFT (Specified Investment Flow-Through) partnership rules may re-characterize certain publicly-traded partnerships as corporations, with a potential anti-deferral election for non-resident investors; (vi) HST/GST transitional elections for real property held by the LP on registration

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual gross worldwide taxable supplies of a Canadian supplier's commercial activity; registration becomes mandatory once taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters exceed CAD 30,000. Non-residents carrying on retail business in Canada automatically become registrants once the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded; however, for non-residents that do not carry on business in Canada, zero-rating rules may still require voluntary registration to claim input tax credits or rebates for imported goods

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for a 0% GST rate under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 of the ETA if: (1) the supplier ships the goods to a destination outside Canada; or (2) possession or ownership is transferred in Canada to a non-resident and the goods are exported as soon as reasonably practicable after the supply (generally evidenced as within 90 days for a commercial sale, with binding contractual terms); basic groceries (including most Asian vegetables, condiments in non-prepare state, rice, noodles, and packaged ingredients) are permanently zero-rated under Schedule VI Part III. Offshore services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 9 when the services are in relation to exported goods or performable wholly outside Canada.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully floating and generally convertible currency regime. There are no statutory exchange controls affecting current account payments, capital movements, dividend/profit distributions, or liquidation proceeds for Canadian limited partnerships. An Alberta LP with non-resident partners may freely receive capital contributions, repatriate partnership profits, and remit imports for goods. Practical restrictions arise only from bank-level KYC/AML screening, sanctions screening, cross-border wire report thresholds (CAD 10,000 or more under FINTRAC), and tax withholding duties on payments to non-residents (e.g., Part XIII withholding under the Income Tax Act, generally 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty). For an Asian grocery import business, convertibility of USD, CNY, HKD, and other proceeds into CAD is immediate via a licensed Schedule I bank's FX desk, with no central bank approval required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender rule in Canada. The Foreign Exchange Control Board was dissolved in 1951, and the Currency Act / Bank of Canada Act impose no mandatory conversion of foreign-currency export invoices into CAD. An Alberta LP may hold export proceeds in foreign currency, convert at its discretion, or retain them in nostro accounts. While no surrender timeline applies, transactions of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by financial entities; exporters are subject to CBSA export declaration requirements, CRA cross-border reporting (T1134/T1135 where applicable), and general recordkeeping obligations. No minimum conversion ratio exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — no separate foreign exchange administration; banking prudential supervision is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money laundering/anti-terrorist financing surveillance is vested in the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Market conversion and exchange settlement discipline are maintained through Payments Canada and the Canadian Depository for Securities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium for an Alberta LP. Canada does not recognize a standalone 'electronic money institution' licence equivalent to the EU EMI regime; foreign EMIs are treated as unregulated payment facilitators unless they register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, if in-scope, under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada. Offshore EMIs can lawfully be used for minor FX conversions or procurement to a correspondent account, but they cannot act as the primary treasury or settlement rail for an Alberta commercial entity because they generally lack access to Payments Canada LVTS and do not offer escrow-grade insolvency protection. To clear supplier payments safely, the LP should maintain at least one tier-1 Canadian bank operational account and use a FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB only for fringe currency conversion or low-value supplier remittances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a compliant inbound capital contribution, the non-resident partner should remit via SWIFT MT103 / MT210 from an established foreign bank account to the Alberta LP's CAD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The payment instruction must include the full partnership legal name and Alberta LP registration number in the beneficiary field, and the reference field should state 'Capital Contribution – [Partner Name] – [Date]'. Use purpose code/series consistent with the bank's SWIFT manual (e.g., remittance category 5000-series for investment capital; field 70 should read 'INVESTMENT IN ALBERTA LP – CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION'). The bank will verify the underlying partnership agreement, capital account ledger, and foreign-exchange invoice before crediting. Incoming wires exceeding CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC; the bank will classify the payment as a reportable electronic funds transfer. The LP must maintain a contemporaneous capital-account ledger and a copy of the executed subscription agreement to satisfy the bank's due-diligence review and to evidence ownership for CRA without unwinding the pass-through classification.Outbound disbursements for Asian grocery imports should be cleared primarily via SWIFT MT103 for open-account / T/T payments and via commercial documentary letters of credit for large or recurring perishable orders. In Canada, CAD clearing is finalized through Payments Canada LVTS in real time; USD clearing occurs via Fedwire or CHIPS through the bank's US correspondent nostros. For vendor instructions, the LP must use the supplier's full beneficiary bank details with its own account name, and include the supplier contract/invoice number in the remittance field. For high-value processing of more than CAD 100,000, commercial LC drafting should be used to mitigate non-performance risk; the issuance bank will require import invoices, customs declarations, and insurance certificates. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined (15% federal general corporate rate + 8% Alberta provincial corporate rate) for corporate partners of the limited partnership; limited partners are taxed at personal rates on their allocated share of income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory fixed CAD threshold for Local File/Master File; CRA expects contemporaneous arm's length documentation for all cross-border related-party transactions of any material size. Mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting (CbC) applies when consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000; practical review threshold is often CAD 1,000,000 per transaction.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. Instead, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245) and transfer pricing rules (section 247) deny tax benefits to transactions without economic substance. For treaty relief, the Principal Purpose Test per the MLI will deny benefits where a transaction or arrangement lacks a genuine commercial purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. Excise Tax Act parallel penalties for GST/HST evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or condone evasion may be jointly and severally liable for corporate and penalty debts, prohibited from corporate management, and subject to travel arrests and criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 247(2) recharacterizes pricing to arm's length; section 247(3) imposes a penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment (or greater if gross negligence).

Director personal liability: Directors cannot rely on indemnity when they knowingly sign false transfer pricing documentation; CRA may assess director liability for unpaid withholding or remittance amounts.
Shell Company / Sham Structure

Statutory sanction: Application of General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245) to deny the tax benefit, plus interest and a 25% penalty under section 163(2) for false statements or omissions attributable to culpable conduct.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced; directors are personally liable for tax debts and restitution to the Crown if they tolerated or authored the sham arrangements.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Compliance

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 1,000,000 for a corporate entity; individual officers face up to CAD 500,000; criminal prosecution possible for willful evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and convicted for failing to implement AML compliance; travel bans and imprisonment (up to 5 years) apply on conviction for obstructing FINTRAC.
Withholding Tax Non-Remittance

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 227(1): penalty of 10% of the unremitted withholding amount plus interest; also failure to deduct or withhold is an offence leading to a summary conviction.

Director personal liability: Section 227.1 imposes strict director liability for any unremitted withholding taxes, with no statutory exceptions; directors may face civil seizure of personal assets, garnishment of wages, and travel restrictions.
