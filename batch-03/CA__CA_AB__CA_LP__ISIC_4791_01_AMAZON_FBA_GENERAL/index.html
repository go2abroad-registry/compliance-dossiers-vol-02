>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Alberta limited partnerships are governed principally by the Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c P-3, and the Limited Partnerships Act, RSA 2000, c L-7, including their regulations. Alberta does not maintain a general public beneficial-ownership register for Alberta limited partnerships equivalent to the federal register for certain CBCA corporations. The federal Canada Business Corporations Act significant-control register regime does not apply solely because an entity is an Alberta limited partnership. The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, SC 2000, c 17, and its regulations impose customer identification, beneficial-owner verification, recordkeeping, and reporting duties on covered reporting entities, including relevant financial institutions and certain financial intermediaries; they do not establish a universal LP formation UBO filing.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

An Alberta limited partnership must maintain an Alberta registered office as required by the Limited Partnerships Act and applicable registry requirements. A commercial registered-office or agent service may be acceptable if it meets those requirements and can receive official records and notices. A mailbox-only address may be insufficient. Banks, Amazon, Stripe, Airwallex, tax authorities, and counterparties may separately require evidence of operating address, management, inventory, or business activity. For an Amazon FBA cross-border retailer, a virtual registered office does not by itself establish tax residence, permanent establishment, GST/HST status, or commercial substance.

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, Alberta Corporate Registry, Service Alberta (filings submitted electronically via Corporate Registry Connect or through a licensed registry agent).

Governing statute

Limited Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c L-8 (creation of limited partnership by registration, s 3; filing and contents of the Declaration; maintenance of a register of limited partners), supplemented by the Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c P-3 (default partnership rules) and the Business Entities Registration Act, SA 2021, c 17 together with the Business Entities Registration Regulation, Alta Reg 160/2010 (registry filings and fee schedule).

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership (Form LP1), registered under the Limited Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c L-8; legal existence is effective on the date of registration, and the Registrar issues a Certificate/Confirmation of Registration. Base state filing fee CAD 120 (approx. USD 90), excluding optional name reservation and registry-agent service fees.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Alberta limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed solely by the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow or trust account is required by Alberta law. A limited partner's capital contribution is due as stipulated in the agreement, and the limited partner's liability is limited to that agreed contribution.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to a virtual FBA operation with no owned or leased industrial premises in Alberta. If an Alberta office or warehouse is subsequently occupied, the LP must obtain: (i) fire safety certification from the local Safety Codes Officer under the Safety Codes Act and Alberta Fire Code; (ii) structural load and occupancy clearance under the Alberta Building Code; and (iii) an Alberta OHS (Occupational Health and Safety) program registration covering workplace hazard assessment if employees are engaged in local retail, returns, or administrative activities.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Alberta limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed solely by the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow or trust account is required by Alberta law. A limited partner's capital contribution is due as stipulated in the agreement, and the limited partner's liability is limited to that agreed contribution.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — ISIC 4791 (retail sale via Internet with Amazon FBA fulfillment) involves no manufacturing, warehousing, or industrial activity in the LP's own name. Should the LP later open a physical office or distribution facility in Alberta, the relevant authorities become the municipal business licensing department (e.g., City of Calgary or City of Edmonton) and Alberta Municipal Affairs for building occupancy and zoning compliance.

Category: No provincial operating or manufacturing license is mandated for online marketplace retail under Alberta law. The Consumer Protection Act (Alberta) applies informationally but imposes no licensing condition on e-commerce resellers. A municipal Business Licence is required only if the LP maintains a physical commercial premises in an Alberta municipality; a pure virtual FBA operation with no local premises is exempt from municipal licensing.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (canada.ca/cra-my-business-account). Alberta's provincial income tax is administered federally by the CRA under the National Tax Collection Agreement; Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Register the limited partnership with the Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) under the Partnership Act (Alberta) or as an extra-provincial LP if formed elsewhere; obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number. Step 2 - Apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (RT) using Form RC1 or online BRO, disclosing the LP's legal name, general partner details, Canadian business address, and Amazon FBA commercial activity. Step 3 - Request an Import/Export (RM) program account where inventory will be imported into Canada for FBA fulfillment. Step 4 - If CRA requires, file Form RC59 (Tax Representation) and post security under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 299(1) prior to activation of the non-resident account. Step 5 - File the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return; each non-resident partner must hold a Canadian tax identifier (SIN or BN) to report allocated income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election exists; entity classification follows CRA interpretive positions on the Income Tax Act (ITA) and ETA. Available elections for this Alberta LP include: (i) fiscal period election under ITA s. 220(3.1); (ii) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident rental income to reduce Part XIII withholding; (iii) ITA s. 217 election for non-resident employment income; (iv) ETA Quick Method election (ss. 224.01-224.04) for qualifying registrants; (v) hybrid mismatch offset elections under ITA Part XVIII (including rules centered on ss. 18.4 and 12.7) to eliminate double deductions; and (vi) ETA s. 273 joint election for closely related corporations, potentially usable by the corporate general partner of the LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada over four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA ss. 148(1) and 148(5)); for non-resident sellers, only supplies made in Canada count toward this threshold. A non-resident LP with FBA inventory in Canada exceeding this threshold is required to register for GST/HST; voluntary registration is available below threshold. GST on imported FBA inventory is also payable at customs by the importer of record, with input tax credits available to the registrant.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V ETA where the registrant exports goods 'as soon after the supply as is reasonable' (s. 1); goods are delivered to a common carrier for export (s. 2); or, for services, where supplied to a non-resident (s. 7 excluding services linked to Canadian real property, Canadian-situated goods, or individuals physically in Canada). In an Amazon FBA general model, domestic FBA sales are taxable at 5% GST; export shipments from Canadian FBA inventory to foreign buyers must be supported by transport documentation evidencing foreign destination. The supplier must retain proof of export and payment/repatriation within 180 days as prudent evidence (Canada has no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark aligns with financing and refund-review practice).

  • CBSA Digital Export Reporting (DER) declaration with transaction reference number
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill showing Canadian origin and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract / purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank credit confirmation of foreign payment
  • CRA Form GST11 evidence of exportation or equivalent alternative proof
  • Canadian import accounting documentation (CBSA B3 / CARM commercial entry) for FBA inventory

Canada has no mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API tax reporting regime. Invoices must contain prescribed ETA information and records must be retained for six years. The CRA supports the voluntary Pan-Canadian Peppol BIS 3.0 e-invoicing standard (2023 onward), but participation is optional and no approval is required before issuing invoices.

The CRA cross-matches zero-rating claims against CBSA export declarations. Deficient documentation triggers reassessment of the supply at 5% GST within the four-year normal reassessment period (extended for misrepresentation), disallowance of refunded amounts, daily interest, late-filing penalties under ETA s. 280(1), and a false-statement penalty of not less than CAD 500 and up to 25% of the understated net tax under ETA s. 327.1. Knowing participation in a refund fraud scheme is a criminal offence carrying fines of 25%-50% of the amounts and imprisonment up to five years. The ITA adds s. 163(2) gross negligence penalties of 50% on misstatements affecting partnership income allocation, and the general partner and each limited partner remain jointly and severally liable for the LP's GST/HST obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible, free-floating currency regime on both current and capital accounts. There are no exchange-licensing, prior-approval, quota, or repatriation-permit requirements affecting capital contributions to an Alberta Limited Partnership, Amazon FBA settlement proceeds, or profit distributions to non-resident partners. Remittance frictions are tax-only: non-resident partners are taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on Canadian-source business income allocated through the partnership; Part XIII withholding applies only to qualifying interest, rent, royalty, dividend, or fee payments made by the LP; and GST/HST registration may be mandatory if the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada above the CAD 30,000 threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian federal and Alberta law impose no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion timeline, or earmarking requirement on export or e-commerce FBA receipts. Amazon settlement proceeds received in CAD may be retained, converted to USD, hedged, or remitted at the taxpayer's discretion, subject only to ordinary bank FX-risk limits and to FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting for wire movements of C$10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting with the Department of Finance Canada for foreign-exchange and official-reserve policy, Payments Canada as system operator of Lynx and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS), and FINTRAC as the AML/ATF supervisor under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as a multi-currency conversion and disbursement layer for FBA payouts, FX conversion, and supplier payments; MEDIUM-LOW as a substitute for the primary deposit account. Airwallex (FINTRAC-registered money services business in Canada) and Wise provide global accounts, local CAD/USD routing, and near-real-time clearing; Stripe supplies Canada-hosted acquiring and settlement via ACSS. Legal constraints under the Bank Act and PCMLTFA: foreign-headquartered EMIs cannot accept Canadian deposit balances, are not CDIC-insured, and must back wallet balances with a custodial account at a Schedule I bank. The AB LP should therefore maintain the tier-1 bank treasury account as the anchor and use EMI rails only for working-capital conversion and disbursement.

Statutory deliverableDeadline window Government feeDelinquency sanctions
Alberta Limited Partnership Annual Return Annually on or before the anniversary date of the limited partnership registration; file changes to registered particulars when they occur. $22 USD The prescribed fee is generally CAD 30, with the USD equivalent varying by exchange rate. Late filing may result in registry late fees, a notice of default, and cancellation or striking of the limited partnership registration after the applicable statutory notice and compliance period. No universal automatic strike-off period should be assumed.
Partnership Information Return (Form T5013) Within five months after the end of the partnership fiscal period. For a December 31 year-end, the usual deadline is May 31. $0 USD Late-filing penalty is generally CAD 25 per day, subject to a minimum of CAD 100 and a maximum of CAD 2,500, where the return is required and late. The CRA does not ordinarily strike the Alberta partnership from the provincial registry for this filing default.
Alberta Corporate Income Tax Return Not generally applicable to the limited partnership itself because an Alberta limited partnership is ordinarily fiscally transparent. Tax reporting is generally performed by the partners, subject to their status and the nature of the income. $0 USD Not applicable to the limited partnership where no Alberta corporate income tax return is required. Partners may incur tax, interest, and penalty exposure for inaccurate or late reporting.
