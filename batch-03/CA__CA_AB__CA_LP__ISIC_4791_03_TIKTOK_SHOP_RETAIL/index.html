Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), operating through Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE; Alberta Corporate Registry is the provincial registry for limited partnership registration and delivers the provincial entity number.

E-government filing portal

Register the Alberta Limited Partnership under the Partnership Act (Alberta) through Alberta Corporate Registry; then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online, paper Form RC1, telephone, email, or an authorized representative. For GST/HST, add the 'RT' program account to the BN. The 9-digit BN plus RT program account is the statutory Canadian tax and VAT identifier. Non-resident foreign entities may apply directly for a non-resident GST/HST registration without a Canadian provincial incorporation; an Alberta LP may be registered through the same BN enrolment process once its provincial filing is active.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Register the Alberta Limited Partnership under the Partnership Act (Alberta) through Alberta Corporate Registry; then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online, paper Form RC1, telephone, email, or an authorized representative. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; partnership classification follows legal fact and the Income Tax Act. Available elections for an Alberta LP include: partnership non-calendar fiscal period election under section 249.1(4); partner designations and allocations under section 96; GST/HST annual filing election and quick method election for eligible small suppliers; and non-resident GST/HST rebate filings under section 252. For cross-border hybrid mismatches, Canada applies specific anti-hybrid allocation rules rather than elective classification, and the LP cannot unilaterally elect out of pass-through treatment for Canadian tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies from commercial activity in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, including products sold remotely to Canadian consumers through platforms such as TikTok Shop. Once the threshold is exceeded, a non-resident or domestic vendor must register for GST/HST unless all supplies are zero-rated; marketplace facilitation rules may impose registration obligations on the platform or the seller depending on the fulfillment and legal agent structure.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Tangible personal property exported by the vendor is zero-rated if there is documentary proof of export and the goods leave Canada within a reasonable time, normally within 60 days. Services supplied to a non-resident person may be zero-rated as exported services when the supply is not realistically consumed in Canada, is not performed in respect of Canadian real property, and is not in connection with tangible personal property ordinarily situated in Canada. TikTok Shop retail goods shipped directly to international consumers from Alberta qualify as zero-rated exports only when delivery occurs at a destination outside Canada and the vendor retains the required evidence.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) B13A Export Declaration or Canadian Export Reporting System evidence for commercial exports, or the courier/postal customs declaration for low-value e-commerce shipments
  • Transport document evidencing departure from Canada and delivery abroad, such as a Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier shipping label with destination address
  • Commercial invoice showing buyer identity, description of goods, HS codes, quantities, and export reason code
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or payout ledger from TikTok_Shop_Payout / Wise_B2B evidencing receipt of sale proceeds in foreign currency or equivalent, including conversion records

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance of invoices and has no digital stamping or pre-clearance API requirement. GST/HST registrants may issue electronic invoices and are required to retain complete electronic records; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or acceptable EDI/XML transmission. CRA accepts Peppol BIS 3.0 invoice exchange voluntarily, and payment rails such as TikTok_Shop_Payout and Wise_B2B must preserve structured audit evidence of gross payments, commissions, refunds, and chargebacks.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year or, in cases of neglect, fraud, or misrepresentation, extended six-year reassessment period, denying zero-rating retroactively if export documentation is missing or false. The uncollected GST becomes immediately payable with compound daily interest. Civil penalties under ETA section 280.1 include a gross-negligence penalty of at least CAD $5,000 and up to 50% of the net tax understated; false registration or willful evasion may lead to prosecution, fines, and imprisonment under ETA section 327. Export zero-rating claims audited through CRA's Offshore and International Enforcement Programs are subject to enhanced scrutiny when foreign payment rails and non-arm's-length logistics are present.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement and imposes no exchange-control authorization requirements for inward or outward capital flows, cross-border wire transfers, dividend-equivalent remittances, or profit repatriation. No regulatory distinction exists between current-account and capital-account transactions for banking purposes; banks apply only standard KYC, sanctions, and FINTRAC large-cross-border-transaction reporting (threshold CAD 10,000 or more for import or export of currency or monetary instruments, and for international electronic funds transfers). Practically, a non-resident partner of an Alberta LP can repatriate distributable profits without central-bank approval; the relevant friction is tax, not FX control: a non-resident partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP is taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on its share of Canadian-source partnership income, and passive payments (e.g., interest, royalties, management fees) paid to non-residents remain subject to Part XIII withholding tax at statutory rates (25%, typically reduced by tax treaty). Partnership distributions per se are not subject to Part XIII withholding, but the bank will require a tax-residency declaration (Form NR301 for the entity and counterparty forms for non-resident beneficial owners) and a standing Forms W-8/W-9 matrix for CRS/FATCA reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. Canada has abolished all surrender requirements for foreign currency earned by residents and non-resident-owned entities. An Alberta LP may retain export proceeds indefinitely in any currency, whether USD, CNY, or CAD, at any Canadian-chartered bank or authorized foreign bank branch. Nevertheless, three compliance obligations attach: (i) FINTRAC reporting — any international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more (or an amount that the bank suspects relates to a criminal offence or required to be reported) must be reported by the financial intermediary; (ii) Income Tax Act records — the LP must retain export transaction documentation, foreign-currency translation bases, and supporting invoices for six years as required under section 230, with respect to GST/HST zero-rated export supplies under the Excise Tax Act; (iii) statistical reporting — direct investment and cross-border transaction filings requested by Statistics Canada must be completed. For a TikTok Shop retail operation, the platform settlement in USD/CNY may arrive via a licensed payment intermediary; such receipts are taxable export revenue, and the CAD-equivalent values must be recorded. No surrender window or conversion ratio exists, and deferral of conversion is unrestricted.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada maintains no dedicated exchange-control administration equivalent to a standalone Foreign Exchange Administration; foreign exchange policy authority is exercised by the Bank of Canada in conjunction with the Department of Finance Canada, with prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions under the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), payments-system oversight by Payments Canada, and anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act, the Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating fiat currency with no administered exchange-rate parity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for disbursement and payout collection; Low for primary capital custody. The EU/UK term 'Electronic Money Institution' has no direct Canadian licensing equivalent; offshore EMIs operating in Canada must register as money services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, where carrying on retail payment activities in Canada, must be registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. Wise (Wise B2B) is an authorized FINTRAC MSB and is a practical settlement rail for TikTok Shop payouts, small-ticket supplier payments in USD/CNY, and treasury concentration in multi-currency wallets; however, Canadian law prohibits non-bank entities from taking deposits, and funds held with an offshore EMI are not CDIC-insured and do not constitute deposits at a Canadian bank. The LP should therefore maintain a Schedule I bank operational account as the master treasury account, with Wise B2B used only as a pass-through clearing utility, not as the account of record for capital escrow, tax payments, or statutory filings. Canadian distribution and partnership capital accounts must be registered to the LP at a domestic chartered bank; any purchaser of goods or settlement of platform proceeds from offshore EMIs must be reconciled to the bank account to satisfy the bank's ongoing KYC and FINTRAC expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound contributions from non-resident limited partners are executed by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202/MT205 for interbank funding) to the LP's CAD or USD account at a Schedule I bank. Canada requires no capital-inflow declaration or central-bank purpose code; nevertheless, the remittance message must carry a full audit trail in Field 70 (Remittance Information) stating 'LP Capital Contribution - CA_AB' together with the LP's registration number, the specific partner name, and the reference clause of the LP Agreement, because the bank will apply the statement to its large-cross-border-transaction reporting to FINTRAC where the amount equals or exceeds CAD 10,000 (or where suspicious). For a USD contribution, the bank will deliver via its US correspondent (e.g., the Canadian bank's US affiliate or Fedwire participant) into a USD-denominated clearing account held in the LP's name at the Canadian bank; for CAD, settlement runs through LYNX (the Bank of Canada's high-value payments system) via Payments Canada. If the contribution originates from an offshore jurisdiction, the bank may request an independent source-of-funds declaration, an audited or compiled balance sheet of the sender, and confirmation that the sender operates under a valid corporate existence. For inbound amounts that would trigger the Investment Canada Act, no approval is needed for passive minority capital contributions to an LP used for e-commerce, but the non-resident investor should file the mandatory reporting form where the acquisition of control of a Canadian business occurs; for a newly formed LP, only a notification obligation applies and must be handled by counsel. All inbound amounts must be recorded in the LP's capital accounts in the functional currency, CAD, with FX equivalencies at the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate for financial reporting and tax purposes.Outbound disbursements for inventory procurement, logistics, TikTok Shop supplier settlements, advertising payments (e.g., to Bytedance/TikTok ad platforms), and cross-border fees rely on multiple clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct bank-to-bank beneficiary payment with textual reference dense enough to meet supplier invoicing requirements and bank's reference in Field 70; (ii) MT700 commercial letters of credit where the LP imports from a China-based manufacturer requiring documentary LC protection, with the issuing bank being a Schedule I bank and the advising bank in China via CIPS or SWIFT; (iii) Wise B2B / Airwallex / Skye Pay-class MSB rails for small-denomination repetitive disbursements, such as freight forwarding and micro-vendor payments, subject to FINTRAC 'eligible MSB' verification and the bank's approval of the MSB as an approved third-party payment provider; (iv) Web/API payouts via TikTok Shop settlement to seller balance, which are then repatriated monthly to the LP's operational account; (v) for CAD domestic supplier payments, LYNX for high-value items or Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for low-value ACH-equivalent payments. USD disbursements from the CAD-based account require the bank to execute a spot FX (or forward) conversion by internal dealing desk, with a transaction confirmation retained under the applicable investment industry rules. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for disbursement and payout collection; Low for primary capital custody. The EU/UK term 'Electronic Money Institution' has no direct Canadian licensing equivalent; offshore EMIs operating in Canada must register as money services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, where carrying on retail payment activities in Canada, must be registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. Wise (Wise B2B) is an authorized FINTRAC MSB and is a practical settlement rail for TikTok Shop payouts, small-ticket supplier payments in USD/CNY, and treasury concentration in multi-currency wallets; however, Canadian law prohibits non-bank entities from taking deposits, and funds held with an offshore EMI are not CDIC-insured and do not constitute deposits at a Canadian bank. The LP should therefore maintain a Schedule I bank operational account as the master treasury account, with Wise B2B used only as a pass-through clearing utility, not as the account of record for capital escrow, tax payments, or statutory filings. Canadian distribution and partnership capital accounts must be registered to the LP at a domestic chartered bank; any purchaser of goods or settlement of platform proceeds from offshore EMIs must be reconciled to the bank account to satisfy the bank's ongoing KYC and FINTRAC expectations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Alberta LP entity level (fiscally transparent under s.96 ITA); 23% combined headline for a corporate partner with an Alberta permanent establishment (15% federal + 8% Alberta general corporate rate); progressive federal/Alberta marginal rates apply to individual partners.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory Part XIII rate 25% (s.212(2)). LP income allocations to non-resident partners not attributable to a deemed PE are withholdable at 25%; treaty-reduced rates apply (e.g., Canada-US dividends 5% with 10%-or-more voting ownership otherwise 15%; Canada-China and Canada-UK 5% with substantial shareholding otherwise 15%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Within five months after the LP fiscal year-end (Income Tax Regulations s.229(2)); a December 31 fiscal year-end is due by May 31 of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis CAD threshold; contemporaneous Master File/Local File documentation is mandatory on a reasonable-efforts basis under s.247(4). CRA compliance review focus begins with cross-border non-arm's-length transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000 annually. Country-by-Country reporting is required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000 (Form RC4649). A 10% transfer-pricing penalty applies where the net adjustment exceeds CAD 5,000 (s.247(3)).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute; substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245), permanent establishment rules (s.253 and treaty Article V), and the Supreme Court of Canada Continental Bank requirement of genuine business purpose. An Alberta LP must conduct real e-commerce activity: physical inventory or third-party logistics in Canada, employees or agents soliciting sales, executed TikTok Shop seller agreements, and substantive governance decisions by the general partner. A limited partner who participates in management or control forfeits limited liability under the Alberta Partnership Act (RSA 2000, c. P-3, s.75(1)) and the partnership may be recharacterized as a taxable corporation for Canadian tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded; on summary conviction a fine up to CAD 200,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; on indictment a fine up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years (s.239 ITA); CRA criminal referral and public naming.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, penalties, and interest; courts may impose travel bans, freezing orders, and asset seizure; criminal records and extradition exposure apply.
Gross Negligence / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (s.163(2)); repeat late-filing penalty of 10% plus 2% per month; interest accrues at CRA prescribed rates compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be assessed personally under s.227.1 for unremitted amounts; the limited liability shield can be pierced where false filings evidence participation in control.
Failure to Withhold Part XIII and Payroll Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: Full liability for unremitted amounts plus interest and penalties (s.215(1) for Part XIII; s.227(8) for payroll); additional 10% penalty for gross negligence in non-remittance.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are personally liable for the entire unremitted amount plus interest under s.227.1; due-diligence defence is available only with documented supervision and prompt remediation.
GST/HST Registration and Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: Liability for all uncollected GST/HST, interest at 6% arrears rate, penalties of 6% of unremitted net tax plus a late-remittance penalty, public disclosure of assessments and potential director liability.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST under ETA s.323; limited partners remain shielded only if entirely passive.
AML/ATF and Unregistered Money Services

Statutory sanction: Administrative Monetary Penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for entities and CAD 500,000 for individuals under the PCMLTFA; failure to report suspicious transactions is a separate offence; money laundering charges under Criminal Code s.462.31 carry imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners face personal liability for AMPs; criminal prosecution of senior officers, travel bans, and asset freezes; banking termination and reputational sanctions.
General Anti-Avoidance Rule and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization and denial of the tax benefit (s.245); mandatory 25% penalty on the tax benefit under 2024 federal legislation; reassessment beyond the normal limitation period; Canada's anti-treaty-shopping jurisprudence denies benefits to arrangement lacking substance.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the avoidance scheme are personally assessable for penalties and interest; cross-border injunctions and fraudulent conveyance actions may be pursued against personal assets.
