Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes.html; GST/HST registration portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/register-gst-hst-account.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) and any applicable program accounts. Registration is completed online through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). For a limited partnership, the partnership itself is eligible to be assigned a BN for GST/HST if it is carrying on business in Canada and is not a small supplier (i.e., worldwide taxable supplies do not exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four calendar quarters). The registration process requires furnishing the partnership's legal name, Alberta registration number, deemed residence address, and the general partner's details. CRA issues a BN with an 'RT' program account for GST/HST. Alternatively, registration may be completed by mail or fax with supporting documents evidencing the Alberta Partnership Registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The Income Tax Act does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent by default, and income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners. For GST/HST, a partnership may elect to have a representative member under the Excise Tax Act to file and remit GST/HST on behalf of the partnership. Other elections available to non-resident partners include the s. 216 election for rental income (tax payable on gross rents via Form NR6 and a T1 return) and the s. 219 election for non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada. Hybrid mismatch rules under s. 18.2, s. 18.3, and s. 20.01 may impose corrective measures in cross-border structures notwithstanding the partnership's pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (excluding supplies of financial services, goodwill, and capital property); if exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. Zero-rated exports count as taxable supplies for the registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of tangible personal property exported by the supplier or on its behalf, or delivered to a common carrier for export to a destination outside Canada, provided the supplier obtains and retains sufficient documentary evidence. A dropshipping business operating from Alberta may zero-rate goods sold to non-resident customers when the goods are shipped from Canada to a foreign address and title transfers outside Canada. Services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated if they qualify as exported services under s. 9 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (e.g., services rendered to a non-resident and performed wholly outside Canada). Third-party warehousing in Alberta that fulfils international consumer orders may qualify if the supplier is the exporter and the books and records substantiate export.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration in the form of an approved export report (e.g., Form B13A or electronic CERS export declaration)
  • Commercial invoice and pro forma invoice (if applicable)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing carriage from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Proof of export such as carrier certificate, customs entry, or export transaction record
  • Inward remittance certificate or banking records evidencing payment received from the non-resident customer
  • Packing list and warehouse dispatch records (for dropshipping provenance)

Canada does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing stamping, or a government API validation mandate for GST/HST. Tax invoices must contain prescribed information (supplier name, BN, date, taxable amount, rate, and tax charged). Electronic invoices (EDI, PDF, XML) are permitted. The CRA may require prior approval for certain electronic invoicing systems, but there is no continuous digital stamping requirement. Businesses should maintain an audit-trail record for exported goods and support zero-rating claims.

The CRA may reassess a taxpayer within four years of the normal assessment period where zero-rated export treatment is claimed without substantiation; this limitation period is extended indefinitely where the taxpayer made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud. The taxpayer is liable for uncollected GST/HST, interest, and penalties, including a gross negligence penalty of up to 50% of the excess amount claimed and failure-to-file penalties of 5% plus 1% per month. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims may lead to criminal prosecution under s. 327 of the Excise Tax Act, carrying fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment. Dropshipping operations must ensure timely repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days or document reasonable grounds; non-repatriation may trigger CRA audit and loss of zero-rating on the basis that the transaction is not a genuine export.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. An Alberta limited partnership may generally receive foreign capital, pay foreign suppliers, hold foreign-currency accounts, convert funds, and remit distributions or other permitted payments offshore without prior Bank of Canada approval. Banks must nevertheless verify the commercial purpose, source of funds, beneficial ownership, sanctions status, and tax characterization of transactions. Partnership distributions, interest, royalties, management fees, and other cross-border payments may trigger Canadian withholding, information-reporting, transfer-pricing, thin-capitalization, treaty, or taxable-presence considerations. Non-resident partners may receive partnership allocations or distributions subject to applicable Canadian tax rules; banking approval does not determine tax clearance. Payments connected with sanctioned persons, prohibited jurisdictions, trade controls, fraud, or unexplained third-party activity may be blocked, rejected, reported, or escalated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general deadline requiring exporters to sell foreign-currency receipts for Canadian dollars. Export receipts may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the partnership's discretion. The partnership should retain invoices, purchase orders, shipping or delivery evidence, customs records where applicable, payment records, customer identity information, and accounting support sufficient for bank, tax, AML, and audit review. Foreign-currency balances and transactions must be recorded and reported under applicable Canadian accounting and tax rules, including foreign-exchange gain or loss treatment.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing exchange controls, capital controls, mandatory currency conversion, or routine foreign-exchange licensing. Relevant oversight also includes the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering compliance, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated financial institutions, the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for tax reporting and withholding, and Global Affairs Canada for sanctions administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and supplier disbursement; Low as a substitute for a Canadian operating bank account or regulated treasury account. Stripe and PayPal may support merchant acquiring or payment services subject to underwriting, merchant-category restrictions, reserve requirements, transaction monitoring, chargeback controls, customer-location analysis, and settlement eligibility. An offshore electronic-money institution cannot automatically provide Canadian deposit-taking, Canadian payment-account, or Canadian trust services. The partnership should use a Canadian bank for core operating funds, capital contributions, tax payments, payroll, and controlled liquidity. Fintech balances should not be treated as insured Canadian bank deposits unless expressly held through an applicable deposit-taking structure. The platform, account holder, beneficial owners, fulfillment chain, refund policy, product categories, supplier countries, and source of goods must be disclosed accurately.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route partner capital through a Canadian bank account held in the Alberta limited partnership's legal name, preferably from an account held in the contributing partner's verified legal name. Use a bank wire or SWIFT transfer with the exact beneficiary name, account number, transit and institution details, and a clear payment reference such as "capital contribution under partnership agreement". Canada does not prescribe a universal foreign-exchange purpose-code system for ordinary commercial wires; the bank's internal purpose, source-of-funds, and transaction-information fields must be completed accurately. Provide the partnership agreement, contribution notice, partner identity and ownership evidence, source-of-funds documents, remitter-to-partner relationship, and expected transaction profile before funds arrive. Do not route capital through personal accounts, unrelated third parties, payment platforms, or supplier accounts. Reconcile each receipt to the capital ledger and retain wire messages, bank statements, and accounting entries. Turnover: No statutory audit is imposed solely by the Alberta Partnership Act on a limited partnership. There is no revenue, asset, or partner-count threshold that triggers a mandatory independent audit. An audit becomes obligatory only if the partnership agreement, a lender or investor covenant, a CRA audit demand, or a court order requires one; corporate audit rules under the Business Corporations Act do not apply.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined headline rate (15% federal corporate income tax rate + 8% Alberta general corporate income tax rate); for an Alberta Limited Partnership the liability is statutorily borne by the partners under s. 96(1) of the Income Tax Act (ITA), each corporate or individual partner being taxed on its allocable share of the LP's Canadian-source business income.
