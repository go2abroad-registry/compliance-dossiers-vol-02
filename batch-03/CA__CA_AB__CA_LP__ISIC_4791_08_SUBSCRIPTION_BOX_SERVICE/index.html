Applicable AML statute: Alberta limited partnerships are principally governed by the Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c P-3, including its limited-partnership provisions and registration requirements. Alberta does not maintain a generally applicable public beneficial-ownership register for limited partnerships equivalent to the federal register of individuals with significant control for federal corporations. The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, SC 2000, c 17, and its regulations impose customer-identification, beneficial-ownership verification, recordkeeping, and reporting duties on covered reporting entities, including financial institutions and certain financial intermediaries; they do not create a universal LP filing obligation. The federal Canada Business Corporations Act, RSC 1985, c C-44, Part XVIII, applies to federal corporations, not an Alberta LP. Alberta's Corporate Transparency Act, SA 2021, c C-25.5, should not be treated as imposing a general public beneficial-ownership filing on an LP absent a specific applicable provision.

No general rule requires this subscription-box business to maintain a customer-facing Alberta storefront. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - national tax administration for GST/HST, federal and Alberta corporate income tax, and partnership information returns; taxpayer portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO); export documentation is administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Single Window.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the limited partnership under the Alberta Partnership Act with the Alberta Corporate Registry and obtain the Alberta registration number (extraprovincial registration if formed outside Alberta). Step 2: Apply to CRA for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with GST/HST program account via Business Registration Online or Form RC1; the BN is the single tax identifier for GST/HST, payroll, and Alberta corporate income tax (no separate Alberta tax ID is required). Step 3: For a non-resident-owned LP, name an authorized Canadian resident representative in the registration application. Step 4: Register the LP in its own name for GST/HST as a deemed person under the Excise Tax Act once taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold. Step 5: Enroll for the T5013 partnership information return obligation and ensure the general partner has authority to file the T5013 Summary and issue T5013 slips to each partner.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under the Income Tax Act (Canada), an Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent: the LP computes income using partnership accounting but pays no tax itself; each general and limited partner includes its allocable share of income, loss, and tax credits in its own return. Under the Excise Tax Act, however, the partnership is a distinct 'person' and must hold its own GST/HST registration once the CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold is exceeded. The LP is not tax-exempt and receives no exempt-zone treatment; all commercial subscription box sales delivered in Canada are subject to GST.

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic check-the-box regime; entity classification follows the Income Tax Act definition of partnership. Applicable elections: (i) Excise Tax Act s. 273(1) election between a registrant partnership and a partner to treat certain supplies between them as made for nil consideration, removing self-supply from GST/HST; (ii) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations, not available to a limited partnership; (iii) U.S. federal Form 8832 check-the-box election may independently classify the Canadian LP for U.S. tax purposes without altering Canadian classification; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2-18.4) impose mandatory deduction/non-inclusion adjustments when foreign classification diverges from Canadian treatment and provide no curative election; (v) CRA advance income tax rulings and GST/HST advisory opinions may be sought to confirm pass-through treatment before cross-border subscription operations commence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the GST/HST small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148, computed on worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada excluding financial services and supplies of capital property; recurring subscription box sales are taxable supplies, so the LP will exceed the threshold and must register and charge 5% GST on all box deliveries to Canadian customers. If structured as a non-resident supplier of low-value goods, the separate simplified non-resident registration regime also imposes mandatory registration once Canadian sales exceed CAD 30,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Canada qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act only when the supplier or its agent exports the goods by commercial or personal conveyance and retains the evidence prescribed by the Evidence of Export Regulations (SOR/92-249). For the subscription box business, domestic deliveries to Canadian customers are taxable at 5% GST (Alberta); only boxes shipped to foreign customers qualify for zero-rating, and only if the LP: (a) sells under a contract of export made before the goods are shipped; (b) obtains destination-country customs evidence of arrival; and (c) keeps contemporaneous commercial and payment records. The curation/kitting service component is also zero-rated as a service supplied to a non-resident who is not in Canada at the time the service is performed. Input tax credits attributable to zero-rated exports are recoverable, generating net GST refunds when export volume exceeds domestic taxable sales.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or Canadian Export Single Window electronic declaration filed when shipment value exceeds CAD 2,000 or for regulated goods
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or courier receipt evidencing physical departure of goods from Canada
  • Commercial Invoice identifying the Alberta LP as exporter, its CRA Business Number, destination country, HS commodity codes, values, and export contract reference
  • Destination-country customs entry or bonded cartage record per SOR/92-249 evidencing arrival and delivery of the goods
  • Subscription contract and payment settlement evidence, including processor settlement reports, SWIFT credit advices, or foreign-currency bank statements substantiating receipt of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing API, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. Requirements: (i) GST/HST registrants must issue valid invoices containing supplier name, Business Number, date, description of supply, and GST charged; (ii) registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically; (iii) remittances exceeding CAD 10,000 must be paid electronically; (iv) all books and records, including digital invoices, shipping contracts, and export evidence, must be retained for six years and produced in readable electronic form upon CRA request.

CRA may reassess beyond the ordinary four-year limitation period where zero-rating is claimed through misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. Where export evidence is missing or false, CRA retroactively voids the zero-rating, assesses 5% GST as uncollected tax, disallows and claws back input tax credits connected to the exported goods, and charges interest from the original filing due date. Penalties include a 25% gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 285 for false statements, a general failure-to-file or failure-to-remit penalty under ETA s. 280, and prosecution fines up to CAD 100,000 on summary conviction or CAD 1,000,000 on indictment with imprisonment under ETA s. 327 for wilful evasion. Export substantiation records must be retained for six years, and repeat non-compliance may result in loss of the net GST refund entitlement and mandatory security requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts under a free-floating CAD regime. The Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1952, and no exchange-control approvals, repatriation permits, or conversion quotas apply to a Canadian Limited Partnership. Subscription-box current-account receipts (card settlements, wire payments) and capital-account partnership contributions are freely convertible into CAD. Profit remittances and capital distributions to non-resident limited partners are unrestricted at the treasury level, but may attract Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally 25% resident rate, reduced by applicable treaty) for amounts characterized as interest, dividends, or certain partnership allocations; return-of-capital distributions are not subject to withholding. The Investment Canada Act requires notification/review only for control-acquisition thresholds above statutory monetary limits, which does not impede ordinary capital injections.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory surrender ratio, or repatriation requirement for export or cross-border service proceeds, including subscription-box sales earned in foreign currency. Exports may be funded into any Canadian or offshore account freely. The only applicable obligations are: (i) FINTRAC reporting by the bank of international electronic fund transfers (IETF) of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour period; (ii) record-keeping for cross-border currency instruments over CAD 10,000 under the Customs Act; and (iii) corporate tax reporting of foreign-source income and the requirement to translate foreign-currency revenues into CAD for income tax under the Income Tax Act functional-currency rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) serves as the central monetary authority. Formal foreign-exchange administration is shared: Department of Finance Canada holds policy authority under the Currency Act and the Financial Administration Act; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises Authorized Foreign Banks and domestic banks under the Bank Act; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers cross-border transaction monitoring under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as a supplemental disbursement and FX overlay rail; Low as a sole primary bank. Legal constraints: Canadian-domiciled fintech EMIs must register as money service businesses (MSB) with FINTRAC and, since the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime became operational, with the Bank of Canada as payment service providers (PSPs) unless an exemption applies. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) may operate for payout routing and virtual-account issuance, but cannot accept deposits in Canada without a bank license; their CAD settlement must sit on a Schedule I bank account or an MSB/PSP safeguarded account. Recommended architecture: primary CAD/U.S. dollar bank accounts at RBC or BMO; EMI rails used only for disbursement to beneficiaries in low-value foreign currencies and for processor-driven supplier payouts, with funds prefunded by SWIFT from the Canadian bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partner contributions are routed via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's CAD-denominated operational account at a Schedule I bank. Canada does not require a government-issued purpose code; the bank mandates a payment narrative referencing the executed subscription agreement and capital-call notice. The receiving bank performs OFAC and Canadian sanctions screening and files a FINTRAC IETF report for amounts equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 within 24 hours. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High as a supplemental disbursement and FX overlay rail; Low as a sole primary bank. Legal constraints: Canadian-domiciled fintech EMIs must register as money service businesses (MSB) with FINTRAC and, since the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime became operational, with the Bank of Canada as payment service providers (PSPs) unless an exemption applies. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) may operate for payout routing and virtual-account issuance, but cannot accept deposits in Canada without a bank license; their CAD settlement must sit on a Schedule I bank account or an MSB/PSP safeguarded account. Recommended architecture: primary CAD/U.S. dollar bank accounts at RBC or BMO; EMI rails used only for disbursement to beneficiaries in low-value foreign currencies and for processor-driven supplier payouts, with funds prefunded by SWIFT from the Canadian bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined Federal and Alberta general corporate rate (15% Federal + 8% Alberta provincial). The CA_LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and ITA s. 96; each partner is taxed on its agreed share of the LP's income at the partner's own corporate or personal rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident persons (Income Tax Act s. 212(2)); Alberta imposes no separate provincial dividend withholding tax. For a CA_LP, cash distributions to non-resident limited partners are not classically 'dividends', but the CRA requires s. 105 withholding at 15% of the non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source partnership income, applied as an advance payment toward the final Part XIII liability when the partner files an NR7/NR4 clearing return. Domestic statutory rate on the net of amounts not treaty-protected remains 25%.

Due within 5 months after the LP's fiscal period end if any partner is a corporation, or 6 months if all partners are individuals; the LP's fiscal period is generally December 31 unless a grandfathered non-calendar period under s. 249.1 ITA applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Penalty benchmark under s. 247(3) ITA: a taxpayer is liable for a transfer-pricing penalty where its gross revenue exceeds CAD 10,000,000 for the year and the aggregate value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 for goods, services, and intangibles, or CAD 5,000,000 for intercompany loans. Local File / Master File-style contemporaneous documentation must be completed before the due date of the T2/AT1 return to substantiate the arm's-length result and secure the 'reasonable efforts' defense; the CA_LP as a pass-through must ensure its corporate partners hold the documentation.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, s. 245 ITA) instead of a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations regime. Transactions executed by the CA_LP that are artificial, lack real economic substance, and misuse or abuse the Act's provisions are recharacterized, and the resulting tax benefit is denied. Further, s. 246 ITA taxes inadvertent shareholder and partner benefits. Alberta does not maintain an ESR regime, but the CRA examines commercial justification, the general partner's actual management presence in Alberta, and the alignment of the LP's profit allocation with economic contribution; a shell-like LP without genuine Canadian management and business operations is a primary GAAR and residency-risk target.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: s. 239(1) ITA: upon summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years; upon indictment, imprisonment for up to 5 years and a similar monetary fine; in addition, penalized interest plus civil reassessment increases. Taxpayers may also face a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the misstatement amount under s. 163.2 ITA.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors who knowingly participate can be made personally liable for the full tax debt plus interest through CRA civil collection measures, including Canadian writs of seizure and sale; criminal courts may issue cross-border travel restrictions or seizure of passports; controlling persons face extradition risk and imprisonment for tax fraud at the federal level.
Transfer-Pricing Documentation Failure and Gross Valuation Misstatement

Statutory sanction: s. 247(3) ITA penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment, reduced by any Part I.3 tax, and not less than CAD 2,500; interest and reassessments apply to the full adjustment; documentation failure independently triggers the penalty even where the pricing is ultimately arm's-length.

Director personal liability: Directors or general partners who authorized the filings without reasonable due diligence are joint and severally liable under s. 227.1 ITA for any unremitted withholding amounts linked to the adjustment; CRA may issue a director's liability assessment and pursue personal bank accounts and real estate; travel bans are possible where criminal negligence is asserted under s. 239(2) ITA.
Shell Company / GAAR Abusive Avoidance

Statutory sanction: GAAR under s. 245 ITA: the entire tax benefit is denied and the transaction is recharacterized on a reasonable basis; a dedicated GAAR penalty of 50% of the tax benefit under s. 163.2(6) plus interest. The CRA may also invoke s. 163(2) for false statements and s. 246(1) for shareholder/partner benefits, subjecting the recipient to full inclusion of the benefit.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the limited partnership's veil only where the entity is used for fraud or as a sham; where the veil is pierced, general partners and controlling directors face civil restitution for all avoided taxes, punitive damages in egregious cases, restriction of cross-border movement through judicial orders, and confinement under criminal fraud provisions of the Criminal Code (RSC 1985, c. C-46, s. 380).
AML / Proceeds of Crime and Unremitted Withholding

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA of up to CAD 500,000 per violation for entities (and CAD 100,000 for individuals); criminal fines and imprisonment on conviction; Part XIII unremitted amounts attract a 10%–20% penalty under s. 227(8)-(9) ITA plus daily compound interest and full-tax reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are personally assessed under s. 227.1 ITA for all unremitted withholding taxes, penalties, and interest; FINTRAC compliance officers may impose compliance orders; for criminal AML violations, principals risk property seizure, an international travel-ban order pending trial, and imprisonment for up to 5 years; corporate veil protection is unavailable where the entity is a mere instrumentality of the wrongdoing.
