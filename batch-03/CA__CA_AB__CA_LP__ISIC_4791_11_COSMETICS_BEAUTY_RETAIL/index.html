Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and GST/HST NETFILE filing system; Alberta provincial revenue matters are administered by Alberta Treasury Board and Finance, although Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax and no separate provincial tax registration is required for a limited partnership.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Determine registration duty under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 240: a foreign-owned Alberta LP that carries on business in Canada must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account before making its first taxable supply in Canada if total worldwide and Canadian taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters. Step 2 — File an application through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), by telephone, or by submitting Form RC1 together with the LP's Alberta Partnership Act registration details, the responsible general partner's identification, and the intended program accounts (RT for GST/HST; RP for payroll if the LP employs staff). Step 3 — For each non-resident limited partner, provide the partner's legal name, jurisdiction of tax residence, and proportionate share; CRA requires a Schedule 50 form and may request a non-resident account designation. Step 4 — Appoint an authorized representative with a CRA RepID to manage the BN, the GST/HST NETFILE access code, and the My Business Account portal. Step 5 — Register before launch of the Canadian e-commerce operation; electronic applications are typically processed within one to three business days. No minimum capital or government pre-approval applies to foreign ownership of an Alberta limited partnership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — An Alberta Limited Partnership is a Canadian partnership within Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96: income and losses flow through to partners and the partnership itself is not a taxable entity for income tax purposes. The partnership must file a T5013 information return, while each general or limited partner reports its allocable share in its own T1, T2, or T3 return. Territorial scope — a non-resident partner is taxable in Canada only on income from a business carried on through a permanent establishment in Canada (ITA ss. 2(3), 115 and applicable tax treaty Article VII); for GST/HST purposes the partnership is nevertheless a separate person under ETA s. 221(1). Alberta does not tax the partnership itself; Alberta corporate or personal income tax applies at the partner level. No tax-exempt zone designation exists in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada has no general check-the-box election; an LP cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation. Available elections include: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 (Form T1100) to align the LP's year-end with the calendar year of partners; (ii) ITA s. 98 rollover elections on partner admission, retirement, or partnership continuation for tax-deferred treatment; (iii) ITA s. 216 election by a non-resident partner performing services in Canada to report Canadian-source income on a net basis; (iv) voluntary GST/HST registration of a non-resident person under ETA s. 177(1) in advance of the threshold; and (v) alignment of hybrid entity treatment under CRA Income Tax Folio S4-F3-C1 where a foreign partner's jurisdiction treats the Alberta LP as an opaque corporation — the partner must conform Canadian classification to foreign classification to avoid deduction/non-inclusion mismatches, and ITA s. 18.4 denies deductions arising from hybrid mismatch arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of total taxable supplies, measured globally and in Canada, over any four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148(1)(d) small-supplier rule). A non-resident LP that crosses the threshold must register. Since July 1, 2021, marketplace facilitators such as Shopify are deemed to make the supply of goods sold through their online marketplace (ETA s. 152.01), so the LP may be relieved from collecting GST/HST on marketplace-facilitated Canadian domestic sales, but the LP must nonetheless register for its own direct sales and to recover input tax credits on its Canadian business expenses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1, a supply of tangible personal property (cosmetics and personal-care products) is zero-rated when the seller exports the goods, or exports them on behalf of the non-resident purchaser, as part of the supply and the goods are exported within 60 days after delivery. For online direct-to-consumer retail, the LP must (i) obtain and retain non-resident status evidence for the buyer (foreign delivery address, payment origin, IP/geolocation, country-of-residence declaration), (ii) physically ship the goods to a destination outside Canada, and (iii) maintain contemporaneous export documentation. Digital and offshore services (hosting, advertising, payment facilitation) supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 6, provided the service is not in respect of land or tangible personal property situated in Canada. Zero-rating preserves full input tax credit recovery for the LP.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA Form B13A or the electronic export declaration generated through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) and accepted by the Canada Border Services Agency at the port of export
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — carrier-issued transport document (ocean, air, courier manifest, or truck waybill) evidencing physical removal of the goods from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank or payment-provider settlement evidence linking the foreign purchaser's payment, including Shopify Payments or PayPal settlement statements, to the specific export invoice
  • Non-resident customer dossier — purchase order, foreign delivery address, IP/country code, and customer country-of-residence declaration
  • Export inventory reconciliation — commercial invoice, packing list, and internal shipping log matching SKUs to the B13A export declaration and bill of lading

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or tax-authority API-based e-invoice validation. The CRA is advancing a voluntary Peppol-compliant national e-invoicing framework (Pan-Canadian Trust Framework) but no mandate is in force. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants whose annual gross revenue exceeds CAD 1 million; all registrants must retain books, invoices, and point-of-sale records in readable form for six years (ETA s. 286.1). Shopify Payments and PayPal settlement reports serve as the principal invoice and payment evidence; no further government clearance or pre-stamping is required.

CRA may reassess the LP's GST/HST returns within four years of filing (ETA s. 298), or without a time limit where the zero-rating claim was fraudulently or negligently misstated. Unsupported export claims are reversed at the 5% GST rate, input tax credits are denied, and interest at the prescribed rate is charged from the original due date. Late-filing penalties under ETA s. 280(2) accrue at 1% of the amount plus 0.25% per full month for up to 12 months. False or misleading statements attract penalties under ETA s. 285.1; tax evasion under ETA s. 296 or ITA s. 239(1) carries fines of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. ETA s. 272.1 imposes joint and several liability on partners for partnership GST/HST debts to the extent of their share where the partner knew or ought to have known of the failure. CRA high-risk audit criteria for e-commerce exporters require contemporaneous, not reconstructed, evidence: settlement proceeds must be traceable to the Canadian LP's bank accounts, and any diversion of export proceeds to offshore accounts without complete documentation triggers full clawback, gross negligence penalties, and referral to the CRA Criminal Investigations Program. The 180-day inward remittance benchmark is applied as the internal treasury ceiling for closing export invoices; failure to repatriate or document settlement within that period materially increases the risk that CRA will re-characterize the export as a domestic taxable supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account bases under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act; no exchange-control approval, prior registration, conversion cap, or repatriation permit applies to inward capital contributions, trade settlements, or distributions. Profit remittance by the CA_LP to non-resident partners is legally unrestricted, but the partnership must withhold 15% of the non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source income under section 194 of the Income Tax Act (subject to treaty reduction, e.g., 15% under the Canada-US treaty), and must retain Form T5013 allocation records and Part XIII remittance documentation. The Investment Canada Act imposes no notification for a passive operating-capital injection into an LP unless the enterprise value/control thresholds are met.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation deadlines for export receipts. The CA_LP may retain export proceeds in CAD or foreign currency in any domestic or foreign account. Statutory reporting is limited to CBSA/Statistics Canada trade declarations for goods exported and FINTRAC obligations applicable to reporting entities: international EFTs at or above CAD 10,000, large cash transactions at or above CAD 10,000, and incoming/outgoing international EFT reports.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole central bank and monetary authority. Canada maintains no standalone foreign-exchange administration; exchange-control policy is shared with the Department of Finance Canada, while currency-related AML/CFT supervision of money services businesses is exercised by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Rating: Medium for settlement/collection rails, Low for treasury clearing. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (combined federal 15% + Alberta general 12%; LP is pass-through, entity-level rate not applicable)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders; not applicable to LP distributions (pass-through). For LP, non-resident partners are taxed on allocated income under Part I, and distributions are generally not subject to additional withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for services transactions; CAD 5,000,000 for goods transactions (per transaction or aggregate with same non-resident). Master File required if total revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty shopping provisions require genuine economic substance. Partnerships must demonstrate real business activity, employees, and decision-making in Canada to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the understated tax (gross negligence); criminal prosecution with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes if they participated in the evasion; travel bans and seizure of personal assets possible
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties up to 25% of the tax avoided

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for corporate tax debts if they knowingly participated in the sham arrangement; potential criminal charges for fraud
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 2,000,000 for entities; criminal penalties for deliberate non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned (up to 5 years) if they knowingly fail to comply with AML obligations
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation incomplete), and interest on underpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the transfer pricing mispricing; potential for criminal charges in egregious cases
