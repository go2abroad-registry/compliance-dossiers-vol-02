Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account; GST/HST program account (RT) administered at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.

E-government filing portal

The Limited Partnership must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a separate RT (GST/HST) program account. Foreign entities without Canadian presence apply using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or CRA Business Registration Online; non-resident registrants use Form RC459. The LP must also register with the Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES) to obtain an Alberta corporate access number. After registration, the LP files an annual T5013 partnership information return and each partner reports allocated income; non-resident partners may require their own BN or Canadian tax account for withholding obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a Canadian limited partnership is not a taxpayer under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (ITA); income is computed at the partnership level and flows through to partners. Residents are taxed on worldwide income; non-residents only on Canadian-source income. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is deemed a person under ETA section 272 and registers in its own name.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election under federal income tax; the LP is mandatorily flow-through. Available elections include: (i) non-calendar fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1; (ii) GST/HST quick method election under ETA s. 227 for qualifying small suppliers; (iii) appointment of an agent or representative by a non-resident registrant under ETA s. 272.1; (iv) for cross-border classification, IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election may treat the Canadian LP as an association taxable as a corporation for U.S. purposes, creating hybrid status constrained by Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4–18.6).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold, ETA s. 148). Non-resident e-commerce sellers of tangible goods in Canada must register once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 and taxable supplies are made in Canada. Marketplace facilitator and platform rules may shift the registration and collection obligation to the platform operator; Stripe and PayPal are payment rails and do not themselves trigger registration unless contracted as merchant of record.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property qualify for 0% GST under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, item 1 where the supplier retains evidence satisfactory to the Minister that the goods were exported. Pet supplies sold through e-commerce to non-resident customers qualify if shipped to a foreign destination by the LP or a fulfillment agent; drop-shipment supplies fall under Schedule VI, Part V, items 2 and 3. Services such as after-sale support may be zero-rated under Part VII when rendered outside Canada or in respect of exported goods. Any supply delivered to a Canadian address is taxable at 5% GST regardless of the purchaser's residency.

None — Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, API-based invoice pre-approval, or mandatory e-invoicing for GST/HST. CRA promotes a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing standard for B2B transactions and accepts electronic records provided they are complete, legible, and auditable. The LP may issue electronic invoices via e-commerce systems but must retain full supporting documentation for periodic CRA verification.

CRA may reassess within 4 years of the taxation year (unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default) to reverse zero-rating, recover uncollected GST, and deny corresponding input tax credits. Gross negligence penalty is the greater of CAD 500 and 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded (ETA s. 285); false statements or omissions attract penalties under ETA s. 280. Criminal evasion is an offence under ETA s. 327 and may result in imprisonment and fines. Partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's net tax obligations under ETA s. 272. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX-repatriation deadline for export zero-rating; the 180-day parameter reflects CRA administrative review expectations for currency settlement and supporting documentation, and stale or unsupported FX evidence materially increases audit and clawback risk.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full free-floating convertibility with no capital-account restrictions; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII status since 25 March 1994, guaranteeing current-account convertibility without exchange-control approval. An Alberta Limited Partnership may freely receive foreign currency, remit profits, repay partner loans, and redeploy capital. The only operative constraints are tax-administered: Part XIII withholding tax at 25 percent (treaty-reduced to between 5 and 15 percent) on distributions of net income to non-resident partners, and the requirement for the LP to file NR301/NR4 returns. Whether a remittance is treated as income distribution or tax-free capital return is governed by the partnership agreement and capital accounts, not by FX decree.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist in Canada. There are no statutory timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation requirements for export proceeds or electronic-commerce settlement. The Alberta LP may hold one hundred percent of foreign-currency receipts (e.g., USD) indefinitely in a designated foreign-currency bank account. The only statutory reporting obligations are FINTRAC's international electronic funds transfer report (EFTR) for any transfer at or above CAD 10,000, filed by the financial institution within 15 business days, and the cross-border physical currency or monetary-instrument report at the same CAD 10,000 threshold for cash entering or leaving Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - the central bank and monetary authority, and regulator of the retail payments ecosystem under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; prudential regulation of banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces cross-border funds transfer and currency reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM - viable exclusively as an ancillary collection and settlement rail, not as the primary treasury depository. Fintech EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Koho, Wealthsimple Cash, Stripe Treasury) are registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA and, unlike Schedule I banks, are legally incapable of taking commercial deposits under s. 91(15) of the Constitution Act, 1867. Funds held at an MSB or payment service provider benefit from neither CDIC deposit insurance nor OSFI prudential oversight. The compliant architecture for this Alberta LP is: Stripe/PayPal card settlement into the Schedule I bank operating account; daily treasury sweeps; and any pre-settlement processor float capped and disclosed in the treasury policy. The RPAA registration regime administered by the Bank of Canada provides a regulatory wrapper for EMIs but does not confer bank-deposit status.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital contributions should be routed via SWIFT MT103 with Field 70 narrative stating: CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO [CA_LP] - PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT DATED [DATE] - [PARTNER NAME]; Field 50K must name the remitting partner and Field 57A the receiving Schedule I bank. No exchange-control approval is required. For CAD, use the Canadian Large Value Transfer System; for USD, use the bank's cross-border book transfer or Fedwire into its US subsidiary; convert via the bank's FX desk if a different currency. The KYC file should pre-identify the expected capital injection amount. The bank automatically files a FINTRAC EFTR for every incoming international transfer at or above CAD 10,000. Structure the injection as a capital-account credit, not a shareholder loan, to avoid Part XIII interest withholding. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined federal-provincial headline for active business income of an Alberta corporate taxpayer (federal 15% + Alberta 8%). The Alberta Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and therefore has no entity-level corporate income tax; the rate applies to the allocated share where the partner is a corporation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident. A Canadian Limited Partnership is not a corporation; distributions of accumulated capital or profit to partners are not 'dividends' and are not generally subject to Part XIII WHT, provided the amount is a return of capital or allocation of income already taxable under Part I on self-assessment. If the LP is recharacterized as a corporation under foreign law, distributions may be reclassified as dividends and the 25% rate can apply.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions per fiscal period triggers Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) and mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File). Multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more may also be required to file a Country-by-Country report and maintain a Master File.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in some offshore centres. The CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, s. 245 ITA) to deny tax benefits from arrangements lacking genuine economic substance. For an Alberta LP, the limited-partner liability shield is lost if a limited partner participates in control (Alberta Partnership Act), and the LP must maintain a registered Alberta office, file its annual registration, and avoid a sham characterization. E-commerce operations should maintain board meeting records, marketing contracts, and operational substance in Alberta to withstand CRA challenge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus arrears interest. Criminal prosecution under s. 239(1) ITA can lead to a fine not exceeding 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to two years; the partners are jointly and severally liable for the tax attributable to a Canadian-source business.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the corporate general partner who authorize false returns are personally exposed to criminal liability, restitution orders, travel restrictions pending trial, and debarment from managing other Alberta companies.
Shell Company and Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to annihilate tax attributes and impose gross-negligence penalties of 50%. Alberta Partnership Act makes a participating limited partner jointly and severally liable for all partnership debts; the Corporate General Partner can be stripped of limited liability and the LP may be struck from the Alberta registry for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner can be personally liable for partnership debts when the general partner is a bare shell and lacks sufficient capital; courts may pierce the corporate veil and order equitable compensation, asset freezes, and director disqualification.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Transparency

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative penalties up to CAD 500,000 for entities and CAD 100,000 for individuals under the PCMLTFA; criminal money-laundering offences under the Criminal Code carry imprisonment up to ten years. Alberta's corporate transparency registry may also impose late-filing fees or strike the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or authorize an unregistered cash-intensive operation or fail to file beneficial-ownership returns can be held personally liable for FINTRAC penalties and may face seizure orders, travel bans, and prison terms.
