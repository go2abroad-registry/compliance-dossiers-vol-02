Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — official portal: Canada.ca/CRA; business registration at CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). Alberta does not operate a separate provincial income tax administration; corporate and income tax registration is administered federally through the CRA.

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned Alberta Limited Partnership (CA_LP) must first obtain a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account. The standard procedure for a non-resident entity is (i) complete Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or apply online via CRA Business Registration Online, indicating the federal tax account type 'RT' (GST/HST); (ii) provide the partnership's Alberta registration certificate, foreign registration particulars, authorized representative information, and the partnership's fiscal period; (iii) specify the expected effective date of first taxable supply in Canada; (iv) if the entity has no Canadian physical presence, appoint an authorized agent/representative with a valid Canadian address for CRA correspondence; and (v) upon issuance, CRA assigns a 15-character program account identifier in the format BN-RT-0001. The same BN may later be extended with payroll (RP), corporate income (RC), or customs (RM) program accounts if required.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — The foreign-owned Alberta Limited Partnership (CA_LP) must first obtain a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account. The standard procedure for a non-resident entity is (i) complete Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or apply online via CRA Business Registration Online, indicating the federal tax account type 'RT' (GST/HST); (ii) provide the partnership's Alberta registration certificate, foreign registration particulars, authorized representative information, and the partnership's fiscal period; (iii) specify the expected effective date of first taxable supply in Canada; (iv) if the entity has no Canadian physical presence, appoint an authorized agent/representative with a valid Canadian address for CRA correspondence; and (v) upon issuance, CRA assigns a 15-character program account identifier in the format BN-RT-0001. The same BN may later be extended with payroll (RP), corporate income (RC), or customs (RM) program accounts if required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box election. The Alberta Limited Partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (Canada): the LP itself is not a taxpayer, and income is allocated to and taxed at the partner level. No entity-level classification election is available to convert the LP into a taxable corporation. Relevant statutory elections include (i) a partnership or partner election to use alternative loss-allocation or fiscal-period rules; (ii) a non-resident partner election under section 216 of the Income Tax Act to file a net-basis Canadian income tax return rather than relying on Part XIII withholding; and (iii) for GST/HST purposes, a registrant may make an election to have taxable supplies of certain closely related persons or joint venture participants treated as made by the registrant, and a 'partnership joint election' may be used to simplify accounting for partnership assets. Hybrid mismatch rules under Part I of the Income Tax Act apply to deny double-deduction or deduction/non-inclusion benefits in cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold. An entity must register for GST/HST if worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; below that threshold registration is voluntary but required for claiming input tax credits. The threshold is computed on the worldwide taxable supplies of the partnership and its associated entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The online retailer may claim the 0% GST rate on exports of oversized furniture and home decor if (i) the goods are physically delivered by the supplier from Canada to a destination outside Canada; or (ii) title and possession are transferred to a non-resident recipient and the goods are exported by the recipient within 60 days of the supply, provided the goods are not consumed or put to use in Canada prior to export. In all cases the supplier must hold documentary evidence sufficient to prove that the goods were actually exported. Supplies of offshore services (e.g., freight arrangement or post-sale support) are zero-rated only where the recipient is outside Canada and the services are not performed in Canada, or otherwise meet the Schedule VI zero-rating conditions. Exported goods are not subject to Alberta retail sales tax because no such tax exists; however, the seller must still properly account for any incidental domestic supplies (e.g., failed-delivery resale within Canada) at 5% GST.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A form or Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) approved electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing movement of the oversized goods from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/foreign-currency payment proof confirming receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and order documentation matching the customs declaration
  • Carrier tracking records and proof of delivery outside Canada

Canada does not currently mandate a national real-time e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping regime. Electronic invoices in PDF/XML are acceptable if they retain integrity and readability. CRA does not operate an API-linked fiscal clearance portal for invoice stamping; however, GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE (GST34) or via a registered GST/HST filing service, and CRA has an online portal for accounts. Since 2024 the federal government has signaled convergence toward the PEPPOL pan-Canadian e-invoicing standard for B2B procurement, but it remains voluntary for non-government-supply transactions. Oversized home-furniture e-commerce sellers must nevertheless retain complete electronic transaction logs, invoice sequencing, and reconciliation records to demonstrate output tax on taxable domestic sales and support zero-rating on export sales.

CRA may deny zero-rating where export documentation is incomplete or demonstrates the goods remained in Canada or were returned and resold domestically, applying the normal assessment rules under the Excise Tax Act. Reassessments may issue up to four years after the filing date, and the limitation period is extended indefinitely if the registrant has made a misrepresentation that is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default. Penalties for fraudulent or false statements include (i) the GST/HST gross negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 10% of the net tax misstatement and (ii) the new penalty for wilful failure to report, which raises the penalty to the greater of CAD 1,000 and 25% of the amount where the taxpayer knowingly fails to correct false statements. Interest runs from the due date at prescribed CRA rates, and criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act/Excise Tax Act can lead to fines and imprisonment in serious fraud cases. CRA's indirect taxation audit teams specifically scrutinize e-commerce exporters for fabricated bills of lading and fake overseas addresses; any clawback of zero-rating will also trigger denial of input tax credits tied to the export supply plus reduction of any refund or net tax remittance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating currency with no exchange-control restrictions on inward investment, dividend distributions, profit repatriation, or loan repayments. Capital inflows into a Canadian limited partnership are unrestricted, though transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 in a single cross-border wire are subject to FINTRAC reporting by financial intermediaries. Profit remittances to non-resident partners are permitted without exchange-control approval; the only statutory impediment is Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), which imposes a 25% base withholding rate on deemed distributions to non-residents unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty (e.g., Canada-U.S. treaty reduces to 15% on dividends for portfolio holders, with further reduction for substantial participation).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriating export proceeds. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign-currency receipts in offshore or onshore foreign-currency bank accounts. Notwithstanding the absence of surrender rules, all cross-border trade-related payments must be reported under Statistics Canada's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) and to FINTRAC when the wire exceeds CAD 10,000. The enterprise must maintain underlying commercial documentation (commercial invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations) to substantiate the current-account nature of remittances.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with supervisory enforcement by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money-laundering oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange control authority comparable to SAFE; exchange matters are governed by the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).
