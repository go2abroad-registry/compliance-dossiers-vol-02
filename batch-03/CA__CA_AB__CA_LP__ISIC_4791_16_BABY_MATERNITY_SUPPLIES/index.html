Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Ottawa; principal portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html) and GST/HST NETFILE/REVNET. Alberta corporate income tax is administered federally by the CRA on behalf of Alberta Treasury Board and Finance / Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA). Alberta does not impose a provincial sales tax, so the sole indirect tax in CA-AB is the 5% federal GST.

E-government filing portal

For the non-resident Alberta LP selling baby/maternity goods online (ISIC 4791 / NAICS 458110): (1) Analyze 'carrying on business in Canada' - a non-resident supplier making taxable supplies in Canada and not qualifying as a small supplier (i.e., total taxable supplies made in Canada equal or exceed CAD 30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters) must register for GST/HST under ETA s. 240; (2) Apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account (RT) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) by mail/fax; non-resident applicants must provide a Canadian representative/agent with authority and either a Canadian mailing address or a properly declared foreign address; (3) Upon approval the LP obtains a BN-RT registration certificate; Alberta does not issue a separate partnership TIN because the LP is fiscally transparent, and partners report allocated income through the T5013 information-return process; (4) Alberta corporate income tax accounts (WP/TP) are opened only where a corporate partner itself carries on business in Alberta through a permanent establishment; (5) No federal or provincial statutory license is required for online maternity/infant e-commerce, though standard municipal business licensing may apply at the place of operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under ITA s. 96, the Alberta Limited Partnership is not a taxpayer for income tax purposes; income and loss flow through to general and limited partners, who are taxed at their own rates, and non-resident partners are generally entitled to treaty-protected treatment as U.S. residents but remain subject to Canadian withholding/Part I tax on Canadian-source amounts absent an applicable treaty tie-breaker. For GST/HST purposes the LP is itself a 'person' (ETA s. 221(1)) and must hold its own registration. Territorial scope: supplies made in Canada (delivery to Canadian consumers) are subject to 5% GST; goods exported are zero-rated. The LP is not located in a tax-exempt zone: Alberta has no PST/HST and its 8% corporate income tax (for the 2024/2025 rate year) applies only at the level of corporate partners, not to the transparent LP.

Available election forms

Canadian tax law does not provide US-style 'check-the-box' elections; classification follows the actual legal form and the ETA/ITA statutory rules, subject to Canada-US treaty residence analysis for hybrid LPs. Available elections: (1) Voluntary GST/HST registration for a small supplier (ETA s. 240(2)) to claim input tax credits before crossing the CAD 30,000 threshold; (2) Partnership alternative fiscal-period election (ITA s. 249.1(4)/(5)) permitting a non-calendar tax year consistent with all partners; (3) Functional-currency election (ITA s. 261), allowing the LP to keep books and file returns in a qualified foreign currency (e.g., USD) with CRA conversion rules; (4) ITA s. 216 and s. 217 elections for non-resident persons to report certain net income instead of gross Part XIII withholding; (5) ETA s. 141.02 election among partners to allocate input tax credits in a simplified manner; (6) Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 as amended in 2021) which restrict deductions on structured payments producing deduction/non-inclusion outcomes with a foreign partner, and for which no elective carve-out is available - the CRA follows OECD BEPS Action 2 principles.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 measured over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters plus the current quarter, based on taxable supplies made in Canada (the small-supplier rule, ETA ss. 148(2) and 240). Once a non-resident e-commerce LP exceeds CAD 30,000, registration is mandatory and GST at 5% must be collected on Canadian consumer sales. If the LP sells through a non-resident marketplace facilitator, the platform may bear the collection duty under the 2021 simplified GST/HST framework for non-resident vendors; direct-to-consumer sales by the LP require its own registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a supply of tangible personal property that is exported by the supplier or delivered to a carrier/consignee for export is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1, provided export occurs promptly, generally within 60 days of delivery. Services: a supply of a service to a non-resident person is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 unless the service relates to real/immovable property situated in Canada, tangible personal property located in Canada at the time the service is performed, or the recipient is a GST/HST registrant; digital customer-support and returns-management services supplied for offshore end-consumers must be structured so that the recipient is abroad and the service is performed in material part outside Canada. Claim mechanics: the LP reports zero-rated export supplies on its GST/HST return, claims input tax credits for Canadian warehousing, logistics, advertising, and marketplace fees on the same return, and, if credits exceed tax collected, receives a refund from the CRA, generally issued within 30 days of filing.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the foreign consignee and evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list describing the goods, quantities, sale price, and Incoterms
  • Foreign-exchange receipt evidence (SWIFT MT103, bank statement, or Stripe/PayPal settlement record) proving the cross-border remittance from the buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. The CRA promotes voluntary e-invoicing through the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 network, and B2G e-invoicing is being phased in for supplies to the Government of Canada. The mandatory digital touchpoints are electronic filing of GST/HST returns (via GST/HST NETFILE/REVNET or commercial tax software) and electronic record retention in a CRA-acceptable, timely and legible format under ETA s. 286. Invoices may be issued in paper or electronic form, but must satisfy the content requirements of ETA ss. 169/170, with a simplified invoice threshold of CAD 150.

Although the ETA does not impose a hard statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline (unlike certain Asian export regimes), the CRA applies a 180-day documentary benchmark for substantiating export transactions. Where export evidence is deficient, the CRA reassesses the supply as taxable at 5% GST, disallows associated input tax credits, and assesses arrears interest and penalties: gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the amount (ETA s. 285), false-statement penalties up to CAD 25,000 (ETA s. 280), a penalty for wilful evasion under ITA s. 239, and criminal liability under ETA ss. 327-329 with imprisonment up to five years in cases of fraudulent export zero-rating. Reassessments may be issued within the normal four-year limitation period, extended to seven to ten years where misrepresentation or neglect is present.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account; there are no exchange controls, repatriation permits, or currency allocation mechanisms. Profit remittance is unrestricted; Canadian-source income allocations to non-resident partners are taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on a net basis with treaty foreign-tax credit relief, while enumerated payment types (interest, rent, royalties, dividends) attract Part XIII withholding at 25% statutory, often treaty-reduced to 0-15%. Documentary hurdles are limited to FATCA/CRS self-certification and W-9/W-8BEN/W-8BEN-E forms to evidence beneficial ownership and tax residence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline on export receipts. CAD and foreign-currency proceeds may be retained indefinitely in onshore or offshore accounts. The only compliance trigger is FINTRAC reporting of international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, plus PCMLTFA record-keeping on large cash and monetary instruments.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) prudentially regulates Schedule I banks; FINTRAC enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); the Department of Finance Canada administers the Investment Canada Act (ICA) as a foreign-investment review statute, not a foreign-exchange control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium: Feasible as a payment-rail overlay only; Low as a substitute for a Schedule I deposit account. Stripe and PayPal, the target payment rails, are not deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and must settle merchant proceeds into a Canadian bank account; they operate as FINTRAC-registered payment service providers/MSBs. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) may provide virtual IBAN/Rails in USD/EUR/GBP and mid-market FX conversion for paying overseas maternity-supply suppliers, but they cannot lawfully hold LP statutory capital, access Canadian clearing, or serve as the CRS/FATCA tax-reporting bank. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Standard routing: the investor's bank sends a SWIFT MT103 to the LP's CAD/USD account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must carry the exact LP legal name, the Alberta Registration Number, and the label 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' per the Alberta Limited Partnership Agreement; the remitter name must match the partner of record to avoid a FINTRAC hold. For amounts equal to or above CAD 100,000, give the bank's International Banking Centre 48-hour pre-notice with a copy of the contribution agreement. For Stripe/PayPal proceeds, enable CAD settlement in the merchant dashboard; no provincial SWIFT purpose code exists, but all cross-border international EFTs of CAD 10,000+ are automatically reported to FINTRAC. For U.S. partner injections, the LP and each partner must have FATCA/CRS status documented (W-9 or W-8 forms); no exchange-control permit is required.Supplier payments: SWIFT MT103 for direct settlement; MT202 COV for bank-to-bank cover where an intermediary bank is involved. For first-time or high-value overseas factory sourcing, use Irrevocable Confirmed Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 with ISBP745 compliance, supported by commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, and quality inspection certificate; settle at sight or 30-90 days usance. For domestic Alberta and Canadian suppliers, use Lynx (high-value real-time gross settlement) for urgent payments and ACSS/EFT for day-to-day disbursements. E-commerce refunds are executed via Stripe/PayPal reverse rails; merchant settlement sweeps daily or T+2 into the LP operating account. Execute FX via the bank's forward desk at mid-market plus 10-15 pips for CAD/USD, keeping USD receipts in a USD-enveloped sub-account until disbursement to avoid double conversion.Deliverable OTC FX Forwards (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) for commercial hedging of inventory and supplier exposure, tenor up to 12 monthsFX Swaps (CAD/USD) for rolling liquidity conversion and trade settlementCross-Currency Swaps (fixed/floating) under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with CSA collateralCAD/USD FX options and zero-cost collars for downside protection on high-margin maternity-supply inventoryCME-listed Canadian Dollar futures and options (6C) for exchange-traded transparent hedgingNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) permissible only where the underlying settlement currency is non-convertible; for CAD pairs, onshore deliverable forwards are the standard and NDF is redundant

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium: Feasible as a payment-rail overlay only; Low as a substitute for a Schedule I deposit account. Stripe and PayPal, the target payment rails, are not deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and must settle merchant proceeds into a Canadian bank account; they operate as FINTRAC-registered payment service providers/MSBs. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) may provide virtual IBAN/Rails in USD/EUR/GBP and mid-market FX conversion for paying overseas maternity-supply suppliers, but they cannot lawfully hold LP statutory capital, access Canadian clearing, or serve as the CRS/FATCA tax-reporting bank. Legal constraint: any EMI transmitting or holding funds into Canada must be registered as an MSB under the PCMLTFA and must not act as an unlicensed bank. Recommended architecture: Schedule I bank for primary treasury and settlement; fintech rails only for sub-CAD 50,000/month supplier payouts and small-ticket FX conversion.
