Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada – GST/HST administration; CRA My Business Account portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html; Alberta does not levy a separate provincial sales tax, so the CRA is the sole GST/HST authority for this jurisdiction.

E-government filing portal

An Alberta limited partnership must obtain a federal CRA Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST account. Registration is made via Form RC1, Request for a Business Number, or through CRA My Business Account. Foreign entities may apply for a Non-Resident Business Number where the partnership has Canadian nexus; proof of legal existence, Alberta registry documentation, Canadian agent authorization, and estimated value of taxable supplies are required. The partnership itself is treated as a separate person for GST/HST purposes, and the general partner is responsible for statutory filings and remittances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; for GST/HST the partnership is a distinct registrant, and Alberta imposes no provincial capital or sales tax at the entity level.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. The limited partnership may rely on statutory provisions such as the Income Tax Act s.216(1) election for non-residents deriving Canadian rental income and the ETA s.273 joint election between closely related GST/HST registrants. Hybrid mismatch treatment is governed by specific federal ITA rules; Alberta has no separate entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of total worldwide taxable supplies in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident digital/supply via platform scenarios may trigger mandatory GST/HST registration without relying on the small-supplier threshold in specific circumstances.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property, including automotive aftermarket parts, are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where goods are exported from Canada by the supplier, the recipient, or a common carrier as part of a continuous export journey, and the supplier retains prescribed evidence. Export sales to non-residents for online retail qualify where the goods are shipped outside Canada and the vendor is a GST/HST registrant.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or CBSA Canada Customs Invoice)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/payment rail evidence of foreign-currency settlement

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority e-invoicing, API stamping, or digital stamping. GST/HST returns must be electronically filed via CRA GST/HST NETFILE or through trusted service provider APIs; records supporting zero-rated exports must be retained in electronic or paper format and produced upon audit demand.

CRA may reassess incorrectly zero-rated supplies, deny input tax credits, impose penalties under Excise Tax Act s.285 up to 20% of improperly claimed refund/credit amounts, add gross-negligence penalties, and charge arrears interest. Fraudulent export claims may result in summary or indictable prosecutions with further monetary penalties and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account under Canada's IMF Article VIII obligations. No prior regulatory approval is required for capital subscription, trade settlement, or profit remittance; the only structural friction for a non-resident partner of an Alberta Limited Partnership is Part XIII withholding tax at 25% (reduced under applicable tax treaties) on income allocations and distributions, plus FINTRAC reporting by the receiving bank for any international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more. Online auto-parts retail receipts may be held in USD without restriction, but books, records, and GST/HST filings must be maintained in CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no minimum retention currency requirement for export or e-commerce receipts in Canada. All proceeds from ISIC 4791 online auto-parts sales may remain in the currency of receipt; the only obligation is FINTRAC threshold reporting of CAD 10,000 or more for both incoming and outgoing electronic funds transfers, together with CBSA physical-cash declaration on Form E311 when CAD 10,000 or more is transported in or out.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the monetary authority but holds no exchange-control mandate; Canada has had no foreign-exchange control regime since the Foreign Exchange Control Act lapsed in 1951. Cross-border transaction monitoring and AML/CFT oversight of money services businesses are exercised by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; banking prudential supervision falls to OSFI and, for provincially chartered Alberta institutions, the Alberta Treasury Board and Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary cross-border settlement rail, Low as the primary treasury bank. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may only operate in Canada as FINTRAC-registered money services businesses; they cannot accept CDIC-insured deposits, issue Canadian Schedule I bank account numbers, or provide commercial lending, escrow, or letter-of-credit facilities. They remain useful for segregated FX float and low-cost supplier disbursements, but all regulated e-commerce revenue must ultimately settle into a Canadian chartered-bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Alberta limited partnership level where the partnership is treated as a flow-through entity. Income is allocated to partners and taxed at the partner level. Canadian-controlled private corporations generally face combined federal and Alberta rates of approximately 23% on eligible small-business income within applicable limits and approximately 23% to 30% on other income, depending on corporation status and income type.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on taxable dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident, subject to treaty reduction. An Alberta LP distribution is not automatically a dividend because an LP is generally flow-through; withholding must be analyzed by payment character, partner status, Canadian-source income, and applicable provisions, including rules for amounts paid or credited to partnerships with non-resident members.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 Partnership Information Return is generally due within 5 months after the partnership fiscal period-end. T5013 slips and the related summary are generally due by the last day of March following the calendar year to which the slips relate. Each partner separately reports its allocated income or loss in its applicable Canadian tax return. A non-resident partner may have Canadian filing obligations under the Income Tax Act.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no general monetary threshold that automatically makes a Master File or Local File mandatory in the OECD terminology. Contemporaneous documentation is required for non-arm's-length non-resident transactions under section 247 of the Income Tax Act when the statutory conditions apply. A T106 information return is generally required where reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate for the year, subject to exclusions and detailed rules. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to an ultimate parent entity or surrogate parent of an MNE group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million, or the Canadian-dollar equivalent prescribed for the relevant year. CRA may request documentation, including functional analysis, benchmarking, agreements, calculations, and global-group information.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance remains relevant under transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance rule, treaty entitlement and beneficial-ownership analysis, corporate residence, partnership classification, sham-transaction principles, and source-of-income rules. The LP should maintain commercially credible functions, personnel, decision-making, contractual rights, risk control, inventory evidence, banking records, and operational records in proportion to its claimed activities. A Canadian registered address alone does not establish Canadian substance or treaty entitlement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction may result in fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years under the Income Tax Act. Civil penalties may include 50% gross-negligence or false-statement penalties, reassessment, interest, and seizure or collection proceedings.

Director personal liability: Partners, general partners, officers, and other persons who authorized, assented to, participated in, or acquiesced in the conduct may face prosecution and personal fines or imprisonment. Limited liability does not protect a person from personal criminal conduct. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from bail conditions, judicial orders, or enforcement proceedings.
Failure to File Partnership Information Returns

Statutory sanction: Prescribed late-filing, electronic-filing, omission, and information-return penalties may apply, together with reassessment, interest, compliance notices, and restrictions on administrative relief. Partner-level penalties may arise separately.

Director personal liability: A general partner and responsible officers may be assessed for penalties arising from their own conduct. Limited partners are not automatically personally liable solely because of their status, but protection may be lost for unauthorized management, fraud, guarantees, or statutory personal-liability provisions.
GST/HST Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unremitted GST/HST, denial of input tax credits, prescribed interest, late-filing penalties, gross-negligence penalties, and potential prosecution. Tax collected and not remitted may be subject to trust and collection measures.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate partner or responsible persons may face director-liability assessments for unremitted GST/HST where statutory conditions are met. A person who knowingly participates in fraud may face criminal prosecution and imprisonment.
Improper Non-Resident Withholding

Statutory sanction: The payer may be liable for the unwithheld amount, prescribed arrears interest, and failure-to-deduct or failure-to-remit penalties. Treaty reduction without valid residency, beneficial-ownership, or entitlement evidence can be denied.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may incur personal liability for source deductions and related amounts under statutory director-liability rules where they failed to exercise due diligence. Criminal liability may arise for deliberate evasion or falsification.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income under section 247, deny deductions, impose an additional 10% transfer-pricing penalty on certain unreported adjustments, assess interest, and apply the general anti-avoidance rule where applicable.

Director personal liability: Personal liability is not automatic for a transfer-pricing adjustment, but responsible persons may face penalties or prosecution for deliberate false statements, obstruction, fraud, or participation in an abusive arrangement.
Shell Company, Sham Partnership, or Beneficial-Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may recharacterize the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, apply the general anti-avoidance rule, reassess income and withholding, impose penalties and interest, and refer suspected fraud or money laundering for prosecution.

Director personal liability: General partners, agents, beneficial owners, and professional facilitators may be personally liable for fraud, tax evasion, proceeds-of-crime, or money-laundering offences. Conviction can result in substantial fines, forfeiture, and imprisonment; travel bans are court-dependent rather than automatic.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Sanctions Breaches

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties, account restrictions, asset seizure or forfeiture, compliance orders, and criminal prosecution may apply. Proceeds-of-crime and money-laundering convictions can result in significant fines and imprisonment, including up to 10 years or more depending on the offence.

Director personal liability: Individuals who direct, authorize, facilitate, or knowingly participate may be prosecuted personally. Corporate or partnership form does not shield criminal participants. Travel restrictions, passport surrender, bail conditions, or prohibition orders may be imposed by a court.
Consumer, Product, and Automotive-Part Compliance

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include corrective orders, product recalls, injunctions, administrative penalties, consumer restitution, civil damages, seizure, and prosecution under applicable federal and Alberta consumer-protection and product-safety laws.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal exposure for knowing participation, statutory offences, fraudulent representations, or failure to comply with enforcement orders. Imprisonment is offence-specific and not automatic.
Corporate and Partnership Governance Failure

Statutory sanction: Possible loss of limited-liability protection, civil claims, accounting orders, injunctions, dissolution proceedings, registry penalties, and personal liability for obligations incurred during unauthorized conduct.

Director personal liability: An LP has a general partner rather than directors as its primary managing person. The general partner is ordinarily liable for partnership obligations; limited partners may become exposed if they exceed protected activities or commit independent wrongdoing.
