Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership. No escrow or deposit account is required. Each limited partner's liability is limited to the amount of capital actually contributed or agreed to be contributed under the partnership agreement.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: occupancy must satisfy the Alberta Fire Code and, where required by the landlord or municipal inspector, fire marshal inspection and an approved fire safety plan. Structural: no special structural load inspection is required for ordinary e-commerce shelving, but commercial racking and storage above prescribed loads must be engineered and inspected under OHS law. Occupational: the general partner must comply with the OHS Act and OHS Code for manual handling, electrical equipment, ergonomic workstation safety, and WCB registration if employees are engaged.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a liberalized foreign exchange regime with full convertibility for current account transactions under the Exchange Control Act. Capital account transactions require prior approval for certain investments exceeding thresholds, but direct equity investments and profit remittances are generally permitted without restriction. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal, subject to tax compliance and beneficial ownership verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds is required under Canadian law. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds or convert them at market rates. However, all export transactions must be reported to the Bank of Canada via the Electronic Data Interchange Reporting System (EDIR) if exceeding certain thresholds, with no fixed conversion ratio mandated.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions such as Stripe and PayPal offer high cross-border viability for an Alberta LP engaged in online jewelry and watches merchandising. Legal constraints include mandatory FINTRAC registration as a money services business, enhanced due diligence for high-risk merchant categories, and adherence to FATF standards. Offshore digital EMIs must navigate potential CFC attribution rules and transfer pricing documentation requirements when facilitating cross-border flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from foreign partners or investors are processed via SWIFT international wire transfers to the LP's Canadian dollar account at a Tier 1 bank. Required purpose codes include 'EQTY' for equity contributions or 'LOAN' for intercompany loans. Verification protocols mandate supporting documentation such as subscription agreements, capital contribution notices, and CRA Form T1134 for reporting foreign property holdings. All transfers are subject to FINTRAC suspicious transaction reporting and beneficial ownership verification.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements are facilitated through SWIFT MT103 messages for wire transfers or issuance of Commercial Letters of Credit via correspondent banks. For jewelry and fine timepieces, payments to suppliers are routed in CAD or freely convertible currencies, supported by underlying commercial invoices, purchase orders, and delivery confirmations.
