Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Canada or services performed outside Canada for non-resident clients qualify for zero-rated GST treatment. Eligibility requires verifiable proof of export or performance location.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There is no exchange-control licensing, central-bank pre-approval, or quantitative restriction on conversion of CAD into USD, EUR, or other major trading currencies. The practical hurdles for an Alberta Limited Partnership distributing to non-resident partners are tax-engineered rather than FX-engineered: (i) Part XIII withholding tax at 25% statutory rate, reduced by tax treaty, applied to amounts paid or credited to non-resident limited partners by reason of Income Tax Act subsection 212(20); (ii) requirement to file an NR6 withholding plan or a section 216 return for non-resident partners expecting Canadian-source allocations; (iii) creditor-protection/solvency constraints on distributions under the Alberta Partnership Act; and (iv) Form T1135 reporting by partners owning specified foreign property above the CAD 100,000 cost threshold. No branch-profit tax applies because the LP is fiscally transparent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada does not require statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation of export or e-commerce receivables within any timeline. The Alberta LP may hold USD, EUR, and other foreign-currency balances in domestic multi-currency accounts at Schedule I banks or at FINTRAC-registered MSBs. Compliance duties are structural: (a) FINTRAC reporting of incoming and outgoing EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more, in each case where the institution is aware of the amounts; (b) Form T1135 reporting by partners whose specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000; (c) recognition of gains and losses on foreign receivables using Bank of Canada published exchange rates for income-tax conversion; and (d) arm's-length transfer-pricing documentation where cross-border related-party MRO procurement is involved.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the monetary authority and final lender of last resort, while formal exchange-rate policy responsibility rests with the Minister of Finance. OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) supervises the clearing institutions and deposit-taking banks; FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administers cross-border electronic-fund-transfer reporting and AML/ATF obligations; Payments Canada operates the Lynx RTGS high-value settlement system for Canadian-dollar payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary payout/collection rail; Low-Medium as a primary treasury depository. Canada does not issue European-style electronic-money institution licences. An offshore EMI cannot accept demand deposits or conduct deposit-taking business unless authorized under the Bank Act; it may, however, operate as a Money Services Business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC and, if providing retail payment services to Canadian users, as a Payment Service Provider (PSP) registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Multinational platforms (Wise Platform, Airwallex, OFX) are legally viable for B2B MRO supplier payouts, but funds held with them are not CDIC-insured and such platforms cannot serve as the capital escrow, withholding, or statutory settlement account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% (15% federal + 8% Alberta provincial corporate reference rate; the Alberta Limited Partnership is generally a flow-through entity, so this rate applies to corporate partners rather than to the LP itself)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII) for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders. Alberta LPs do not pay dividends; a non-resident limited partner's allocated profit is treated as Canadian-source business income and taxed under Part I unless relief is available under an income tax treaty. Distributions representing the return of capital are not subject to Part XIII withholding.

The T5013 slip must show each non-resident partner's share and, if applicable, cite the treaty article and related Form NR301 filed with the partnership or CRA. Late or incomplete T5013 filing: C$25 per day per partner up to 100 days, with a minimum of C$100 and a maximum of C$5,000. Gross negligence penalties can reach 50% of understated tax, plus back interest and further penalties for omitted Canadian-source income; repeated failure can also result in criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1 million per non-arm's-length non-resident counterparty for contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File/TP Memo); C$750 million consolidated group revenue triggers mandatory Master File and Country-by-Country Report (Form RC4646) filing for the ultimate parent or Canadian constituent entity.

Economic substance scope: There is no standalone Canadian ESR regime, but the GAAR (s.245), foreign-affiliate anti-avoidance rules (s.212.3 and s.18(5)), and transfer-pricing rules (s.247) require real economic substance. An Alberta LP that operates solely as a paper conduit for industrial MRO e-commerce revenue, books high-margin purchases from related foreign suppliers, and lacks employees, premises, decision-making, commercial risk, and operating substance will be attacked on a look-through basis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False or Incomplete Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under s.163(2) of the Income Tax Act equal to 50% of the understated tax plus arrears interest; criminal conviction under s.239(1) Income Tax Act and s.380 Criminal Code, exposing the offender to fines up to 200% of the amount evaded and imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: For a limited partnership, the general partner is personally liable. Directors and officers of the general partner can be jointly and severally assessed for unpaid payroll source deductions, GST/HST, or Part XIII amounts under ss.227.1 and 160; they may also face travel restraints after criminal charges are laid and can be stripped of limited-liability protections on fraud or knowing participation in false filing.
Transfer Pricing - Non-Arm's-Length Mislabelling

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under s.247(2); penalty under s.247(3) of up to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where no contemporaneous documentation exists, escalating to 25% for gross negligence; interest is charged on all reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are not automatically liable for transfer-pricing penalties, but they become personally liable if they directed or approved the pricing, signed false declarations, or transferred assets to non-arm's-length persons after the liability arose (s.160). Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the general partner is a self-dealing shell.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abusive Structure

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to deny the tax benefit, reassess on a look-through basis for up to 10 years, and impose a 25% penalty (50% in fraud cases) on the tax advantage; the LP may be denied treaty relief and deemed to have a foreign head office if it lacks central management and control in Alberta.

Director personal liability: General partners remain jointly and severally liable for all LP debts, including tax liabilities; directors of the corporate general partner may be exposed to personal liability where the general partner asset is stripped, or where they knowingly execute shams, and may face civil restitution orders and international travel bans pending criminal fraud claims.
AML Compliance - Money Laundering/Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: up to C$500,000 for individuals and C$2 million for entities per violation; criminal money-laundering under s.462.31 Criminal Code carries up to ten years’ imprisonment and asset-forfeiture orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for AML penalties if they authorized, acquiesced in, or failed to take reasonable measures to prevent the violation; the general partner’s directors can be disqualified or removed, and a limited partner who participates in AML misconduct loses limited-liability status to the extent of active involvement.
