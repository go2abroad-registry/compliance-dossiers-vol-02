Applicable AML statute: Primary federal AML/CFT legislation is the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, SC 2000, c 17, together with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Regulations, SOR/2002-184. FINTRAC guidance requires applicable reporting entities to identify and verify beneficial ownership, including ownership or control of 25 percent or more, and to determine controlling persons. Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

An Alberta limited partnership must maintain an Alberta registered office and the prescribed records and service arrangements required by its registration and partnership documentation. A commercial registered-office or virtual-office provider may generally be used if it provides a real Alberta address, accepts service of process, maintains reliable records, and satisfies the registry agent, bank, payment processor, tax authority, and AML/KYC requirements. A mailbox-only, accommodation-only, or undisclosed nominee address is unsuitable. For online retail of hydroponics and gardening equipment, a virtual registered office is generally permissible at the formation stage, but it does not establish operational substance, inventory presence, employees, management, tax residence, or eligibility for banking and payment services. If goods are stored, imported, fulfilled, or returned in Alberta, the partnership should maintain accurate warehouse, fulfillment, customs, books-and-records, and customer-service information.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Portal via https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-number.html; Alberta partnership registration administered by the Alberta Corporate Registry (https://www.albertaregistries.ca/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta Limited Partnership must: (1) register as a limited partnership under the Alberta Partnership Act through the Alberta Corporate Registry (obtaining a provincial registration number and filing a Declaration of Partnership / Statement of Particulars with General Partner details); (2) obtain a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' via CRA My Business Account, or by filing a GST/HST registration application (Form RC460 for a non-resident registrant); (3) complete GST/HST account activation effective the date the LP ceases to be a small supplier or chooses voluntary registration; and (4) if it has any Canadian-resident or Canadian-source income, file annually T5013 Partnership Information Return and withhold on non-resident partners' shares under ITA Part XIII unless treaty-reduced. The BN is the sole statutory TIN anchor for GST/HST remittances and corporate registry cross-referencing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — under Canadian domestic law an Alberta Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes by default (ITA s. 96): the partnership is not a taxpayer; income, loss and tax credits flow through to General and Limited Partners proportionately. The LP may nonetheless be subject to Alberta provincial rules on deemed non-resident partnership registrations, and a Canadian partnership that is wholly owned by US members may be treated as a corporation under the Canada-US Treaty if it issued preferred stock or otherwise lacks partnership indicia, but no election is required to be pass-through in the ordinary cross-border garden-equipment e-commerce structure.

Available election forms

Canada does not administer a US-style 'check-the-box' regime; CRA determines entity status under domestic jurisprudence. Statutory elections relevant to this LP include: (a) an 'electing Canadian partnership' election under ITA s. 96(8.1) to be taxed as a corporation — available with unanimous partner consent, rarely used and requiring prior CRA consent; (b) hybrid mismatch election under ITA s. 18.4–18.6 (BEPS Action 2) for allocations subject to US check-the-box arrangements, enabling certain import-timing and dual-consistency adjustments; and (c) GST/HST partnership election under ETA s. 273 — allowing one partner to act as the registrant representative and to collect/remit GST on behalf of the partnership, which is mandatory for a non-resident LP using a Canadian agent General Partner.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1)(a): a non-resident e-commerce seller (including an Alberta LP selling online garden hydroponics equipment) becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant when the cumulative worldwide consideration from taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) by the LP and its associated persons exceeds CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. For non-resident platform sellers in respect of Canadian delivery addresses, the threshold is computed inclusive of supplies facilitated through electronic platforms, and registration is mandatory before the first sale if the person has a reasonable expectation of exceeding the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods sold by the Alberta LP to a non-resident customer for delivery outside Canada are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V, s. 6, provided the goods are exported 'as commercial goods' or are purchased for export by the non-resident and exported by the vendor within 60 days of delivery. Services (including online configuration, remote horticulture consulting, or logistics support provided from Alberta to overseas buyers) are zero-rated under s. 7 if the service is supplied to a non-resident and 'in respect of' neither real property in Canada nor tangible personal property in Canada at the time of performance; for intangibles supplied by way of license (e.g., online instruction manuals), Part IV s. 2 zero-rating applies if the intellectual property is for use exclusively outside Canada. The LP must hold contemporaneous evidence that title passed outside Canada and that the buyer is a non-resident at the time payment is received.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A export declaration, CADEX/ECI record or electronic export reporting for low-value postal/parcel shipments)
  • Commercial Invoice / Airway Bill or Bill of Lading identifying the Alberta LP as shipper and the non-resident as consignee
  • Carrier proof of export (FedEx/DHL/UPS or Canada Post export confirmation, foreign customs stamp, or terminal receipt showing arrival at an overseas port)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank wire record (SWIFT MT103 or acquirer settlement statement in foreign currency) evidencing payment of the invoiced export sale
  • Zero-rated export declaration and GST/HST return (Form GST/HST Return or Form GST189 for non-resident refund claims, as applicable)

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control regime for standard commercial invoices. CRA adopted the PEPPOL e-invoicing framework for federal procurement (mandatory for B2G suppliers as of 2023), and CRA accepts electronic records provided they are legible, authoritative and digitally verifiable. For e-commerce LPs selling via Stripe/PayPal, CRA requires the following: (a) issuance of electronic receipts containing GST registration number, supplier name, sale date, tax rate and amount; (b) preservation of audit-ready system logs in an accessible digital format for six years; (c) compliance with the mandatory platform reporting regime for marketplace operators (TSC1101/TSC1102 information returns for high-value sellers); and (d) no requirement for sequenced fiscal stamps, but CRA may impose penalties under ETA s. 281.1 for failure to keep billing records in an electronically acceptable format.

CRA administers an 'export substantiation' risk-flag program for e-commerce merchants; insufficient evidence of export within a typical 180-day commercial settlement window results in the zero-rating being denied and reassessment of GST at 5% on the full invoice value, plus arrears interest computed from the original remittance due date. Specific penalties: (a) ETA s. 280(1) failure to keep books and records — up to CAD 4,000 per occurrence; (b) ITA s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty — 50% of the net understated tax or overstated ITC; (c) ETA s. 298 evading payment or obtaining a rebate by false pretence — fine up to CAD 25,000 and/or 12 months imprisonment; (d) retroactive clawback period of four years for ordinary reassessment, extended to an unlimited period in the case of misrepresentation or fraud (ETA ss. 298-298.1, ITA s. 152(4)); CRA also publishes designated 'non-credible export' criteria and will disallow ITCs attributed to exports without matching FX inflows, and may order payment forfeiture under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for deliberately fraudulent export invoicing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital accounts under the Currency Act; there is no general exchange-control authorization requirement for cross-border remittances, capital subscriptions, or profit repatriation. Profit remittances from a Canadian limited partnership to non-resident partners are governed by Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, generally imposing a 25% withholding tax absent treaty relief (commonly reduced to 5%–15% under the Canada-US and other tax treaties). Repatriation must be supported by audited or internally reviewed partnership capital accounts, milestone-based profit allocations, and transfer pricing documentation where cross-border related-party services or goods are involved. Non-resident limited partners may contribute capital freely, but the partnership should file appropriate Canada Revenue Agency reports, including the T5013 Schedule 50 for partner allocations and Foreign Income Verification Statement T1135 if foreign property thresholds apply. No currency blockage, repatriation consent, or central-bank conversion approval exists under current Canadian federal law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; exporters may retain and use foreign currency freely. A Canadian-resident exporter, including a limited partnership carrying on active business, must nevertheless monitor cross-border electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, as the bank or money services business acting as the reporting entity is required to report such movements to FINTRAC. Foreign-currency bank accounts held outside Canada aggregating over CAD 100,000 in tax cost must be reported by partners on Form T1135. Export declarations remain applicable under the Canadian Export Reporting Program for controlled or regulated goods, but garden hydroponics equipment generally falls outside export licensing requirements unless destined for comprehensive sanctions targets. The absence of surrender obligations permits the LP to time conversions strategically and to hold USD or other currencies in correspondent accounts coordinated with the clearing bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium for offshore digital EMIs and non-Canadian fintech payment platforms. Canadian law does not issue a separate EMI licence comparable to the EU E-Money Directive; any entity engaged in money transfers, remittance services, or payment facilitation may be treated as a Money Services Business and must register with FINTRAC as well as with provincial regulators where applicable. Offshore EMIs cannot serve as the primary deposit-taking or escrow bank for the Canadian LP without Canadian authorisation and may create unlicensed deposit-taking risks. Stripe and PayPal are acceptable as downstream collection gateways and payout rails, but treasury control should be centralised through a Schedule I Canadian bank holding the capital escrow and operational accounts. The bank should be positioned as the sole clearing counterparty for foreign exchange and wire settlements; offshore fintech rails may be used only for customer collections, not for operational liquidity custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident capital contributions should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the investing partner's own bank account to the Canadian LP's capital escrow account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The wire must include the limited-partner name, the partnership name, and a purpose reference using the bank's acceptable code for equity financing or 'capital contribution to Canadian limited partnership.'
