Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) acting as the central tax administration; online portals include CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account. Provincial entity registration for an Alberta Limited Partnership is administered by the Alberta Corporate Registry. Alberta does not impose a provincial sales tax or separate VAT; corporate income tax for Alberta is collected by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

The Alberta LP must first be registered or extra-provincially registered with the Alberta Corporate Registry. For tax identity, the LP's general partner or authorized representative files Form RC1 Request for a Business Number with the CRA electronically via Business Registration Online or by mail/fax. The application requires the LP legal name, Alberta registration number, principal business activity (NAICS 493110), names and addresses of all partners, and a request for a GST/HST program account. Non-resident partners should supply their foreign tax identification numbers; if there is no Canadian-resident general partner, a resident agent for service must be designated. The CRA assigns a nine-digit Business Number and separate program accounts (e.g., RM, RP, RT, RC) with appropriate effective dates.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. An Alberta LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act; income is computed at partnership level and allocated to partners. Available elections include a partnership non-calendar fiscal period election, a section 216 election for non-resident partners, and a joint election for nil consideration on supplies between closely related registrants for GST/HST purposes. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.2 and 18.3 impose deduction limitations and do not offer elective relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small supplier threshold computed on worldwide revenue from taxable supplies (other than zero-rated supplies) made in Canada over the preceding four calendar quarters; registration is compulsory once exceeded, and voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated when the goods are exported by the seller in compliance with ETA Schedule VI, Part V, Section 1 and CRA proof-of-export rules. Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated under Section 9/10 only when the service is not performed in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. Therefore warehousing and fulfillment services performed on inventory physically located in Canada are generally taxable at 5% GST even when the recipient is a non-resident; a separately contracted international freight forwarding leg may be zero-rated if structured and documented as a distinct supply of transportation from Canada to a foreign destination.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration or approved electronic export report for goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill showing the foreign destination
  • Proof of export retained in records, including carrier evidence and customs release documentation

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous electronic authentication of GST/HST invoices. Registrants must issue paper or electronic invoices containing prescribed particulars, including the Business Number, date, recipient identity, description and tax amount, and must retain records for six years. Exports require CBSA electronic export declarations via Canadian Government EDI or the appropriate CBSA portal; no digital stamp is applied.

CRA will deny zero-rating and reassess the supply at 5% GST plus interest and penalties where documentary export proof is insufficient or not obtained within the applicable prescribed time. Civil penalties include a false-statement penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and a percentage of the misstatement (often up to 25%), gross-negligence penalties, and potential criminal prosecution for deliberate evasion. Input tax credits connected to Canadian warehouse expenses can be retrospectively disallowed and recovered, with a general four-year reassessment period extended to six years for neglect, carelessness, or wilful default.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current- and capital-account convertibility as an Article VIII member of the IMF; there is no licensing, approval, or quota system governing capital injections, profit remittances, or foreign-currency account holding. The CAD trades under a free float. The only compliance frictions for an Alberta Limited Partnership are: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding on distribution of Canadian-source income allocations to non-resident partners (25% statutory, treaty-reducible), which affects repatriation structuring rather than convertibility; (ii) CDD/UBO disclosure at account opening; (iii) mandatory FINTRAC reporting of cross-border electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iv) Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting via the servicing bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds; exporters may retain, invoice, and disburse in any currency. Statutory obligations are confined to transparency: (i) CBSA export reporting via B13A/CAED for commercial shipments exceeding the low-value threshold (approximately CAD 2,000 per shipment), due within 15 calendar days after the month of export, with e-commerce low-value parcels generally exempt; (ii) Statistics Canada balance-of-payments returns for goods/services flows through the servicing bank (threshold-based monthly surveys); and (iii) FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting for any transfer of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the sole issuing and monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of Schedule I banks under the Bank Act; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/CFT supervisory authority under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada operates no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration; residual authority derives from the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, the Bank Act, and PCMLTFA reporting obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High viability for operational last-mile disbursement: Airwallex, Wise Business, and Payoneer operate in Canada as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses under the PCMLTFA, supporting US ACH, UK FPS, EU SEPA, and China local e-payment rails with favorable FX spreads and rapid settlement. Medium-to-Low viability for capital escrow and high-value clearing: non-bank EMIs lack OSFI prudential supervision and CDIC deposit insurance, and Schedule I banks will not treat EMI references as sufficient source-of-funds evidence. Recommended architecture: Schedule I chartered bank as the liquidity anchor for capital, clearing, and derivatives; fintech EMI overlay restricted to documented settlement-friction reduction with an audited payment-flow matrix.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate rate (15% federal + 8% Alberta provincial) applicable to corporate partners of the LP; reduced combined rate of 11% (9% federal small business + 2% Alberta small business) applies to Canadian-controlled private corporation partners on active business income up to CAD 500,000. The Alberta Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent; the statutory rate applies at the partner level under the Income Tax Act (Canada).
