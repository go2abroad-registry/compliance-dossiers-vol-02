VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (exceeded in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters of taxable supplies); non-resident partnerships carrying on business in Canada must register once the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI Part V when the supplier has documentary evidence the goods were exported as part of the supply. Cold storage/handling services supplied to a non-resident in respect of goods destined for export can qualify as zero-rated where the goods are not consumed in Canada; services in respect of goods to be delivered or consumed in Canada remain taxable at 5%. Registration and contemporaneous written evidence of the non-resident recipient are required.

  • CBSA Export Declaration filed via the Canada Export Reporting System showing departure from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list describing the exported goods
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air/rail/road) or equivalent transport document naming the shipper and overseas consignee
  • Inward remittance evidence, e.g., SWIFT MT103 credit advice confirming settlement of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or tax-authority API clearance mandate in Canada. CRA accepts electronic records and requires invoices in a readable format under ETA s. 223. Federal B2G e-invoicing follows the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS 3.0 framework; B2B e-invoicing is optional. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via CRA NETFILE or EDI/API.

CRA may reassess within four years (ten years for misrepresentation or wilful default) to claw back zero-rated output tax and related input tax credits where export evidence is deficient. Civil penalties under ETA s. 285.1 and ITA s. 163(2) may reach 50% of net understated tax; criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 can impose fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment. Failed export substantiation causes full reassessment with arrears interest and denial of refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible. The Canadian dollar is free on both current and capital account; Canada repealed its Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951 and imposes no licensing, approval, or surrender requirement on capital inflows, profit remittance, or repatriation. Remittance friction is Tax-driven rather than FX-driven: Part XIII non-resident withholding (statutory 25%, treaty-reducible under the Canada-US treaty), Part I reporting for non-resident partners of the Limited Partnership (T5013 and NR4 filings), and thin-capitalization/interest-stripping limits under sections 18(4)-18(7) and 212(3) of the Income Tax Act (Canada). For a pass-through Alberta LP, the general partner must ensure non-resident partners' treaty entitlements are documented via NR301/NR302 filings to prevent excess withholding on cash distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no obligation to maintain export proceeds in CAD. Practical compliance is limited to FINTRAC's reporting obligation for international electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more within a 24-hour period. For cold-chain pharmaceutical storage revenue, exporting banks require underlying commercial invoices, transport bills of lading, and temperature-monitoring records to classify inbound proceeds as legitimate export receivables rather than crypto-adjacent or controlled-substance flows.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), payments-system administration via Payments Canada (Lynx and ACSS), and AML/CTF supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority analogous to a SAFE-style regulator; residual policy authority rests with the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act and the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms can provide multi-currency wallets and wire-style settlement, but two statutory constraints limit use as the primary treasury rail: (i) any foreign EMI operating for a Canadian merchant must be registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and Alberta's provincial money-services licensing regime imposes bonding and compliance duties; (ii) a refrigerated pharmaceutical warehouse requires credit-clearing, documentary collection, and possibly L/C facilities that EMI services do not offer. Viability rating: Medium as a supplemental collections rail, Low as the sole banking relationship. The entity should open a Schedule I bank account first, then layer an EMI only for cross-border collection in USD/EUR subject to FINTRAC registration and dual KYC approval.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23.0% combined (15% federal + 8% Alberta general corporate rate) at the corporate partner level; 0% at the Limited Partnership level, which is fiscally transparent.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on Canadian-source dividend and income distributions to non-residents. For an Alberta LP, distributions are not legal dividends; nevertheless, the partner's allocated Canadian-source income is subject to 25% withholding under Part XIII unless reduced by an applicable DTA. Alberta itself imposes no additional provincial withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by the last day of the fifth month after the partnership's fiscal period-end; for a December 31 fiscal period end, the return is due May 31 of the following calendar year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Six months after the corporate partner's taxation year-end; for a December 31 year-end, the return is due June 30 of the following year. Quarterly instalments are due when taxable income exceeds CAD 3,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50 million gross revenue or CAD 1 million controlled transaction threshold is the commonly applied CRA contemporaneous-documentation trigger; however, no minimum transaction amount exempts a partnership from maintaining documentation if a transaction is non-arm's-length. A country-by-country report is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding CAD 750 million. The Alberta LP should maintain a Local File for warehousing/sub-lease transactions and a Master File if it is part of a multinational group; the general partner must document the intercompany cold-storage fee methodology before filing the return.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; instead CRA uses the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), the substance-over-form doctrine, and transfer-pricing recharacterization under ITA s.247(1)/(2) to disregard artificial legal steps lacking economic substance. For an Alberta LP, maintaining real refrigerated premises, operating employees, utility records, insurance, and physical inventory control is essential; a limited partner who participates in management control loses the limited-liability shield under the Alberta Limited Partnership Act, while a general partner remains jointly and severally liable.
