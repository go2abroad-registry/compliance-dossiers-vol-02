Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes: income, losses, deductions, and credits are computed at partnership level and allocated to partners under the Income Tax Act. There is no Canadian equivalent to the United States check-the-box election for converting a Canadian limited partnership into a corporation or disregarded entity. The partnership may elect or designate specific statutory treatments where available, including applicable GST/HST filing-period elections, fiscal-period and accounting-method choices, and partnership-level tax elections prescribed by the Income Tax Act or Excise Tax Act. A Canadian partnership may also be classified differently under foreign tax law, including by a United States entity-classification election, but that foreign election does not change its Canadian legal or Canadian income-tax classification. Hybrid-mismatch analysis is required where payments, losses, or deductions are claimed in multiple jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, excluding supplies of financial services and certain other excluded amounts, measured under the small-supplier rules over the applicable preceding and current calendar-quarter periods. The threshold applies to the person and associated persons under the statutory aggregation rules. A non-resident may be required to register without relying on the threshold where it carries on business in Canada and makes taxable supplies in Canada. Voluntary registration is permitted in qualifying circumstances.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods may generally be zero-rated at 0% GST when they are exported from Canada in circumstances meeting Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, including shipment or delivery outside Canada and retention of objective evidence establishing exportation. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated under the exported-services rules where the statutory recipient, use, benefit, and service-type conditions are satisfied. Recruitment, placement, crewing, and vessel-management services require transaction-specific analysis because zero-rating may be denied where the service is performed in relation to Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, litigation, personal services performed in Canada, or another statutory exclusion. The supplier must verify the customer's non-resident status, the contractual recipient, the location and nature of the services, the place of performance, the vessel and crew arrangements, and whether the service benefits a person in Canada. Zero-rating does not eliminate Canadian income-tax, payroll, customs, employment, or regulatory obligations.

  • Commercial invoice and contract or purchase order identifying the non-resident customer, goods or services, consideration, and delivery terms
  • CBSA export declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) where an export declaration is required
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, cargo manifest, or equivalent carrier evidence for exported goods
  • Proof of delivery or receipt outside Canada and carrier, freight-forwarder, or customs-broker records
  • Accounting records, customer correspondence, service reports, crew-placement records, vessel documentation, and evidence supporting the non-resident recipient and offshore benefit for exported services
  • Bank statements, remittance records, and payment documentation linking the consideration to the exported supply; Canada does not generally require an Inward Remittance Certificate as a statutory export zero-rating document

Canada does not generally require real-time GST invoice clearance, mandatory digital tax stamping, or continuous transaction controls for ordinary domestic invoices. GST registrants must issue prescribed invoices or other documentary evidence sufficient to support tax collection, input tax credits, rebates, and audit verification. Electronic invoices and records are generally acceptable if they preserve reliability, readability, accessibility, and audit integrity. GST/HST returns and remittances may be filed electronically through CRA systems when required or elected. Commercial export declarations are filed through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), subject to applicable export-reporting requirements. Payroll, corporate, partnership, and other electronic filing obligations may apply independently of invoicing.

Canada has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no universal 180-day repatriation rule for GST zero-rating. CRA may reassess GST where zero-rating is unsupported, including by assessing uncollected GST, denying input tax credits or rebates, and charging arrears interest from the applicable statutory date. False statements or omissions may attract administrative penalties, including gross-negligence penalties generally equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit or refund. Repeated or fraudulent conduct may trigger additional penalties, reassessments, record-keeping consequences, director or partnership liability exposure where applicable, and criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act. Exporters should retain documentary evidence for the applicable statutory retention period, generally at least six years from the end of the relevant year, subject to extended periods for unresolved objections, appeals, or CRA requirements.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, tax rules, court orders, and financial-institution compliance review. No general prior approval is required to contribute capital to, lend to, or repatriate funds from an Alberta limited partnership. Banks may require evidence of source of funds, the partnership’s legal authority, beneficial ownership, transaction purpose, and underlying contracts. Profit allocations and distributions remain subject to applicable Canadian and foreign tax rules; withholding and reporting obligations can arise for non-residents depending on the payment’s character and the partners’ circumstances. A partnership agreement and reliable allocation records should support distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory requirement to repatriate or surrender export proceeds and imposes no mandatory conversion ratio or general statutory conversion deadline. Maritime crewing and vessel-management service receipts may be received and retained in foreign currency, subject to contract terms, bank controls, sanctions, tax reporting, and any applicable customer or financing restrictions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Canada’s central bank). Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control authority imposing routine controls on foreign-currency conversion or cross-border capital transfers. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers Canada’s anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing reporting regime; it is not an exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payments and foreign-exchange services; low as a substitute for a Canadian business deposit account where the provider is an offshore electronic-money institution. Canada does not grant an offshore EMI automatic authority to take deposits or provide regulated payment services in Canada. Verify the provider’s Canadian legal status, applicable Bank of Canada registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act where relevant, safeguarding and insolvency arrangements, supported currencies, settlement locations, and correspondent-bank access. Use a Canadian financial institution for core operating liquidity and payroll funding; do not treat a payment account as a Canadian bank deposit or assume deposit insurance.
