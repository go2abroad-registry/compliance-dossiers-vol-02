Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO); provincial registration touchpoint for Alberta is the Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta), but tax registration remains federal via the CRA (GST/HST NETFILE and Canadian Business Registry).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident limited partnership intending to supply marine bunkering in Canada, the foreign entity must first register with the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) and open a GST/HST program account (RM0001). The application is made through CRA's Business Registration Online, in writing (Form RC348 for non-resident accounts, as applicable), or by phone. Required supporting information includes the LP's partnership agreement, foreign registration particulars, Canadian agent for service of notices, proof of identity for partners with 10% or greater interest, and a description of Canadian commercial activities. If the partnership's place of business, ownership, or activity changes, CRA must be notified; the BN is the sole tax identification number used for GST/HST, income tax, customs, and payroll in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; an Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes: the limited partnership is not a taxpayer, and each partner is separately liable to tax on its allocable share of income. For GST/HST, however, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and is required to account for tax on its own GST/HST accounts where it makes taxable supplies in Canada. The LP is not subject to Alberta provincial corporate income tax directly; Alberta corporate income tax applies only to corporations.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under the Income Tax Act; partnership transparency is mandatory and may not be affirmatively selected. For GST/HST, a non-resident partnership may voluntarily elect to be registered as a non-resident and file using a branch or non-resident GST/HST account. Hybrid mismatch elections are constrained by Canadian anti-hybrid rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4, 18.5, 20(1)(ccc)) which can deny deductions for payments described in deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; limited partnerships may be materially affected where a non-resident partner's home jurisdiction treats a payment as deductible while Canada does not include a corresponding positive income amount.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — a person (including a non-resident partnership) making taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, is registrable if such supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any calendar quarter or in the four preceding calendar quarters. For a non-resident, the nexus is determined by taxable supplies made in Canada; supplying bunker fuel to ships in Canadian territory generally triggers the obligation if the threshold is met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to property exported from Canada and to supplies made in Canada that are part of an export or international freight transaction. For marine bunkering, a supply of fuel and lubricants to a ship engaged in international commercial shipping will be zero-rated where the ship departs to a foreign destination and the supplier retains evidence that the goods are actually consumed on a voyage to a destination outside Canada. Supplies for domestic voyages or coasting trade remain subject to the 5% GST. Zero-rating can also apply to services supplied to a non-resident if the service is for the ship's operation and the ship is not at the time a commercial ship based in Canada. Documentary proof must support the vessel's international voyage status, the physical delivery of bunker fuel to the vessel, and the vessel's departure from Canada.

  • Customs manuscript or electronic export declaration filed with the Canada Border Services Agency at point of export
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic) evidencing carriage to a foreign port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer record proving actual payment from abroad
  • Bunkering delivery receipt or supplier's loading report showing vessel name, port of delivery, quantity, and international voyage identification

Canada does not mandate a real-time tax clearance or fiscal digital stamping regime. GST/HST returns are filed periodically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE (electronic). E-invoicing (e.g., PEPPOL or other structured formats) is voluntary; where used, it must still comply with CRA's invoicing content requirements. The CRA does not operate an API-based real-time clearance or digital pre-approval mechanism for sales invoices. Tax invoices for supplies exceeding CAD 30 must include supplier name and registration number, date, brief description, tax charged, and the customer's name or business name; simplified invoices may be used below that amount.

CRA may deny zero-rating on a retrospective basis where documentary evidence is incomplete or contradictory; a reassessment can impute tax on the full consideration plus interest. Under the Excise Tax Act, false statements and wilful evasion attract penalties from 25% to 200% of the tax sought, plus interest; fraud or conspiracy criminalizes repeat offences. Additionally, the CRA may claw back related input tax credits (including fuel input credits) after a zero-rating denial, and the Income Tax Act's gross negligence penalty may apply where export documents are fabricated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account items under the Currency Act and the Canadian foreign exchange framework. There are no exchange controls, no central bank approval requirements for profit remittances, and no repatriation licence. An Alberta Limited Partnership may freely distribute earnings in CAD or foreign currency to non-resident limited partners; all profits are remitted after accounting for Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, typically 25% subject to treaty reduction. No FX authority approval or post-transaction conversion certificate is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender regime. The Alberta LP may retain bunkering and lubricant supply receipts in the original invoiced currency or convert them freely at commercial banks. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory timeframe for repatriating export proceeds. Only reporting obligations exist under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for incoming or outgoing international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and the Customs Act may require declaration of monetary instruments in physical transit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; foreign exchange administration is not vested in a separate sovereign body, with OSFI regulating FX market participants and FINTRAC enforcing cross-border payment reporting and AML obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech/EMI viability is Low as a primary treasury rail for this Alberta LP. A bank or payment service provider storing funds, issuing payment instruments, or providing account-bound clearing must comply with the Bank Act or the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), and registration with the Bank of Canada is expected. Offshore EMIs cannot issue standby or commercial letters of credit, are not acceptable as settlement infrastructure for Canadian bunkering trade counterparties, and generally do not satisfy Alberta legal accounting requirements. They can serve only as an ancillary collections channel if registered under RPAA or acting through a Canadian Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident limited partners must route by SWIFT MT103 or equivalent wire to the Alberta LP's designated operational or escrow account at a tier-1 Canadian bank. The beneficiary account title must match the registered LP name, and the wire narrative should state 'Limited Partner Capital Contribution' and reference the LP name and investment identification number. Bank compliance will require the capital commitment schedule and underlying LP agreement before accepting the wires. There is no Canadian FX approval requirement, but the bank may require a balance-of-payments notification under Statistics Canada reporting using the Canadian-developed classification code for direct investment equity. All incoming international wires above CAD 10,000 trigger FINTRAC international electronic funds transfer reporting by the receiving Canadian bank.Operational marine fuel payments and lubricant supply disbursements should be executed by SWIFT MT103 over the Bank of Canada Large Value Transfer System for CAD-denominated settlement or over CHIPS/SWIFT for USD. For bunker stem purchase commitments, commercial documentary letters of credit are the preferred rail, with the Alberta bank as issuing bank and the foreign fuel supplier as beneficiary against presentation of commercial invoice, bill of lading or bunker receipt, and inspection certificate. Standby letters of credit are used to secure performance obligations to port authorities, vessel owners, and long-term fuel suppliers; they are issued as clean undertakings payable on first demand.
