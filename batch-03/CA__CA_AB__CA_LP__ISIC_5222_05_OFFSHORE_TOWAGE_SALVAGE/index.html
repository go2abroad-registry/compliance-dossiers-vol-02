Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - exclusive federal administrator of income tax, Goods and Services Tax (GST), payroll remittances, and the Excise Tax Act; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Alberta Tax Collection Agreement. Alberta Limited Partnership registration is effected through Service Alberta (Alberta Corporate Registry) under the Partnership Act. CRA business registration portal: Business Registration Online and My Business Account.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Alberta Limited Partnership (LP) with Service Alberta to obtain the provincial registration number; (2) apply to the CRA for the statutory Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' - paper filing is mandatory where there is no Canadian-resident general partner; (3) activate the GST/HST account on the same BN with the Alberta 5% GST rate code; (4) activate a CRA payroll (RP) account where Canadian marine crew are employed; (5) file the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually; and (6) ensure non-resident partner compliance - non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on their allocable share of Canadian-source business income and must file a Canadian T1/T2 return or assert treaty relief where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the Alberta LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act: income, gains, losses, and tax credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners under ITA s. 96. The LP itself pays no federal or Alberta entity-level income tax, and Alberta imposes no provincial capital tax on LPs. A non-resident partner is deemed to carry on business in Canada where the LP carries on business in Canada, rendering such partner taxable in Canada on its allocable Canadian-source income.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election: a limited partnership is mandatorily classified as a pass-through 'Canadian partnership' under ITA s. 102, and no elective entity-classification regime exists at the federal or Alberta level. The applicable statutory election and anti-hybrid mechanisms are: (a) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.16-18.19, which automatically disallow deductions for payments to hybrid entities or through hybrid branches and do not provide a benign elective override; (b) a narrow partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 96(3)/(8.4); and (c) the Canada-US Treaty 'fiscally transparent entity' provisions where a US interposed entity is used, supported by CRA advance income tax rulings or Form NR301 treaty-based withholding relief for Part XIII amounts.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 240(1), measured over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters on worldwide taxable supplies including zero-rated supplies. A non-resident LP crossing this threshold while carrying on commercial activity in Canada must register for GST/HST; voluntary registration is available to recover input tax credits on Canadian fuel, berthing, repairs, and logistics inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 of the ETA, a supply of a service to a non-resident is zero-rated where the service relates to real property situated outside Canada, goods situated outside Canada at the time the service is performed, or is performed wholly outside Canada. Deep-sea towage and salvage services therefore qualify for 0% GST where the towed or salved vessel is outside Canadian waters and the recipient (shipowner, protection and indemnity club, insurer, or non-resident operator) is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST. Where the service is performed on a vessel situated in Canadian waters or supplied to a Canadian-resident recipient, the supply is taxable at 5%. The LP must retain evidence of the recipient's non-resident status (written statement, registration documents, or treaty certificate), the offshore situs of performance (GPS/AIS voyage logs and towage/salvage contract), and the commercial settlement flow.

  • Customs Export Declaration - B13A Export Declaration and ACI eManifest in the CBSA Single Window Initiative where Canadian-origin marine equipment or consumables are laden on the tug in the same operation, evidencing the export movement of any controlled goods from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill - transport document together with the towage/salvage contract, Lloyds Open Form, and AIS/GPS track records proving that the towage route and salvage situs remain outside Canada and that the service recipient is situated abroad
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank statement or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of the export consideration from the non-resident customer into the LP's Canadian bank account within the prescribed repatriation window

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government API pre-clearance, or digital stamping of invoices. GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed tax invoices under ETA ss. 221-223 and CRA GST/HST Memorandum 18-1. Electronic GST/HST return filing is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million, and the CRA supports voluntary PEPPOL-based e-invoicing under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing initiative. No statutory B2B e-invoicing or fiscal clearance obligation currently applies.

The CRA treats export zero-rating as a high-risk area: on audit, failure to substantiate non-resident status, offshore performance, or receipt of funds results in reassessment of the supply at 5% GST, denial of input tax credits on Canadian inputs, and compounding interest under ETA s. 280. Ordinary reassessments may be issued within four years, extending to seven years in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud (ETA s. 298). Civil penalties include the ETA s. 285 false-statement penalty (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax sought to be suppressed) and the s. 285.1 gross-negligence penalty of up to 50% of the tax, plus criminal exposure under the ETA offence provisions for intentional evasion. The CRA's specialized GST/HST export verification program routinely requires production of the full contract file, Lloyd's salvage arbitration awards, class society survey records, AIS vessel tracking data, and underlying FX receipts before zero-rated treatment is accepted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar is freely floating and there are no exchange control restrictions on the repatriation of capital, liquidation proceeds, or operational profits. Profit distributions to non-resident partners of an Alberta limited partnership are generally not subject to conversion restrictions; however, Part XIII withholding taxes may apply to certain payment types, subject to tax treaty relief under a Canada-U.S. or other applicable treaty. The partnership is treated as pass-through for Canadian tax purposes, and service fees, charter hire, and salvage awards are remittable through the Canadian banking system without prior regulatory approval. No currency licensing or surrender requirements exist. For maritime towage/salvage contracts, invoicing may be made in CAD or any mutually agreed foreign currency; there is no mandated repatriation timeline. Certain invoices and remittances are monitored under FINTRAC rules for large transactions of CAD 10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in Canada to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or to a designated authorized dealer. Exporters and service providers, including offshore towage and salvage operators, may retain all hard currency earnings in foreign-currency bank accounts either onshore in Canada or offshore as permitted by the Canada Business Corporations Act or Alberta Partnerships Act. No conversion ratio is imposed. Nevertheless, in order to satisfy the enhanced know-your-customer and anti-money-laundering (AML) expectations of Alberta clearing banks, the limited partnership should retain full documentation of each foreign remittance, including the native SWIFT message, the underlying salvage or towage agreement, and a board-approved treasury resolution confirming the non-resident allocation of funds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority) and FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) for cross-border transaction reporting; the foreign exchange framework is under the Currency Act and the Exchange Control Act (now largely non-restrictive)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Medium with strong caveat. Foreign, offshore electronic-money institutions (EMIs) that are not duly authorized under Canadian payments law cannot lawfully maintain a settlement account in Canada, nor can they clear CAD payments through Payments Canada (LVTS/Real-Time Rail). Such offshore EMIs may only be used as a non-clearing intermediary if they have obtained a Canadian money services business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC and have a corresponding banking relationship with a federally regulated Canadian Tier-1 bank. For offshore towage and salvage receivables in foreign jurisdictions, an EMI may be useful for collection of overseas invoice proceeds, but the ultimate liquidation into CAD must be done through a domestic clearing bank. In practice, the Alberta limited partnership should maintain direct accounts with Tier-1 banks and treat EMIs as sub-accounting / liquidity visibility tools only. The primary legal constraint is the Retail Payment Activities Act (Canada) and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, requiring registration and AML compliance for any Canadian payment activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections to the Alberta LP must be structured as sworn capital contributions from either the general partner or a designated limited partner. The routing must be a SWIFT MT103/202 with the beneficiary account at the Alberta clearing bank, and the beneficiary party must be identified with the LP's legal name and postal address. The recommended purpose code for the wire is S301 (Settlement of Liabilities), S302 (Transfer of Own Funds) or a specific commercial contract code if the injection is a loan. To prevent involuntary regulatory classification, the partner must issue a corporate resolution, a side letter confirming the funds are not derived from maritime piracy, smuggling, or sanctions evasion, and the payment must be cleared using the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) / Real-Time Rail for same-day final settlement. For any injection exceeding CAD 10,000, FINTRAC reporting is mandatory and for injections exceeding CAD 100,000, full source-of-funds documentation is required, including bank statements, vessel purchase-sale agreement, or a salvage guarantor's letter.Outbound trade disbursements for offshore towage and salvage settlement are typically executed via SWIFT MT103 for operating costs, MT202 for interbank transfers, or a commercial letter of credit (LC) for the supply of bunkers, provisions, lubricants, spare equipment, and crew payroll. For most Alberta clearing banks, payment against an original LOF award requires verified copies of the salvage settlement agreement and a 'full-set' legal opinion to release funds. Internationally, dual rails are used: (a) the Canadian LVTS/Real-Time Rail for domestic disbursements in CAD, and (b) the SWIFT network through the bank's correspondent banking partners for foreign-currency disbursements. For large salvage awards over USD 1 million, a transistor confirmation from the insuring club and a formal request from the appointed surveyor must be included, otherwise the outflow is queued for enhanced compliance review. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000. Aggregate cross-border non-arm's length transactions (inclusive of intercompany towage charter fees, salvage assistance charges, management fees, and financing) in a taxation year trigger the contemporaneous documentation expectation under CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R. Large multinational groups may additionally be required to maintain global Master File documentation and country-by-country reporting where the group-wide revenue threshold is met.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the offshore Economic Substance Regulations model. However, the Canada Revenue Agency applies the general anti-avoidance rule under Income Tax Act section 245, the common law 'sham' doctrine, and residence/permanent establishment principles to disregard a conduit Alberta LP lacking commercial substance. An LP engaged in offshore towage and salvage must maintain real operational presence in Alberta, including actual contracts, employees, vessel registration or charter arrangements, decision-making functions, and physical offices.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / false statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1): on summary conviction, a fine of 50%–200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus up to 2 years' imprisonment; on indictment, a fine of 100%–300% plus up to 5 years' imprisonment. Civil gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) is equal to 50% of the understated income tax.

Director personal liability: Officers, directors, and managing partners who participated in or acquiesced to the evasion are personally liable for unremitted source withholdings under ITA s.227.1. Criminal prosecution can result in imprisonment, fines, court-ordered passport surrender, and international travel restrictions.
Shell company / artificial arrangement lacking substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under the general anti-avoidance rule (s.245(2)); penalties for false statements or misrepresentations under s.163.2; and equitable fraud remedies if the arrangement is deemed a sham. Tax benefits may be fully denied with interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for amounts assessed to the corporation or partnership if they profited from the sham arrangement or caused false information to be presented to CRA. Civil fraud actions can require personal restitution, and criminal proceedings may lead to fines or imprisonment.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment under ITA s.247(2) to reflect arm's length prices; penalty under s.247(3) equal to 10% of the transfer pricing adjustment amount in excess of the lesser of CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue; plus interest and possible tax evasion penalties where documentation is intentionally false.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed for unremitted withholding or non-payment only in defined trust situations; more directly, directors who sign or approve false T106 information returns or deliberately transfer cash without allocation may face fines, prosecution, and personal disgorgement orders.
AML / FINTRAC compliance failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties imposed by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, with per-violation fines for a corporation typically up to CAD 500,000 depending on the compliance failure; laundering proceeds of crime under Criminal Code s.462.31 is an indictable offence punishable by up to 10 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct, authorize, or participate in a money laundering offence can be prosecuted personally. They also face civil liability, disqualification from serving as a director, regulatory bans, and potential extradition or international travel restrictions when criminal charges are pending.
