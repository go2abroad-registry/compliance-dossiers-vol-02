Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax under ITA s. 96, with income, gains, losses and credits flowing through to each partner under the partnership agreement. Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; classification follows the ITA and Excise Tax Act. Available elections include the ITA s. 97(2) tax-deferred transfer of capital property to the partnership, ITA s. 96(6)-(8) cessation and rollover elections, and ETA s. 177 qualifying-partnership nil-consideration election. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s. 18(4) operate automatically to disallow deductions for designated hybrid arrangements and do not require an affirmative election.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange-control permits, surrender quotas, conversion bands, or repatriation approval requirements for foreign direct investment, debt services, or profits. An Alberta limited partnership may freely convert CAD/USD and remit profits to non-resident partners, subject to underlying solvency, partnership cash-flow availability, Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding and partnership information return obligations, and FINTRAC cross-border wire reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Canada. Export receipts denominated in USD or other foreign currencies may be retained abroad or in Canadian bank foreign-currency accounts without forced conversion or deadline. The only applicable requirements are standard reporting of electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, record-keeping under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and use of Bank of Canada daily exchange rates for Canadian-dollar accounting and tax conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange administration and prudential supervision are complemented by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), with anti-money-laundering and cross-border transaction reporting administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary rail; foreign EMI platforms are not licensed as Canadian banks, do not have direct access to Payments Canada Lynx/ACSS settlement, generally cannot issue commercial letters of credit or customs bonds, and must rely on a Canadian chartered bank agent or FINTRAC-registered money services business for clearing. Their practical use is limited to non-core collection sub-accounts, employee expense cards, or low-value disbursements, with funds ultimately swept to a Schedule I bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (combined federal 15% and Alberta general 12%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% for non-resident partners on distributions treated as dividends; reduced under tax treaties (e.g., 15% under Canada-US treaty).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 5 months after the partnership's fiscal year-end (e.g., if year-end December 31, due May 31 of the following year).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for transactions with non-resident non-arm's length parties (for documentation); if gross revenue exceeds CAD 1 million, additional documentation required; Master File required if revenue exceeds CAD 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply; partnerships must demonstrate real economic activity, including physical presence, employees, and business purpose, to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of corporate partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes under s.227.1; travel bans and asset seizures possible.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Reassessment and penalties under GAAR; interest and penalties up to 50% of tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they authorized or participated in the arrangement.
AML/Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to CAD 2,500 per partner per year; additional penalties for failure to disclose; AML penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for violations if they knowingly failed to file or comply with AML obligations.
