Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for an Alberta limited partnership; the initial aggregate capital contribution of limited partners must be declared on Form LP-1 and may consist of money, property or services valued as accepted by the General Partner; withdrawal of a declared contribution, or failure to fund a promised contribution, can expose the limited partner to creditor liability under ss 20 and 38(3); port authorities will additionally require a terminal security and performance bond.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification per the Alberta Fire Code; structural load inspection of shore cranes and stacking aprons to CSA S16 and CSA B167; dockworker workplace clearance under Part 4 of the Alberta OHS Code addressing chemical and dust exposure; bulk solids dust-explosion controls to NFPA 61; and an Emergency Response Plan approved by the municipal fire department before commercial operations.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for an Alberta limited partnership; the initial aggregate capital contribution of limited partners must be declared on Form LP-1 and may consist of money, property or services valued as accepted by the General Partner; withdrawal of a declared contribution, or failure to fund a promised contribution, can expose the limited partner to creditor liability under ss 20 and 38(3); port authorities will additionally require a terminal security and performance bond.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — national tax administration and filing portal: 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO) at www.canada.ca/revenue-agency; GST/HST NETFILE for indirect tax returns; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) CARM portal for export and customs accounts; Alberta corporate income tax and partnership reporting are administered by CRA under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement, while the Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) performs the statutory registration of the limited partnership.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Extra-provincially register the Alberta limited partnership with the Alberta Corporate Registry under the Alberta Limited Partnership Act to obtain the LP registration number. Step 2: Apply for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1; the LP must open the relevant program accounts: RT (GST/HST) — mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD $30,000; RP (payroll) — if the terminal employs Canadian staff; and RM (customs) — for CARM export/import declarations. Step 3: Each non-resident partner must hold or obtain a Canadian tax identifier (BN or, for individuals, a SIN/ITN) to be reported on T5013 Schedule 50 and Form NR3010; a non-resident partner without a Canadian filing presence is identified through the LP's BN together with the partner's foreign TIN. Step 4: File Form T5013 Partnership Information Return within five months after the fiscal year-end; the LP is the reporting partnership and is not itself assessed to Part I income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Under ITA s. 96 the Alberta LP is fiscally transparent: income, losses, and tax attributes flow through to the partners, and the LP pays no federal or Alberta income tax on its own account. Territorial scope: business income is allocated to permanent establishments (Alberta PE for the terminal), and non-resident partners carrying on business in Alberta through that PE are taxable in Canada on their Alberta allocable share. Exceptional rule: a publicly traded 'SIFT partnership' (ITA s. 197) is deemed to be a corporation resident in Canada and is taxed on its non-portfolio earnings; a privately held bulk port stevedoring LP will not be a SIFT. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under ITA s. 96 the Alberta LP is fiscally transparent: income, losses, and tax attributes flow through to the partners, and the LP pays no federal or Alberta income tax on its own account. Territorial scope: business income is allocated to permanent establishments (Alberta PE for the terminal), and non-resident partners carrying on business in Alberta through that PE are taxable in Canada on their Alberta allocable share. Exceptional rule: a publicly traded 'SIFT partnership' (ITA s. 197) is deemed to be a corporation resident in Canada and is taxed on its non-portfolio earnings; a privately held bulk port stevedoring LP will not be a SIFT. The entity is not located in a tax-exempt zone and requires Port Authority / marine terminal operating licenses before commencing activities.

Available election forms

Canada has no elective 'check-the-box' regime; entity classification follows statutory rules and the backward tracing principle in ITA s. 96. Available elections and mechanisms: (i) ITA s. 97(2) — transfer of property to the LP at an agreed amount; (ii) ITA s. 96(2) — partner-level allocation of capital cost allowance and eligible capital amounts; (iii) ITA s. 216 — non-resident partner election for net-basis taxation of Canadian business income instead of Part XIII gross-basis withholding where applicable; (iv) treaty-based 'net basis' elections for non-resident partners claiming benefits under a tax treaty; (v) hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory rather than elective — ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.5 deny deductions for payments to hybrid entities or under hybrid financial instruments creating double deductions or deduction/non-inclusion mismatches, and the pending expansion of those rules under the OECD BEPS Action 2 model must be monitored.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. No prior authorization is required for trade-related receipts, capital inflows, profit remittances, or redemption/withdrawal of limited partnership capital. Current-account transactions, including stevedoring service fees and port tariffs invoiced to non-residents, are freely transferable. Capital-account flows, including subscriber capital contributions from non-resident limited partners and onward equity funding, are unrestricted, subject only to (i) Investment Canada Act review if an acquisition of control of a Canadian business is involved, (ii) FINTRAC electronic fund transfer reporting above CAD 10,000, and (iii) prospective Part XIII withholding tax on income allocations distributed to non-resident partners, the rate being 25% in the absence of treaty relief (15% under the Canada–US Tax Convention for US residents holding less than a controlling 90% interest, subject to domestic exemption for Canadian-resident partnership allocations). Profit remittances from a Canadian-resident Alberta limited partnership pass through the general partner and are not blocked; remittance hurdles are limited to documentary evidence of the partnership allocation, NR4 or T5013 forms, and compliance with Part XIII withholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, no holding-period restriction, and no designated timeline for export receipts. An Alberta stevedoring/terminal operating partnership may retain export proceeds in foreign-currency bank accounts maintained at Canadian Schedule I banks or authorized foreign banks, convert such proceeds at market-determined rates, and deploy them freely. Reporting obligations are limited to FINTRAC large-cash/EFT reports (CAD 10,000 threshold), CBSA cross-border currency declarations (CAD 10,000 or equivalent), and, for Canadian controlled partnerships, any applicable foreign-account/property reporting under the Income Tax Act (T1135) where foreign investment or foreign bank account thresholds are met.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with decentralized FX administration; banking conduct and prudential oversight rest with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (Canada) and the Retail Payment Activities Act, while anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision and large cross-border transaction reporting are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low for primary treasury reliance and High as an ancillary rail only. Canada does not have an 'electronic money institution' (EMI) licence; the Retail Payment Activities Act mandates registration with the Bank of Canada for payment-service providers performing electronic funds transfer, clearing, or settlement. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Corpay, etc.) can operate cross-border corridors but cannot function as deposit-taking banks in Canada, cannot extend CDIC-insurance status, and require a federally regulated bank partner or local agent for CAD clearing; fees and FX spreads are attractive for small ticket operational payments, but the capital escrow function for a port terminal facility and the SWIFT-based settlement of large stevedoring/tariff collections should remain in Schedule I banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires an inbound SWIFT MT103 from the remitting non-resident partner's bank to the LP's Canadian Schedule I operating account, with narrative/remittance information coded 'Capital Contribution — Limited Partnership Equity Injection' for FINTRAC transparency. The bank will require (i) the equity subscription schedule, (ii) confirmation that the partner is the named remitter and also named as limited partner, (iii) beneficial-ownership certification and partner register, and (iv) if the injection exceeds CAD100,000, a copy of the notarized resolution authorizing the injection. The receiving bank credits via the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day value, or via ACSS where the transaction is settled through the Automated Clearing Settlement System if below high-value thresholds; all transfers at or above CAD10,000 are subject to FINTRAC EFT reporting. For a newly created LP preparing to acquire an existing port terminal, the general partner must also confirm once the transaction closes that no Investment Canada Act review threshold is triggered and that Part XIII withholding risk on subsequent distributions is documented.Outbound disbursements for terminal construction, bulk-loading equipment (conveyor systems, ship-loaders), and dredging contracts settle through the following rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for urgent vendor or vessel payments, (ii) domestic ACSS payments via Canadian clearing for Alberta-based suppliers, (iii) commercial Letters of Credit (Documentary L/C) under UCP600 for cross-border equipment purchases, utilising the bank's trade-finance desk at the Calgary/Edmonton corporate banking centre, and (iv) Standby Letters of Credit to the Port Authority as performance/security bonds. For stevedoring terminal tariff collections from shipping lines and freight forwarders, the LP should maintain a mono-line collection account that instructs the bank to convert foreign-currency deposits into CAD via standing instruction and to remit quota-share fees to the Port Authority via LVTS.
