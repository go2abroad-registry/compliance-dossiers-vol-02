Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
Alberta limited partnerships do not have a board of directors; the general partner acts for the partnership under the certificate and partnership agreement. The general partner hereby approves the limited partners' subscriptions and contributions in the amounts recorded in the subscription documents, subject to acceptance and applicable law. The registered office stated in the filed certificate is adopted as the partnership's registered office. The general partner appoints [name] as authorized signing officer and [name] as records and compliance officer, with authority to complete formation filings, obtain tax and business registrations, open partnership accounts, and execute routine formation documents. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No general Alberta industrial or freight-forwarder licensing board administers a general NVOCC operating licence. Transport Canada administers federal marine transportation statutes; the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs-broker licensing. These regimes apply only where the business performs activities that trigger them.

Category: No general Alberta operating or manufacturing permit is identified for an office-based ocean freight forwarder/NVOCC that does not operate vessels, act as a licensed customs broker, or conduct separately regulated activities. Municipal business licensing and premises approvals may apply. A customs broker acting for importers must obtain a CBSA customs broker licence; vessel operation and carriage activities require a separate federal regulatory assessment.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export declarations; Alberta Corporate Registry for provincial LP registration. Portal: CRA My Business Account (GST/HST, Payroll, Import/Export program accounts).

E-government filing portal

1) Register the Limited Partnership under the Alberta Partnership Act via the Alberta Corporate Registry, obtaining a Corporate Access Number (CAN); 2) Apply to CRA for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or My Business Account, with Program Account codes: GST/HST (RT), Payroll (RP), Import/Export (RM); 3) Non-resident partners requiring Canadian tax identification obtain a Trust/Non-Resident BN through CRA's Non-Resident Income Tax program; 4) Mandatory GST/HST registration once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, or voluntary registration for NVOCCs making zero-rated export transportation services (to claim input tax credits); 5) Register with CBSA for exporter reporting obligations and G7/CAED filing capability via the CARM client portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no IRS-style check-the-box election; Canadian LPs are statutorily flow-through under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96 (income computed at partnership level, tax borne by partners). Available elections: (a) ITA s. 216(1) election permitting a non-resident partner to file a Part I return on Canadian-source income rather than Part XIII withholding; (b) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(8); (c) BEPS hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.4-18.6) providing structured-arrangement tracing and designated-entity elections; (d) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156 closely-related-person election and s. 271 partnership specified-member GST/HST filing election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies per calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); voluntary registration permitted for NVOCCs making zero-rated export supply arrangements so that input tax credits on brokerage, stevedoring, and IT costs can be recovered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part VI for: (a) a supply of a service of transporting goods from a place in Canada to a place outside Canada; (b) a supply of a service of transporting goods into Canada; (c) a supply of a service of arranging for the said transportation, which covers NVOCC ocean freight brokerage. Eligibility requires the underlying transportation service to be zero-rated, the goods to be physically exported (typically evidenced within 60 days), and, for services supplied to non-residents, the non-resident recipient must not be registered under GST/HST. Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is retained as the standard documentary-evidence and treasury benchmark for confirming the cross-border consideration received.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA G7/CAED filing or CARM export report showing CUSNUM, exporter information, and Canadian export authorization)
  • Bill of Lading (original oceangoing B/L or sea waybill evidencing Canadian load port, foreign discharge port, and foreign consignee)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank confirmation evidencing receipt of freight payment in foreign currency from the non-resident account)
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list matching container numbers and declared export value
  • Carrier master bill / slot charter confirmation and non-resident consignee identity records

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime in Canada. CRA operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service aligned with the Peppol network (BIS Billing 3.0). Mandatory electronic filing applies: GST/HST returns must be e-filed via GST/HST NETFILE (or EDI/API for high-volume filers); export transactions must be declared electronically through CBSA CARM / Single Window Initiative using G7/CAED export declarations or e2M batch API interfaces for high-volume freight forwarders.

CRA 'Export and Cross-Border Review' program conducts targeted audits of zero-rated NVOCC claims. ETA s. 280 imposes a penalty of 40% of net tax where a false statement is made knowingly; ETA s. 281(1) imposes a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 or 25% of net tax understated due to misrepresentation; interest at prescribed rates compounds on clawbacks of input tax credits and reversed zero-rating. Assessment periods extend from 4 to 7 years, or indefinitely for fraud/culpable neglect (ETA s. 298; ITA s. 152(4)). Criminal tax evasion (ETA s. 327; ITA s. 239) carries fines of up to 200% of evaded amounts and imprisonment of up to 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no general exchange-control permits, no mandatory Central Bank pre-approval for repatriation, and no profit-remittance hurdle for an Alberta Limited Partnership (LP) with non-resident partners. Dividends, distributions, management fees, interest, and liquidation proceeds may be remitted in CAD or any freely convertible currency, subject to Part XIII withholding tax obligations, transfer pricing documentation, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds. Capital contributions from non-resident limited partners are freely admitted, but any foreign direct investment in sectors subject to the Investment Canada Act must be reviewed separately. In practice, clearing banks require a written purpose code, a distribution resolution or capital-call notice, and proof of the underlying commercial transaction before processing a large outward remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no time-limited repatriation obligation for ocean freight forwarding or NVOCC service revenues. Export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency bank accounts both inside and outside Canada, or converted into CAD at any time. However, all receipts exceeding CAD $10,000 or equivalent must be reported to FINTRAC in the normal course of suspicious/large transaction monitoring, and full evidence of the underlying export transaction (export declaration, carrier bill of lading, commercial invoice, freight contract) must be retained for Canada Revenue Agency audit purposes and for bank enhanced due diligence on cross-border freight-forwarding activity.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Canadian monetary authority); foreign exchange administration resides with the Minister of Finance and the Canada Border Services Agency for cross-border movement reporting; prudential bank oversight is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing supervision is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, use only as an auxiliary corridor, never as the sole treasury rail. Internationally licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Business, Airwallex, and NIUM may provide practical cross-border collection and disbursement for freight payments in multiple currencies, but they are not regulated deposit-taking institutions in Canada. An offshore EMI does not replace a Canadian-chartered bank account for capital escrow, regulatory capital, or statutory trust obligations. Any EMI soliciting Canadian clients, holding Canadian funds, or conducting money-services business in Canada must register as a Money Service Business with FINTRAC and comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. In addition, an Alberta LP should confirm that its partnership agreement authorizes accounts at non-Canadian financial institutions, and that disbursements through the EMI preserve a complete audit trail of underlying NVOCC transactions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opacity of non-resident limited partners and layered ownership of the Alberta LP.

Root cause: Opacity of non-resident limited partners and layered ownership of the Alberta LP.

Defense evidence: Provide the federal Certificate of Limited Partnership from the Alberta Corporate Registry; full partnership agreement and all amendments; complete register of partners with names, jurisdictions, tax identification numbers, and ownership percentages; notarized passport copies of each ultimate beneficial owner; corporate registry extracts for all corporate partners; a written source-of-wealth and source-of-funds declaration; an organizational chart evidencing the direct and indirect ownership chain; and a banking reference letter from a regulated home-jurisdiction financial institution.
NVOCC freight forwarding creates high transaction volumes with consignees, foreign carriers, and counterparties in elevated-risk jurisdictions.

Root cause: NVOCC freight forwarding creates high transaction volumes with consignees, foreign carriers, and counterparties in elevated-risk jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Maintain a commercial transaction file for each shipment: signed freight-forwarding or NVOCC service contract; standard house bill of lading and master bill of lading; consolidated ocean manifest; shipper and consignee KYC records; inventory of cargo type, weight, container number, and port pair; customs export/import declarations; underlying commercial invoices; sanctions screening log demonstrating negative hits or cleared hits; and contractual evidence of the commercial substance of the transaction.

Root cause: Rapid in-and-out capital injection or distributions through third-party funding without clear economic purpose.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident capital contributions should be routed through SWIFT MT103 from the limited partner's own bank account directly to the LP's dedicated Canadian operating or escrow account. The payment must reference the specific capital-call notice date and the LP's bank account description, with purpose code clearly stated as capital contribution or subscription, not as trade settlement. The bank should receive a pre-advised capital-call notice and the partnership agreement excerpt authorizing the account. For inbound wires of CAD $10,000 or more, the LP must retain the full wire message, partner identification, source-of-funds evidence, and capital-call documents for FINTRAC and bank compliance.
