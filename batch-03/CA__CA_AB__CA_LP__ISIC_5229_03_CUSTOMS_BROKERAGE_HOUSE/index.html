Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), which administers the federal Income Tax Act, Excise Tax Act, and Business Number (BN) registration; online portal is the CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency. Customs and export declaration administration is conducted by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), including the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal for importer/exporter accounts and electronic customs filings.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Canadian limited partnership should: (1) register the Alberta limited partnership with the Alberta Corporate Registry through Service Alberta's Corporate Registry system and obtain the Alberta corporate access number; (2) apply to CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or CRA Business Registration Online, selecting the applicable tax accounts, including GST/HST, Import/Export, and any payroll or corporate income tax account required; (3) obtain from CRA the RT-type GST/HST registration number and the import/export account linked to the BN; (4) obtain a CBSA customs broker licence under the Customs Act because the entity's ISIC 5229 business is a licensed customs brokerage; and (5) ensure that non-resident partners provide their foreign tax identification numbers to the partnership for CRA T5013 partnership information return purposes, with non-resident individuals applying for a Canada Revenue Agency Individual Tax Number (ITN) using CRA Form T1261 if a Canadian tax identifier is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - an Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act; the limited partnership itself does not pay federal or provincial income tax, and each partner is taxable on its allocated share of the LP's income or loss. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate registrant person in respect of its commercial activities, but partners remain jointly and severally liable for the GST/HST obligations of the partnership to the extent provided by the Excise Tax Act and the Alberta Limited Partnership Act.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election for Alberta limited partnerships; partnership transparency is mandatory and an LP cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation. Available statutory elections are administrative and include: the GST/HST closely-related election under ETA subsection 156(1) to treat certain intercompany supplies between registrants as made for nil consideration; the small-supplier optional registration election under ETA section 95 where total taxable supplies are below the CAD 30,000 threshold; and elections for reporting periods and filing frequencies. The LP must also consider the T5013 partnership information return requirements and any provincial registrations in Alberta.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies made by the person in Canada during the last four consecutive calendar quarters, before deducting input tax credits, being the 'small supplier' threshold under subsection 148(1) of the Excise Tax Act. Non-residents carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same registration threshold, and a non-resident registrant must account for GST/HST on taxable supplies made in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, a supply of goods is zero-rated where the goods are exported by the supplier in commercial quantities and the supplier retains the prescribed documentary evidence of export. A supply of a service to a non-resident can be zero-rated only if the service falls within the applicable zero-rating provision and does not meet any exception, such as a service in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. For a licensed customs brokerage, the eligibility must be established for each transaction: the brokerage fee may be zero-rated where the service is supplied to a bona fide non-resident and the underlying goods are actually exported from Canada, but a service performed on goods in Canada or for a Canadian importer will generally be taxable at 5% GST. The broker must document the non-resident status of the client, the place of performance, and the export movement of the goods, and must not rely solely on the customer's general export status.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian B13A / CBSA export declaration or CAED/G7 EDI record)
  • Bill of Lading (original or certified copy, showing Canadian port of loading and foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign-exchange settlement evidence demonstrating export proceeds receipt

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance e-invoice stamping, or government-authorization digital stamping regime for GST/HST. The CRA permits electronic invoicing in EDI, XML, or PDF format provided the invoice contains the mandatory disclosure elements under ETA sections 221 to 223 and the corresponding regulations. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE/API. For customs transactions, CBSA requires electronic transmission of export and import declarations through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal or approved EDI platforms, including the Canadian Export Reporting System; there is no additional government invoice stamp.

Export zero-rating is tightly audited by the CRA. If the LP cannot produce sufficient export evidence, if the goods are diverted back into Canada, or if the supply is mischaracterized as an export service, the CRA may reverse the zero-rating and assess GST/HST with arrears interest and penalties. The normal GST/HST assessment window is four years, but it can be extended in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default. False statements and omissions may trigger gross-negligence penalties at 25% of the relevant net tax amount, and the CRA may also apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule where a transaction is structured to obtain a tax benefit. In a limited partnership, general partners are exposed to joint and several assessment for GST/HST debts, and the relevant partners and licensed customs broker representatives may additionally face CBSA monetary penalties or criminal prosecution in cases of wilful evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada accepted IMF Article VIII obligations on 25 March 1952 and operates full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no approvals, quotas, or surrender requirements for capital injections, trade settlements, or profit remittance by a Canadian limited partnership. The only financial frictions are tax-based: Part XIII withholding under the Income Tax Act (25% statutory on dividends, interest, rents, and royalties paid to non-residents, subject to treaty reduction, e.g., 15% dividends under the Canada-US Convention) and the requirement for a CBSA customs broker licence under s.9 of the Customs Act, which imposes a Canadian presence and surety-bond condition on the licensed entity but does not restrict foreign ownership or convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation window on export proceeds received by a customs brokerage house or its importer clients. Receivables and fee income may be held, invoiced, and settled in CAD or any freely convertible foreign currency. Reporting obligations are limited to Statistical balance-of-payments declarations and FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reports where the C$10,000 threshold is met.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary and financial system authority; the Department of Finance Canada owns exchange-rate policy; OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) prudentially supervises federally chartered banks; FINTRAC administers the AML/CFT regime; Payments Canada operates the Lynx high-value wire system and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). Canada has maintained no exchange-control legislation since the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low. Canada does not issue EU-style Electronic Money Institution licences. Non-resident digital payment firms such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer may operate in Canada only by registering as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and by complying with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which commenced mandatory compliance in 2024. A foreign-owned CA LP may use these platforms for auxiliary multi-currency sub-ledgers and low-value vendor disbursements, but the platforms cannot act as the principal banking rail for the licensed customs brokerage duty collection account, for the CBSA surety bond, or for capital escrow. The operational treasury account must be held with a Schedule I chartered bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or investor directly to the LP's operational or escrow account held at the designated Schedule I bank. The wire instruction must include the beneficiary LP name, the Alberta corporate or extra-provincial registration number, and a purpose description classified as 'Direct Investment - Equity Injection into Limited Partnership' for balance-of-payments and Statistics Canada statistical compliance. For USD injections, the Canadian bank clears through its New York Fedwire/CHIPS correspondent and posts USD internally; CAD-denominated capital is settled with finality through Payments Canada Lynx on the same value date. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (flow-through limited partnership; no entity-level federal or Alberta corporate income tax). Corporate partners are taxed at combined rates: federal 15% + Alberta 8% = 23% general; eligible Canadian-controlled private corporation small business income taxed at 9% federal + 2% Alberta = 11% on first C$500,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for non-resident recipients of Canadian-source dividends; Canada-US treaty reduces the rate to 5% for corporate shareholders owning 10% or more of voting shares, and to 15% for portfolio dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 per non-arm's-length foreign counterparty in a taxation year for mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under ITA section 247(4). Master File requirements apply to multinational groups exceeding C$500 million in gross revenue or C$200 million in consolidated assets.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to those in other jurisdictions; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA section 245), substance-over-form jurisprudence, and mandatory disclosure rules require genuine commercial purpose, actual management presence in Alberta, meaningful business activity, and preservation of the limited partnership's commercial substance. An Alberta LP used solely to route customs brokerage commissions to a low-tax jurisdiction without employees, premises, or business risk will be reassessed, treaty benefits denied, and additional withholding taxes imposed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under ITA section 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; criminal conviction under ITA section 239(1) carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to 2 years. CRA may also impose a gross negligence penalty on the partnership and all responsible partners.

Director personal liability: General and limited partners who knowingly participate or acquiesce in the evasion are jointly and severally liable under ITA section 160. Directors of corporate general partners face personal liability for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under ITA section 227.1, plus travel bans and asset freeze orders under collection protocols.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA application of GAAR to recharacterize the allocation and deny treaty benefits; reassess the partnership and partners for undeclared Canadian business income; impose 25% Part XIII withholding on distributions to non-resident partners; apply gross negligence penalties of up to 50%.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be personally assessed for unremitted Part XIII withholding under ITA section 227.1 and are exposed to restitution orders, prohibition from serving as corporate directors, and criminal prosecution for participating in an artificial transaction.
Unlicensed Customs Brokerage and CBSA Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Customs Act and CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalty regime, fines up to C$50,000 for individuals and C$250,000 for corporations per occurrence; licence suspension or revocation; seizure of goods; criminal prosecution may trigger imprisonment of up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly direct unlicensed brokerage activities can be prosecuted personally, fined, and imprisoned; CBSA may also impose personal liability for unpaid duties and penalties on directors of the corporate licensed customs broker.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties of up to C$1 million for corporations and C$500,000 for individuals per violation; criminal breach of the Act may result in imprisonment of up to 5 years; CBSA may revoke the customs broker licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be personally liable for FINTRAC penalties if they authorized or acquiesced in reporting failures; courts may issue personal compliance orders, travel restrictions, and director bans, and may pierce the corporate veil to enforce restitution.
