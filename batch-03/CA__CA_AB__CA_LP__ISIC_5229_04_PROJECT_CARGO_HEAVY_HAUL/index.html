Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — national authority for income tax, GST/HST, and Business Number (BN) administration; online portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-my-business-account.html). Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs and export declaration requirements. Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) is the provincial revenue authority, but Alberta imposes no Provincial Sales Tax and no separate corporate tax filing beyond the federal-provincial collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) from CRA. Non-resident applicants submit Form RC1A, Request for a Business Number — Non-Resident, or Form RC1 where a Canadian address exists, or apply online through Business Registration Online when the entity has a Canadian postal code. The applicant selects the GST/HST program account (RT) at the same time; the BN is the statutory tax identification number and takes the format '123456789 RT 0001'. CRA will require proof of foreign incorporation, the LP agreement, the designated partner or resident agent, and the nature of Canadian commercial activity. Registration is voluntary below the small-supplier threshold, but mandatory if the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier. The Alberta Limited Partnership should also be registered as an extra-provincial registered limited partnership under the Alberta Partnership Act, but the CRA BN is the operative tax identity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A limited partnership is fiscally transparent by statutory rule under section 96 of the Income Tax Act; partners report their allocated shares of income, gains, and losses directly. For GST/HST purposes the partnership itself is generally treated as a registrant and person. Available statutory elections relevant to this structure include the section 156 Excise Tax Act election for closely related corporations/partnerships and, for non-resident suppliers, the designation of a resident agent under section 269 Excise Tax Act with CRA approval. Hybrid mismatch rules are not elective and apply as law, with the general anti-avoidance rule available to CRA where arrangements artificially defer or eliminate tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold measured against worldwide revenue from taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies, excluding exempt supplies) in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; for a newly established business, the threshold applies in a single calendar quarter. A person making taxable supplies in Canada who ceases to be a small supplier must register for GST/HST. Voluntary registration is available to non-residents below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply qualifies for 0% GST when it is an export of tangible personal property, a service performed wholly outside Canada for a non-resident, or a transportation/freight arrangement service in respect of goods exported from Canada. For the heavy-haul project cargo logistics scope, inland cartage, craneage, terminal handling, ocean or rail freight, and out-of-gauge preparation services supplied to a non-resident principal in respect of cargo leaving Canada qualify for zero-rating if documentary proof of export is retained. Supplies consumed in Canada, or made to residents for domestic use, remain taxable at 5%.

  • Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A / Form B13 Customs Export Declaration, or the approved electronic export report, identifying exporter of record, HS classification, OOG dimensions, and value
  • Commercial invoice and project cargo contract showing the non-resident principal and stating the supply as zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V or Part VII of the Excise Tax Act
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill, including inland pre-carriage tickets and ocean or rail waybills for heavy-haul equipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds from the non-resident payer
  • Freight manifest, haulage tickets, border clearance records, and destination terminal delivery receipts
  • Packlist, heavy-lift survey certificate, and lifting/engineering plan corroborating the out-of-gauge cargo movement
  • Proof of allocation of the specific export invoice to the related project and matching payment entries in the limited partnership's books

No federal or Alberta real-time e-invoicing, digital stamping, or fiscal clearance mandate exists. A GST/HST registrant must issue invoices containing prescribed information, including the BN/GST/HST registration number, invoice date, description of supplies, and tax charged. Electronic invoices are acceptable if the accounting system retains complete, legible records and grants CRA officers access on audit. CRA does not require API clearance or pre-authorization of invoices; electronic returns and remittances are mandatory once registered and are filed through CRA My Business Account or GST/HST NETFILE in CRA-accepted formats.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess the supply at 5% GST/HST if the export documentation, cargo movement, or foreign-currency receipt is incomplete, inconsistent, or lacks commercial substance. The normal reassessment period is four years, but CRA may reassess beyond that period for misrepresentation or fraud, and unlimited reassessment applies for wilful default. Penalties include compounded daily interest, late-remittance penalties, and gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the amount sought to be evaded under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act. Intentional false statements, false export declarations, or diversion of the goods to the domestic market may lead to criminal liability under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act. Export refunds and input tax credits remain subject to clawback until the mandated documentation bundle and repatriated proceeds are fully substantiated and retained in the LP's books.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) enjoys full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account. Canada maintains an open capital account consistent with the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements; no exchange controls, no repatriation licenses, and no profits-remittance restrictions exist. Profit distributions from the Alberta Limited Partnership (CA_LP) to non-resident limited partners are subject to 25% Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) s.194, subject to treaty reduction, but no FX obstacle attaches to such remittances. CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/CNH transactions clear freely in the interbank market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes zero statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation obligations on export receipts; 100% of export proceeds may be retained in CAD or any freely convertible currency. Sole compliance duties are FINTRAC-driven: cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting for CAD 10,000 and above, large cash transaction reporting at CAD 10,000 and above, and suspicious transaction reporting. These are AML/ATF obligations, not currency controls.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money-laundering oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for primary treasury anchoring. Canada does not recognize European-style Electronic Money Institution licenses; payment service providers must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada and simultaneously register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may offer CAD-denominated virtual accounts useful as sub-collection rails, but none can issue irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit, participate in LVTS clearing, provide committed FX hedges, or hold project escrow with covenant-level controls. Charter a Tier 1 bank for all material flows; retain a single EMI only for low-value disbursements.

Wire must cite the bank's payment purpose code for direct investment/capital contribution (BPM6-aligned, e.g., 'S0108' capital or 'F100' direct investment equity), and for amounts above CAD 500,000 the contribution must be pre-notified to the bank with the General Partner's capital call notice. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined federal-provincial general corporate rate (Federal 15% + Alberta 8%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on Canadian-source dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident beneficial owner; treaty-reduced rates commonly 15% for portfolio shareholders and 5% for related companies; LP profit distributions themselves are not dividends and are generally not subject to withholding at the partnership level

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA administrative expectation: contemporaneous Local File documentation is required for controlled cross-border transactions where the aggregate value exceeds CAD 1 million for goods/services or CAD 500,000 for royalties, interest, and management fees; Master File and Country-by-Country Report become mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated global revenue exceeding CAD 1 billion. Alberta imposes no separate TP documentation threshold.

Economic substance scope: Alberta has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, CRA will apply the sham doctrine, s.245 GAAR, and transfer pricing rules under s.247 to disregard transactions lacking economic substance. A limited partnership must demonstrate real employees, premises, revenue risk, and management decision-making; otherwise its tax attributes may be recharacterized or allocated to the Canadian controlling principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1): criminal conviction carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years; s.163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; in addition, CRA assesses interest, CCA/CGI, and s.162 late-filing penalties

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be assessed for withheld amounts under s.227.1; principals and de-facto officers face personal criminal fines, seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 5 years
Shell Company or Artificial Entity Without Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the partnership as a sham; s.245 GAAR recharacterizes the whole transaction so that revenue is taxed as income of the Canadian controller; gross-negligence penalties of 50% apply and all denied expenses are re-added

Director personal liability: The general partner and controlling directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax and penalties; civil restitution of distributed assets can include worldwide personal assets; courts may issue non-departure/travel-ban orders and criminal sanctions for document fabrication
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income under s.247(2); a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment applies under s.247(3) when the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 50,000 and 10% of gross revenue and adequate contemporaneous documentation was not maintained; arrears interest compounds on all reassessed amounts

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted tax and penalties if they directed, authorized, or acquiesced; gross-negligence misrepresentation penalties under s.163.2 of up to CAD 100,000 may be assessed against individuals who signed false TP documents, and criminal prosecution is available in egregious cases
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities; FINTRAC can issue compliance orders; criminal sanctions for laundering proceeds of crime include fines up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment for up to 10 years

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed, authorized, or consented to contraventions can be personally assessed for administrative monetary penalties; criminal convictions expose directors to personal fines, forfeiture and restitution orders, travel bans, and imprisonment
