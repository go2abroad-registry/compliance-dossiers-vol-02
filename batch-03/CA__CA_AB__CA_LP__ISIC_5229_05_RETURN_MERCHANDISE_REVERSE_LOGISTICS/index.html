Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — sole administering authority for federal income tax and the Goods and Services Tax / Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST). Alberta corporate and personal income tax is also administered by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; Alberta levies no provincial retail sales tax. Key portals: CRA My Business Account, Business Registration Online (BRO), GST/HST NETFILE, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) CARM portal.

E-government filing portal

1) Confirm the Alberta Limited Partnership (registered under the Alberta Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c P-3, via Service Alberta Corporate Registry) is carrying on business in Canada and is not a small supplier. 2) Apply through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), or submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' by mail/fax to the International Tax Services Office in Ottawa for non-resident partnerships; attach the Alberta registry LP number, partnership agreement, and, where applicable, a Canadian resident agent designation. 3) CRA issues a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) and opens program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), RC (corporate income tax where relevant) and RM (customs import/export). 4) Non-resident registrants without a Canadian physical presence may use mailing/fax channels; online registration generally issues the BN immediately, while non-resident paper filings may take 2–4 weeks. 5) File the federal T5013 Partnership Information Return and the Alberta Partnership Information Return annually; each non-resident partner must report its allocated Canadian-source income through its own Canadian return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The Alberta LP is fiscally transparent for federal and Alberta income tax: income, gains, losses and credits flow through to partners, and the partnership files information returns rather than paying entity-level income tax, subject to SIFT rules if publicly traded. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is a separate 'person' and registrant under the Excise Tax Act. Non-resident partners are permitted and are taxable on their allocated Canadian-source income. Alberta has no territorial tax exemption or free-zone regime applicable to this LP.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is fixed by statute. Relevant elections available to the LP include: (i) functional currency election under ITA s. 261 if audited financial statements are presented in an eligible foreign currency; (ii) GST/HST election under ETA s. 273 to deem supplies between the partnership and a non-arm's-length partner/person to be at fair market value; (iii) selection of the partnership's fiscal period for information-return purposes; (iv) election of GST/HST filing frequency (monthly, quarterly, annual) and instalment basis under ETA s. 238; and (v) treaty-based relief filings (e.g., Form NR301/NR302) by non-resident partners to reduce Canadian withholding or taxable amounts. Hybrid-mismatch and excessive-interest rules apply at partner level and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold computed on the partnership's worldwide GST/HST taxable supplies for the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148; non-resident carrying-on-business rule in ETA s. 240). A non-resident LP exceeding CAD 30,000 in taxable supply revenue must register and collect GST; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP may zero-rate (0% GST) the following supplies to non-resident principals in its reverse-logistics and returned-goods model: (i) goods exported by the LP as supplier to a destination outside Canada where the goods are delivered outside Canada as part of the supply (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 1–6); (ii) freight-forwarding and freight-arranging services in respect of a transportation service that begins in Canada and ends outside Canada (ETA Schedule VI, Part VII, ss. 8–13) — the primary qualifying activity under ISIC 5229 / NAICS 488510; and (iii) services supplied to a non-resident that are performed wholly outside Canada. Services physically performed in Canada on returned goods, such as inspection, grading, refurbishment, and repackaging, are generally taxable at 5% GST because ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 excludes services performed in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; however, where returned goods move continuously out of Canada as part of an export chain, CRA policy may support zero-rating. Each claim must be supported by contemporaneous export documentation.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Declaration (Form B13A or CERS electronic export transaction record with unique transaction number)
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill / courier consignment waybill evidencing direct export from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and underlying purchase order or RMA documentation linking the shipment to the reverse-logistics contract
  • Cargo control and customs release documents (CBSA), including carrier proof of exit from Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103, or Airwallex/Local ACH settlement evidence proving payment of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Warehouse, inspection, and quality-grading records showing the goods' arrival, disposition, and ultimate export

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-approval API regime exists in Canada. CRA accepts paper or electronic invoices containing the prescribed particulars (ETA s. 3 and GST/HST Memorandum 7-4): supplier name and Business Number, invoice date, recipient identification where an ITC is claimed, terms, unit price, consideration, GST/HST rate and amount. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework, based on the Peppol EN 16931 standard, is voluntary. Cross-border export declarations are transmitted electronically through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) and the CARM portal.

CRA and CBSA jointly audit export zero-rating claims with strict evidentiary review. A mischaracterized zero-rating claim results in reassessment of 5% GST on the full consideration, plus compound daily interest under ETA s. 280 and civil penalties up to 20% of the net tax for false statements or wilful participation (ETA ss. 280, 285). Erroneous refunds and input tax credits are recoverable with interest from the date paid (ETA s. 296). Where a claim is fraudulent or part of a scheme, criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 may apply, with fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment. CRA may also extend the normal 4-year reassessment window (ETA s. 298) where a return contains a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. Contemporaneous export bundles, CBSA transaction numbers, and FX repatriation records are essential to reduce clawback exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency regime with unrestricted capital account transactions and complete current account convertibility. Profit remittances for qualified Alberta Limited Partnerships face no statutory hurdles beyond standard corporate tax filings under the Income Tax Act and FINTRAC compliance obligations. Beneficial ownership disclosure is required via corporate registry extracts and director identification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of proceeds is required under Canadian law. Inbound reverse logistics returns and cross-border receipts must be deposited into licensed Canadian bank accounts and reported for tax purposes. Voluntary conversion to CAD is permitted without statutory ratios, subject only to PCMLTFA reporting thresholds and beneficial ownership verification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Airwallex and comparable fintech EMI platforms are viable for Canadian LPs engaged in reverse logistics, subject to FINTRAC registration, AML/KYC standards, and bridging to local bank rails for full CAD settlement integration. Offshore digital EMIs are permissible for inbound funding but require local operational accounts for regulatory compliance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and Alberta provincial general corporate income tax rate of 23% (federal 15% + Alberta 8%), applicable to a corporation or a corporate partner allocated income from a limited partnership. Non-resident partners are subject to Part I tax on Canadian-source income at applicable treaty or statutory rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident partnership to a non-resident partner (deemed dividends to the extent of income allocations), absent treaty relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T5013 must be filed no later than five months after the partnership's fiscal year-end. For a December 31 fiscal year-end, the deadline is May 31 of the following calendar year. Each partner's income tax return is due April 30 (or June 15 for self-employed partners, with balance due April 30).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian TP documentation obligations apply to transactions with non-arm's length non-residents exceeding CAD 1 million (gross revenue) in a taxation year. The Local File and Master File documentation must be prepared if the aggregate value of such transactions exceeds the threshold; the CRA may impose penalties of CAD 24,000 per instance for failure to provide documentation, reduced on a sliding scale for co-operation.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a general Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) for domestic partnerships, but the 'economic substance' of a transaction determines whether it is recognized for Canadian tax purposes under the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) framework. The Limited Partnership must have real operational substance in Alberta, including physical premises, active management by general partner, employees involved in reverse logistics, and actual commercial risk allocation. A lack of economic substance can trigger application of the Canada–US treaty's Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clause, denial of treaty benefits, and reassessment under GAAR.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Omission of Income

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act, subsection 239(1), tax evasion is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment up to five years and fines up to 200% of the tax evaded; a summary conviction carries a fine up to 50% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to two years. Civil penalties under subsection 163(2) equal 50% of the understated tax. The CRA may also impose a 10% repeated-failure penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner of the limited partnership may be personally liable under the Income Tax Act, subsection 160(1) for transfers of property to non-arm's length persons, and under subsection 227.1 for unremitted payroll or withholding taxes. A director who knowingly participates in, or acquiesces to, tax evasion faces personal criminal liability, including imprisonment, fines, disqualification as a director, travel bans upon criminal charges, and civil restitution orders. The CRA may pierce the corporate veil of the corporate general partner if it is found to be a sham or used for a fraudulent purpose.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / TP Penalty

Statutory sanction: Penalty under subsection 247(4) of the Income Tax Act: CAD 24,000 for each failure, reduced to CAD 12,000 if the taxpayer co-operated and provided partial documentation. The CRA may reassess to reflect arm's length prices, increasing taxable income and Part XIII withholding, plus interest on arrears. In egregious cases, a 10% 'gross negligence' penalty under subsection 163(2) applies.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be liable for unpaid Part XIII withholding tax under subsection 227.1, including penalties and interest, where they fail to exercise care and diligence. Directors who knowingly sign false TP documentation face criminal liability for false statements under subsection 239(1). Travel bans are possible under Canada's criminal code for offenders facing indictment.
Shell Company / General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: GAAR (subsection 245(2)) allows the CRA to deny the tax benefit and recharacterize the transaction, resulting in additional tax, interest, and penalties up to 50% of the tax avoided. The Canada Revenue Agency may use the sham doctrine to disregard the partnership entirely for tax purposes, treating the non-resident partner as directly earning Canadian business income (creating a permanent establishment). Treaty benefits are denied under LOB clauses.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are exposed to personal liability for unremitted taxes (including reassessed amounts) if they knowingly participated in the avoidance arrangement. Civil fraud penalties under subsection 163(2) can be applied to the director of the general partner, and criminal fraud or conspiracy charges may result in imprisonment and travel bans.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), failure to register as an MSB or report suspicious transactions can trigger penalties up to CAD 500,000 for a corporation. Criminal laundering under the Criminal Code carries imprisonment up to 14 years and fines at the court's discretion; asset forfeiture under the Criminal Code and civil forfeiture statutes applies.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the general partner can be prosecuted personally for money laundering conspiracy, punishable by imprisonment up to 14 years. Directors may also be subject to civil forfeiture orders, travel bans, and professional disqualification. A director who knowingly allows the partnership to process third-party funds without AML compliance is personally liable for restitution to victims and regulatory penalties.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Part XIII Tax

Statutory sanction: The partnership is liable for the full amount of unwithheld tax, plus interest and penalties under subsection 227(8). The penalty is 10% of the deficiency if the failure to withhold was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence. Criminal charges for wilful failure to remit can lead to imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the unreported withholding tax under subsection 227.1, with no ability to rely on the due-diligence defence if they failed to supervise the financial officer. The CRA can issue director liability assessments against the personal assets of directors, and can enforce via garnishment, seizure, and travel bans on criminal charges.
