Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to Alberta limited partnerships; capital contributions are contractually defined in the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow account is mandated by Alberta statute, though banks may require escrow-in-trust arrangements for letter-of-credit-backed rail freight settlements.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC); Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal at canada.ca; Alberta Corporate Income Tax is administered federally by the CRA under a tax collection agreement, with no separate Alberta corporate tax registration portal.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Alberta: (1) verify GST/HST registration obligation under ETA s. 240 since the LP is a 'person' for GST/HST; (2) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 via CRA Business Registration Online or by mail, indicating partnership nature; (3) if required, open a GST/HST account referencing the BN; (4) provide certified evidence of legal existence of the foreign LP, the partnership agreement, and partner identification; (5) appoint a Canadian-representative for service (CRA Form RC59 for non-residents if applicable); (6) obtain a CRA-issued non-resident partnership account number; non-resident partners must separately supply their home-jurisdiction TIN equivalents on Form CRA-required schedules, and non-resident partners without Canadian TINs may need an individual/corporate TIN for withholding reporting.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Alberta: (1) verify GST/HST registration obligation under ETA s. 240 since the LP is a 'person' for GST/HST; (2) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 via CRA Business Registration Online or by mail, indicating partnership nature; (3) if required, open a GST/HST account referencing the BN; (4) provide certified evidence of legal existence of the foreign LP, the partnership agreement, and partner identification; (5) appoint a Canadian-representative for service (CRA Form RC59 for non-residents if applicable); (6) obtain a CRA-issued non-resident partnership account number; non-resident partners must separately supply their home-jurisdiction TIN equivalents on Form CRA-required schedules, and non-resident partners without Canadian TINs may need an individual/corporate TIN for withholding reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' entity election; a limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax with income allocated to partners under Income Tax Act s. 96, subject to the specified partnership rules. Available elections include: election under s. 96(9) to compute income separately; s. 93.1(1) and s. 108(1) elections for cross-border partnerships; designated partnership and 'specified partnership' elections affecting fiscal period; GST/HST elections under ETA s. 156 for net tax computation between closely related or financially interrelated registrants; provincial partnership elections in Alberta respecting deemed fiscal year and capital cost allowance pooling; hybrid mismatch-informed elections and filings under ITA ss. 18.9, 20.2, 95(2)(f)(ii) and related administrative positions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over a calendar quarter and not carrying on tax-non-resident small-supplier exceptions; non-resident suppliers of prescribed digital and intangibles services to Canadian consumers are deemed to carry on business and may be required to register under the simplified foreign supplier registration regime effective July 1, 2021 (Bill C-30), with no general small-supplier exemption for those remote suppliers unless below prescribed thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI, Part V and s. 1 if the goods are exported by the supplier as a condition of the supply and proof of export is retained; zero-rating also covers transportation services of exported goods (ETA Sch. VI ss. 7, 23, 24), freight-forwarding services in respect of exported goods (Sch. VI s. 8), and related transshipment/intermodal rail services for goods exported promptly from Canada. Offshore services supplied to non-residents relating to realty or tangible personal property located outside Canada may be zero-rated under Sch. VI s. 22. Transactions must be structured so the freight forwarding and expediting supply is directly attributable to goods exported from Canada to qualify at the 0% rate; otherwise domestic GST at 5% applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A or CBSA ACI/CCD e-declaration and cargo movement reference)
  • Bill of Lading / Through Bill of Lading or intermodal transport document
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and SWIFT wire evidence of consideration received
  • Customs broker or freight forwarder transactional certificates and rail waybill evidence of shipment routing from Alberta to an export gateway
  • Proof of export contemporaneous records accepted by CRA (e.g., CBSA stamped/gateway confirmation, rail interchange-to-destination records)

Canada has not implemented a mandatory real-time fiscal e-invoicing clearance regime with digital stamping; however, GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies above CRA thresholds must file returns and remit electronically (GST/HST NETFILE/TELEFILE and My Payment/My Business Account); CRA requires prescribed electronic records for zero-rating substantiation; a CRA consultative/implementation path to PEPPOL-based e-invoicing interoperability is anticipated; foreign suppliers under the simplified regime may be assessed on an estimated basis absent compliant electronic records.

CRA assesses exporters for unsubstantiated zero-rating and clawing back putative nil-rated treatment with GST at 5% plus interest, extending the limitation period where misrepresentation is grossly negligent; civil penalties up to the greater of CAD 25,000 or 25% of tax avoided under ETA s. 280 apply for gross negligence, s. 285 for false statements, and wilful evasion may lead to criminal sanction under ETA s. 327 with imprisonment and unlimited fines. CRA audit teams require a demonstrable correlation between intermodal rail services and actual export certificates, and retroactive reassessments plus clawbacks are sustained where the exporter fails to provide the mandatory export documentation bundle, referencing the doctrine of contemporaneous proof under the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full IMF Article VIII convertibility on both current and capital accounts. No exchange-control approval, no repatriation licence, and no profit-remittance ceiling apply to a Calgary LP. Inbound foreign capital is screened only under the Investment Canada Act (ICA) when it acquires control of an existing Canadian business; passive contributions to a new LP are exempt below the 2025 WTO-investor review threshold of C$1,089 million. Outbound profit distributions to non-resident limited partners are unrestricted but trigger Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding at 25% statutory (10-15% typical treaty-reduced rate) on income not attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirements in 1951 under the Currency Act and the Foreign Exchange and Currency Deposits Act. A rail freight forwarder may retain 100% of USD, EUR, or CNY export receipts in any bank account worldwide; there is no statutory conversion timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no Bank of Canada reporting duty on the exporter.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) holds central monetary authority; OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) supervises deposit-taking institutions; FINTRAC administers AML surveillance. Canada operates no standalone foreign-exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium (ancillary use only). Canada has no statutory 'EMI' licence class; a foreign electronic money institution (e.g., a UK FCA-authorized EMI or an MSB like Wise/Currencycloud) may operate cross-border only by registering with FINTRAC as a Money Service Business and partnering with a Schedule I bank for settlement. It cannot hold the LP's escrowed capital, issue commercial letters of credit, or open a C$ core account. Recommended architecture: use the EMI for low-value multi-currency collections and CNH market access, while the Capital Escrow Account and LC rails remain with RBC/BMO.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: The Alberta Partnership Act imposes no statutory audit on limited partnerships. A mandatory independent audit is triggered only where: (1) the partnership agreement or a lender or insurance covenant so requires; (2) the general partner is a reporting issuer or an Alberta/federal business corporation that fails the unanimous-shareholder audit-exemption threshold; (3) CRA demands audited financial statements to support the T5013; or (4) the LP qualifies as a mandatory T5013 partnership with aggregate property fair market value exceeding CAD 10 million.
