Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for Alberta limited partnerships. Partnership capital contributions are contractually fixed in the Declaration and subscription schedule. There is no escrow account requirement for ordinary partnership capital contributions.

Workplace clearance must be confirmed by an Alberta OHS-authorized safety officer under the Occupational Health and Safety Act and OHS Code, covering emergency evacuation, temperature monitoring, hazardous materials handling, and employeeet training.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online business portal: 'My Business Account' (Mon dossier d'entreprise); customs administration: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA); Alberta provincial registry: Service Alberta Corporate Registry.

E-government filing portal

Apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST account (account code RM0001) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1. An Alberta limited partnership is a separate 'person' for GST/HST and registers once it makes taxable supplies in Canada exceeding CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding calendar quarters; voluntary registration is permitted. The partnership does not file an income tax return but must file an annual T5013 Partnership Information Return and issue T5013 slips to partners; non-resident partners obtain Canadian tax identification as individuals/corporations for Part I self-assessment and Part XIII withholding. A foreign LP carrying on business in Alberta must also file an extra-provincial registration under the Partnership Act (Alberta) and an Alberta annual return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96; no federal or Alberta income tax at the LP level; income and loss flow through to partners. Alberta imposes no provincial corporate tax on the partnership. GST/HST applies at the partnership level as a distinct 'person' under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act; supplies are attributed to the LP for registration, collection, and ITC purposes. Non-resident partners remain taxable in Canada on Canadian-source partnership income and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on certain passive allocations.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Canadian law. The LP's flow-through status is statutory, subject to specific anti-deferral rules such as the SIFT regime (ITA s. 197) for publicly traded partnership interests, which can recharacterize the entity as a notional corporation. Relevant elections include: (i) GST/HST 'closely related' supply election (ETA s. 156) applicable between a corporation and a partnership where the corporation is closely related to all partners; (ii) reasonable partnership allocation agreements under ITA s. 103; (iii) non-resident elections under ITA s. 216 (Canadian real property net income) and ITA s. 217 (certain taxable benefits); and (iv) treaty-relief filings for reduced Part XIII withholding.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada within a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters ('small supplier' threshold, ETA s. 148); mandatory GST/HST registration once exceeded, with aggregation of closely related persons. Alberta levies no provincial sales tax, so the effective indirect rate in Alberta is 5% federal GST; HST at 13%-15% may arise on certain cross-province supplies under place-of-supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST/HST applies to: (i) supplies of goods exported from Canada by the supplier or recipient as soon after the sale as is reasonable (Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1, ETA); (ii) freight transportation services for goods moving from Canada to a place outside Canada (Part V, ss. 12-14); (iii) freight-arranging services (e.g., GDP pharma freight forwarding) where the underlying transportation is zero-rated (Part V, s. 14); and (iv) services supplied to a non-resident person if the service does not relate to Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada and is not rendered to an individual present in Canada (Part V, s. 7). The LP preserves full input tax credit (ITC) entitlement. Canada imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline for zero-rating purposes; the 180-day benchmark is a contractual/foreign-jurisdiction concept, not a Canadian legislative requirement.

  • Commercial invoice issued by the CA_LP logistics entity describing GDP pharma freight services
  • B13A Canadian Export Declaration filed electronically via CBSA CAERS for physical export legs
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air waybill) evidencing carriage to a non-Canadian destination
  • Signed service contract or purchase order from the non-resident principal
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent SWIFT/ACH settlement evidence
  • Health Canada GDP-compliant records: temperature excursion logs, chain-of-custody manifests, and delivery confirmation for medicinal goods

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime. Invoices may be issued electronically if they contain the prescribed information under the Input Tax Credit Information (GST/HST) Regulations: supplier's Business Number, invoice date, description of supply, and GST/HST rate or amount paid. Export documentation must be transmitted electronically to CBSA via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) / Canada's Single Window initiative; the CRA does not require pre-approval of invoices or API-based fiscal clearance for B2B supplies.

CRA may reassess an export zero-rating claim as taxable within the normal 4-year reassessment window (extended to 7 years in cases of misrepresentation or neglect), assess 5% GST on the full consideration, and add daily compound interest. Penalties include: ETA s. 285.1 for false statements or omissions - the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the tax evaded; late-filing penalties under s. 280; revocation of refunds and deemed-trust claims; joint and several liability of general partners for the LP's GST/HST debts; and criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ETA s. 327.1. The Voluntary Disclosure Program may provide relief only for non-deliberate failures; knowingly false export documentation precludes relief and exposes the LP to retrospective clawbacks plus gross-negligence penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely convertible floating currency under the Currency Act. Canada maintains no general exchange-control regime: current-account and capital-account movements (capital contributions, partnership loans, trade settlement, and profit/dividend repatriation) are unrestricted and do not require central bank approval. The Bank of Canada does not ration or convert currencies for private entities; market convertibility is supported by deep spot/forward interbank liquidity. The material constraints are non-FX in nature: FINTRAC reporting for international transfers over CAD 10,000 and structured transactions, OSFI sanctions-screening obligations using the Canadian Consolidated Autonomous Sanctions List, CRS/FATCA automatic exchange declarations, and Canadian tax characterization. For an Alberta Limited Partnership, the right of a non-resident limited partner to receive current or liquidating distributions is governed by the Alberta Partnership Act and the LPA, and such distributions may be remitted freely in any currency; however, an allocated Canadian-source business income share remains taxable in Canada under Part I (through the partnership) and may be subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding if the payment is legally a dividend, interest, royalty, or management fee rather than a pure partnership distribution of previously taxed income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, retention timetable, or repatriation deadline for merchandise or services receipts. A GDP pharma freight forwarder and logistics operator in Alberta may invoice in CAD or any foreign currency, may open foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks, and may retain 100% of export earnings without Bank of Canada involvement. Inbound freight and logistics service revenue is classified as non-merchandise export under Statistics Canada's BOP reporting, and the operational duties are limited to: (i) CBSA customs declaration accuracy for goods moving under a freight-forwarding bond; (ii) evidence-based trade documentation retention under PCMLTFA (five-year rule); and (iii) FINTRAC reporting of every international wire transfer exceeding CAD 10,000. No conversion ratio applies to an Alberta LP.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central monetary authority, operator of Lynx high-value payment system, and retail payment supervisor under the Retail Payment Activities Act; prudential banking supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision is exercised by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Administrative sanctions and entity screening are coordinated by Global Affairs Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act, the Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act, and the United Nations Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High as a treasury auxiliary, Low as a primary settlement rail. Canada does not license 'electronic money institutions' as a distinct bank class; non-bank payment service providers are regulated under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), must register with the Bank of Canada, and, if performing FX dealing or remittance, must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, and similar) may offer multi-currency wallets, expedited vendor payouts, and embedded FX conversion, but they do not hold Canadian deposits, are not CDIC-insured, and are not the equivalent of a Schedule I bank account. Such EMIs may be used for corridor-specific disbursements (e.g., southeast Asian cold-chain carriers or EU customs agents) provided the LP maintains a primary operating account at a Schedule I bank, sweeps wallet balances daily, and retains the full payment-reconciliation file to satisfy FINTRAC five-year record-keeping and bank 'know-your-customer's-customer' expectations. The KYC viability is highest with RBC and BMO, which accept MSB/EMI counterparty documentation in their commercial onboarding packages.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a non-resident partner capital contribution or partnership loan, route via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's primary CAD or USD account at the Schedule I bank. CAD credits settle in Canada through Lynx, the Bank of Canada high-value payment system; USD credits enter through the bank's US correspondent using CHIPS or Fedwire, with the Canadian bank maintaining a nostro account at the correspondent. Use ISO 20022 purpose code CAPC (Capital Contribution) or the corresponding SWIFT field 77T purpose element, and in field 70 enter: 'Contribution to Alberta LP [name], capital account per LPA Section [X], commitment schedule reference'; include the partnership's CRA program account and the partner's CRS/FATCA self-certified tax identification number. The bank will verify the capital contribution against the LPA, source-of-funds documentation, and UBO declarations; an Investment Canada Act analysis letter should confirm that the contribution is a non-controlling, portfolio-level partnership interest below activation thresholds (or file the applicable post-closing notification). No Bank of Canada approval is required; FINTRAC will receive the bank's report for any CAD 10,000+ international transaction, and the partnership must post the contribution to its capital-account ledger contemporaneously with the wire.Primary outbound rail: SWIFT MT103 international wire for cross-border disbursements to pharma carriers, cold-chain logistics depots, customs agents, and foreign maintenance vendors. For high-value or high-risk pharmaceutical lots, use a documentary Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the BMO, RBC, or TD trade desk, with the beneficiary's bank as advising bank; the LC must be compliant with UCP 600 and include GDP certification documents among the required presentation. Domestic Canadian disbursements (Alberta warehousing fees, CRA remittances, CBSA duties, and intra-provincial carrier payments) are cleared via Direct ACH credit through ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) under Payments Canada rules in CAD. USD-denominated payments inside Canada may be cleared by a Fedwire/CHIPS correspondent transfer or converted at the treasury desk at a same-day or tom-next rate. Every outbound payment must match the commercial invoice, B/L or air waybill, packing list, and the corresponding receivables/payables ledger, and must be pre-screened against the Canadian sanctions list and the bank's internal denied-party filter before release.Outright deliverable CAD/USD and CAD/EUR forward contracts (vanilla FX forwards) transacted with a Schedule I bank or its regulated brokerage affiliate, tied to forecast freight disbursements and capital repatriation schedulesCanadian-dollar / US-dollar foreign-exchange swaps (tom-next, spot-next, and one-week) used for settlement-date balancing between the LP's CAD operating account and USD disbursement accountsCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) permitted only where a non-resident partner has delivered a hard-currency term loan to the LP and the partnership needs to convert floating CAD liabilities into fixed USD obligationsVanilla FX options (CAD call/USD put and USD call/CAD put) purchased on a zero-premium or low-premium basis for corridor-specific freight payment exposure, with a maximum tenor of 12 months and no speculative resaleNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally not permissible for CAD-correlated exposure because CAD is a fully deliverable currency; an NDF is acceptable only for a third-currency emerging-market leg embedded in a freight contract (e.g., INR, PHP, or VND settlement) where CAD delivery into that jurisdiction is impossible, executed with an approved global bank counterparty and micro-hedged to the exact invoice amount

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High as a treasury auxiliary, Low as a primary settlement rail. Canada does not license 'electronic money institutions' as a distinct bank class; non-bank payment service providers are regulated under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), must register with the Bank of Canada, and, if performing FX dealing or remittance, must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, and similar) may offer multi-currency wallets, expedited vendor payouts, and embedded FX conversion, but they do not hold Canadian deposits, are not CDIC-insured, and are not the equivalent of a Schedule I bank account. Such EMIs may be used for corridor-specific disbursements (e.g., southeast Asian cold-chain carriers or EU customs agents) provided the LP maintains a primary operating account at a Schedule I bank, sweeps wallet balances daily, and retains the full payment-reconciliation file to satisfy FINTRAC five-year record-keeping and bank 'know-your-customer's-customer' expectations. The KYC viability is highest with RBC and BMO, which accept MSB/EMI counterparty documentation in their commercial onboarding packages.
