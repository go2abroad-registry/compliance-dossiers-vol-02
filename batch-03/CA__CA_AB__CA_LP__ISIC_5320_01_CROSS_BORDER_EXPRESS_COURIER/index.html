Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary business portal: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html) and 'GST/HST NETFILE' for indirect tax filing.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident person carrying on a commercial activity in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' directly with the CRA. No Alberta provincial tax registration is required for Limited Partnerships because Alberta does not impose a provincial value-added tax or retail sales tax; Alberta Corporate Registration under the Partnership Act is a separate legal-registry requirement not constituting a tax registration. The BN is the single statutory account identifier for GST/HST, payroll, corporate income tax, and import/export program accounts. A foreign Limited Partnership may register for the GST/HST account via Form RC1 or the 'Non-Resident Business Number Registration' service; CRA will issue a BN followed by '_RT' for the GST/HST program account. Foreign partners are not separately registered by the partnership for income tax; they report their allocated Canadian-source income through the partnership's annual information return (Form T5013).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election. The Limited Partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada): the partnership is not a taxpayer, and each partner's share of income, loss, and taxable capital gains is computed as if the partnership were a separate person and then attributed to the partner. Non-resident limited partners are taxable in Canada on their Canadian-source business income unless relieved by treaty. Elective mechanisms relevant to this structure include: (i) Article IV(6) of the Canada-US Tax Treaty and similar treaty provisions that permit partners to elect to file as a corporation if the partnership is also eligible for equivalent US check-the-box treatment, where applicable; (ii) subsection 216(4) election by non-resident partners to compute Canadian tax on rental income on a net basis (not applicable to express courier business income); (iii) GST/HST subsection 156(1) election to treat closely related corporations as a single person for GST/HST purposes (limited relevance to a partnership); and (iv) hybrid mismatch elections under Part 6.5 and section 18.2 of the Income Tax Act to align deduction-timing outcomes for instruments characterized differently in Canada and a foreign jurisdiction (relevant only if the LP issues hybrid instruments).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for both current and capital account purposes. Canada maintains no general system of exchange controls or prior notification regime for foreign direct investment, though non-resident dispostions of Canadian business assets may trigger Investment Canada Act review. Profit and capital remittances are generally unrestricted, but dividends paid by a Canadian partnership beneficiary to a non-resident partner are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the federal Income Tax Act unless treaty-reduced. Limited partnership distributions to non-resident partners must be processed through the Canadian banking system without Bank of Canada approval, while maintaining accurate NR4/T5013 reporting and Canada Revenue Agency forms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation in Canada to surrender export proceeds, file within any timeline, or convert a fixed ratio of export receipts to Canadian dollars. Exporters and service providers, including cross-border express courier operators, may hold CAD or designated foreign currency accounts. Customs and trade documentation obligations, including Form B13 export declarations and underlying shipping manifests, must be retained. Transfer pricing rules apply to related-party cross-border service and freight revenue arrangements.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada, acting with the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering enforcement, as no standalone Foreign Exchange Administration body exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a standalone treasury rail. A foreign electronic money institution (EMI) is not considered a Canadian Schedule I or Schedule II bank and cannot accept deposits in Canada, issue Canadian deposit liabilities, or provide the same clearing and payment guarantees as a regulated bank. Foreign EMIs could theoretically operate as money service businesses under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and as retail payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act once registered with the Bank of Canada, but enhanced due diligence, Canadian nexus requirements, and transaction volume constraints make them suitable only as a supplemental collection or remittance channel. A Schedule I Canadian bank is mandatory for capital escrow, liquidity buffers, and SWIFT clearing.

Compliant routing for non-resident LP capital injections should use an internationally recognized SWIFT MT103 wire with the beneficiary account held by the Alberta LP at a Schedule I bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Pass-through entity: no Alberta or federal corporate income tax at the LP level. Corporate partners taxed at combined federal + Alberta general corporate rate of 23%; individual partners taxed at Alberta progressive rates up to 48.0% (2025 combined top marginal).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory rate under ITA Part XIII for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation. An Alberta LP is fiscally transparent: ordinary profit distributions to non-resident partners are not dividends and are not subject to Part XIII withholding, but rather are allocated as Canadian business income taxable under Part I on a net basis, subject to treaty PE relief. A distribution out of invested capital or a guaranteed payment characterized as interest may trigger separate withholding rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions with the same non-resident person in a taxation year — exceeding this triggers the mandatory T106 filing threshold. In addition, CRA expects contemporaneous Local File/Master File documentation for any controlled transaction regardless of size when a transfer-pricing adjustment or penalty is contemplated, and the documentation must be prepared before the tax return filing deadline.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, the ITA's general anti-avoidance rule (s.245) plus judicial substance-over-form doctrines apply. CRA will deny deductions, reallocate income, and impose penalties where an Alberta LP is interposed without real operations, employees, aviation/logistics assets, or Canadian premises, or where the sole purpose is to manufacture treaty benefits or avoid permanent establishment characterization. Cross-border express courier operations must evidence actual Canadian substance: owned/leased aircraft, sorting depots, delivery fleet, consignor/consignee contracts, regulatory permits, and active payroll management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under ITA s.239: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; arrears interest compounded daily plus all provincial/federal penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions (ITA s.227.1) and unremitted GST/HST (Excise Tax Act s.323) if they consented, participated, or failed to exercise due diligence. Limited partners participating in management or control can be personally liable for partnership obligations, and criminal prosecution can lead to travel restrictions, passport seizure, and imprisonment of the controlling person.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Sham Documentation

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(3) transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment, applicable where the gross transfer-pricing adjustment exceeds C$5 million and the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to establish arm's length prices. CRA may also disallow the deduction and reallocate income, with interest and penalties from the original filing date.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the Alberta LP is directly liable for the LP's tax debts and penalties; directors of a corporate general partner may be held liable where they directed the sham transfer-pricing scheme. Criminal liability for fraud or forgery of documentation can trigger imprisonment, cross-border arrest warrants, and travel bans while under investigation.
Non-Resident Withholding and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Payer is liable for the full unreported Part XIII withholding amount (25% of gross payments), plus interest, civil penalties, and failure-to-withhold fines. Treaty benefits may be denied under the principal-purpose test, with retroactive reassessment and potential GAAR penalties. Repeat offenders face prosecution and fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the general partner can be held personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes if they knowingly failed to withhold. Veil-piercing applies where the LP is a sham or a mere instrumentality; personal bankruptcy of the controlling director, travel bans, and immigration holds can be pursued in serious criminal cases.
Money Laundering / AML and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under PCMLTFA up to C$3,000,000 for corporations and individuals, with imprisonment up to five years for serious non-compliance, failure to report a suspicious transaction, or false statements. Federal sanctions violations can result in fines up to C$1,000,000 and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be personally liable for corporate fines where they knowingly participated in AML failures or sanctioned deliveries. For a limited partnership, the general partner's directors face criminal accountability; travel bans and extradition are available where money laundering is linked to cross-border organized crime.
