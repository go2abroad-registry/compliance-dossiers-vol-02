Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Business Registration Online (BRO) - https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services/business-owner-registration-online.html

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first register the Alberta Limited Partnership with the Alberta Corporate Registry (CorpRegistry) to receive a provincial incorporation/registration certificate. Then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1; for GST/HST, activate the GST/HST account under the same BN. Non-resident entities with no Canadian physical presence must appoint a resident agent for service of notice. The BN is issued in real time; GST/HST registration is either effective on the date of exceeding the small supplier threshold or the requested effective date. Required documents include partnership agreement, proof of Alberta registered address, and identification of the General Partner.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal income tax; the Alberta LP is mandatorily fiscally transparent for both federal and Alberta income taxes. Hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act (ss. 18.4-18.5) apply automatically without elective measures. Under GST/HST, a partnership may make certain elections with members (e.g., section 273 election for closely related parties) but none alter the fundamental pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

30,000 CAD - small supplier threshold measured on worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters or in a single quarter; non-residents carrying on business in Canada must register once the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is available before exceeding the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST) applies to supplies of goods physically exported from Canada in accordance with normal commercial practice, provided documentary proof of export is retained. Services supplied to a non-resident that are performed wholly outside Canada (or supplied for use outside Canada in limited cases) may also be zero-rated. For condiment and perishable hotpot supplies, the goods must be exported prior to consumption or use in Canada, and the supplier must hold sufficient evidence that the goods left Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (via CBSA ACI/AMS or Canada Border Export Declaration manifest)
  • Bill of Lading or other carriage/documentary evidence of international shipping (e.g., airway bill, ocean bill, Cargo/Freight receipt)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank payment/SWIFT evidence proving settlement by the non-resident purchaser

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing mandate, or digital stamping requirement exists at the federal or Alberta level. Electronic invoices compliant with the Excise Tax Regulations (prescribed information) are acceptable. The Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing framework is voluntary and interoperable with business-to-business exchanges.

CRA reviews export claims for adequate evidence. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST with interest from the date the tax would have been payable; penalties include a 6% negligence penalty or 100% gross negligence penalty for false statements, and a third-party penalty of 500 CAD to 10,000 CAD under Excise Tax Act s. 327.1. Four-year limitation period for reassessment applies unless misrepresentation, in which case it is unlimited. Note: Canada does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline for GST/HST zero-rating; the 180-day figure is an internal compliance benchmark for export contract terms and documentary evidence.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally restrict foreign ownership, capital contributions, dividend distributions, partner distributions, loan repayments, interest payments, or repatriation of lawful business profits. No statutory approval is normally required for an Alberta limited partnership to receive foreign capital or remit funds abroad. Practical hurdles are bank-level source-of-funds, beneficial-ownership, sanctions, tax-residency, transfer-pricing, withholding-tax, and transaction-purpose reviews. Payments to non-residents may require Canadian withholding tax analysis, including possible Part XIII withholding on interest, royalties, management fees, or other specified amounts, subject to treaty relief and documentation. The partnership must maintain records supporting capital-account movements, allocations, related-party transactions, and the commercial purpose of each remittance. Large or unusual international transfers may trigger enhanced due diligence, regulatory reporting, or temporary payment holds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline for converting foreign-currency receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the partnership's discretion. The partnership should retain invoices, customs records where applicable, contracts, shipping or delivery evidence, payment confirmations, and reconciliation records. Financial institutions may request transaction-purpose information under their risk-based AML programs. Currency conversion gains and losses should be recorded under the applicable accounting framework, and tax reporting must reflect the relevant Canadian-dollar measurement and foreign-exchange consequences.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no separate foreign-exchange administration imposing general exchange controls or mandatory export-proceeds surrender. Relevant supplementary authorities include the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision, the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for tax reporting and withholding, and the Department of Finance Canada for federal financial-sector policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary collections, foreign-currency wallets, and payment administration; Low as a substitute for a primary Canadian operating bank account. A foreign electronic-money institution is not automatically authorized to accept deposits, provide payment services to Canadian end users, or conduct regulated retail payment activities in Canada. The provider must be appropriately licensed or registered in each relevant jurisdiction and may be subject to Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act and Bank of Canada registration requirements where its activities fall within the statutory scope. Funds should not be represented as Canadian deposit-insured balances unless held through an eligible deposit-taking institution. The partnership should verify safeguarding, insolvency treatment, Canadian availability, sanctions controls, settlement geography, data access, chargeback procedures, and the provider's authority to serve Canadian merchants. Use of a foreign EMI does not remove Canadian tax, AML, books-and-records, consumer-payment, or foreign-exchange reporting obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route capital through a Canadian commercial-bank account held in the legal name of the limited partnership or its authorized general partner. Use a dedicated capital or funding sub-account where available; Canada does not generally require a statutory capital escrow account for an Alberta limited partnership. Before remittance, provide the bank with the executed limited partnership agreement, contribution or loan instrument, ownership chart, beneficial-owner information, source-of-funds evidence, expected amount and currency, remitter identity, and intended use. Use SWIFT MT103 or the bank's equivalent cross-border wire instruction, with accurate originator, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, and payment-purpose information. There is no universal Canadian FX purpose-code regime; use the receiving bank's internal transaction-purpose classification and state 'partner capital contribution', 'partner loan', or another accurate purpose. Reconcile the receipt to the partnership capital or loan ledger, retain the SWIFT confirmation and FX ticket, and verify whether any related-party interest, fee, or other remittance requires withholding-tax analysis. Do not route capital through personal accounts, unrelated third parties, nominee accounts, or payment processors without documented legal and commercial justification.Use Canadian bank wires, SWIFT MT103, domestic EFT, Canadian high-value payment rails where supported by the bank, and card or merchant-acquiring settlement for ordinary restaurant operations. For imports or material equipment purchases, use a commercial letter of credit, documentary collection, or open-account wire subject to counterparty risk approval. A commercial LC should identify the applicant, issuing bank, beneficiary, amount, currency, expiry, required documents, Incoterms, inspection or insurance requirements, and sanctions restrictions. Match every payment to an approved purchase order, invoice, contract, customs or shipping document where applicable, and verified beneficiary account. SWIFT messaging does not itself establish trade compliance; the partnership remains responsible for sanctions screening, import requirements, tax treatment, fraud controls, and accurate accounting. For recurring suppliers, use dual approval, beneficiary-change call-back verification, payment limits, segregation of duties, and daily bank-to-ledger reconciliation. No general Canadian FX approval or export-proceeds conversion is required for ordinary trade payments.Deliverable CAD/foreign-currency forwards with an authorized Canadian bank or appropriately regulated dealer, supported by an identified underlying exposure, documented hedge policy, counterparty limits, and confirmation procedures.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies or markets where physical delivery is restricted or impractical, subject to the dealer's jurisdictional authority, applicable derivatives documentation, sanctions controls, collateral terms, and evidence of the underlying commercial exposure.Currency swaps or cross-currency swaps for recurring funding, intercompany loans, or longer-term currency liabilities, subject to ISDA or equivalent documentation, credit support terms, valuation controls, and accounting treatment.Foreign-currency options, collars, and participating forwards may be used where commercially justified and approved under the partnership's risk policy; premiums, break costs, leverage, and liquidity requirements must be documented.Foreign-currency bank accounts and natural hedging through matching foreign-currency receipts and payments are generally permissible. Hedging should not be used for speculative trading unrelated to the partnership's commercial exposures, and derivative confirmations should be reconciled to exposure registers and financial-statement disclosures.

Turnover: The Alberta Partnerships Act imposes no mandatory audit solely by virtue of LP status; independent audit becomes compulsory when: (i) the LP is a reporting issuer under applicable securities legislation; (ii) the partnership agreement or unanimous partner resolution requires audited statements; (iii) a lender, regulator, or the CRA demands audited financials; or (iv) the LP is party to regulated franchise or licensing arrangements requiring audited operating results, such as master franchise reporting covenants.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate rate (15% federal Part I.1 + 8% Alberta) applied at the partner level; the Alberta Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent (pass-through) and therefore no entity-level CIT. For a Canadian-controlled private corporate partner eligible for the Small Business Deduction, the combined rate on active income is 11% (9% federal + 2% Alberta).
