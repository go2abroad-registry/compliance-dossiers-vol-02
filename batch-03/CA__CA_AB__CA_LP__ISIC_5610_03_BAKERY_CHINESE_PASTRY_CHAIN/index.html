Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Alberta limited partnerships have no statutory minimum paid-up capital; each limited partner must contribute cash or property (not services) in an amount stated in the Declaration under s 51. No escrow account is legally mandated, but capital calls and escrow terms may be imposed by the Limited Partnership Agreement.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST registration and Business Number portal (My Business Account); Alberta Corporate Registry for extra-provincial limited partnership registration and Alberta corporate income tax administered under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act through the CRA

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Verify the limited partnership’s extra-provincial registration in Alberta and obtain Alberta corporate registry filings where applicable. Step 2: Determine if GST/HST registration is mandatory based on Canadian taxable supply volumes or voluntary registration needs. Step 3: Complete Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Accounts' through CRA’s Business Registration Online, by mail, or through an authorized representative. Step 4: For a limited partnership, list the partnership as a new GST/HST registrant and identify the general partner and Canadian service address; CRA will issue a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with an RT-program GST/HST account. Step 5: Register for any other CRA programs required (e.g., payroll RP account if hiring staff). There is no separate Alberta provincial tax registration number: Alberta corporate income tax is reported and administered through CRA using the same BN, while municipal/regulatory licenses are handled separately.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; an Alberta limited partnership is statutorily transparent for income tax and cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation. Each partner is allocated income/loss under sections 96-103 of the Income Tax Act. For cross-border hybrid mismatch purposes, no domestic hybrid-mismatch election is available; however, a partnership may elect to become a GST/HST registrant and may make section 96(3) partnership-year elections. For non-resident partners, Canadian legal classification governs, subject to treaty provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 per consecutive 12-month period (small supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s. 148); for non-resident suppliers carrying on business in Canada, the same threshold generally applies, but mandatory registration may arise for certain non-resident digital suppliers or where input tax credits are claimed; registration is available voluntarily from first taxable supply

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported 'directly' by the supplier or on its behalf are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. The bakery and central kitchen must demonstrate through documentary evidence that the goods were sold to a non-resident and physically removed from Canada, with no use or consumption inside Canada. Exported bakery products, including Chinese pastries for international franchisees, qualify for 0% GST, and full input tax credits may be claimed for related inputs. Services supplied to non-residents that relate to an export sale or the business of the non-resident and are not performed primarily in Canada are also zero-rated. Domestic sales of certain basic groceries may be zero-rated, but take-out or restaurant supplies remain taxable at 5% GST.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Canadian Customs Form B13A or electronic export declaration (Canadian Export Declaration/CAED) evidencing the exporter, description, quantity, and destination of goods
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent shipping document (airway bill, rail waybill, Cargo Control Document) showing exporter and consignee with final destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/SWIFT credit documentation proving that export proceeds were received in Canada and repatriated within the statutory period

No federal real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal digital stamping, or API clearance mandate exists in Canada. GST/HST invoices may be issued electronically, and CRA accepts electronic records if they are complete, legible, and accessible. Registrants must retain invoices and supporting records as required under Excise Tax Act s. 286, but no government pre-clearance of invoices is required.

CRA may reassess and recover the uncollected GST/HST, impose arrears interest, and apply statutory penalties where a zero-rating claim is not substantiated or is based on false documentation. For knowingly false or misleading statements, CRA can assert a penalty under the Excise Tax Act of the greater of CAD 5,000 and 50% of the amount by which the refund amount was overstated. Gross negligence in claiming export refunds may also trigger the Income Tax Act s. 163.2 penalty. In clear cases of fraudulent export zero-rating, CRA may recommend criminal prosecution under s. 327 of the Excise Tax Act, with fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment, plus retrospective clawback of the export refund with no limitation within the normal reassessment period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, tax requirements, and transaction-specific restrictions. An Alberta limited partnership may generally receive foreign capital, pay bona fide suppliers and royalties abroad, and remit distributions without a general central-bank authorization. Banks will require source-of-funds, beneficial-ownership, transaction-purpose, and supporting-contract evidence. Partnership distributions remain subject to applicable Canadian and Alberta tax, withholding, filing, and non-resident-partner rules; tax clearance or withholding may be required depending on the income and recipient. A non-resident partner may have Canadian tax filing and payment obligations. Confirm the partnership's tax characterization and any withholding with Canadian tax counsel.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general Canadian statutory deadline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. Export proceeds may generally be retained in Canadian or foreign currency and held in Canadian or foreign accounts, subject to contractual, tax, sanctions, bank-compliance, and applicable statistical-reporting requirements. There is no general requirement to repatriate or convert export earnings.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general exchange-control authority imposing routine foreign-exchange approval, mandatory conversion, or export-proceeds surrender. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers applicable anti-money-laundering reporting obligations; it is not the central bank. Statistics Canada administers specified international transactions reporting under the Statistics Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment processing and certain cross-border transfers; low as a substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking institution for core operating deposits, lending, or complex treasury. An offshore electronic-money institution is not automatically authorized to accept deposits or provide regulated payment services in Canada. Verify Canadian registration and applicable Retail Payment Activities Act obligations, safeguarding arrangements, settlement access, currency coverage, and account ownership before use. Maintain a Canadian bank account for payroll, tax payments, POS settlement, and contingency liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Receive contributions into an account titled in the Alberta limited partnership's legal name, using a bank wire through correspondent institutions and the bank's accepted SWIFT instructions. Provide the sender with the beneficiary name, account number, institution and transit details, SWIFT/BIC where applicable, and a precise remittance narrative such as "limited partnership capital contribution" or "partner loan under agreement dated [date]." Canada has no universal FX purpose-code system for ordinary inward investment; do not invent or reuse a foreign jurisdiction's purpose code. Provide the bank with the partnership agreement, contribution or loan documentation, investor identity, source-of-funds evidence, and ownership information before or promptly after receipt. Distinguish equity-like contributions from debt in the legal documents, books, tax reporting, and payment narrative. Resolve intermediary-bank fees, currency conversion instructions, and beneficiary-charge arrangements in advance. Maintain records supporting any applicable Statistics Canada reporting and tax filings.Use bank-initiated SWIFT payments, commonly MT103-format customer credit transfers, for royalties, imported ingredients, equipment, and other documented cross-border payables. Match each transfer to an approved invoice, contract, purchase order, and delivery or service evidence; verify beneficiary changes by an independently authenticated channel. For higher-value or documentary-risk purchases, arrange a commercial documentary letter of credit through the Canadian bank, with terms, documents, and expiry aligned to the supply contract. Consider documentary collections or bank transfer for established counterparties. Apply sanctions screening, fraud controls, beneficiary validation, and tax/withholding review before release. No general Canadian foreign-exchange approval or export-proceeds conversion rule applies; the sending bank may nevertheless request purpose and supporting documents under its compliance policies.
