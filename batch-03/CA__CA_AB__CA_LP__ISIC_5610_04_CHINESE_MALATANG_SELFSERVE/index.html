Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax administration; Business Registration Online / My Business Account portal for TIN and GST/HST program accounts; Alberta Corporate Registry for provincial partnership registration under the Partnerships Act.

E-government filing portal

Register the Alberta Limited Partnership with the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain the legal provincial registration. Then apply to the CRA for a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or Form RC1, using the LP as the registration type. The BN is the statutory tax identifier; open a separate GST/HST program account (BN suffix 'RT') if taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold. Non-resident partners are identified through CRA non-resident withholding accounts, and the LP should provide CRA with the partnership agreement and partner ownership details as requested.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Register the Alberta Limited Partnership with the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain the legal provincial registration. Then apply to the CRA for a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or Form RC1, using the LP as the registration type. The BN is the statutory tax identifier; open a separate GST/HST program account (BN suffix 'RT') if taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold. Non-resident partners are identified through CRA non-resident withholding accounts, and the LP should provide CRA with the partnership agreement and partner ownership details as requested.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian tax law. The Alberta LP is statutorily fiscally transparent for income tax purposes, with income allocated among partners pursuant to the partnership agreement and subject to CRA reasonableness reallocation. There is no hybrid-mismatch election; Canada's anti-hybrid rules in the Income Tax Act apply automatically. For GST/HST purposes the LP itself is a registrant and is treated as a distinct person.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the last four consecutive calendar quarters or in the current quarter; a registrant must charge and remit GST/HST if this threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated where the goods are exported from Canada by the supplier or a non-resident recipient and documentary evidence of export is retained. A supply of services or intangible property (including franchise royalty and licence rights) to a non-resident is generally zero-rated if the supply is not in respect of Canadian real property, tangible property located in Canada, or a service performed in Canada. For this fast-casual franchise master licensing model, royalty payments received from foreign franchisees for IP used wholly outside Canada are eligible for the 0% GST/HST rate. The supplier must obtain commercial evidence of the non-resident status of the recipient and of the foreign use of the licensed rights.

  • CBSA export declaration or customs export documentation for tangible goods shipped from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing foreign destination, consignee, and date of export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire confirmation evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from the non-resident party
  • Executed franchise or master licence agreement and royalty schedule substantiating the IP royalty stream
  • CRA-acceptable non-resident status evidence and foreign-use documentation for intangible property

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime. CRA accepts paper and electronic invoices, but each GST/HST invoice must contain prescribed information such as supplier name, BN, date, description, and tax amount. There is no national e-invoicing clearance mandate for this industry.

CRA may audit export zero-rating claims and reverse any amount not substantiated with contemporaneous export, payment, and non-resident-use records. Reversed amounts become unremitted GST/HST, subject to interest and penalties; gross-negligence penalties can reach 50% of the tax understatement, and fraudulent claims may lead to criminal prosecution. CRA may also reassess outside the normal limitation period where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation. Export documentation and inward remittance evidence should be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally impose exchange controls, capital controls, mandatory repatriation, or prior approval for foreign ownership, capital contributions, dividend payments, royalty payments, loan repayments, or ordinary commercial remittances. Transfers remain subject to bank sanctions screening, beneficial-ownership verification, source-of-funds checks, anti-money-laundering requirements, tax withholding, transfer-pricing rules, partnership allocation rules, and transaction-specific regulatory requirements. A Canadian limited partnership is generally tax-transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes, but the partnership agreement, Alberta partnership law, the partners' residence, and applicable treaty and withholding rules determine the tax treatment of distributions and allocations. Non-resident remittances may require withholding, including on certain Canadian-source interest, dividends, royalties, management fees, rents, or other amounts. Payments to non-residents should be reviewed under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, treaty relief, information-reporting rules, and transfer-pricing documentation requirements. Large international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, or multiple related transfers meeting the statutory threshold within the applicable period, may be reportable by the financial institution to FINTRAC. Cross-border currency or monetary-instrument transportation exceeding CAD 10,000 must generally be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring exporters to convert or repatriate foreign-currency receipts. The partnership may retain eligible foreign-currency proceeds in Canadian or offshore accounts, subject to contractual banking terms, tax reporting, sanctions compliance, books-and-records obligations, and applicable foreign-account reporting. Exporters should document the commercial purpose, counterparty, invoice, shipping or service evidence, beneficial ownership, and foreign-exchange rate applied to each receipt. Foreign-currency transactions must be translated and reported for Canadian tax and accounting purposes under the applicable financial reporting framework. If the business imports goods, customs, GST, provincial tax, and trade-documentation requirements apply separately from foreign-exchange rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration requiring routine approval of ordinary commercial foreign-exchange transactions. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions supervises federally regulated banks; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada administers anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist-financing reporting obligations; the Canada Border Services Agency and Canada Revenue Agency administer specified cross-border reporting and tax rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment collection, foreign-exchange execution, and international transfers; Low to Medium as the sole operating bank. Canada does not use the European Economic Area electronic-money-institution passporting regime. An offshore digital EMI generally cannot directly provide regulated Canadian deposit-taking, payment, money-services, or stored-value services to Canadian customers merely by holding an offshore licence. Viability depends on the provider's Canadian registration or licensing position, its contractual banking partners, permitted client categories, safeguarding model, settlement location, sanctions controls, and treatment of client funds. A Canadian money services business must generally register with FINTRAC and comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Payment services may also be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act and Bank of Canada supervision when the provider falls within its scope. The LP should maintain a Canadian bank account for payroll, rent, taxes, merchant settlement, and regulatory continuity, and use a fintech only after confirming Canadian legal coverage, funds safeguarding, withdrawal rights, fee transparency, data access, and business-account eligibility.
