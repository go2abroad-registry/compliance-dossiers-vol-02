Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - GST/HST Program and Business Registration Online (BRO) portal accessible through CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Alberta has no provincial sales tax or provincial VAT authority for indirect taxes; provincial corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via CRA's Business Registration Online portal or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts). Step 2: Activate the GST/HST program account by adding the two-letter program account identifier 'RT' to the BN, creating a BN/RT registration number. Step 3: For a foreign-owned Alberta Limited Partnership, the general partner must submit the registration, certify the partnership is carrying on commercial activity in Canada, and be prepared to provide the Alberta Partnership Registration certificate, the limited partnership agreement, and evidence of the LP's Canadian permanent establishment. Step 4: CRA issues the GST/HST registration effective from the date commercial activity commenced or from the requested effective date; the LP then files GST/HST returns via GST34 or CRA online services. Step 5: Obtain an Alberta corporate tax program account (CRA-administered) where non-resident partners have Canadian-source allocable income subject to Alberta's flat 8% provincial corporate rate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The Alberta LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act section 96: the LP itself does not pay federal or provincial income tax; partnership income, gains, losses, and deductions are allocated directly to partners according to the partnership agreement. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is a separate registrant and must charge GST when it carries on commercial activity in Canada above the small supplier threshold.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election for entity classification; partnerships are statutorily flow-through. Available elections include: (1) Income Tax Act section 97(2) election to transfer partnership property to a taxable Canadian corporation on a tax-deferred rollover; (2) Income Tax Act section 22 election for transfer of accounts receivable; (3) Excise Tax Act (ETA) section 156 election treating supplies between closely related registrants as made for nil consideration; (4) ETA section 167 joint election on supply of a business as a going concern; (5) ETA section 150 election to value a non-arm's-length supply at cost; (6) ETA section 273 election applicable to financial institutions. Hybrid mismatch arrangements involving partnerships are constrained by Income Tax Act sections 92 to 94 and 603.1 (hybrid mis-match rules), but no elective classification mechanism exists for a Canadian LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the ETA section 148(1)(c) small supplier threshold. A person making taxable supplies exceeding CAD 30,000 in a current calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (worldwide taxable supplies) must register for GST/HST. A specialty tea salon lounge with retail sales above this level must hold an active BN/RT account. Non-resident vendors and digital-platform operators supplying taxable goods or services in Canada may be subject to mandatory registration under the simplified non-resident GST/HST framework even below the complaint threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies eligible for 0% GST: (1) tangible personal property (TTP) exported by the supplier as part of the supply - ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 1, where the supplier ships or delivers TTP to a destination outside Canada or delivers TTP to a common carrier for export and retains proof of export; (2) services supplied to non-residents - ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7, where the service is not performed in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, is not rendered to an individual present in Canada, and is not a specified personal or literary/artistic service; (3) intangible personal property supplied to non-residents - ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 10. On-premises tea service, prepared beverages, and salon consumption are taxable at 5% GST. Only packaged loose-leaf tea and ceramic teaware shipped outbound by the LP to non-resident customers at a destination outside Canada may qualify for zero-rating, provided documentary proof of physical export is retained.

  • B13A Canada Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A) - mandatory for commercial goods exported from Canada, with electronic filing through the Canadian Export Reporting System (for goods valued over CAD 2,500 or as required by the Customs Act);
  • Commercial Invoice in English or French containing full description, quantity, unit price, currency, terms of sale, and Canadian 10-digit HS classification;
  • Original transport document - ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or highway/truck waybill with CBSA released export confirmation for land-border shipments;
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange receipt evidencing receipt and settlement of export proceeds in the LP's Canadian bank account;
  • GST/HST zero-rated supply reconciliation schedule, shipping manifest, courier tracking trail, and carrier invoice retained under ETA section 286

Canada operates no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control regime, and no digital stamping requirement. CRA does not mandate live government API validation of invoices. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework is voluntary and based on the PEPPOL business-to-government standard; public-sector purchasers commonly require PEPPOL-format E-115 XML e-invoices through Canada's national e-invoicing service. The tea lounge POS must nonetheless retain complete books and records in English or French (ETA section 286; Income Tax Act section 230) and must be able to produce electronic records in a readable and accessible format upon CRA audit.

Canada does not impose a standalone statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is applied as a conservative commercial benchmark requiring export proceeds to be settled, credited to Canada, and fully documented within the normal invoicing cycle. Penalties for improper export zero-rating: ETA section 285(1) imposes a 25% penalty on the amount by which a claimed refund or remittance exceeds the GST properly payable, increasing to 50% where the claimant acted knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence; prescribed-rate interest accrues on unreported or under-remitted net tax under ETA section 280; refunds and credits may be reassessed or clawed back within the normal four-year reassessment period, which is indefinitely extended in cases of misrepresentation or fraud under ETA section 298; criminal prosecution for tax evasion under ETA section 327 can result in fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment of up to five years on indictment; moreover, the CRA may hold the general partner jointly and severally liable for partnership GST/HST liabilities and deny zero-rating entirely where documentary proof of export is incomplete.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII (accepted 1952). The Canadian dollar (CAD, ISO 4217: CAD) floats freely with Bank of Canada policy-rate management but no exchange-control licensing, no surrender obligations, and no prior approval for profit remittance, royalty payments, or capital repatriation. An Alberta LP may hold foreign-currency accounts at any Canadian chartered bank without regulatory restriction; the only applicable frictions are CRA Part XIII withholding (25% standard, treaty-reduced) on non-resident partner distributions, T5013/NR4 reporting, and foreign-property reporting (T1135) where specified foreign property exceeds the cost threshold

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory window for bringing export receipts onshore; receipts may be retained in CAD or any foreign currency. FINTRAC Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report (Form E667/E677) applies to monetary instruments at or above CAD 10,000; otherwise treasury must document the source of all commercial receipts for CRA audit and Alberta Partnership Act record-keeping standards

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central bank, monetary authority, and retail-payment supervisor under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of deposit-taking institutions; FINTRAC as the AML/CFT transaction-reporting authority; Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (supplementary rail only). Canada does not recognize the EU EMI licence; a fintech provider must be registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and registered under the Bank of Canada RPAA regime. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All partner capital contributions must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire for US-origin) to the primary bank's CAD account. Canada uses no IBAN; remitters must use the beneficiary bank's SWIFT BIC, institution number (3 digits), transit number (5 digits), and account number (7-12 digits). Field 70 must state 'PARTNER CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — [partner legal name] — ALBERTA LP'; field 71A must be 'OUR' to absorb correspondent fees and guarantee net settlement; USD-denominated injections should first arrive in the LP's USD sub-account to avoid forced conversion. Treasury must add an MT199-style dual call-back verification to defeat business-email-compromise wire fraud. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined Alberta/federal general corporate rate (15% federal + 8% Alberta); 11% combined rate if a Canadian-controlled private corporate (CCPC) partner qualifies for the small business deduction on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident recipient. Since an Alberta Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent, ordinary partnership profit allocations are not subject to dividend withholding tax; however, any payment characterized as a dividend, or a distribution following a corporate status election, will be treated under the statutory 25% Part XIII rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not prescribe a standalone CAD threshold for a Local File or Master File. Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed when the aggregate fair market value of reportable cross-border transactions with a non-resident non-arm's-length person exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750 million are subject to Country-by-Country Reporting. Every taxpayer, regardless of size, must maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under s. 247(4) to avoid penalties if a transfer-pricing adjustment is made.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulation regime comparable to UAE or BVI ESR. The functional equivalent is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), the sham doctrine, partnership anti-avoidance rules, and CRA's permanent-establishment guidelines. To sustain an Alberta Limited Partnership, the entity must maintain real economic substance: a leased physical tea salon, full-time employees, independently negotiated supplier contracts, a bank account, business records, and genuine management decisions in Alberta. A paper-only LP with no actual activity or personnel is subject to recharacterization, GAAR reassessment, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under ITA s. 239 carries a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years; civil reassessment includes the full understated tax, daily compound interest, and a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understatement under ITA s. 163(2).

Director personal liability: For an Alberta Limited Partnership, the general partner is personally liable for partnership debts and tax obligations; a corporate general partner's directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions under ITA s. 227.1. Individuals who authorize false T5013 filings face criminal liability, and courts may issue travel restrictions as bail conditions pending trial.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disallow the partnership structure under GAAR (s. 245 ITA), reattribute the income to the controlling principals, deny loss carryforwards, and impose a penalty of up to 10% of the reassessed amount under ITA s. 245(4); reassessment may be issued beyond the normal four-year limitation period in cases of misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Under the Alberta Partnerships Act, a limited partner who participates in the conduct of the LP's business loses limited liability. The general partner and any corporate director who acted in bad faith can be held personally liable for the full tax liability, civil penalties, and interest; a sham determination enables full veil piercing.
Transfer-Pricing Mispricing / Non-Arm's-Length Fees

Statutory sanction: CRA adjusts the transaction price to the arm's length amount under ITA s. 247(2); a penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment is imposed under s. 247(3) if contemporaneous documentation is not provided; late or incomplete T106 filings carry separate penalties; interest runs on all reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: OS of the corporate general partner who knowingly participate in the mispricing, or who fail to maintain required documentation, are exposed to personal civil penalties under the Income Tax Act; if the conduct is part of a conspiracy to evade tax, individual executives face criminal prosecution and financial restitution orders.
Money Laundering / AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 500,000 for an individual and CAD 1,000,000 for an entity; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code s. 462.31 is punishable by imprisonment for up to 10 years; FINTRAC can also issue a compliance order and suspend or terminate the business registration.

Director personal liability: Company directors, officers, and designated compliance officers can be personally named in FINTRAC administrative penalty orders; individuals directing the structuring activity may be charged as parties to money laundering under Criminal Code s. 21; asset freezes, travel bans, and civil forfeiture may accompany enforcement proceedings.
