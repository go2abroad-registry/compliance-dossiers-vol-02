Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - registration, assessment and collection of GST/HST, federal corporate income tax, and Alberta corporate income tax under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement; operational portals are Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-taxes, My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE. Alberta-specific counterpart: Alberta Corporate Registry (alberta.ca/registries) issues the Limited Partnership Certificate of Registration, and Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (ATRA) administers provincial tax statutes; Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax and no separate provincial VAT, so CRA remains the single indirect tax authority.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Alberta Limited Partnership at Alberta Corporate Registry - complete NUANS name search, file the Record of Limited Partnership, provide a registered address in Alberta, and appoint a resident registered agent; obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number and Certificate of Registration. Step 2: Apply to CRA for the partnership's federal Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or Form RC1, selecting the 'Partnership' legal type and requesting a GST/HST account (RT program) plus a payroll account if Canadian employees will be engaged; CRA issues a 15-character BN in the format 123456789 RT 0001. Step 3: If the LP is non-resident owned and controlled, CRA requires the representative partner to mail Form RC1 and Form RC871 to the International Tax Services Office; registration is mandatory once gross worldwide taxable supplies connected with Canada exceed C$30,000 in any four consecutive quarters. Step 4: Each non-resident partner must provide its foreign tax identification number and a certificate of residence for treaty purposes; CRA does not issue a separate partnership TIN beyond the BN. Step 5: After BN activation, the LP must file periodic GST/HST returns, a T5013 Partnership Information Return allocating income to partners, and the partners include Alberta corporate income tax on their T2 returns using the federal BN; no additional Alberta tax registration or provincial sales tax account is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - for Canadian income tax purposes the Alberta LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s.96: the partnership itself is not a taxpayer, computes income or loss at the partnership level, files the T5013 information return, and allocates income/loss to partners who are taxed in their own hands. Alberta does not impose entity-level income tax on the LP. For US federal income tax, the Canadian LP is a foreign eligible entity that defaults to partnership classification under Treas. Reg. sec. 301.7701-3(b)(2)(i)(B) when at least one general partner has unlimited liability, which is the standard structure. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - for Canadian income tax purposes the Alberta LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s.96: the partnership itself is not a taxpayer, computes income or loss at the partnership level, files the T5013 information return, and allocates income/loss to partners who are taxed in their own hands. Alberta does not impose entity-level income tax on the LP. For US federal income tax, the Canadian LP is a foreign eligible entity that defaults to partnership classification under Treas. Reg. sec. 301.7701-3(b)(2)(i)(B) when at least one general partner has unlimited liability, which is the standard structure. Territorial scope: Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source business income and dispositions of taxable Canadian property allocated through the LP, subject to any income tax treaty.

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic 'check-the-box' classification election for partnerships. Available statutory elections and special-status mechanisms: (1) US federal Form 8832 check-the-box election under Treas. Reg. sec. 301.7701-3(c) to confirm or overturn the default partnership/association classification of the Canadian LP for US investors; (2) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s.240(2.1), allowing the LP to register before crossing the C$30,000 compulsory threshold and to recover input tax credits and support export zero-rating; (3) ETA s.156 related-party election to treat qualifying non-arm's-length supplies between the LP and its corporate general partner as nil or fair-value consideration for GST purposes; (4) ITA s.96(8) partnership fiscal-period election permitting a non-calendar year-end where all partners' taxation years coincide; (5) hybrid mismatch treatment - Canada has not enacted BEPS Action 2 domestic hybrid legislation, so relief relies on the Canada-US Treaty Article IV(7) fiscally transparent entity clause and the Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test; no domestic hybrid election exists; (6) Alberta partnership legislation contains no tax classification election - fiscal effects flow solely from the ITA and ETA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 - a GST/HST registrant is required where taxable supplies made in Canada, including digital supplies and advertising inventory connected to Canada, exceed C$30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s.240(1)); non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold; zero-rated export revenues count toward the threshold, so registration may be compulsory even when all game revenue originates offshore; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under ETA Schedule VI: (a) supplies of services made to a non-resident person (s.7) where the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and not rendered to a person in Canada; (b) supplies of intangible property rights, including copyright and software licences, made to a non-resident (s.10) where the right is not for use predominantly in Canada; (c) exports of tangible goods (s.6). For the hyper-casual gaming model: (1) App Store and Google Play royalty/licence settlements - supplies of game IP and online-game rights to non-resident platform operators such as Apple Distribution International and Google Ireland Ltd. qualify for 0% under s.10 to the extent the platform's sublicence is not predominantly for use in Canada; (2) ad-network revenue - the supply of advertising inventory to non-resident ad-tech buyers such as AppLovin and AdMob/Google Ireland qualifies for 0% under s.7 where the contracted recipient is non-resident and the advertising is not directed at Canadian audiences; the Canadian-impression share must be treated as taxable at 5% unless marketplace or agency rules shift collection responsibility to the platform; (3) direct sales of digital game content to Canadian consumers are always taxable at 5% GST. Substantiation requires non-resident self-certification (W-8BEN-E or equivalent), contracts identifying the offshore recipient as principal, country-level download and impression reports demonstrating place of supply under ETA s.143.2, zero-rated invoices with recipient-country codes, and bank settlement records. No physical customs clearance applies to pure digital exports; there is no statutory FX repatriation deadline in Canada, the 180-day figure being a customary transfer-pricing documentation benchmark.

  • ETA s.286 books and records: zero-rated sales ledger with recipient-country jurisdiction codes and tax exemption indicator
  • Non-resident recipient evidence: W-8BEN-E, CRS self-certification, foreign tax identification number, or certificate of residence for each platform counterparty (Apple, Google, AppLovin, AdMob)
  • Executed distribution and monetization agreements: Apple Developer Program License Agreement, Google Play Distribution Agreement, AppLovin Publisher Agreement, and AdMob Terms
  • Foreign payout and FX records: AdMob and AppLovin settlement reports, bank SWIFT or inward remittance statements evidencing USD/EUR receipts from Ireland and US intermediaries
  • If physical goods are exported (promotional merchandise or gift cards): Canadian Export Reporting System B13A export declaration, bill of lading or waybill, and ETA Schedule VI s.6 export evidence

None - Canada has no mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transaction-level API pre-clearance for GST/HST. Registrants must issue invoices containing the registration number, date, supplier name, taxable amount, and tax amount (ETA s.223) and must retain books and records in Canada or electronically accessible (ETA s.286). CRA has adopted a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on Peppol PINT, but participation is optional. Filing occurs through GST/HST NETFILE or the GST Access API on a periodic return basis, not transaction-by-transaction clearance; the zero-rated export status is documented on the invoice as '0% GST'.

CRA export-verification audits focus on the recipient's non-resident status, the place of supply under ETA s.143.2, and whether digital services were in fact 'rendered to a person in Canada'. Where zero-rating is denied, CRA reassesses net GST, claws back input tax credits, and charges arrears interest at the prescribed rate compounded daily under ETA s.280. The gross-negligence penalty is the greater of C$250 and 25% of the net tax understated (ETA s.285). Misrepresentation, wilful default, or fraud extends the normal four-year reassessment window indefinitely under ETA s.298. Criminal tax evasion under ETA ss.326-327 carries fines of up to 200% of the amount sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years. Late or misleading T5013 partnership filings attract separate administrative penalties. For US investors, parallel exposure includes IRC sec. 6662 accuracy-related penalties of 20-40% and potential subpart F or hybrid-transfer-pricing adjustments, making contemporaneous zero-rating documentation essential.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible current account regime with unrestricted profit remittances for bona fide corporate operations. Capital account transactions are liberalized for routine equity injections and intercompany funding; prior approval from the Department of Finance is required only for portfolio investments exceeding CAD 100 million or certain real estate transactions. No surrender obligations apply to current account proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Exporters must submit periodic reports to Statistics Canada under the Export and Import Permits Act and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Funds may be freely converted through authorized foreign exchange dealers without statutory conversion ratios.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
