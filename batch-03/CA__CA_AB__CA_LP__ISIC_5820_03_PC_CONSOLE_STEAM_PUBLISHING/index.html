Partially permissive. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership; capital contributions are contractual and are set out in the limited partnership agreement, with no escrow account required. A limited partner's liability is confined to the unpaid balance of its agreed contribution under s. 64(2) of the Partnership Act.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Alberta Fire Code, structural load clearance per the National Building Code of Canada applicable to the leased office occupancy, and an Alberta occupational health and safety clearance covering workstation ergonomics, electrical safety, and emergency preparedness; no special civil defense permit applies to software publishing operations.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing authority applies to digital game publishing (ISIC 5820 / NAICS 511210), which is an office-based and non-manufacturing activity; the incidental registrations are the municipal business licence under Step 5 and, where applicable, participation in Alberta Innovation and Advanced Education digital media incentive programs, neither of which constitutes an industrial operating permit.

Category: None required. No manufacturing, broadcasting, or telecommunications operating permit is applicable to PC and console video game publishing via Steam or PlayStation Store; Steamworks and PlayStation Partner agreements are commercial contracts, not government licenses, and the limited partnership need only maintain a municipal business licence where physical premises are maintained.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box elections available. Partnerships are classified as pass-through entities under the Income Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch rules are addressed through domestic anti-avoidance provisions under section 245 of the Income Tax Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated GST/HST applies to exports of goods and services to non-resident customers located outside Canada. For digital services such as video game distribution via platforms like Steam, zero-rating is available when the service is provided to non-residents and the recipient is outside Canada, supported by proof of export.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment from foreign customer

GST/HST returns must be filed monthly electronically via CRA My Business. Large businesses are subject to real-time reporting requirements under the GST/HST Modernization Act. Digital invoicing and API integration for fiscal stamping are mandated for registrants exceeding specified transaction thresholds.

Failure to substantiate export claims results in GST/HST being charged at the standard rate with retrospective application. Penalties include 200% of the tax due, plus interest, and potential criminal prosecution for fraudulent claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has unrestricted current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; free remittance of profits, royalties, and partner distributions is permitted without mandatory reserve or conversion requirements. The only frictions are fiscal: non-resident limited partners face Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, reduced under treaties such as Canada-US) on Canadian-source income, and cross-border payments to non-residents may require CRA T-forms. Steam and PlayStation receipts in USD are freely convertible through licensed banks without Bank of Canada approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for software export proceeds; Canada does not operate an export-proceeds surrender regime. Reporting obligations apply at the transaction level: FINTRAC/Canada Border Services Agency require a Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report (E677) for physical currency or bearer monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more crossing the border, and banks file Large Cash Transaction Reports (LVCTR/CTR) for cash flows at that threshold. The Alberta LP must retain SWIFT MT103 statements and platform payout reports to substantiate the export nature of proceeds for audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; exchange-control and cross-border financial oversight administered jointly by the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Currency Act and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Canadian registered payment service providers (e.g., Wise, Stripe, Payoneer) can facilitate small-volume payout collection and FX conversion but are not classified as banks under the Bank Act and cannot provide capital escrow, interbank SWIFT settlement, or commercial credit. Offshore EMIs (UK/EU-licensed) are legally constrained from holding an Alberta LP's CAD treasury as a primary account due to absence of Canadian provincial nexus; they are viable only as supplementary payout-receiving channels for Steam/Valve or PlayStation flows, with funds swept to a Schedule I bank account within 24 hours.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from foreign limited partners must route via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's CAD or USD operating account with the Bank of Canada Balance of Payments classification purpose code 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (code 3500) in field 70; beneficiary field must exactly match the registered Alberta LP name. Wire memos must quote the LP contribution clause and partner ID. Steam/Valve payouts (remitted via JP Morgan in USD through ACH or wire to the designated USD sub-account) and PlayStation Store payouts should use purpose code 'Computer Software Services – Other Receipts' (BOP code 2560) for customs/statistical clarity. Incoming wire verification requires the platform payout statement, Steamworks withholding summary, and publisher agreement to satisfy the bank's inward-wire examination desk for recurring digital-revenue credits.Foreign disbursements (licensor royalties, middleware fees, overseas art-development expenses) clear via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'Royalties and License Fees' (code 2660); domestic CAD payments use Payments Canada ACSS or EFT to avoid unnecessary FX double-conversion. For very large one-off licensor settlements, a Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) is permissible but economically redundant for digital software; if used, it must be irrevocable and confirmed, with settlement against a presentation pack comprising the electronic revenue-share report and platform-generated sales ledger.
