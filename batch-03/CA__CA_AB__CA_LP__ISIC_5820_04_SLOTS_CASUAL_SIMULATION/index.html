Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital portal: My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). CRA administers federal GST and federal and Alberta corporate income tax under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act. The Alberta Limited Partnership must also be registered with the Alberta Corporate Registry (Registrar of Companies) under the Partnership Act (Alberta), which is separate from tax registration.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Alberta LP with the Alberta Corporate Registry and obtain a registration number; (2) Apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts (RT for GST/HST, RP for federal tax, RM for Alberta tax) using Form RC1, or Form GST50 for non-residents, supported by the partnership agreement and proof of an Alberta registered office; (3) File the T5013 Partnership Information Return for each fiscal period; (4) Non-resident partners must obtain their own BN or non-resident tax identifier and file Canadian returns on their allocated Alberta-source income, either under Part I of the Income Tax Act or under Part XIII using Form NR6; (5) Open a Canadian-dollar settlement bank account to document Inward Remittance Certificates and export proceeds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — An Alberta LP is fiscally transparent for federal and Alberta income tax: the partnership itself pays no income tax; each partner is taxed on its allocated share of partnership income. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is a distinct person under ETA s. 123 and is itself the registrant liable to collect and remit GST on taxable supplies made in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; partnership transparency is the statutory default. Available elections include: s. 96(3)/96(5) ITA elections respecting partnership deemed dispositions; Form NR6 and s. 216 ITA elections allowing non-resident partners to file Part I returns rather than suffer Part XIII withholding; s. 221(2) ETA Quick Method election; s. 156 ETA closely related person elections; and hybrid-mismatch and EBIE constraints under ITA ss. 18.2-18.21 and 18.4-18.5, which override elective structures for cross-border interest and royalty arrangements.

5%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated exports include: (i) tangible personal property physically exported as soon as is reasonably possible after possession transfers (ETA Sch. VI Pt. V s. 6); and (ii) exported services and rights, including computer software and digital gaming licences (Sch. VI Pt. V ss. 7 and 10), supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and has no Canadian establishment, where the service or intangible is consumed wholly outside Canada. For the social casino app, Apple/Google in-app purchases by foreign end-users qualify for zero-rating only where storefront, GeoIP, billing-country and payment-rail data demonstrate non-Canadian consumption; sales to Canadian-resident players are taxable at 5% GST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A / CAED filed through the CBSA GFTD portal) — applicable to physical exports; digital services are generally not subject to customs declarations
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (carrier-issued transport document evidencing physical export of goods)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (descriptive evidence of exported goods, destination and value)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103 credit advice issued by a Canadian authorized bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Apple App Store Connect / Google Play Console payout and transaction reports allocating sales by storefront, jurisdiction and currency

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) or digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary and is being adopted under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework based on PEPPOL BIS 3.0. GST/HST returns and remittances must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE; invoices are not pre-cleared or pre-approved by CRA. Registrants must retain books, records, platform payout statements and transaction logs for at least six years in English or French.

Export zero-rating is a CRA audit priority. Unsubstantiated claims are reassessed with GST, compounded daily prescribed-rate interest and penalties: ETA s. 280 (5-10% late-filing penalty), s. 281.1 (25% of unremitted amounts), s. 285 (gross negligence penalty of CAD 250 or 25% of the net tax understatement, whichever is greater) and s. 286 (record-keeping penalties); criminal liability may arise under s. 381. ITA s. 163.2 third-party penalties may apply to tax planning. CRA reassessment windows are 4 years (7 years where waivers apply), but no limitation period applies for fraud, misrepresentation or wilful default.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully free-floating Canadian dollar with unrestricted current and capital account convertibility. There are no statutory exchange controls, no prior-approval regime for profit remittances, and no restriction on an Alberta Limited Partnership distributing profits to non-resident limited partners, other than the standard federal withholding tax on such distributions. The Canadian dollar is a G10 deliverable currency, and interbank settlement through CLS and SWIFT is uninterrupted. Practical hurdles are limited to sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds. Funds held in foreign currency inside Canada may be freely deposited, converted, or transferred; the only fiscal requirement is to value foreign currency balances at the Bank of Canada noon rate for tax and bookkeeping purposes in CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose mandatory surrender, repatriation, or conversion ratios for export or service receipts. A Canadian resident LP earning app-store proceeds (Apple IAP / Google Play Billing) in USD or foreign currency may retain the balances in foreign bank accounts indefinitely, subject to (i) proper Canadian account reporting for deposits exceeding CAD 100,000 at Canadian banks, (ii) FINTRAC reports for any cross-border electronic fund transfer exceeding CAD 10,000, and (iii) documentary evidence of foreign-source service income for CRA Part XIII and non-resident partner allocation purposes. The functional currency election under the Income Tax Act permits CAD reporting for foreign receipts, but no exchange control permission is needed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Foreign exchange administration is decentralized: the Bank of Canada operates the international reserves framework and acts as fiscal agent, while the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises deposit-taking institutions, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/CFT obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The Minister of Finance retains residual authority over exchange controls under the Currency Act, but no active foreign exchange licensing regime applies to ordinary cross-border payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium primary viability. A UK FCA-authorized or EU-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) cannot lawfully hold CAD deposits for a Canadian resident without registering as a Money Services Business (MSB) under the PCMLTFA or operating through a Canadian chartered bank sponsor as an agent. Unregistered EMI activity is an offense. As a secondary disbursement rail for low-value, non-core vendor payments, an MSB-registered fintech (e.g., a Canadian entity with FINTRAC MSB registration) may be used, provided all partner distributions, tax filings, and capital injections flow solely through a Schedule I bank. EMI accounts are not acceptable for capital escrow accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All foreign limited-partner capital contributions must be received by SWIFT MT103 wire into the Tier 1 bank's Capital Escrow Account in CAD (or USD sub-account, with segregated balance). Required purpose codes are those identifying partnership capital contribution, e.g., SWIFT usage: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO ALBERTA LIMITED PARTNERSHIP — CA_AB LP REGISTRATION No. [XXX]'. The originating partner must provide an officer's certificate confirming source of funds; FINTRAC reporting is mandatory for any electronic fund transfer of CAD 10,000 or more entering Canada. Wire messages must include full originator details (name, address, account number) and full beneficiary details; incomplete MT103 fields will be rejected by OSFI-regulated clearing. No Canadian exchange control approval is required. If the contribution enters in USD and will be deployed in CAD, the bank treasury desk executes a spot conversion at the LP's written instruction under the account mandate. Any subsequent flow-back to a non-resident partner must be reported in the LP's T5013 information return and reflected in the non-resident partner slip.Vendor and supplier payments above CAD 3,000 are executed via SWIFT MT103 through the Tier 1 bank's clearing channel; domestic Canadian disbursements use large-value payment system (LVTS) or EFT batch. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% entity-level; flow-through LP income is allocated to partners. Corporate partners are subject to the combined 23% general corporate rate (15% federal + 8% Alberta).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII of the Canada Income Tax Act) on Canadian-source dividends paid to non-residents. Limited partnership profit allocations are not dividends; accordingly, no Part XIII withholding applies to the standard distribution of a Canadian LP's allocated income to its partners.
