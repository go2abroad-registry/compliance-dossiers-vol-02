Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada – Business Registration Online portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/register-your-business.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Alberta must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or by using CRA's Business Registration Online. The BN is the single tax identifier for corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts. For GST/HST registration, the entity must file Form/GST/HT1 or add a GST/HST account through CRA My Business Account. Non-resident limited partnerships register using a Canadian agent or representative and must provide proof of foreign incorporation and Alberta extra-provincial registration (if applicable). The BN is granted immediately online; CRA will issue a separate GST/HST account number (BN with 'RT' suffix) upon registration. There is no separate Alberta corporate tax registration; Alberta levies provincial corporate income tax administered by CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity carrying on business in Alberta must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or by using CRA's Business Registration Online. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election. A limited partnership (LP) registered in Alberta is statutorily a pass-through entity under the Alberta Partnership Act and Income Tax Act (Canada): the LP itself is not a taxpayer; income/losses flow through to partners. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is treated as a separate 'registrant' but is still a pass-through for income tax. No elective entity classification is available for Alberta LPs; however, a partnership may make certain elections under the Income Tax Act (e.g., section 216(1) for non-resident rental income, or section 152(4) waivers) and under Excise Tax Act (e.g., Part IX, section 238 election to have members jointly/severally liable for GST/HST). Hybrid mismatch elections under proposals in the Code may apply only to foreign partnerships with Canadian nexus, but Alberta LPs are not eligible for check-the-box treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST registration is mandatory for 30,000 CAD in annual worldwide taxable supplies (including taxable supplies of goods, services, and intangible personal property). For entities, the threshold is determined on a consolidated 'small supplier' basis; once exceeded, registration is compulsory within 29 days. The 30,000 CAD threshold applies to Alberta, which has no provincial sales tax; therefore the effective indirect tax rate is 5% GST only. Non-residents with no permanent establishment but with Canadian supplies must also register if threshold exceeded; voluntary registration permits input tax credit recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating applies to supplies of goods that are exported from Canada (Excise Tax Act, Part V, Schedule VI) and to certain services performed for non-residents, including services directly in connection with real property outside Canada, or services to non-residents where tangible personal property is exported or the service is supplied for use exclusively outside Canada. An esports platform providing tournament listing, matchmaking, streaming subscriptions, or digital advertising to non-resident customers must establish that the service is supplied to a non-resident and is not for use in Canada. Pure cloud/digital services supplied to Canadian customers are taxable at 5% GST in Alberta; supplies to non-residents are generally zero-rated if the recipient is a non-resident and the service is performed primarily outside Canada. To claim zero-rating, the supplier must maintain adequate evidence of export and non-resident status.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing mandate, or digital stamping regime exists for GST/HST. Standard electronic and paper invoices are permitted. Invoices must contain vendor name, BN, date, description, amount, and GST/HST payable. For B2B transactions, CRA accepts EDI and XML/PDF. The Canadian federal government is progressively adopting Peppol-based e-invoicing for procurement (voluntary), but it is not mandatory for private-sector B2B sales. Alberta has no provincial e-invoicing requirements. No continuous transaction control or API-based clearance applies.

CRA may reassess net tax, deny the zero-rating, and recover input tax credits claimed on inputs used for zero-rated exports if documentary requirements are not met. False or negligent statements attract penalties under Excise Tax Act, section 285 (greater of $250 and 25% of the tax avoided), as well as gross negligence penalties under Income Tax Act, section 163(2) (up to 50% of the understated tax) and interest on arrears. Criminal fraud penalties may apply for deliberate misrepresentation. CRA has vigorous audit programs for non-resident exporters, and requires retrospective recovery of input tax credits plus penalties if zero-rating is found to have been improperly claimed.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally impose prior approval, mandatory repatriation, capital controls, or compulsory conversion of foreign currency receipts. A Canadian limited partnership may generally receive foreign capital, hold foreign-currency accounts, pay non-residents, and remit distributable profits or partnership returns, subject to partnership agreements, tax compliance, withholding obligations, sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, lender covenants, and bank source-of-funds review. Canadian withholding tax may apply to certain payments to non-residents, including interest, dividends, royalties, management fees, and other prescribed amounts. Treaty relief generally requires valid residency documentation and beneficial-owner analysis. Payments involving U.S. dollars may also be subject to U.S. correspondent-bank screening and reporting requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or general deadline requiring conversion of export receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may remain in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency accounts in Canada or abroad, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, payment-services, and contractual requirements. Certain cross-border monetary instruments and electronic funds transfers may trigger reporting by financial institutions or other reporting entities, including large-cash reporting and electronic-funds-transfer reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and associated regulations. These are reporting obligations, not surrender or conversion requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing exchange controls. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist-financing obligations for reporting entities, while the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated financial institutions. Alberta corporate and partnership law is administered primarily through the Alberta Corporate Registry and relevant provincial authorities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and operating payments; low as a substitute for a Canadian commercial bank account. An offshore electronic-money institution or foreign payment platform may provide merchant acquiring, virtual accounts, wallets, or cross-border payout services only within the scope of its home-jurisdiction authorization and the services permitted in Canada. It may not represent itself as a Canadian deposit-taking institution or accept deposits in Canada without the required authorization. Canadian payment-services activity may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act and Bank of Canada supervision when the provider is in scope. Money-services businesses may require FINTRAC registration and must maintain written compliance programs, customer identification, beneficial-owner verification, recordkeeping, suspicious-transaction reporting, sanctions controls, and applicable electronic-funds-transfer reporting. Stripe and PayPal availability, reserve policies, settlement currencies, chargeback exposure, and account continuity are contract- and risk-review dependent and should not replace a bank-controlled treasury account.

Defense evidence: Certificate or proof of Alberta registration; executed limited partnership agreement; business plan; detailed operating model for the esports platform; projected transaction volumes; contracts or letters of intent; website and product evidence; initial capitalization schedule; opening balance sheet; tax-registration status; and written explanation of the expected account activity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods transactions; CAD 500,000 for services; CAD 250,000 for intangibles (per transaction or aggregate with non-arm's length non-residents)

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and specific anti-deferral rules for partnerships. The CRA may challenge transactions lacking economic substance. For Limited Partnerships, the general partner must have real substance; limited partners must not participate in management to retain limited liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties: 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: General partners are personally liable for the partnership's tax debts. Directors of a corporate general partner may be liable for unpaid withholding taxes (s. 227.1). Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under GAAR, deny treaty benefits, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax benefit. The partnership may be deemed a sham, leading to full tax liability.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of the corporate general partner may be jointly and severally liable for taxes, interest, and penalties. Personal assets may be seized.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 2,000,000 for entities. Criminal penalties include imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined and imprisoned if they knowingly fail to comply. The CRA and FINTRAC may impose travel bans and freeze corporate assets.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices, impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation incomplete) plus interest. The penalty is reduced to 5% if documentation is provided.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be liable for penalties if they authorized the non-compliant pricing. Personal liability may extend to unpaid taxes and penalties.
