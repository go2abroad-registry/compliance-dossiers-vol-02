Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal, https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; customs export matters administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must apply for a Business Number (BN) using CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1E (Request for a Business Number – Non-Resident). The BN plus program account suffix 'RT' constitutes the GST/HST registration number (e.g., 123456789RT0001). Registration is mandatory where the LP's worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available once the LP begins commercial activity in Canada. Non-resident general and limited partners must each provide their own personal or corporate tax identifiers; withholding-based NR6/NR4 processes apply to certain Canadian-source non-resident allocations.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership must apply for a Business Number (BN) using CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1E (Request for a Business Number – Non-Resident). The BN plus program account suffix 'RT' constitutes the GST/HST registration number (e.g., 123456789RT0001). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box regime. Available partnership and tax elections include: (i) income tax fiscal-period election under Income Tax Act subsection 249(4) permitting a partnership non-calendar year-end; (ii) GST/HST Quick Method election under Excise Tax Act section 221 for qualifying small suppliers; (iii) Income Tax Act section 216 election allowing a non-resident partner to file a net-basis return for rental income; (iv) simplified GST/HST accounting elections under ETA sections 229 and 231. Hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.6 and 18.7 are mandatory disallowance rules, not elective mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); mandatory registration for non-resident persons exceeding this threshold, with voluntary registration permitted for persons carrying on commercial activity in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Canadian LP producing and globally distributing vertical micro-dramas through a streaming app may zero-rate its digital supplies under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, section 17 where the supply of intangible personal property is made to a non-resident person and the property is not used, consumed, or enjoyed in Canada. Services performed for a non-resident recipient may also qualify under Schedule VI, Part V (section 7) where the services relate to exported property or are performed wholly outside Canada. The operator must document that the digital download or stream is delivered to and consumed by non-resident end users; supplies to Canadian subscribers remain subject to 5% GST.

  • Commercial invoice annotated with the zero-rating certification wording 'Zero-rated export supply – Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act'.
  • Non-residence evidence for digital services: IP geolocation logs, device and billing-address records, and payment processor settlement files (Apple IAP / Google Play Billing / Stripe) demonstrating the consumer location outside Canada.
  • Signed distribution agreement identifying the merchant-of-record, the platform's non-resident status, and the designated place of consumption of the micro-drama content.
  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) and Bill of Lading / Air Waybill where physical media or cinematographic deliverables are shipped across a border.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency conversion statement from a Canadian bank, or electronic payment settlement evidence, proving export proceeds were received by the Canadian entity from the non-resident counterparty.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing mandate currently applies in Canada. Registered suppliers must issue GST/HST invoices containing prescribed particulars under Excise Tax Act section 223 (supplier identity, BN, description, consideration, tax rate and amount) no later than the filing deadline for the reporting period. The CRA is facilitating voluntary adoption of Pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoicing, with federal adoption expected progressively between 2025 and 2028; this is an interoperability standard, not a mandatory clearance system.

CRA may deny zero rating where documentary proof of export or non-resident consumption is deficient, reassessing net tax with arrears interest and failure-to-file penalties under ETA section 280. False or omissive statements attract a penalty of 25% of the net tax or refund at issue (ETA section 285.1), and gross negligence carries an additional 50% penalty (ETA subsection 285.1(2)). Export zero-rating fraud, including circular invoicing or falsified non-resident records, may trigger criminal prosecution under ETA sections 327-330 with fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years, plus director liability under ETA section 323. The normal four-year reassessment period is extended to ten years for misrepresentation or neglect, and there is no limitation period for fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible across both current and capital account transactions; no exchange-control or capital-repatriation restrictions exist under federal law. Profit remittance is unrestricted, but payments of interest, royalties, and limited-partnership distributions to non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (default 25%; reduced under the Canada-US tax treaty to 15% or lower where applicable). Because the Alberta Limited Partnership is pass-through, non-resident partners are also assessable under Part I on Canadian-source business income; capital contributions and equity redemptions are not subject to withholding. No exchange-control permit or foreign-investment approval is required for inbound capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no statutory repatriation timeline. Receipts from the global distribution of micro-dramas may be retained in foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks or held through regulated payment processors, provided that all PCMLTFA (Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act) reporting thresholds are met, including FINTRAC cross-border EFT reports for transactions at or above CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration oversight is shared with the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act, with supervisory jurisdiction over regulated deposit-taking institutions exercised by OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM - RESTRICTED. Canada does not recognize EU/UK Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licenses as bank licenses. A foreign EMI (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business) can only operate in Canada as a Money Services Business registered with FINTRAC and, where it carries on retail payment activities, must be registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). An offshore EMI cannot serve as the principal settlement rail for the Alberta LP's CAD treasury. It is viable solely as a collection gateway (top-up via Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, and Stripe) with periodic sweeps into a Schedule I bank account; capital escrow, operational clearing, and FX hedging must remain with a regulated Canadian bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined reference rate (Canada federal 15% general corporate rate + Alberta 8% general corporate rate); not applicable at the Alberta limited partnership entity level because the LP is fiscally transparent, and each corporate partner is subject to this rate on its share of partnership income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident; an Alberta LP itself does not pay dividends and non-resident partners are generally taxed net-basis under Part I on their distributive share, unless the distribution is treated as a dividend under a hybrid or interposed-entity rule.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than six months after the end of the partnership's fiscal period; where the fiscal period ends on December 31, the due date is June 30 of the following calendar year

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than six months after the end of the corporation's taxation year; installments of estimated tax are payable quarterly, with the final installment due two months after the taxation year-end for CCPCs and three months for other corporations

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-resident related persons (triggers mandatory Form T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under ITA s.247); country-by-country reporting (CbCR) is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of CAD 1,000,000,000 or more; Master File and Local File documentation must be kept contemporaneously and supplied on CRA request without a separate monetary threshold when requested.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no general economic substance regulations comparable to UAE ESR or Cayman substance regime. Instead, ITA s.245 GAAR and common-law sham doctrine apply: an Alberta limited partnership must demonstrably carry on real commercial activities, maintain its own bank accounts, employ personnel, execute contracts in its own name, and bear proportionate economic risk. A conduit LP with no substantive business may be disregarded or recharacterized, treaty-benefit claims denied, and the limited-partner status revoked.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil: 50% gross negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) plus interest. Criminal: prosecution under ITA s.239(1) — fine from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years; criminal courts may impose restitution orders and prohibit transfer of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participated in or are found to have condoned the evasion are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 for unpaid withholdings; corporate veil may be pierced for fraudulent conduct, imposing personal civil restitution; travel bans under the Criminal Code can be imposed pending prosecution or collection; imprisonment potential up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess the partnership as non-existent under the sham doctrine, allocate income back to the controlling person, apply the GAAR at s.245(2) to deny the tax benefit, and assess a 10% transfer-pricing penalty if adjustments relate to intercompany charges; provincial registry may accept the Declaration but cannot validate economic substance.

Director personal liability: A limited partner who exercises any control over the LP loses the statutory limited-liability shield under the Alberta Partnership Act and becomes jointly and severally liable for all debts of the partnership; directors of the corporate general partner may be personally liable for the LP's remittances and for fraudulent-conveyance exposure where assets are stripped.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and its Regulations, administrative monetary penalties up to approximately CAD 500,000 for individuals and CAD 1,000,000 for entities for repeat violations; criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code s.462.31 carries imprisonment up to 10 years upon indictment.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers may face criminal charges as parties to money-laundering; FINTRAC compliance orders and AML penalties attach personally in cases of willful blindness; courts have imposed personal restitution and travel bans where directors removed funds from Canada.
Part XIII Withholding / Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: Withholding failure penalty under ITA s.227(8): 10% of the amount withheld that was not remitted (20% if the failure is made knowingly or under gross negligence); CRA may add daily interest from the original due date; a director's liability assessment can collect the unremitted amounts from the corporate general partner's directors.

Director personal liability: Directors of a Canadian corporate general partner are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII or payroll withholding amounts under s.227.1, even if the partnership is dissolved; civil collection can extend to personal bank accounts, and individuals who avoid payment may be subject to CRA travel-against-individual proceedings, including passport revocation in serious criminal tax cases.
