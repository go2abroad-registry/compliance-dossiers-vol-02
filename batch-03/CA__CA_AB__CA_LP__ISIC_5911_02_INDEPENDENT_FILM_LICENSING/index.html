Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no government escrow account is required under the Alberta Partnership Act. The Declaration must disclose the aggregate capital contribution agreed by the limited partners (cash, property, or services). Contributions are recorded in the partnership records, not remitted to the Registrar; a limited partner with an unpaid or withdrawn contribution forfeits limited-liability protection under section 65(3) of the Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax administration under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and Excise Tax Act (ETA); primary portals are CRA 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca. Alberta's provincial tax authority is the Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA), but Alberta corporate income tax is collected federally by the CRA on Alberta's behalf; Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax. The Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) handles extra-provincial Limited Partnership legal registration under the Limited Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c L-8 (a corporate-registry step, not a tax TIN issuance).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: A foreign LP must obtain a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (format 123456789RT0001) — the sole Canadian tax identifier — when it 'carries on business in Canada' (ETA s. 175.1, interpreted under Thatcher and Geodetic Research) or makes any taxable supplies in Canada. Apply via BRO, by paper Form RC1E, or for non-residents via Form GST104 'Application for Non-Resident Registration'. Step 2: For ISIC 5911 film licensing, if the LP is the licensor of rights to a Canadian distributor, the Canadian licensee must withhold Part XIII non-resident tax (25%, or treaty-reduced) on royalties paid to the non-resident partners; the LP should file Form NR601/NR4 information if required and document each partner's residency. Step 3: If the LP establishes a physical Alberta presence, file the extra-provincial Limited Partnership registration with Service Alberta; this is a non-tax legal-capacity filing. Step 4: Identify a Canadian-resident general partner as the partnership's GST/HST representative (ETA s. 273(1)) and file GST/HST returns electronically using the BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no 'check-the-box' election; an Alberta Limited Partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96 (partners, not the LP, are taxed on allocated income). Relevant elections include: (i) a joint election under ETA s. 273(1) designating a general partner as the GST/HST representative; (ii) an election under ETA s. 273(2) to disregard certain internal partnership supplies for GST/HST; (iii) a non-resident partner's optional 'net-basis' Part I election under ITA s. 216(1) — rarely applicable to film licensing; (iv) anti-hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 12.4, 18.6, 20.2, 94) apply automatically where a US partner's check-the-box election creates a hybrid structure, with no elective opt-out; treaty withholding reduction is claimed through Form NR301 (the underlying Canada-US treaty, income from copyright royalties, may reduce Part XIII withholding to 0% where the beneficial owner is a US resident and the payment is not connected with a Canadian permanent establishment).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1), measured on taxable supplies made in Canada in the current or preceding four calendar quarters (for a non-resident person, only supplies made in Canada are counted). A foreign LP licensing films to Canadian distributors will normally exceed this threshold and must register before making the first taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For ISIC 5911 film licensing, a taxable supply of a motion-picture copyright or distribution right to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 23(1)(a) where the right is not for use, performance or delivery in Canada; a supply acquired for use exclusively outside Canada also qualifies under s. 23(1)(b). Copyright publication and distribution rights may alternatively qualify under s. 24 of Part V where the work is to be published/distributed outside Canada. Licensing to a Canadian distributor for Canadian theatrical or streaming exhibition remains taxable at 5%. Related offshore services (e.g., post-production supervision supplied to a non-resident) may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 where the recipient receives the benefit outside Canada. The 0% rate is claimed on the GST/HST return, and input tax credits (ITCs) on related production/acquisition costs are preserved.

  • Signed international distribution or copyright licence agreement evidencing the territorial grant outside Canada and the non-resident licensee's identity and address
  • GST/HST invoice under ETA s. 221(1) showing the supply as '0% — Zero-rated export supply' with the licence fee, date, and both parties' names and BNs/tax IDs
  • Customs Export Declaration filed with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the Canadian Export Reporting System (Form B13A) or ACI eManifest for any physical media (film prints, DCPs, masters) crossing the border
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or courier carrier manifest naming an overseas destination and consignee for physical exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 wire record, or Letter of Credit presentation evidencing receipt of the licence fee from the non-resident payer
  • Certificate of residency of the recipient/licensee (tax authority residency certificate or Form NR301 counterpart) retained for six years

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping (continuous transaction controls) regime. Electronic invoices (PDF, EDI, XML) are legally valid if they contain the prescribed GST/HST information under ETA s. 221(1) and s. 3 of the GST/HST Regulations: supplier name and BN, recipient name, date, description of the supply, and tax rate (including '0%'). Canada is progressively adopting PEPPOL-BIS e-invoicing for federal B2G procurement (targeting ~2025-2026), but private-sector cross-border film-licence invoicing remains paper/PDF/EDI-permitted. Registered suppliers must retain invoices and support records for six years under ETA s. 286.

If CRA reclassifies the supply as one for use in Canada (e.g., the Canadian distributor's onward sub-licence is exercised or exhibition occurs in Canada), the 0% rate is reversed and 5% GST is assessed with arrears interest under ETA s. 280 (currently around 9% per annum, reset quarterly). Civil penalties: ETA s. 281.1 false-statement penalty (5% of the understated tax plus 5% per month of continuous default, capped at 50%); ETA s. 285.1 gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the understated tax (10% in reduced culpability cases); criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 for evasion. Reassessment window is four years for ordinary filings (ETA s. 298(3)) and unlimited in cases of misrepresentation (ETA s. 298(5)). Transfer-pricing adjustments to interposed non-arm's-length licensors under ITA s. 247(2) attract an additional 10% gross-misreporting penalty under ITA s. 247(3). CRA cross-references CBSA export declarations, ISAN/Vers. number records, bank remittance data, and the six-year document retention duty under ETA s. 286. Canada imposes no separate statutory FX-repatriation cut-off; the 180-day benchmark reflects standard banking and trade terms, and an unreasonable delay in collecting the foreign receivable may trigger reassessment of the zero-rated sale and related GST interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. There is no prior-approval requirement for remitting film-licensing royalties, partnership distributions, interest, or capital returns to non-resident limited partners. Canadian dollar repatriation of export proceeds is permitted without limit and without mandatory conversion. The only substantive hurdles are non-FX in nature: (i) Part XIII withholding at 25% (treaty-reduced) on Canadian-source payments such as royalties for use in Canada or partnership income allocated from a Canadian permanent establishment; a non-resident partner's share of foreign-source licensing income is generally exempt from Canadian withholding if no Canadian PE exists; (ii) the Investment Canada Act net-benefit review for a non-resident acquisition of control of a Canadian cultural business, including film distribution, at a reduced direct-acquisition threshold of CAD 5 million book value or the WTO enterprise-value threshold for private-sector acquirers; obtaining a legal opinion that the LP's international licensing activity does not constitute a Canadian film-distribution business is recommended; and (iii) FINTRAC reporting obligations for cross-border electronic funds transfers above CAD 10,000 performed in the course of business, due within five business days. All other capital and profit-repatriation movements are unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio exists. Film-licensing royalties earned in USD or other foreign currencies may be retained, invoiced, and settled freely. Tax-reporting rules replace exchange controls: the LP must translate all foreign-currency revenue to Canadian dollars on its T5013 partnership information return using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate; realized foreign-exchange gains on settlement of USD receivables are recognized under subsection 39(1) of the Income Tax Act unless the gains are income-account; GST/HST zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V, section 10 applies to the supply of intangible property (copyright licences) made by the LP to non-residents for use exclusively outside Canada, supported by licensee declarations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) is the monetary and payments oversight authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially regulates Schedule I/II chartered banks; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for wire-transfer and large-cash reporting; the Department of Finance Canada and Global Affairs Canada jointly administer economic sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act. No standalone foreign-exchange control administration exists: Canada abrogated exchange controls with the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951 and has since maintained a free-floating currency accepted under IMF Article VIII.
