Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for Alberta limited partnerships; capital contributions are fixed by the partnership agreement and must consist of money or property (a limited partner may not contribute services without risking general-partner liability); no escrow or capital verification account is required by the Alberta registrar.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and federal income tax administration; Alberta Treasury Board and Finance for Alberta provincial corporate income tax administered by the CRA. The CRA's My Business Account portal and Business Registration Online are the primary electronic services.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Alberta as a limited partnership must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) and the applicable program accounts, including a GST/HST account (account suffix 'RT') if required or voluntarily registered. The application may be completed through Business Registration Online, by paper Form RC1, or by an authorized Canadian representative. For a foreign limited partnership, the applicant must provide legal entity proof, partnership agreement details, partner identification, and any Alberta extra-provincial registration documentation. The CRA BN is the statutory tax identifier for indirect tax purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity carrying on business in Alberta as a limited partnership must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) and the applicable program accounts, including a GST/HST account (account suffix 'RT') if required or voluntarily registered. The application may be completed through Business Registration Online, by paper Form RC1, or by an authorized Canadian representative. For a foreign limited partnership, the applicant must provide legal entity proof, partnership agreement details, partner identification, and any Alberta extra-provincial registration documentation. The CRA BN is the statutory tax identifier for indirect tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

There is no U.S.-style check-the-box election in Canada. The tax classification of an Alberta limited partnership is statutory flow-through under the Income Tax Act (Canada). A partnership may elect a non-calendar fiscal period under subsection 249.1(9), and non-resident partners may be subject to Canadian partnership compliance filing rules. Hybrid mismatch rules under Canadian law are mandatory adjustment provisions, not elective classifications, and no check-the-box election exists for hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000; a limited partnership carrying on commercial activity in Canada ceases to be a small supplier once its total taxable supplies, including those of associated persons, exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or across the preceding four calendar quarters. Voluntary GST/HST registration is available below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible personal property may be zero-rated if the supplier sells and exports the property in accordance with CRA proof-of-export documentation rules. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated when they are included in Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act and are supplied for use wholly outside Canada or are otherwise exported services, provided the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant and the supply is not in respect of Canadian real estate or tangible personal property physically delivered in Canada. Commercial CGI, visual effects, and 3D animation production services supplied to non-resident clients for foreign exploitation can qualify for zero-rating where the statutory tests and documentary evidence requirements are met.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A or Canada Border Services Agency electronic export report)
  • Bill of Lading, ocean or rail waybill, or air waybill issued by a common carrier
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract evidencing the transaction
  • Proof of physical export (carrier receipts, port documents, or foreign customs entry)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank wire/payment settlement confirmation showing funds received from the non-resident purchaser

Canada has no mandatory national electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime; therefore no CRA digital stamping or invoice pre-clearance is required. GST/HST tax invoices must contain prescribed information including the supplier's Business Number, recipient name, description of supply, and tax amount. Electronic invoices are expressly permitted, but all invoicing and export records must be retained for six years. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns through NETFILE, EFT, or the GST/HST electronic filing API, but this does not constitute invoice-by-invoice fiscal clearance.

CRA may disallow zero-rating where proof-of-export documentation is missing or inadequate. Reassessments and refund clawbacks may be made within the normal four-year statutory period, and that period is extended where a taxpayer filed a false statement or committed misrepresentation. Gross negligence penalties under the Excise Tax Act may be up to 25% of the net tax understated, and tax evasion or fraudulent zero-rating claims may result in criminal prosecution, monetary penalties based on tax evaded, interest, and possible imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible and Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 1 December 1951. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are free from exchange-control approval requirements. A CA_LP may lawfully receive foreign-currency capital contributions from non-resident limited partners, maintain USD/EUR clearing accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks, and remit profits abroad without Bank of Canada or Department of Finance authorization. Profit remittance hurdles are fiscal and regulatory rather than convertibility-based: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at the statutory 25% on Canadian-source income allocated or distributed to non-resident limited partners, subject to treaty reduction and the applicable characterization rules of the Income Tax Act; (ii) FINTRAC reporting of any international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more; (iii) bank-level CRS/FATCA self-certification and source-of-funds due diligence; and (iv) Regulation 105 withholding on payments for non-resident services where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not require export proceeds to be surrendered, imposes no mandatory conversion ratio, and prescribes no statutory repatriation window. The CA_LP may retain VFX and 3D animation service receipts in USD, EUR, or CAD indefinitely in Canadian or offshore accounts. The only relevant constraints are FINTRAC EFT reporting thresholds and the bank's internal account-purpose monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; prudential regulation of banks is exercised by OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions); cross-border EFT and AML surveillance is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration, as the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed with effect from 1 December 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High as a supplementary rail, with material legal constraints. Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex-style platforms operating in Canada generally hold FINTRAC money-services-business (MSB) registration and may be used for low-value B2B receipts and international contractor disbursements; however, they are not deposit-taking institutions and cannot lawfully hold partnership capital in escrow or provide credit-linked trade products. Offshore EMIs without FINTRAC MSB registration and without compliance with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) payment-services registration regime face legal barriers when soliciting Canadian-resident entities, and their accounts typically create downstream correspondent friction with Tier-1 Canadian banks. Recommended posture: use registered EMIs only for sub-threshold operational disbursements; maintain the Capital Escrow Account and all investor-facing liquidity exclusively at a Schedule I Canadian bank.

CRA may estimate the Alberta allocation on default, reassess partners on that estimate, and the general partner becomes exposed to interest, penalties, and audit costs.
Annual GST/HST Return (Federal Value-Added Tax Remittance and Reconciliation) For an annual-filer registrant: within three months after the LP's fiscal year end. For quarterly and monthly filers: within one month after each reporting period end. Annual filing is available where annual taxable supplies do not exceed CAD 1.5 million; exported 3D animation services may be zero-rated if documentary criteria are met. $0 USD Late filing attracts a penalty of 5% of the unremitted net tax plus 1% per complete month late (maximum 12 months) under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act. Gross negligence penalties under section 285 may reach 25% of any deficiency. Habitual non-filing may cause CRA to cancel the GST/HST registration, preventing the LP from charging or recovering GST/HST while the partners remain jointly liable for outstanding partnership net tax.
Annual return deadline

Anniversary-based: due within 60 days after each anniversary of the limited partnership registration date. A registered agent may file on behalf of the LP; no extension is available for this filing.

Late filing penalties

There is no statutory late fee; enforcement is by offence fines and administrative strike-off. After two consecutive missed annual returns, the Registrar may strike the LP off the Alberta registry, causing loss of legal capacity and dissolution. Restoration requires formal reinstatement plus outstanding fees. Each general partner may also be liable for an offence fine of up to CAD 5,000 (approx. USD 3,650) per offence, plus daily continuing fines for willful default.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No automatic statutory audit is imposed on an Alberta limited partnership under the Partnership Act (Alberta). A compulsory independent audit arises only where: (i) the limited partnership agreement or an investor side letter imposes an audit covenant; (ii) commercial lenders financing the 3D animation production require audited financial statements, typically above CAD 5,000,000 gross revenue; (iii) the LP claims the Alberta Film and Television Tax Credit or a federal film tax credit requiring production-cost certification and CRA audit; or (iv) CRA formally requires audited financials under sections 231.6/231.7 of the Income Tax Act. As a practical benchmark, an audit becomes necessary for a mid-market VFX studio at approximately CAD 1,000,000–5,000,000 annual revenue, depending on financing structure.
