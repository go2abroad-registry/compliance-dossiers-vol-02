Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c P-3, Part 2 (Limited Partnerships), ss. 50-77; Business Names Registration Act, RSA 2000, c B-9 (name registration). The CA_LP is registered, not incorporated, by means of a statutory declaration filed with the Registrar of Companies; the general partners sign the declaration, and limited partners are admitted under the partnership agreement made pursuant to the Act.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership. Capital contributions, whether cash or in-kind, are fixed solely by the partnership agreement and may be staged or subscribed on a deferred schedule. No escrow deposit or pre-funded capital account is required under the Partnership Act; however, limited partners are liable only up to their agreed and actually paid contributions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); GST/HST NETFILE for indirect tax filings.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Limited Partnership (LP) formed in Alberta must register for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with CRA via Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online or by mail. For GST/HST, open an RT program account under the same BN using Form GST1 (or via the Business Registration Online portal). Each LP is also obligated to file T5013 Partnership Information Return; each partner, whether resident or non-resident, requires a Canadian taxpayer identifier (SIN, ITN, or BN) to report allocated income. Registration steps: (1) obtain BN; (2) register RT account if small supplier threshold exceeded or if taxable supplies are made and registration is compulsory; (3) appoint a resident Canadian representative or authorized agent for service of process where applicable; (4) file Form RC461T or equivalent for customs/export registration only if goods are exported under drawback or duty deferral regimes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No Canadian 'check-the-box' regime exists. Key statutory elections: (a) Partnership fiscal period election (ITA s. 249.1(4)) to adopt non-calendar year-end; (b) Election in ITA s. 216 by a non-resident LP partner to file on a net-of-Canadian-source-property-income basis; (c) GST/HST non-resident election to register voluntarily or to apply deemed registration for rebate purposes; (d) Quick Method election (GST/HST) where eligible; (e) Election under ITA s. 18.2/18.4 for hybrid mismatch arrangements, elective treatment of 'co-investment' instruments or similar hybrid equity/liability rules; (f) Joint election per ITA s. 40(3.1) for partnerships involving capital property. The LP itself is transparent for federal and Alberta income tax; all income, credits, and GST/HST implications pass through pro rata to partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (worldwide taxable supplies, including taxable and zero-rated supplies, measured over any single calendar quarter and the preceding four quarters under the 'small supplier' rules in ETA s. 148). A non-resident LP that solicits sales in Canada or supplies activities in Canada generally has a mandatory registration obligation even below the threshold if it carries on business in Canada; exporters of zero-rated services may register voluntarily to claim input tax credits on Canadian inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedules VI (goods) and VII (services) where supplies are made to a non-resident person (including a foreign LP) who is not registered under GST/HST, provided the supply is not delivered or performed in Canada (purely offshore services). AI subtitle transcription and voice dubbing supplied to a non-resident studio qualify where: (a) the recipient is a non-resident person at the time the supply is made; (b) the recipient is not registered for GST/HST; and (c) the service does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The service must be 'exported' in the sense that the recipient acquires it for consumption wholly outside Canada. For digital services, zero-rating is also available when supplied by a GST/HST registrant to a non-resident recipient through a cross-border electronic supply chain, provided the place of supply rules (ETA s. 142) place the supply outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A / G13A for physical goods; not applicable to pure digital services, where a commercial invoice and electronic export record serve as evidential basis)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (for any physical media deliverables)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/PSP payment evidence (Stripe, Wise) showing settlement from the non-resident client
  • Signed master service agreement or purchase order identifying the non-resident recipient, service description, and jurisdictional scope
  • Technical evidence of offshore performance (server logs, project metadata, delivery receipts, timesheets) corroborating that the subtitle/voice services were supplied without Canadian performance nexus

No statutory real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance/digital stamping regime exists at federal or Alberta level. CRA requires that all invoices issued by a GST/HST registrant contain the supplier's name, BN/RT account, transaction date, description, and GST rate/amount, but issuance is via ordinary PDF/XML. CRA operates an optional PEPPOL-based e-invoicing pilot (e-invoice platform) for business-to-government and commercial use; participation is voluntary and does not replace audit obligations. Payment rails (Stripe, Wise) have no tax-clearance interface; taxpayers must self-report on Form GST34 returns. A register of 'specified invoices' must be retained for six years for audit traceability.

CRA may assess false or fraudulent zero-rating claims under ETA s. 296, recovering unpaid GST plus compound interest at prescribed rates and imposing: (i) failure-to-file penalties (1% of unreported tax plus 0.25% per month, capped at 12 months); (ii) gross negligence penalties up to 50% of the tax avoided (ETA s. 285); (iii) fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment for intentional evasion under ETA s. 327. Retrospective reassessments extend to 7 years where misrepresentation attributable to negligence, carelessness, or wilful default (ITA s. 152(4)(a)(i) applied by analogy in ETA assessments), and indefinitely for fraud. Additionally, CRA can deny refund/input tax credit claims, cancel GST/HST registration, and, in cross-border contexts, invoke the Minister's authority to require security under ETA s. 299. Payment rails settlement lacking a proper 'INVOICE+REMITTANCE' audit trail is treated as a red flag; clawback of input tax credits and denial of zero-rating are retrospective to the date the supply was first made.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains fully liberalised capital and current account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange-control approvals for capital injections, profit distributions, royalties, or loan repayments. All major currencies can be freely converted through commercial banks. Profit remittance hurdles are fiscal rather than exchange-control: non-resident limited partners may have Canadian tax filing obligations on Canadian-source partnership income, treaty-based withholding relief applies only with appropriate tax residency certificates and CRS/FATCA self-disclosures, and no blocking or conversion restrictions are imposed by the Bank of Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no mandatory export proceeds surrender regime, no statutory repatriation timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no obligation to route export receipts through a designated government depository. Export proceeds may be held in foreign-currency accounts with Canadian banks, reinvested offshore, or converted to CAD at commercial exchange rates. For tax purposes, unrealised foreign-exchange gains or losses are generally recognised only on realisation in accordance with the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is not vested in a standalone exchange-control authority. The Department of Finance Canada retains policy authority over financial regulatory frameworks, FINTRAC supervises AML/CTF reporting for cross-border electronic funds transfers, and Payments Canada operates the Lynx high-value clearing system for CAD transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as an ancillary disbursement rail only. Foreign EMIs, including Wise and similar institutions, are not chartered banks in Canada and cannot accept deposit liabilities under the Bank Act. They must remain FINTRAC-registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) when providing remittance, FX conversion, or payment-anchored services to Canadian entities. The Alberta LP may use these rails for high-volume low-value content licensing and subtitle vendor payments, but the primary clearing, capital escrow, and commercial LC functions should remain with a Canadian Schedule I bank. Legal constraints include ongoing FINTRAC MSB registration, limited availability of CAD Lynx RTGS access, and inability to issue documentary letters of credit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing begins with the remitting partner instructing its foreign bank to send a SWIFT MT103 wire. The message must include the remitter's fully identified account, the beneficiary bank's SWIFT BIC for the selected Canadian Schedule I bank, and the beneficiary party block identifying the Alberta LP by legal name, general partner, and registered branch address. In the field 70 or instruction line, the remitter should clearly state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO CA_LP - EQUITY FUNDING' or 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - PARTNERSHIP FUNDING'. Canada does not prescribe a mandatory central-bank purpose code, but the financial institution will map the payment to an ISO 20022 remittance classification for FINTRAC reporting. Any inbound electronic transfer of CAD 10,000 or more is reportable to FINTRAC by the Canadian receiving institution; no prior approval is required. For CAD settlement, funds clear in real time through Lynx; for USD, settlement occurs through SWIFT correspondent banking or US domestic ACH/CHIPS depending on the routing. There is no compulsory conversion to CAD, and participating partners may retain original currency balances to minimise FX exposure.Operational disbursements for subtitle production, dubbing talent, AI voice license fees, and content-rights settlements should be effected using SWIFT MT103 for single cross-border payments, or commercial documentary letters of credit for high-value milestone-based content acquisitions. Domestic CAD vendors are paid through Lynx RTGS wires, Payments Canada automated clearing settlement, or bank-affiliated payroll corridors.
