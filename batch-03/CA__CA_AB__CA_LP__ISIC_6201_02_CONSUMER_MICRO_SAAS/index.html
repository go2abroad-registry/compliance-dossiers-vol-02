Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA), via My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Alberta participates in the federal tax collection agreement; no separate Alberta income tax collection authority for LPs.

E-government filing portal

Foreign LP obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) with GST/HST program account by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and certain program accounts), and where carrying on business in Alberta, completes extra-provincial registration through Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES). Supporting documents include foreign entity registration certificate, Alberta address/agent for service, and completed ownership/partner information for the general partner. The BN enables GST/HST collection, import/export duties, and payroll administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Alberta Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes; the partnership itself is not a taxable entity, and each partner is liable on its share of income, subject to Canadian tax on Canadian-source and treaty-allocated income. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is a separate 'person' and to the extent it is carrying on commercial activity must register if exceeding the small supplier threshold.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election. Canada taxes LPs as partnerships by default; no entity-level income tax election is available. Certain partnerships may file prescribed elections (e.g., deemed year-end, partner allocation elections under the Income Tax Act, and GST/HST election under s. 273 for closely related persons), but hybrid mismatch cookbook-style classification elections are not recognized. Anti-hybrid rules are mandatory and not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); a non-resident carrying on business in Canada and exceeding this threshold must register for GST/HST, and failure to register can trigger statutory requirements despite the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of SaaS and other intangible personal property/services to a non-resident recipient are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the supply is delivered outside Canada or for use/consumption outside Canada and the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant dealing with the supply as part of its commercial activity. Tangible exported goods qualify when exported 'as soon as is reasonably possible' after transfer/payment, with evidence retained.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A) or carrier/customs documentation evidencing export for goods
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or carrier certificate of export proving departure from Canada
  • Inward foreign exchange remittance / payment receipt from the non-resident customer for services or goods
  • Commercial invoice and contract / order documentation showing the zero-rated supply and non-resident recipient
  • Internal business records (correspondence, server logs, or customer onboarding records) supporting delivery outside Canada for SaaS

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, electronic stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime. Electronic invoices are permitted if they satisfy the prescribed information requirements under the Excise Tax Act. Returns can be filed electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or EDI APIs, but transaction-level clearance, digital signature, or API integration is not required.

CRA may reassess within the normal reassessment period (four years after registration/return date; unlimited if misrepresentation), claw back zero-rated treatment, assess GST/HST plus interest, and impose a false-statement penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the amount sought to be evaded. Deficient books-and-records and absence of export evidence can also lead to denial of input tax credits, gross-negligence prosecution, and criminal fines if false claims are knowingly made.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has been an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction since 1952. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible with no prior regulatory approval, no repatriation license, and no profit-remittance hurdle for foreign partners of an Alberta LP. SaaS subscription revenue may be converted at any Canadian or foreign bank and distributed to non-resident partners without Bank of Canada approval. The only operational frictions are bank-level FINTRAC cross-border wire diligence and OFAC/UN sanctions screening, plus CRS/FATCA self-certification of each partner.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None apply. Canada repealed its Foreign Exchange Control Regulations in 1951 and no statute, regulation, or OSFI rule imposes a surrender ratio, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion of export receipts. USD and multi-currency SaaS proceeds received from Paddle/Lemon Squeezy may be held offshore, swept into a Canadian USD account, or converted freely. The sole routine obligation is FINTRAC reporting of international electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more in a 24-hour period, which the servicing bank files automatically.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); bank supervision delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CTF supervision and FINTRAC reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); Canada has no foreign-exchange licensing or exchange-control authority. Finance Canada administers the Investment Canada Act; its review thresholds (C$1.335B enterprise value for WTO-investor acquisitions in 2024) are irrelevant to a greenfield micro-SaaS limited partnership.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Wise Business, Payoneer, and similar foreign e-money institutions (EMIs) cannot offer CDIC-insured deposits or Canadian clearing sort codes and hold client funds as unsecured e-money, not bank-deposit liabilities. OSFI-regulated banks will treat inbound sweeps from EMIs as elevated-risk wire receipts and may require origination evidence. Conversely, Paddle and Lemon Squeezy (Merchant of Record) are fully viable settlement rails for a consumer micro-SaaS and remit via SWIFT/ACH to an underlying Canadian bank account. Therefore, the blueprint is: Paddle/Lemon Squeezy as the customer-facing payment rail, a Schedule I bank account as the primary settlement vault, and an EMI only as an optional FX convenience wallet.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital contributions route via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire/CHIPS for US partners) to the LP's Schedule I bank account at a Canadian financial institution. The wire must include beneficiary bank SWIFT BIC, the LP's CAD or USD account number, and a purpose narrative such as 'Capital contribution – Alberta Limited Partnership'. The bank classifies the credit under IMF BPM6 Direct Investment (equity in a Canadian partnership) and credits the capital account without mandatory CAD conversion. FINTRAC international EFT reporting applies to any wire of C$10,000 or more, so the bank will require a source-of-funds statement and the partnership's capital account schedule. No Investment Canada Act filing is triggered because the transaction is a greenfield formation, not the acquisition of control of an existing Canadian business.Operational trade disbursements use SWIFT MT103 for international vendor payments and contractor compensation; USD payments to US vendors settle via Fedwire/ACH, while CAD payments settle via CPA Lynx (formerly LVTS) for same-day clearing. A commercially import letter of credit (UCP 600) is legally permissible but commercially disproportionate for a micro-SaaS; the practical rails are (a) Paddle/Lemon Squeezy MoR settlement sweeps, (b) standard MT103 outbound wires, (c) US ACH debits for infrastructure costs, and (d) partner distributions by wire subject to the LP agreement's waterfall and the bank's OFAC/UN sanctions screening. Every outbound wire must cite a concrete commercial purpose (e.g., 'SaaS infrastructure hosting', 'contractor compensation') and correspond to executed invoices on file to survive bank trade-surveillance review.Deliverable CAD/USD vanilla FX forwards under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex, executed with an OSFI-regulated Schedule I dealer.CAD/USD cross-currency swaps (fixed-for-floating) permitted where the LP's SaaS revenue is USD-denominated and CAD operating expenses create an underlying exposure.European-style purchased CAD/USD FX options; naked written options are subject to bank suitability and derivatives risk limits and are generally unavailable to a micro-LP.CME Group exchange-traded CAD/USD futures and options on futures, available with central clearing and margin collateral.FX swaps (spot/forward rollovers) for short-term cash-flow hedging of subscription receipts.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are structurally unnecessary for CAD because the currency is freely and fully deliverable; any onshore dealer would require proof of underlying exposure, and speculative offshore NDFs would fail OSFI conduct standards.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Not taxable at the LP level (flow-through). Combined federal+Alberta headlin rates for a corporate partner with a permanent establishment in Alberta: 23% (15% federal + 8% Alberta); small-business corporate partner: 11% (9% federal + 2% Alberta). Individual partners are taxed at their marginal federal+Alberta personal rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 3 months after the fiscal period-end if all partners are Canadian-resident individuals; otherwise within 5 months after the fiscal period-end; but never later than the earliest T2 filing due date of any corporate partner (6 months after that corporation's year-end).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before the LP/partnership's tax-filing deadline (i.e., 3 or 5 months after fiscal year-end as applicable; where a corporate partner exists, aligned with the T2 due date of 6 months after that partner's year-end).
