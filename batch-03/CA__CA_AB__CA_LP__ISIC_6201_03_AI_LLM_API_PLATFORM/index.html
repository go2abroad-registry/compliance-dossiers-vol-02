Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) — official national portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account; Alberta counterpart for LP/registry compliance: Alberta Corporate Registry and Alberta Finance and Treasury Board / Tax and Revenue Administration.

E-government filing portal

Canada does not issue a standalone TIN/VAT ID; the CRA Business Number (BN) is the statutory tax identifier and the GST/HST program account (BN ending in RT0001) is the VAT ID. An Alberta LP obtains the BN as follows: (1) file the Declaration of Limited Partnership with the Alberta Corporate Registry (or extra-provincial registration if formed outside Alberta) and obtain the Alberta entity number; (2) file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) through CRA Business Registration Online, using the non-resident registration route and attaching a certified certificate of existence/good standing for every foreign partner or foreign LP; (3) declare the intended GST/HST registration, available ITC activity, and Canadian place of business/proposed permanent establishment; (4) receive from CRA the BN and activate the GST/HST account; (5) if non-resident partners will receive Canadian-source income, open the applicable Part XIII/Part I withholding account; and (6) maintain the BN, Alberta registry number, and foreign TINs in the required CRA books and records for at least six years.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — the Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and Alberta income tax; the partnership files no entity-level income tax (a T5013 information return is filed only for allocation reporting) and each partner reports allocated shares of income/loss. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is a separate statutory person and may be a small supplier or mandatory/voluntary registrant; this indirect-tax status does not alter direct-tax pass-through treatment. — Canada does not issue a standalone TIN/VAT ID; the CRA Business Number (BN) is the statutory tax identifier and the GST/HST program account (BN ending in RT0001) is the VAT ID. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and Alberta income tax; the partnership files no entity-level income tax (a T5013 information return is filed only for allocation reporting) and each partner reports allocated shares of income/loss. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is a separate statutory person and may be a small supplier or mandatory/voluntary registrant; this indirect-tax status does not alter direct-tax pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

No Canadian analogue to the U.S. check-the-box election exists, and an Alberta LP cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation. The principal elections relevant to this structure are: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration election under section 240(2) of the Excise Tax Act if below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; (ii) partnership fiscal-period election and partner fiscal-period alignment elections under the Income Tax Act; and (iii) certain Alberta Corporate Registry elections regarding extra-provincial registration. Hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act apply automatically and do not offer a check-the-box or elective remedial mechanism; no election is available to avoid hybrid mismatch adjustments.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada accepts Article VIII obligations of the IMF and observes the OECD Codes of Liberalisation. No central bank approval, exchange permit, or capital account certificate is required for capital contributions, foreign revenue remittances, loan repayments, or distributions to non-resident limited partners of an Alberta Limited Partnership. Operational frictions are limited to: (i) Part XIII withholding tax at 25% on allocated income distributions to non-resident partners unless reduced by treaty (with appropriate NR3016/NR302 forms); (ii) FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting by financial intermediaries for any cross-border electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) bank-level source-of-funds verification where inflows originate from crypto-assets such as USDC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, no repatriation window, and no statutory conversion ratio on export proceeds or AI API service fees. USD, EUR, or multi-currency balances may be retained indefinitely in corporate treasury accounts. The only compliance element is transactional: every cross-border wire at or above CAD 10,000 executed through a Canadian financial institution must generate a large cross-border currency transaction report filed with FINTRAC, requiring the LP to maintain accurate originator and beneficiary details and a substantiated purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) in a prudential supervisory role, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CTF enforcement under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The federal Bank Act, SC 1991, c. 46, and the Canadian Payments Act govern clearing and settlement, while provincial securities regulators (Alberta Securities Commission) and the Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA) monitor stablecoin dealings that may constitute securities or derivatives.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for merchant collections and fast FX conversion; Low for primary treasury custody, escrow, or capital subscription banking. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Payoneer, and Mercury are not 'banks' under the Bank Act and cannot offer Canadian deposit-taking, CDIC insurance, or direct Lynx RTGS settlement. They must register as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC if they service Canadian clients. Deposits held with such EMIs are not CDIC-insured and may be subject to segregated client money rules, which are inadequate for LP capital escrow. Recommended structure: maintain the Schedule I bank treasury anchor for all capital and tax-sensitive flows; use a FINTRAC-registered EMI exclusively as a 26-market collection sleeve for token receipts, with automated daily sweeps of funds to the primary bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign limited partner capital injections must be routed by SWIFT MT202/MT103 to the LP's designated Capital Escrow Account at a Schedule I bank, referencing the specific subscription agreement and capital call number in the remittance narrative. The LP must provide a capital contributions register and source-of-funds letters. For USD contributions, funds may be held in a USD suspense account and converted to CAD via a spot contract, or retained for direct payment of USD-denominated cloud compute costs. No CRA approval is required for capital additions, but each partner's capital account must be properly recorded to support T5013 and any future NR4 withholding calculations.
