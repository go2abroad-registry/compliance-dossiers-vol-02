Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for Alberta limited partnerships. Contributions are fixed by the Limited Partnership Agreement and may be cash, property, or promissory notes; no escrow account is mandated for capital contributions. Form LP-1 must declare the contributions made or to be made by each limited partner. At least one general partner must be resident in Canada.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), under the Minister of National Revenue; CRA Business Portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-portal.html; Alberta corporate and partnership income tax is administered by CRA under the Canada–Alberta Tax Collection Agreement; Alberta Registries administers LP registration under the Partnership Act (RSA 2000, c P-3).

E-government filing portal

The Alberta Limited Partnership must first be registered with Alberta Registries (or, if formed outside Alberta, registered as an extra-provincial limited partnership). For federal tax identity, the LP applies for a CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or, for non-resident partnerships, by filing RC391 (Request for a Business Number and Certain CRA Program Accounts) or by contacting CRA Business Registration. The BN is the statutory tax identification number; the LP must open a BN/GST-HST program account if taxable supplies in Canada exceed the small-supplier threshold. The LP files partnership information returns on Form T5013 when it carries on business in Canada and has Canadian-resident partners or Canadian-source income; non-resident partners may require a Canadian representative for service of process. Alberta does not issue a separate provincial tax ID for a limited partnership; provincial income tax liabilities are settled through CRA-administered returns using the same BN.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. An Alberta LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act: the partnership is not generally a taxpayer; each partner includes its share of partnership income or loss. For GST/HST purposes, however, a partnership is treated as a separate person under subsection 272(1) of the Excise Tax Act and may be a GST/HST registrant in its own capacity. A non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada creates Canadian-source business income for its non-resident partners, subject to Part I tax and information-reporting obligations.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election is available under Canadian law. Entity classification is determined by statutory and common-law legal form. An Alberta LP is a partnership, not an electable corporation. Canada's hybrid-mismatch rules (Income Tax Act sections 18.2 and 18.3) may reduce or deny deductions for payments involving hybrid entities, but they do not provide an affirmative check-the-box election. No other relevant entity classification elections apply to an Alberta LP for federal income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: a supplier becomes required to register once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. Non-resident suppliers of digital services and remote cross-border supplies using the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration framework are subject to the same CAD 30,000 economic threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (i) tangible personal property exported by the vendor from Canada under Schedule VI, Part V, Division I of the Excise Tax Act, provided documentary evidence of export is obtained; and (ii) services and intangible personal property, including mobile app licences and downloads, supplied to non-resident persons, provided the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada and is not for use wholly in Canada. For app-store sales, the vendor must establish non-resident customer status through records such as billing addresses, app-store territory data, IP addresses, and the customer's download account country. Supplies of digital apps to Canadian residents through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing are generally subject to 5% GST unless a statutory zero-rating provision applies. Zero-rating is claimed on the GST/HST return and may give rise to a refund.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or CERS electronic export reporting) for physical exports where required by the Customs Act; not applicable to pure digital downloads
  • Commercial invoice and app-store sales report from Apple IAP or Google Play Billing showing transaction-level sales territory
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or other carrier transport document for physical exports
  • Evidence of export such as customs stamp, export coder, carrier certificate, or logistics release proof
  • Executed software licence agreement, terms of service, or app-store merchant agreement demonstrating supply is to a non-resident recipient
  • App-store settlement statement, bank statement, or inward remittance/foreign-currency records evidencing receipt of foreign payment

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or government e-stamping regime for GST/HST. Registrants must issue sales invoices or simplified receipts containing prescribed information under section 221 of the Excise Tax Act, and must retain electronic records that support the tax position. Returns and payments are submitted electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE/portal system, but this is not a real-time clearance mechanism. Where Apple IAP or Google Play Billing is used, the app-store payment reports and settlement statements must be retained and reconciled to the LP's books for audit support.

CRA regularly audits export zero-rating claims. Failure to maintain sufficient documentary evidence can result in reassessment of output GST/HST, interest, and penalties, plus retroactive clawback of previously allowed zero-rated refunds. The CRA may reassess within four years of the assessment date, or seven years where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect is shown. Civil penalties may include the greater of fixed amounts and percentages of understated tax, and gross-negligence or false-statement penalties can reach 50% of the net understatement. Egregious cases may be referred for criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and the Criminal Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible hard currency. Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement, prohibiting exchange restrictions on current international transactions and discriminatory currency arrangements; capital-account movements are likewise unrestricted. There is no convertibility licensing, no central-bank approval for capital inflows, no exchange allocation, and no statutory restriction on profit remittance by a non-resident limited partner. Practical hurdles are fiscal and documentary only: a non-resident partner is subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on taxable allocations or distributions (default 25%, reduced under an applicable income tax treaty), and to CRS/FATCA reporting. The Alberta limited partnership must also satisfy bank-level source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership verification when converting foreign-currency inflows into CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation deadline, or compulsory conversion ratio for export proceeds or app-store revenues. An Alberta LP may retain Apple IAP and Google Play Billing proceeds in USD/EUR or any currency and convert to CAD at will. The only statutory obligations are informational: every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by the financial intermediary to FINTRAC as an Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR), and any person physically importing or exporting currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more must file the Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report (CBSA Form E677). No central-bank declaration is required for standard SWIFT business payments below those thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), constituted under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), is the central monetary authority. Canada maintains no standalone foreign-exchange control administration; prudential supervision of banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), payment-system governance in Payments Canada, and cross-border anti-money-laundering surveillance in FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The Minister of Finance retains residual statutory powers over exchange-rate intervention through the Currency Act and the Exchange Fund Act, although intervention is exceptional. For a mobile utility app business, no sector-specific foreign-investment screening applies under the Investment Canada Act at the limited-partnership scale.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM-HIGH as a secondary clearing engine; LOW as the sole or settlement bank. Canadian law reserves deposit-taking to chartered banks, trust and loan companies, and cooperative-credit associations; an offshore digital EMI (e.g., a UK FCA-authorised or EU-licensed electronic-money institution) cannot legally present itself as a Canadian bank or offer CDIC-insured accounts to the LP. An EMI may, however, register as a Foreign Money Services Business (MSB) via FINTRAC and, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPA) with its Bank of Canada registration regime becoming effective in 2025, may also require registration as a Retail Payment Service Provider. Practically, a registered EMI (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, or similar) may hold pre-funded balances in an omnibus account at an authorised Canadian bank, execute foreign-currency payouts, and offer tight-margin FX conversion; those balances remain uninsured and rank as unsecured claims in an EMI insolvency. Recommended structure: operate a Tier-1 bank account as the ledger-of-record, use the EMI only for vendor disbursement, mass payouts, and spot FX optimisation, and sweep the EMI ledger back to the Tier-1 account at least monthly. Direct deposit of Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlements into an EMI must be screened against geographical prohibition lists (e.g., OFAC sanctions), and the EMI must maintain a compliant merchant descriptor to avoid transaction misclassification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All non-resident LP contributions route as SWIFT MT103 wire transfers (or Canadian wire for domestic partners) to the Capital Escrow Account at the Tier-1 bank. Each credit must be pre-registered by a capital-call notice containing: (a) the LP's full legal name, (b) capital-account number, (c) contribution amount and currency, (d) source-country partner name and address, and (e) the payment reference 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - [PARTNER NAME] - [ACCOUNT NUMBER]'. For USD remittances to a Canadian-dollar escrow account, the sponsoring bank typically receives funds at its U.S. correspondent (e.g., BMO Bank N.A. or RBC Bank) and forwards to Canada through SWIFT, converting to CAD at a specified rate. The bank will tag the purpose under the Canadian Balance of Payments classification as 'FDI inflow - equity participation' for Statistics Canada reporting. FINTRAC automatically receives EFTR filings on any single or cumulative 24-hour transaction at or above CAD 10,000. At amounts above CAD 1 million, the bank will require each partner's source-of-funds attestation before crediting and will not accept third-party payments (payments originating from an account not in the partner's own name). Clearing latency: 1-3 business days for USD/EUR/GBP; same-day for CAD-denominated wires originating within Payments Canada.The treasury deploys four clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for all international supplier invoices, intercompany licensing fees, contractor payroll, and non-resident distributions, each MT103 including an electronic beneficiary reference and the appropriate purpose phrase (for distributions: 'DISTRIBUTION PER PARTNERSHIP RESOLUTION [DATE]'); (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) for high-value equipment or outsourcing contracts above C$50,000, issued by the Tier-1 bank and confirmed by a beneficiary-country advising bank; (3) ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) / EFT for all CAD-denominated domestic vendor payments, insurance, and hosting subscriptions, executed via the bank treasury interface with next-day settlement; and (4) PSP aggregation for mass low-value payouts (e.g., affiliate, bug-bounty, or beta-testing networks) routed through Wise or Airwallex or the bank's virtual-account module, avoiding fractional SWIFT fees. Apple IAP and Google Play Billing net settlements arrive by electronic transfer from Apple Distribution International (Ireland) or Google Asia Pacific (Singapore), as applicable, and are swept automatically to the Operational Account. If CAD conversion is sought, FX is priced under a two-way quote from the bank's treasury; otherwise the funds may be retained in USD under a USD sub-ledger. Non-resident partner distributions require a formal General Partner resolution, Part XIII withholding may be applied inside the wire (funds sent net of Canadian withholding tax), and the T5013 reporting will be completed by the tax preparer.Deliverable vanilla FX forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP, CAD/CNH) up to 24-month tenors under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Canadian credit-support annex; suitable for locking the CAD/USD conversion of quarterly Apple and Google proceeds or of capital-call draws.Plain-vanilla European FX options (purchases only) providing asymmetric budget protection for anticipated capital expenditures (e.g., USD-denominated cloud procurement); resale of options is treated as speculative trading and is prohibited under the treasury policy.Cross-currency swaps (XCCY/CCIRS) converting USD-denominated operational obligations into CAD-floating or CAD-fixed equivalents for multi-year supplier contracts, with each leg separately documented.CME Group and Montreal Exchange (MX) CAD/USD and CAD/EUR futures, used only when the underlying exposure notional is equal or larger and executed exclusively through a registered derivatives dealer; transactions may be physically or cash-settled at the LP's election.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for genuinely non-convertible emerging-market settlement currencies (e.g., CNH, INR, IDR) where the app treasury faces content-localisation, advertising, or distribution costs; NDFs on CAD are not market-conventional because the CAD spot market is fully deliverable and unrestricted.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: MEDIUM-HIGH as a secondary clearing engine; LOW as the sole or settlement bank. Canadian law reserves deposit-taking to chartered banks, trust and loan companies, and cooperative-credit associations; an offshore digital EMI (e.g., a UK FCA-authorised or EU-licensed electronic-money institution) cannot legally present itself as a Canadian bank or offer CDIC-insured accounts to the LP. An EMI may, however, register as a Foreign Money Services Business (MSB) via FINTRAC and, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPA) with its Bank of Canada registration regime becoming effective in 2025, may also require registration as a Retail Payment Service Provider. Practically, a registered EMI (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, or similar) may hold pre-funded balances in an omnibus account at an authorised Canadian bank, execute foreign-currency payouts, and offer tight-margin FX conversion; those balances remain uninsured and rank as unsecured claims in an EMI insolvency. Recommended structure: operate a Tier-1 bank account as the ledger-of-record, use the EMI only for vendor disbursement, mass payouts, and spot FX optimisation, and sweep the EMI ledger back to the Tier-1 account at least monthly. Direct deposit of Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlements into an EMI must be screened against geographical prohibition lists (e.g., OFAC sanctions), and the EMI must maintain a compliant merchant descriptor to avoid transaction misclassification.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate income tax rate for Alberta corporations: 15% federal plus 8% Alberta. A qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation may generally access an 11% combined small-business rate on eligible active business income up to the applicable CAD 500,000 business limit. A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and does not itself pay corporate income tax; income and losses are allocated to partners.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on taxable dividends paid or credited to a non-resident, subject to an applicable tax treaty. Common treaty outcomes include 5% for a qualifying corporate shareholder meeting ownership and limitation-on-benefits conditions and 15% for other qualifying dividends. The exact rate depends on the recipient's residence, beneficial ownership, ownership percentage, treaty article, and anti-treaty-shopping rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally due within five months after the end of the partnership fiscal period. For a December 31 year-end, the normal deadline is May 31 of the following year. T5013 slips and partner information must be provided within the same statutory filing cycle.

Deadline: · Frequency:

A corporation generally files within six months after its taxation year-end. Any balance is generally due two months after year-end, or three months for qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations meeting statutory conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions is the principal statutory threshold commonly relevant to contemporaneous documentation requirements and Form T106 reporting, subject to the precise transaction categories and exceptions. For multinational enterprise groups, the Canadian country-by-country reporting threshold is generally EUR 750,000,000 of consolidated group revenue, converted under the applicable rules. Canadian rules do not use a universal OECD-style Local File threshold for every transaction; documentation must be sufficient to support arm's-length pricing whenever section 247 applies.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone general Economic Substance Regulation regime equivalent to certain offshore ESR statutes. Substance is nevertheless relevant under the Income Tax Act, including the general anti-avoidance rule, sham doctrine, beneficial-ownership principles, transfer-pricing rules, thin-capitalization rules, foreign-affiliate and FAPI rules, treaty anti-abuse provisions, and principal-purpose or limitation-on-benefits provisions. An LP claiming treaty or Canadian tax outcomes should maintain genuine business activity, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, decision-making records, contractual control, risk assumption, IP functions, and commercially coherent cash flows.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, arrears interest, gross-negligence penalty generally equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit, civil fraud penalties, and criminal prosecution. Tax evasion convictions can result in fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years under the Income Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face prosecution, fines, imprisonment, and director-liability assessments for unremitted source deductions where statutory conditions are met. Corporate separateness is not a shield for personal participation in fraud. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty but may arise through criminal-process orders, bail conditions, or enforcement proceedings.
Failure to Withhold or Remit Non-Resident Tax

Statutory sanction: Payer assessment for the unwithheld amount, applicable late-remittance penalties, interest, failure-to-file penalties, and potential director liability for source deductions and remittances.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for corporate source deductions and withholding amounts where the statutory due-diligence defense is unavailable. Criminal liability may arise for deliberate obstruction, falsification, or fraud.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust Canadian income under Income Tax Act section 247, impose a transfer-pricing penalty generally equal to 10% of the amount of an adjustment not reasonably documented or reported, deny deductions, reassess withholding, and apply interest. Recharacterization may occur where the legal form does not reflect the commercial substance.

Director personal liability: Directors are not ordinarily personally liable merely because an adjustment is made, but personal exposure can arise from knowing participation in false records, fraud, obstruction, source-deduction failures, or criminal tax conduct.
Partnership Misclassification and Loss Trading

Statutory sanction: Denial or reallocation of partnership losses, application of at-risk and limited-partner rules, reassessment of partners, interest, penalties, and possible GAAR application. A partnership may also be recharacterized for specific tax purposes based on its legal and commercial facts.

Director personal liability: General partners may have broader contractual and statutory liability than limited partners. Limited partners may lose limited-liability protection if they participate in management contrary to applicable provincial law or if the structure is otherwise disregarded. Fraudulent conduct can create personal civil and criminal exposure.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under beneficial-ownership, limitation-on-benefits, principal-purpose, anti-conduit, or GAAR principles; reassessment of withholding; interest; penalties; and possible prosecution where records are knowingly false.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly establish or operate a conduit structure may face civil recovery, director-liability claims, penalties, and criminal prosecution. A travel ban is not automatic but may be imposed by a court in criminal proceedings.
Undeclared Foreign Affiliate, Foreign Asset, or Related-Party Reporting

Statutory sanction: Failure-to-file penalties, extended reassessment periods, denial of deductions or foreign tax relief, interest, gross-negligence penalties, and prosecution for deliberate concealment. Applicable filings may include T106, T1134, T1135, and country-by-country reports.

Director personal liability: Personal liability generally depends on the specific tax, withholding, payroll, or criminal provision. Knowing falsification or obstruction may expose directors and officers to fines and imprisonment.
GST/HST and Digital-Commerce Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Assessment of uncollected GST/HST, interest, late-filing and late-remittance penalties, denial of unsupported input tax credits, and possible prosecution for fraud or deliberate evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unremitted GST/HST in accordance with statutory director-liability provisions, subject to available due-diligence defenses. Deliberate evasion can result in criminal fines and imprisonment.
AML, Proceeds of Crime, and Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Asset seizure and forfeiture, administrative monetary penalties, prosecution under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and Criminal Code, and sanctions-related criminal or administrative consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and beneficial owners may face personal fines, imprisonment, forfeiture, prohibition orders, and court-imposed travel or bail restrictions. Corporate liability does not prevent prosecution of individuals who authorized, directed, or knowingly facilitated the conduct.
Records Destruction and Obstruction

Statutory sanction: Record-keeping penalties, adverse evidentiary inferences, extended reassessment periods, gross-negligence or fraud penalties, prosecution, fines, and imprisonment for obstruction or related criminal conduct.

Director personal liability: Individuals who direct or participate in destruction, obstruction, or fabrication may be personally prosecuted and imprisoned. Civil veil-piercing is fact-specific and is not automatic solely because the corporation or LP is insolvent.
