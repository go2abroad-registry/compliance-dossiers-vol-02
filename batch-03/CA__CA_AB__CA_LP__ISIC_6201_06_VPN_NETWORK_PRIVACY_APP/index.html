Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership, and no escrow account is required. The limited partners must make the capital contribution set out in the limited partnership agreement, and distributions or capital withdrawals are restricted where the partnership would be rendered insolvent. The contribution may consist of cash or property, but a limited partner who contributes services may be treated as a general partner under Alberta law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification must be secured under the Alberta Fire Code for any occupied office space, together with a building occupancy certificate or inspection clearance. Occupational health and safety clearance is required under the Occupational Health and Safety Code for workstations, electrical installations, emergency exits, and IP/telecom equipment rooms; no hazardous occupancy clearance is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Alberta Finance. CRA business portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/account-services/business-account.html). Alberta Corporate Registry handles extra-provincial limited partnership registration.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the foreign limited partnership as an extra-provincial LP with the Alberta Corporate Registry via the Alberta Business Portal to obtain the Alberta entity/registration number. Step 2: Request a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Form RC1 or through the Business Registration Online service. Step 3: Open a GST/HST (RC) tax account under the BN for federal indirect tax purposes; open a payroll or corporate income tax account only if the partnership is itself a statutory taxpayer, which is not the default because a Canadian LP is fiscally transparent. Step 4: Use the CRA BN as the general tax identification number on all returns, customs declarations, and cross-border documentation. Non-resident partners may be required to file or be identified through CRA residency disclosure processes, but the LP is the registrant for GST/HST and payroll where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — under Canadian Income Tax Act (ITA), a Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent; the LP itself is not a taxpayer, and taxable income or loss is allocated to partners under ITA s.96 and taxed at the partner level. Non-resident partners remain subject to Canadian withholding taxes or Part I tax on Canadian-source partnership income depending on treaty and residency.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box or entity classification election; foreign entity characterization follows domestic legal form and treaty residence rules. A Canadian LP may elect a non-calendar fiscal period under ITA s.96(8), and partners may jointly elect or revoke under ITA s.96(8.4). For GST/HST purposes, the LP may elect a reporting period and year-end under Excise Tax Act s.243. Hybrid mismatch and loss-timing rules are addressed through ITA provisions rather than elective check-the-box regimes; no standalone hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 measured either in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s.148(1)). For non-resident digital service suppliers, the same CAD 30,000 de minimis applies to supplies made to Canadian customers, although a simplified GST/HST registration scheme is available for large-volume offshore digital service and platform operators.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods exported from Canada, supplies are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s.1 where possession of the goods transfers in Canada but delivery occurs outside Canada. For VPN and digital services/intangible personal property, a supply made to a non-resident customer is generally zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 or s.21 where the service or intangible is not performed or used in connection with real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property in Canada, or a supply of such property to the recipient. The supplier must maintain contemporaneous evidence that the recipient is a non-resident: registered business address or individual residential address outside Canada, IP geo-location for digital products, contractual delivery outside Canada, and payment from a non-Canadian bank account or FX payment gateway.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) declaration or B13A Export Declaration, plus applicable export permits, for physical hardware or media where goods physically leave Canada
  • Commercial invoice and signed service contract or terms of service clearly stating the non-resident customer, the service description (VPN/privacy software subscription), and the basis for zero-rating under Schedule VI
  • Bank-issued SWIFT/FX wire confirmation or inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident customer in foreign convertible currency

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is mandated by the CRA. A GST/HST registrant must issue invoices in the prescribed form under Excise Tax Act s.221, including vendor BN, transaction description, and tax status. Electronic records must be retained when the books are kept electronically. Canada has introduced a voluntary Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing standard based on the Peppol framework, but it is not yet mandatory and does not involve a real-time government clearance or API digital stamping requirement.

CRA may reassess an exporter within four years for ordinary cases, or ten years in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, fraud, or willful default. If zero-rating is unsupported by documents, the GST becomes demandable, subject to arrears interest and late-filing penalties. Gross negligence penalties under Excise Tax Act s.285 equal to 50% of the understated tax can be imposed for knowingly participating in false documentation, and criminal evasion under s.327 may result in fines of up to CAD 100,000 and two years' imprisonment for third-party tax preparers; for corporate offenders, fines can reach CAD 500,000. Export misstatement can also trigger a clawback of input tax credits previously attributed to exported supplies plus a 25% penalty on the tax amount involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, bank screening, and transaction-specific legal restrictions. There is no general approval requirement to convert or remit legitimate business funds. Banks may require source-of-funds evidence and commercial documentation. A non-resident partner's Canadian-source income may trigger Canadian tax reporting or withholding depending on the income characterization and applicable rules; partnership allocations and distributions should be reviewed separately from the cash remittance, with treaty eligibility and withholding obligations assessed before payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory deadline requiring exporters to repatriate export proceeds and no mandatory foreign-currency conversion or surrender ratio. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign currency or held in domestic or foreign accounts, subject to contractual terms, sanctions, tax, reporting, and bank compliance requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority). Canada has no general foreign-exchange control administration or exchange-control licensing regime. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions supervises federally regulated banks, while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada administers federal anti-money-laundering reporting and compliance requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and cross-border payout services; low as a substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking bank account or for holding material operating balances. A foreign electronic money institution does not thereby become authorized to take deposits in Canada. Confirm the provider's Canadian availability, safeguarding and insolvency terms, CAD settlement capability, and allocation of responsibility for Canadian AML obligations. A provider conducting money services business in Canada may be subject to FINTRAC registration and compliance requirements. Stripe and Paymentwall may support eligible payment processing, but acceptance is subject to underwriting, product terms, and applicable payment-network rules.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A Canadian limited partnership is generally not a separate income-tax-paying entity. Its income or loss is allocated to its partners under the Income Tax Act. For a taxable Canadian corporation partner earning general-rate income, the combined federal and Alberta rate is generally 23% (15% federal plus 8% Alberta); eligible small-business income may generally be taxed at a combined 11% (9% federal plus 2% Alberta), subject to eligibility and business-limit rules. Rates depend on the partner, income character, and applicable tax year.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

A partnership distribution is generally not a dividend merely because it is paid to a partner; its Canadian tax treatment follows the underlying allocated income and applicable partnership rules. Where a Canadian corporation pays a dividend to a non-resident shareholder, the domestic Part XIII withholding rate is generally 25%, subject to treaty reduction and statutory conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The T5013 return and partner slips are generally due within 5 months after the partnership's fiscal period-end. A partner's income tax return is due under the deadline applicable to that partner; a corporate partner's T2 return is generally due 6 months after its taxation year-end, with tax payment generally due earlier.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million is a commonly relevant aggregate threshold for T106 reporting of covered non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents, not a general threshold below which the arm's-length principle or contemporaneous-documentation obligations cease to apply. Canada does not impose a universal Local File/Master File filing threshold in the same form as some jurisdictions. Taxpayers must make or obtain contemporaneous documentation under Income Tax Act section 247; the statutory documentation deadline is generally the taxpayer's documentation due date for the relevant year.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no general corporate Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Canadian tax consequences nevertheless depend on the actual legal and commercial arrangements, residence and permanent-establishment facts, transfer-pricing arm's-length terms, beneficial ownership, and applicable anti-avoidance provisions, including the general anti-avoidance rule. Alberta has no separate general ESR regime.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer pricing and non-resident related-party transactions

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 247 permits transfer-pricing adjustments and a penalty generally equal to 10% of specified net adjustments where statutory conditions are met, including materiality and documentation requirements. T106 and other information-return penalties may also apply, in addition to tax, interest, and potential treaty adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors are not ordinarily personally liable for the partnership's income tax solely by reason of office. Personal exposure may arise under specific statutory director-liability provisions, for knowing participation in offences, or under applicable partnership law; the limited-partner's liability protection depends on compliance with governing law and the partnership agreement.
Late, incomplete, or false tax information returns

Statutory sanction: Daily and fixed information-return penalties, interest on unpaid or unremitted amounts, and potentially gross-negligence or criminal consequences may apply under the Income Tax Act. A payer may remain liable for required withholding plus interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate payer can be jointly and severally liable for specified unremitted source deductions or withholdings under statutory conditions, subject to due-diligence and limitation rules. Liability is not automatic and depends on the payer's legal form and the facts.
Unremitted non-resident withholding tax

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for the withholding shortfall, interest, and statutory penalties. A recipient's treaty claim does not by itself relieve the payer of withholding obligations where required evidence was not obtained.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate payer may face statutory liability for certain unremitted amounts if the legal prerequisites are met. A limited partnership's general partner may have exposure under partnership and other applicable laws; the result is distinct from ordinary limited-partner liability.
Tax evasion, fraud, or wilful false statements

Statutory sanction: Conviction under the Income Tax Act can result in fines and imprisonment; penalties may include fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years, with higher exposure for certain indictable offences. Exact charges and penalties depend on the offence and prosecution.

Director personal liability: Individuals who authorize, participate in, or direct an offence may be prosecuted personally. Civil tax assessments, fines, and imprisonment are distinct from piercing the corporate veil; travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty.
Sham, nominee, or misrepresented partnership arrangements

Statutory sanction: Authorities may recharacterize transactions, deny tax benefits, assess tax, interest, and penalties, and pursue applicable tax, corporate, or criminal remedies. A sham finding is fact-specific; an LP or non-resident ownership is not unlawful by itself.

Director personal liability: General partners and individuals involved may incur liability under the governing partnership statute and applicable civil or criminal laws. Limited liability is not a defense to an individual's own fraud or statutory offence; travel bans are not an automatic consequence.
