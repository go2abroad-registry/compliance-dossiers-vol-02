Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, including CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account portal, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export/import declarations; Alberta Registries (Service Alberta) for limited partnership registration under Alberta’s Partnership Act.

E-government filing portal

The CRA Business Number (BN) is the Canadian federal tax identification number; the GST/HST program account is the corresponding VAT-type identifier. Step 1: Register the Alberta Limited Partnership with Alberta Registries under the Partnership Act and obtain the Alberta LP registration certificate and entity number. Step 2: Apply for the CRA Business Number through Business Registration Online; select the entity type for a registered partnership, provide Alberta as the province, and identify the general partner(s). Step 3: Open the required CRA program account for GST/HST (program code RT) if the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier, or if voluntary GST/HST registration is desired; a separate partnership income tax account is not required because the LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes. Step 4: Receive the final BN and program account number, and use it for GST/HST filing, invoicing, and input tax credit claims. Step 5: If non-resident partners are present, file the applicable T5013 partnership information return and supporting schedules, maintain partnership records in Canada, and ensure any required non-resident withholding obligations are met.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no federal check-the-box election or elective entity-classification regime; an Alberta LP is mandatorily treated as a fiscally transparent partnership under the Income Tax Act unless a foreign jurisdiction independently classifies it for its own tax purposes. Available elections relevant to an LP include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier under the Excise Tax Act; (ii) an election to use a non-calendar fiscal period for the partnership; and (iii) specified partnership-related elections under the Income Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch and anti-hybrid rules are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions, not elections, and may recharacterize arrangements involving a foreign transparent entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. A person is generally a small supplier exempt from mandatory GST/HST registration where the total value of taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, made in Canada in the current calendar quarter or the preceding four calendar quarters does not exceed CAD 30,000. For non-resident digital-economy suppliers of remote digital services or intangibles into Canada, a separate CAD 30,000 annual revenue de minimis applies to the mandatory simplified GST/HST registration regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An Alberta LP may apply zero-rated treatment to exported supplies under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. ERP/CRM and vertical cloud services are eligible as exported supplies of services or intangible personal property where the recipient is a non-resident of Canada, the recipient is not registered for GST/HST in respect of the supply, and the service or software is not used, consumed, or performed in Canada. Physical exports must be exported by the seller and must satisfy the CBSA documentary rules. Exports of goods, offshore services, and software licences to non-residents may therefore be invoiced at 0% GST/HST, with input tax credits on related Canadian inputs claimed through the GST/HST return.

  • CBSA customs export declaration (B13A or approved electronic export reporting) where physical goods are exported
  • Bill of lading or airway waybill showing shipment from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and executed supply agreement describing the ERP/CRM cloud service, contract parties, and contractual basis for zero-rating
  • Bank payment record, SWIFT confirmation, or inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of foreign-currency consideration
  • Evidence of non-resident status of the recipient, such as certificate of incorporation, tax residence certificate, or signed declaration confirming the supply is not used in Canada

Canada does not operate a national real-time tax clearance, continuous transaction-control, digital stamping, or mandatory API e-invoicing clearance regime. Taxpayers may issue electronic invoices provided the content satisfies the Excise Tax Act invoice requirements and the electronic record remains readable and accessible. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA My Business Account or approved services. Books and records must generally be kept in Canada or elsewhere with CRA permission and retained for six years.

CRA may reassess an LP within the normal four-year period and without limitation in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default, to claw back zero-rated treatment, disallow input tax credits, and assess GST/HST on the full value of the supply plus compound daily interest. False zero-rating or export documentation can trigger statutory false-statement and gross-negligence penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including penalties of up to 50% of the tax sought to be evaded in serious cases. Deliberate schemes may lead to criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, loss of refund entitlements, and publication of CRA enforcement outcomes. Although the Excise Tax Act does not prescribe a fixed foreign-currency-repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark is used to support the bona fides of the export for audit purposes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally impose capital controls, mandatory conversion of foreign currency, or restrictions on repatriation of profits, partnership distributions, loan proceeds, or investment capital. An Alberta limited partnership may receive and hold foreign currency and remit funds offshore, subject to its partnership agreement, contractual banking restrictions, sanctions, anti-money-laundering requirements, tax withholding and reporting obligations, and documentary support for the commercial purpose and beneficial ownership of the payment. Canadian-source payments to non-residents may require withholding under the Income Tax Act, including applicable treaty reductions. A flow-through limited partnership must separately analyze allocation, filing, withholding, treaty eligibility, transfer-pricing, thin-capitalization, and non-resident reporting consequences. Banks may delay or reject transactions lacking satisfactory source-of-funds, beneficial-ownership, tax, sanctions, or transaction-purpose evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No Canadian statutory rule generally requires exporters to surrender export proceeds, convert them into Canadian dollars, or complete conversion within a prescribed period. No mandatory conversion ratio applies to software, cloud, SaaS, or other digital-service export receipts. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the partnership's discretion. The partnership should retain contracts, invoices, delivery evidence, payment records, and foreign-exchange confirmations, and should comply with applicable customs, GST/HST, income-tax, sanctions, export-control, and statistical-reporting obligations. Bank-specific account terms, merchant-acquirer settlement rules, and anti-money-laundering review may impose operational documentation requirements but are not general statutory surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration comparable to a capital-control authority. Financial institutions and certain payment-service providers are also subject to federal oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, and, where applicable, the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection and foreign-exchange operations, but not a complete replacement for a Canadian bank account. Canada does not use a single statutory electronic-money-institution licensing category equivalent to the European Union EMI regime. A Canadian or foreign fintech may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act, FINTRAC registration and AML obligations, provincial securities or money-services rules, contractual safeguarding requirements, and the laws of its home jurisdiction. Stripe and comparable processors may support SaaS billing, card acquiring, direct debit, and settlement, subject to eligibility, underwriting, reserve, chargeback, prohibited-business, and geographic rules. Offshore digital EMI accounts can be used only where the provider permits Canadian limited partnerships, completes Canadian and beneficial-owner KYC, supports the relevant currencies and payment corridors, and legally provides the service to Canadian users. Customer funds should not be treated as unrestricted corporate deposits, and payment processing balances should not be used for unrelated treasury or client-money activities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Offshore digital EMI accounts can be used only where the provider permits Canadian limited partnerships, completes Canadian and beneficial-owner KYC, supports the relevant currencies and payment corridors, and legally provides the service to Canadian users. Customer funds should not be treated as unrestricted corporate deposits, and payment processing balances should not be used for unrelated treasury or client-money activities.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No monetary threshold exempting standardized documentation; CRA requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for all controlled transactions involving non-residents and for the LP’s cross-border service agreements. However, the CRA transfer pricing penalty applies only where the net adjustment exceeds CAD $5 million or the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts to determine arm’s length prices; documentation satisfying OECD standards reduces penalty exposure.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain British-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) and the 'de facto control' doctrine pierce LPs that lack substance. A Canadian limited partnership must maintain offices, employees, contractual counterparties, and management activities in Alberta to protect pass-through treatment and to avoid reclassification as a taxable corporation or a sham. The non-resident owner of the LP should not be a bare nominee.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Failure to Report Canadian Income

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act s.238(1) and s.239, conviction for tax evasion results in a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, plus a penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s.163. Civil penalties reach CAD $20,000 for first violations and CAD $50,000 for subsequent violations; CRA may demand interest on arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors of the LP’s general partner are personally liable for unremitted amounts (ITA s.227.1); senior executives may be criminally liable with imprisonment up to five years. CRA may issue travel bans, freeze bank accounts, and impose director's liability certificates.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA can assess DND (Dishonest/Negligent) penalties under s.163(2) of 50% of the misreported income; the LP may be deemed a corporation under ITA s.96(6) and retroactively subject to full corporate tax at 23%; treaty anti-abuse rules deny treaty-based withholding reductions.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the veil to hold the general partner’s directors personally liable for obligations of the de facto corporation; they may also be subject to a CRA director's liability assessment up to the amount of unpaid taxes, plus an injunction preventing re-organization of assets abroad.
Improper Profit Repatriation / Dividends vs Distributed Profits

Statutory sanction: CRA will recharacterize the payment as a taxable dividend or royalty under ITA s.214(2); Part XIII withholding tax of 25% becomes immediately payable, plus arrears interest at the prescribed rate plus 4.5%; gross negligence penalty of 10% of the distributed amount if mischaracterization is found wilful.

Director personal liability: General partner directors are liable for the scarcity of partnership assets after a distribution (Alberta Business Corporations Act s.119 and Partnership Act s.13); non-resident withholding omissions can trigger personal assessments under ITA s.227 in the amount of the unwithheld tax, and a director may be banned from serving as a director of any Canadian federal corporation for up to five years.
AML / Money Laundering (Structuring Customer Payments)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, failure to register as an FMSB or report suspicious transactions carries fines up to CAD $1 million per violation for first offenders; PCI DSS violations may trigger contractual penalties. Senior management may receive imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly condone AML violations incur personal criminal liability under s.73 of PCMLTFA; the Alberta Securities Commission may issue cease-trade and administrative penalties up to CAD $1 million; a director can be extradited under Mutual Legal Assistance Treaties and may be subject to global travel restrictions while a proceeds-of-crime investigation proceeds.
