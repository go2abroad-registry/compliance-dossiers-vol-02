Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Alberta limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed solely by the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow account requirement applies at formation; partnership capital may be subscribed in cash or in specie.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises must obtain a Fire Safety Compliance inspection under the Alberta Fire Code (SA 2000, c F-9), a structural load confirmation for the leased commercial space, and, if employees are engaged, an Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) clearance and workplace hazard assessment under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Alberta), RSA 2000, c O-2. Privacy and data-handling controls under PIPEDA are also adopted for subscriber data (no additional physical licence).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Alberta limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed solely by the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow account requirement applies at formation; partnership capital may be subscribed in cash or in specie.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), accessible via My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Alberta's provincial corporate income tax is administered and collected by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act through a Tax Collection Agreement; non-income provincial taxes are handled by Alberta Treasury Board and Finance; entity legal registration is handled by Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES, Service Alberta).

E-government filing portal

The general partner of the non-resident Alberta limited partnership must: (1) request a Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1, opening a GST/HST account (BN-RT) and, where relevant, income tax and import/export accounts; (2) use the CRA non-resident registration path before invoicing Canadian customers when the LP has no Canadian physical presence; (3) register the extra-provincial limited partnership with Alberta Corporate Registry under the Partnership Act (Alberta) to obtain the Alberta registration certificate; (4) file an annual T5013 partnership information return allocating income/loss to partners on a calendar-year basis; and (5) document each partner's residence status and complete applicable NR-series forms for non-resident partners. Standard non-resident registrations are processed within approximately 20 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; an LP is flow-through by statute (ITA ss. 96-103), with the LP filing only an information return. Relevant elections and mechanisms: (a) ITA s. 103 alternative partner allocation of income; (b) ITA s. 216 (rental income) and s. 216.1 (non-resident services) elections; (c) ETA s. 211.1 and related simplified registration elections applicable to non-resident digital platform suppliers; (d) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 94.2-94.4, which neutralize deduction/no-inclusion outcomes and will scrutinize any US Form 8832 check-the-box election made by or with respect to the LP or its partners; and (e) elections under ETA s. 158/191 respecting partnership property and GST/HST joint elections. No statutory entity-classification election is available under Canadian federal law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold over four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148(1)). A non-resident limited partnership with a general partner resident in Canada is statutorily deemed to be carrying on business in Canada (ETA s. 241(2.1)) and is required to register regardless of turnover, with no small-supplier exemption. A non-resident supplier of specified digital products to individuals resident in Canada must register under the July 1, 2021 e-commerce framework once it exceeds the CAD 30,000 registration-making supply threshold and cannot claim the small-supplier exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of EdTech language-learning services and intangible platform access are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 7 and 10 where made to a non-resident person who is not present in Canada at the time of supply and where the supply is not made in the course of the recipient's Canadian commercial activity. For digital supplies to individuals, the place-of-supply rules in ETA Schedule IX (ordinary residence of the recipient) govern: a supply to an individual ordinarily resident outside Canada is zero-rated, while a supply to an individual ordinarily resident in Alberta is taxable at 5%. Where the end user pays through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing, the distribution platform operator is treated as the supplier of the digital product to the end user for GST/HST purposes; the LP should zero-rate only its own supply to the non-resident platform operator where the statutory conditions are met. The supplier must obtain contemporaneous evidence of non-residence (geoIP, billing address, storefront country, payment method, and self-certification) and maintain a complete audit trail.

None — Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, or digital stamping. Voluntary Peppol-based e-invoicing is available under CRA's Pan-Canadian Trust Framework, but paper and PDF invoices remain valid. However, ETA s. 286 requires every registrant to retain records (tax invoices, contracts, and payment transaction logs from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing) in a readable, machine-accessible format for at least six years to substantiate GST filings, zero-rating claims, and input tax credits.

CRA may reassess GST/HST beyond the four-year general reassessment period (ETA s. 298(2)) and income tax beyond the four-year normal period (ITA s. 152(4)) where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, or where fraud is proven, creating unlimited retrospective exposure. False or unsupported zero-rating claims attract: (i) an ETA s. 285 penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax misstated or refund overclaimed; (ii) an ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated income tax; (iii) prescribed-rate interest compounded daily; and (iv) criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 for tax evasion, with fines and imprisonment, plus general Criminal Code fraud exposure. CRA export and transfer-pricing audits will recharacterize an unsubstantiated export supply as a Canadian taxable supply, retroactively imposing GST, penalties, and interest, with the general partner potentially liable for the LP's deficiency. Note: Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline for services exports; the 180-day benchmark reflects prudent treasury and EDC trade-finance practice, while CRA relies on arm's-length documentation and source evidence to verify the export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible with no exchange-control restrictions applying to current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittances and capital repatriation from an Alberta limited partnership (LP) are unrestricted; however, distributions or deemed allocations to non-resident partners may attract Canadian Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory rate of 25% unless reduced under an applicable income tax treaty, calculated on the partner's taxable Canadian-source share of partnership income. No central bank approval, license, or conversion ratio applies to subscription proceeds, operating revenue, or liquidation distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds or foreign-currency receivables. EdTech subscription receipts from Apple and Google platforms may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely. Reporting obligations apply under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): electronic fund transfers of CAD $10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC; cross-border currency or monetary instrument movements exceeding CAD $10,000 must be declared at Canadian border services; and certain life insurance, trust, or large virtual-currency transactions trigger additional record-keeping requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority, operating jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the federal banking regulator and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the financial intelligence and AML/ATF reporting agency. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange administration equivalent to a SAFE-type control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. Licensed non-bank financial technology providers and electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Business, Stripe Treasury, and Payoneer may support multi-currency collections of Apple App Store and Google Play proceeds through locally registered PSP arrangements, but they operate under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration framework rather than as Canadian chartered banks. These entities cannot legally hold limited-partner capital in a manner equivalent to a CDIC-insured deposit, often require a sponsor-bank arrangement for account validation, and may impose sub-account concentration limits that conflict with escrow segregation duties. Recommended use: operational collections and low-value disbursements only; prohibited use: statutory partner capital escrow or fiduciary treasury concentration.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined Canada/Alberta general corporate rate 23% (federal 15% + Alberta 8%); the CA_LP is a fiscally transparent limited partnership, so this rate is borne by corporate/individual partners on their allocable share, not by the LP.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII WHT) on non-resident dividends; for a Canadian LP, no dividend withholding occurs at the LP level because distributions are not dividends but returns of capital/partnership draws; a corporate partner that later pays dividends to a non-resident shareholder is subject to the 25% statutory rate before treaty reduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File/contemporaneous TP documentation is expected for all controlled cross-border transactions where the aggregate non-arm's length transaction value exceeds CAD 1,000,000 (T106 mandatory filing threshold); CRA Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain dedicated 'Economic Substance Regulations' akin to the UAE/OECD pillar two, but economic substance is enforced through judicial substance-over-form, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), CRS/beneficial ownership audits, and treaty abuse provisions under OECD BEPS; the Alberta LP and its partners must demonstrate real management, actual employees, operational assets, and business purpose to avoid recharacterisation of transactions or denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false declarations

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution is available under ITA s.239 and Criminal Code fraud provisions: fines up to 200% of the evaded amount plus interest; on conviction, imprisonment for up to 5 years for tax evasion and up to 14 years for fraud; civil penalties under ITA s.163(2) of up to 50% of additional tax assessed; CRA can also audit and reassess beyond the normal 3-year limit for misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors and corporate partners who caused, consented to, or acquiesced in the evasion may be personally liable jointly and severally for the entity's unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under ITA s.227.1 and ETA s.323; civil fraud exposes them to restitution, freezing orders, travel bans/asset seizure, and in egregious cases imprisonment. Bankruptcy does not discharge these liabilities.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.247(3), penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment (without offset of other adjustments) if documentation and reasonable efforts standards are breached; interest and much larger income tax reassessments also apply; CRA may seek a court-ordered privilege waiver for tax-accounting work product.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false T106 returns or directed bogus transfer pricing can be assessed as directors for unremitted tax and penalties; for gross negligence, criminal culpability attaches and directors can be imprisoned for up to 14 years under the Criminal Code. Outside of Canada, tax authority notices may trigger international asset freeze and extradition processes.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial ownership failures

Statutory sanction: Registration and compliance obligations under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative fines up to CAD 500,000 per violation; FINTRAC may issue directives; criminal sanctions up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years for money laundering offences under the Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally required by FINTRAC to ensure AML programme implementation; failure can lead to personal prohibition from financial-sector activity, fines, and imprisonment. The regulatory practice pierces the veil where the LP is a shell and directors have been knowing participants in laundering funds.
Repatriation and unreported distributions

Statutory sanction: Part XIII WHT at 25% will be assessed on the gross amount paid or credited; in addition to the WHT, CRA assesses interest, late-filing penalties, and may treat the recipient as having a permanent establishment; if treaty abuse is found, relief is denied and the entire amount may be re-assessed under GAAR with a 50% penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who sanctioned unlawful distributions can be held jointly and severally liable for withholding tax and penalties; in severe cases, personal criminal liability attaches for conspiracy to defraud the Crown, leading to imprisonment and travel bans. Restitution orders force directors to repay amounts personally to the tax authority.
