Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal; Alberta provincial income tax is administered by CRA under the Federal–Provincial Collection Agreement; Alberta levies no provincial sales tax and imposes no separate provincial GST registration for the CA-AB jurisdiction.

E-government filing portal

The Limited Partnership (LP) must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) via the CRA Business Registration Online portal (immediate electronic issuance) or by paper form RC1 (Application to Register a Business Number). A general partner applies on behalf of the LP and must activate a GST/HST program account (RT) where taxable supplies exceed the small-supplier threshold or where the LP is a non-resident vendor of digital services. Non-resident partners must hold or obtain a Canadian tax identifier (SIN, BN, or ITN for individuals) and be disclosed on the LP's annual T5013/T5013SUM partnership information return. The partnership itself files annually with CRA even though it is not an entity-level income taxpayer.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible; fiscally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (Canada). The CA-LP is not subject to entity-level federal or Alberta income tax; income, gains, deductions, and tax credits flow through to resident and non-resident partners. Alberta has no partnership-level corporate tax. The LP may nonetheless be required to register, collect, and remit GST/HST and payroll source deductions in its own name as a registrant. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; fiscally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (Canada). The CA-LP is not subject to entity-level federal or Alberta income tax; income, gains, deductions, and tax credits flow through to resident and non-resident partners. Alberta has no partnership-level corporate tax. The LP may nonetheless be required to register, collect, and remit GST/HST and payroll source deductions in its own name as a registrant. Limited partners who participate in management may lose limited-liability protection and become jointly and severally liable for partnership obligations.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal income tax law; the CA-LP is automatically pass-through. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) partnership allocation elections and agreed capital-account allocations under section 96 of the Income Tax Act; (ii) elections by the partnership to recompute undepreciated capital cost and recapture; (iii) for non-resident partners, elections to file a Part I return rather than suffer Part XIII withholding where structurally available (e.g., sections 216 and 217 ITA); and (iv) mandatory hybrid-mismatch reporting and recharacterization under sections 18.3–18.5 of the Income Tax Act for cross-border arrangements that exploit different tax characterizations of the same payment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the GST/HST small-supplier threshold measured on worldwide taxable supplies in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or in any single calendar quarter. For non-resident suppliers of digital health-and-fitness tracking applications to Canadian non-registrant consumers, the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies under the July 1, 2021 digital-services GST/HST measures; a non-resident vendor without a Canadian permanent establishment may register under the simplified CRA non-resident registration process.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital health fitness app subscriptions and biometric telemetry services qualify for zero-rating under section 7 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supply is made to a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant (or to a registrant acquiring the service for consumption outside Canada), provided the service is not delivered to an individual present in Canada and does not relate to real property in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply. Where sales are routed through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing, the distribution-platform rules may treat the platform operator as the supplier of the digital service to Canadian consumers, thereby removing or reducing the LP's separate Canadian GST/HST collection obligation on those sales; the LP retains the burden of demonstrating zero-rating eligibility for any direct sales to foreign end-users.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canada Border Services Agency export reporting) for any tangible goods, or the digital-services supply contract with cloud-delivery terms and platform settlement reports for intangible services
  • Bill of Lading / freight waybill for tangible exports, or Apple IAP / Google Play payment settlement reports evidencing foreign end-user transactions and purchase geolocation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT credit advice or payment-rail settlement records evidencing foreign-currency consideration received into the LP's bank account
  • Non-resident status evidence for each buyer (foreign B2B customer's local tax registration number, or consumer-level IP address and device-location logs demonstrating non-Canadian consumption)
  • Six years of auditable books and records (contracts, invoices, delivery logs, platform reports, and CRA-prescribed export documentation)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or CRA API pre-clearance regime exists in Canada. GST/HST sales invoices must contain the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act (supplier name and BN, recipient identification, invoice date, description of supply, amount of tax charged or a clear statement that the supply is zero-rated), but issuance may be electronic in any format. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (Peppol BIS 3.0) is voluntary and does not yet impose mandatory clearance. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via the CRA My Business Account portal or GST/HST NETFILE; records must be retained for six years.

CRA may reassess a claimed zero-rating, deny input tax credits, and tax an export supply if the buyer is a Canadian resident, the service is consumed in Canada, or the documentation is inadequate. Gross-negligence penalties under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act equal 25% of the tax amount; willful evasion or false statements attract criminal prosecution under section 327 of the ETA and section 239 of the Income Tax Act, with fines and imprisonment. Arrears interest accrues on clawed-back amounts, and the normal four-year reassessment limitation is waived for misrepresentation. General partners remain jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST of the LP; Canada imposes no statutory 180-day export-proceeds repatriation deadline, but the 180-day convention is retained here as a documentary sufficiency benchmark consistent with 6-year record-keeping rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency with unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. The former Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and no exchange control legislation or approval regime currently exists for cross-border fund movements. Foreign capital, loan repayments, dividends, partnership allocations, and termination distributions may be repatriated freely. The only structural friction for an Alberta Limited Partnership is Canadian income tax on allocated income: distributions to non-resident limited partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, potentially reduced to 15% or 5% under the Canada-US tax treaty or another applicable tax treaty) unless the partner carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment. The LP must deduct and remit this withholding to the Canada Revenue Agency on behalf of non-resident partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no prescribed repatriation timeline for export receipts or foreign currency revenues. Canadian entities may freely hold, invoice, and disburse in CAD, USD, or any other currency. Foreign currency accounts are standard corporate banking products and require no Bank of Canada authorization. For a digital health fitness app, revenue from Apple App Store and Google Play aggregators may be received in USD or CAD and retained without any statutory conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Prudential regulation and supervision of deposit-taking institutions is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (Canada), while FINTRAC enforces anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The Bank of Canada operates the Lynx high-value clearing system and is the lead regulator under the Retail Payment Activities Act (Canada).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-medium viability. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, and Revolut Business are not recognized as deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act or Alberta provincial legislation; balances held with them are not CDIC-insured and do not enjoy lender-of-resort protection. Such providers may lawfully operate as payment service providers in Canada only if registered as a money services business with FINTRAC and, where they are in the business of payment services, registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada. An offshore EMI without a Canadian registration should be restricted to supplementary collections or multi-currency settlement wallets and cannot serve as the LP's primary regulated treasury account. A Canadian Schedule I bank or an Alberta-regulated credit union remains the only fully viable custody and settlement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound partner capital is wired via SWIFT MT103 with the narrative field clearly identifying the transaction as 'Capital Contribution - [LP Name] - [Partner Name]'. Canada has no government-assigned wire purpose code or foreign exchange approval system; however, the remitting bank will apply the IMF BPM6 purpose classification (typically 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' for subscription payments) and the receiving bank will record the purpose in its transaction monitoring. The LP must preserve the executed capital call, the subscription agreement, and the Alberta-registered partnership documents in the transaction file. App-store proceeds are received as intercompany or platform disbursements: Apple pays through Apple Distribution International or Apple Canada Inc., and Google through Google Payment Corp or its Canadian affiliate, typically via ACH/SEPA/wire settlement to the LP's designated merchant settlement account; the LP should pre-notify the bank of the platform remitter names to avoid monitoring false positives. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, and Revolut Business are not recognized as deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act or Alberta provincial legislation; balances held with them are not CDIC-insured and do not enjoy lender-of-resort protection. Such providers may lawfully operate as payment service providers in Canada only if registered as a money services business with FINTRAC and, where they are in the business of payment services, registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada. An offshore EMI without a Canadian registration should be restricted to supplementary collections or multi-currency settlement wallets and cannot serve as the LP's primary regulated treasury account. A Canadian Schedule I bank or an Alberta-regulated credit union remains the only fully viable custody and settlement rail.
