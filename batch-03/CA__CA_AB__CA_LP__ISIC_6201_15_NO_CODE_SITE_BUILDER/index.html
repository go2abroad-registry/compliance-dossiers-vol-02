Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — the federal tax authority under the Canada Revenue Agency Act. Principal digital portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and the Business Registration Online service (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register.html). Provincial counterpart: Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (Treasury Board and Finance) — Alberta administers its provincial corporate income tax through the CRA under an administrative services agreement; Alberta does not operate a provincial GST/PST or HST regime.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Limited Partnership (LP) must obtain a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN), which constitutes the Canadian statutory tax identification number including the T5013 partnership identifier and the GST/HST program account (RT). Procedure: (1) complete Form RC1 — Request for a Business Number, or use CRA Business Registration Online, in the legal name of the Alberta Limited Partnership; (2) open the GST/HST program account (RT) as a distinct 'person' (partnership) if annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or if taxable supplies are made in Canada in the course of a commercial activity; non-resident LPs may use Form GST360 or the CRA 'Non-Resident GST/HST Registration' pathway under section 242.1 of the Excise Tax Act; (3) open a payroll program account (RP) only if employees are engaged in Canada; (4) file the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually (fiscal-year-end returns are due within five months after the partnership fiscal period-end); (5) where the LP carries on business in Alberta, register the extra-provincial limited partnership with the Alberta Corporate Registry (this is a corporate-law identifier, not a tax TIN, but must be obtained before the LP can open a provincial bank account or enter into binding Canadian contracts). The LP must appoint a tax representative or authorized contact for CRA correspondence where the general partner is not resident in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification regime. For income tax, the LP is a fiscally transparent partnership under sections 96–103 of the Income Tax Act (income and losses flow through to general and limited partners); for GST/HST, the partnership is a separate legal 'person' registered in the partnership name under section 240(1) of the Excise Tax Act, with the partners jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST. Available statutory elections include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration for small suppliers under subsection 178.1(4) ETA; (ii) partnership fiscal-period election under subsection 249.1(4) ITA (all members may elect a non-calendar fiscal period); (iii) election by a partnership to treat a supply to a member as being made at nil value for closely related persons under section 156 ETA where the ownership threshold is met; (iv) the section 216 ITA election for non-resident partners receiving only passive Canadian-source income (rent or timber royalties) to compute Part I tax separately; (v) hybrid mismatch elections under the Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (implemented in the ITA effective 2022) allowing taxpayers to elect to avoid a deduction/non-inclusion mismatch in cross-border partnership structures; and (vi) on the US side only, an entity classification election under US Form 8832 by a US parent to treat the Canadian LP as an eligible entity — this is not a Canadian election and has no effect on CRA classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — being the annual worldwide threshold for taxable supplies made by a 'small supplier' under section 148(1) of the Excise Tax Act. For the non-resident LP supplying SaaS no-code builder services, registration becomes mandatory when the LP's worldwide taxable revenue exceeds CAD 30,000, even if no supplies are made to Canadian persons; once registered, the LP must charge GST on Canadian supplies and may claim input tax credits. Since July 1, 2021, online marketplace deemed-supplier rules apply: where the LP sells through a platform that is the merchant of record (e.g., Paddle), the platform may be the deemed registrant in respect of those sales, while direct sales through Stripe Billing are attributed to the LP for threshold and registration purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, specifically paragraph 7 of said Part V, for a supply of intangible personal property (IPP) in the form of access to the no-code website builder and visual web template software. Conditions: (i) the recipient is a non-resident person who is not registered under the ETA and the supply is not for use in Canada; or (ii) the recipient is a non-resident GST/HST registrant that acquires the IPP otherwise than for supply in Canada or for use in Canada. The place-of-supply rules in Schedule IX ETA (sections 2.2–2.6) deem a supply of IPP to a recipient that is an individual to be made in the province/country where the individual is habitually resident, and to a registrant recipient to be made in the country where the recipient's relevant establishment that receives the supply is located. Therefore, subscription sales to end customers in the United States, the EU, and the Asia-Pacific region through Stripe Billing or Paddle are 0% GST supplies when the billing metadata shows a foreign billing address, foreign IP geolocation, and no delivery to a Canadian establishment. Sales to Alberta or other Canadian customers are taxable at 5% GST. The LP must maintain the substantive export documentation bundle described below and ensure its standard terms do not grant the foreign customer the right to use the service in Canada in a manner that would cause the supply to be received in Canada. If the LP also exports tangible media (e.g., USB template libraries, physical server appliances), zero-rating of the goods is available under Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 upon proof of export within 60 days of the sale.

  • Customs Export Declaration: CBSA B13A Export Declaration filed electronically through the Canadian International Trade Single Window or the Canadian Automated Export Declarations portal for any physical goods exported in connection with the service (including pre-packaged template libraries) exceeding CAD 2,000 in value; for pure digital SaaS deliveries no B13A clearance is given, and the LP must retain a written internal export-methodology memo together with server application logs and domain/DNS registration records evidencing offshore provisioning.
  • Bill of Lading: for tangible media and hardware shipments the original or electronic bill of lading (including carrier waybill and delivery confirmation) must be retained; where the export is purely digital, the equivalent evidentiary instrument is the automated licence-provisioning report and IP activation records generated by the Stripe Billing/Paddle subscription platform evidencing delivery of keys or access credentials to the foreign recipient.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank SWIFT/MT103 credit advices, Stripe Billing payout reports, and Paddle merchant-of-record settlement statements demonstrating that the export consideration is received in the LP's bank account (in CAD or foreign currency), together with the executed Master SaaS Agreement, the foreign customer order form, and geo-IP access logs establishing that the service was consumed outside Canada.

No real-time transactional fiscal clearance, digital stamping, Continuous Transaction Controls, or API-based tax-authority clearance applies in Canada. The CRA accepts electronic invoices provided they are retained in a CRA-readable electronic format under section 286.1 of the Excise Tax Act (records must be archived machine-readable and accessible on demand with the ability to produce a 'report from a qualified auditor' if requested). The only mandatory e-invoicing standard is for suppliers selling to the Government of Canada (PEPPOL BIS 3.0 / SAP Ariba / GCDOCS), which is inapplicable to the export SaaS model. The LP should nevertheless configure Stripe Billing and Paddle to emit invoice metadata recording customer jurisdiction, service delivery location, contract order date, and applicable GST treatment, as these records constitute the fiscal invoice package for CRA audits.

Although Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation rule under the Excise Tax Act, treating 180 days as the maximum settlement window is a CRA-recognized best-practice benchmark for proving genuine export consideration and reducing recharacterization risk. The CRA's four-year reassessment period under subsection 298(1) ETA applies to GST/HST returns; the limitation period is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default is established. Specific clawback and penalty exposure: (i) false statements in a zero-rating claim attract a penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 5% of the tax that would have been payable (subsection 280.1(1) ETA); (ii) knowingly making a false statement attracts a penalty of at least 5% of the net tax under section 285 ETA and constitutes an offence under section 327 ETA — on conviction, fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years; (iii) failure to register when the threshold is exceeded triggers a penalty of 5% of the net tax not remitted together with 1% monthly interest charges (section 280 ETA); (iv) the full amount of GST/N T reassessed is retroactively clawed back on export revenues, plus prescribed interest, and the LP's GST/HST registration may be cancelled retroactively; (v) under subsection 272.1(1) ETA, the general partner and every partner of the LP are jointly and severally liable for all GST/HST amounts owed; and (vi) under the Income Tax Act, transfer-pricing adjustments (section 247) and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245) may be asserted where the LP prices cross-border subscription sales at non-arm's-length amounts or where circular financing exists between the LP and a foreign parent corporation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada generally imposes no prior approval, quota, mandatory repatriation, or general capital-control requirement for inbound investment, intercompany funding, dividend distributions, service payments, loan repayments, or ordinary foreign-exchange conversion. Transactions remain subject to bank AML and sanctions screening, beneficial-ownership verification, tax reporting, transfer-pricing rules, withholding-tax rules, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, sanctions legislation, securities and derivatives regulation, anti-bribery controls, and documentation of the commercial purpose and source of funds. An Alberta limited partnership is generally taxed on a flow-through basis, subject to its specific partnership agreement, partner residency, income characterization, permanent-establishment analysis, and applicable federal and provincial filing obligations. Payments to non-resident partners or service providers may trigger Canadian withholding, information-reporting, or treaty-analysis requirements. Profit remittance is not generally blocked by exchange controls, but the bank may require partnership resolutions, financial statements, tax forms, invoices, distribution calculations, and evidence that the payment is not a disguised transfer, prohibited transaction, or sanctions-related payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or general deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into Canadian dollars or deposited with a domestic bank. Export receipts may normally remain in foreign currency, be held in a permitted foreign-currency account, or be converted at the entity's discretion. The entity should retain contracts, invoices, shipping or service-delivery evidence, payment confirmations, customer identity information, and tax records. Large or unusual international transfers may be reported by financial institutions under AML rules. Cash or monetary instruments crossing the Canadian border with a value of CAD 10,000 or more must be declared to the Canada Border Services Agency; this rule does not generally apply to ordinary electronic bank transfers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada does not operate a separate foreign-exchange administration or general exchange-control authority. Financial institutions and payment businesses are additionally supervised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, the Department of Finance Canada, the Canada Revenue Agency, and applicable provincial securities and consumer-protection regulators.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment acceptance and payout support; Low as a substitute for a Canadian operating bank account unless the provider is properly authorized for the relevant Canadian activity. Canada does not provide a single domestic electronic-money-institution passport. An offshore EMI may provide services only within the scope permitted by its home-country authorization and Canadian law. Canadian payment-service activities may fall within the Retail Payment Activities Act and require Bank of Canada registration, risk-management controls, safeguarding arrangements, incident reporting, and operational-resilience compliance when applicable. Deposit-taking, payment-account, money-transfer, foreign-exchange, and merchant-acquiring functions may require Canadian authorization or registration and may also constitute money-services-business activities supervised by FINTRAC. Stripe Billing and Paddle can be commercially suitable for software subscriptions, but approval is separate from bank-account opening and depends on merchant underwriting, customer geography, product terms, chargeback risk, sanctions screening, tax treatment, and beneficial-owner verification. Funds should be settled to an account held in the legal entity's name or through a documented and contractually permissible payment structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a wire transfer from an account held by the documented investor, partner, or lender directly to the Alberta limited partnership's Canadian operating account or designated foreign-currency account. The remittance should identify the legal recipient, partnership name, account number, currency, amount, and a precise purpose such as 'limited-partner capital contribution under agreement dated [date]' or 'shareholder or partner loan under agreement dated [date]'; an Alberta limited partnership should use the legally accurate partner-contribution or partnership-loan description rather than a share-capital description. Provide the bank with the executed contribution or loan agreement, partner or general-partner approval, ownership information, source-of-funds evidence, expected-use schedule, and repayment or distribution terms. Avoid third-party funding, personal accounts, cash deposits, informal payment processors, and split transfers designed to avoid scrutiny. Reconcile the incoming wire to the partnership ledger and retain SWIFT confirmation, bank statements, accounting entries, and tax records. Canada generally has no central-bank pre-approval or universal capital-control filing for this transfer, but the bank may delay or reject it under AML, sanctions, correspondent-bank, or source-of-funds controls.For ordinary software, hosting, contractor, and other commercial payments, use bank wire transfers, domestic Canadian clearing, or the payment method specified by the counterparty. A SWIFT MT103 or equivalent customer-credit-transfer message is appropriate for cross-border supplier or service payments and should contain accurate originator, beneficiary, bank, invoice, contract, currency, and purpose information. For larger or higher-risk procurements, a commercial documentary letter of credit may be issued or advised through a Canadian bank, subject to credit approval, collateral, UCP 600 terms where incorporated, documentary requirements, sanctions screening, and shipping or service-performance evidence. For digital services with no physical shipment, use an appropriate service-payment structure rather than inventing trade documentation. Maintain invoices, contracts, acceptance evidence, tax forms, withholding analysis, beneficiary verification, payment approvals, and reconciliation records. Canadian banks may apply correspondent-bank charges, sanctions screening, enhanced due diligence, and reporting obligations even though ordinary outbound payments are not subject to general exchange controls.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with an authorized Canadian bank or properly regulated dealer, supported by an identifiable forecast or committed exposure and documented maturity, currency, notional amount, and settlement instructions.Non-deliverable forwards where the relevant currencies, counterparties, and settlement terms are legally available, with cash settlement, valuation, collateral, and reporting terms documented. NDF availability is bank- and currency-specific and may be restricted by local law in the reference-currency jurisdiction.Currency swaps or cross-currency swaps for documented recurring or longer-term currency exposures, subject to dealer credit approval, ISDA or equivalent documentation, collateral terms, valuation, close-out, and applicable Canadian derivatives rules.Foreign-exchange options or collars where commercially justified and approved by the bank, with clear disclosure of premium, maximum liability, exercise mechanics, and suitability risks.Spot foreign-exchange transactions for operational conversion and settlement. The entity should maintain a written treasury policy, board or general-partner authority matrix, counterparty limits, exposure records, independent confirmation, valuation controls, and accounting treatment. Hedging should not be conducted as unlicensed dealing, speculative proprietary trading, or customer-facing financial intermediation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payment acceptance and payout support; Low as a substitute for a Canadian operating bank account unless the provider is properly authorized for the relevant Canadian activity. Canada does not provide a single domestic electronic-money-institution passport. An offshore EMI may provide services only within the scope permitted by its home-country authorization and Canadian law. Canadian payment-service activities may fall within the Retail Payment Activities Act and require Bank of Canada registration, risk-management controls, safeguarding arrangements, incident reporting, and operational-resilience compliance when applicable. Deposit-taking, payment-account, money-transfer, foreign-exchange, and merchant-acquiring functions may require Canadian authorization or registration and may also constitute money-services-business activities supervised by FINTRAC. Stripe Billing and Paddle can be commercially suitable for software subscriptions, but approval is separate from bank-account opening and depends on merchant underwriting, customer geography, product terms, chargeback risk, sanctions screening, tax treatment, and beneficial-owner verification. Funds should be settled to an account held in the legal entity's name or through a documented and contractually permissible payment structure.
