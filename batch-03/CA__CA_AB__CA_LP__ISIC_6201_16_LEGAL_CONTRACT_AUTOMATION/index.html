Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; national portals: My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement; Alberta has no provincial sales tax, making CRA the sole indirect tax authority for the jurisdiction.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta LP: (1) complete extra-provincial registration of the foreign partners with the Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES) as a legal prerequisite; (2) apply for the LP's 9-digit Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1, selecting the RT (GST/HST) program account and, if applicable, the RP (payroll) account; the LP receives no corporate income tax program account because it is fiscally transparent; (3) register within 30 days of the moment taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; (4) each partner reports its allocated income under its own TIN, and non-resident partners may be required to appoint a Canadian agent for service and post security at CRA's demand; (5) file the annual T5013 partnership information return (Summary and partner slips) within five months of fiscal year-end.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible in both current and capital accounts. Canada has fully liberalized trade and capital flows; there are no exchange controls, no central-bank approval for foreign direct investment, and no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, loan repayments, or dissolving capital from an Alberta Limited Partnership (LP). The principal friction is tax-related, not currency-related: distributions to non-resident limited partners are subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.)), unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty. The LP, as a pass-through vehicle, must file annual partnership information returns and issue NR4 or T5013 slips as required, and funds remitted abroad must be reported under FINTRAC thresholds when exceeding CAD 10,000 in a single transaction or series.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender export receipts, no mandated conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline. Foreign-currency receivables of a Canadian-resident LP may be retained in domiciliary accounts or converted freely at commercial bank rates. The only obligations are regulatory and evidentiary: suspicious-transaction reporting and large-cross-border-transaction reporting to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17), and for AMT/AML due diligence, a complete audit trail of invoicing, remittance, and conversion must be retained.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority and lender of last resort; prudential banking supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and cross-border transaction monitoring enforcement is vested in the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). No separate foreign-exchange administration or capital-control agency exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, with material Canadian regulatory constraints. Non-resident e-money institutions are not automatically permitted to take deposits from Canadian residents. A foreign EMI entering Canada without an authorized EMD license is effectively restricted to a money-service-business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC and, as of September 2024, must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c.23) with the Bank of Canada to process retail payments. A non-resident EMI cannot act as the primary operating bank for the Canadian LP because the LP needs a Canadian distribution account, an Alberta nexus for GST/HST filing, and auditable clearing rails for USD/CAD conversion. It may be viable only as a supplementary inbound-collection rail if paired with a Tier-1 domestic operating account. Feasibility for the legal-tech use case is therefore limited to payment facilitation, not treasury custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For compliant inbound investment, the LP should instruct non-resident partners to execute a SWIFT MT103 wire identified in the 'Ordering Customer to Beneficiary' narrative as 'Alberta Limited Partnership capital contribution' and include: full legal name of the LP, Alberta registration number, and general partner reference. Canada does not assign a national foreign-exchange purpose code for routine capital contributions, but the beneficiary bank requires a clear source-of-funds designation; the wire must be accompanied by a PDF or secure message containing the capital account stating the contribution date and amount. 25% under Part XIII on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; treaty rates: 5% for beneficial owners resident in treaty jurisdictions holding at least 10% of voting shares (e.g., Canada-U.S. and Canada-UK treaties), 15% for portfolio shareholders; for the Limited Partnership, profit repatriation to non-resident partners is ordinarily effected as partnership income allocations, not dividends, and is taxed under Part I at graduated rates (individuals) or Part I plus Part XIV branch tax of 25% (corporate partners), with the branch tax reduced to 5% for U.S. resident corporations under the Canada-U.S. treaty

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's-Length Transactions with Non-Residents) filing is required where the aggregate value of reportable transactions with a non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the fiscal period; or CAD 50,000 for transactions involving guarantees of a corporation's obligations (processing threshold); CRA also requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for any transaction above CAD 1,000,000, and in all cases the taxpayer bears the burden of demonstrating arm's length pricing; recommended Master File and Local File aligned with OECD Chapters IV-V

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist in Canada or Alberta; however, economic substance is enforced indirectly through: (i) GAAR under subsection 245(2) of the Income Tax Act, which recharacterizes avoidance transactions lacking a bona fide business purpose; (ii) the Canada-U.S. and other DTA limitation-on-benefits and 'beneficial ownership' provisions, and Canada's commitment under BEPS Action 6 as an MLI signatory, denying treaty benefits to letterbox entities; (iii) the 'business purpose' test applied by CRA in audit; and (iv) the partnership 'at-risk' and 'limited partner loss' rules which restrict loss claims for passive limited partners. The LegalTech SaaS partnership must maintain board minutes, IP assignment records, payroll and premises evidence in Alberta, and avoid circular financing or tax-indifferent investor arrangements

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Upon summary conviction, a fine of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; upon indictment, a fine of 50%-200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years (subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act); CRA criminal investigations may also involve the Royal Canadian Mounted Police and Federal Prosecution Service

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's/partnership's unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under subsection 227.1(1); the due-diligence defence requires proof of reasonable care in supervision, a high evidentiary bar; in criminal tax prosecutions, travel bans, passport surrender, and asset freezes are commonly imposed as bail conditions, and conviction leads to a criminal record and potential extradition exposure
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income under section 247(2); a transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue (subsection 247(3)); failure to file T106 attracts a penalty of CAD 100 per day up to CAD 25,000 (and separate penalties for each partner); if a false statement is made knowingly, the gross negligence penalty of 50% of the tax attributable applies under subsection 163(2)

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false T5013/T106 documents or authorize mispricing can be prosecuted under section 239; the s. 227.1 director's liability extends to any penalties assessed, not merely the tax, because they are deemed 'liable for the amount' owed by the entity; indemnification by the partnership is void as contrary to public policy
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR (subsection 245(2)) denies the tax benefit on a standalone basis, with interest compounded; the Supreme Court of Canada in 'Deans Knight' confirms GAAR applies where transactions lack a real economic substance and circumvent the object of the provisions; CRA may also deny treaty relief under the MLI's principal purpose test and assert Part XIII withholding at 25% on all outbound amounts; a 50% GAAR penalty on the denied tax benefit has been legislatively proposed and is expected to apply to avoidance transactions entered into after April 6, 2022 (subject to enactment status)

Director personal liability: Directors who are generals partners or controlling minds of a shell LP face personal assessments for unremitted Part XIII tax and penalties under s. 227.1; in cases of fraudulent conveyance (e.g., asset stripping from the LP to a related entity before audit), courts will pierce the corporate veil and make personal restitution orders; criminal forfeiture of proceeds of crime is possible under the Criminal Code
AML / PCMLTFA Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations/partnerships per violation (and up to CAD 500,000 for individuals) for an entity found to be an unregistered MSB; criminal liability for laundering proceeds of crime: up to 10 years imprisonment under section 462.31 of the Criminal Code; the current use of Stripe/Credit Card rails for the partnership's own revenue is classified 'Low Risk Standard' and does not trigger MSB registration, but any expansion into third-party fund custody creates immediate obligations

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be personally named in FINTRAC AMPs; senior officers who knowingly direct transactions to evade the registration requirement or who permit laundering may be convicted of indictable offences and imprisoned; travel restrictions and asset freezing can be imposed under proceeds-of-crime legislation; additionally, professional licensing bodies may revoke certifications of lawyer-directors involved in LegalTech escrow functions
Failure to Remit Part XIII Withholdings

Statutory sanction: The full unremitted amount becomes collectable from the partnership; an arrears interest charge and a penalty under subsection 227(9) apply (5% of the amount plus 1% per month up to 12 months); CRA may assess the non-resident partner directly for tax due under Part I or Part XIII with exposure to double taxation if the treaty-credit structure fails

Director personal liability: General partners of the Alberta LP are personally liable under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act for the unremitted amounts, including penalties and interest; in an audit, CRA routinely pursues personal assets (bank accounts, receivables, personal residences) upon issuance of a director's liability assessment, and serious non-remittance may be prosecuted as criminal tax evasion with imprisonment up to 5 years
