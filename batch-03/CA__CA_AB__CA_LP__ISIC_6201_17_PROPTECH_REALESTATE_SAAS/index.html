Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital requirement and no escrow-account rule applies to an Alberta Limited Partnership. The Declaration must state each limited partner's contribution, and the contribution must be in money or other property having value, not services (Partnership Act s. 55). Contributions may be funded in cash on subscription or by agreed installments under the partnership agreement; no paid-up capital filing is required.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - canada.ca/taxes, including the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal. Alberta does not administer a provincial sales tax; Alberta corporate income tax is administered separately by the Alberta Treasury Board and Finance (Tax and Revenue Administration).

E-government filing portal

A foreign or domestic limited partnership must first obtain a CRA Business Number (BN). Procedure: (1) submit Form RC1 'Request for GST/HST Registration' or apply online through CRA Business Registration Online; (2) provide the partnership's legal name, governing jurisdiction, principal address, and a Canadian contact/agent if non-resident; (3) CRA issues a 9-digit BN with a program account code (e.g., 'RT' for GST/HST); (4) the LP must make all subsequent filings electronically via CRA My Business Account; (5) registration is mandatory if taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 over the preceding four calendar quarters, or if the LP is a non-resident engaged in Canadian commercial activity above that threshold; registration below the threshold is voluntary, subject to limited exceptions for non-resident real property vendors.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: an Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes, with each partner reporting their respective share of income, losses, and capital attributes; for GST/HST purposes, however, the LP is a separate 'person' required to register, collect, and remit tax on its taxable supplies.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act section 216 election for non-resident partners to be assessed on net Canadian rental income instead of gross Part XIII withholding; (ii) Excise Tax Act section 156 election for closely related corporations/partnerships to treat certain supplies as made for nil consideration for GST/HST; (iii) joint venture elections under the Excise Tax Act to designate one participant as GST/HST filer; and (iv) hybrid mismatch arrangement rules under amended Canadian tax law, which apply automatically and do not permit elective neutrality for cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (the small supplier threshold); registration is mandatory upon crossing that amount, and non-resident suppliers of Canadian real property should confirm whether a lower or immediate registration obligation applies under the Excise Tax Act and CRA administrative policy.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of cloud/SaaS services to a non-resident (not registered for GST/HST) is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 of the Excise Tax Act if the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada and is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada. For a PropTech platform providing property management and rental screening software, zero-rating may apply where the customer is outside Canada, the subscription is paid from outside Canada, and the software is accessed and used primarily outside Canada. Zero-rating is denied if the service relates to Canadian real property, Canadian residents, or Canadian property management operations; in such cases GST/HST at 5% must be charged. The supplier must maintain documentary evidence of non-resident status and place of use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) for any physical hardware or tangible deliverables, where applicable
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for any exported tangible goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/ACH/SWIFT evidence showing payment from a non-resident bank account
  • Executed SaaS subscription agreement or cloud service order stating the non-resident customer's jurisdiction
  • Customer tax identification number, business registration certificate, or passport/official ID demonstrating non-resident status
  • Technical usage logs, IP geolocation reports, or customer location attestation showing the place of use is outside Canada

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate. A GST/HST invoice must include prescribed particulars: supplier name and GST/HST registration number, invoice date, description of supply, total amount, tax payable, and any zero-rated or exempt designation. Electronic invoices and e-records are accepted provided they are legible and retained; CRA may require electronic access to records. No government-operated API clearance or digital stamping applies.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export claims within the normal four-year reassessment period, with no time limit where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, or wilful default is established. On audit failure, CRA retroactively disallows zero-rating, recovers any claimed input tax credits/refunds, and charges daily compound interest on the resulting 5% GST/HST liability. The Excise Tax Act imposes penalties for false statements or omissions of the greater of CAD 250 and up to 25% of the net tax amount, with gross negligence penalties potentially reaching higher amounts; criminal prosecution is possible for fraudulent export zero-rating claims, and partners of an LP may face civil liability to the extent of the partnership's compliance obligations and applicable indemnity provisions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with full convertibility for current account transactions under the Foreign Exchange Act. Capital account transactions are subject to review and potential restrictions by the Minister of Finance for national economic policy reasons. Profit remittances to non-resident partners are generally permitted with supporting documentation including beneficial ownership certificates and tax filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must surrender all export proceeds to a Canadian bank within 90 days of receipt under the Export and Import Permits Act. No mandatory conversion ratio applies; proceeds must be converted to Canadian dollars unless specific exemptions are granted by the Bank of Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
