Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Alberta limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed contractually in the Limited Partnership Agreement and may be settled by cash or in-kind subscription, with no escrow or minimum deposit requirement. Under s. 55 of the Partnership Act, a limited partner is liable only to the extent of its unpaid committed capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Alberta Fire Code (Safety Codes Act) with a municipal safety codes permit; standard office structural loading (B/C occupancy) is accepted without engineering re-certification; occupancy and workplace clearance under the Alberta Occupational Health and Safety Code, including lighting, first-aid, and emergency-exit compliance, is completed before employee occupation.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Alberta limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed contractually in the Limited Partnership Agreement and may be settled by cash or in-kind subscription, with no escrow or minimum deposit requirement. Under s. 55 of the Partnership Act, a limited partner is liable only to the extent of its unpaid committed capital.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a general U.S.-style check-the-box election for an Alberta limited partnership. A valid partnership is generally taxed under the flow-through rules of section 96 of the Income Tax Act, with income and losses computed at partnership level and allocated to partners; the partnership generally is not itself liable for income tax on ordinary partnership income. The partnership may have mandatory or elective filing and fiscal-period consequences under the Income Tax Act and may be subject to special anti-avoidance, specified investment flow-through partnership, and non-resident-partner rules. Canada has enacted hybrid mismatch rules that may deny deductions or include amounts where cross-border characterisation differences create a mismatch; those rules do not constitute an entity-classification election. U.S. or other foreign tax elections do not determine Canadian classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies, generally measured over a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, subject to the detailed small-supplier rules. The threshold applies to the registrant and associated persons and generally excludes certain exempt supplies and specified financial services. Voluntary registration is permitted, and the simplified GST/HST regime for qualifying non-resident digital-economy suppliers uses a CAD 30,000 threshold measured over the applicable 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, a supply may generally be zero-rated where the goods are exported from Canada in circumstances meeting the Excise Tax Act rules, including export by the supplier or delivery to a recipient for export, with prescribed documentary evidence. For SaaS and other services, zero-rating is fact-specific. A service supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated where the recipient is non-resident and the statutory conditions are satisfied, subject to exclusions for services in respect of real property or tangible personal property in Canada, personal services performed in Canada, services connected with litigation or regulatory proceedings in Canada, and other prescribed exclusions. Electronically supplied software, platform access, data processing, and telematics services require analysis of the recipient, place of use, contractual supply, Canadian-user access, and the specific zero-rating provisions. Services supplied to Canadian-resident customers generally attract GST even if the supplier is non-resident. Documentary support should include customer residency evidence, contracts, billing records, user-location and access data where relevant, payment records, tax determinations, and evidence that no statutory exclusion applies. Input tax credits or GST rebates require valid invoices and records establishing that the inputs were acquired for commercial activities; zero-rated exports do not require GST collection but may preserve input tax credit entitlement under the regular GST regime.

  • For exported tangible goods: Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) export declaration or other applicable customs filing, commercial invoice, packing list, transport document such as bill of lading or air waybill, and proof of export or delivery outside Canada.
  • For electronically supplied logistics-fleet SaaS and offshore services: a customs export declaration and bill of lading are generally not applicable because no tangible goods cross the border. Maintain the executed customer contract, customer residency and business-address evidence, service description, invoices, payment records, access or usage records, and evidence supporting the statutory zero-rating provision.
  • An inward remittance certificate is not a general Canadian statutory requirement for GST zero-rating or GST refund claims. Bank statements and payment records should nevertheless be retained where they corroborate the supply and recipient.
  • Where exported goods are involved, retain customs records and supporting commercial documentation for the CRA audit-retention period and reconcile export declarations with invoices and accounting records.

Canada and Alberta do not generally require real-time electronic invoice clearance, government digital stamping, or continuous transaction-control reporting for ordinary SaaS supplies. GST registrants must issue compliant invoices or receipts when required, retain electronic books and records, and provide prescribed information such as the supplier name, BN/GST number where applicable, date, description, consideration, and tax amount or applicable rate. CRA electronic filing and payment obligations apply to registered businesses. Electronic records must be reliable, accessible, and retained for the statutory period, generally six years from the end of the relevant year. Sector-specific electronic invoicing requirements may arise under a government contract but are not a general Alberta tax requirement.

Canada has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for claiming GST zero-rating or input tax credits. CRA may reassess tax where zero-rating is unsupported, the customer is not eligible, the service falls within an exclusion, or the transaction was mischaracterized. The supplier may become liable for uncollected GST, interest, and applicable administrative penalties. False statements or omissions may attract gross-negligence penalties generally equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit, subject to the Excise Tax Act rules; failure-to-file, failure-to-remit, and repeated non-compliance penalties may also apply. Fraudulent claims can result in denial and recovery of input tax credits or rebates, assessment of interest and penalties, extended reassessment periods, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion or false documentation. Any reassessment may include retrospective GST, interest, and penalties for all affected reporting periods.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% general combined rate (Federal 15% + Alberta 8%); 11% combined for Canadian-controlled private corporation active business income up to CAD 500,000 (Federal 9% + Alberta 2%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII statutory rate for dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation; for the Alberta LP, profit distributions to limited partners are not dividends and are generally taxed as allocated partnership income under Part I; foreign corporate partners may also be subject to Part XIV branch tax on retained Canadian PE earnings

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No monetary threshold for contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation; Canadian taxpayers and partnerships must document all non-arm's-length cross-border transactions. Form T106 reporting is required when reportable transactions with a non-resident exceed CAD 1,000,000 in fair market value; Country-by-Country reporting applies when consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000; Local File and Master File are prepared as disciplined evidence to rebut penalty assessments

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; instead, CRA applies ITA s.245 GAAR, sham doctrine, and s.247(2) recharacterization. An Alberta LP engaged in fleet dispatch and telematics SaaS must demonstrate real staff, active decision-making, telematics software, client contracts, and Canadian operational substance; a letter-box LP or flow-through conduit will fail economic-substance scrutiny and may be assessed at partner level, including branch tax and GAAR penalties

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Unfiled Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s.239(1): fine from 50% to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under s.163(2); CRA interest, asset seizure, and deemed-trust claims

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct or assent to evasion are jointly and severally liable for penalties and unpaid tax; courts may order civil restitution, freeze assets, impose travel bans as bail/restraint conditions, and support extradition for aggravated cross-border conduct
