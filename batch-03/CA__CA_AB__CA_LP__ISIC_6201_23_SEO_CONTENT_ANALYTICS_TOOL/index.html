Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Corporate Registry, Service Alberta (Registrar of Companies, Government of Alberta)

Governing statute

Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c P-3 (Alberta), Part 3 (Limited Partnerships) - s. 51 (registration by Declaration of Limited Partnership), the provisions preserving limited liability of limited partners, and the non-application of the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) to limited partnerships.

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership (Alberta Corporate Registry Form LP-1)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 51 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow deposit applies to Alberta limited partnerships. The LP must have at least one general partner with unlimited liability and at least one limited partner. The capital contribution (cash or property) must be agreed and paid at the time or terms stated in the partnership agreement; funds may be held in any Canadian bank account and no escrow is mandated by the Partnership Act.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Alberta Fire Code and local fire authority inspection; structural load and occupancy clearance under the Alberta Building Code for the leased Class-A office suite; occupational health and safety clearance and hazard assessment under the OHS Act, SA 2020, c O-2.2 and the Alberta OHS Code; civil defence clearance: not applicable for an office-based SaaS establishment.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online system, My Business Account, Represent a Client, and the GST/HST program account. Alberta has no provincial sales tax; Alberta corporate and partnership filings are administered through the Alberta Corporate Registry, while federal GST/HST administration is conducted by the CRA.

E-government filing portal

An Alberta limited partnership should obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) and, where applicable, a GST/HST program account identified by the RT suffix. The filing sequence is: (1) complete Alberta partnership registration and obtain the provincial registration evidence; (2) obtain a BN through CRA Business Registration Online, CRA-authorized representative channels, or the CRA non-resident registration process; (3) submit entity name, Alberta registered office, partnership agreement details, formation date, business activities, responsible partners, foreign ownership information, fiscal year-end, and authorized-contact information; (4) apply for the GST/HST account using Form RC1 or the applicable CRA online registration workflow; (5) provide identity, authority, and formation documents if requested; and (6) maintain CRA account information and file required GST/HST returns. A foreign partner does not generally need a Canadian individual TIN merely to hold an LP interest, but may require a Canadian tax identification number for Canadian tax filings, withholding, treaty claims, or CRA correspondence. The LP may also require a payroll, import-export, or other CRA program account depending on its activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election for an Alberta limited partnership. A valid Canadian partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes, with partnership income and losses allocated to partners under the Income Tax Act and the partnership agreement, subject to statutory restrictions. Available elections are transaction-specific rather than entity-classification elections, including applicable partnership fiscal-period, rollover, allocation, foreign-affiliate, and treaty-related elections. Classification for foreign tax purposes may differ from Canadian treatment; the partnership should obtain a formal Canadian and relevant foreign-jurisdiction classification analysis. Hybrid-mismatch rules, foreign affiliate rules, non-resident withholding provisions, and treaty limitation provisions may apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated supplies, measured under the small-supplier rules. The threshold is generally tested over a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. Registration may be required earlier where the LP ceases to qualify as a small supplier or voluntarily registers. Non-resident digital-economy suppliers may be subject to specialized GST/HST registration rules, depending on the customer base, supply characterization, and applicable platform or simplified-registration regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods may generally be zero-rated when exported from Canada and the supplier retains satisfactory evidence that the goods were exported in accordance with the Export and Imported Goods and Services provisions. Services supplied to a non-resident may qualify for zero-rating where the statutory conditions are satisfied, but exclusions apply, including certain services connected with Canadian real property, tangible personal property, litigation, personal services, and services performed in Canada. SaaS, data-crawling, SEO analytics, and electronically supplied services require supply-by-supply analysis, including customer residency, contractual recipient, place-of-supply facts, performance location, use or enjoyment, and whether the service is an excluded Canadian-connected service. A non-resident customer alone does not automatically establish zero-rating.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, description, value, and destination
  • Export declaration or applicable Canadian Border Services Agency export reporting record where legally required
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier record, or other carrier evidence for exported goods
  • Proof of delivery or receipt outside Canada
  • Contracts, purchase orders, customer residency records, and billing records
  • Evidence supporting non-resident status and the applicable zero-rating provision for services
  • Payment records or bank statements evidencing the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general Canadian statutory requirement
  • GST/HST return, general-ledger, and tax-invoice records supporting the zero-rated supply and any input tax credit claim

Canada has no general federal real-time electronic-invoice clearance or continuous transaction-control mandate for ordinary B2B SaaS supplies. Electronic invoices and records are permitted if they preserve reliability, readability, accessibility, and auditability. GST/HST invoices must contain information required by the CRA, including supplier identity and BN/GST-HST account details where applicable, date, description, consideration, tax rate or amount, and customer information where required for input tax credit documentation. There is no general federal digital tax stamp or pre-approval requirement. Alberta has no provincial sales-tax invoicing regime. Specialized public-sector procurement or contractual e-invoicing requirements may apply separately.

Canada has no general foreign-exchange repatriation requirement or statutory deadline for export proceeds. CRA may reassess GST/HST where zero-rating is unsupported, including assessment of tax that should have been collected, denial or recovery of input tax credits and refunds, applicable arrears interest, and penalties. Gross-negligence penalties may generally equal 50% of the understated net tax or overstated refund or credit, subject to the statutory calculation. False statements, omissions, fabricated export evidence, or obstruction may create additional civil penalties and, in serious cases, criminal exposure. Export records and GST/HST books must be retained for the applicable statutory period, generally six years from the end of the relevant year, subject to CRA extensions and special rules. Refunds may be clawed back with interest where the claim was paid on the basis of ineligible or fraudulent documentation.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted the IMF Article VIII obligations and operates a freely floating, fully convertible currency with no exchange-control settlement approvals. Current-account convertibility is unrestricted: the ISIC 6201 / NAICS 541511 SEO content-analytics tool generates subscription and licensing revenue that may be received in any currency without Bank of Canada or Finance Canada authorisation, and digital services exported from Canada benefit from GST/HST zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act. Capital-account convertibility is equally open: non-resident limited partners may inject cash or assets into the Alberta LP without any foreign-exchange decree, exchange-rate band, or conversion quota. The relevant frictions are not FX-control but rather: (i) FINTRAC reporting by the receiving bank for any wire transfer, including partner capital subscriptions, of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) federal income-tax compliance under the Income Tax Act, which treats the LP as fiscally transparent, obliges filing of the T5013 Statement of Partnership Income, and taxes each non-resident partner on its allocated share of Canadian-source business income, with the possibility of treaty relief; (iii) Alberta Partnership Act formalities requiring a registered Alberta office, the General Partner's consent, and the Statement of Limited Partnership to be updated for capital changes; and (iv) Investment Canada Act review, which applies only where a foreign acquisition triggers the high monetary review threshold for a 'Canadian business' and does not ordinarily apply to a passive subscription into a lean software start-up LP. Profit remittance presents no statutory hurdle: there is no repatriation tax, no exit-licence, no black-market exchange mechanism, and no prohibition on distributing cash to non-resident partners, provided the LP maintains accurate capital accounts so that distributions represent after-tax or tax-free capital streams rather than untaxed earnings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable / zero surrender obligation. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to sell export receipts to the central bank or a designated dealer. Cross-border receipts from the SEO analytics and keyword-research software subscriptions are freely retained in CAD, USD, EUR, or any other convertible currency, and may be payable into the LP's operational or capital escrow accounts at a Tier-1 Canadian bank. The only mandatory compliance obligations are: (a) the banks automatically report to FINTRAC all electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (b) the LP must retain commercial invoices, transfer records, and customer agreements for at least five years under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act / money-service-business record-keeping standards; and (c) where the bank requests a statistical classification, the LP should confirm the purpose code 'INVS' or 'CASH' and the bank will record the inflow on its Bank of Canada balance-of-payments return as non-resident direct investment in a Canadian partnership. No surrender certificates, no central-bank settlement tickets, and no exchange-control endorsements are required for a Canada-resident limited partnership exporting digital services.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank of Canada, responsible for monetary stability, Canadian dollar issuance, the national large-value payment-system oversight, and joint management with the Minister of Finance of the Exchange Fund Account. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange licencing commission or capital-controls bureau. Regulatory responsibility for the cross-border banking and FX compliance of an Alberta limited partnership is distributed among: the Bank of Canada (currency, payment-system and retail-payment-activity supervision), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (prudential regulation of Tier-1 banks under the Bank Act and related statutes), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing surveillance, including CAD 10,000 wire and cash transaction reporting), the Department of Finance Canada (economic sanctions and Exchange Fund Account policy), the Alberta Corporate Registry (registration of the limited partnership under the Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c. P-7), and the Alberta Securities Commission (derivatives dealing and over-the-counter hedging regulatory framework for Alberta financial-market participants). The practical effect for the Alberta LP is that no exchange-control approval is required for inbound or outbound funds; instead, transaction-level compliance is enforced through the banks and FINTRAC, with market-conduct and sanctions screening applied at the point of payment settlement.
