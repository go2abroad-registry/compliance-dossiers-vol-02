Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal GST/HST registration and income tax administration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/business-number.html; provincial counterpart: Alberta Registries / Alberta Corporate Registry (Corporate Access Number issuance).

E-government filing portal

An Alberta Limited Partnership (CA_LP) obtains its statutory tax identity by: (1) filing the declaration of partnership under the Alberta Partnership Act at Alberta Registries and acquiring the Alberta Corporate Access Number; (2) applying to the Canada Revenue Agency for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or Form RC3484 (GST/HST and VAT Registration); (3) adding a GST/HST program account (five-digit 'RM' suffix) to the BN; (4) where general partners are non-residents, filing Form RC59 (Business Representative) and complying with non-resident withholding obligations. Online BRO applications generally yield an immediate BN and GST/HST account; paper applications process in 5–10 business days. A foreign-controlled anti-cheat middleware LP should register voluntarily even below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold so it can issue 0% GST export invoices to non-resident game publishers and claim input tax credits.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — An Alberta Limited Partnership (CA_LP) obtains its statutory tax identity by: (1) filing the declaration of partnership under the Alberta Partnership Act at Alberta Registries and acquiring the Alberta Corporate Access Number; (2) applying to the Canada Revenue Agency for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or Form RC3484 (GST/HST and VAT Registration); (3) adding a GST/HST program account (five-digit 'RM' suffix) to the BN; (4) where general partners are non-residents, filing Form RC59 (Business Representative) and complying with non-resident withholding obligations. Online BRO applications generally yield an immediate BN and GST/HST account; paper applications process in 5–10 business days. A foreign-controlled anti-cheat middleware LP should register voluntarily even below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold so it can issue 0% GST export invoices to non-resident game publishers and claim input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

For Canadian federal income tax, the Alberta LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96; income, gains, losses, GST obligations and input tax credits flow through to partners, and no Canadian 'check-the-box' election exists. For U.S. federal tax purposes, the CA_LP may file Form 8832 and elect partnership pass-through status under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3. Indirect tax elections available under the Excise Tax Act include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration below the CAD 30,000 threshold (ETA s. 240); (ii) a joint election to treat related persons as a single registrant (ETA s. 273.1); and (iii) partnership capital-contribution/self-supply elections. Canada does not provide a standalone hybrid-mismatch election; hybrid arrangements are policed through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) and BEPS Action 2 commentary.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies, actual or anticipated, over any four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 240); once exceeded in a quarter, GST/HST registration becomes mandatory as of the threshold-crossing day, and gaming anti-cheat middleware LPs typically register voluntarily to zero-rate export sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available under ETA s. 16 and Schedule VI, Part V where (a) tangible property is exported by the supplier, (b) a service is supplied to a non-resident person and is not performed in Canada for a Canadian person, or (c) intangible personal property, including downloaded software licences and intellectual property rights, is supplied to a non-resident recipient in circumstances where the licence is not used in Canada by a registered person. Kernel-level anti-cheat middleware (SDK licences, key activation, cloud attestation, telemetry analytics, and threat-rule updates) delivered by download or API to non-resident game publishers qualifies for the 0% GST rate where the recipient is a non-resident person, the service/IP is effectively used outside Canada, and payment is traceable to the exporter.

  • B13A Canada Customs Export Declaration / CBSA ACI e-manifest where tangible components or hardware are exported
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill confirming export before Canadian use (or electronic download/activation log for intangible exports)
  • Proof-of-Export Certificate / non-resident declaration executed under ETA Regulations s. 10
  • Commercial invoice or zero-rated GST/HST invoice containing the CA_LP's BN and explicit zero-rated designation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / Stripe payout record evidencing foreign-exchange repatriation of export consideration

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API telemetry for GST/HST invoices. However, every GST/HST invoice must include prescribed particulars (ETA Regulation s. 3: supplier name, BN, invoice date, recipient identification, nature of supply, consideration, and tax charged). CRA accepts EDI and machine-readable invoices and audits using the Standard Audit File for Tax (SAF-T) format. Federal procurement is migrating to PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoicing in phased mandates for federal suppliers, but no provincial e-invoicing or QR-stamping regime applies in Alberta. Exporters of anti-cheat software should retain digital licence-activation logs, SDK delivery receipts, and non-resident customer master data to satisfy CRA digital-compliance data matching with CBSA export declarations and banking records.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export claims within four years, and without limitation where fraud, wilful misrepresentation, or gross negligence is established; the reassessment claws back unremitted GST/HST, denies input tax credits, and compounds arrears interest. False or misleading statements attract ETA s. 285 penalties (CAD 100 or 25% of net tax) and s. 281.1 gross-negligence penalties (greater of CAD 100 and 50% of net tax). Customs Act ss. 32 and 40 can impose administrative monetary penalties up to 200% of the value of dishonestly declared goods, while Income Tax Act s. 239(1) exposes partners to fines up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment. CRA's Digital Compliance program and CBSA cross-validate export declarations, FX inflows, and licensing data; adherence to a strict 180-day repatriation substantiation window materially reduces audit exposure for the CA_LP.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Capital account transactions are generally permitted subject to FINTRAC reporting and beneficial ownership disclosure. Profit remittance to non-resident limited partners is unrestricted provided tax compliance and source-of-funds verification are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or statutory conversion ratio applies. Export receipts must be repatriated to Canada and reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Conversion occurs at prevailing market rates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility for digital electronic money institutions such as Stripe under the Canadian Payment Services Act. Legal constraints include FINTRAC registration, anti-money laundering program compliance, and provincial licensing for cross-border payment services.
