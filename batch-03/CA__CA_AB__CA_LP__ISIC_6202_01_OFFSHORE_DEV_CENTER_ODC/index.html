Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). National digital portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business.html and My Business Account at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html. Alberta provincial registry: Service Alberta / Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES) at https://www.alberta.ca/corporate-registry for extra-provincial Limited Partnership registration; joint federal-provincial one-stop registration is available via the Alberta Business Registry.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) from CRA in the exact legal name of the Limited Partnership (LP), filed online through BRO or by paper Form RC1. Step 2: Register the Alberta LP extra-provincially under Alberta's Limited Partnership Act if formed outside Alberta, or obtain the provincial certificate of registration if Alberta-domiciled; this is done via CORES. Step 3: Activate CRA program accounts under the same BN: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll, if Canadian employees are hired), RC (income tax; not applied to the LP itself but used for corporate partner instalments where applicable). The LP itself files a T5013 Partnership Information Return but pays no federal income tax. Step 4: For non-resident foreign partners or a foreign-domiciled limited partnership, submit certified constitutional documents (registration certificate, partnership agreement, powers of attorney) and appoint a Canadian agent for service under the Alberta Limited Partnership Act; CRA requires an authorized representative (Form RC59 or online authorisation) for non-resident entities. Step 5: CRA normally issues the BN and GST/HST account confirmation within 1-2 weeks for online applications and 4-8 weeks for paper filings. Step 6: Register separately for municipal business licenses (City of Calgary / City of Edmonton) if a physical office is opened. Step 7: If the LP hires staff, register with the Alberta Workers' Compensation Board (WCB) and obtain a WCB account number. Step 8: No Revenu Québec registration is needed unless the LP carries on activities in Quebec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; pass-through status for LPs is statutory under Income Tax Act (ITA) s.96 and Excise Tax Act (ETA) s.272.1, not elective. Available elections and statutory mechanisms: (a) ITA s.216(1) election allowing a non-resident partner to report Canadian-source partnership income on a net basis where applicable; (b) ETA s.150 election under which closely related registrants may elect to treat otherwise taxable inter-party supplies as exempt or zero-rated for input tax credit (ITC) management; (c) ETA s.156 election for supplies between closely related corporations (useful where Canadian corporate partners use the LP as a co-venture vehicle); (d) ETA s.211 election for change-in-use of capital property (deemed self-supply rules); (e) hybrid mismatch compliance tracking and elections under ITA ss.18.4 and 18.5, which neutralise deduction/non-inclusion mismatches for partnerships with US or EU tax-transparent partners; (f) ITA s.247(11) election allowing the taxpayer and CRA to jointly adjust transfer-pricing consequences facing the ODC; (g) provincial allocation of partnership income follows the T5013 permanent-establishment formula, with Alberta using the standard federal-provincial allocation schedules; no Alberta-specific LP tax election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports, financial services, and goodwill) over any four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s.240). Below this threshold the LP is a small supplier and is generally exempt from GST/HST registration. Voluntary registration is permitted and is strongly recommended for an offshore development centre (ODC) because it allows full input tax credits on Canadian procurement and zero-rating of exports. There is no separate provincial threshold for Alberta because Alberta has no provincial VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 (which zero-rates services) and Part VII (which zero-rates goods), a supply of services qualifies for 0% GST/HST when: (a) the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered under the ETA; (b) the service is not performed in Canada, or, if performed in Canada, is not performed primarily for the benefit of a person resident in Canada; and (c) the service does not relate to real property located in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed (with limited exceptions). An ODC providing offshore software engineering, maintenance, DevOps, and related IT services to a non-resident client qualifies under section 7, so no GST/HST is charged and no export refund mechanism is required. Instead, the LP claims full input tax credits on its Canadian operating expenses (SaaS tools, hosting, hardware, telecommunication, rented premises) through its ordinary GST/HST net-filing return; where input tax credits exceed output tax, a net refund is payable by CRA. If the non-resident recipient is itself registered for GST/HST and acquires the service for use in a Canadian commercial activity, the more specific place-of-supply rules under ETA ss.142 and 143 may make the supply taxable at 5%; a professional tax opinion on the supply map should accompany the file.

  • Master Services Agreement / Offshore Development Centre contract identifying the non-resident counterparty, commercial terms, IP assignment, and place of performance (Canada vs. abroad)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime exists in Canada (unlike LATAM, EU, or India). The applicable mandates are: (1) every GST/HST registrant must issue invoices containing prescribed particulars (ETA s.223: seller BN, date, description, total consideration, and GST/HST amount payable); (2) electronic invoices in machine-readable formats (PDF, XML, TXT) are permitted and must be retained in a format accessible to CRA for six years; (3) mandatory GST/HST NETFILE e-filing applies to all registrants with taxable turnover equal to or exceeding CAD 1 million; registrants below this threshold may file electronically or by paper; (4) federal B2G e-invoicing through PEPPOL-compliant channels is mandatory for contracts with Public Services and Procurement Canada (PSPC) where applicable; (5) payments are remitted through CRA My Payment, online banking, or wire transfer; (6) there is no API-connected clearance or pre-approval of invoices at the federal or Alberta provincial level.

CRA treats zero-rating claims on software services as a medium-high audit focus, particularly where the supplier is a related-party ODC. Substantiation failures trigger the following consequences: (1) Reassessment within the normal 4-year window (ETA s.298 and ITA s.152(4)), with no time limit where misrepresentation, fraud, or a filed waiver exists; (2) Reclassification of the supply as taxable at 5% GST/HST, denial of input tax credits, and assessment of net tax plus prescribed daily compound interest (currently approximately 9% per annum); (3) Penalty for a false statement or omission in a GST/HST return: the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax attributable to the false statement (ETA s.285(1)); (4) Late-filing and late-remittance penalties under ETA s.280, accruing at 1% of the unpaid amount plus 0.25% per month for up to 12 months; (5) Income tax gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under ITA s.163(2) applies to partnership allocations and net income misstatements; (6) Transfer-pricing adjustments under ITA s.247 permit CRA to reset the fees charged by the Canadian ODC to arm's length, with resulting additional Canadian income tax, interest, and penalties; failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days of invoicing without commercial justification may trigger an imputed interest benefit or deemed dividend under ITA s.15 and Part XIII withholding tax at 25% on non-resident partners; (7) ETA record-keeping rules (s.286) require retention of books and documents for six years, and missing export evidence leads to automatic denial of zero-rating and ITCs. Criminal prosecution for wilful evasion is possible under ITA s.239 and ETA s.327.1 in egregious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible, non-controlled currency regime for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Non-resident investors may freely inject equity or debt capital into an Alberta Limited Partnership and repatriate profits, dividends, interest, royalties, or liquidation proceeds without exchange-control permits, conversion quotas, or central bank approval. The only practical impediments are standard anti-money-laundering (AML) verification, FINTRAC reporting for electronic transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, sanctions screening, FATCA/CRS classification, and any applicable Canadian withholding tax (Part XIII) on distributions or payments to non-residents. For an offshore development centre, recurring cross-border transfers will be examined for commercial substance, transfer pricing consistency, and beneficial ownership clarity, but no currency conversion gate applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or any mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in any foreign currency, held in Canadian or offshore bank accounts, and deployed directly for trade settlements, payroll, and operational disbursements. Banks may request the underlying export or service agreement, invoice, and evidence of the commercial transaction to comply with AML and sanctions due diligence, but no exchange-control timeline or conversion mandate applies in Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking regulation and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border transaction monitoring; Canada does not maintain a centralized exchange-control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for auxiliary collection and low-value vendor disbursement rails; Low for primary treasury settlement and capital-holding purposes. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions such as Wise B2B and Payoneer can provide efficient cross-border payment rails, but they are not chartered Canadian Schedule I banks and cannot serve as the primary account for receiving equity capital injections or for clearing large trade transactions. Canadian law, including the Retail Payment Activities Act framework, requires domestic payment service providers to be registered or recognized; foreign EMIs operating without Canadian licensing create counterparty, segregation, and uninsured deposit risk. The Alberta LP should maintain a primary bank account at a federally regulated Canadian bank and use fintech EMIs only for specific low-balance disbursement workflows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The recommended inbound rail is a SWIFT MT103 wire from the investing entity's own bank account to the Alberta LP's bank account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The wire should identify the beneficiary precisely as the Alberta LP, include the LP registration number, and reference either 'Equity Contribution to Alberta Limited Partnership' or 'Partner Loan to Alberta Limited Partnership'. Canada does not operate a mandatory transaction purpose-code system, but FINTRAC requires reporting of all incoming electronic transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, and the receiving bank will perform sanctions, politically-exposed-person, and source-of-funds screening. Supporting documentation should include a capital subscription agreement, a resolution of the general partner authorising the transfer, an updated partner register, and, for loans, an executed loan agreement with interest-rate and withholding-tax terms. Wire transfers from tax- haven or high-risk jurisdictions will likely be rejected absent a detailed explanation and underlying contract documents.For outbound settlement to foreign software vendors, cloud-service providers, subcontracted engineers, or licensors, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 single-payment transfers and, for high-value or documentary transactions, Commercial Letters of Credit or Standby Letters of Credit. CAD-denominated payments to Canadian participants clear through Payment Canada's Large Value Transfer System or real-time retail rails; USD and other foreign-currency payments settle through correspondent banking networks. Every cross-border outgoing wire equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 is subject to FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting. Banks will require underlying invoices, service agreements, beneficiary KYC details, and transfer-pricing explanations before releasing payments to related or foreign counterparties. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for auxiliary collection and low-value vendor disbursement rails; Low for primary treasury settlement and capital-holding purposes. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions such as Wise B2B and Payoneer can provide efficient cross-border payment rails, but they are not chartered Canadian Schedule I banks and cannot serve as the primary account for receiving equity capital injections or for clearing large trade transactions. Canadian law, including the Retail Payment Activities Act framework, requires domestic payment service providers to be registered or recognized; foreign EMIs operating without Canadian licensing create counterparty, segregation, and uninsured deposit risk. The Alberta LP should maintain a primary bank account at a federally regulated Canadian bank and use fintech EMIs only for specific low-balance disbursement workflows.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate rate (federal 15% + Alberta 8%); 11% combined CCPC small business rate (federal 9% + Alberta 2%) on eligible active business income up to CA$500,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) levied on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Treaty relief commonly reduces this to 5% for corporate shareholders holding at least 10% voting interest (e.g., Canada-US DTA) and 15% for portfolio shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a fiscal period ending on or after March 22, 2017, the return is due within five months after the partnership's fiscal year-end. A calendar year-end partnership (December 31) must file by May 31.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within six months after the end of the corporation's taxation year. For a December 31 year-end, the filing deadline is June 30. Income tax balances are generally due within two months for large corporations and three months for certain Canadian-controlled private corporations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no explicit statutory monetary threshold for Local File/Master File preparation, but CRA expects contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for controlled cross-border transactions involving the ODC; moreover, a multinational group with consolidated annual revenue of at least CA$750 million must file a Country-by-Country report and associated notification in Canada.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) as in OFC regimes; however, the Income Tax Act's general anti-avoidance rule (section 245) and CRA treaty-shopping guidance require a genuine business purpose, substantive management, payroll, assets, and risk assumption in Alberta. An offshore software development centre that is a mere shell in Canada, without technical staff, physical premises, or real software engineering activity, will be vulnerable to recharacterization, GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and penalty exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under section 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; criminal prosecution under section 239(1) with a fine up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers face personal civil liability for tax arrears under section 160, including recovery against personal assets; criminal convictions carry imprisonment, loss of professional standing, and travel restrictions in extradition or mutual assistance proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the transfer price to the arm's-length amount; section 247(3) imposes a penalty of 10% on the net transfer-pricing adjustment if the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to be accurate and have contemporaneous documentation; arrears interest and non-deductibility penalties may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct deficient transfer-pricing documentation may be assessed personally for failure to withhold taxes on related-party payments; personal assets may be garnished through section 160 assessments, and directors may be barred from re-entering Canada pending collection.
Withholding Tax Remittance Failure (Part XIII)

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount plus daily interest charged under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act; gross negligence penalties may reach 50%; CRA may issue a registered security interest against the non-resident's Canadian property.

Director personal liability: Directors and partnership general partners are jointly and severally liable under section 227.1 for unremitted source deductions and withholding taxes; bankruptcy does not automatically discharge that liability, and CRA can pursue personal bank accounts, wage garnishments, and non-resident exit/interdict measures.
Shell Company / Abuse of Limited Partnership Structure

Statutory sanction: CRA may invoke GAAR (section 245) to deny the tax benefit, recharacterize the partnership as a bare trust, reallocate income to the foreign principal, and impose a 25% gross-negligence penalty; the Alberta Corporate Registry may revoke or refuse to renew the LP registration, and provincial securities/regulatory penalties may arise.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors, and controlling partners may be assessed personally for the entire Canadian tax liability through partnership and transparency rules; they may face a prohibition on leaving Canada pending tax collection, seizure of personal property, and criminal liability under section 462.31 for money laundering if the shell structure is used to disguise the origin of funds.
