Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal tax administration for GST/HST, income tax and T5013 partnership information returns. Digital portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html) and Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/business-registration-online.html). Provincial counterpart for entity formation: Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta / CORES) under the Partnership Act (RSA 2000, c. P-3). Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers import/export program accounts (RM) where applicable.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Register the Alberta Limited Partnership under the Partnership Act (RSA 2000, c. P-3) through the Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta or authorized service provider) to obtain the provincial registration file number; no minimum capital is required and non-resident partners are permitted. Step 2 - Obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) for the partnership from CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; the BN is the master identifier for all CRA program accounts. Step 3 - Register the LP itself as the GST/HST registrant under program account 'RT' (GST/HST); under ETA s. 123(1) a partnership is a 'person' for GST/HST purposes, so the partnership, not the individual partners, holds the registration and collects tax. Step 4 - Add other program accounts only if needed: 'RC' (corporate income tax - inapplicable to a flow-through LP unless it elects opaque treatment), 'RP' (payroll for any Canadian employees), 'RM' (import/export - not applicable to pure digital service exports). Step 5 - For foreign partners or foreign parent entities: CRA's non-resident registration desk accepts applications without a Canadian bank account; foreign identification documents and a completed representative authorization (or power of attorney) are required; the simplified non-resident digital-services GST/HST registration applies to foreign sellers making specified supplies to Canadian recipients. Step 6 - File the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually under the partnership BN and issue T5013 slips to all partners. Typical timelines: provincial registration 1-3 business days; CRA BN and GST/HST registration immediate when filed online or 2-4 weeks by paper.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - For Canadian income tax purposes the Alberta LP is fiscally transparent: no entity-level Part I tax is imposed; each partner's allocable share of income/loss is computed under ITA s. 96 and taxed in the hands of the partner. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is a separate 'person' (ETA s. 123(1)) and must register, collect and remit GST in its own name. Territorial scope: the LP carries on business in Canada through its Alberta establishment, making it a Canadian-resident partnership subject to Canadian tax on worldwide income as allocated to resident partners and on Canadian-source income allocated to non-resident partners; GST applies to supplies made in Canada based on place-of-supply rules. Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax (only 5% federal GST) and no provincial capital tax or general payroll tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - For Canadian income tax purposes the Alberta LP is fiscally transparent: no entity-level Part I tax is imposed; each partner's allocable share of income/loss is computed under ITA s. 96 and taxed in the hands of the partner. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is a separate 'person' (ETA s. 123(1)) and must register, collect and remit GST in its own name. Territorial scope: the LP carries on business in Canada through its Alberta establishment, making it a Canadian-resident partnership subject to Canadian tax on worldwide income as allocated to resident partners and on Canadian-source income allocated to non-resident partners; GST applies to supplies made in Canada based on place-of-supply rules. Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax (only 5% federal GST) and no provincial capital tax or general payroll tax. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable - Canada and Alberta maintain no free-zone or special economic zone exemption for digital services.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box regime; entity classification is statutory. Available elections directly relevant to an Alberta LP: (i) Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty Article IV(7) fiscal-transparency election - a U.S. entity (LLC or LP) that is fiscally transparent for U.S. tax may be recognized as fiscally transparent in Canada for treaty benefit purposes (CRA administrative position); (ii) ETA s. 273 joint election between a partnership and a registrant partner to treat supplies of taxable services as made for nil consideration; (iii) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations (inapplicable to a pure partnership but available if corporate subsidiaries are inserted); (iv) GST quick method (ETA s. 227) and annual filing elections for eligible small registrants; (v) ITA partnership elections regarding fiscal period and computation of income under s. 96; (vi) hybrid-mismatch rules under OECD BEPS Action 2 have been announced by the Department of Finance but are not yet enacted - no election currently exists to override a foreign opaque/transparent mismatch, and the LP should monitor draft legislation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in a 12-month period (the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1)). For the non-resident digital-services regime effective July 1, 2021, the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies to specified digital supplies made to Canadian recipients, determined under the 'Canadian recipient' rules (at least two of: Canadian IP address, Canadian billing address, Canadian bank account information, or Canadian telephone number). For a Canadian-resident Alberta LP, once combined taxable worldwide supplies including Apple IAP and Google Play Billing revenue exceed CAD 30,000, GST registration is mandatory from the earlier of the first day of the following month or quarter; voluntary registration is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported digital services and intangible property is available under ETA Schedule VI. Part V, s. 7 zero-rates a supply of a service made to a non-resident person unless the service is in respect of real property situated in Canada, is in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or is rendered to an individual present in Canada at the time the service is rendered. Part IV zero-rates supplies of intangible personal property (including digital content and licences) made to non-resident persons. For the voice-streaming app: (i) subscriptions, virtual gifts and in-app consumables sold to end consumers resident outside Canada are zero-rated when billing address, payment method country code and IP geolocation are all non-Canadian; (ii) B2B advertising, licensing and API services supplied to non-resident platforms and ad networks are zero-rated under the same s. 7 test; (iii) supplies to Canadian consumers are taxable at 5% GST regardless of the seller's location; (iv) where Apple or Google acts as app-store platform operator, the platform-operator deemed-supplier rule applies only to non-resident, non-registered sellers - a Canadian-resident LP registrant is the principal supplier and must collect and remit GST on Canadian consumer transactions; (v) no zero-rating is available where the service is consumed in Canada or enjoyed by an individual present in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / ACI eManifest) - not applicable to pure digital service exports; required only if physical goods (e.g., branded merchandise) are shipped from Canada and should not be relied upon for zero-rating substantiation
  • Bill of Lading - not applicable to service exports; substituted by execution and delivery logs evidencing service provision from Canadian servers to non-resident customers (account UID, IP-geolocation metadata, device fingerprint, session timestamps)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or Apple/Google payout settlement statements evidencing receipt of subscription and in-app purchase revenue from non-resident end users and B2B counterparties
  • Non-resident status substantiation: for B2B customers, an executed declaration of non-residency and GST/HST registration status (CRA attestation substantively equivalent to GST189 or U.S. W-8BEN-E); for B2C customers, billing address, payment method country code and IP-geolocation data captured at checkout

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-clearance obligation or digital stamping mandate. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million; registrants below this threshold may file electronically through GST/HST NETFILE. Since 2024, the voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service operates over the Peppol network (OpenPeppol PINT specifications) but is not linked to tax clearance or pre-clearance validation. Invoices must display the Business Number and GST registration number; simplified invoices are permitted for supplies under CAD 150. All records, including invoices, Apple/Google payment settlement reports, server logs and IP-geolocation data, must be retained for six years and, if stored outside Canada, must be accessible electronically to CRA upon demand.

CRA conducts data-driven audits of digital-platform settlement data, cross-matching Apple/Google payout records against declared Canadian versus foreign revenue and IP-geolocation analytics; zero-rating is denied where Canadian presence indicators (IP, SIM, billing address, payment method) are not rebutted by documentary evidence. Consequences of a failed zero-rating claim: (i) reassessment for uncollected 5% GST plus compound daily interest at the CRA prescribed rate (basic rate plus 4 percentage points; approximately 9% for arrears in late 2024) from the date remittance was due; (ii) the normal reassessment window is 4 years, but is unlimited where a misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud; (iii) penalties: failure to register - the greater of CAD 250 and 1% of collectible GST; failure to file - the greater of CAD 250 and 1% of net tax; false statements or omissions made knowingly or with gross negligence - the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the deficiency; (iv) partner liability: each general partner of the LP is jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST obligations under ETA s. 272.1, while limited partners' liability is generally capped at contributed capital and undistributed returns; (v) deliberate evasion may attract criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment. Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is a documentary-control standard - unrepatriated revenue does not per se invalidate zero-rating, but prolonged non-repatriation weakens the evidentiary chain supporting the foreign-supply characterization and may invite economic-substance scrutiny.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with full convertibility for both current and capital accounts. Profit remittances to foreign partners or owners are permitted without prior approval, subject only to standard tax filing and beneficial ownership verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirement applies to export receipts. Digital service revenues, including in-app monetization from voice streaming, may be converted freely on the spot market through authorized dealers. Proceeds must be reported to Canada Revenue Agency for tax purposes within statutory timelines.

25.0% federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; LP profit distributions to partners are not dividends and are not subject to dividend withholding, but each partner's share is taxed as ordinary income in the partner's hands and, for non-resident partners, is collected through Part I or Part XIII mechanisms as applicable

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory minimum threshold for Local File or Master File; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is expected by the CRA where intercompany transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate for a taxation year, and the Country-by-Country (CbC) Report is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least CAD 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Alberta has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), the doctrine of substance over form, and treaty anti-abuse rules (including Limitation-on-Benefits clauses) to disregard artificial arrangements; the LP must demonstrate real commercial substance in Alberta, including actual staff, physical or cloud infrastructure contracts, user-facing operational decision-making, and a valid commercial purpose for intercompany platform and IP arrangements

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: On indictment, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment for up to five (5) years under ITA s.239; on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment of up to two (2) years less a day; civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under ITA s.163(2); CRA prescribed interest compounded daily on all reassessed amounts

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who direct, assent to, or participate in the filing of false returns are personally liable for civil penalties, may be prosecuted for fraud and tax evasion, and face criminal travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition from treaty partners
