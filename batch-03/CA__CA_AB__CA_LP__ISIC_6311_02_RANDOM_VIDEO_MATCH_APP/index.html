Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully and freely convertible under Canada's IMF Article VIII obligations; no exchange-control approvals apply to capital or current-account transactions. Profit remittance by an Alberta Limited Partnership to non-resident partners is subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax of 25% on the partner's Canadian-source business income share, unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable income tax treaty (e.g., Canada-US Convention). Non-resident partners may file a Canadian income tax return to claim treaty relief; there are no other legal restrictions on transferring funds abroad after Canadian tax obligations are settled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements. Exporters and service providers may retain and freely dispose of foreign-currency earnings; however, for income tax purposes, foreign-currency receipts must be translated into Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or another acceptable rate for reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange policy is administered jointly with the Minister of Finance, while AML/EFT supervision of financial institutions is shared by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low. An offshore e-money institution (EMI) holding a non-Canadian authorization (for example, a UK FCA EMI licence) is not a recognized deposit-taking institution in Canada and cannot independently operate payment accounts or hold client funds within the country. To process the target payment rails (Apple IAP and Paymentwall), the Canadian LP must route through either a regulated Canadian bank or a FINTRAC-registered money services business (MSB) that is also authorized under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada. The offshore EMI may serve only as a white-label technology or card-processing supplier, but it must not touch the LP's funds or Canadian user funds unless it itself is FINTRAC-registered and RPAA-licensed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is required for designated transactions with non-arm's length non-residents when the aggregate value of such transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the taxation year. A Master File and Country-by-Country (CbC) Report are mandatory only if the multinational group’s consolidated revenue is at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: No stand‑alone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist in Canada; however, the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, s.245 of the Income Tax Act) and the statutory “substance over form” doctrine to disregard transactions that lack genuine commercial substance. Partners and the LP must maintain a real business presence (employees, premises, decision-making) to avoid GAAR reassessments and to secure treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239(1) Income Tax Act: fine between 50% and 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Civil penalty for gross negligence: 50% of the understated tax; plus daily late-filing interest and cumulative penalties.

Director personal liability: Corporate directors of the general partner and any partner exercising management control may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, penalties, and interest; courts can pierce the veil to seek restitution. Criminal conviction may trigger travel bans and seizure of personal assets.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under s.245 GAAR denies the tax benefit and imposes interest plus a 50% penalty (exciseable at up to CAD 100,000 per Davidson‑type transaction). In an abusive transfer pricing adjustment, an additional 10% penalty on the net adjustment may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are exposed to civil liability for reassessed amounts, penalties, and interest if they authorized or allowed the sham structure. Personal travel bans and director prohibition orders may be issued in serious tax-avoidance or evasion cases.
AML / ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING (FINTRAC)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for entities and CAD 500,000 for individuals; criminal penalties up to five years’ imprisonment where there is intentional non‑compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and partners who participated in or authorised the non-compliant AML program may be personally liable for FINTRAC sanctions; they may also be subject to travel bans and director disqualification orders where enforcement authorities prosecute.
