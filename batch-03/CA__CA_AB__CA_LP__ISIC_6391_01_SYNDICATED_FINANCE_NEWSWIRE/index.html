Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) via Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA); Alberta corporate income tax (flat 8%) is administered by CRA under the federal-provincial collection agreement and reported on the T2 return; Alberta's Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) administers only sub-federal levies (e.g., Tourism Levy), which do not attach to financial newswire API feeds.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Alberta Limited Partnership under the Partnership Act through the Alberta Corporate Registry (ACRS); (2) apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1, selecting the GST/HST 'RT' program account and designating non-resident status where partners are offshore; (3) supply the Alberta registration certificate, partnership agreement and each partner's foreign TIN/entity identifier; (4) register for GST/HST once the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold ceases to apply (compulsory) or voluntarily (elective); the partnership is deemed the registrable person under ETA s. 272.1; (5) obtain the T5013 partnership information-return profile: the LP files T5013 (with allocation to each resident/non-resident partner) but pays no federal or Alberta income tax at entity level; (6) each non-resident partner carrying on business through an Alberta permanent establishment must register with CRA for its own compliance profile and pay Alberta corporate tax on its allocable share.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; the Alberta LP is per se fiscally transparent for income tax (ITA s. 96: partners include their shares; LP files only T5013) and is the deemed taxable person for GST/HST (ETA s. 272.1). Elective mechanisms available: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier (ETA s. 240(2)); (ii) partnership fiscal-period election for a non-calendar reporting period (ITA s. 249.1(4), CRA-concurred); (iii) Quick Method GST/HST remittance election (ETA s. 253), generally inadvisable for a zero-rated export-heavy feed; (iv) designation of a partner as the partnership's representative for GST/HST filings; (v) hybrid mismatch: the 2021 BEPS Action 2 rules (ITA ss. 12.5 and 18.5 et al., effective 1 July 2022) are mandatory, not elective, so the offshore syndicated newswire structure must be documented to show no deduction/non-inclusion mismatch arising from the LP's transparent character.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST 'small supplier' threshold measured on a single calendar quarter or the last four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident supplier of digital newswire content must register once it ceases to qualify as a small supplier in Canada).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% GST rate applies to supplies of financial newswire/API services made to a non-resident recipient where the service is neither rendered to an individual physically in Canada at the time of performance nor in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada (ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7). Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V with commercial export evidence. The LP must obtain the recipient's foreign VAT/TIN registration, offshore billing address and a contract clause covenanting use outside Canada; input tax credits on Canadian overhead remain fully refundable through the LP's periodic GST/HST net-filing return. Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline for services; the 180-day field below operates as the contractual remittance benchmark for Swift-wire settlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canadian Export Declaration filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (GTR)) for any physical goods leg
  • Bill of Lading or airway consignment note evidencing physical export from Canada where goods are bundled with feed hardware
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Swift-wire confirmation evidencing receipt of offshore consideration
  • Non-resident recipient certificate: foreign VAT/TIN, offshore billing address and contract clause confirming use of the service outside Canada (ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7 substantiation)
  • GST/HST zero-rating support schedule reconciled to the LP's GST/HST returns and T5013 filings

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or pre-issuance CRA authorization of invoices. A voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing interoperability standard (Peppol-based) is under development but remains optional. Mandatory GST/HST invoice content is governed by ETA s. 223 and the GST/HST Information Regulations: the LP's BN/RT program account, invoice date, vendor and recipient identification, description of supply, taxable amount and GST payable; simplified invoices are permitted only for small taxable supplies.

CRA applies strict documentary scrutiny to zero-rated export claims. Failure to substantiate the non-resident-recipient test results in reassessment of the supply at 5%, clawback of associated input tax credits, daily-compounding prescribed-rate interest, and exposure to gross-negligence penalties (up to 50% of understated net tax plus the CAD 250 minimum false-statement penalty) under the Excise Tax Act; the normal four-year reassessment window is extended without limit where misrepresentation or fraud is found, and systemic abuse may trigger criminal prosecution and disqualification from future export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada maintains no general exchange-control regime applying to current or capital account transactions; investment inflows, profit distributions, and loan repayments may be made freely. However, wire transfers and cash movements of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the financial institution. Non-resident partners receiving syndicated-newswire royalties or distributions are generally subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, subject to treaty reduction. No central-bank approval or exchange permit is required for capital subscription payments into an Alberta limited partnership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory obligation to surrender export receipts to the central bank. A Canadian LP may retain export-related revenue in foreign currency or convert it voluntarily through a licensed dealer. All settlement and transfer instructions remain subject to ordinary FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential supervision of banks through the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and financial-transaction reporting oversight through the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Canada does not recognise the EU electronic-money-institution passport. A foreign fintech or EMI may only provide payment services in Canada through a FINTRAC-registered money-services business or by partnering with a Canadian Schedule I bank. An offshore EMI account cannot serve as the primary treasury rail for a Canadian LP because it lacks local deposit-taking status and may create unlicensed payment-service exposure.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% entity-level tax for the Alberta Limited Partnership (flow-through); comparative general corporate headline rates in the jurisdiction are 8% (Alberta) + 15% (federal) = 23% combined for an Alberta-resident corporation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII rate applies to dividends paid by Canadian-resident corporations. An Alberta LP is not a corporation, so profit draws and distributions by the LP to partners are not treated as dividends and are not subject to Part XIII withholding; non-resident partners are instead taxed under Part I on their allocated Canadian-source business income at progressive rates (up to 33% federal; Alberta high-income surtax applies separately). Certain payments made by the LP to non-residents (management, administration, or technical fees) are subject to the 25% Part XIII WHT under ITA s. 212(1)(a).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The later of March 31 immediately following the calendar year in which the partnership's fiscal period ends and five months after the fiscal period end; in practice a December 31 year-end LP files by March 31.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Canada does not impose a separate Local File/Master File filing threshold; the CRA may demand contemporaneous documentation for any transaction value, and prudent practice requires maintaining a full OECD-aligned TP documentation file for any controlled cross-border transaction exceeding C$1,000,000.
