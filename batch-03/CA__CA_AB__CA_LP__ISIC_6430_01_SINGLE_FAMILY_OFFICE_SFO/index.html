Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — the single federal administrator for the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST). Alberta corporate and non-resident income tax is also administered federally by the CRA under the Alberta–Canada tax collection agreement. Official portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals/business-account.html); provincial tax administration is via Alberta Treasury Board and Finance, Tax and Revenue Administration (https://www.alberta.ca/taxes).

E-government filing portal

An Alberta Limited Partnership is a flow-through entity and does not obtain a stand-alone TIN; each partner is assessed on its allocated share. The LP, acting through its general partner, applies for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 or by using the online Business Registration Online (BRO) service at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/business-number.html; program accounts are appended to the BN as RT (GST/HST), RP (income tax), RC (payroll), and RZ (non-resident withholding). A non-resident corporate partner must first obtain its own BN or individual Tax Number (ITN) from CRA, supported by certified foreign registration documents and treaty-status declarations (NR301/NR302 where relevant); non-resident individuals use a SIN or ITN. The LP must simultaneously hold an Alberta corporate-registry registration (corporation number) under Alberta's Partnership Act, which must be declared during BN activation. The LP then files T5013 Summary and T5013 Supplementary returns annually, and the general partner files NR4 information returns for non-resident partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box election exists; pass-through treatment is the statutory default for an Alberta LP. Relevant elections and hybrid-mismatch regimes include: (i) ITA s. 102 designates a partnership as a 'Canadian partnership' only if all members are Canadian-resident persons; (ii) ITA s. 96(3) deems a partnership with a corporate or non-resident specified member to be a separate person for foreign accrual property income (FAPI) attribution, a hybrid-mismatch countermeasure; (iii) ITA s. 97(2) allows a tax-deferred rollover election for property contributed by partners to the LP; (iv) ITA s. 216 permits a non-resident partner to elect net-basis Part I tax on Canadian-source real or immovable property income instead of gross-basis Part XIII withholding; (v) Excise Tax Act s. 273 enables a joint election eliminating GST/HST on supplies between the LP and a closely related corporation; (vi) ITA ss. 18.4–18.6 and ETA s. 274 comprise the hybrid-mismatch and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) provisions that disallow structured benefits. For U.S. federal tax purposes, the Alberta LP is an eligible entity that defaults to partnership classification under Treasury Regulation § 301.7701-3(b)(2)(i)(B) unless an affirmative Form 8832 election is filed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Canada maintains no exchange-control legislation; the former Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and no successor statute governs outward or inward repatriation. Non-resident partners of the Alberta LP may freely remit capital, dividends, interest, and liquidation proceeds out of Canada, subject only to: (a) Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) non-resident withholding tax at conventional 25%, reduced under applicable tax treaties (e.g., 15% portfolio / 5% qualifying-parent dividend rate under Article X of the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention), and (b) mandatory reporting where a resident partner holds specified foreign property above CAD 100,000 (CRA Form T1135). Profit-repatriation hurdles include the bank's regulatory obligation to screen for sanctions, PEPs, and suspicious transaction reporting; no administrative approval is required from the Bank of Canada or any other federal body. The Alberta LP's foreign capital contributions are non-resident direct investment and may generally be structured as equity or shareholder loans without pre-clearance, provided the bank records the commercial purpose and classification code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory settlement timeline; exporters and investment entities may hold foreign currency indefinitely. For the ISIC 6430 family office, non-operating capital inflows are not 'export proceeds'; however, the following statutory controls apply: (a) cross-border physical currency or monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to the CBSA on Form E677; (b) bank-to-bank wire transfers require no FINTRAC pre-approval, but the receiving institution must complete a Large Cash Transaction Record (LCTR) only where physical currency is involved and must retain wire-transfer records for five years under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); (c) any conversion of USD/CAD in excess of CAD 2 million per transaction is reported to the Bank of Canada's semi-annual FX turnover survey under the FX and Derivatives Market Survey, not as a surrender duty.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary and payment-systems authority; prudential banking supervision is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing supervision is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); provincial securities oversight for the family office's investment-advice activities falls under the Alberta Securities Commission (ASC). No standalone foreign-exchange administration exists in Canada; the FX market is a free-floating, fully deregulated regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rated Low-Medium as primary treasury rail and Medium as an auxiliary collection/disbursement portal. Canadian-fintech and foreign-fintech electronic-money institutions (e.g., Wise Platform, Brex, Mercury, N26's Canadian white-label) may open CAD and USD accounts for an Alberta LP, but they are not 'banks' for OSFI purposes; they lack CDIC deposit insurance, cannot clear directly through Payments Canada's Lynx/ACSS systems without a sponsoring Schedule I bank, and their custodial structures are not eligible for the Securities_Custody rail. Legal constraints include registration under the federal Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada; final registration compliance was due September 2025, and an SFO using an unregistered foreign EMI to pool family funds breaches the RPAA's payment-function requirements. For a Canada-based Single Family Office, a fully regulated Schedule I bank with a trust-company affiliate (e.g., RBC Trust or TD Trust) is the only viable custody and treasury counterparty; foreign EMIs are permissible only for low-balance float, never for capital escrow.

(7) physically sign and upload an inward-remittance declaration identical to FINTRAC's wire-transfer record with the source funding account statements; (8) if the funding exceeds USD 1,000,000, the bank will request a one-day pre-notification and will perform enhanced due diligence, including refreshing UBO screenings; (9) once credited, the capital must be booked to the Capital Escrow sub-account, not the operational account, to evidence 'subscribed capital' for audit and CRS-mapping.Clearing rails for outbound disbursements: (a) high-value CAD payments above CAD 25,000 settle over Lynx, the Bank of Canada's real-time gross settlement system operated by Payments Canada; (b) all other CAD disbursements use the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) with a 1-3 day settlement cycle and require a dual-authority e-signature mandate from the GP and one 25%+ limited partner; (c) cross-border trade settlements are instructed via SWIFT MT103 for client credit or MT202 COV for bank-to-bank cover, each carrying the full remitter beneficiary line and an invoice or contract reference; (d) securities purchases settle on a Delivery Versus Payment (DVP) basis through the Canadian Depository for Securities (CDS) using SWIFT MT543 (delivery) / MT540-42 (payment) instructions, or via Euroclear/Clearstream if the family office acquires European instruments; (e) commercial letters of credit are permissible only for the SFO's adjunct operating entities; for the pure treasury mandate, a standby LC is acceptable as collateral support for a leasing or trade position; (f) every outgoing transfer over CAD 10,000 is recorded with the underlying economic rationale and kept in the bank's 5-year PCMLTFA record; (g) Canada does not use IBAN; outbound USD disbursements clear through the bank's US correspondent (e.g., BMO Harris, RBC Bank (Georgia)) and the instruction must include the US ABA/transit routing in field 57A.Deliverable CAD/USD forward contracts with tenors to 24 months executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Credit Support Annex (CSA) collateralised by cash or Canadian government securities; standard documentation under the Canadian Foreign Exchange Committee's FX Settlement Risk Reduction Protocol.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) referenced to CAD are generally unnecessary because the CAD is fully convertible, but an NDF with USD settlement is permitted where the family office wishes to avoid a Canadian legal-delivery obligation; such NDFs are executed off-shore with a Schedule I bank's London/New York desk and settle net in USD.Cross-currency swaps (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) under ISDA/CSA for long-term balance-sheet hedging of the family office's fixed-income and real-estate investments; permissible without OSFI hedge-designation approval provided the swap is documented as a true derivatives transaction and margin is posted as cash or government bonds.Exchange-traded CAD/USD futures and options on the CME Globex or the Montreal Exchange (MX), executed through the bank's registered dealer affiliate; position limits and daily mark-to-market margin are prescribed by the exchange rather than by OSFI.Vanilla FX options, zero-cost collars, and participating forwards for hedging near-term custodial disbursements; available from the bank's FX Options desk with a minimum notional of CAD 500,000 and a documented portfolio-level hedging intent.Dynamic spot/fwd laddering combined with a standing FX conversion mandate to manage monthly USD inflows from foreign family assets; each conversion is recorded under the bank's standard FX dealing terms with no speculative trading permitted absent a separately approved mandate from the Alberta Securities Commission-registered investment manager.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rated Low-Medium as primary treasury rail and Medium as an auxiliary collection/disbursement portal. Canadian-fintech and foreign-fintech electronic-money institutions (e.g., Wise Platform, Brex, Mercury, N26's Canadian white-label) may open CAD and USD accounts for an Alberta LP, but they are not 'banks' for OSFI purposes; they lack CDIC deposit insurance, cannot clear directly through Payments Canada's Lynx/ACSS systems without a sponsoring Schedule I bank, and their custodial structures are not eligible for the Securities_Custody rail. Legal constraints include registration under the federal Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada; final registration compliance was due September 2025, and an SFO using an unregistered foreign EMI to pool family funds breaches the RPAA's payment-function requirements. For a Canada-based Single Family Office, a fully regulated Schedule I bank with a trust-company affiliate (e.g., RBC Trust or TD Trust) is the only viable custody and treasury counterparty; foreign EMIs are permissible only for low-balance float, never for capital escrow.
