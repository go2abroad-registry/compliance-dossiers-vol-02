Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online service and My Business Account portal. Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration administers applicable Alberta provincial tax programs, but Alberta has no general provincial sales tax.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta limited partnership should obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) by submitting the required business registration information through Business Registration Online, by telephone, or by filing Form RC1 where applicable. The partnership must provide its legal name, Alberta formation details, registered address, responsible individuals, activity description, and partner information. The BN is used to establish applicable program accounts, including GST/HST, payroll, import-export, and information-return accounts. A partnership required to file a T5013 Partnership Information Return must obtain and use a partnership tax account. A non-resident partner may separately require a CRA non-resident tax account and must report Canadian-source income or partnership allocations under applicable Canadian rules. CRA representative authorization is generally required for advisers to access or administer the accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is generally classified under Canadian law according to its legal form and is not ordinarily subject to entity-level income tax on allocated partnership income. Income, losses, and specified items are generally allocated to partners under the partnership agreement and the Income Tax Act. Canada does not provide a general U.S.-style check-the-box election for Canadian partnerships. Potential statutory mechanisms include the section 97(2) tax-deferred transfer election for qualifying transfers of property to a Canadian partnership, section 85 elections where applicable to transfers to a taxable Canadian corporation rather than to the partnership itself, and elections or designations applicable to specific income, loss, capital-cost, or foreign-affiliate rules. Canadian hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences where an arrangement produces deduction or inclusion mismatches involving foreign jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies, measured under the small-supplier rules, generally triggers mandatory GST registration when the threshold is exceeded in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. Special rules apply to charities, public institutions, non-residents, associated persons, specified listed financial institutions, and supplies made through digital-economy platforms. A business may register voluntarily before the mandatory threshold is reached.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are generally zero-rated where the statutory GST/HST export conditions are satisfied, including exportation from Canada and the required delivery, possession, or shipping evidence. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated only where a specific GST/HST zero-rating provision applies; merely billing a non-resident or receiving payment offshore is insufficient. Services relating to real property, tangible personal property, or activities performed in Canada may remain taxable. Financial services are generally exempt rather than zero-rated, and administration of employee share plans or trusts requires transaction-specific analysis because the treatment depends on the precise service and contractual structure. A registrant claiming input tax credits or GST rebates must retain evidence linking the costs to commercial activities and zero-rated supplies.

  • Canadian export declaration or applicable Canadian Border Services Agency export-reporting record, where required
  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, recipient, goods, value, and export destination
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or other carrier evidence of export
  • Proof of delivery or importation in the destination jurisdiction, where relevant
  • Purchase order, contract, and customer correspondence supporting the export transaction
  • GST/HST invoice and accounting records supporting the zero-rated treatment
  • Foreign-exchange or banking records evidencing payment, where relevant; Canada does not generally require an inward remittance certificate

Canada has no general real-time GST invoice-clearance mandate, no universal government e-invoicing platform, and no general digital tax-stamping requirement. Electronic invoices and electronic books are generally acceptable if they preserve reliability, readability, integrity, and audit accessibility. GST invoices must contain prescribed information, including supplier identity and GST registration number where applicable, transaction date, description, consideration, tax rate, and tax amount or the information required for input tax credit support. Registered persons generally file GST/HST returns electronically through CRA systems where electronic filing is required or selected.

Canada has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. CRA may reassess GST/HST, deny input tax credits or rebates, and charge arrears interest where zero-rating is unsupported or records are inadequate. A false statement or omission attributable to gross negligence may attract a penalty generally equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the understated tax or overstated credit. Failure-to-file, failure-to-remit, repeated non-compliance, and prescribed-record violations may generate additional penalties. Fraudulent claims may result in civil penalties, reassessment for prior periods, repayment of improperly obtained refunds or credits, interest, prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. Customs misdeclarations may trigger separate administrative monetary penalties, seizure, forfeiture, or prosecution under the Customs Act and related legislation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility exists on both current and capital accounts. The CA_LP may freely receive employee share-plan contributions, sponsor capital, and trust settlement proceeds in CAD or foreign currency and may remit distributions without exchange-control authorization or conversion mandates. The only frictions are fiscal: non-resident partners bear Part I income tax allocations passed through T5013 returns, treaty-reduced where applicable (notably the Canada-US treaty and CUSMA), and the ESOP trust must satisfy Income Tax Act section 149.01 conditions, including annual T3 trust return filing, to preserve the employee-ownership trust tax deferral.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules exist. Canada does not impose statutory export-proceeds surrender timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation windows; the CA_LP may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in designated foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks without balance limitations, including USD positions held for ESOP share settlement and payroll funding.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and operator of the Lynx real-time gross settlement system for high-value CAD payments; prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions rests with OSFI under the Bank Act, AML/ATF supervision with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and economic sanctions administration with Global Affairs Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act and United Nations Act. Canada observes IMF Article VIII obligations, so no central-bank or exchange-control approval is required for cross-border commercial transfers, capital injections, or profit remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low feasibility. FINTRAC-registered EMIs (Wise Canada, Airwallex Canada) and offshore digital EMIs are acceptable only as auxiliary rails for small FX conversions and incidental cross-border payroll disbursements, because trust and escrow assets of an ESOP administrator must be held with an OSFI-regulated Schedule I bank or CDIC-insured deposit-taker to preserve trust-law segregation and investor protection. An offshore EMI without Canadian MSB registration cannot lawfully transmit money to Canadian beneficiaries under PCMLTFA section 11.1 and therefore cannot serve as the primary clearing rail or custody depository.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Sponsor capital and employee payroll contributions arrive by SWIFT MT103 using the 9-digit Canadian Clearing Code (0 plus 3-digit institution number plus 5-digit transit) into segregated CAD sub-accounts settled same-day on Lynx RTGS; USD tranches clear through the bank's US subsidiary or Fedwire/CHIPS correspondent and are converted at the bank's disclosed mid-rate quote. All cross-border EFTs at or above CAD 10,000 are reported to FINTRAC on an Electronic Funds Transfer Report, so MT103 field 70 must carry a structural purpose code such as ESOP-CONTRIBUTION / PAYROLL-WIRE / CA_LP-6430 and each remitter must be a named sponsor to avoid aggregation, misclassification, and EDD escalation.Disbursements settle via (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct creditor and beneficiary payments; (ii) commercial letters of credit and standby LCs issued under UCP 600/eUCP for cross-border acquisition of plan shares, negotiated at the issuing Calgary or Toronto branch; (iii) Fedwire/CHIPS through the bank's US clearing corridor for USD-denominated settlements; and (iv) Lynx RTGS for domestic CAD wires and payroll transfers. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the partnership level. An Alberta Limited Partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Partnership Act (Alberta); the LP itself pays no federal or Alberta corporate income tax and files information returns only. Each partner is taxed on the allocated share of income. Reference combined headline corporate rate for an Alberta-resident corporate partner: 23% (15% net federal general corporate rate after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% General Rate Reduction, plus 8% Alberta general corporate rate). Individual partners are taxed at progressive federal and Alberta marginal rates.
