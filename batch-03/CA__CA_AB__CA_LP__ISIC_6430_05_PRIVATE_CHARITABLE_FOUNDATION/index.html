Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); registration via Business Registration Online (BRA) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or domestic Alberta limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online. If GST/HST registration is required, the partnership is registered as a separate person for indirect tax; partners report income individually. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; an Alberta LP is fiscally transparent for income tax: no federal or Alberta income tax at the partnership level; each partner is taxable on allocated income under Income Tax Act s. 96 and Alberta ITA. Non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source income, subject to Part XIII withholding unless a full Part I tax return is filed.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or general corporate classification election exists under Canadian tax law. A private charitable foundation engaged in endowment management will typically be organized as a corporation or trust, not an LP; if an LP is used for an underlying investment portfolio, available elections include (i) optional s. 216 non-resident partner return for rental or timber income, (ii) ETA s. 156 election for nil consideration between closely related registrants, and (iii) designation as a specified investment flow-through is not elective. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Canada has no elective de-hybridization; rules under ITA ss. 18.4 to 18.6 apply non-electively.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 for ordinary commercial activity; CAD 50,000 for charities and public service bodies, measured by worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and reasonable expectation in the current quarter

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0% GST/HST) applies to qualifying exports of goods that are supplied to a non-resident and exported from Canada within a specified time, and to certain services performed for non-residents where the benefit is received outside Canada, such as investment advisory, endowment administration, or grant-management services for a charitable foundation. Zero rating is conditional on documentary evidence proving export or offshore service performance; failure to retain evidence makes the supply taxable at 5%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A or Electronic Export Report)
  • Bill of Lading / Waybill / Air Waybill
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract
  • Proof of payment / Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Carrier's certificate or shipping/transport records evidencing physical export
  • GST/HST schedule or full zero-rating substantiation file

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. There is no CRA pre-clearance API for invoices. Invoices must contain prescribed GST/HST information and be made available; electronic invoices (PDF, EDI, XML) are accepted provided they are legible, complete, and preserved in a CRA-acceptable electronic format for audit.

CRA may deny zero rating retroactively and reassess GST/HST, interest and penalties where export documentation is deficient. Gross negligence or concealment penalties under the Excise Tax Act may reach 25% of the tax understated; willful evasion can lead to criminal prosecution, fines and imprisonment. CRA has a four-year normal reassessment period, which is extended indefinitely for misrepresentation or fraudulent omissions, and retrospective clawback of net tax refunds is available where zero-rated claims are unsupported.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally require prior approval for foreign investment, capital contributions, loan repayments, dividends, partnership distributions, or conversion between Canadian dollars and foreign currencies. Banks must nevertheless apply sanctions screening, beneficial-ownership verification, source-of-funds controls, tax-reporting procedures, and transaction-monitoring requirements. Non-resident withholding tax, treaty entitlement, transfer-pricing rules, thin-capitalization rules, partnership allocation rules, and charity-specific expenditure-responsibility rules may affect remittances. An Alberta limited partnership is generally not itself the standard legal form for registration as a Canadian registered charity; charitable status should normally be implemented through an eligible corporation, trust, or other qualifying structure and confirmed with the Canada Revenue Agency before representing the LP as a charitable foundation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general Canadian statutory deadline requires export receipts to be repatriated, deposited with a Canadian bank, or converted into Canadian dollars. No mandatory conversion ratio applies. Exporters must comply with applicable customs, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, tax, accounting, and contractual reporting requirements. Foreign-currency receipts should be supported by invoices, contracts, shipping or service evidence, and bank records. Where charitable funds are received or distributed cross-border, the entity must preserve records demonstrating the charitable purpose, recipient due diligence, grant authorization, and compliance with Canadian charity rules, sanctions, and anti-terrorist-financing requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing exchange controls or mandatory export-proceeds surrender. FINTRAC administers federal anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist-financing obligations applicable to reporting entities, including financial institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment operations and Low to Medium as a substitute for a Canadian operating bank account. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment service provider may be used only where its licensing, Canadian client-onboarding model, payment-rail access, safeguarding arrangements, sanctions controls, and service terms support the proposed activity. It does not replace an Alberta or Canadian bank account for entity verification, restricted charitable funds, capital documentation, tax payments, or regulated grant administration. A foreign payment service provider operating in Canada may have obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, including registration with FINTRAC, compliance-program requirements, recordkeeping, reporting, and identity verification. Canadian residents may face additional limits under the provider's licensing perimeter and correspondent-bank policies.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Alberta limited partnership entity level because an LP is fiscally transparent. For taxable partners the combined federal/Alberta general corporate rate is 23% (federal 15% + Alberta 8%); the Canadian-controlled private corporation active business rate is 11% on the first CAD 500,000 (federal 9% + Alberta 2%). A registered charity/private foundation is not itself an LP; it must be an incorporated company or trust and is exempt under ITA s. 149(1)(l).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII for dividends paid by a Canadian corporation. An Alberta LP distribution is not a dividend; income allocations are taxed to the partner under Part I or exempt depending on the partner's status. Payments made by the LP that are Canadian-source fees, royalties, rent or interest to a non-resident partner are generally subject to 25% Part XIII withholding unless reduced by treaty or relieved under domestic provisions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: aggregate fair market value of reportable transactions with a non-resident related/controlled partner triggers mandatory Form T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. A Country-by-Country Report is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least CAD 750,000,000. CRA expects OECD-aligned Local File and Master File documentation on a risk-based basis.

Economic substance scope: Alberta and Canada do not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, the common law sham/agency doctrines and ITA s. 245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule require the LP to have genuine entrepreneurial substance. For a charitable endowment vehicle, CRA examines whether the LP has an actual office, employees, local governance, independent investment decision-making, and whether grant-making is made exclusively to qualified donees. A bare nominee LP with no Canadian management or no genuine charitable operation is fatal and may be disregarded for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: IT A s. 239(1): summary conviction fines of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment imprisonment up to 5 years; reassessed tax plus arrears interest remains payable.

Director personal liability: Directors/shareholders who directed, authorized or assented to the evasion are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under ITA s. 227.1. A limited partner who participates in management control forfeits its limited-liability shield and becomes personally liable.
Shell Company / Conduit lacking Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to cancel the tax benefit, impose a 50% GAAR penalty under ITA s. 245(5), reassess at ordinary rates, and add compound interest. Sham arrangements may be treated as non-existent, causing all income to be attributed to the actual owner.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner can be personally liable for unremitted taxes and withholding. Limited partners exercising control lose LP limited liability and may face personal liability for partnership obligations.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 per contravention; Criminal Code money laundering convictions carry imprisonment up to 10 years; statutory penalties may be applied for every day the failure continues.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors may be personally fined where they were in a position to prevent the contravention and failed to do so. They may also be prosecuted for knowingly directing laundering transactions or obstructing a FINTRAC examination.
Charitable Foundation / Qualified Donee Misuse

Statutory sanction: CRA will deny charitable registration and impose Part I taxation on all receipts. If a separate registered charity is involved, revocation under ITA s. 188 applies, with revocation tax on retained property, penalties on false receipts, and prohibition on re-registration.

Director personal liability: Directors or trustees are jointly and severally liable for unauthorized charitable disbursements, unpaid revocation tax, and restitution for breach of fiduciary duty; court-ordered removal and personal monetary orders are common.
Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalization Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA adjusts income on an arm's length basis under ITA s. 247(2), imposes a 10% penalty on the net transfer-pricing adjustment under s. 247(3), denies interest deductions under s. 18(4) where the debt-to-equity ratio exceeds 1.5:1, and charges statutory interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for TP adjustments alone, but if the adjustment creates unpaid Part XIII withholding on a deemed dividend or non-resident distribution, they may be personally liable under ITA s. 227.1 for the unremitted amount.
Failure to Withhold/Report Non-Resident Payments

Statutory sanction: The payer/withholding agent is liable for the full unremitted amount, interest, and penalties. The non-resident remains liable for any shortfall. Gross negligence or wilful blindness can attract criminal penalties.

Director personal liability: Under ITA s. 227.1, a director of a corporate general partner that knowingly or negligently failed to withhold is personally liable for the entire unrecovered amount unless the director exercised due diligence.
