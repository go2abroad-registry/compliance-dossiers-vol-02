Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — administered via Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account portal, and GST/HST NETFILE; provincial filing with Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) for the Alberta Limited Partnership registration.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Alberta LPs must first register the partnership with Service Alberta to obtain a corporate access code, then apply to CRA through Business Registration Online (paper alternative: Form RC1) to secure a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with requisite program accounts, including GST/HST, import/export, payroll (if applicable), and income tax information returns for partnership filing. Non-resident general partners must disclose their foreign tax identification numbers; the LP may voluntarily register for GST/HST even below the CAD 30,000 threshold to recover input tax credits, but registration is mandatory once carrying on business in Canada and exceeding the threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — an Alberta LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (Canada): partnership income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to, and taxed in the hands of, the general and limited partners, including non-resident partners. Territorial_Scope applies: resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source partnership allocations and Canadian lump-sum/withholding items. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is a separate 'person' capable of being a registrant, and financial services supplied to Canadian residents are generally exempt supplies. — Foreign-owned Alberta LPs must first register the partnership with Service Alberta to obtain a corporate access code, then apply to CRA through Business Registration Online (paper alternative: Form RC1) to secure a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with requisite program accounts, including GST/HST, import/export, payroll (if applicable), and income tax information returns for partnership filing. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Alberta LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (Canada): partnership income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to, and taxed in the hands of, the general and limited partners, including non-resident partners. Territorial_Scope applies: resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source partnership allocations and Canadian lump-sum/withholding items. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is a separate 'person' capable of being a registrant, and financial services supplied to Canadian residents are generally exempt supplies.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate an IRC § 7701 check-the-box regime; entity classification is statutory. Available elections include: (i) Excise Tax Act (ETA) section 156 election for nil consideration between closely related registrants; (ii) GST/HST Quick Method accounting election for small suppliers whose annual taxable supplies do not exceed the prescribed limit (generally CAD 400,000); (iii) ETA section 211 election to treat an entity as a selected financial institution for financial-services GST/HST rules; and (iv) income tax elections concerning hybrid mismatch conformity (Income Tax Act sections 18.4 and 18.6), which constrain deductibility of payments giving rise to no inclusion or double deduction outcomes for non-Canadian partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident partnership carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST once this threshold is met and must generally obtain a BN before making taxable supplies in Canada; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI sections 6 and 10 where a registrant supplies tangible personal property and exports it as part of the supply, or transfers possession to a common carrier for direct export. Services: zero-rated under Schedule VI section 7 where a registrant supplies a service to a non-resident recipient and the service is not caught by the exclusionary rules (e.g., not services relating to Canadian real property, not delivered to an individual present in Canada, and not a supply made to a Canadian-resident registrant). For a Venture Capital GP, management, administrative, and investment-advisory services supplied to a non-resident fund or to non-resident limited partners can qualify for zero-rating if the services are performed outside Canada or are supplied to a non-resident and do not constitute exempt 'financial services' as defined in ETA section 123(1), noting that management or administrative services ordinarily provided by a financial institution are carved out of the financial-services definition.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Declaration (CAED / G7) with export permit references where applicable
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and executed service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and the nature of the zero-rated supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 or debit-advice record documenting repatriation of export proceeds to Canada

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-clearance API regime for B2B invoices. CRA requires all electronic records to be retained in an accessible, legible, and durable format; e-invoicing is voluntary and increasingly aligned with the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard for business-to-government and business-to-business exchange. A registrant issuing GST/HST invoices must state the supplier's Business Number, invoice date, description, tax amounts, and, in a partnership context, clearly identify the legal partnership entity and its registrant status.

CRA may reassess and claw back GST/HST refunds under ETA section 285 within the normal four-year limitation period (unlimited in cases of misrepresentation or fraud), impose penalties for false statements under ETA sections 281.1 and 281.5 (generally between CAD 250 and 25% of the tax or refund at issue), apply gross-negligence and interest charges on reassessed amounts, and pursue criminal prosecution under ETA section 327 for tax evasion. Income Tax Act section 245 (GAAR) and reporting penalties also apply to aggressive export zero-rating or artificial loss/refund structures involving non-arm's-length foreign partners. Retrospective clawbacks and interest run from the original filing date, and penalties escalate for repeat or organized fraudulent export schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account bases. Inbound capital subscriptions, outbound investments, distributions, and profit remittances by a Canadian limited partnership are unrestricted. No Bank of Canada or Department of Finance permit is required. The only material remittance hurdle is fiscal: distributions or guaranteed payments to non-resident limited partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, often treaty-reduced to 5–15%), which is a tax obligation and not an FX-control measure. Payments are freely routed through CAD or USD accounts maintained in Canada or abroad.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds or inbound investment receipts. Exporters and fund managers may retain foreign currency offshore or in domestic foreign-currency accounts. Administrative reporting only: any person carrying C$10,000 or more (or foreign equivalent) across the Canadian border must report to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), and financial entities must report cross-border electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. These thresholds do not apply to the underlying capital-call receipts when held through a Schedule I bank's account.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the sole currency-issuing and monetary policy authority, with payment system oversight (Lynx, ACSS). Exchange control is not administered by a dedicated exchange authority; prudential regulation is allocated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement, and no ex-ante exchange-control approval is required for cross-border current or capital transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury rail; acceptable as a secondary and disbursement-only rail. 'EMI' licences obtained in the UK, EU, or Singapore confer no passporting rights in Canada. Any non-bank fintech must register federally as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC, appoint a Canadian AML compliance officer, and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act supervision by the Bank of Canada. Alberta imposes no separate provincial money-services licence. FINTRAC-registered payment platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) may be used for low-value capital-call remittances and operating reimbursements, but they cannot replicate the settlement finality, escrow integrity, and same-day CLS settlement of a Schedule I bank.

SWIFT field 70 must contain 'Capital Call No. <ref>, Fund ID, Investor ID'; field 50K must match the executed subscription agreement; field 59 names the Canadian LP. CAD-denominated subscriptions are advised to settle through Lynx (the Bank of Canada's large-value system) with a zero-balance operating linkage. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for the Alberta Limited Partnership itself (fiscally transparent flow-through entity). The corporate general partner is taxed at 23% combined federal (15%) and Alberta (8%) general corporate rate; 11% combined rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (federal 9% + Alberta 2%).
