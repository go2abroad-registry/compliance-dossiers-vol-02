A virtual office is generally permissible for an Alberta limited partnership provided that a physical address in Alberta is maintained where the Registrar and the public can effect service of process during normal business hours. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Excise and GST/HST Directorate; online portal 'My Business Account' at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership that makes taxable supplies (rental or sale of real property) in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (RT). Application is made to the CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, selecting 'Partnership' as the entity type and the real-estate leasing or sales NAICS code. The applicant must provide the Alberta partnership registration certificate, the partnership agreement, the general partner's legal identifiers, the foreign jurisdiction of registration, and, where required, a Canadian contact or authorized representative. CRA issues the BN; the partnership must register for GST/HST on the RT account immediately before the effective registration date or in any case where the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act for Canadian income tax purposes: the limited partnership itself does not pay federal or provincial income tax; each partner is taxable on its allocated share of income. Non-resident partners earning rental income from Alberta real property are subject to Part XIII withholding on gross rental payments in the absence of a Canadian net-income return election. For GST/HST, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and is required to register for the federal GST at the 5% rate in Alberta, which has no provincial sales tax or HST component.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election under domestic tax law. Relevant statutory elections for this entity type include: (i) the Income Tax Act s.216(1) election, which permits a non-resident recipient of Canadian rental income to file a Canadian income tax return on a net basis rather than suffer gross-basis Part XIII withholding; and (ii) the Excise Tax Act s.156 election for closely related registrants to treat certain taxable supplies as having been made for nil consideration, subject to the ownership and control tests. For cross-border partners, a US federal Form 8832 election may be available solely for US tax classification; it does not affect Canadian tax characterization. Hybrid mismatch treatment is governed by anti-hybrid measures and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, not by elective classification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully and freely convertible on both current and capital account. There are no statutory foreign exchange controls on inbound or outbound transfers of funds for an Alberta limited partnership operating as a real estate investment trust, including for profit repatriation, capital distributions, interest, rent, or disposition proceeds. Non-resident investors may freely convert CAD income into other currencies and remit abroad without central bank approval, subject only to income tax withholding and corporate reporting obligations. The Investment Canada Act may require notification or review for foreign direct investment, but this is an investment-review mechanism, not a currency-control mechanism. Canada's commitments under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement are fully satisfied; there is no separate current account restriction on payment transfers for international transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines requiring export proceeds to be surrendered to a central bank or converted into local currency, and no mandatory conversion ratios are imposed for export receipts in Canada. Any CAD or foreign-currency export proceeds may be retained, controlled, and disposed of freely by the exporting entity. For an Alberta REIT with negligible export activity, the applicable compliance burden is limited to FINTRAC cross-border wire reporting thresholds and the Income Tax Act's functional-currency reporting rules. If the entity elects to use a functional currency other than CAD for Canadian tax purposes, all transactions must still be reported in that selected currency under the Income Tax Regulations; accumulated proceeds remain freely convertible without surrender or timing constraints.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority and federal lender of last resort; comprehensive exchange control administration rests with no single central authority. This is because Canada maintains a fully liberalized capital account with no general exchange control law. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates chartered banks, trust companies, and other deposit-taking institutions conducting foreign exchange business, while FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) supervises cross-border wire reporting and money service business AML compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) that are not Canadian-regulated authorized foreign banks, deposit-taking entities, or registered money services businesses under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) exhibit Low-Moderate viability for serving as primary clearing rails for an Alberta REIT. Overseas-licensed EMIs may lawfully process ancillary vendor payments and FX conversions, but they cannot hold client trust funds in the manner of a Canadian Schedule I bank without becoming subject to OSFI insolvency and deposit insurance standards. A Canadian-registered MSB or FINTRAC-registered payment-service provider with an Ontario or Alberta money services licence, or a federal payment-service provider transitioning under the Retail Payment Activities Act, provides moderate viability for low-value ACH/direct debit streams. The REIT's core capital, distributions, and mortgage settlements must remain on regulated bank rails to satisfy Alberta Partnerships Act and unitholder protection expectations. Use of restricted-license EMIs for high-value cross-border settlement is prohibited under prudent treasury policy and not legally recommended.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from foreign limited partners must be routed over final settlement systems via SWIFT MT103 or Canadian global wire standards. The receiving bank will require the originating bank to transmit a specific purpose code; while Canada retains no central exchange-control code, the bank's compliance narrative typically demands 'REIT capital subscription' (or in OSC terminology 'Equity Injection for Canadian Real Property') and an end-to-end confirmation that the transfer is non-repatriable until full AML validation. Accepted financial reference narratives include 'SC' (Subscription for Trust Units), 'L' (Loans), and 'E90' (Other Direct Investment) for reporting under the Statistics Canada International Transactions Reporting System. Funds must be received in the capital escrow account, never commingled with operational distributions. If the inbound wire exceeds 10,000 CAD, the bank automatically files an Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC within a legally mandated time frame. Beneficial ownership breakdowns and source-of-funds documents should accompany the wire narrative to prevent a 72-hour preservation hold.For outbound disbursements from the Alberta REIT—including construction payments, property acquisition settlements, lender repayments, and unitholder distribution wires—the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for high-value international payments, Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary LCs) for cross-border construction and trade counterparties, and Canadian ACH (direct deposit via the Real-Time Rail or Payments Canada's Standard Settlement) for domestic and cross-border recurring low-value obligations. All outbound wires must be signed by two authorized account signatories and include an International Electronic Funds Transfer Report if above threshold. Cross-border disbursements to foreign property vendors should use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit, especially for development transactions; lease revenue disbursements may be aggregated into a monthly CAD payment pool. The REIT's account must contain a dedicated liquidity sub-ledger ensuring no overdraft of the operational account when facing unexpected FX settlement delays.Commercial banks in Canada are authorized to provide OTC plain-vanilla foreign exchange forward contracts for CAD/USD and all major convertible currencies to hedge the REIT's income and distribution exposure. No bank-product eligibility filing is required for a purchase via an OSFI-regulated institution; the maturity normally ranges from overnight to 24 months.FX options (call or put) on major currency pairs are permissible for hedging anticipated distributions and debt-service payments; options must be entered into with a licensed dealer and accounted for in AUD/IFRS hedge documentation.Cross-currency swap agreements are permissible between an Alberta REIT and a Schedule I bank where the REIT issues fixed-rate CAD debt and converts proceeds to USD for a foreign property acquisition, provided the transaction is documented under an ISDA Master Agreement and marked-to-market daily.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only when the underlying exposure is an emerging-market currency with non-convertible offshore status; CAD is freely convertible, so NDFs for CAD/USD are not a necessary or customary instrument for this treasury blueprint. The array is therefore deliberately minimal but compliant with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions' business-conduct standards.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Canadian LP entity level (pass-through); combined federal + Alberta corporate rate for taxable corporate partners is 23% (federal 15% + Alberta 8%); top combined federal + Alberta marginal rate for resident individuals is approximately 48%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Partnership allocations to non-residents are not dividends: the LP itself is pass-through. Under subsection 194(1), the partnership must withhold 25% Part XIII tax on a non-resident partner's Canadian-source income allocation, subject to security or waiver. For a corporate partner resident in the US, treaty rate on business income attribution is 0% if no permanent establishment; otherwise Part I tax applies with credits.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T5013 Return: 5 months after partnership fiscal year-end if all partners are Canadian residents; 15 months after fiscal year-end if any non-resident partner exists. T5013 slips generally due to partners by March 31 following a calendar-year fiscal year-end, or within 5 months of a non-calendar year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold exists under s.247; contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is mandatory for every non-arm's length cross-border transaction entered into by the Canadian partnership. For CRA audit selection, transactions exceeding C$1,000,000 are commonly scrutinized; large partnerships with aggregate international related-party transactions exceeding C$5,000,000 should also prepare a Master File.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. However, under GAAR (s.245), an avoidance transaction that lacks economic consequences or business purpose will be reassessed. Treaty relief is only available if the non-resident entity has actual substance and the transaction satisfies the principal purpose test under the OECD Multilateral Instrument.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty under s.163.2: the greater of C$100,000 and 50% of the understated tax; late-filing penalties up to C$10,000 per return; criminal prosecution under s.327 ITA and Criminal Code fraud: fines up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding taxes under s.227.1 ITA. CRA may freeze personal bank accounts, issue travel bans under writ of seizure, and obtain civil restitution from directors who authorized or participated in evasion.
Shell Company / Sham Trust

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to deny the tax benefit, reassess the income to the general partner, and apply a 50% gross-negligence penalty; the partnership may be dissolved as a sham under Alberta Partnership Act, triggering immediate capital gains recapture.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner face personal liability for the denied tax benefit plus interest; limited partners may have liability if the 'limited' status is invalidated due to participation in control, causing the corporate veil to be pierced.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to C$100,000 for reporting non-compliance; for money laundering, Criminal Code penalties include 10 years imprisonment; assets obtained through offence are subject to forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly fail to implement AML policies are personally liable and may be disqualified from serving as directors; personal travel bans and criminal convictions may be imposed for laundering proceeds of foreign corruption.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA reassesses under s.247(2) if the aggregate adjustment exceeds C$50,000; the TP penalty is 10% of the gross misstatement, up to C$100,000 for large corporations; failure to provide contemporaneous documentation adds C$2,500–C$100,000 exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed if they signed false transfer pricing reports; foreign interposed entities can be challenged via treaty collection assistance, and directors of the Canadian general partner are liable for the resulting tax and penalties.
