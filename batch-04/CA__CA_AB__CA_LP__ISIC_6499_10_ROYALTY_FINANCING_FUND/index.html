Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an Article VIII IMF member and has had no foreign exchange control legislation since the repealed 1952 Exchange Control Act. The CAD floats freely under the Bank of Canada's inflation targeting regime; both current-account items (royalty payments, service invoices, trade receivables) and capital-account items (limited partner capital contributions, equity injections, portfolio advances) are fully convertible into USD, EUR, GBP, or other major currencies without ministerial approval or prior central bank license. The only frictions are practical: (i) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposes a 25% statutory withholding tax on royalties, interest, and dividends paid to non-residents, subject to treaty reduction (e.g., 0%-10% under the Canada-US Treaty), which requires the paying bank to obtain Form NR301 / W-8IMY before processing the remittance; (ii) banks apply standard enhanced due diligence for offshore investors; and (iii) all regulated Canadian payment flows must be settled through clearing members of Payments Canada. Profit remittance and return of capital to foreign LPs are unrestricted and do not require Bank of Canada approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose surrender, mandatory conversion, or stipulated repatriation timelines for export proceeds, royalty income, or cross-border portfolio receipts. The only statutory overlay is the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, which requires (i) reporting of any physical cross-border movement of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more to the Canada Border Services Agency, and (ii) FINTRAC reporting by banks of large EFT (CAD 10,000+) within prescribed operational timelines (no surrender of the currency itself). A CA_LP royalty financing fund may freely invoice and settle in USD or CAD and may retain USD balances in a U.S.-dollar sub-account at a Canadian Schedule I bank without conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa, with operational supervision of federally chartered deposit-takers delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and payment supervision under the Retail Payment Activities Act delegated to the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Supervision unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low as the principal depository; High as an auxiliary payment rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Currencycloud, etc.) are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and cannot hold the underlying capital commitments of a Canadian LP or provide CDIC insurance. From 2024, the Retail Payment Activities Act requires any entity providing payment functions (holding funds, initiating transfers, or maintaining payment accounts) to Canadian parties to be registered as a Payment Service Provider with the Bank of Canada; a foreign EMI operating without Canadian registration and without a FINTRAC MSB registration cannot lawfully provide account services to the fund. The recommended architecture is a tiered account structure: Schedule I Canadian bank accounts as the operational and escrow core, with an authorized Canadian PSP / MSB-linked EMI used only for high-volume, low-value cross-border disbursements to U.S. and EU portfolio companies, and even then only when the EMI sweeps daily to a Canadian chartered bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound LP capital contributions are routed via SWIFT MT103 (field 70 with purpose code 'CAPITAL CALL' or 'LP CONTRIBUTION') or via Fedwire from U.S. LPs using the bank's U.S. correspondent details. Contributions in USD are received into the bank's USD clearing sub-account and either held in USD or converted to CAD through an outright FX spot transaction settled on T+2 via CLS or the bank's internal book. For amounts above USD 1,000,000, the GP issues a pre-notification (SWIFT GPI tracking) with the LP's account details and source-of-funds documentation to the onboarding manager. The revenue-based financing fund must use a dedicated Capital Escrow Account (trust account naming, e.g., 'CA_LP Capital Escrow Account, in trust') with each capital call evidenced by (i) the executed capital-call notice, (ii) an updated LP capital schedule, and (iii) a bank account confirmation letter. Inbound funds are verified against the bank's AML/CFT sanctions screening and matched to the LP register before being released to the Operating Account.Outbound advances to portfolio digital brands are settled via SWIFT MT103 (bank-to-bank credit transfer) denominated in USD, EUR, GBP, or CAD, with bank charges coded 'OUR' (71A) to preserve the contractual advance amount. Each disbursement references the executed Royalty Financing Agreement and the merchant's company ID, and the bank matches the 50K ordering-party chain to the GP to prevent cuckoo-smurfing red flags. For portfolio contracts requiring collateral or advance payment security, the clearing rail may include a Commercial or Standby Letter of Credit issued under SWIFT MT700 by the Canadian bank on the fund's account, subject to OSFI large-exposure limits. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Canada – Alberta combined general corporate rate of 23.0% (15% federal Canada Corporate Income Tax plus 8% Alberta provincial general corporate rate). As a Limited Partnership, the entity is fiscally transparent: no entity-level corporate tax is levied; partners are taxed on allocated income at their respective federal/provincial rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation. For a Limited Partnership, profit distributions are normally capital returns if the LP’s income has already been taxed to partners; however, if the LP is treated as a corporation under Part XIII or makes deductible payments, a 25% rate applies before treaty reduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian transfer pricing documentation has no single statutory Local File / Master File medal threshold. CRA requires continued Form T106 reporting of non-arm’s length transactions with non-residents when the aggregate value of transactions with each related non-resident exceeds CAD 1 million per year; contemporaneous documentation must be prepared and provided to CRA within 3 months of a request if total transactions exceed that threshold. Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting applies under the OECD style framework where consolidated group revenue is at least CAD 750 million. An Alberta LP with royalty financing across borders should maintain a prudential Local File if its intercompany royalty financing transactions exceed CAD 1 million to avoid penalties under ITA s.247(3)-(4).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the modern onshore Economic Substance Regulations; the doctrine is common-law substance-over-form, the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under ITA s.245, and the CRA sham doctrine. An Alberta LP used as a royalty-financing fund must demonstrate economic substance through real employees, registered office, independent decision-making, commercial risk, and actual business activity. Absent substance, CRA can re-characterize financing transactions as unjustified dividend distributions, deny deductions under s.245, apply thin-capitalization limits, or disregard the LP as an artificial conduit. Alberta’s limited-partner shield may also be pierced under the Alberta Partnership Act if limited partners participate in management control.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1): summary conviction fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) is at least $500 and up to 50% of the understated tax; Alberta provincial fines mirror the federal penalty.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors who knew of the evasion are liable for the partnership’s unreported tax under ITA s.160 and s.227.1, including personal civil restitution, travel restrictions, and in serious cases criminal imprisonment. Limited partners may lose the LP liability shield if they took part in management or knowingly assisted in the false return.
Shell Company and GAAR Disallowance

Statutory sanction: ITA s.245(2) disallows the tax benefit and re-characterizes the transaction; penalty under s.245(6) is up to 15% of the disallowed benefit, plus interest retroactive to the original assessment. CRA may also assess transfer-pricing adjustments and Part XIII withholding on the entire royalty stream.

Director personal liability: Directors can be made personally liable for corporate/partnership tax amounts assessed as having been transferred to non-arm’s length parties under ITA s.160; if GAAR is found, directors who orchestrated the conduit may face penalty assessments and inability to travel pending payment of outstanding assessments.
AML and Unregistered Money Services Business

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million per violation; criminal indictment penalties up to CAD 500,000 and/or 5 years imprisonment; Alberta Consumer Protection Act imposes additional administrative fines for unregistered provider activity.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the general partner who knowingly engage in unregistered MSB activity are personally punishable and jointly and severally civilly liable to third parties for losses; travel bans can be imposed under AML enforcement proceedings, and limited partners involved in settlement activity lose limited liability.
Withholding Tax Failure on Distributions to Non-Residents

Statutory sanction: ITA s.227(8) imposes a 10% penalty plus 6%-interest-per-annum on the amount not withheld; the payer remains liable for the gross withholding tax; for a corporation, the unpaid amount becomes a director liability under s.227.1; for a partnership, the general partner is jointly liable.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are personally assessed for the full unremitted amount plus interest and penalties unless they exercised reasonable diligence. Non-resident partners who fail to produce a TRC lose treaty relief and are assessed at the statutory 25% rate; repeat offenders may face CRA criminal prosecution and border-exit travel bans.
Investment Fund and Prospectus Violations

Statutory sanction: Alberta Securities Act: administrative penalty up to CAD 1 million per infraction, disgorgement of profits, issuing director suspension; criminal penalties for fraud or unregistered trading include imprisonment up to 5 years and fines of CAD 5 million.

Director personal liability: Directors, issuers, and promoters are jointly and severally liable under the Securities Act for losses incurred by purchasers, including rescission damages and interest. A court may also bar the persons from becoming directors and issue a national travel ban pending hearing.
