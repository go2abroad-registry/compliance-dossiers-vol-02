Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html); Alberta Finance and Treasury Board applies only at the level of corporate partners since the limited partnership is transparent for Alberta provincial tax purposes.

E-government filing portal

The Alberta limited partnership (LP) must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA using Business Registration Online or Form RC1 by selecting 'Partnership' as the legal type. A trust/partnership account (RT) is created. Each general and limited partner, including non-resident partners, must have their own BN if carrying on business in Canada. Foreign partners with no other Canadian filing obligations must register for a non-resident account (NR) for Part XIII withholding and NR4 information returns (Form NR7 to request a NR account). Alberta does not issue a separate provincial tax identification number for LPs; the federal BN is used for all Alberta filings. The registration is ordinarily completed electronically within one business day; CRA may request supporting partnership agreements and a confirmation of the Alberta registration under the Partnership Act (Alberta).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. Under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and section 16 of the Partnership Act (Alberta), a limited partnership is fiscally transparent: the LP does not file an income tax return or pay federal or Alberta corporate income tax. Each partner is allocated its share of income, gains, losses, and credits and computes tax at its own rate. A non-resident partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP's permanent establishment is taxable under Part I on the taxable income of the PE (section 115), and a non-resident corporate partner may also be subject to Part XIV branch tax on after-tax earnings attributable to the PE (25% statutory rate, subject to treaty reduction). For goods and services tax (GST/HST) purposes, captive reinsurance premiums are exempt financial supplies under Part VII of Schedule V to the Excise Tax Act; the partnership has no obligation to register for GST/HST unless it also makes taxable (non-exempt) supplies exceeding the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and section 16 of the Partnership Act (Alberta), a limited partnership is fiscally transparent: the LP does not file an income tax return or pay federal or Alberta corporate income tax. Each partner is allocated its share of income, gains, losses, and credits and computes tax at its own rate. A non-resident partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP's permanent establishment is taxable under Part I on the taxable income of the PE (section 115), and a non-resident corporate partner may also be subject to Part XIV branch tax on after-tax earnings attributable to the PE (25% statutory rate, subject to treaty reduction). For goods and services tax (GST/HST) purposes, captive reinsurance premiums are exempt financial supplies under Part VII of Schedule V to the Excise Tax Act; the partnership has no obligation to register for GST/HST unless it also makes taxable (non-exempt) supplies exceeding the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold. Alberta has no provincial sales tax and no capital tax applicable to partnerships.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a 'check-the-box' election analogous to the U.S. Internal Revenue Code; the LP's fiscal transparency is statutory and cannot be electively altered. Elections available to the captive reinsurance LP and its partners include: (a) a joint election under subsection 97(2) to defer the recognition of gain on contributed capital assets to a partnership; (b) a joint election under subsection 96(3) to fix a non-calendar partnership fiscal period; (c) an election under subsection 111.5(2) to surface losses of a foreign affiliate for use in computing the partnership's income; (d) an election under subsection 152(3.1) to waive the normal reassessment period in an audit; and (e) group reporting elections under the OECD hybrid mismatch rules (Sections 2.5 through 2.19 of the Income Tax Act, applying from 2019-2023) to disclose or deem deductible/non-deductible amounts. For GST/HST, no election can convert the exempt supply of reinsurance into a taxable supply. Alberta's minimal partnership registry filing does not require a separate tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: a person becomes a GST/HST registrant when its taxable supplies (other than zero-rated) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Because captive reinsurance premiums are exempt financial supplies, they are excluded from the computation of taxable supplies; registration is therefore generally not mandatory for the LP. If the LP separately provides any taxable advisory, management, or services to Canadian recipients, or imports services subject to the self-assessment rules, the CAD 30,000 threshold must be monitored. Voluntary registration may be elected but provides no input tax credit recovery on exempt insurance outputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Captive reinsurance is a financial service under paragraph (l.2) of the definition of 'financial service' in subsection 123(1) of the Excise Tax Act; it is therefore exempt, not zero-rated, under section 8 of Part VII of Schedule V. Exempt status means no GST/HST is charged on premium income, and the LP is generally ineligible to claim input tax credits for related inputs (e.g., legal, actuarial, and third-party administration costs). Zero-rating under Schedule VI is not available to insurance or reinsurance premiums. Ancillary non-financial services (e.g., consulting, claims-settlement advisory services) supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST may qualify for zero-rating under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI, provided the services are not performed in connection with real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the services are performed. Goods exported from Canada by the LP, if any (e.g., office equipment sold abroad), may be zero-rated under sections 1 to 5 of Part V of Schedule VI, subject to prescribed documentary evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or Canadian Automated Export Declaration) for any tangible goods exported from Canada — typically inapplicable to reinsurance operations
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Control Document evidencing physical export of goods where zero-rating is claimed
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 confirmation, or bank statement evidencing offshore payment for exported goods or zero-rated services
  • Reinsurance policy, contract, endorsements, and bordereaux demonstrating the risk assumed from the cedant
  • GST/HST exemption certificate signed by the non-resident recipient when claiming zero-rating under Schedule VI Part V section 7
  • Due-diligence file confirming the non-resident recipient is not registered for GST/HST

None. Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital tax stamping. CRA accepts both paper and electronic invoices; the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL framework supports voluntary B2B e-invoicing but is not compulsory. For audit purposes, invoices issued by the captive reinsurance LP must be retained in English or French for six years and must contain the supplier's BN, recipient name, date, description, and amount, whether the supply is exempt or zero-rated. There is no CRA pre-clearance obligation for each invoice; compliance is enforced at audit.

CRA imposes a normal reassessment period of four years from the date of the notice of assessment; the period is extended to ten years where a taxpayer has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. Export zero-rating claims and GST/HST refunds are actively audited: a failure to produce the required export documentation within six years results in a denial of the zero-rating claim and a full clawback of any refund paid, plus interest and penalties. Gross negligence penalties under subsection 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act can reach 25% of the understated net tax, and criminal tax evasion under subsection 327(2) and section 239 of the Income Tax Act carries fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. A captive reinsurer that artificially restructures exempt reinsurance income or incurs non-arm's length expenses may also be subject to the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in section 245 of the Income Tax Act and to transfer-pricing adjustments under section 247, both of which carry 10% to 25% transfer-pricing penalties (or 50% for a gross valuation misstatement). Canada has no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is cited as a de facto audit benchmark for intercompany export receivables and is not a legal requirement, but materially delayed repatriation may trigger transfer-pricing re-characterization as a loan or dividend, Part XIII withholding, and foreign accrual property income rules for Canadian corporate partners.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Both current-account flows (reinsurance premiums, claims settlements, commutations) and capital-account flows (LP capital contributions, distributions, surplus releases, hybrid financing) are unrestricted for non-resident limited partners. Profit and dividend remittance faces no convertibility hurdle; only tax withholding (Part XIII) and reporting obligations (T5/T106, AES, FINTRAC EFT) apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender ratio, mandatory conversion timeline, or repatriation holding period on export proceeds or reinsurance premium receipts. Foreign-currency balances (e.g., USD premiums) may be held indefinitely in domestic or correspondent accounts and converted at freely negotiated market rates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); FX regulatory oversight shared with Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act. Canada maintains no standalone foreign exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. Canada does not recognize offshore EMI (e.g., UK FCA or EU-licensed EMI) authorizations as a basis for deposit-taking or insurance-reserve custody. Any Canadian-facing payment service provider must register as a money services business with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and comply with OSFI's Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) regime. Offshore EMIs cannot hold segregated statutory reserve accounts, issue SWIFT MT103 in a direct-clearing capacity, or provide reinsurance trust/letters of credit collateral management. They may serve only as ancillary FX conversion or payroll-type disbursement tools, never as the primary treasury rail for underwriting reserves.
