E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST account by filing Form RC1 or registering online through My Business Account, generally required when taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. If the partnership carries on business in Alberta, file an Extra-Provincial Limited Partnership Registration with Alberta Corporate Registry. A Canadian-source partnership income allocation and information return (Form T5013) must be filed by the partnership if it is a Canadian partnership earning Canadian-source income. The foreign LP itself is not issued a separate income tax TIN; each partner's tax obligation is reported through the partnership information return and the partners' own Canadian tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: an Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes; the partnership computes income or loss and allocates it to limited and general partners, who are taxable on their respective shares. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate person registrant. The territorial scope of Canadian income taxation is based on Canadian-source business income and the permanent establishment rules, while GST/HST applies to taxable supplies made in Canada in the course of a commercial activity.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election comparable to the U.S. regime. Entity status for Canadian federal income tax follows the Income Tax Act and, for foreign entities, is determined by domestic law, treaty, and the relevant CRA administrative interpretation. Limited partners and general partners can allocate income and losses under the partnership agreement subject to arm's length and anti-avoidance rules. Section 18.4 and related hybrid mismatch provisions under the Income Tax Act may recharacterize cross-border payments in excessive hybrid arrangements; no comparable elective hybrid-mismatch opt-in is available. Under the Excise Tax Act, elections such as closely related corporation elections (section 156) are generally unavailable to partnerships; a partnership may choose to use actual or net tax computations (e.g., quick method election at 80% requirement) where applicable for GST/HST.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: a person conducting commercial activity in Canada must register for GST/HST once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in either a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, unless exempt. Non-residents carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same small supplier threshold; registration may also be required for non-residents who solicit orders in Canada, even if below threshold, in certain circumstances.

Canada has no national continuous transaction control (CTC) regime, no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance through CRA, and no digital stamping or government pre-clearance of invoices. GST/HST returns are filed electronically using GST/HST NETFILE or paper return if eligible; records and electronic invoices must be retained in an auditable format. EDI/API transmission may be used with CRA for electronic returns and assessment, but there is no statutory requirement for invoice-level clearance or e-invoice XML file submission to the tax authority.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a freely floating currency with no general exchange-control legislation. Both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible for residents and non-residents. Profit remittances, including dividends, interest, royalties, and legal-fee repatriations, are not subject to central-bank approval or quantitative limits. The only regulatory burdens are standard Canadian withholding tax on outbound payments under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, FINTRAC reporting of large or suspicious cross-border transactions, and Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting for international service transactions. Settlement of litigation awards into or out of Canada is unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or statutory remittance timeline for export proceeds. Legal services fees earned by a Canadian limited partnership are treated as cross-border service revenue, not as 'export proceeds' subject to surrender. The partnership may maintain foreign-currency accounts with authorized Canadian banks and may convert receipts at commercial rates without regulatory restriction. Incoming settlement wires for IOLTA trust accounts must be reconciled under Law Society of Alberta trust accounting rules and reported to FINTRAC if equal to or above CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); banking prudential oversight by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision by Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate-to-low as primary treasury rails. Authorized digital banks and regulated fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise Platform, Mercury, or other FINTRAC-registered money service businesses) can serve as secondary disbursement channels for operational FX payments and small cross-border transfers. However, under Law Society of Alberta rules, client trust funds (IOLTA) must be held with a Canadian chartered bank, authorized foreign bank branch, or trust company licensed to accept deposits in Alberta. Unregulated offshore EMIs cannot hold lawyer-client settlement monies and must not be designated as the trust account provider. For non-trust operational liquidity, a registered fintech EMI is viable only if it maintains a Canadian money services business license, applies FINTRAC screening, and submits to the same sanctions and AML obligations as a schedule I bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Nil at the Alberta LP entity level because a Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (Canada). Each partner is taxed on its allocated share. For a non-resident corporate partner carrying on business through an Alberta PE, the combined federal (15%) and Alberta general (8%) corporate rate is 23%. If no PE, Part I tax applies at a net 25% plus potential Part XIV branch tax; both may be modified by applicable tax treaty.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Not applicable to LP profit distributions because partnership allocations are not dividends. If the business is effectively transferred into a Canadian corporation and a dividend is paid by that corporation to a non-resident corporate partner, the statutory Part XIII rate is 25%. Under the Canada–US treaty the rate is 5% for a shareholder owning at least 10% of voting stock and 15% for all other cases.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (T106 Information Return and a master/local file) must be prepared when the aggregate fair market value of all transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000 per fiscal year. A further threshold applies: a T106 disclosure penalty if the value of reportable transactions exceeds CAD 5,000,000 and the annual gross revenues of the partnership exceed CAD 10,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA) re-characterises abusive tax-avoidance transactions that lack genuine economic substance. The CRA will verify the operational reality of an Alberta LP by scrutinising: (i) a signed LP agreement with profit/loss allocation ratios; (ii) real office presence and active management minutes; (iii) fully-paid capital contributions; (iv) actual performance of commercial litigation services for consideration; and (v) independent, rational contractual terms for any inter-portfolio cross-charge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Omission

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239(1), upon conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for a term not exceeding 5 years (indictable) or 2 years less a day (summary conviction). The CRA may additionally assess a civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s.163(2).

Director personal liability: The general partner of the LP and its directors are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's unpaid source withholdings and GST/HST under ITA s.227.1 and ETA s.323; criminal prosecution of the officers can lead to a court-ordered travel ban (Criminal Code s.517) and asset-freezing orders.
Artificial Arrangement / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA s.245 denies the tax benefit and re-characterises the allocation; re-assessed income is subject to full federal and Alberta tax plus arrears interest. If the GAAR transaction is shown to be wilful, a 50% gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) can also be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner can be personally assessed for the re-allocated tax under s.227.1 where the corporation fails to pay; the director may face a common-law fraudulent conveyance action and a court travel restriction during the investigation.
Transfer Pricing Misallocation / Failure to File Schedule 57

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.247(3), a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment is imposed, in addition to reinstatement of the income and a withholding tax assessment on the adjusted non-resident payments; the underlying service fee is disallowed as a deduction.

Director personal liability: Members of the general partner are jointly liable for the penalty if the partnership assets are insufficient; directors who knowingly participated in mispricing face personal civil restitution and, in egregious cases, criminal liability for aiding tax evasion.
AML / FINTRAC Trust-Account Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 1,000,000 for a corporation and CAD 500,000 per individual; criminal prosecution for failing to keep required records carries up to CAD 200,000 in fines and imprisonment for up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers of the GP may be personally charged as directing minds of the offence; penalties include imprisonment, a court-ordered travel ban pending trial, and disqualification from serving as director of any legal trust corporation in Canada or in mutual-recognition jurisdictions.
