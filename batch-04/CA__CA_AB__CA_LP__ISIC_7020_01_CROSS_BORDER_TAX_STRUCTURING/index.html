Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal income tax, GST/HST, and Alberta corporate income tax administration under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement; Alberta Treasury Board and Finance / Alberta Revenue for provincial tax policy and administration. Primary portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Alberta Corporate Registry at https://www.alberta.ca/corporate-registry.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities forming an Alberta limited partnership must first complete extra-provincial registration under the Partnership Act with the Alberta Corporate Registry and appoint an Alberta agent for service. After registration, the partnership must: (1) obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1, requesting a GST/HST program account (RT) where the registration threshold is met or voluntary registration is desired; (2) request a payroll deductions account (RP) if employees are engaged; (3) request a CBSA import/export program account if physical cross-border movement occurs; and (4) file the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return. The LP itself is not required to file a corporate income tax return; income/loss is allocated to partners and reported at the partner level.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - Under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act, the Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent; income is computed at the LP level and allocated to partners under the partnership agreement. No entity-level federal or Alberta income tax is imposed on the LP. Non-resident partners are taxable only on allocated Canadian-source business income attributable to a permanent establishment in Alberta, subject to applicable income tax treaty relief. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act, the Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent; income is computed at the LP level and allocated to partners under the partnership agreement. No entity-level federal or Alberta income tax is imposed on the LP. Non-resident partners are taxable only on allocated Canadian-source business income attributable to a permanent establishment in Alberta, subject to applicable income tax treaty relief. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is separately registrable as a person.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; limited partnership status is statutory and cannot be converted to corporate classification. The material elections for cross-border structuring include: (1) ITA subsection 102(2) Canadian partnership election to preserve partnership continuity and eligibility for tax-deferred rollovers; (2) ITA subsection 97(2) election to establish an agreed cost amount for property contributed to the LP; (3) ITA subsection 98(3) election on partnership distribution/termination to achieve tax-deferred transfer to partners; (4) ITA subsection 216(1) election by a non-resident partner to file a net-basis return for Canadian-source rental/royalty income rather than pay Part XIII gross withholding; and (5) Excise Tax Act section 156 election among closely related registrants to treat intercompany supplies as nil consideration for GST/HST. No elective relief exists under Canada's hybrid mismatch rules; those rules apply automatically to deny deduction or require inclusion for hybrid mismatch arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of total gross taxable supplies (GST/HST-exclusive) made in Canada. A person ceases to be a small supplier when taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters, and mandatory registration applies from the earlier triggering transaction. Non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada in the course of a commercial activity are subject to the same threshold; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act where the supplier exports the goods as part of a continuous uninterrupted journey to a destination outside Canada and retains documentary proof of export. Services supplied to a non-resident person are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act where the recipient is not resident in Canada, the service is not supplied to an individual present in Canada at the time of performance, and the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time it is performed. International tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory services rendered by the Alberta LP to non-resident clients will qualify as zero-rated offshore services where the recipient is non-resident and the service does not touch Canadian situs property or require performance by an individual in Canada. The supplier must maintain auditable export evidence for six years.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13-A or electronic export report transmitted through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System or equivalent carrier manifest data) evidencing that the goods left Canada
  • Bill of lading, air waybill or other transport document showing the foreign destination and continuous uninterrupted movement where applicable
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and underlying purchase contract or work order identifying the non-resident buyer, description, quantity, value and currency
  • Inward remittance certificate issued by the Canadian receiving financial institution, SWIFT MT103, bank wire advice or comparable banking evidence of settlement of the export invoice
  • Customs broker certified export proof or CBSA proof-of-export report where a licensed customs broker facilitated the export

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no pre-authorisation of invoices. Electronic invoices are valid if they contain the prescribed information under subsection 221(1) of the Excise Tax Act, including supplier name and BN/GST registration number, invoice date, recipient name, description/quantity, consideration, and tax amount. Since 1 April 2023, suppliers invoicing federal government departments must transmit electronic invoices through a Peppol-certified Access Point; no comparable B2B or B2C mandate exists. Tax records and invoices must be retained for six years.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims using CBSA cargo and manifest data, financial institution records, exchange-of-information data, and GST/HST risk profiling. Where documentation is inadequate or the transaction fails the continuous uninterrupted movement test, CRA will recharacterize the supply as taxable, assess 5% GST on the export consideration, claw back input tax credits previously claimed on inputs used in the export supply, and charge compounded daily statutory interest from the original filing due date. Gross negligence or knowingly false statements attract a penalty under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act of at least CAD 250 and up to 50% of the net tax understated. Criminal prosecution for evasion may be commenced under section 326 of the Excise Tax Act or section 380 of the Criminal Code, exposing the taxpayer and its directors/general partner to fines and imprisonment. The normal four-year reassessment period does not protect taxpayers where a misrepresentation was made with neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud; in such cases CRA may reassess and claw back refunds without time limitation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. The CAD is freely floating and there is no exchange-control licensing, investment-conversion permit, or profit-remittance approval requirement in Canada. The only remittance hurdle is fiscal: Canadian-source profits allocated to non-resident partners remain taxable in Canada under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada), and specific streams (management fees, royalties, rents, and amounts deemed to be dividends or interest) attract Part XIII withholding (statutory 25%, treaty-reducible upon valid tax-residency certification). The Alberta limited partnership must file T5013 and T106 information returns with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and remit all withholdings. No Tobin tax, stamp duty, or mandatory conversion spread applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Canada. There is no statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation window, or earmarking requirement for export proceeds or foreign-currency receipts. Exporters and service firms may hold foreign currency indefinitely in authorized-dealer accounts maintained with Schedule I banks and may convert at free-market rates without central-bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary and financial-system authority; Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate and monetary policy coordination; FINTRAC (under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, SC 2000, c. 17) as the statutory cross-border electronic funds-transfer reporting authority; Payments Canada as the clearing and settlement infrastructure operator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability with material legal constraints. Canada does not grant passporting rights to foreign EMI or digital-bank licences. A non-resident payment service provider that solicits Canadian clients must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, for retail payment activities, is subject to the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act oversight regime (notification phase open since November 2024, supervision phased through 2025). Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business) can be used as satellite multi-currency wallets, but they cannot provide domestic CAD escrow, CRA remittance wiring, Lynx clearing, or a Canadian branch presence. Feasibility rating: Medium for auxiliary treasury needs, Low as the primary banking or escrow rail. SWIFT correspondent banking through a Schedule I sponsor bank remains the mandatory core.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined federal (15%) and Alberta general (8%) corporate income tax rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident recipient. An Alberta Limited Partnership is not an entity subject to Part XIII dividend withholding on ordinary profit distributions; the non-resident partner is taxed under Part I on allocated income. If a Canadian corporation uses an LP as a flow-through and later declares a dividend, the 25% statutory rate attaches on the corporate distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the taxation year-end. The balance of tax payable is due two months after year-end for general corporations, or three months for Canadian-controlled private corporations eligible for the small business deduction.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within five months after the end of the partnership’s fiscal period (e.g., by May 31 for a December 31 year-end). Copies of partner slips (T5013) must be sent to partners by the same filing due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of cross-border non-arm's-length transactions triggers Form T106 and mandatory contemporaneous local file documentation. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, the Income Tax Act employs the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245), sham doctrine, and the principle of commercial substance. For an Alberta LP in tax structuring/transfer pricing advisory, CRA will disregard paper-only allocations if there is no real office, no Canadian-resident staff, no board decision records, or no actual control over risk. Non-resident partners must not engage in activities that create a Canadian permanent establishment; passive limited partner status must be supported by the partnership agreement and actual behaviour.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: summary conviction fine up to CAD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; indictment fine up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil s.163(2) gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the misrepresented amount, plus arrears interest and compounding penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and general partners may be held personally, jointly and severally liable for withheld statutory amounts and civil penalties. Courts will pierce the LP veil where the entity is a mere facade; personal assets can be seized, directors may face travel bans, and a conviction triggers mandatory criminal record and imprisonment up to five years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to arm's-length consideration; penalty under s.247(3) of 10% of the aggregate adjustment when reasonable documentation efforts were not made; if the adjustment is attributable to gross negligence or wilful misrepresentation, the 50% s.163(2) penalty applies on aggregate reassessed amounts. Tax can be re-allocated among partners, and late-payment interest runs from the original filing date.

Director personal liability: TP documentation obligations run to the taxpayer entity, but officers and directors who knowingly sign false T106/T2 statements can be held liable for gross negligence penalties. Directors who direct or authorise artificial transfer-priced invoices may be tried for tax fraud and face shareholder derivative claims, regulatory prohibition orders, and imprisonment up to five years.
Shell Company / Sham Transaction / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under s.245 GAAR, the CRA may disregard the avoidance transaction and reassess on the economic substance basis. An additional GAAR penalty of 25% (increased to 50%+ in certain recent proposals) of the federal tax benefit is applicable. The CRA may also impose administrative sanctions up to CAD 25,000 for knowingly filing false or incomplete partnership returns and may revoke the partnership's business number.

Director personal liability: Directors and promoters who design sham offshore mind-management arrangements may be prosecuted under s.239; civil restitution is available to CRA and shareholders. Individuals responsible may be prohibited from serving as directors, subject to cross-border tax travel bans, and sentenced to imprisonment for up to five years.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance Default

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for partnerships/entities and CAD 100,000 for individuals; repeated or egregious violations can lead to compliance orders, revocation of the business license, and criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code for money laundering. Non-resident partner nominees are not exempt from Canadian AML legislation.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers, directors, and general partners who knowingly conceal beneficial owners or fail to implement FINTRAC controls can be personally fined and criminally charged. The Criminal Code imposes up to 10 years' imprisonment for laundering proceeds of tax-fraud offences; directors face travel bans and seizure of personal assets during prosecution.
