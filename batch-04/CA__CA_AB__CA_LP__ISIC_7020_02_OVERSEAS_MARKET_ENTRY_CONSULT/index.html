Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) is the competent federal tax authority; Alberta corporate income tax is administered and collected by the CRA under the Alberta Tax Collection Agreement on behalf of Alberta Finance and the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration. Principal portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account — https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta Limited Partnership (LP): (1) Determine whether the LP carries on business in Canada and whether a Canadian permanent establishment exists under ITA s. 253; (2) Form the LP under the Alberta Partnership Act and obtain the Alberta Corporate Registry Corporate Access Number; (3) File CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online through BRO, attaching constituent documents (LP certificate, partnership agreement, general partner resolutions, and non-resident partner registry extracts where applicable); (4) Receive the 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts — e.g., RT0001 (GST/HST), RC0001 (income tax remittance), RP0001 (payroll, if any), and RM0001 (import/export account, if any); (5) Register for GST/HST under ETA s. 240 when the small-supplier threshold is exceeded, or voluntarily to claim input tax credits; non-resident suppliers carrying on business in Canada must obtain a BN before invoicing; (6) File the annual partnership information return T5013 and issue T5013 Statement of Partnership Income to all partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — An Alberta Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes: the LP computes income at the partnership level (ITA s. 96) but is not a taxpayer; income, gains, losses, deductions, and credits flow through to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement, and tax is assessed on the partners (limited partners subject to the at-risk and limited-recourse anti-avoidance rules).

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic U.S.-style check-the-box election; Alberta LP transparency is statutory. Elective mechanisms include: (i) ITA s. 97(2) tax-deferred rollover election on transfers of property to the partnership; (ii) ETA s. 273 joint election for GST/HST on certain supplies between the partnership and its partners; (iii) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240(2.1) for small suppliers and non-residents; (iv) for U.S. tax classification, the LP may file IRS Form 8832 to elect partnership or corporate status (a corporate election creates a hybrid entity); (v) Canada's hybrid mismatch regime (BEPS Action 2 proposals, including the enacted excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) rules in ITA s. 18.2 and pending hybrid-mismatch measures) denies deductions or includes amounts where payments are characterized inconsistently between Canada and the foreign partner's jurisdiction.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a freely convertible currency regime for current account transactions under IMF Article VIII. Capital account transactions are subject to Bank of Canada review and approval for certain investments exceeding CAD 1 million. Profit remittances to foreign investors are generally permitted without restriction, subject to tax withholdings and reporting under the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to a designated bank or central bank is required. Exporters must repatriate proceeds to Canada within contractually stipulated timelines. All foreign exchange transactions are executed at prevailing market rates with no mandatory conversion ratios; reporting to the Bank of Canada occurs where applicable under trade statistics requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and is not subject to income tax at the partnership level. Allocated business income is taxed at partner level. For an Alberta-resident corporate partner, the general combined federal and Alberta corporate rate is generally 23%: 15% federal plus 8% Alberta. An eligible Canadian-controlled private corporation may generally access an approximately 11% combined small-business rate on qualifying active business income within the applicable business limit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The general Canadian Part XIII withholding rate on taxable dividends paid or credited to a non-resident is 25%. A treaty may reduce the rate, commonly to 5% for qualifying corporate ownership and 15% for other treaty-resident recipients, subject to the applicable treaty, limitation-on-benefits rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, and anti-avoidance provisions. A limited partnership distribution is not automatically a dividend; its withholding treatment follows the character and source of the underlying amount and applicable partnership rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The partnership information return and related T5013 slips are generally due within five months after the end of the partnership fiscal period. A Canadian partnership fiscal period generally cannot exceed 12 months. Partners have separate filing deadlines under the Income Tax Act and are not relieved from their own filing obligations by the partnership filing.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The corporate partner's T2 is generally due six months after the end of its taxation year. Any balance is generally payable two months after year-end, with a three-month payment rule potentially available to qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations meeting statutory conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 is the principal threshold commonly relevant to T106 reporting for aggregate non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents. Canada does not use a single universal transaction-value threshold that automatically eliminates the contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation requirement. Contemporaneous documentation is generally required under Income Tax Act section 247 where non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exist. Canada has no general standalone Local File/Master File filing regime equivalent to every OECD implementation; large multinational groups may have Country-by-Country reporting and related documentation obligations where consolidated group revenue meets the applicable EUR 750 million equivalent threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate UK-style Economic Substance Regulations that apply generally to Alberta limited partnerships. Substance remains relevant under the transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance rule, treaty anti-abuse principles, beneficial-ownership analysis, sham and factual-recharacterization doctrines, partnership allocation rules, and permanent-establishment analysis. The partnership should maintain genuine decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, commercial records, functional control over risks, service evidence, and arm's-length pricing. A partnership formed solely to obtain treaty benefits or create artificial deductions is high risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil reassessment, tax, arrears interest, gross-negligence penalties, and possible criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act. Tax evasion convictions can result in fines of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years, subject to the applicable offence and court determination.

Director personal liability: General limited-liability protection does not shield a person who personally commits fraud or tax offences. Directors and controlling persons may face prosecution, restitution or fines, and imprisonment. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from bail conditions, warrants, probation, or court orders.
Failure to Remit Payroll, GST/HST, or Part XIII Withholding

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unremitted amounts, prescribed interest, civil penalties, and enforcement actions. Responsible directors may be assessed personally under statutory director-liability rules, subject to due-diligence defenses and limitation periods.

Director personal liability: Personal assessment, garnishment, liens, seizure, and possible prosecution may apply. Imprisonment is possible for deliberate criminal conduct but is not the ordinary consequence of an administrative arrears assessment. Travel bans are not an automatic tax remedy.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment under Income Tax Act section 247, denial of deductions, secondary adjustments where applicable, arrears interest, and transfer-pricing penalties generally equal to 10% of the adjustment where the statutory conditions are met and reasonable efforts were not made.

Director personal liability: The primary exposure is normally at partnership, corporate-partner, or partner level. Personal exposure may arise for knowing participation in false reporting, fraud, obstruction, or other offences. Civil liability and criminal sanctions are fact-dependent.
Shell Partnership, Sham, or Artificial Treaty Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, application of the general anti-avoidance rule, recharacterization of income, withholding-tax reassessment, interest, civil penalties, and possible partnership or permanent-establishment reassessment.

Director personal liability: Partners and managers may face civil claims, reassessment, and penalties. Criminal liability requires proof of an applicable offence such as fraud or tax evasion. No automatic veil piercing, travel ban, or imprisonment follows merely from low substance; these consequences require additional unlawful conduct or court action.
Anti-Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties, compliance orders, seizure or forfeiture, and prosecution under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and Criminal Code. Criminal penalties may include substantial fines and imprisonment depending on the offence.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and responsible individuals may be prosecuted personally for participation, authorization, counselling, or obstruction. Assets may be restrained or forfeited. Travel restrictions may arise from bail, warrants, or court orders, not automatically from a compliance deficiency.
Unlawful Distribution and Insolvency Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Recovery actions, fraudulent-conveyance remedies, creditor claims, interest, penalties, and possible insolvency or criminal proceedings depending on the conduct.

Director personal liability: A limited partner's liability is generally limited to the statutory framework and preservation of limited-partner status. A general partner, managing person, or director of a corporate general partner may face personal liability for breach of duty, oppression, fraudulent conveyance, knowing participation, or statutory remittance liabilities.
