Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Alberta limited partnership with the Alberta Corporate Registry and obtain the certificate of limited partnership. Step 2: Apply to CRA for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) through BRO or Form RC1, selecting the GST/HST program account (RT) and, where applicable, the import/export account (RM) or payroll account (RC). Step 3: Non-resident partners must appoint a resident Canadian agent for service of process and file the CRA authorized-representative designation for GST/HST (Excise Tax Act s. 299). Step 4: Use the BN/RT account for all GST/HST returns and T5013 Partnership Information Return filings; a T5013 is mandatory where any partner is a non-resident or a corporation, or where the partnership has six or more partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; classification is statutory. The Income Tax Act (ITA) treats LPs as flow-through: income is allocated to partners. Relevant elections: (1) partnership fiscal period election under ITA s. 249.1(9); (2) GST/HST registration and annual/quarterly filing elections under Excise Tax Act (ETA) ss. 240 and 251(1); (3) ETA s. 156 election for supplies between closely-related registrants (limited utility for LPs); (4) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6, effective 1 July 2022) afford no election but grandfathered arrangements entered into before 9 October 2019; (5) non-resident partners may elect under ITA ss. 115/128.1 in respect of taxable Canadian property dispositions. Alberta TIER offset registrations are administrative, not tax elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold). Mandatory registration if worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents soliciting orders in Canada are subject to the same threshold, and registration is compulsory upon exceedance even where all supplies are zero-rated. Below threshold, registration is voluntary to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI s. 5. ESG carbon auditing, advisory, and offset-brokerage services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI s. 7 where: (a) the recipient is a non-resident not registered under GST/HST; (b) the service is not rendered to an individual present in Canada; (c) the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property located in Canada at the time of supply; and (d) the recipient has no Canadian permanent establishment. Zero-rating fails if the audit or offset services pertain to Canadian assets or a Canadian-resident beneficiary. Carbon offset credits themselves are intangible personal property; their sale is taxable at 5% unless the credit is exported and delivered outside Canada, and brokerage commissions to non-residents qualify for zero-rating only where the s. 7 conditions are met. The LP must document the non-resident status and foreign destination in its records.

  • B13A Export Declaration (CBSA) or electronic Automated Export Declaration for goods above CAD 2,000 or controlled goods
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing movement of goods to the non-resident destination
  • GST/HST invoice endorsed with 'Zero-rated under Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act' and the LP's BN/RT program account
  • Signed services agreement confirming non-resident recipient, absence of Canadian PE, and absence of Canadian risk or assets
  • SWIFT MT103 wire receipt and foreign-exchange conversion record evidencing inward consideration from the non-resident
  • T5013 Partnership Information Return and foreign-partner allocation schedules
  • Carbon-offset chain-of-title registry extracts and Alberta TIER offset transfer confirmations where applicable

No real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. CRA promotes voluntary Peppol-compliant e-invoicing under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (Budget 2023); federal procurement is expected to require e-invoices by 2026. GST/HST returns must be transmitted electronically via GST/HST NETFILE where annual taxable revenue exceeds CAD 1.5M; registrants must retain machine-readable records for six years and produce them on CRA demand.

CRA audits the substantive non-resident nexus before accepting zero-rating. Inadequate substantiation triggers reassessment at 5% GST plus daily compound interest under ETA ss. 280-283. Civil penalties: ETA s. 285 — false statement or omission — greater of CAD 250 and 25% of net tax; ETA s. 285.1 — gross negligence — greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax sought to be evaded; ITA s. 163(2) — 50% of understated partnership income. Criminal evasion under ETA s. 327: up to CAD 500,000 fine and five years' imprisonment on indictment. Reassessment is open for four years (ETA s. 298; ITA s. 152), with no limitation period in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter here is the internal documentary-control convention the LP should observe to align with CRA record-keeping and audit expectations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both the current and capital account. There are no exchange-control approvals, no surrender requirements, and no formal prior-approval regime for cross-border capital flows. Profit remittance from an Alberta limited partnership to non-residents is unrestricted from a currency perspective but may attract Part XIII withholding tax on interest, royalties, management fees, dividends and certain other payments under the Income Tax Act; the partnership will issue a NR4 or T5013 forms, and non-resident limited partners should file a section 216 or composite return to address remittance hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts can be retained in any currency in Canadian or foreign bank accounts without forced repatriation or conversion. Businesses with monthly export proceeds in excess of C$0 (if a controlled foreign subsidiary is used) should still consider CRA reporting obligations, and all cash transactions at or above C$10,000 trigger FINTRAC large cash transaction reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with delegated registration and oversight functions for financial institutions performed by OSFI and anti-money-laundering supervision by FINTRAC; Canada maintains no separate exchange control administration such as a Foreign Exchange Authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not able to clear through Canadian payment rails unless they hold a Canadian money services business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC or obtain a limited-audience payment service provider licence under the Retail Payment Activities Act. In practice, most Canadian Tier 1 banks require direct account relationship at a Canadian branch, making pure offshore EMIs viable only as a correspondent payment gateway, not as the primary corporate treasury account. Legal constraints include DORA/KYC reciprocity, inability to accept CAD dollar deposits without a deposit-taking licence, and the absence of CDIC insurance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

An Alberta limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and does not pay corporate income tax at the partnership level. Taxable income is allocated to partners under the Income Tax Act (Canada). For an Alberta-resident taxable Canadian corporation receiving allocated income, the general combined federal and Alberta corporate income tax rate is approximately 23%: 15% federal plus 8% Alberta. An eligible Canadian-controlled private corporation may qualify for an approximately 11% combined small-business rate on eligible active business income within the applicable business limit: 9% federal plus 2% Alberta.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Canada generally imposes 25% withholding tax on taxable dividends paid or credited to non-residents under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. A Canadian LP normally does not pay dividends because it is not a corporation; distributions to partners are generally partnership distributions and must be analyzed under partnership, source, allocation, and withholding rules. A dividend paid by a Canadian corporate partner or subsidiary may qualify for a reduced treaty rate. Under the Canada-United States treaty, the rate is generally 5% where the beneficial owner is a qualifying company controlling at least 10% of the voting power, and 15% in other standard cases, subject to limitation-on-benefits and other treaty conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not use a general OECD-style monetary threshold that automatically triggers a separately named Local File or Master File for every taxpayer. Contemporaneous documentation is required under section 247 of the Income Tax Act for non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents where the statutory transfer-pricing conditions apply. The detailed documentation requirement generally applies where the aggregate value of the relevant transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the taxation year, subject to statutory exceptions and the specific transaction analysis. Canada requires an arm's-length adjustment under section 247 where conditions are not arm's length. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to a Canadian-resident ultimate parent or surrogate parent of an MNE group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million in the preceding fiscal year. A Canadian entity may also have a T106 filing obligation when the applicable aggregate non-arm's-length non-resident transaction threshold is exceeded.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone UAE-style or Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations applicable generally to an Alberta limited partnership. Substance is nevertheless material under the general anti-avoidance rule, transfer-pricing rules, treaty beneficial-ownership analysis, principal-purpose and treaty-abuse principles, partnership anti-avoidance rules, sham-transaction doctrine, and judicial tests concerning the taxpayer's actual legal and commercial relationships. The LP should maintain genuine decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capability, books and records, contractual control, client delivery evidence, market-based pricing, and commercially coherent allocation of risks and functions. A carbon-credit or ESG structure lacking personnel, control over functions, meaningful client activity, or evidence of services may be challenged as a sham, agency arrangement, conduit, or inappropriate income-allocation structure.
