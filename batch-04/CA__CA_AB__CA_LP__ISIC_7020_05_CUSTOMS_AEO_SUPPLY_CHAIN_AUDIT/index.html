Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal GST/HST, income tax and business number administration; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) - customs/export declarations. Alberta provincial income tax is administered by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act on behalf of Alberta Treasury Board and Finance; Alberta has no provincial sales tax and no separate PST/VAT registration. Filing portals: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online at canada.ca; GST/HST NETFILE.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine nexus - register the Alberta limited partnership under the Alberta Partnership Act (RSA 2000, c. P-3) before tax registration. Step 2: Obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online via Business Registration Online, by mail or by fax; the BN is the single federal TIN. Step 3: Activate the required program accounts under the same BN: 'RT' for GST/HST, 'RC' for corporate income tax (if the general partner is a corporation), 'RP' for payroll, and 'RM' for import/export where applicable. Step 4: A non-resident limited partnership or general partner applies using Form RC4591 (GST/HST Registration for Non-Residents) or through the CRA Non-Resident Account (NRA) service; CRA issues the BN immediately for online applicants. Step 5: No separate Alberta provincial tax ID is required because Alberta corporate income tax follows the CRA BN; a GST-registered partnership must display its BN/RT number on all invoices and may claim input tax credits (ITCs) on Canadian business inputs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Income Tax Act purposes; income and losses are allocated to partners and reported on Form T5013, with the general partner acting as the responsible representative. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is a distinct 'person' and registers separately under the Excise Tax Act. No entity-level federal or Alberta corporate income tax is imposed on the LP itself, and non-resident partners remain taxable only on their allocable share of Canadian-source income (subject to treaty relief).

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections and mechanisms: (1) partnership fiscal year-end election under ITA s. 249.1(6); (2) election under ITA s. 150(2.1) to file a complete partnership information return where the LP has non-resident members; (3) election under ITA s. 216(4) for non-resident partners to compute Part XIII tax on rental income; (4) treaty-based characterization of fiscally transparent entities under Canada-US Income Tax Convention Article IV(7), which allows CRA/IRS to determine whether LP income is attributable to a resident of a contracting state; (5) hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance rules (ITA ss. 18.4, 20.2, 93.1, 94.2, 95, 96 and 113) which deny or recharacterize deductions on imported and transparent mismatches; (6) the SIFT trust election is generally inapplicable to a private LP. All elections are made by timely filing the prescribed form or written notice with the CRA before the applicable due date.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - a person (including a non-resident LP) must register for GST/HST when the total consideration for taxable supplies made in Canada, other than exempt supplies and supplies outside the scope, exceeds C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four consecutive calendar quarters that end in the current quarter; voluntary registration is permitted before the threshold is met to claim ITCs on Canadian business inputs and to zero-rate export services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP's supply of AEO security audit, supply-chain compliance, and consulting services to a non-resident client qualifies for 0% GST as an exported service under Schedule VI, section 7 of the Excise Tax Act provided: (i) the recipient is a non-resident at the time the supply is made; (ii) the recipient is not registered for GST/HST and does not acquire the service for consumption in Canada; (iii) the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada; (iv) the service is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada when performed; and (v) the service is not rendered to an individual who is present in Canada. Digitally delivered audit reports and software-based data are zero-rated under Schedule VI, section 10 as intangible personal property supplied to a non-resident. If inspection kits or other goods are shipped out of Canada, Schedule VI, section 16 zero-rates the goods where proof of export is obtained and the goods are exported within the prescribed time. Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline for GST zero-rating; the 180-day window adopted in this dossier is a documentary substantiation and export financing convention. The LP should obtain a signed non-resident declaration, the foreign client TIN, the executed contract, invoices, and payment evidence before claiming the refund.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Canada Customs Coding Form B13A or CBSA Export Cargo Report transmitted via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) for physical goods movements
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (with Cargo Control Number) evidencing physical export from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank foreign-currency credit advice substantiating settlement of the export receivable by the non-resident client

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transaction-level API reporting mandate exists at the federal level or in Alberta. CRA accepts electronic invoices and electronic records under ETA s. 286.1 where reliability and legibility standards are met. A voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and PEPPOL standards is available but not mandatory. Registrants with total annual taxable supplies exceeding C$1,000,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or certified software; smaller registrants may file paper. No per-invoice fiscal code or QR stamp is required; CBSA export declarations for goods are transmitted through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES).

CRA scrutiny of zero-rated export claims requires retention of the full documentary bundle; failure to substantiate triggers reassessment of net tax at 5% GST plus arrears interest (ETA s. 280). Civil penalties include 50% of the net tax for knowing false statements (ETA s. 285) and a minimum C$250 penalty per false statement (ETA s. 286(1)); gross negligence and misrepresentation of refunds attract the same 50% of net tax penalty. Criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 327(1) is punishable on summary conviction by a fine up to C$25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 6 months, and on indictment by a fine up to C$100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. The normal reassessment period is four years under ETA s. 298, with no limitation where neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud is established. Export zero-rating fraud can additionally lead to CBSA verification actions, seizures under the Customs Act, revocation of Authorized Economic Operator (AEO) status, and application of the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

The Alberta limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and does not itself pay Canadian corporate income tax. Taxable income is allocated to partners. If income is earned through an Alberta corporation, the combined federal and Alberta general corporate rate is generally 23% (15% federal plus 8% Alberta); the combined small-business rate may be 11% on qualifying active business income within the applicable limits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic Canadian Part XIII withholding rate on dividends paid or credited to a non-resident is generally 25%. An LP distribution is not automatically a dividend. The tax treatment depends on the character of the underlying amount, the partnership's status, the recipient's status and applicable Canadian source rules. A Canadian corporation paying an eligible dividend to a treaty-resident beneficial owner commonly applies a treaty rate of 5% where the ownership and other conditions are met, and 15% in other qualifying cases; the applicable treaty must be checked.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 return is generally due within five months after the partnership fiscal period-end. T5013 slips are generally due within three months after the partnership fiscal period-end. A partnership that is required to file must generally have a CRA partnership account and comply with electronic-filing requirements. The fiscal period must comply with Canadian partnership fiscal-period rules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Register before commencing activities requiring a CRA account. Corporate tax balances are generally due two months after the corporate year-end, or three months for eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations meeting the statutory conditions; the return is generally due six months after year-end. These corporate deadlines do not convert an LP into a corporation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation where non-arm's-length cross-border transactions are subject to the Canadian transfer-pricing rules. Canada does not use a single universal local-file threshold equivalent to all OECD jurisdictions. A practical statutory reporting threshold commonly relevant to T106 filing is aggregate non-arm's-length non-resident transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000, subject to transaction-category rules and exceptions. Canadian contemporaneous documentation must be prepared by the filing due date and provided to CRA within three months of a written request. Canada generally does not impose a standalone OECD-style Master File requirement on every taxpayer; country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational enterprise groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million in the preceding fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no general standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to the UAE ESR rules. CRA may nevertheless challenge arrangements under transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance rule, specific anti-avoidance provisions, treaty anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership principles, sham doctrine, purpose-based treaty provisions and common-law substance-over-form principles. The LP should maintain genuine Canadian functions, decision-making, personnel or contractors, premises or operational capability, risk control, commercial contracts, banking records, time records and evidence that fees reflect services actually rendered. A low-risk audit business with no personnel, no operating capability and circular related-party charges is a material audit and recharacterization risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, arrears interest, civil gross-negligence or false-statement penalties, and prosecution under the Income Tax Act or Excise Tax Act. Tax-evasion convictions can result in fines of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years under the Income Tax Act, subject to the applicable offence and judicial determination.

Director personal liability: An LP has general and limited partners rather than directors in the corporate sense. General partners, authorized signatories and persons who participated in the offence may face prosecution, personal fines, imprisonment, restitution and professional or immigration consequences. Corporate directors may face statutory director-liability assessments for certain unremitted source deductions and GST/HST, subject to due-diligence defences. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty but may arise through criminal process, bail conditions, enforcement or immigration action.
Failure to withhold or remit non-resident tax

Statutory sanction: The payer may be liable for the unwithheld amount, applicable penalties, prescribed interest and information-return penalties. CRA may deny treaty reductions and assess the domestic rate. Deliberate non-compliance may lead to prosecution.

Director personal liability: Responsible corporate directors may be assessed personally for certain source-deduction and GST/HST remittances under statutory director-liability rules. General partners and persons exercising effective control may face civil recovery, prosecution and imprisonment where conduct is knowing or fraudulent.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess income under section 247 of the Income Tax Act, deny deductions, impose a transfer-pricing adjustment penalty generally equal to 10% of the adjustment where statutory conditions are met, and charge interest. Corresponding adjustments are not automatic.

Director personal liability: The principal exposure is normally entity-level tax and penalties, but individuals may face personal civil or criminal exposure for knowingly participating in false reporting, fraud, document destruction or conspiracy.
Shell partnership, sham or abusive treaty structure

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard sham steps, apply the general anti-avoidance rule, deny treaty benefits, reallocate income, impose withholding and reassess penalties and interest. The partnership may also face provincial registration, civil and contractual consequences.

Director personal liability: General partners and controlling persons may face civil restitution, tracing claims, injunctions, director or officer assessments where applicable, prosecution for fraud or tax offences, and imprisonment for proven criminal conduct. Corporate veil piercing is fact-dependent and is not automatic.
Canada Revenue Agency recordkeeping and information obstruction

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties, court-enforced compliance orders, adverse evidentiary inferences, reassessment using estimates and prosecution may apply under the Income Tax Act, Excise Tax Act or related legislation.

Director personal liability: Individuals who direct or authorize obstruction may be prosecuted personally. Civil recovery and imprisonment are possible for contempt or statutory offences; travel restrictions may arise only through applicable criminal or court processes.
Customs, import-export and AEO integrity violations

Statutory sanction: The Canada Border Services Agency may issue assessments, penalties under the Administrative Monetary Penalty System, seize or detain goods, suspend or cancel trusted-trader privileges, and pursue prosecution under customs or criminal legislation. GST and customs duties may be reassessed with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners and responsible officers may be personally prosecuted for knowing participation, false declarations, smuggling or fraud. Conviction can result in fines, forfeiture, imprisonment and restrictions on operating or importing activities.
Anti-money-laundering and proceeds-of-crime exposure

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC compliance penalties, transaction reporting consequences, account freezes, forfeiture, criminal prosecution under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act or Criminal Code, substantial fines and imprisonment may apply.

Director personal liability: Controlling persons, general partners and participants may face personal fines, restitution, asset forfeiture, prosecution and imprisonment. Travel bans or passport restrictions may be imposed through criminal proceedings or court orders, not as an automatic administrative penalty.
Payroll and worker-classification non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of source deductions, CPP and EI, interest, penalties, employer-liability assessments and employment-law claims. Gross-negligence or intentional conduct can materially increase penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate employer may face personal statutory assessments for unremitted source deductions, subject to due-diligence rules. General partners and controlling persons may face personal liability where they directed or knowingly participated in the default.
