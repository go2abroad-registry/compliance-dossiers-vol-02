Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Partnership Act (Alberta) imposes no minimum paid-up capital or statutory escrow/blocked deposit account for a limited partnership. Capital contributions are fixed exclusively by the Limited Partnership Agreement and may be funded in cash or property by wire transfer; the registered office in Alberta must be maintained at all times.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety: compliance with the Alberta Fire Code (National Fire Code – 2023 Alberta Edition) including municipal fire safety plan approval for the office leasehold. Structural/Occupancy: building occupancy permit under the Safety Codes Act (Alberta) and, where applicable, structural load verification by a licensed engineer for high-density server racks or heavy equipment. Occupational Health: compliance with the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Alberta) and OHS Code – hazard assessment, first aid arrangements, and emergency response plan; a documented OHS programme is mandatory for employers with 20 or more workers.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST, payroll and income tax administration; Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html) and Represent a Client for non-resident entities. In Alberta, provincial partnership registration and corporate registry services are administered by Service Alberta through the Alberta Corporate Registration System (CORES).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta Limited Partnership claiming GST zero-rating on exported CAD/BIM services, the LP must first be properly created and registered under Alberta's Partnership Act. The LP or its designated partner then obtains a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA by enrolling through Business Registration Online (BRO), by mail using Form RC148, or by telephone. The application must include the partnership's legal name, Alberta registration number, address, fiscal year, and the completion of the GST/HST account (RT) designation. Non-resident partners must also provide the CRA with identity documents and a Canadian contact or agent if required. If the LP has no physical presence in Canada, CRA may still issue a BN where the LP is carrying on a business in Canada through a deemed supply nexus. After the BN/RT account is active, the LP must file GST/HST returns electronically or by paper and maintain all supporting documentation for zero-rated exports.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible – An Alberta Limited Partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act: the partnership is not a taxable entity; income, gains, losses, deductions and credits flow through to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. However, for GST/HST purposes, the LP is treated as a separate registrant person under section 123 of the Excise Tax Act. With respect to foreign partners, the Canada–U.S. tax treaty and Canadian domestic hybrid rules may affect withholding and permanent establishment characterization. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – An Alberta Limited Partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act: the partnership is not a taxable entity; income, gains, losses, deductions and credits flow through to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. However, for GST/HST purposes, the LP is treated as a separate registrant person under section 123 of the Excise Tax Act. With respect to foreign partners, the Canada–U.S. tax treaty and Canadian domestic hybrid rules may affect withholding and permanent establishment characterization. The LP itself remains a distinct GST/HST registrant for all taxable supplies made in or from Canada.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a United States-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. Entity classification is determined by provincial partnership law and CRA administrative interpretation. The LP and its partners may nonetheless make specific elections: (1) under subsection 97(2) of the Income Tax Act to transfer property to a partnership on a rollover basis at adjusted cost base; (2) under subsection 96(3) of the Income Tax Act to compute partnership income at the partnership level; and (3) for GST/HST, an election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act to have a qualifying member or a designated member report and remit GST/HST on behalf of the partnership. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules in sections 18.4 to 18.6 and subsection 212.3(2) of the Income Tax Act may recharacterize certain payments or deny deductions if a partnership interest is structured with hybrid or fiscally transparent attributes inconsistent with Canadian tax policy.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: a person, including an Alberta LP, is a small supplier and is not required to register for GST/HST unless the value of its worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada (including those of associated persons and the preceding four-calendar-quarter lookback) exceeds CAD 30,000. A non-resident supplying CAD/BIM services into Canada may have mandatory registration if it is carrying on a tax supply nexus in Canada and exceeds this threshold. Voluntary registration is available to facilitate claiming input tax credits and zero-rating of exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An Alberta LP supplying engineering, CAD, BIM, and structural drafting services to non-resident clients is eligible for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V (supplies to non-residents) of the Excise Tax Act where: (a) the recipient is a non-resident person; (b) the service is not supplied to an individual physically present in Canada; (c) the service is not performed in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed; and (d) the service is not a service that is to be performed wholly or partly in Canada in circumstances where the supply is not a supply of an advisory, consulting, or professional service made to a non-resident. Physical exports of drawings, models, or other tangible deliverables shipped out of Canada may also qualify under Schedule VI, Part VI or Part VII, provided the seller must export them (or cause them to be exported) as a condition of the supply. The supplier must retain contemporaneous commercial documentation, including invoices, contracts, and bank settlement evidence, to substantiate zero-rating at the time of audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A or electronic CAED), where applicable for physical goods or media
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / multimodal carriage document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange bank confirmation evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Executed contract and commercial invoices identifying the non-resident purchaser, the services supplied, and the place of supply
  • Delivery and performance records, including BIM file transmission logs, engineering or drafting work product, and evidence of creation outside Canada or effective use outside Canada

No national real-time clearance or mandatory API fiscal stamping regime exists in Canada. The CRA does not operate a continuous transaction control or digital stamping system. Suppliers may issue electronic invoices provided they are readable, complete, and retain the required disclosure elements under subsection 221(1) of the ETA and section 286 of the ETA. Invoices must identify the supplier, recipient, the date, the supply amount, and the GST/HST charged or a declaration that the supply is zero-rated. EDI and electronic record-keeping systems are acceptable if accessible to CRA on request. For exported services, supplementary records must establish non-resident recipient status and offshore place-of-supply.

The CRA may scrutinize export zero-rating claims through a GST/HST audit, verifying that the recipient is truly a non-resident, the services were not performed for Canadian real/personal property, and payment was received from abroad. If zero-rating is denied, the LP will be reassessed for 5% GST on the full invoice amount, plus interest under section 280 of the ETA. For false or negligent statements, a gross negligence penalty under section 285 of the ETA may be imposed in an amount up to 50% of the unreported tax. Fraudulent misrepresentation, falsification of documents, or deliberate concealment can lead to criminal prosecution under section 326 of the ETA and section 239 of the Income Tax Act, with fines and imprisonment. The normal four-year reassessment period may be extended indefinitely where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation, omitted material facts, or committed fraud in filing the return. The LP remains liable to repay any input tax credits claimed on zero-rated supplies if supporting export documentation fails audit, and the partners may be jointly and severally liable for penalties and interest attributable to fraudulent or negligent partnership filings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions under the Bank of Canada Act. Profit remittances to foreign partners are permitted without prior approval but are subject to 25% withholding tax on dividends and mandatory FINTRAC reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Capital account transactions require no surrender but must comply with anti-money laundering and beneficial ownership verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement for surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts. All export transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC within prescribed timelines if involving cash or large-value movements. No fixed conversion ratios apply; repatriation occurs freely upon compliance with reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
