Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; portal: My Business Account at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html

E-government filing portal

Register through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), Form RC1, or by calling 1-800-959-5525. The Limited Partnership obtains a Business Number (BN) and, where required, a GST/HST program account. Non-resident partners and foreign LPs should use CRA's non-resident registration procedures; an Alberta-based LP may need a Canadian representative or address for CRA correspondence. Alberta imposes no separate provincial sales tax registration, but the LP must report GST at the federal level.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. A Limited Partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act. Limited elections available include partnership fiscal period elections and certain foreign tax credit / hybrid mismatch arrangements; however, no election alters the LP's partnership classification for Canadian tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters; exceeding the threshold mandates GST/HST registration

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported and delivered outside Canada may be zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act if the supplier retains documentary proof of export. Services, including factory inspection, testing, and quality-control activities, may be zero-rated where supplied to a non-resident who is not present in Canada at the time of supply and where the service is not directly related to Canadian real or tangible property nor consumed in Canada.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) or applicable electronic export declaration via CARM
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent shipping documentation
  • Proof of export (customs stamp, carrier certificate, terminal receipt)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire evidence (for FX repatriation)

No federal or Alberta real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoicing API. CRA permits electronic invoices provided full integrity and accessibility. Invoicing to federal departments is required via the Federal Government E-Invoicing Solution using PEPPOL BIS 3.0; this is a federal procurement requirement, not a tax clearance mandate.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year period, or beyond if misrepresentation or fraud is established. If export zero-rating is not substantiated, the GST becomes payable, payable interest under ETA s. 280 is assessed, and penalties may apply under ETA s. 285 (false statements) and Income Tax Act s. 163(2) for gross negligence, which can reach 50% of the tax evaded. Fraudulent schemes may also lead to criminal prosecution and seizure of documents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully and freely convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Profit remittance, partnership distributions, capital repatriation, loan repayment, and redemption of capital contributions may be executed without central-bank approval or documentary licensing. The only practical constraints are tax-related: Part XIII withholding of 25% (treaty-reducible) applies to the Canadian-source income distributed to non-resident LP partners, with NR4 and T106 reporting. Under the Investment Canada Act, a foreign partner injection into the Alberta LP is typically a notifiable (non-reviewable) acquisition for an ISIC 7120/NAICS 541380 agency well below the prescribed enterprise-value review threshold; no cultural-business review applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation window for export proceeds or service-inspection fees. The LP may retain USD, EUR, CNY (via offshore CNH), or CAD as earned. For CRA income measurement, service revenue and cash balances must be translated to CAD using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate (or a reasonable consistent rate), and no currency surrender certificate is required at year-end.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central bank; OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) as prudential supervisor for federally chartered banks; FINTRAC as the financial intelligence unit administering the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada does not operate a foreign-exchange control licensing regime; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and neither the Bank of Canada nor Finance Canada issues permits for cross-border or FX transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise (Wise Business, formerly TransferWise) — feasibility: HIGH for operational disbursements and low/mid-value FX conversion; LOW for capital escrow or commercial LC issuance. Wise operates in Canada as a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business, not a Schedule I bank; it offers fully remote onboarding for the Alberta LP with multi-currency account details (CAD, USD, EUR, GBP, CNY-covered corridors). Legal constraints: no CDIC deposit insurance, no escrow/trust account capability for partnership capital, and Wise may refuse or delay onboarding if the QC agency's subcontractor payment pattern shows mixed beneficiary names across high-risk jurisdictions without underlying contracts; therefore Wise should be used strictly for sub-CAD 10,000 operational payments and reconciliation, while the chartered bank remains the primary clearing rail.
