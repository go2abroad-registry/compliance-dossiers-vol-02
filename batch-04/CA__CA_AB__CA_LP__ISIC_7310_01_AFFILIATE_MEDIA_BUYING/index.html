Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalised, freely floating currency regime with complete current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar is freely tradable and no statutory restrictions exist on profit remittance, foreign-currency bank balances, or dividend/partnership distribution repatriation. Structural friction arises exclusively from tax and reporting mechanics: (i) distributions by the Alberta limited partnership to non-resident limited partners must be split into return of capital (exempt from withholding) and Canadian-source income (Part XIII withholding of 25%, generally reduced to 15% or 0% under the Canada-US tax treaty where the US partner provides a valid IRS Form W-8BEN-E and meets treaty-limitation tests); (ii) every cross-border electronic fund transfer of CAD 10,000 or more is automatically reported to FINTRAC without prior authorization; and (iii) the bank applies Enhanced Due Diligence ('Medium-Risk Enhanced' tier for performance media buying) to each capital call, requiring source-of-funds evidence before value is credited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement, statutory repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export proceeds in Canada. A resident business may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in USD or any convertible currency. Legal obligations are limited to: (a) preserving ledgers and supporting documents under Income Tax Act s. 230; (b) computing all Canadian tax attributes in CAD functional currency; and (c) reporting electronic funds transfers over CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC. For the media-buying model, USD receipts from US affiliate networks should be swept daily from payment gateways into a USD operating account and periodically converted at a board-approved trigger rate to stabilise CAD net income.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with supervisory coordination by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated banks, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border funds monitoring; the Department of Finance Canada holds exchange policy jurisdiction. Canada requires no exchange-control approval for any FX transaction.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

No entity-level income tax applies to a limited partnership (CA_LP); income is allocated to partners. Combined headline corporate rate in Alberta for corporate partners: federal 15% + Alberta provincial 8% = 23%. For Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) partners qualifying for the small business deduction, the combined rate is reduced to 11% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (as of 2024).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for taxable dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced by an income tax treaty. For an LP, ordinary profit distributions allocated as business income are not subject to Part XIII withholding if the non-resident partner is taxable under Part I on the Canadian-source business income; however, amounts paid by the LP to non-residents that are characterized as dividends, management fees, interest, royalties, or rental income are subject to the appropriate 25% statutory withholding regime.

Use the foreign TIN (e.g., US EIN, UK UTR) and, if treaty relief is claimed, attach a valid Certificate of Residence / TRC issued by the partner's home tax authority. Late filing of T5013: CAD 25 per day, minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500; for a subsequent or knowing failure, CAD 100 per day up to CAD 25,000. Failure to issue T5013 slips: CAD 25 per slip per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500). Late T2 filing for corporate partners: 5% of balance owing plus 1% per full month (up to 12 months).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for mandatory filing of Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's-Length Transactions with Non-Residents); contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File/Master File) is expected for controlled transactions of any materiality when CRA audits; Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Alberta and Canada have no Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to those in zero-tax jurisdictions. Substance is instead enforced through: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, (ii) transfer-pricing recharacterization under section 247, and (iii) treaty anti-abuse / limitation-on-benefits clauses. The LP must maintain demonstrable commercial substance: physical office or management presence in Alberta, active digital media buying operations, real client contracts, payroll, and operating bank accounts in the entity's own name.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment (up to 2 years on summary conviction). Civil consequences: full reassessment plus interest, gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under subsection 163(2), and suspension of CRA programs.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the LP is jointly and severally liable for all partnership obligations and civil tax penalties. A corporate general partner's directors and officers may be personally assessed for unremitted source deductions or Part XIII withholdings under director liability provisions; upon laying of criminal charges, travel bans, border flags, and personal seizure orders can be imposed.
Abusive Transfer Pricing / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing reassessment under subsection 247(2) plus penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment under subsection 247(3). If the arrangement is also abusive, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 can nullify the tax benefit and impose a penalty equal to 25% of the benefit, plus interest and compounding late payment penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner can be liable for the LP's transfer-pricing penalties where they knowingly participated in or accepted the artificial arrangement. General partners are jointly and severally liable for all tax debts of the partnership, and a non-resident director may face travel restrictions, asset freezes, and personal civil enforcement during a CRA or Public Prosecution Service investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering / FINTRAC Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), administrative monetary penalties can reach up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for entities and significant penalties for individuals; criminal enforcement for knowingly failing to report or obstructing FINTRAC can result in imprisonment of up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the general partner can be personally named in FINTRAC penalty proceedings where they directed or acquiesced in AML breaches. Federal enforcement may also lead to seizure orders, restrictions on international wire transfers, removal of business banking relationships, and travel bans pending criminal trial.
