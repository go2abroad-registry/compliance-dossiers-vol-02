Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); registration and account services are available through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portals. Alberta provincial tax administration is handled by Alberta Treasury Board and Finance; Alberta does not impose a general provincial sales tax.

E-government filing portal

Obtain an Alberta partnership registration and Alberta registry number as applicable before applying for federal tax accounts. Apply to the CRA for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) and the required program accounts, including a GST/HST account where registration is required or elected. A Canadian representative may submit the application through BRO if eligible; otherwise, use the CRA's applicable non-resident business-registration process. Provide the partnership agreement, Alberta registration particulars, business and mailing addresses, partners' identity and residency details, and an authorized contact. A BN is not itself a GST/HST account number. A non-resident partnership carrying on business in Canada or having Canadian-source income may also have Canadian partnership information-return and withholding obligations; a Canadian BN does not determine treaty residence or eliminate those obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election for an Alberta limited partnership. A partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes: it computes income at partnership level, but partners include their allocated shares under the Income Tax Act. Specific statutory elections may apply to particular transactions, including an eligible subsection 97(2) rollover on a transfer of property to a Canadian partnership; these do not elect entity classification. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules may deny or adjust deductions or inclusions in specified cross-border arrangements and do not provide a general classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in Canada by the person and associated persons, generally measured over a single calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. A person exceeding the applicable small-supplier threshold must register and collect GST/HST as prescribed. Non-residents carrying on business in Canada may be required to register regardless of the small-supplier threshold; the facts and applicable registration rules must be assessed. Alberta's applicable general rate is 5% GST, with no Alberta provincial sales tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying exported goods may be zero-rated where the statutory export conditions are met, including applicable restrictions on delivery, use, or acquisition in Canada; retain evidence that the goods were exported. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated only when a specific Excise Tax Act provision applies and its conditions are satisfied. The general exported-services rule has exclusions, including specified services relating to real property in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, and services performed in Canada in specified circumstances. Digital, creator-management, advertising, and agency services require contract-by-contract analysis of the recipient, service, place of performance, and statutory exclusions; a foreign customer or foreign-currency payment alone does not establish zero-rating. A registered person may generally claim input tax credits for eligible GST/HST incurred in commercial activities, subject to documentation and statutory restrictions.

  • For exported goods where required: CBSA export declaration and proof of export, including applicable Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) records or other authorized reporting evidence.
  • Commercial invoices, purchase orders, contracts, and shipping records identifying the goods, purchaser, destination, and transaction.
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier records, or other carrier documentation evidencing shipment and delivery outside Canada.
  • For exported services: executed agreement, invoices, evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and address, service-delivery records, and evidence supporting the statutory zero-rating provision relied upon.
  • Bank, payment-processor, or platform settlement records may support payment tracing but are not generally an Inward Remittance Certificate requirement under Canadian GST/HST law.

Canada has no general GST/HST real-time e-invoice clearance, government invoice-stamping, or API pre-approval mandate for ordinary businesses. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices or receipts containing prescribed information when required, retain adequate books and records, and file returns through CRA channels. Separate platform, payment-provider, or procurement-system requirements do not constitute a general tax-clearance mandate.

Canada has no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The CRA may reassess incorrectly zero-rated supplies, deny unsupported input tax credits or refunds, and assess tax, interest, and applicable penalties. False statements, omissions, or gross negligence may attract penalties under the Excise Tax Act, and deliberate tax evasion may result in prosecution. Exporters should retain records supporting the supply's characterization, recipient status, export or place-of-supply evidence, and claimed credits for the applicable statutory retention period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both current and capital accounts under Canada's IMF Article VIII obligations. No exchange-control approvals, foreign-investment licences, or profit-repatriation registrations apply to partnership distributions, management fees, or capital repayments. Non-resident limited partners are, however, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at the statutory rate of 25%, generally reduced to 5% to 15% under an applicable tax treaty, and to CRS/FATCA reporting; the Alberta LP may pay out in CAD, USD, or other currencies without central bank authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no conversion ratio for foreign-currency receipts earned from creator-management, advertising, or MCN services. All export proceeds may be retained in CAD or any foreign currency. Mandatory compliance is limited to: (i) CBSA cross-border currency declaration for physical cash movements of CAD 10,000 or more; and (ii) FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting and travel-rule record-keeping for any wire or transfer of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); banking supervision is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing administration is performed by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Wise Business (Wise Canada Inc., an FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business) is viable as a secondary disbursement rail, USD conversion utility, and micro-payment engine for TikTok creator payouts. It is not a substitute for a Tier 1 Canadian bank because: (i) funds are pooled and not CDIC-insured; (ii) no committed overdraft, commercial letter of credit, or scalable escrow service is offered; (iii) large or repetitive payment flows may trigger Wise's Acceptable Use Policy review absent documented creator contracts; (iv) MSB counterparties must be disclosed to, and accepted by, the primary Tier 1 bank; and (v) all CAD clearing must ultimately settle through a Canadian Payments Association participant.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections from non-resident partners must arrive via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire for USD-origin funds) to the designated Tier 1 Canadian bank. The payment must use ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPT' for capital transfer or 'FDI' for direct investment, state the LP's full legal name and account number, and include complete FINTRAC travel-rule originator data. Before first credit, the LP must deliver a fully executed contribution package comprising the partnership agreement, capital call notice, General Partner resolution, and UBO source-of-wealth files; otherwise the bank may place a hold or return the funds. No Bank of Canada approval is required; USD receipts may be maintained in a USD-value account and converted only under a valid FX order governed by the LP's treasury policy.Outbound creator distributions and trade settlements clear through four rails: (i) ACSS batch EFT for domestic Canadian creators; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border creator remittances and licensing settlements, using purpose code 'USTR' with a creator reference narrative; (iii) Wise B2B API for non-resident creator micro-payments below CAD 5,000, with full originator and beneficiary travel-rule data; and (iv) commercial letters of credit (documentary or standby) reserved exclusively for content-IP acquisition transactions, issued by the Tier 1 bank with 100% collateral coverage. All outbound wires are subject to dual-authorization controls, a standing FX limit, and a business-purpose narrative embedded in the payment instruction.Deliverable CAD/USD FX forwards (non-speculative, tenor up to 12 months) executed under a master treasury agreement or a 1992 ISDA Master Agreement.FX swaps (CAD/USD) for managing creator payout cycles and conversion timing without extending interest-rate risk.Cross-currency swaps (CAD/USD) permitted only with a Canadian chartered bank counterparty and only for funded balance-sheet hedging of operating receivables.Currency options, collars, and knockout forwards to cap conversion costs on high-volume TikTok payout flows.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not standard for CAD because CAD is a fully deliverable currency; where exotic-currency hedging is required, NDFs may be executed through a registered Canadian bank's offshore desk, subject to an ISDA Credit Support Annex and non-speculative use.

25% for non-resident dividends from Canadian corporations; for LP distributions, withholding tax on non-resident partner's share of income at 25% unless reduced by treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5 million in cross-border transactions with non-residents or CAD 50 million gross revenue (per CRA guidance).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and substance requirements for treaty benefits apply. The CRA may scrutinize transactions lacking economic substance, especially for treaty shopping or aggressive tax planning.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239) with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under s. 227.1 if they fail to withhold or remit. For LP, general partners are jointly and severally liable for partnership obligations, including tax debts.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the partnership under GAAR, reassess partners, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax benefit. Criminal charges for fraud if intentional.

Director personal liability: General partners may face personal liability for all partnership debts, including tax liabilities, if the partnership is deemed a sham. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed under the Income Tax Act.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities per violation. Criminal penalties for money laundering under the Criminal Code: imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined or imprisoned if they knowingly participate in or fail to prevent AML violations. Travel bans and seizure of assets may occur.
