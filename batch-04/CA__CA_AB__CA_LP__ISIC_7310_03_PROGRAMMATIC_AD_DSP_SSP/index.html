Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; business registration portal: Business Registration Online (bro-cra.gc.ca) and My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). For Alberta, the Alberta Corporate Tax service is administered federally by CRA, with no separate provincial sales tax authority.

E-government filing portal

Foreign limited partnership (LP) must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' (paper) or via CRA Business Registration Online. Non-resident partnerships without a Canadian share capital or physical presence may register by mail/fax; attach Form RC1 and supporting documents. Alberta LP must also be registered with the Alberta Corporate Registry (Corporate Registry Service) and file an Alberta Partnership Annual Return. The BN serves as the statutory tax ID for GST/HST, payroll, and corporate tax accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have check-the-box elections. An LP is fiscally transparent for income tax; however, it may elect a non-calendar-year fiscal period under ITA s. 249.1(5) if certain conditions apply. For GST/HST, the partnership is a separate 'person' and may make elections under ETA s. 156 (closely related entities) and s. 177 (partnership fiscal year) if eligible. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 may disallow deductions for certain payments recharacterized by foreign jurisdictions; an election under ss. 18.2(4)/18.3(3) may be available to reduce double taxation. For non-resident partners, a s. 216 election may be used to file a net rental income return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident is not required to register unless the threshold is exceeded or the person is a platform operator required to collect GST/HST. For foreign digital services, a special domestic supplies threshold of CAD 30,000 may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI Part V, supplies of services to a non-resident recipient are generally zero-rated if the service is not performed in Canada and is not an excepted supply (e.g., advertising services may qualify if provided to a non-resident for offshore audience; the supplier must retain proof that the recipient is non-resident and the supply is for consumption outside Canada). Goods exported from Canada in accordance with the Customs Act and ETA s. 7 are zero-rated. The supplier must hold evidence satisfying the 'official or prescribed evidence' requirements under ETA s. 45 and Schedule VI Part V, ss. 7-18. Standard documentary proof includes a commercial invoice, contract, and proof of export/payment.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada does not require real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government e-invoicing. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns via CRA's GST/HST NETFILE is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,000,000. E-invoicing is voluntary and must comply with GAAP/CTA standards; PEPPOL and Pan-Canadian Trust Delivery Network standards are emerging.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated supply within the normal assessment period of four years from the date of the return; where misrepresentation, neglect or fraud is involved, the limitation period is waived. Penalties for false zero-rating claims include Excise Tax Act s. 285(1) gross negligence penalty of 50% of the net GST understated, s. 280 interest, and s. 327(1) criminal sanctions for willful evasion (up to CAD 10,000 or double the amount evaded, plus imprisonment). Retrospective disallowance of input tax credits and reversal of refunds are also applied, with no sunset for fraudulent claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada generally permits unrestricted receipt and remittance of investment capital, service revenue, royalties, interest, dividends, partnership distributions, and sale proceeds, subject to tax, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, beneficial-ownership, transfer-pricing, withholding, and financial-institution verification requirements. An Alberta limited partnership normally does not require prior foreign-exchange approval to receive non-resident contributions or remit distributable cash. Non-resident partners may be subject to Canadian withholding or other tax obligations depending on the character and source of income, applicable treaty relief, partnership activities, and whether the partnership carries on business in Canada. Banks may delay or reject payments pending source-of-funds evidence, beneficial-owner verification, tax documentation, sanctions screening, or explanation of unusually complex payment flows. Cross-border related-party loans, guarantees, management fees, royalties, and other connected-party payments should be supported by written agreements, arm's-length pricing, transfer-pricing records, and applicable Canadian tax filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general deadline requiring conversion of foreign-currency receipts. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the entity's commercial discretion. The partnership should maintain complete invoices, contracts, delivery or performance evidence, customer identity records, receipt reconciliations, and accounting records. Foreign-currency balances and transactions must be recorded under applicable Canadian accounting policies and tax rules. Large cash transactions, suspicious transactions, and certain international electronic funds transfers may trigger FINTRAC reporting by the reporting financial institution; these reporting obligations do not constitute a surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general exchange-control administration requiring routine approval for ordinary commercial foreign-exchange transactions. Applicable oversight also includes the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering compliance, the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for tax reporting, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated financial institutions, and applicable Canadian securities regulators for derivatives and market-conduct matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment operations and low-to-medium as a substitute for a Canadian bank account. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment service provider may support foreign-currency collections, virtual accounts, card settlement, and international transfers only within the provider's licensing perimeter and risk policy. It is not automatically a Canadian deposit-taking institution, cannot represent itself as a Canadian bank, and may not provide Canadian-dollar deposit, payment, or money-services functionality without the required Canadian authorization or registration. A non-Canadian money services business serving Canadian persons may have FINTRAC registration and reporting obligations. Customer funds may be safeguarded rather than insured deposits, and account availability can be restricted for Canadian limited partnerships, non-resident partners, advertising-platform flows, high-volume pass-through activity, or third-party settlement. Use a Canadian bank as the primary treasury account and an approved fintech as a documented secondary rail after confirming licensing, safeguarding, settlement, sanctions, chargeback, data, and account-termination terms.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route partner contributions and shareholder or related-party funding through an account held in the legal name of the Alberta limited partnership, using SWIFT or correspondent-bank transfers with the exact legal name, Canadian account details, originating institution, remitter identity, and clear payment narrative such as "limited partnership capital contribution" or "partner loan under agreement dated [date]." Canada generally does not impose standardized foreign-exchange purpose codes for ordinary commercial wires; the bank may nevertheless request an internal purpose classification and supporting documents. Pre-notify the bank for material or unusual receipts. Match each receipt to the executed contribution, subscription, or loan agreement; retain the SWIFT confirmation, remitter bank details, source-of-funds evidence, and ledger posting; and avoid third-party funding unless the relationship and agency basis are documented. Confirm whether the transfer creates Canadian withholding, thin-capitalization, transfer-pricing, foreign-affiliate, partnership allocation, or information-reporting consequences. Do not route capital through personal accounts, undisclosed omnibus wallets, or unrelated collection accounts.Use SWIFT MT103 or the bank's current ISO 20022 equivalent for identified supplier, publisher, technology, cloud, media, and professional-service payments. 25% under Part XIII for dividends paid by a Canadian corporation; for the Alberta LP, the non-resident partner's Canadian business income share is generally taxed net under Part I where the LP has a Canadian permanent establishment, but passive or guaranteed allocations and distributions that are treaty-unprotected attract Part XIII at 25%, treaty-reduced to 15% (portfolio) or 5% (foreign corporate shareholder holding directly 10% or more of voting rights).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

T5013 must be filed within 5 months after the partnership fiscal year-end (e.g., May 31 for a December 31 year-end); T2 return due 6 months after the corporate partner's fiscal year-end; personal partner returns due April 30 (or June 15 for self-employed).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 mandatory when aggregate non-arm's-length transactions with a single non-resident exceed C$1,000,000 per taxation year; CRA administrative guidance expects Master File and Local File contemporaneous documentation where Canadian gross revenues exceed C$10,000,000 or the group is in the largest segment; Country-by-Country Report required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least C$1.25 billion.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substance is decisive for: (i) LP residence under the central management and control test; (ii) treaty benefits under the Canada–US Limitation on Benefits clause or the MLI Principal Purpose Test; and (iii) GAAR application under ITA 245. The Alberta LP must maintain an active operational presence—local employees, board meetings, contracts, servers, bank accounts, and real commercial risk. A letter-box LP acting as a conduit for non-resident owners will be re-characterized and stripped of treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction: fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; indictment: fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years under ITA 239; civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of unreported tax under ITA 163(2).

Director personal liability: General partners (and directors of a corporate general partner) are jointly and severally liable; directors who knowingly sign or assent to false filings face personal fines, CRA asset seizure, travel bans, and extradition for serious tax offences.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under ITA 247 to the arm's-length price; penalty of 10% of the net upward adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5 million and 10% of gross revenue; interest accrued from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors may be sued for negligence and misfeasance by co-partners and creditors; knowing participation in mispricing schemes triggers personal liability under the Income Tax Act and possible criminal prosecution.
Shell Company / GAAR / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR disallowance under ITA 245, reclassification of distributions as deemed dividends or royalties subject to 25% Part XIII, interest, and penalties; denial of relief under the Limitation on Benefits or Principal Purpose Test; gross negligence penalty of up to 50%.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors may be assessed personally for unremitted withholding under ITA 227.1/227.2; scheme promoters face ITA 163.2 tax-planning penalties up to C$100,000 or 100% of gross fees from the arrangement.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to C$1,000,000 for individuals and C$2,000,000 for entities per violation under the PCMLTFA; criminal prosecution and asset forfeiture for money laundering under Criminal Code s.462.31 with imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and general partners with knowledge of unreported suspicious flows face criminal collusion charges, personal disgorgement, and imprisonment; FINTRAC compliance orders may prohibit management roles.
GST/HST and Payroll Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: GST assessment plus 6% annual prescribed interest compounded daily; late remittance penalty of 6% of the amount plus 1% per month; payroll penalties up to 10% of unremitted amounts, increased to 20% for repeated failures.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are personally liable for unremitted GST/HST and source deductions under ETA s.323 and ITA s.227.1; CRA may seize personal bank accounts, garnish personal wages, and obtain civil judgments.
