Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Ottawa; federal business registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) via https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html and CRA My Business Account portal. Alberta provincial registry: Service Alberta, Corporate Registry (https://www.alberta.ca/corporate-registry-services), operating under Alberta's Partnership Act (R.S.A. 2000, c. P-3) and Business Corporations Act; the Corporate Registry issues the Alberta LP registration number and maintains the public record for limited partnerships.

E-government filing portal

(1) Name search and reservation: the foreign sponsors of the Alberta limited partnership (LP) must reserve the LP name through an authorized Alberta Corporate Registry service provider and file the initial registration (Limited Partnership Declaration) naming the general partner; a foreign general partner that is itself an extra-provincial corporation may need concurrent extra-provincial registration. (2) Alberta Registration Number issuance: upon filing, the Alberta Corporate Registry issues a unique Alberta LP registration number, which must be maintained through annual filings. (3) CRA Business Number (BN): the LP then applies for a 9-digit CRA BN by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' – a single application covers federal program accounts, including the GST/HST account (RT program account suffix), payroll (RP), and any Part XIII non-resident withholding account (RN); RC1 may be filed online via BRO or by fax/mail to the applicable CRA Tax Centre. (4) Partners' tax status: the LP is fiscally transparent; the LP files an annual partnership information return (T5013) but pays no income tax, and each partner includes its allocable share of income or loss under Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 96; a non-resident partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP is liable to Part I income tax on its Canadian-source business income and must file a T1 or T2 return, while passive distributions to non-residents may attract Part XIII withholding. (5) GST/HST registration: registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold) and is available voluntarily to non-resident persons; the non-resident LP without a Canadian establishment may be required by the CRA to furnish security or a bond in connection with its GST/HST account. (6) Digital mandate: after registration, the LP must open CRA My Business Account access, mandate an authorized representative (Form RC59), and, once annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1 million, file GST/HST returns electronically and remit electronically.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; an Alberta limited partnership is fiscally transparent by operation of law. Statutory elections applicable in practice: (i) non-calendar fiscal period election for a partnership under ITA s. 249.1(4), allowing a year-end other than December 31 where all partners so agree and the conditions are met; (ii) voluntary GST/HST registration election under Excise Tax Act s. 240(1), permitting a non-resident to register before reaching the small-supplier threshold; (iii) Quick Method of accounting election under ETA s. 223 for registrants with annual taxable supplies (including associates) not exceeding CAD 400,000; (iv) annual GST/HST filing election under ETA s. 238(1) for small registrants; and (v) mandatory, non-elective hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2-18.4 (effective 1 July 2022), which deny deductions for dual-inclusion and deduction/non-inclusion mismatches in cross-border arrangements and admit no elective carve-out. No entity classification election is available or required; the CRA determines the status of foreign entities by reference to their domestic legal form.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – the 'small supplier' threshold: a person carrying on commercial activity in Canada must register for GST/HST where the worldwide value of taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies but excluding financial services) by the person and all associated persons exceeds CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the aggregate of the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Non-residents are subject to the same CAD 30,000 measure and may register voluntarily regardless of threshold; once registered, GST must be charged on all taxable supplies made in Canada unless specifically zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods – a supply of tangible personal property is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 20-22, Excise Tax Act where the supplier transfers ownership under a contract requiring delivery outside Canada, or ships/dispatches the goods outside Canada as a result of the supply within 60 days after the invoice date, and retains documentary evidence of export. Services – a supply of a service to a non-resident person is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 23 where the non-resident is not registered under the Excise Tax Act and the service is not supplied in respect of real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or to a non-resident individual present in Canada; ASO/SEO localization, app-store optimization, and multilingual organic-search services supplied by the Alberta LP exclusively to overseas app publishers with no Canadian consumption generally qualify for s. 23 zero-rating. Intangible property – a supply of intangible personal property (digital marketing content, localized metadata, software, app assets) to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 25, provided the non-resident furnishes a written declaration of non-registration and foreign use. In all cases, the LP must maintain a complete audit trail: executed service agreements, invoices, non-resident declarations, board or payment-rail records, and correspondence establishing that the recipient is a non-resident and that the supply is not consumed or enjoyed in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system; there is no mandatory continuous transaction control, government API clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance invoice authorization regime. GST/HST registrants are permitted to issue electronic invoices (PDF, EDI, Peppol XML) provided the prescribed invoice content under ETA s. 169(2) and the CRA's record-retention rules are satisfied. Electronic filing is mandatory: GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1 million must file returns electronically (GST34 through NETFILE/XML or a CRA-approved filing method), and large businesses must remit electronically. The CRA participates in a voluntary Peppol-based e-invoicing interoperability pilot beginning in 2023, but adoption is optional and does not trigger any fiscal stamping obligation.

CRA compliance policy for export zero-rating and rebates, following the 15 July 2019 GST/HST refund-evidence reforms, requires the registrant to hold export evidence and non-resident status proof before refunds are issued; where evidence is deficient, the CRA reverses the zero-rating, reassesses GST at 5% on the previously zero-rated value, and charges compound daily arrears interest at the prescribed rate under ETA s. 280 from the original due date of the remittance. Additional penalties include: civil false-statement and gross-negligence penalties under ETA s. 285.1 equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax that would have been payable had the false statement or omission not been made; failure-to-file and failure-to-remit penalties under ETA s. 280 for late returns and remittances; and, where the LP or its principals wilfully participate in fraudulently claiming export zero-rating, criminal liability for tax evasion under ETA s. 327, punishable on indictment by fines of between 50% and 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years, as well as prosecution under s. 380 of the Criminal Code (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-46). The GST/HST avoidance rule in ETA s. 274 permits the CRA to disregard artificial export structures and claw back refunds with interest from inception. Reassessment limitation periods: CRA may reassess within four years of the filing date; the period is extended to eight years where a return was filed with a misrepresentation (including omission of export documentation) attributable to carelessness, neglect, or wilful default, and there is no limitation period where the conduct constitutes fraud (ETA ss. 298(2)-(3)).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating exchange-rate regime. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, and Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement (effective March 25, 1952). There are no exchange controls, no capital-repatriation permits, and no restrictions on inbound capital contributions to, or outbound profit distributions from, an Alberta limited partnership. Practical hurdles are fiscal and reporting only: (i) a non-resident partner's share of income from an Alberta LP carrying on business in Canada is generally taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act, with possible Part XIII withholding exposure on distributions and a reduction in withholding rates under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention (1980) where the partner is a US resident; (ii) FINTRAC requires the financial institution to file an Electronic Funds Transfer Report for every international transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) the LP must report non-arm's length transactions with non-residents on Form T106. The LP may freely hold CAD, USD, and multi-currency operating accounts; no conversion or central-bank approval is required to credit or remit funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. Cross-border service receipts, including app-store revenue remittances from Apple, Google, and international ASO clients, may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency. The only obligations are reporting: (i) Canadian banks automatically file FINTRAC EFTR-L reports for international electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) the LP must substantiate cross-border revenue in its books for CRA; and (iii) the LP must file Form T106 if payments to non-arm's length non-residents exceed CAD 1 million in a taxation year or if such payments exceed CAD 100,000 and are deductible. Conversion of USD/EUR receipts into CAD is an elective treasury decision, not a legal requirement, and should be staged to minimize FX drag.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision of authorized banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering supervision and cross-border financial transaction reporting sit with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); the Department of Finance Canada administers federal financial-sector policy. Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration agency because exchange controls were abolished with the repeal of the Exchange Control Act in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium - legally permissible but structurally constrained. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Inc.) and Payoneer (Payoneer Canada ULC / Payoneer Inc.) operate in Canada as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs), not as Schedule I banks. They may lawfully be used for low-value inbound app-store collections, contractor payouts, and cross-border B2B disbursements, and they accept the CA_LP as an entity with a US parent/general partner. However, they are not viable as the primary treasury account because: (i) they cannot settle directly through Canada's Lynx / ACSS payment systems; (ii) balances are not CDIC-insured and are held via partner banks in pooled/safeguarded arrangements; (iii) no regulatory-compliant capital-contribution or hedge-settlement account can be maintained; and (iv) enhanced FINTRAC, FATCA, and CRS due diligence will still be required. Recommended architecture: Wise/Payoneer as a secondary inbound rail for app-store revenue in USD/EUR with automatic sweeps to the primary Schedule I bank account within 48-72 hours; all capital injections, tax payments, and hedge settlements must run through a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Routing: US GP's bank -> SWIFT MT103 -> Schedule I Canadian bank (e.g., RBC SWIFT BIC ROYCCAT2, TD SWIFT BIC TDOMCATTTOR, BMO SWIFT BIC BOFMCAT2, Scotiabank SWIFT BIC NOSCCATT, CIBC SWIFT BIC CIBCCATT) -> LP's CAD or USD operational account. Wire protocol: field 50K must identify the US GP/LLC as ordering customer with full address; field 59 must identify the LP's exact registered legal name and account number; field 70 or 77B must clearly state: 'INCAP - CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO [LP NAME], ALBERTA PARTNERSHIP REGISTRATION NO. [XXXXXXX], LP AGREEMENT DATED [DATE]'. Do not use the words 'INVOICE', 'SALES', or 'SERVICE FEE' in the capital-injection purpose field. Canada does not maintain a regulatory purpose-code taxonomy comparable to China's SAFE PIN system; the free-text SWIFT purpose is the operative compliance record and should be retained with the wire confirmation. For transfers above CAD 100,000, the bank will likely require the executed capital-call notice, the LP agreement excerpt, and a source-of-wealth statement as a precondition for crediting. FINTRAC EFTR filing is automatic for international EFTs of CAD 10,000+. Verification: the bank confirms the ordering bank, the US regulatory status of the GP, and OFAC/sanctions screening before final crediting; CAD credits clear same-day via Lynx if received before 15:00 ET; USD credits via US correspondent generally settle in 1-2 business days. The LP should maintain a USD operating account to avoid unnecessary conversion at the bank's FX desk.Contractor and vendor payments in CAD: use Payments Canada ACSS/EFT domestic rail for routine payments and the Lynx large-value transfer system for transactions above CAD 1,000,000. Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) are legally permissible but commercially suboptimal for advertising, ASO, and localization services; opening commissions of 1-2% plus confirmation fees are rarely justified for service contract terms and will normally be used only where a foreign client's procurement policy mandates documentary collection. App-store revenue collections from Apple and Google should be routed either (i) directly by ACH/wire to the LP's USD account at the Canadian bank, or (ii) to a Wise/Payoneer USD virtual account with a scheduled sweep of balances to the primary Canadian account within 48-72 hours to satisfy entity-transparency and concentration-risk requirements. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium - legally permissible but structurally constrained. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Inc.) and Payoneer (Payoneer Canada ULC / Payoneer Inc.) operate in Canada as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs), not as Schedule I banks. They may lawfully be used for low-value inbound app-store collections, contractor payouts, and cross-border B2B disbursements, and they accept the CA_LP as an entity with a US parent/general partner. However, they are not viable as the primary treasury account because: (i) they cannot settle directly through Canada's Lynx / ACSS payment systems; (ii) balances are not CDIC-insured and are held via partner banks in pooled/safeguarded arrangements; (iii) no regulatory-compliant capital-contribution or hedge-settlement account can be maintained; and (iv) enhanced FINTRAC, FATCA, and CRS due diligence will still be required. Recommended architecture: Wise/Payoneer as a secondary inbound rail for app-store revenue in USD/EUR with automatic sweeps to the primary Schedule I bank account within 48-72 hours; all capital injections, tax payments, and hedge settlements must run through a Schedule I bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate rate (federal 15% + Alberta 8%); Alberta small-business rate is 2% for eligible CCPCs on active business income under CAD 500,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident; Alberta does not impose a separate dividend withholding tax. For an Alberta LP, ordinary profit allocations are not dividends, but amounts that are in substance distributions of Canadian-source non-business income may be recharacterized and subjected to Part XIII to the extent required.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No separate CAD-denominated Local File/Master File filing exists under Canada's domestic rules; however, Form T106 (Supplementary Information for Non-Arm's-Length Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed when aggregate reportable cross-border related-party transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000, and CRA contemporaneous documentation under s. 247 is expected at that level. A country-by-country report is mandatory for Canadian-parent MNEs with consolidated annual revenue of at least CAD 1,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations code; the substance doctrine is embedded in the GAAR (s. 245), transfer-pricing rules (s. 247), and treaty substance-over-form jurisprudence. An Alberta LP carrying on ASO/SEO localization must demonstrate real personnel, premises, decision-making functions, and economic risk-taking; a shell LP that merely books profit on behalf of non-resident principals can be ignored or recharacterized by CRA, denied DTA relief, and may attract Part XIII withholding on deemed distributions. If the LP is part of an MNE group above the EUR 750m threshold, Pillar Two top-up tax applies with a substance-based income exclusion calculated on payroll and tangible assets in Alberta.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess to the arm's-length price under s. 247(2), deny interest deductions for excessive thin capitalization under s. 18(5), assess Part XIII gross-up on deemed non-resident payments, impose a 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s. 247(3) on the net adjustment amount, and compound arrears interest.

Director personal liability: For an LP, the general partner and any corporate managing partner are jointly and severally liable for partnership obligations; directors of a corporate general partner can be personally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding under s. 227.1, plus a civil penalty equal to the amount withheld where the withholding duty was knowingly disregarded.
Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil fraud/misrepresentation penalty under s. 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; criminal tax evasion under s. 239(1) is punishable by a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to two years; proceeds-of-crime money laundering under the Criminal Code may add fines and imprisonment up to ten years.

Director personal liability: Courts will permit veil-piercing where the LP is a sham or fraud vehicle; general partners are jointly liable for tax restitution and penalties, and directors of a corporate general partner may face criminal fraud charges, court-ordered restitution, and travel restrictions imposed as bail or recognizance conditions.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard or recharacterize the partnership under s. 245 GAAR, deny treaty benefits under the principal purpose test, impose a 50% misrepresentation penalty, cancel GST/HST and payroll program accounts, and retroactively assess all Canadian-source income to the non-resident beneficiaries.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil is pierced for fraud, sham, or unjust enrichment; directors and general partners are personally liable for restitution of tax and penalties where the LP is found to be an instrument of tax avoidance; regulatory travel bans or temporary passport restrictions can be ordered in criminal tax or money-laundering proceedings.
Anti-Money Laundering / Client Funds Handling

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, failure to report suspicious transactions or register an MSB can trigger administrative penalties up to CAD 500,000; knowingly laundering proceeds attracts up to ten years' imprisonment and possible forfeiture of assets; the CRA can also reassess unreported income at gross-negligence penalty rates.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the AML breach are personally liable for penalties; travel bans and freezing orders can be issued under criminal restraint provisions, and directors can be extradited for predicate offences committed outside Canada.
