Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible in both current and capital accounts. Canada imposes no exchange restrictions on inward or outward investment, and an Alberta Limited Partnership may freely receive capital contributions, pay operating expenses, and remit profits or partnership distributions to non-resident partners in any currency. The only practical hurdles are fiscal: distributions that constitute income from a Canadian business (as opposed to passive income) are sourced to Canada under the Income Tax Act, subject to Part XIII withholding (25% general or treaty-reduced rates) on certain passive categories, and non-resident partners carrying on business through a Canadian partnership may become liable to file Canadian tax returns and pay tax on their allocable share of partnership income attributable to a permanent establishment. Profit remittance itself is unrestricted by exchange control; there is no requirement for repatriation, conversion, or central-bank authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds. An Alberta Limited Partnership may lawfully retain retainer receipts, royalties, and other commercial income in any currency in multi-currency accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks without forced conversion to CAD. For compliance, the Financial Transactions Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) requires reporting financial institutions to report all cross-border wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 per transaction, and the entity must retain underlying retainer invoices, client contracts, and board-approved treasury directives evidencing the commercial purpose of all currency holdings. There is no export certificate or proceeds-surrender formality applicable to ISIC 7310 public relations services.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Canada's central bank. Foreign-exchange administration in Canada is decentralized: the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially regulates authorized banks, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers cross-border transaction surveillance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and Global Affairs Canada administers economic sanctions. No separate Foreign Exchange Administration or exchange-approval authority exists; Canada has maintained liberalized current- and capital-account transactions since the repeal of exchange controls in the 1950s.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as a supplemental settlement rail, Low as a primary treasury platform. European Union or United Kingdom Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) — such as Revolut Business, Qonto, or Currencycloud — are not licensed under the Canadian Bank Act as Schedule I banks and cannot provide direct access to Payments Canada's Lynx/Real-Time Rail clearing, CAD settlement accounts, or CRA-recognized official deposit documentation. FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) in Canada (including Wise Platform, Payoneer, and Stripe Treasury facilitators) may be used for ancillary cross-border disbursements such as sub-threshold payments to overseas journalists, syndicated media partners, and independent contractors, but they are subject to enhanced due diligence under PCMLTFA Section 11.1 and many major correspondent banks impose restrictive thresholds on MSB-originated flows. A financial technology platform may be layered onto a Schedule I bank treasury but should never replace the licensed banking spine for an Alberta LP with material overseas retainers.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Entity-level N/A for Limited Partnership (pass-through); notional combined federal and Alberta corporate rate is 23% (15% federal + 8% Alberta) if the business were carried on in a taxable Canadian corporation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Domestic Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a taxable Canadian corporation to a non-resident is 25%; partnership distributions from the Alberta LP are not dividends, but allocations of Canadian business income to non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on a net basis and require the filing of partnership information returns plus partner-level Canadian returns.

The T5013 Partnership Information Return is due within 150 days after the partnership fiscal period-end; where any partner is a non-resident, the return must be filed before the deadline to avoid penalties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Canadian-resident taxpayer, including partners in the Alberta LP, is subject to transfer-pricing documentation obligations under section 247 and must contemporaneously prepare and retain a Local File where intercompany transactions with non-resident related parties exceed an aggregate of CAD 1,000,000 in the fiscal period; a Master File should be maintained for multinational groups and a Country-by-Country report is mandatory where consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000. In practice, the CRA requires documentation to be prepared no later than the partner's tax filing due date.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply identical Economic Substance Regulations to partnership structures, but a wholly artificial Alberta LP lacking real Canadian management, premises, staff and operational risk allocation will be disregarded or recharacterized under the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), common law economic substance principles, and treaty abuse provisions; the LP must maintain substantive PR project management, Canadian payroll, active client-facing activity, and business decision-making in Alberta to support business-profits treaty claims and Part I tax residency allocations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Intercompany Fee Stripping / Transfer-Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA may recharacterize the payment and adjust the transfer price under subsection 247(2); reassessment increases taxable income and imposes s.247(3) penalties of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment (subject to a CAD 5,000 minimum deduction), plus interest compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner, as well as any limited partner who participates in management control, can be jointly and severally liable for the partnership's unpaid tax, withholding, and penalties under subsection 227.1(1) and Alberta Partnership Act rules; culpable officers may also face travel bans on attempted departure during a collection freeze and, in egregious cases, criminal prosecution for fraudulent misrepresentation.
Unfiled or Late T5013 Partnership Information Return

Statutory sanction: Under subsection 162(10), a penalty of CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500 per partnership period; on demand, an additional penalty under subsections 162(1) and 162(5) equal to 1% of the unreported income amounts, with gross-negligence penalties under subsection 163(2) of 50% of the income understatement.

Director personal liability: General partners with management authority are personally liable for the penalty; limited partners who knowingly distribute funds without settling the CRA debt may be personally assessed; directors of the corporate general partner face restitution and prohibition-of-departure orders.
Shell Company Abuse / Artificial Treaty Routing

Statutory sanction: CRA can deny the treaty benefits and assess Part XIV branch tax or Part I tax on the full gross revenue, impose 25% Part XIII withholding on payments that were mischaracterized, add penalties for false or misleading statements, and levy 10% transfer-pricing penalties; under Canada's OECD BEPS ratification, the Principal Purpose Test rules may disallow the entire corporate structure benefit.

Director personal liability: Any director who signs a false T5013 or knowingly assists in a sham arrangement may be liable for restitution plus a penalty of up to CAD 1,000,000 on criminal conviction; personal assets and overseas travel may be restricted under civil enforcement measures.
Mischaracterization of PR Service Fees as Royalties

Statutory sanction: CRA recharacterizes the payment by substance over form under subsection 247(2) and sections 245 GAAR, applies Part XIII withholding of 25% plus interest, imposes penalties under section 227(1) for failure to deduct and remit equal to the unremitted amount plus 10%, and may issue a foreign-investment reporting assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and limited partners acting as managers are personally liable for unremitted source deductions and are subject to civil restitution orders; repeat intentional mischaracterization constitutes tax evasion and imprisonment penalties of up to two years under section 239 of the Income Tax Act.
Money-Laundering / Unlicensed Cross-Border Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Failure to comply with Canada's Proceeds of Crime and Terrorist Financing Act when an intermediary is involved can lead to administrative monetary penalties of CAD 1,000,000 for officers, criminal fines, and forfeiture of funds; for cross-border wire movements of CAD 10,000 or more, a Financial Transaction Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) report is mandatory.

Director personal liability: Directors of an LP general partner can be personally criminally liable for directing the transfer of funds that circumvent reporting; assets can be seized for restitution, and departures may be prohibited pending trial.
