Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Corporate Registry, Service Alberta, Government of Alberta, through Registry Xpress or an authorized Alberta Registry Agent.

Governing statute

Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c P-3, ss 44, 50, 51(1), 52, 53(1), 56; registered limited partnerships are filed with the Alberta Corporate Registry rather than incorporated under the Business Corporations Act.

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership in the prescribed form under the Partnership Act, Alberta.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership; limited partners may contribute cash, property, or services at agreed values, and no escrow or trust account is statutorily required for capital.

Funding & unlock path: Where a physical design office is opened, obtain a fire safety inspection and clearance from the municipal fire authority, comply with the Alberta Building Code and structural load requirements for the leased premises, and register with Alberta Occupational Health and Safety for the workforce. No special occupational clearance beyond standard OHS obligations applies to subscription-based design service delivery.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership; limited partners may contribute cash, property, or services at agreed values, and no escrow or trust account is statutorily required for capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); registration and account services are available through the CRA Business Registration Online service and CRA My Business Account. Alberta has no separate provincial general sales tax authority or provincial sales tax registration for ordinary supplies.

E-government filing portal

Obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and the applicable program accounts. A foreign limited partnership carrying on business in Canada or required to register for GST/HST generally applies to the CRA for a BN and GST/HST account using Business Registration Online where eligible, or the CRA's prescribed registration process. Provide partnership legal name, formation jurisdiction and documents, Canadian business address or contact details, business activities, start date, partners and authorized representative information. A non-resident may need to contact the CRA directly if online registration is unavailable and may be required to provide security. GST/HST registration is generally required when the partnership is not a small supplier and makes taxable supplies in Canada; voluntary registration may be available. A BN is not a corporate income-tax TIN, and an LP generally does not obtain a separate income-tax account solely because it exists: partnership income is generally reported by partners. A T5013 partnership information return may be required under the CRA filing rules. Non-resident partners may require Canadian tax identification and applicable withholding or tax-return compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election for a Canadian limited partnership. A partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes, with income and losses allocated to partners under the Income Tax Act. Classification is determined under Canadian law and the partnership agreement and facts; there is no general election to treat the LP as a corporation or to elect hybrid-mismatch status. Specific statutory elections may apply to particular transactions but do not change the LP's general classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) over a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, subject to the statutory small-supplier tests and exceptions. Special rules apply to non-resident digital-economy suppliers and platform operators supplying Canadian consumers; the applicable registration regime depends on the supplier's facts and whether it carries on business in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying exported goods are generally zero-rated where the statutory conditions are met, including export from Canada and required supplier records. Certain services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, but exclusions and conditions apply; a customer's non-residence alone does not establish zero-rating. Design and digital subscription services require a supply-by-supply analysis, including the recipient, place and manner of performance, connection to Canadian real property or tangible personal property, and any applicable excluded service category. Zero-rating is not the same as exemption; eligible registrants may generally claim input tax credits attributable to zero-rated supplies.

  • Export declaration and applicable Canadian customs export reporting evidence, where required
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier record, or other carrier evidence identifying the goods and destination
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, and proof of delivery or export
  • Customer identity and residence evidence, service agreement, deliverables, and records substantiating the recipient and place of supply for exported services
  • Inward remittance or banking records where available and relevant; Canada does not impose a universal inward remittance certificate requirement for GST zero-rating

Canada does not generally require real-time tax-authority invoice clearance, fiscal stamping, or mandatory nationwide e-invoicing APIs. GST registrants must issue invoices or receipts containing prescribed information when required and retain adequate books and records; electronic records are permitted if accessible and compliant with CRA record-keeping requirements.

Canada has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for qualifying export proceeds. The supplier must substantiate zero-rating and retain supporting records; CRA may reassess GST, deny related input tax credits or refunds, and impose arrears interest and applicable late-filing or other penalties. False statements or omissions may attract penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including gross-negligence penalties, and serious cases may result in prosecution. Reassessments are subject to statutory limitation periods and exceptions, including those applicable to misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible floating currency for both current and capital account transactions, compliant with IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange control restrictions on profit remittances out of Alberta. Non-resident limited partners may repatriate distributable earnings subject to domestic withholding tax rules (generally Part XIII of the Income Tax Act), including a mandatory 25% withholding on passive income unless reduced by treaty, and to automated CRS/FATCA reporting. Foreign currency accounts may be held by residents and non-residents alike without Bank of Canada approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, held in CAD, or freely converted through authorized financial intermediaries without reporting to OSFI, subject only to FINTRAC transaction reporting thresholds (large cash and wire reports).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign-exchange regulatory oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money-laundering supervision by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: High for inbound international client collections via Wise and Stripe, and medium for primary operational settlement. Legal constraints: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), any entity holding or transmitting funds on behalf of Canadians must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC. Wise is a registered MSB in Canada and provides CAD and USD clearing with local ACH and SWIFT routing. Stripe is likewise registered and acts as a payment facilitator, not a deposit-taking institution. True EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licensing is not recognized at the federal level in Canada; a foreign EMI operating from the EU or UK may not accept deposits from Canadian clients without MSB registration, and an Alberta LP using an offshore EMI as a core external Treasury platform may attract anti-avoidance and sanctions concerns. For low-value recurring subscription receipts, Wise and Stripe present the most cost-efficient inbound rail, while domestic Tier-1 bank accounts remain mandatory for larger capital movements and CRS/FATCA transparency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For all inbound capital contributions, use SWIFT MT103 wires to the LP’s CBDC (Canadian domestic) account held at a Tier-1 bank. Field 50K (ordering customer) must exactly match the banking registry and partnership agreement; Field 59 must contain the legal name ‘ALBERTA UI UX DESIGN LP’ and its CA-AB account number. In Field 70, include the purpose code ‘CAPITAL_CONTRIBUTION’ and a continuous compliance reference number. For US-domiciled partners, use Fedwire/ACH via the bank’s US banking pass-through network to eliminate double FX. When the transfer originates in a non-CAD currency, attach a payment schedule and FX quotation from the beneficiary bank; for USD, route via the bank’s reciprocal ‘USD Clear’ facility. Verification procedures include: match sender name and account with prior notification; confirm the payer is an authorised partner or lender; run sanctions and negative-list screening; capture transfer details for FINTRAC and automatically create the CRS/FATCA record.The primary clearing rail for trade settlements is SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments to overseas contractors, licensing fees, or operating expenses. For recurring platform subscription and vendor payments, Wise is the most efficient rail, supporting low-value multi-currency conversion with open-market FX rates within registered MSB limits. Domestic CAD disbursements follow Interac e-Transfer for micro-transactions and EFT/ACH for payroll and supplier payments. For USD payments to US residents, the bank’s continental automated clearing house mirror is preferred, or a SWIFT MT103 if the counterparty is outside North America.
