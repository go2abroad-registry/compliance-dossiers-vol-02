Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) — federal tax authority; business and GST/HST registrations through CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html). With respect to Alberta corporate tax, CRA collects provincial corporate income tax on behalf of Alberta under a tax collection agreement; Alberta-specific registrations (extra-provincial LP registration) are filed with Service Alberta / Alberta Corporate Registry.

E-government filing portal

An Alberta limited partnership (LP) must first be formed or extra-provincially registered with Alberta Corporate Registry, obtaining the Alberta goods/services registration identification. The LP then applies to CRA for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 or online through My Business Account, indicating a partnership structure and registering a GST/HST program account (RT). Where the LP makes zero-rated export supplies, an exporter-related program account may also be added. Non-resident foreign entities not already registered in Alberta and carrying on business in Canada may use the CRA non-resident GST/HST registration portal (or Form RC1) to obtain an RT account once the CAD 30,000 registration threshold is met; the BN functions as the statutory TIN for the partnership, and partners report their allocable shares using the LP's BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election; an Alberta LP is automatically transparent for income tax purposes and not itself taxable. Partners separately take into account partnership income. Relevant statutory elections: (i) GST/HST election under Excise Tax Act permitting one partner or a representative to act as the partnership's registrant and file returns; (ii) partnership fiscal-period election under Income Tax Act s.249.1; (iii) election under s.216 for non-resident partners to file net-basis returns on certain Canadian-source amounts; and (iv) use of prescribed allocation and ATN/T5013 schedules. Hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act ss.245.1–245.8 are mandatory in cross-border arrangements and do not permit elective declassification, although ordering rules may be elected.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (small-supplier threshold), determined over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and the current calendar quarter; for non-resident suppliers of digital services or intangible personal property to Canadian recipients, registration is generally required once worldwide taxable supplies to Canadian recipients exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or the preceding four quarters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI (Part V) and s.21.5 for a supply of intangible personal property (e.g., stock-photo licence or digital asset) to a non-resident recipient where the property is not used, or is used only minimally, in Canada. Services physically performed on exported goods and certain supply-chain services to non-residents may also be zero-rated. For a stock-photo AI platform, the supplier must establish the non-residency of each licensee, the place of use (foreign IP address, download metadata, and business information), and that payment is sourced from outside Canada in the ordinary course. Contractual terms should contract out of Canadian use where commercially feasible.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) where physical deliverables are shipped
  • Bill of Lading / equivalent transport document for tangible exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or payment-processor settlement report (Stripe/PayPal) evidencing foreign-source settlement
  • Commercial invoice and stock-photo licence agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and restricting use outside Canada
  • Digital delivery logs, geolocation records, and subscriber residency evidence for zero-rated intangible supplies

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API-based tax stamping, or pre-authorization regime exists at the federal level for GST/HST. Registrants may issue electronic invoices if they contain prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act and supporting books/records are retained. Electronic filing via CRA GST/HST NETFILE is mandatory for registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million; otherwise, filing may be on paper or online. Digital service platforms are not subject to separate invoice clearance in Alberta.

CRA applies heightened scrutiny to zero-rated export claims. The supplier must retain the documentation bundle for at least six years. If CRA disallows zero-rating, GST/HST becomes payable at 5% from the date of supply, with arrears interest and penalties. A four-year reassessment limitation period applies, but reassessment is unlimited where a false statement, neglect, or wilful misrepresentation is established. Penalties include false-statement penalties under Excise Tax Act s.285(1), gross-negligence penalties under Income Tax Act s.163(2) if income taxes are affected, and criminal sanctions/forfeiture for fraudulent refund claims under s.327.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. The nation maintains no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, or partner distributions, subject only to compliance with the Income Tax Act (Canada), including withholding tax under Part XIII for non-resident partners when distributions exceed taxable earnings or when certain cross-border payments are made. There are no administrative pre-approval requirements for capital injections or outward remittances, though cross-border transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC and all wire transfers must include originator and beneficiary information per Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory surrender or mandatory conversion timeline for export proceeds. The Alberta incorporated limited partnership may retain receipts in CAD or any foreign currency held in a domiciled or offshore account, and may convert funds at freely negotiated market rates through any CFA-licensed bank or foreign exchange dealer.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC); prudential banking supervision by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering supervision by Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Canada does not maintain a dedicated 'Foreign Exchange Administration' agency, as foreign exchange control is market-based under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex can provide ancillary transactional account rails and foreign-exchange services to an Alberta LP. However, these institutions are not banks regulated by OSFI; any entity operating within Canada as a foreign money service business (MSB) must register with FINTRAC, appoint a Canadian agent for service, and comply with PCMLTFA obligations. Canadian banks may not accept funds routed solely through an unlicensed EMI without additional due diligence. Therefore, an Alberta LP should maintain a Tier-1 Canadian bank account as its primary clearing anchor and use EMI rails as sub-accounts or global collection accounts on a case-by-case basis.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All foreign capital contributions must enter via the Canadian banking system as a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer (or equivalent electronic credit transfer) clearly referencing the LP name, the account number at the Tier-1 Canadian bank, and the purpose code 'Capital Contribution' or 'Injection of partner funds'. For funds from the United States, the bank may accept US-draft via Automated Clearing House (ACH) or Fedwire, but must ensure an English-language proof of origin and the LP’s Alberta registration number are included in the payment remarks. If aggregate injections exceed CAD 10,000, the bank will file a Large Cash Transaction Report for cash, and FINTRAC requires a Suspicious Transaction Report if origin details appear incomplete. Non-resident partners should route money through their own individually identified bank account rather than a third-party or escrow service to avoid triggering enhanced due diligence.Standard clearing rail for cross-border royalty and photographer payouts is SWIFT MT103 single credit or MT101 bulk transfer, with CAD or USD correspondent routing through Canadian banks. Domestic receipts from Canadian stock photo buyers may be cleared via automated funds transfer (EFT) using a Payment Canada clearing code. For high-value content licensing contracts exceeding CAD 100,000, the LP may accept a commercial documentary credit (Commercial LC) issued and confirmed through a Canadian bank, with expedited MT798/MT700 messaging. Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: General partners may be personally liable for civil penalties; directors of corporate general partners may face personal liability for unpaid taxes and penalties
Shell Company

Director personal liability: Limited partners may lose limited liability if they participate in management; general partners remain fully liable; directors of corporate general partners may face personal liability for unpaid taxes
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participate in AML violations; travel bans may be imposed in serious cases
