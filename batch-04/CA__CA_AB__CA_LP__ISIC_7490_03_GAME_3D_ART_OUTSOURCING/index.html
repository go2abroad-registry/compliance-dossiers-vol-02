Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada. Business portals: 'My Business Account' and 'Business Registration Online' (BRO) at canada.ca. Provincial counterpart: Alberta Corporate Registry for Limited Partnership (LP) registration under the Partnership Act (Alberta); Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration for partner-level provincial income tax, with no provincial sales tax administration because Alberta imposes no PST/HST provincial component.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Register the Alberta Limited Partnership with the Alberta Corporate Registry (Corporate Access Number) to obtain legal existence and the registration certificate; no minimum capital is required. Step 2 - Obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) by applying to the CRA via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number'); the BN is the statutory TIN. Step 3 - Add the GST/HST (RT) program account under the same BN when the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada above the CA$30,000 small-supplier threshold, or immediately upon a voluntary registration election; an LP with only zero-rated export services to non-resident clients should still register voluntarily to claim input tax credits (ITCs). Step 4 - For a non-resident-controlled LP, appoint a Canadian resident representative for service of process and tax notices pursuant to Excise Tax Act (ETA) section 274.1(1) before the RT account is activated. Step 5 - Each non-resident corporate partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP must obtain its own BN and file Canadian income tax (Part I) and Alberta corporate allocable-share returns; individual non-resident partners require a CRA-issued individual tax number for treaty-based relief filings (e.g., NR72/NR74 forms), and the general partner must file a T5013 Partnership Information Return and issue T5013 slips.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Alberta LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes: it files no income tax return and each partner includes its allocable share of income/loss in its own Part I or Part XIII return, subject to limited partner at-risk and adjusted-cost-base rules. For GST/HST the LP is a separate statutory 'person' registered with its own BN/RT account. Non-resident ownership is fully permitted; non-resident partners are taxable on the Canadian-source allocable share and are entitled to treaty protection under the Canada-US Convention (or other applicable treaty) where a permanent establishment threshold applies.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; Alberta LPs are statutorily passed through unless the entity is a publicly listed SIFT (Specified Investment Flow-Through) partnership under Income Tax Act (ITA) section 197, which is inapplicable to a private AAA game-art studio. Available elections: (1) Voluntary GST/HST registration under ETA s. 240(1), including the ability to elect to have all partnerships supplies treated as taxable; (2) Partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(7); (3) For US members, the Canadian tax classification of the LP follows Article IV(7) of the Canada-US Convention (Fifth Protocol) fiscally-transparent look-through rules; the US member may file IRS Form 8832 to select US federal classification, but the CRA will respect the LP's underlying Canadian law pass-through status; (4) Hybrid mismatch importation-timing elections under ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5, 20.2 and 94B may be used to align deduction timing where a non-arm's length US member recognizes a tax advantage, but they do not alter entity-level classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CA$30,000 - the small-supplier threshold measured on total taxable supplies made in Canada (plus associated persons) in the current and immediately preceding four calendar quarters. Zero-rated export services to non-resident game studios are taxable supplies made in Canada where physically performed in Alberta and therefore count toward the registration test; an export-only LP that crosses the threshold must register, and any export-only LP under the threshold is nonetheless well advised to register voluntarily to recover ITCs attributable to its zero-rated export activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, sections 9 and 10. Section 9 zero-rates a supply of a service made to a non-resident person; section 10 zero-rates a supply of intangible personal property (completed 3D models, rigs, and licensed IP) made to a non-resident person. The Alberta LP's AAA game asset sculpting, character rigging, and outsourcing services qualify provided: (i) the recipient is a non-resident at the time of supply; (ii) the service is not performed in respect of real property situated in Canada; (iii) the service is not performed in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada; and (iv) the delivered digital assets are not acquired by the non-resident for direct consumption or use in Canada relating to Canadian situs property. No CRA advance ruling is required; the supplier self-assesses the zero-rate and must retain contemporaneous evidence of the recipient's non-resident status (certificate of incorporation, registered office, permanent-establishment address, and the recipient's own GST/HST registration status if any).

  • For pure digital delivery of 3D art assets, no Canadian customs export declaration is applicable; the substantiation bundle is: (a) executed client contract / statement of work naming the non-resident publisher and its jurisdiction of incorporation; (b) invoice endorsed 'Zero-rated - ETA Schedule VI s. 9 / s. 10' with the LP's BN and RT program account; (c) digital delivery log (secure file-transfer portal audit trail, upload timestamps, hash values, and acceptance certificate from the non-resident client); (d) foreign-currency remittance evidence - SWIFT MT103, wire confirmation, or PayPal settlement report showing funds received from abroad and credited to the LP's Canadian bank account; (e) for physical media shipments only (hard drives, physical art packages), the CBSA Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) for goods valued over CA$2,000.
  • Customs Export Declaration - Form B13A required exclusively for physical outbound shipments exceeding CA$2,000; inapplicable to electronic transfer of 3D assets.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill - required only where tangible deliverables are shipped; for pure digital asset delivery, the digital handoff and acceptance certificate serves as the delivery attestation.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - CRA prescribes no standard certificate; the bank's SWIFT confirmation or PayPal payout statement operates as the inward remittance record substantiating the foreign-origin payment and FX conversion.

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no pre-clearance API, no digital stamping regime, and no continuous transaction control system exists in Canada. The CRA requires only that registered suppliers issue invoices satisfying ETA s. 223(1): supplier name, GST/HST registration number (BN + RT program account), invoice date, recipient identity and address, total consideration, and the tax amount or a zero-rating declaration. Electronic invoices, PDFs, and machine-readable formats are accepted. Canada is voluntarily adopting the Pan-Canadian Peppol-based e-invoicing standard (Budget 2024 framework), but adoption is not mandatory for service exporters. Books and records must be retained for six years from the end of the fiscal year (ETA s. 286).

Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day period is an internal treasury benchmark for collection discipline. Within the ordinary four-year reassessment window under ETA s. 298 (unlimited where the LP has committed misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default), the CRA may claw back any improperly zero-rated supply and assess the 5% GST, arrears interest compounded daily at the prescribed quarterly rate, and penalties. Where the false zero-rating claim is made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence, ETA s. 285(1) imposes a penalty equal to the greater of CA$250 and 25% of the tax sought; criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ETA ss. 327 and 339 can add fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment on conviction. In parallel, the general partner and partners face ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalties equal to 50% of the understated income tax. The LP's right to claim ITC refunds is also forfeited where the claim window (two years after the end of the reporting period) expires or where the refund claim is based on misleading documents. Intra-group exports to a US parent or affiliate are additionally reviewed under the ITA s. 247 transfer-pricing rules; a mispriced service fee can be re-characterized as taxable Canadian-source business income of the non-resident partner with corresponding 10% withholding exposures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under a free-floating regime with no exchange controls on either current-account or capital-account transactions. No prior approval from the Bank of Canada or any FX authority is required to convert CAD, to open foreign-currency accounts, or to repatriate profits. Profit remittance to non-resident partners of an Alberta Limited Partnership is governed by Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25% on dividends, rents, royalties, and certain partnership distributions, subject to tax-treaty reduction), and by the partnership attribution rules under the Income Tax Act under which a non-resident partner carrying on business in Canada may also be subject to Part I tax and must file a CRA partnership information return. Capital contributions are non-taxable, but the bank requires documentary evidence that the inflow is equity and not a loan or service fee.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign currency indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts. For tax reporting, foreign-currency export earnings must be translated to CAD using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate (or another CRA-acceptable rate) at the relevant balance-sheet date. For AML purposes, cross-border wire receipts above CAD 10,000 are reported by the bank to FINTRAC; the partnership must therefore maintain sufficiently itemized export invoices and commercial contracts as defence documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole central bank. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange-control agency; the FX policy mandate resides with the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bank of Canada Act, while prudential bank supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money-laundering supervision by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium-High as a supplementary collection or disbursement rail, but Low as a primary settlement account. Wise (authorized as a UK EMI under the FCA and registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business) and PayPal (FINTRAC-registered MSB) may legally receive funds for the partnership, execute subcontractor payouts, and process client collections. However, neither is a member of Payments Canada, cannot hold a clearing account with the Bank of Canada, and imposes aggregate transfer caps and enhanced-verification thresholds. A Canadian Schedule I bank account remains mandatory as the final treasury sink. Legal constraints: the EMI or MSB must maintain active FINTRAC registration; the partnership must disclose the fintech relationship to its primary bank; and funds in foreign digital EMIs must be swept periodically to the Canadian operating account to avoid treating the foreign EMI as the bank of record.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: non-resident partner initiates a SWIFT MT103 to the LP's capital escrow account or operational account at the Canadian bank. Field 50K must show the contributing partner's full legal name and address; field 59 must show the LP's exact registered name and account number; field 70 must read: 'Capital contribution to Alberta Limited Partnership [name], partnership agreement dated [date]; no goods/services.' Canada does not prescribe a formal purpose-code table, so the bank relies on the narrative text and accompanying capital-commitment schedule to characterize the inbound flow as an equity contribution. For inflows above CAD 100,000, the bank applies enhanced source-of-wealth due diligence. The partnership must provide an executed capital-contribution resolution, an escrow agreement, and a general-partner declaration confirming the capital nature; this characterization prevents Part XIII withholding and GST/HST exposure. If the contribution is received in USD, the bank credits a USD sub-account or executes a book transfer at the bank's corporate FX rate.Primary cross-border rail: SWIFT MT103 for all subcontractor, supplier, and contractor settlements, clearing through the Canadian bank's correspondent network (Payments Canada Lynx for domestic CAD, and Fedwire/CHIPS correspondents for USD). For milestone contracts where the client requires banked payment certainty, a Confirmed Irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP600 is permissible, although game-art BPO stages are typically invoiced against deliverables and settled by MT103. CAD disbursements settle through Payments Canada Real-Time Rail (RTR) or Lynx; USD disbursements settle on the Canadian banks' US-dollar clearing nodes. Each outbound payment must cite the underlying service agreement and import/export-supporting invoice, the services must be zero-rated GST/HST exports if rendered to foreign publishers, and payments must circulate only to the named production entity or approved contractor list. Currency conversion is executed at the bank's corporate FX desk and settles T+0 for CAD/USD.Deliverable CAD/USD FX forwards (T+2 settlement, tenors out to 24 months) under an ISDA 2002 Master AgreementFX swaps (spot and forward legs) for treasury cash-flow managementCross-currency basis swaps (CAD-USD) utilisable when a hard-currency financing drawdown is requiredVanilla CAD/USD FX options (calls and puts) for revenue hedging with IFRS hedge-accounting eligibilityNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing emerging-market currencies (e.g., INR, PHP, BRL, TRY) where domestic deliverability is restrictedCurrency swaps under a bank-approved master agreement documented at the parent-asset-holding level

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High as a supplementary collection or disbursement rail, but Low as a primary settlement account. Wise (authorized as a UK EMI under the FCA and registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business) and PayPal (FINTRAC-registered MSB) may legally receive funds for the partnership, execute subcontractor payouts, and process client collections. However, neither is a member of Payments Canada, cannot hold a clearing account with the Bank of Canada, and imposes aggregate transfer caps and enhanced-verification thresholds. A Canadian Schedule I bank account remains mandatory as the final treasury sink. Legal constraints: the EMI or MSB must maintain active FINTRAC registration; the partnership must disclose the fintech relationship to its primary bank; and funds in foreign digital EMIs must be swept periodically to the Canadian operating account to avoid treating the foreign EMI as the bank of record.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Limited partnerships are fiscally transparent in Canada; no entity-level Alberta/Canada corporate tax applies. The applicable benchmark for an associated corporation is 23% combined (federal 15% general CIT + Alberta 8% general provincial CIT); individual partners face Alberta top combined marginal rates up to 48% on allocated income, and non-resident corporate partners are subject to Part I tax at treaty-effective rates after the federal-provincial abatement.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII statutory rate: 25% on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder. For this LP vehicle, there is no entity-level dividend; profit allocations to non-resident partners are treated as business income subject to Part I withholding under s. 153(1)(p) at 15% for individuals and 25% for corporations, while returns of contributed capital and debt principal repayments are free of Canadian withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's-Length Transactions with Non-Residents) is mandatory when reportable transactions with any related non-resident exceed CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. CRA administratively requires a contemporaneous Local File where the Canadian entity's gross revenue exceeds CAD 20,000,000, and a Master File where the MNE group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 2,000,000,000. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000 (approx. CAD 1,100,000,000).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate an offshore-style ESR code; the substantive test is enforced indirectly through: (i) ITA s. 96.1 at-risk rules for limited partners; (ii) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245), which recharacterises artificial transactions lacking a real business purpose; and (iii) treaty benefit denial via Limitation-on-Benefits and Principal Purpose Test provisions. An Alberta LP acting as a conduit for 3D-art outsourcing must maintain payroll, studio premises, client contracts, and decision-making in Alberta to support its Canadian allocations and treaty claims.
