Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership. The Declaration must state the aggregate actual capital contribution of the limited partners, which may be contributed in cash, property, or services as set out in the partnership agreement. No escrow or mandatory capital-holding account is required at formation; however, for immigration-client work under the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants (CICC) rules, all client funds must be held in a segregated Canadian trust/escrow account maintained by a regulated financial institution.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the municipal fire marshal under the Alberta Fire Code, confirming means of egress, fire alarms, and suppression systems; structural live-load and occupancy clearance under the Alberta Building Code for the office floor; Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) clearance under the OHS Act and OHS Code covering general duty, hazard assessment, first aid, and emergency preparedness; Workers' Compensation Board (WCB) assessment account registration; personal information and client-file security controls in accordance with PIPEDA for immigration advisory records.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax registration administered via Business Registration Online (BRO) and the 'My Business Account' portal; GST/HST program accounts (RT) issued under the Excise Tax Act (ETA). Provincial contact: Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) for LP formation under the Partnership Act (Alberta) and Alberta Treasury Board & Finance for provincial tax matters (Alberta levies no provincial sales tax and no provincial VAT).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Alberta Limited Partnership with the Alberta Corporate Registry and obtain the Alberta registration number. Step 2: Apply for the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or Form RC1; a non-resident applicant without a Canadian presence must complete Form RC1 with designated foreign contact details and may be required to provide evidence of the place of effective management. Step 3: Open the GST/HST program account (BN-RT0001) with CRA; registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters, but voluntary registration is strongly recommended for a study-abroad/immigration placement LP that intends to zero-rate export advisory services. Step 4: No separate tax identification number is assigned to the LP for income tax purposes; the LP is fiscally transparent and each partner (general and limited) reports its allocable share under ITA s. 96; CRA requires partnership identification of all partners (name, Canadian SIN/BN or foreign tax ID, share percentages) and completion of the T5013 Partnership Information Return with T5013 slips. Step 5: Non-resident partners with Canadian-source business income must either file a Part I corporate/individual return or be subject to Part XIII withholding on passive distributions; a non-resident general partner may need to appoint a Canadian agent or address for service before obtaining clearance certificates (ITA s. 116 applies where the partnership disposes of taxable Canadian property).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Alberta LP is fiscally transparent for income tax (ITA s. 96): income and losses are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners in accordance with the limited partnership agreement, with no entity-level federal or Alberta corporate income tax. Territorial_Scope: Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source income (business income carried on through the LP, or passive Canadian-source distributions) subject to applicable tax treaty relief. For GST/HST purposes, the ETA definition of 'person' includes a partnership, so the LP is a distinct registrant holding its own RT account; however, the partners remain jointly and severally liable for the LP's GST/HST remittances. The LP is not a 'tax-exempt zone' entity and has no entitlement to preferential exemption; Alberta's 0% provincial component means only the federal 5% GST applies.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' classification regime; entity classification follows the Partnership Act (Alberta), ITA s. 96, and CRA administrative policy. Available statutory elections relevant to this LP: (i) a non-resident partner may elect under ITA s. 216 to file a Part I return on net Canadian rental/business income instead of facing Part XIII gross-basis withholding; (ii) the LP may elect a non-calendar fiscal period only with CRA authorization — in practice the fiscal period is the calendar year; (iii) under the ETA, the LP may file the s. 156 joint election (closely related entities) for inter-entity supplies and may elect under s. 211 for financial services (generally irrelevant here); (iv) hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed by ITA ss. 18.4–18.7, which deny a partnership deduction where the same payment is not included in the counterparty jurisdiction (or is deductible elsewhere); such arrangements must be disclosed under the mandatory disclosure rules (ITA s. 237.3) on Form RC312 or RC313. No provincial election exists for LP pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (general 'small supplier' threshold: total worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a person exceeding CAD 30,000 in any single quarter or in the trailing four-quarter period must register for GST/HST. Non-resident suppliers carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold. Below the threshold registration is voluntary; the LP should voluntarily register to recover input tax credits and to legally claim zero-rating on export supplies of advisory services).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 of the Excise Tax Act, a supply of advisory, consulting, placement, or immigration-processing services is zero-rated where all of the following are satisfied: (i) the recipient is a non-resident of Canada at the time the supply is made — verified by foreign passport, permanent-establishment location, or foreign tax-residency certificate; (ii) the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant in respect of the supply; (iii) the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada nor to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed; and (iv) the service is not otherwise deemed to be supplied in, or consumed in, Canada. Offshore study-abroad placement consulting, international student recruitment, Canadian university-application assistance, and golden-visa residence advisory services supplied to a non-resident client from the Alberta LP generally qualify for zero-rating. In contrast, services performed for a foreign national physically present in Canada at the time of performance (e.g., in-Canada study-permit extension, post-arrival settlement, or immigration file prosecution with Canadian government filings) are ordinarily performed in Canada and consumed in Canada — those services are taxable at 5% GST and may NOT be zero-rated. The LP must document the non-resident status of the recipient at the exact time of billing and maintain a per-client export analysis.

  • Signed retainer/engagement contract identifying the non-resident recipient, the scope of study-abroad/immigration services, the place of performance, and the contractual situs of the supply
  • Tax invoice displaying the LP's BN/RT registration number, invoice date, consideration, and the explicit notation 'GST/HST zero-rated pursuant to Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7, ETA' with CAD 0.00 GST
  • Incoming wire transfer / SWIFT MT103 / foreign-currency remittance advice evidencing receipt of non-resident consideration within the 180-day repatriation window, together with FX conversion records and the LP's foreign-currency bank statement
  • Non-resident status substantiation pack: foreign passport bio-page or foreign national ID, foreign address confirmation, and foreign tax-residency certificate (where the client is an entity: certificate of incorporation, registered office outside Canada, and confirmation that the client has no Canadian permanent establishment)
  • Canadian customs B13A Export Declaration is required ONLY where incidental physical goods (e.g., branded brochures, printed application kits, or marketing collateral) are exported cross-border; for pure service exports no customs declaration is filed, but an internal export register must document each contract, recipient, invoice, and wire receipt

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, CTC authorization, digital stamping, or pre-clearance e-invoice regime comparable to Mexico/Italy. CRA has adopted a voluntary Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and PEPPOL-based e-invoicing standard (Canadian Digital Interoperability Blueprint); adoption is accelerating for B2G transactions but remains non-mandatory. Mandatory statutory requirements under the ETA: (i) all GST/HST registrants with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or approved EDI; (ii) every tax invoice must state the supplier's BN/RT registration number, invoice date, recipient name, description of supplies, consideration, GST payable (or explicit 'zero-rated — Schedule VI' notation), and, where applicable, the recipient's BN; (iii) simplified invoices (minimum content) are permitted for supplies under CAD 30; (iv) electronic records and invoices must be retained for six years (or longer where CRA requires) in a readable format, and e-invoices must be produced in a format that CRA can access.

CRA audits of export zero-rating focus on four elements: (1) genuineness of the non-resident status of the recipient; (2) absence of Canadian consumption or Canadian situs of the service; (3) sufficiency of contemporaneous documentation; and (4) timely FX repatriation of fees within 180 days. Where CRA determines that a supply was incorrectly zero-rated, it will reassess GST at 5% on the full consideration, charge compound daily interest at the prescribed rate (currently tiered, approximately 8%-10% annually), and may impose: (i) a late-filing/omission penalty under ETA s. 280 of 5% of the net tax amount plus 1% per full month of delay (maximum 12 months); (ii) a gross-negligence penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 5% of the understated tax where the false zero-rating claim was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence; (iii) revocation or clawback of any input tax credits and rebates previously claimed, with repayment of refunds plus interest; and (iv) criminal prosecution for tax evasion under ETA s. 327 — on summary conviction, fines of up to 50% of the evaded tax and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. The ordinary reassessment window is four years from the original filing; the window is indefinite in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default. Because the LP is a partnership, all general partners (and limited partners to the extent of their involvement in the management or their distributions) are jointly and severally liable for the GST/HST assessment, interest, and penalties; CRA may also assess culpable officers or partners for failure to exercise due diligence in the collection and remittance of GST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada provides de jure full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Canadian dollar freely floats in the interbank market, and no licence, approval, or repatriation permit is required for inward capital contributions, fee disbursements, loan repayments, or profit distributions. Remittance hurdles are exclusively tax-regulatory: (i) the Alberta LP's business income allocated to non-resident limited partners is generally taxable in Canada under Part I of the Income Tax Act; (ii) guaranteed payments, management fees, interest, and royalties paid or credited to non-residents attract 25% Part XIII withholding (treaty-reduced in most jurisdictions); (iii) GST/HST zero-rating of exported consulting services to non-residents is preserved only with complete documentary evidence of the client's non-residency and the place-of-consumption analysis. No exchange-control-based barrier exists to converting or remitting CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export receipts. The LP may retain foreign-currency earnings from international-student placement and immigration-visa advisory services in domestic or offshore foreign-currency accounts indefinitely. For tax purposes, foreign-currency income must be translated into CAD at the Bank of Canada noon spot rate for the relevant date under section 261 of the Income Tax Act, and Form T1135 is required when the cost amount of specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000. All electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more (including 24-hour aggregates) are automatically reportable to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central banking and monetary authority, with exchange-control policy residing in the Crown through the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act and the Exchange Fund Act. There is no dedicated foreign-exchange administration; OSFI supervises authorised deposit-taking institutions, and FINTRAC enforces AML/CFT obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for ancillary settlement liquidity, Low for fiduciary trust functions. Offshore digital EMIs and fintech platforms (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, etc.) must register as money services businesses with FINTRAC and obtain a licence under Alberta's Money Services Businesses Act to lawfully hold or transmit funds for Canadian clients. They can process high-volume low-value payments to overseas universities and visa authorities, but they lack access to Canada's Lynx domestic clearing system and cannot serve as the principal bank. CICC rules require all immigration-consulting client funds to be held in a segregated trust account at a Canadian-regulated deposit-taking institution, which EMIs cannot provide.

Defense evidence: Segregated trust-account ledger; approved disbursement schedule; due-diligence file for each payee; professional undertaking by the licensed Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultant; CICC trust-account compliance confirmation; monthly reconciliation statements with the bank; and proof of professional-liability insurance.

Defense evidence: CICC registration certificate for the responsible RCIC; a certified copy of any governing licence or exemption; provincial consumer-protection registration where applicable; errors-and-omissions insurance binder; and the firm's regulatory complaint history or good-standing letter.

Compliant protocol: (i) execute a capital-call notice and subscription agreement before transfer; (ii) route each wire via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign partner's own same-name bank account to the LP's account at a Schedule I bank; (iii) insert 'LP capital contribution — capital call no. [X]' in SWIFT field 70; (iv) identify the transaction narrative as 'capital contribution to Canadian limited partnership' for Statistics Canada balance-of-payments and FINTRAC coding; (v) provide source-of-funds documentation and a completed partner KYC file at onboarding; (vi) recognise that every wire of CAD 10,000 or more (or 24-hour aggregate) triggers FINTRAC EFT/large-transaction reporting; (vii) file an Investment Canada Act notification for non-WTO partners where applicable. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for ancillary settlement liquidity, Low for fiduciary trust functions. Offshore digital EMIs and fintech platforms (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, etc.) must register as money services businesses with FINTRAC and obtain a licence under Alberta's Money Services Businesses Act to lawfully hold or transmit funds for Canadian clients. They can process high-volume low-value payments to overseas universities and visa authorities, but they lack access to Canada's Lynx domestic clearing system and cannot serve as the principal bank. CICC rules require all immigration-consulting client funds to be held in a segregated trust account at a Canadian-regulated deposit-taking institution, which EMIs cannot provide.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and Alberta general corporate rate of 23% (15% federal + 8% Alberta) applied at the corporate-partner level; the Alberta Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent with no entity-level corporate income tax, and income flows through to partners.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% gross Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, reduced by treaty to typically 15% for portfolio holdings and 5% for substantial shareholdings (e.g., 10% or greater voting control under the Canada-US treaty).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions per taxation year triggers mandatory T106 filing and contemporaneous Local File documentation; Country-by-Country notification (RC4646) applies when the consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000; Master File and Local File must exist prior to the filing deadline to avoid s.247(3) penalties.

Economic substance scope: No formal Economic Substance Regulations statute in Canada; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) and s.247 transfer-pricing rules require documented risk-reward capacity including employees physically performing study-abroad placement and visa-admission decision-making in Alberta; a shell LP with no operational substance will be denied loss allocations, treaty relief, and GST registrations, and will be recharacterized as a sham partnership subject to deemed dividend treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of 50%–200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 2 years on summary conviction or up to 5 years on indictment; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; s.163(2) penalties of CAD 50,000 for false statements or omission of income.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners jointly and severally liable for corporate unpaid tax, penalties, and interest; conviction can trigger travel bans and passport seizure; restitution orders apply to personal assets of directors found to have directed the evasion.
Unlicensed Immigration Practice and AML Deficiency

Statutory sanction: IRPA s.91 penalties up to CAD 100,000 per unlicensed immigration consultant and/or imprisonment up to 2 years on indictment; AML administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation plus criminal sanctions for money laundering under s.462.31; suspension of business licences by Alberta Consumer Protection.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unlicensed immigration activity fines when they direct or supervise the offence; senior officers face prohibition orders; FINTRAC penalty regime assigns direct liability to directors for failure to implement an AML compliance program.
Shell Partnership and Treaty/GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial of the tax benefit under s.245(2); transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment under s.247(3); recharacterization of profit allocations as deemed Part XIII dividends subject to 25% withholding and arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Limited partners forfeit limited-liability protection if they take part in management under Alberta Limited Partnership Act s.13; general partner and directors are personally assessed for the disallowed tax and penalties where the corporate partner is insolvent or the allocation is a mere conduit.
Offshore Non-Disclosure (T1135 / CbC)

Statutory sanction: T1135 penalty of CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500; after formal demand, a penalty of 5% per month of the unreported property's cost amount up to 24 months; failure to file T106 exposes 10% transfer-pricing penalty and Canadian criminal prosecution for false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners civilly liable for unreported foreign property penalties; criminal indictment allows court-ordered asset freezes and travel bans; CRS/FATCA automatic exchange disclosures expose the structure to global tax administration review.
GST/HST and Payroll Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: GST penalty of 1% plus 25% of the unpaid net tax on repeat failures; payroll penalties of 10%–20% of unremitted source deductions plus interest; director's liability under s.323 ETA and s.227.1 ITA for unremitted GST and employee withholdings.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for all unremitted GST/HST and payroll source deductions without a defence, enabling CRA to pursue personal bank accounts, impose liens, and obtain court enforcement orders including temporary travel restrictions.
