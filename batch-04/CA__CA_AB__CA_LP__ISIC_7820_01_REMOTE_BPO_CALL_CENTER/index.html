Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Partnerships, Alberta Corporate Registry, Service Alberta (filings accepted online and through authorized Registry Agents).

Governing statute

Partnership Act (Alberta), RSA 2000, c P-3, Part 2 (Limited Partnerships), ss 51-74; Partnership Regulation, Alta Reg 177/2002; Supplemented by the Safety Codes Act and municipal bylaws for physical premises.

Formation / constitutional filings

Limited Partnership Registration Declaration (Form 1) under s 53(1) of the Partnership Act, filed as 'Registration of a Limited Partnership'.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 26 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow requirement for an Alberta Limited Partnership; capital contributions are contractual, fixed in the Limited Partnership Agreement, and may consist of money or property (limited partners may not contribute services without losing limited liability status).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection and certificate per the Alberta Fire Code (Alta Reg 291/2006) conducted by the municipal fire marshal; building and occupancy permits under the Safety Codes Act; workplace clearance under the OHS Code (Alta Reg 87/2009) covering workstation ergonomics, emergency evacuation planning, first-aid supplies, and WCB coverage certificate; remote workers are covered by a written remote-work safety and data protection policy.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow requirement for an Alberta Limited Partnership; capital contributions are contractual, fixed in the Limited Partnership Agreement, and may consist of money or property (limited partners may not contribute services without losing limited liability status).

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No Alberta industrial licensing authority mandates a sectoral operating permit for ISIC 7820 remote BPO call centers; the operative regulators are the CRTC (for outbound telemarketing/rules) and municipal business licensing (City of Calgary/Edmonton).

Category: Municipal Business Licence (Office / Administrative Services class) and CRTC Telemarketing Compliance Registration for outbound tele-sales; no separate provincial industrial operating permit applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Alberta Limited Partnership obtains a statutory tax identifier through the CRA Business Number (BN) registration process. The LP, acting through its general partner or appointed representative, files Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online via CRA My Business Account or by paper. If the LP makes taxable supplies, a GST/HST account is established concurrently on the same application. The Alberta Corporate Registry issues a provincial corporate registration number, which is not a tax ID. Non-resident partners are not issued a separate TIN; they are addressed through the partnership's designation and, where applicable, CRA withholding account procedures. Required documentation includes the LP's certificate of registration, partnership agreement, identification for partners and the general partner, and non-resident entity evidence (certificate of incorporation, incumbency certificate, or equivalent).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Canadian federal law; entity classification follows the legal form created under provincial law. Elections relevant to the LP include: (i) ITA s. 249.1(4) partnership fiscal-period election, (ii) ITA s. 216/217 elections for non-resident partners to file returns based on taxable Canadian earnings, and (iii) ETA s. 156 election for closely related entities to be treated as a single GST/HST registrant. The quick method GST/HST election is also available to eligible registrants.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada during four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available to non-residents)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The offshore BPO call center services (inbound helpdesk and outbound tele-sales) supplied to a non-resident recipient qualify for zero-rating under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act where: (a) the recipient is a non-resident of Canada; (b) the recipient is not registered for GST/HST; and (c) the service is not performed on or in relation to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. Call center and tele-sales services do not inherently relate to Canadian property, so the supply is zero-rated when all conditions are satisfied. The LP must retain contemporaneous evidence establishing non-resident status and non-registration of the recipient.

  • Signed international service agreement (BPO / call center master contract) identifying the non-resident recipient
  • GST/HST zero-rated export invoice containing service description, period, amounts, and recipient non-resident indicator
  • Non-resident status evidence (certificate of incorporation, W-8 equivalent, official tax residence certificate, or foreign passport/address confirmation)
  • Foreign bank remittance or SWIFT confirmation showing payment received from outside Canada
  • Detailed service delivery logs (call detail records, helpdesk ticket histories, tele-sales activity reports)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing/API filing regime exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information (supplier name, business number, transaction date, description, tax amount). Electronic invoices are permitted and must be accessible and readable by the CRA. GST/HST returns are filed periodically through CRA online services with no pre-clearance.

If the CRA determines that zero-rating was incorrectly claimed (e.g., recipient was not a non-resident, recipient was GST/HST-registered, or service related to Canadian real or tangible personal property), the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST. The CRA may assess unreported tax, interest, and penalties under sections 280–285 of the Excise Tax Act, including a gross negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax. Knowingly providing false information may lead to criminal prosecution, restitution orders, and a complete retroactive clawback of zero-rating benefits without limitation period in cases of fraud or misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with full convertibility on both the current account and the capital account. There are no general exchange controls, no restrictions on the purchase or sale of foreign exchange, and no licensing requirements for inbound capital contributions, loan repayments, or profit repatriation by non-resident limited partners. Profit distributions from an Alberta limited partnership to non-resident partners are subject only to Part XIII withholding tax of the Income Tax Act (subject to income tax treaty reductions), and no exchange control approval is required. Remittance hurdles are limited to standard tax reporting, anti-money-laundering reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000 for cross-border wire transfers), and bank-level compliance checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose mandatory export proceeds surrender, compulsory repatriation timelines, or forced conversion ratios. Export receipts, including inbound payments for remote BPO/call center services, may be retained in any freely convertible currency or converted to CAD at the operator's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange administration and AML surveillance are conducted by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Conditional Medium. Canada does not recognize the EU-style 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) category. Non-bank cross-border payment providers, such as Wise, PayPal and Payoneer, operate as registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and must be registered with FINTRAC. They cannot accept deposits from the public or offer demand/term deposit accounts, which limits their capacity as a primary treasury rail. An MSB can serve as a legal payment overlay for low-value B2B disbursements, but the Alberta LP should maintain a Schedule I bank account as the primary settlement account. Cross-border clearing functionality via MSBs is viable for sub-CCRA thresholds and repetitive payroll-style payments, but may be treated as high-risk by certain banks if used as a pass-through vehicle.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership ambiguity inherent in the limited partnership structure, where the general partner is a separate corporate entity and limited partners may be non-resident tax-haven entities.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership ambiguity inherent in the limited partnership structure, where the general partner is a separate corporate entity and limited partners may be non-resident tax-haven entities.

Defense evidence: Provide the Alberta Corporate Registry abstract showing current general partner and registered office; the full limited partnership agreement; a direct and indirect ownership chart with full legal name, residence, and tax identification number of every individual owning 10% or more of a limited partner or the general partner; a certificate from the general partner identifying ultimate beneficial owners; and confirming that general partner's directors have undergone FINTRAC-compliant identity verification.
Inbound capital injection from a non-resident limited partner or parent company located in a high-risk jurisdiction, creating source-of-funds uncertainty.

Root cause: Inbound capital injection from a non-resident limited partner or parent company located in a high-risk jurisdiction, creating source-of-funds uncertainty.

Defense evidence: Supply audited or reviewed financial statements of the remitting entity for the last two fiscal years; board of directors resolution approving the capital contribution; the signed subscription agreement or partnership contribution schedule; a copy of the sending bank's SWIFT MT103 with a precise purpose code; a bank reference letter from the remitting bank; and a source-of-wealth questionnaire explaining the commercial genesis of the funds.
Discrepancy between the registered Alberta office and the actual operating location, particularly if the BPO call center uses a distributed remote-agent model rather than a centralized office.

Root cause: Discrepancy between the registered Alberta office and the actual operating location, particularly if the BPO call center uses a distributed remote-agent model rather than a centralized office.

Defense evidence: Provide the physical lease or co-working license at the operational hub; the complete telephony/communications infrastructure agreement; a roster of remote agents with signed independent contractor or employment agreements; AML/PPC training records; and the client services contract (e.g., helpdesk and tele-sales agreement) evidencing the legitimate commercial purpose. If distributed agents are used, provide the payroll or settlement system log showing consistent, predictable payment flows.
High-volume, small-denomination disbursements to a large number of remote individual agents, which can trigger transaction monitoring alerts for potential structuring or unlicensed money transmission.

Root cause: High-volume, small-denomination disbursements to a large number of remote individual agents, which can trigger transaction monitoring alerts for potential structuring or unlicensed money transmission.

Defense evidence: Deliver the transaction monitoring policy prepared for the entity; a tiered disbursement schedule aligned to agent contracts; the settlement ledger mapping each payment to an invoice or timesheet; sanctions screening results for all beneficiaries; evidence that all disbursements are made through a regulated bank rail (not cryptocurrency or unstructured peer-to-peer); and a copy of the bank's own AML compliance acknowledgement for low-risk B2C disbursements.
Non-resident or high-net-worth signature authority, where an authorized signatory is physically located outside Canada and the bank cannot intuitively verify identity.

Root cause: Non-resident or high-net-worth signature authority, where an authorized signatory is physically located outside Canada and the bank cannot intuitively verify identity.

Defense evidence: Prepare a notarized delegation of signing authority executed by the general partner; a certified copy of the general partner's directors' resolution appointing the signatory; a personal AML/KYC file for the signatory including passport, tax ID, and EU/UK equivalent of DBS or police certificate; foreign tax residence evidence for FATCA/CRS classification; and a video-verification interview where permitted.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol: inbound capital contributions should be initiated from the non-resident limited partner via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's CAD or USD denominated account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The receiving bank uses Lynx (Canada's RTGS system) for CAD-denominated high-value settlement; USD wires are cleared via SWIFT and corresponding banking networks. The wire must include the LP's legal name, Alberta registration number, a unique client identifier, and a purpose code designating the transfer as a capital contribution (e.g., 'Contribution to limited partnership capital – direct investment'). All cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by the bank to FINTRAC as an Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR). The LP must be prepared to provide a capital contribution certificate and an executed partnership agreement excerpt to demonstrate that the incoming remittance is equity rather than income, thereby avoiding Part XIII withholding on capital and not creating a debt obligation.Outbound trade and operational disbursements should be executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 for international supplier payments and through Canada's ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) for domestic agent, contractor, and vendor payments. Commercial letters of credit (LC) are permissible for procurement of telecom hardware, software licensing, and specialized BPO equipment, with the LP's Canadian bank issuing an LC on behalf of the Alberta limited partnership. High-value USD settlements are cleared via SWIFT or CHIPS through correspondent banks. For smaller recurring outbound payments to offshore contractors or development partners, the bank's international wire portal or a FINTRAC-registered MSB overlay (e.g., Wise B2B) can be used, provided that each disbursement retains a full audit thread and remains below regulatory thresholds requiring separate EFTR filings.
