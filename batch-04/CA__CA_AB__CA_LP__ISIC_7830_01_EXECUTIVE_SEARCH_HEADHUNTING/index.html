Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership. Limited partners may contribute cash, property, or services as agreed in the partnership agreement; no escrow or statutory deposit account is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection certification confirming compliance with the Alberta Fire Code and local fire bylaw; building occupancy permit verifying structural load, egress, and accessibility under the Safety Codes Act; and an Occupational Health and Safety clearance confirming compliance with the OHS Code, including the workplace harassment and violence prevention plan and first-aid requirements.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership. Limited partners may contribute cash, property, or services as agreed in the partnership agreement; no escrow or statutory deposit account is required.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. An Alberta limited partnership may freely receive offshore retainers, invoice foreign clients in CAD or foreign currencies, and remit profits to non-resident partners without exchange-control authorisation. The principal profit-remittance hurdles are fiscal, not monetary: the LP must file all required Canadian income-tax returns, allocate Canadian-source partnership income correctly to non-resident partners, and address any applicable withholding obligations under the Income Tax Act for payments of interest, royalties, or management-type fees. The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) may require an information return for distributions to non-resident partners, but there is no exchange-convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no statutory conversion ratio for inward commercial receipts. Executive-search service fees received from abroad may be held in CAD, USD, or another convertible currency in a Canadian or foreign bank account. All transactions remain subject only to general anti-money-laundering reporting, including FINTRAC large-transaction reporting for cash or mixed-cash deposits of CAD 10,000 or more and to the requirement that wire transfers carry complete originator and beneficiary information.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority responsible for currency issuance and monetary policy. Canada operates a free-floating exchange-rate system, and there is no separate foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control bureau. The Minister of Finance and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervise banks and payment-service providers, but no central-bank approval is required for cross-border funds transfers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an auxiliary payment rail, but Low as a primary treasury or deposit account. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) are not authorised banks in Canada under the Bank Act, cannot accept deposits in CDIC-insured accounts, and cannot issue Canadian IBANs. They may only provide e-money settlement through a Canadian-registered money services business (MSB) or by partnering with a regulated Canadian financial institution. Wise B2B is operationally viable for low-value and mid-value cross-border retainer wires, contractor disbursements, and small vendor payments; however, the primary liquidity pool must be maintained with a Schedule I Canadian bank to reduce MSB counterparty risk, FINTRAC registration complexity, and potential frozen-funds events. For large executive-search fees, a direct SWIFT wire from the client to the LP's Schedule I bank account remains the cleanest rail.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque limited-partnership ownership structure, including nominee limited partners, bearer-like interests, or trust-arranged beneficial ownership.

Root cause: Opaque limited-partnership ownership structure, including nominee limited partners, bearer-like interests, or trust-arranged beneficial ownership.

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the Alberta limited-partnership agreement; current registration extract from the Alberta Corporate/Partnership Registry; register of limited partners; beneficial-ownership register identifying all natural persons owning 25 percent or more; declarations confirming no nominee ownership; bank reference letters for each significant partner; explanation of the commercial purpose of the partnership.
Non-resident partners resident in FATF low- or uncooperative-jurisdictions, or absence of a Canadian resident signatory on the account.

Root cause: Non-resident partners resident in FATF low- or uncooperative-jurisdictions, or absence of a Canadian resident signatory on the account.

Defense evidence: Notarised passports and proof of residential address for all non-resident partners; independent corporate-registry search from the partner's home jurisdiction; source-of-wealth letter; source-of-funds evidence for capital contributions; clearing explanation of the executive-search business model; commitment to maintain a Canadian-resident authorised signatory and to provide annual partnership financial statements to the bank.
High-value inbound retainer wires from foreign clients whose jurisdiction or corporate registration does not align with the stated target-search markets.

Root cause: High-value inbound retainer wires from foreign clients whose jurisdiction or corporate registration does not align with the stated target-search markets.

Defense evidence: Executed C-suite retainer agreement or master services agreement; detailed headhunting scope and search mandate; itemised invoices referencing ISIC 7830 / NAICS 561312 services; corporate register excerpts for the client; correspondence and search-project evidence (e.g., candidate pipeline, interview notes, placement contracts) demonstrating the genuine commercial activity.
Frequent disbursements via Wise B2B or other non-bank payment-service providers to overseas independent search associates or contractors.

Root cause: Frequent disbursements via Wise B2B or other non-bank payment-service providers to overseas independent search associates or contractors.

Defense evidence: FINTRAC MSB registration certificate of the payment-service provider where applicable; signed independent-contractor or partnership-remittance agreements with overseas associates; service invoices from each receiving consultant; a regular disbursement register linking each transfer to a specific search mandate; annual reconciliation of all PSP accounts with the LP's general ledger.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital contribution routing requires a SWIFT MT103 or domestic wire from the partner's own bank account to the LP's Schedule I bank account, with the wire reference clearly identifying the partner, the LP name, and the phrase 'LP Capital Contribution'. No foreign-exchange approval is needed, but the bank may request the partnership agreement and a capital-account statement to distinguish partner contributions from taxable revenue. For wires exceeding CAD 10,000, the bank must capture complete originator information for FINTRAC reporting; statutory purpose is generally recorded as 'other business services' or 'capital contribution'. Investment Canada Act notification is not triggered by ordinary capital contributions to an executive-search LP unless an acquisition of Canadian control is involved, which is not applicable for this headhunting scope.Primary clearing rail for retainer wires is direct SWIFT MT103 from the LP's CAD/USD bank account to the beneficiary's bank account, with full beneficiary identification and a purpose code referring to 'management consulting / executive search services'. For high-value settlements, commercial letters of credit are not a natural fit for executive-search services because there are no shipped goods or transport documents; if a client insists on a trade instrument, an at-sight commercial LC through a Canadian Schedule I bank can be structured around invoice-and-receipt presentation, but SWIFT MT103 remains the industry-standard and lowest-cost rail. Wise B2B is approved as a secondary rail for small and medium payments, contractor disbursements, and urgent cross-border payments, provided every payment is supported by an invoice, engagement letter, and clear commercial rationale.Deliverable foreign-exchange forward contracts (USD/CAD, EUR/CAD, GBP/CAD) executed with a Canadian Schedule I bank treasury desk for commercial retainer-flow hedgingNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted-emerging-market currencies where physical delivery is not possible, only where the underlying exposure is a verified cross-border search feeCross-currency swaps (FX swaps / currency swaps) for larger capital rationing and balance-sheet hedging under an ISDA/CSA master agreement with a qualified Canadian bankPlain-vanilla foreign-exchange options and zero-cost collars used for retainer-billing margin protection, provided they are transacted through a licensed bank dealer and subject to the LP's declared hedging policy

They may only provide e-money settlement through a Canadian-registered money services business (MSB) or by partnering with a regulated Canadian financial institution.
