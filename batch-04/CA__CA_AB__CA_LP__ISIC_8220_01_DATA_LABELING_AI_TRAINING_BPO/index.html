Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), accessed via the My Business Account portal at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals/account-individuals.html

E-government filing portal

Non-resident limited partnerships must apply to the CRA for a Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) registration and a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number'. The BN is a single government identifier used across federal programs. For partnerships, the BN is allocated to the partnership itself; each partner's Canadian-source taxation is tracked separately. The application can be filed electronically via CRA My Business Account or by submitting the paper RC1 to the appropriate tax services office. The BN is issued upon registration and may take up to 15 business days for non-resident applicants. A separate GST/HST account (RT) is part of the BN structure when taxable supplies exceed the $30,000 threshold. No provincial registration is required in Alberta as Alberta does not levy a provincial sales tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

For income tax, a Canadian resident limited partnership may make an election under subsection 96(2.1) of the Income Tax Act to deem the partnership to be a separate person on behalf of all partners in respect of a business carried on by the partnership. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership may elect under section 225 of the Excise Tax Act to treat certain supplies as made by a member (partner) rather than the partnership where the member actively participates in the business. Additionally, for cross-border service supplies, the partnership may rely on the general zero-rating rule under Part V.16 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, functionally mimicking an offshore-service election, but no express statutory 'check-the-box' classification election exists in Canada.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (worldwide taxable supplies) per calendar quarter and per trailing four-quarter test; mandatory registration if exceeded

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated (0% GST) applies to exports of goods and services provided to a non-resident person. For goods, the goods must be exported by the supplier, or the recipient must export the goods within 60 days after the last supply, and the supplier must maintain proof of export. For services, the supply is zero-rated if the service is rendered to a non-resident who is not a GST/HST registrant in Canada, or if the service falls under the general zero-rating provisions (e.g., intellectual property services, commercial advisory services) regardless of where the service is performed. Data annotation and AI training services delivered to non-resident clients fall under the zero-rating rule for commercial services under Paragraph 7 of Part V.16, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA C1/Export Form B13A) where goods are physically exported
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, or gate/road documents) providing evidence of physical movement
  • Commercial invoice issued by the exporting supplier to the non-resident customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Bank of Canada / settlement bank evidence of foreign exchange conversion) or equivalent proof of payment from the non-resident account

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime in Canada. GST/HST registrants are required to issue tax invoices, whether electronic or paper, that contain specific information (seller's BN, invoice date, tax status, and amounts). The CRA accepts electronic records provided they meet normal record-keeping requirements. There is no API-based pre-clearance; however, registrants filing GST/HST returns via the CRA's online system must use approved electronic channels (e.g., GST/HST NETFILE or standardized EDI). Federal procurement suppliers are increasingly required to submit e-invoices through the Government of Canada's e-invoice platform, but this is not a tax clearance mechanism.

The CRA may require export evidence within a 180-day timeline after the supply; failure to document exports or wilfully misrepresenting a supply as zero-rated triggers de-registration, denial of refunds, and immediate reassessment of uncollected GST at 5% plus interest. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a civil penalty of the greater of CAD 1,000 or 40% of the net tax amount misstated for a 'false statement or omission' (subsection 280(1)). For gross negligence, a penalty of up to 50% applies under subsection 280(1)(c). Criminal conviction for tax evasion via fraudulent export claims can result in fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Retroactive clawbacks extend a maximum of four years for normal reassessment, or indefinitely where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default exists.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial and financial transactions. Canada generally has full current-account convertibility and no routine requirement to surrender foreign-currency receipts or obtain prior approval for lawful cross-border service payments, dividends, partnership distributions, loan repayments, or capital contributions. Capital movements are generally unrestricted, subject to AML, sanctions, tax, beneficial-ownership, securities, anti-corruption, and source-of-funds controls. An Alberta limited partnership is ordinarily tax-transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes, but classification and reporting depend on the partnership agreement, partner status, management, permanent-establishment facts, and applicable federal and provincial tax rules. Cross-border payments may require withholding, information reporting, treaty analysis, transfer-pricing support, and documentation of the legal entitlement to payment. Canadian banks may delay or reject transactions lacking a clear commercial purpose, beneficial-owner evidence, tax documentation, or sanctions clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory deadline for repatriating or converting export proceeds and no mandatory foreign-exchange conversion ratio for receipts from computer-vision annotation, AI data-curation, or other exported services. Export-service revenue may remain in a foreign-currency account or be converted at the entity's discretion, subject to contractual, tax, accounting, sanctions, AML, and financial-reporting obligations. The partnership should retain customer contracts, invoices, service-delivery evidence, payment confirmations, foreign-currency ledgers, and applicable GST/HST export-of-service support. Foreign-currency transactions must be translated and reported under the applicable Canadian accounting and tax framework.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration comparable to a capital-control authority. FINTRAC administers federal anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist-financing compliance for reporting entities, while the Canada Revenue Agency administers tax reporting and transfer-pricing rules. The Alberta Treasury Board and Finance administers relevant provincial financial legislation, but does not impose general exchange controls on ordinary corporate receipts or payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and mass payouts; low as a complete substitute for a Canadian commercial bank. Wise Payments Canada Inc. and PayPal Canada may support eligible business payments or payouts, but availability, currencies, recipient countries, transaction limits, account functionality, and prohibited-use rules are product-specific. They are not substitutes for a Canadian deposit-taking bank account, and balances may not receive the same deposit-insurance treatment as deposits at a CDIC member institution. A foreign electronic-money institution cannot be assumed to provide Canadian deposit-taking, trust, lending, or unrestricted payment services. Payment-service-provider obligations under the Retail Payment Activities Act may apply to an entity performing regulated payment functions in Canada; registration does not eliminate AML, sanctions, tax, consumer-protection, or bank-account requirements. Mass PayPal payouts and Wise transfers should be used only for documented payroll, contractor, vendor, or customer-payment purposes and should not be used to obscure beneficial ownership or split transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route partner contributions, shareholder or affiliate loans, and other capital injections through the Canadian operating bank account or a documented foreign-currency account in the legal name of the limited partnership. Use a bank wire with the exact legal name, account number, institution and transit details, and a payment narrative identifying the instrument, such as 'limited partnership contribution' or 'intercompany loan under agreement dated [date]'. Canada does not prescribe universal central-bank FX purpose codes for these transfers. The bank may request the partnership agreement, contribution or loan agreement, partner resolution, source-of-funds evidence, beneficial-owner information, and tax treatment. Reconcile the received amount to the capital or loan ledger, retain the SWIFT payment confirmation, and avoid third-party funding unless the legal and economic basis is documented. For transfers involving restricted jurisdictions, sanctioned persons, or unusual structures, obtain sanctions and AML clearance before execution.Use Canadian bank wires, SWIFT MT103 or equivalent customer-credit-transfer messaging, domestic EFT or wire services, and commercial letters of credit where counterparty risk, documentary control, or shipment evidence warrants it. Each payment should identify the contractual counterparty, invoice number, service period, currency, and commercial purpose.
