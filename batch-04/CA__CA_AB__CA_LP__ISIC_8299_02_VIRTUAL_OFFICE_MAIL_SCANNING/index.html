Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Alberta Corporate Registry, accessed through an authorized Alberta registry agent.

Governing statute

Alberta Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c P-3, Part 2 (limited partnerships), including the provisions governing formation, filing of the limited partnership declaration, and registration of amendments. The limited partnership is not a corporation; its general partner has management responsibility and bears the statutory liability applicable to a general partner.

Formation / constitutional filings

Limited Partnership Declaration, filed with the Alberta Registrar through an authorized registry agent.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership, and no statutory escrow account is required. The partnership agreement should specify each limited partner’s contribution and the general partner’s authority. Confirm registry-agent service charges and current government fees before filing; they may be payable in Canadian dollars and are not represented by the USD amount above.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal building, occupancy, and fire approvals applicable to the premises and intended use. Maintain required fire protection and safe egress. Structural load inspections are not a routine separate licence for an ordinary office, but building-code approvals may be required for alterations or changes of use. Comply with Alberta occupational health and safety requirements, including applicable workplace hazard, ergonomics, and emergency procedures.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta limited partnership, and no statutory escrow account is required. Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. No central-bank permit, foreign-exchange budget, or repatriation quota applies to capital movements, cross-border transfers, or non-resident profit remittances. An Alberta limited partnership may distribute available cash to foreign partners in any currency after Canadian tax compliance; careful distinction is required between active Canadian-source business income allocable to non-resident partners and passive payments (dividend/interest/royalty) that trigger Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act. No conversion or statutory escrow period is imposed on the Canadian side.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender regime and no minimum conversion ratio. Exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency in any onshore or offshore account. All incoming and outgoing electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the financial institution under the PCMLTFA; physical export or import of currency or monetary instruments over C$10,000 must be declared to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). No repatriation timeline exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); banking conduct and AML oversight is shared with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and FINTRAC. Canada maintains no foreign-exchange-rate control authority because the Canadian dollar is fully floating and freely convertible under the Currency Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Offshore EMIs cannot obtain a Canadian bank charter and cannot directly access Payments Canada clearing. An EMI may be used only as a payment-acceptance channel if it is registered with FINTRAC as a foreign money-services business (or relies on a licensed MSB/PSP partner) and holds settlement funds with a Schedule I Canadian bank. For recurring credit-card revenue, Stripe Recurring / Credit Card payments should be settled through Stripe Canada Ltd. into a Canadian bank account; a pure offshore EMI cannot act as the primary treasury clearing rail for CAD receipts.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Virtual-office / CMRA address used as registered office and business address, creating a shell-company or substance mismatch.

Root cause: Virtual-office / CMRA address used as registered office and business address, creating a shell-company or substance mismatch.

Defense evidence: Current CMRA lease or commercial service agreement; Alberta corporate registered-office certificate and notice of change forms; proof of dedicated physical premises and signage (photographs/utility bill); landlord/agent letter confirming unrestricted access; a clear operations narrative showing scanning, document management, and customer correspondence are actually performed from the address.
Opaque limited-partnership ownership for non-resident partners, especially with nominee or trustee layers.

Root cause: Opaque limited-partnership ownership for non-resident partners, especially with nominee or trustee layers.

Defense evidence: Certificate of Limited Partnership; current partnership agreement; complete register of individuals with significant control; beneficial-ownership declaration naming all 25%-or-greater partners; passport copies and 6-month bank reference for each non-resident partner; notarized explanation of capital source and role in the CMRA business.
Non-resident tax nexus and insufficient economic substance in Canada.

Root cause: Non-resident tax nexus and insufficient economic substance in Canada.

Defense evidence: Canada Revenue Agency Business Number, GST/HST registration indicating commercial activity, Alberta corporate/partnership filings, NR6/NR4 withholding plan for non-resident allocations, non-resident partner declarations, Canadian legal-opinion letter, payroll or contractor agreements demonstrating staff activity, and audited or internally prepared financial statements for the LP.
High cross-border wire flows disproportionate to expected CMRA scanning revenue.

Root cause: High cross-border wire flows disproportionate to expected CMRA scanning revenue.

Defense evidence: 3- to 5-year cash-flow forecast; bank statements of the LP; client contracts and recurring subscription/receipt volumes; partner capital-account statements; correspondence matching each inbound wire to a capital contribution resolution; source-of-wealth evidence for large funding amounts; board/general-partner resolutions authorizing each treasury transaction.
Sanctions, PEP, or adverse-media exposure arising from foreign partner jurisdictions.

Root cause: Sanctions, PEP, or adverse-media exposure arising from foreign partner jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Recent sanctions screening report against the consolidated Canadian sanctions list, OFAC, UN, EU, UK, and other relevant listed-persons registers; independent negative-media and PEP search; no-PEP notarized declarations for each owner; law-enforcement or professional reference letters; enhanced transaction monitoring procedures; undertaking to notify bank immediately of any ownership change or sanctions designation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign partners must remit capital by SWIFT MT103 or equivalent cross-border wire into the LP's Canadian capital-escrow or operating account. The SWIFT beneficiary field must contain the exact LP legal name, account number, and Canadian routing code (institution + transit number). The remitter's purpose text must state 'Capital contribution – non-resident partner subscription to Alberta Limited Partnership'. Receiving banks automatically file FINTRAC EFT reports for transfers of C$10,000 or more; the LP should retain the subscription agreement, the partner's board resolution, the SWIFT confirmation, and the bank deposit advice. Any conversion into CAD occurs at the bank's ruling spot rate; there is no official central-bank approval step. Escrow release must be aligned with the capital contribution schedule and cannot be used to camouflage unrelated trade payments.Outbound settlements may be executed through SWIFT MT103 for cross-border supplier/service payments using a described purpose such as 'trade payable/service fee' supported by invoice evidence; through bilateral or confirmed commercial letters of credit (documentary credits) for physical purchases or high-value supply contracts; and through Payments Canada Lynx or ACSS for domestic CAD clearing. CAD/USD payments routed via the Canadian bank's US correspondent are cleared on Fedwire or CHIPS, while CAD high-value payments are settled intra-day on Lynx. All disbursements must be consistent with the LP's CMRA/mail-scanning operating purpose and must never function as unauthorized money transmission or lending.Deliverable outright FX forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) executed under an ISDA/Canadian Master Agreement through a CIRO-regulated derivatives dealer and reserved for hedging known exposure.FX swaps (spot-versus-forward structures) used to shift distribution dates and align non-resident partner cash calls with short-term liquidity.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for longer-term diversification of foreign-currency funding or capital accounts, used only on a hedge basis.Non-deliverable forwards (NDF) are legally permissible for Canadian corporates but are market-nonstandard for CAD because the currency is fully convertible; if a counterparty insists on an NDF, the treasury policy should document the reason and cash-settlement mechanics.
