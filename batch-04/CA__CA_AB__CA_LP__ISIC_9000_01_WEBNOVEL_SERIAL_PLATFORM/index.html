Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. Alberta imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital on a limited partnership; the required capital is determined contractually in the limited partnership agreement. The Declaration of Limited Partnership must state the amount of capital contributed or to be contributed by each limited partner. No escrow account or capital lock-up rule applies at the provincial registry level.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable at formation for a virtual-only operation. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html; Alberta Corporate Registry (AB Corporate Registry) at https://www.alberta.ca/corporate-registry for provincial LP registration.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta Limited Partnership (LP): (1) apply through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by mail using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) to obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) for the partnership; (2) a Canadian-resident general partner may register using his or her Social Insurance Number (SIN), while a non-resident general partner must first obtain an Individual Tax Number (ITN) from CRA or appoint an authorized representative; (3) the LP is registered as a deemed person for GST/HST purposes under Excise Tax Act (ETA) subsection 123(1), and a GST/HST program account (RT) is opened under the BN; (4) a payroll program account (RP) is added only if the LP has Canadian employees; (5) each partner must file a T5013 Supplementary slip, and the LP must file the T5013 partnership information return annually with Form T5013 Summary; (6) where the LP carries on business in Canada but the general partner is non-resident, the partnership may be required to file under the ETA simplified non-resident registration regime if it sells digital services, or it must obtain a provisional identification number pending the issuance of the BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime; the LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act (ITA) subsection 96(1) and is a deemed person for GST/HST under ETA subsection 123(1). Available statutory elections include: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA subsection 249.1(4)-(6); (ii) partner admission or deemed-disposition election under ITA subsection 97(1); (iii) non-resident partner's election under ITA subsection 216(4) and section 216.1 to be taxed on an actual net basis for Canadian-source business, rental, or timber income; (iv) GST/HST closely related corporation election under ETA subsection 273(1) (limited relevance because the LP itself is not a corporation); and (v) for hybrid mismatch exposure arising from an offshore jurisdiction treating the LP as opaque, Canada's hybrid mismatch rules under ITA sections 18.3 to 18.7 and 18.45 mandate self-adjusting disallowance or inclusion rather than any affirmative election; no elective check-the-box is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or cumulative over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters of worldwide taxable supplies (ETA subsection 240(1)); however, non-resident vendors of digital services or digital goods carrying on an 'ordinary commercial activity' in Canada must register from the first dollar of Canadian digital revenue under the July 1, 2021 framework, with no registration threshold. Where subscriptions are sold through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing, those app-store operators are generally deemed under ETA subsections 273.1(2) and 273.1(3) to make the underlying supply on their own behalf and are responsible for collecting and remitting GST/HST on Canadian consumer sales, reducing the LP's direct collection obligation for those sales channels.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital subscription services supplied by the Alberta LP to non-resident subscribers are generally zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V (sections 21 and 23 for intangible personal property and services supplied to non-residents), provided the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada, and the service is not rendered to a non-resident individual while that individual is physically present in Canada. For sales through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the LP must retain evidence of the subscriber's non-resident status, ordinary residence, and geo-location at checkout and at the time of content consumption. CRA requires the LP to exercise reasonable due diligence to verify non-resident status; a mere IP-address check without geo-location or billing-jurisdiction corroboration is generally insufficient. The supply of 'access to a digital platform' is classified as a supply of a service or intangible personal property under ETA section 123, and zero-rating is denied where the Canadian-resident subscriber downloads or reads the web novel while in Canada on a recurring basis.

  • Zero-rated GST/HST sales invoice (or export invoice) clearly indicating 0% GST and referencing Schedule VI, Part V
  • Signed WebNovel subscription agreement or end-user licence agreement (EULA) identifying the non-resident recipient and jurisdiction of ordinary residence
  • Non-resident attestation pack: passport or foreign tax ID, W-8BEN-E / W-8BEN where applicable, or equivalent documented declaration collected at account registration
  • Apple IAP and Google Play settlement reports evidencing the transaction volume attributed to non-Canadian billing addresses and app-store taxes collected by the platform
  • Payment gateway and banking records showing subscription revenue received from foreign payment instruments or foreign bank accounts
  • Subscriber geo-location and device-access logs supporting that the service was consumed outside Canada

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax clearance, or digital stamping. Electronic invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoice standard (PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0) endorsed by CRA. Nevertheless, every GST/HST registrant must issue valid tax invoices or simplified receipts containing the prescribed particulars under ETA subsection 223(1) and section 169, retain full books and records for six years, and provide CRA with electronic access to accounting records and Apple IAP / Google Play settlement data upon audit demand.

Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure in this dossier serves as the LP's internal documentary-control benchmark. Substantive audit and clawback exposure: if CRA determines that a supply was mischaracterized as a zero-rated export and was in fact made to a Canadian consumer (including a non-resident subscriber accessing the platform while physically present in Canada), the zero-rating is reversed and the unreported 5% GST is reassessed without the normal four-year limitation period where the misstatement is attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud (ETA subsection 298(2)). Penalties may include: (i) a 25% gross-negligence penalty under ETA subsection 285(1) of the net tax at issue; (ii) false-statement penalties under ETA subsection 280(1); (iii) daily compounded arrears interest under ETA section 281; (iv) prosecution under ETA section 286 with fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment for serious omissions; and (v) clawback of any input tax credits previously claimed on costs attributable to the improperly zero-rated supply. Because the entity is a limited partnership, the general partner is personally liable for the LP's GST/HST obligations under the Alberta Partnership Act (RSA 2000, c. P-3), and CRA may assess the general partner jointly and severally as the responsible person; an offshore corporate general partner will not be shielded by the LP veil where the LP has failed to sustain adequate non-resident substantiation records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized current and capital account regime with a free-floating Canadian dollar (CAD). The Currency Act R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52 imposes no exchange controls, licensing, or approval requirements on cross-border payments, profit remittances, or capital flows involving an Alberta limited partnership. Distributions and capital repatriations by the LP are constrained solely by tax law (Part I income tax and Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) and by the LP agreement, not by exchange control. All FX transactions are executed at market-determined rates without central bank authorization. Cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more trigger mandatory FINTRAC reporting by the Canadian financial institution, but this is declarative and does not limit convertibility or liquidity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada. The Alberta LP may retain WebNovel export revenue (Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlements) in CAD or foreign currency at its discretion. Informational reporting obligations apply: FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting for cross-border transactions at or above CAD 10,000, Statistics Canada Balance of Payments enterprise surveys for large settlement flows, and CRS/FATCA reporting for non-resident partner allocations. The LP should maintain segregated multi-currency ledgers to substantiate conversion spot rates, Part XIII withholding computations, and partnership allocation calculations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada; prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail; HIGH only as an ancillary payout corridor. European-licensed e-money institutions and non-resident digital banks are not authorized to take deposits in Canada under the Bank Act S.C. 1991, c. 46 and therefore cannot serve as the LP's operating bank. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, MultiPass, Intergiro) may clear low-value disbursements to overseas content creators only if the entity is registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC, maintains its float at a Canadian Schedule I bank, and provides contractual assurance of audit trail access to OSFI and FINTRAC. Use of an unregistered EMI exposes the LP to vicarious liability under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and to frozen-fund risk at the Canadian clearing bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing requires SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from foreign limited partners to the LP's CAD settlement account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The MT103 must state the beneficiary LP's registered legal name and account number, include 'for further credit' instructions, and use ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPI' (Capital Increase) or 'EQUI' (Equity) with the narrative 'Capital contribution to [LP legal name], Alberta Limited Partnership'. The receiving bank will file FINTRAC EFT reports for each tranche at or above CAD 10,000. Funds may be posted to the capital escrow sub-ledger only after the General Partner issues a drawdown notice and executed subscription documents are verified. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

The Alberta LP is fiscally transparent and is not a separate taxpayer. The combined headline corporate rate applicable to a taxable corporate partner is 23% (Federal Part I general rate 15% + Alberta provincial general rate 8%). Individual partners are taxed at graduated marginal rates with an Alberta top combined marginal rate of 48%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII (ITA s. 212(2)) on any dividend paid or credited to a non-resident partner, subject to treaty reduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of cross-border transactions (CRA Information Circular 87-2R benchmark; no express statutory dollar threshold, but without contemporaneous documentation the taxpayer forfeits penalty protection under ITA s. 247(3)).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate Economic Substance Regulations regime for Alberta LPs. Substance is tested through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), which disallows a tax benefit from an avoidance transaction lacking a bona fide commercial purpose, and through the limited-partner 'at-risk' and loss-restriction rules (ITA s. 96), which limit deductible losses to the limited partner's at-risk amount (capital contributed plus share of retained income).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax understated (ITA s. 163(2)); criminal conviction under s. 239(1) carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the corporate general partner who directed, assented to, or acquiesced in the evasion are personally liable for the fine, may be imprisoned, and an arrest warrant enables border apprehension and travel restriction.
Part XIII Withholding Default / Unremitted Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: The LP is liable for the unremitted amount plus interest; a penalty of 10% of the amount applies, increased to 20% for gross negligence (ITA s. 227(8)).

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1 for the unremitted withholding taxes, unless they exercised reasonable due diligence; personal assets and cross-border travel arrangements can be affected by CRA civil collections and criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA adjustment of profit to the arm's-length amount plus a s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000), with interest running from the originating taxation year.

Director personal liability: No automatic director penalty, but directors of the corporate general partner are exposed to personal liability for the LP's resulting unpaid taxes if they approved the transfer pricing scheme without due diligence or signed false declarations.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization under ITA s. 245 (denial of LP tax attributes), imposition of Part XIII withholding on deemed payments, gross-negligence penalties, and criminal prosecution if the arrangement constitutes a sham; dissolution or striking-off by the Alberta Corporate Registry for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: The limited-partner liability shield is pierced under the Alberta Partnership Act (RSA 2000, c P-3, s. 104) when a limited partner participates in control of the business, making that partner personally liable for partnership obligations; corporate directors who certified false filings can also face criminal exposure.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering (FINTRAC)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 on a first violation and CAD 2,000,000 on a subsequent violation for entities (PCMLTFA and associated regulations); on indictment, criminal fines up to CAD 500,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate in, consent to, or are negligent regarding AML violations can be individually fined and imprisoned; FINTRAC may name responsible individuals in AMP proceedings.
