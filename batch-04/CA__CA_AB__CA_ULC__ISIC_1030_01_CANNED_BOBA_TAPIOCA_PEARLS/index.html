Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including CRA My Business Account and the Business Registration Online service. Alberta administers provincial corporate income tax through the CRA under tax-collection agreements; Alberta corporate registry filings are separate from tax registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta unlimited liability corporation (ULC) must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and register the applicable program accounts, including a corporate income tax account (RC) and, when required, a GST/HST account (RT). Incorporation or registration in Alberta does not itself complete all CRA program-account registrations. Apply through CRA Business Registration Online where available or the CRA's prescribed non-resident or business registration process; provide the entity's legal name, Alberta registration particulars, registered and mailing addresses, incorporation documents, business activities, fiscal year-end, directors, and authorized representative information. A non-resident conducting business in Canada may also need a BN and GST/HST registration. GST/HST registration is generally required when the entity is not a small supplier or otherwise has a statutory registration obligation. CRA may require additional information or security from a non-resident registrant. The GST/HST account number is the BN followed by the RT program identifier and account reference.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Canadian ULC is generally a corporation and a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Canada); it is not a domestic pass-through entity. Canada has no general check-the-box election allowing a ULC to elect partnership or disregarded-entity treatment for Canadian tax purposes. A ULC may be treated as fiscally transparent under the domestic law of a foreign jurisdiction, including the United States, depending on its governing law and applicable elections there. That foreign classification does not change its Canadian corporate status and may produce hybrid-entity or treaty-eligibility issues. No general Canadian hybrid-mismatch election converts the ULC into a pass-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, subject to the statutory small-supplier rules and exceptions. The threshold does not necessarily protect non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada or otherwise required to register may have to register regardless of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying goods supplied for export from Canada may be zero-rated where the statutory conditions are met, including applicable requirements that the recipient not take delivery in Canada and that the supplier retain evidence of export. A manufacturer exporting its own goods may make zero-rated export supplies, subject to the applicable rules and documentary substantiation. Services are not zero-rated merely because the customer or payment is offshore; eligibility depends on the specific exported-service provisions, recipient status, place of performance, and statutory exclusions. Zero-rating is distinct from exemption and generally preserves input tax credit entitlement for related GST expenses.

  • Canadian export declaration or applicable electronic export reporting confirmation, where required
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract identifying the goods, foreign recipient, destination, and consideration
  • Carrier-issued bill of lading, air waybill, or other transport document
  • Proof of delivery or other reliable evidence that the goods were exported from Canada
  • Proof of payment or settlement records where relevant to substantiate the transaction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: not a generally prescribed Canadian GST export-zero-rating document

Canada has no general real-time GST invoice-clearance, mandatory tax-authority API, or digital-stamping regime for ordinary domestic business-to-business invoices. GST registrants must issue invoices or other supporting documentation meeting applicable Excise Tax Act requirements, retain adequate books and records, and file GST returns electronically when required. CRA electronic filing and payment obligations may apply according to registrant status and filing requirements.

Canada imposes no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. A registrant claiming zero-rating must retain records sufficient to establish that the statutory export conditions were met. CRA may deny zero-rating and assess GST, interest, and applicable penalties if the evidence is inadequate or the supply does not qualify. False statements or omissions may attract penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including gross-negligence penalties where applicable; deliberate tax evasion may also result in prosecution. Assessment periods and reassessment rules depend on the applicable statutory provisions and facts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for transactions with non-residents; Master File and Local File required if group revenue exceeds CAD 50 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific ESR, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and transfer pricing rules require economic substance. Transactions lacking commercial substance may be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of tax evaded, plus fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years (Income Tax Act s. 238)

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under s. 227.1; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for fraud
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, penalties under GAAR, and criminal charges for tax evasion

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax evasion if they knowingly participated in the scheme
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for corporations; criminal penalties for individuals

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties if they failed to comply with AML obligations
