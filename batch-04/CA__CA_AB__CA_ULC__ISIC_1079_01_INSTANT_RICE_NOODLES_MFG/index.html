Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the ABCA; the subscribing shareholder must pay the agreed consideration for issued shares in full, and the consideration is credited to the subscribed capital account. Escrow is not mandated. For a ULC, shareholders have unlimited liability for corporate obligations on winding-up, and the Articles must clearly disclose this non-limited liability feature.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal GST/HST and federal/provincial corporate income tax administrations; Business Registration Online and My Business Account portals at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Alberta corporate income tax is administered on behalf of Alberta by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the ULC under the Alberta corporate registry and obtain an Alberta Corporate Access Number. Step 2: Use CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO), or file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), to obtain the 15-character CRA Business Number (BN) with program accounts: 'RP' for corporate income tax and 'RT' for GST/HST. Step 3: For export activities, register with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) as the exporter and obtain access to the Canadian Export Reporting System/CERS for customs export declarations. Step 4: If Canadian employees are appointed, open the payroll account 'RC'. A non-resident owner should authorise a Canadian representative using CRA Form RC59 or equivalent written authorisation. The BN is the statutory tax identifier for federal and Alberta tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have an IRC-style check-the-box regime for domestic income tax; an Alberta ULC is a corporation resident in Canada and is taxable at the entity level on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act. The 'Pass_Through_Eligible' classification arises from the ULC's unlimited-liability features, which may, for U.S. federal tax purposes, make it an eligible entity with an available Form 8832 check-the-box election to be taxed as a partnership or disregarded entity. Any such U.S. election must be filed with the IRS and is not recognised by the CRA. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules also apply to deny double deductions in cross-border arrangements involving a hybrid entity such as a ULC, including relevant entity classification elections and analyses under the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold): a resident or non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada must register for GST/HST once its worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or in the preceding four calendar quarters; an exporting manufacturer will normally register voluntarily to support zero-rating and input tax credit claims.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods, including instant rice noodles, are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, Section 7 of the Excise Tax Act if the supplier exports the goods by shipping them to a destination outside Canada. The exporter must be registered for GST/HST and must have tangible evidence of physical export such as shipping documentation, carrier records, and customs declarations. Export goods must generally be exported within a reasonable period. Services supplied to non-residents with respect to goods to be exported, or certain offshore services performed exclusively outside Canada, are also zero-rated. Domestic sales of qualifying basic groceries may be zero-rated separately under Schedule VI, Part III if the product qualifies as food for human consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A/E submitted through the Canadian Export Reporting System/CERS or authorised service provider)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical shipment out of Canada and the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank-generated proof of receipt of export sale proceeds in the foreign currency)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control is mandated in Canada. Electronic invoices are acceptable if the prescribed information and records are retained. Specified registrants with taxable supplies over CAD 1 million per year must file GST/HST returns and make remittances electronically. CRA provides GST/HST Web Services/API for return filing, account updates, and registrations, and accepts EDI and XML formats. There is no federal compulsory tax e-invoicing clearance platform.

CRA treats export zero-rating as a high-risk audit area. The exporter must retain the full customs, shipping, and payment bundle; absence of proof can lead to reassessment at 5% GST on the full supply, plus arrears interest and late-filing/remittance penalties. False statements or omissions made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence attract the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the understated net tax under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act, with potential criminal prosecution for fraud. CRA may also claw back input tax credits and impose additional penalties. While Canada has no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline, this dossier uses the 180-day evidentiary benchmark; failure to evidence receipt of export proceeds within that period weakens the zero-rating substantiation and increases adjustment risk.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no general exchange-control restrictions on the purchase, holding, or cross-border transfer of foreign currency. Profit remittances and dividend distributions by the Alberta ULC may be wired freely to non-resident shareholders, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, generally reduced under the Canada-US treaty to 5-15% depending on shareholding; ULC look-through cases require careful treaty eligibility analysis), FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting thresholds, and standard bank compliance screening. No prior central-bank permit or ministerial consent is required for repatriation of capital or profits. The principal legal hurdles are tax-driven (thin capitalization and earnings-stripping rules, treaty-abuse avoidance) rather than exchange-control driven.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian federal law imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds under the Currency Act, the Exchange Fund Act, or the Bank Act. The Alberta ULC may retain export receipts indefinitely in CAD, USD, or other foreign-currency accounts at a Schedule I or II bank. Mandatory obligations are limited to (i) accurate income/expense recognition for CRA purposes, (ii) reporting of any cross-border physical movement of currency or monetary instruments equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 under the cross-border currency reporting regime administered by CBSA/FINTRAC, and (iii) normal anti-money-laundering record-keeping on wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary, clearing, and Retail Payment Activities Act supervisor; Department of Finance Canada holding residual authority under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Exchange Fund Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. E-18); OSFI as the prudential regulator of federally incorporated banks; FINTRAC as the financial intelligence and AML/ATF reporting unit; Payments Canada operating the Lynx high-value clearing system and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). Canada has no single exchange-control administration equivalent to a SAFE-type authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Conditionally viable as a supplementary payment-and-treasury rail, but not as a primary liquidity or statutory-capital depository. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise for Business, Currencycloud) may open multi-currency transaction accounts for the Alberta ULC and route cross-border TT and low-value trade payments efficiently; however, they are not banks under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) and cannot lawfully take Canadian-dollar retail deposits or provide CDIC-insured balances. CAD clearing must still settle through a duly authorized deposit-taking institution, and an offshore EMI offering services in Canada may itself be subject to registration as a Payment Service Provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada. Legal constraints also include the prohibition on representing EMI balances as bank deposits, limited regulatory recourse in insolvency, and heightened bank-level reputational scrutiny of inbound funds routed through an EMI. Therefore, the mandatory capital escrow account must sit at a Schedule I bank; the EMI may be layered on for operational convenience only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity contributions must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 where an intermediary bank is required) and credited to the dedicated Capital Escrow Account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The wire narrative in field 70 must state the exact share-subscription reference, e.g., 'ALBERTA ULC SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - SUBSCRIPTION AGREEMENT DATED [DATE]', together with the board resolution approving the issuance, the share-certificate number, and the CRA business number where requested. Transfers of CAD 10,000 or more trigger ordinary FINTRAC record-keeping; no central-bank authorization is required. The receiving bank will require a board resolution and escrow instruction letter before disbursing funds to the operating account. Investment Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 28) assessment is mandatory for non-Canadian investors: a greenfield specialty-food manufacturing ULC controlled by WTO/CUSMA investors will generally fall below the adjusted enterprise-value review threshold and require only the applicable post-closing notification or an exemption filing; legal counsel must confirm thresholds as adjusted annually. Under no circumstances should the initial capital injection pass through an offshore EMI, as this will elevate the bank's risk rating and may trigger enhanced-due-diligence denial of the escrow relationship.Trade settlements will run on two principal clearing rails. First, SWIFT MT103/TT payments for high-frequency and lower-value settled transactions (up to approximately USD 1,000,000 per wire) using the Canadian Lynx high-value payment system for CAD legs and the global correspondent network for USD/CNH legs, with Swift gpi tracking for same-day credit confirmation. Second, Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or 30-90 day usance) confirmed by the supplier's Chinese/exporting bank for high-value rice, seasoning, and snail-paste purchases; the LC will be issued by the Schedule I Canadian bank and advised through the supplier's correspondent. CAD-denominated payments clear via Payments Canada Lynx; USD disbursements clear via correspondent banking; CNH is used where the Chinese supplier maintains an offshore yuan account, otherwise USD/CNY NDF settlement occurs at the bank treasury desk. Turnover: Under ABCA section 159, every Alberta corporation must appoint an auditor at each annual general meeting unless the corporation is non-distributing and all shareholders consent in writing to waive the audit. A private Alberta ULC may therefore carry on without an independent audit only where unanimous shareholder consent is renewed annually. No revenue or asset size threshold compels an audit for a non-distributing ULC; however, shareholders holding at least 5 percent of the voting shares may requisition an audit under ABCA section 162, and lenders, CRA or CFIA may externally require audited financial statements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% federal Part I + 11% Alberta provincial = 34% combined statutory headline rate for active business income of an Alberta ULC maintaining corporate tax status. If a valid ULC flow-through election under Alberta law is recognized, the entity remains a corporation for federal Part I purposes but may attribute certain tax attributes; the combined 34% rate remains the baseline for assessing the Canadian corporate entity unless a federal specific incentive applies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder (subsection 212(2)).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The CRA requires a complete contemporaneous transfer pricing (TP) documentation package, including a Local File and Master File, for any non-arm's length cross-border transaction exceeding CAD 1,000,000 in gross annual value (Information Circular IC 87-2R; subsection 247(4)). This applies to transactions with the Chinese parent, the Chinese rice noodle manufacturing affiliate, any Hong Kong sourcing entity, and any global IP licensing entity. The Alberta ULC must maintain documentation for: (1) purchase of rice flour, cassava starch, and seasoning ingredients from China or Southeast Asia; (2) royalties for the 'Luosifen' trademark and retort recipes; (3) shared management, marketing, and IT services; (4) shareholder loans or back-to-back financing. CRA requires documents be prepared before the filing deadline and retained for 6 years; failure to provide documentation within 3 months of a CRA request shifts the burden of proof and triggers penalties.

Economic substance scope: Although Alberta has no general Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) style regime, the CRA applies equivalent substance doctrines through the OECD BEPS Principal Purpose Test (PPT), the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under subsection 245(2), and place of effective management (POEM) analysis. An Alberta ULC must demonstrate real economic substance: physical retort manufacturing plant in Alberta, full-time production employees, actual management and control exercised by Canadian directors (or if controlled by non-resident directors, strategic decisions must be documented), and entrepreneurial risk borne by the Canadian entity. A shell ULC interposed solely to access the Canada–Hong Kong DTA's 5% dividend rate or the Canada–China DTA's 10% rate will be denied treaty benefits under the PPT. The CRA also recharacterizes intercompany charges that lack commercial substance, and if a ULC is a pure flow-through/attribution vehicle with no Alberta activity, the Chinese parent may be deemed to have an Alberta permanent establishment under the Canada–China DTA.
