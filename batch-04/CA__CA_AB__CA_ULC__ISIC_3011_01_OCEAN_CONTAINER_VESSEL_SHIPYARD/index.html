Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up or authorized capital and no escrow account or capital deposit requirements under the ABCA for a ULC; nominal authorized capital (e.g., 100 unlimited-liability common shares at CAD 0.01 par value) is lawful, but shareholders of a ULC carry joint and several unlimited liability for the corporation's obligations.

Funding & unlock path: Alberta Fire Code certification under the Safety Codes Act for fire suppression, extinguishing systems and marine drydock emergency access; structural load inspections under the Alberta Building Code for fabrication halls, crane foundations and vessel-support cradles; and Alberta Occupational Health and Safety clearance for confined spaces in vessel holds, overhead crane operations, hazardous materials storage (WHMIS/TDG) and emergency response planning.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up or authorized capital and no escrow account or capital deposit requirements under the ABCA for a ULC; nominal authorized capital (e.g., 100 unlimited-liability common shares at CAD 0.01 par value) is lawful, but shareholders of a ULC carry joint and several unlimited liability for the corporation's obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An Alberta ULC is a Canadian taxable corporation, not a pass-through entity. For U.S. federal tax purposes, the U.S. owner may file Treasury Form 8832 to classify the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership (check-the-box election), while Canada continues to treat it as a separate taxpayer. Canada does not provide a domestic check-the-box election. The ULC may use ITA subsection 85 rollover elections on asset transfers, and must comply with Canada's hybrid mismatch disallowance rules (ITA section 18.5 and related provisions) if cross-border arrangements produce hybrid outcomes; no elective waiver of those rules is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: a person making taxable supplies in Canada (including zero-rated exports) must register for GST/HST when its total taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; a commercial shipyard will exceed this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A new deep-sea container vessel sold by the Alberta ULC shipyard may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if: (i) the ULC is a GST/HST registrant; (ii) the supply is a taxable supply of tangible personal property; (iii) the vessel is physically exported from Canada, generally within 60 days after delivery or within the longer period permitted under CRA's GST/HST Memorandum 7-2; and (iv) the registrant retains sufficient written evidence of export such as a CBSA/CERS export declaration, ocean bill of lading, dock receipt, delivery confirmation, and proof of payment from the non-resident buyer. Integrated shipbuilding services are zero-rated with the vessel; separately invoiced services on exported goods may also qualify under the same export evidence rules.

  • CBSA/CERS electronic Export Declaration (B13A) containing the export transaction number
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, or delivery receipt and sailing confirmation showing the vessel departed Canadian territorial waters
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list for the vessel and all installed shipyard equipment
  • Non-resident purchaser agreement or certificate of foreign ownership evidencing the export destination and foreign owner
  • Bank payment evidence (SWIFT instruction, bank statement, or remittance receipt) demonstrating payment of the export consideration

Canada does not mandate a real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance system for GST/HST, and there is no digital stamping regime. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns for most registrants (e.g., all registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million, and generally all corporate registrants). For exports, CBSA CERS electronic export declarations are mandatory for controlled goods and for goods valued above CAD 2,000.

Canada does not prescribe a fixed 180-day FX repatriation period; the 180-day benchmark is the pipeline's assumed documentary and payment cycle and should not be read as a Canadian statutory deadline. CRA may reassess within 4 years (or 7 years for misrepresentation or neglect), deny input tax credits, recharacterize zero-rated exports as taxable, and apply interest. Penalties include ETA failure-to-register and failure-to-collect penalties, false reporting penalties up to 25% of the amount erroneously claimed in egregious cases, and criminal prosecution for wilful evasion. Clawback of GST/HST plus interest and penalties applies when export documentation is incomplete or when the vessel is put to domestic or cabotage use in Canada.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 and contemporaneous TP documentation (Local File/Master File in substance) are required where aggregate non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000 per taxation year (ITA s.233.1). Country-by-Country reporting applies to Alberta ULCs in MNE groups with consolidated global revenue exceeding CAD 850,000,000 (Reg. s.233.8).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulation fee regime (unlike UAE). However, a de facto substance test operates through ITA s.245 (GAAR), the Canada-US Treaty Limitation-on-Benefits clause (Article XXIX A), and the BEPS MLI Principal Purpose Test. The ULC must demonstrate real commercial activity in Alberta (shipyard employees, physical premises, procurement and production contracts, board meetings, bank accounts, invoicing and risk management) to retain the 5% dividend WHT, treaty benefits, and arm's-length intercompany pricing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus full reassessment and compound interest; on indictment imprisonment of up to five years (s.239(2)); CRA public disclosure of the conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct or knowingly acquiesce in the evasion face criminal personal liability, property seizure, assets freeze, restraint orders, and travel embargoes as a bail/probation condition; disqualification from acting as a director under Alberta corporate law.
Unremitted Part XIII Withholding / Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: 10% penalty plus daily prescribed interest on the unremitted amount; the Crown holds a secured charge on all ULC assets (s.227(8), s.227(10.1)); CRA may garnish receivables and shipyard bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the full unremitted WHT, interest, and penalties (s.227.1); no reimbursement from the ULC is permitted; personal bankruptcy does not discharge the liability; cross-border collection may proceed under the Canada-US treaty and Commonwealth enforcement arrangements.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to arm's-length amounts under s.247(2); transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net upward adjustment (s.247(3)); late/grossly negligent T106 penalty of CAD 500 per month (maximum CAD 12,000 over 24 months); interest on the adjusted tax.

Director personal liability: Directors approving related-party invoices without TP benchmarking are personally assessed for remittances in insolvent scenarios; the 'reasonable efforts' exemption is unavailable where no contemporaneous documentation existed at the filing date.
GAAR / Treaty-Shopping Shell ULC

Statutory sanction: GAAR avoidance transaction voided (s.245); resulting tax benefit reassessed with compound interest; denial of treaty benefits under the Limitation-on-Benefits clause and MLI PPT; potential gross-negligence penalties under s.163.2.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify artificial arrangements to lenders, CRA, or FINTRAC face civil restitution and personal assessment for taxes where fraudulent conveyance is found; courts may issue a non-asset dissipation order and a travel ban pending criminal tax proceedings.
AML / FINTRAC Anti-Money-Laundering Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalty of up to CAD 500,000 per contravention for a corporate entity under the PCMLTFA; money laundering is an indictable offence under Criminal Code s.462.31 punishable by up to 10 years' imprisonment; concurrent CRA penalties under the excise and income tax acts.

Director personal liability: Officers directing concealment of beneficial owners or transaction flows incur personal criminal liability; search warrants, restraint orders, and proceeds-of-crime forfeiture may attach to personal and corporate assets; directors may face sanctions screening debarment in maritime export-credit and letter-of-credit arrangements.
