Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) administers federal income tax and the federal GST; Alberta corporate income tax is collected by the CRA under the Alberta Tax Collection Agreement; corporate and name registration is completed through the Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta), www.corporate-registry.gov.ab.ca; federal business registration and tax-account enrolment is effected through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, www.canada.ca/businessregistration.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the ULC under the Alberta Business Corporations Act through a registered legal agent; obtain the Alberta incorporation number and articles. Step 2: The Alberta Corporate Registry transmits incorporation data to the CRA, which auto-issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN); if no automatic integration occurs, apply for the BN via BRO. Step 3: Enroll for the Corporate Income Tax program account (RC0001) covering federal Part I tax and Alberta corporate income tax; this is mandatory for all resident corporations, effective within 30 days of incorporation. Step 4: Enroll for the GST/HST program account (RT0001) on BRO as soon as taxable supplies exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or C$30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available to preserve input tax credits and to zero-rate export sales. Step 5: Enroll for the Payroll Deductions program account (RP0001) upon hiring employees and, where dividends, interest, or royalties are paid to non-resident shareholders, for the Non-Resident Withholding program account (RN0001). Step 6: Maintain an Alberta registered office and records address; Alberta removed director residency requirements effective 2021, so wholly non-resident control is permissible, provided the unlimited-liability nature of the ULC is documented in the agency agreements and shareholder resolutions.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: For Canadian tax purposes the Alberta ULC is a resident corporation subject to entity-level Part I federal tax at the general rate of 15% (the 9% small-business rate is unavailable because a non-resident-controlled ULC cannot qualify as a Canadian-controlled private corporation) plus Alberta general corporate income tax at 8%, for a combined 23% headline rate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: For Canadian tax purposes the Alberta ULC is a resident corporation subject to entity-level Part I federal tax at the general rate of 15% (the 9% small-business rate is unavailable because a non-resident-controlled ULC cannot qualify as a Canadian-controlled private corporation) plus Alberta general corporate income tax at 8%, for a combined 23% headline rate. For U.S. federal tax purposes the Alberta ULC is eligible for disregarded-entity treatment where it is 100% owned by a U.S. person and a check-the-box election (Form 8832) is filed; the Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty Article IV(7) coordinates this hybrid status and limits treaty benefits to qualifying persons. GST/HST: the ULC is a distinct 'person' and is a separate registrant in its own right.

Available election forms

1) U.S. Treasury check-the-box election (Form 8832) classifying the Alberta ULC as a disregarded entity, permitting U.S. shareholders to report Canadian-sourced income on a pass-through basis. 2) GST/HST consolidated registration election under ETA s. 273 available to related registrants. 3) Net-tax streamlined accounting election under ETA s. 227 for qualifying small suppliers. 4) Income Tax Act s. 17(9) election deeming a reasonable commercial rate of interest on foreign receivables or shareholder loans outstanding beyond 180 days. 5) Treaty-based Part XIII withholding rate relief on dividends (5% or 0% for qualifying 80%+ corporate shareholders) claimed through CRA forms NR301/NR302.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000: the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148 is exceeded when worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports, exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or C$30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters; registration is then mandatory. Voluntary registrants on the standard 5% net-tax basis may claim full input tax credits. Marketplace platform rules (ETA ss. 241.1 and 242.1) may deem online platforms such as Shopify to be the supplier for sales to Canadian consumers, with no small-supplier exemption for non-resident platform operators.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: designer blind-box toys are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V where the supplier exports the goods or delivers them to a common carrier, customs broker, or exporter for export and obtains prescribed proof of export (ETA s. 21). Services: rotocasting, injection molding, airbrush finishing, design, and IP-licensing services supplied to non-resident recipients are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 5-10 where the service is performed wholly outside Canada, or where the supply relates to goods, intangible property, or real property situated outside Canada and the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST. Zero-rated exports must be reported on the GST return, preserve input tax credits, and attract no GST. Canada imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline on export receivables; the 180-day benchmark is a prudent credit-risk limit aligned with export financing standards and relevant to deemed-interest exposure under ITA s. 17.

  • Form B13A Export Declaration (CBSA) or approved electronic export reporting for goods valued over C$2,000
  • Commercial invoice and packing list reflecting HS/NAICS 339930 classification, value, quantity, and consignee
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or courier proof of dispatch showing the Canadian port of exit
  • CBSA proof-of-export certificate or customs broker's certificate of exportation
  • Foreign purchase order or contract evidencing the non-resident buyer relationship
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation of foreign-currency receipt

Canada has no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, API-reporting, or digital-stamping mandate for GST invoices. The CRA permits electronic record-keeping, requires retention of source books, invoices, and control documents for six years (ETA s. 286), and requires GST/HST and T2 returns to be filed electronically where annual taxable supplies exceed C$1.5M. The Pan-Canadian Peppol BIS 3.0 e-invoicing framework is voluntary, not mandatory; Shopify Payments, Stripe, and local POS transaction logs are admissible audit evidence.

CRA may reassess GST/HST within four years of the filing date (ETA s. 298); the limitation period is unlimited for misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. If export zero-rating is improperly claimed on blind-box sales, the company faces: (i) reassessment of net tax plus compounding daily interest at the prescribed rate plus 4%; (ii) gross-negligence penalties of the greater of C$100 or 25% of the tax evaded (ETA s. 285); (iii) failure-to-file penalties of 1% of the tax due plus 0.25% per month for up to 12 months (ETA s. 280); and (iv) criminal tax-evasion fines of 50%-200% of evaded tax or imprisonment for up to two years (ETA s. 326). Because the entity is an unlimited liability corporation, all Canadian and foreign shareholders are jointly and severally liable for CRA claims, and CRA may also pursue third-party penalties (ETA s. 288) against advisors or agents who participate in fraudulent export claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. An Alberta unlimited liability corporation may generally receive foreign capital, pay foreign suppliers, purchase foreign currency, repay shareholder loans, pay dividends, and remit profits without prior exchange-control approval. Principal constraints are corporate-law solvency requirements, accurate accounting, beneficial-ownership verification, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, transfer-pricing compliance, Canadian withholding tax on specified non-resident payments, and possible Part XIII withholding on dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, or similar amounts. Treaty relief, where available, normally requires beneficial-owner and residency documentation. An Alberta ULC may produce adverse or inconsistent classification outcomes for non-Canadian owners under foreign tax regimes; a cross-border tax classification and hybrid-entity review is required before implementing profit repatriation or shareholder financing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no general statutory deadline for repatriating export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts, be converted at market rates, or be applied directly to permitted business payments. Banks may request invoices, customs records, contracts, shipping evidence, source-of-funds information, and transaction-purpose details. Large cash or equivalent transactions and suspicious activity may trigger FINTRAC reporting, including prescribed reporting for cash transactions of CAD 10,000 or more and electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, subject to applicable aggregation and reporting rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing exchange controls or mandatory export-proceeds surrender. Financial institutions and payment businesses remain subject to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and applicable sanctions administered under the Department of Foreign Affairs, Trade and Development Act and related regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and treasury support; low as a substitute for a Canadian primary operating bank. An offshore electronic-money institution cannot ordinarily provide a Canadian deposit account, Canadian deposit insurance, or unrestricted domestic payment functionality merely by serving an Alberta ULC remotely. Use is subject to the provider's licensing perimeter, Canadian foreign-money-services-business obligations where applicable, sanctions and AML controls, Shopify Payments or Stripe underwriting, settlement-country restrictions, chargeback reserves, transaction-volume limits, and bank-account verification. A Canadian payment service provider or foreign MSB may require registration with FINTRAC or an equivalent regulated status, Canadian agent and recordkeeping controls, and enhanced source-of-funds evidence. Maintain a Canadian bank account for payroll, taxes, supplier payments, refunds, and settlement continuity.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payment collection and treasury support; low as a substitute for a Canadian primary operating bank. An offshore electronic-money institution cannot ordinarily provide a Canadian deposit account, Canadian deposit insurance, or unrestricted domestic payment functionality merely by serving an Alberta ULC remotely. Use is subject to the provider's licensing perimeter, Canadian foreign-money-services-business obligations where applicable, sanctions and AML controls, Shopify Payments or Stripe underwriting, settlement-country restrictions, chargeback reserves, transaction-volume limits, and bank-account verification. A Canadian payment service provider or foreign MSB may require registration with FINTRAC or an equivalent regulated status, Canadian agent and recordkeeping controls, and enhanced source-of-funds evidence. Maintain a Canadian bank account for payroll, taxes, supplier payments, refunds, and settlement continuity.
