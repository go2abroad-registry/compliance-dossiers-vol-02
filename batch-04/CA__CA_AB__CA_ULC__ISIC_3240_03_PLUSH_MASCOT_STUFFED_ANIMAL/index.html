Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act (ETA), Schedule VI, a GST/HST registrant may zero-rate the supply of tangible personal property (the plush mascots) when the goods are supplied to a non-resident and delivered outside Canada for consumption outside Canada, or delivered by the registrant to a common carrier for export. For goods shipped under a normal export sale, the registrant must maintain proof that the goods were physically exported within a reasonable time (generally within 60 days, unless a longer period is reasonable), and if the purchaser exports the goods without the registrant shipping them, the conditions of Part V, Sections 5 or 6 of Schedule VI must be met. Offshore services directly related to exported goods may also be zero-rated.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration (or Canadian Automated Export Declarations report number) for shipments subject to export reporting
  • Commercial invoice showing export sale, destination country, incoterms, and zero-rated tax status
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical transportation outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment proof from the foreign buyer
  • GST/HST export evidence checklist and sales documentation supporting the export exemption

No mandatory real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping is currently required by the CRA. CRA accepts electronic invoices, and GST/HST returns may be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or API integrations. Alberta imposes no separate provincial sales tax filing or clearance system.

CRA may reassess within 4 years of the reporting period, or indefinitely if misrepresentation or fraud is proven. Improper zero-rating claims are subject to the ETA s. 285.1 penalty of 50% of the incorrectly claimed tax refund or credit, plus compounding interest. Knowingly making false statements or evasion can trigger criminal prosecution, fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, and imprisonment. CRA may claw back any previously claimed input tax credits or refunds and reassess to charge GST/HST plus penalties and arrears interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada generally permits unrestricted receipt, retention, conversion, and remittance of export proceeds, dividends, interest, royalties, shareholder-loan repayments, and capital. A Canadian ULC must satisfy corporate-law solvency requirements, contractual restrictions, withholding-tax obligations, transfer-pricing requirements, beneficial-ownership verification, sanctions screening, and bank transaction-monitoring controls before remitting funds. Dividends and other payments to non-residents may be subject to Canadian withholding tax unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty. Related-party financing must be commercially supportable and documented. Cross-border transactions may require tax filings, information reporting, customs records, and records supporting the stated economic purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring conversion of foreign-currency export receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the company's discretion. The company should retain contracts, invoices, shipping records, customs documentation, payment confirmations, and foreign-exchange records. Banks may impose internal settlement, documentation, sanctions, or anti-money-laundering requirements. Electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, or aggregated transfers meeting applicable reporting thresholds, may trigger FINTRAC reporting by the financial institution; this is a reporting obligation and not a surrender rule.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing capital controls or mandatory export-proceeds surrender. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers federal anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist-financing requirements for reporting entities, while the Canada Border Services Agency and the Canada Revenue Agency administer customs, tax, and certain cross-border reporting obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection, foreign-currency conversion, and outbound transfers, subject to provider jurisdiction and product scope. Canada does not operate a single European-style electronic-money-institution licensing regime. A foreign digital EMI or payment provider may be unable to provide unrestricted Canadian payment services without complying with applicable Canadian requirements, including registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act where it performs in-scope retail payment activities, FINTRAC registration as a money services business where applicable, sanctions compliance, client-money safeguarding, privacy obligations, and provincial consumer or securities requirements. An offshore EMI should not be treated as a substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking account, and CAD account availability, Canadian clearing access, insolvency protection, settlement timing, and account termination risk must be contractually assessed.

Route equity contributions and shareholder loans from an account held in the name of the documented investor or lender through a regulated bank using a transparent wire description such as equity contribution, share subscription, or shareholder loan. Canada does not prescribe a universal central-bank purpose-code system for these transfers; the sending and receiving banks may require their own payment-purpose classification. Maintain the subscription agreement or loan agreement, board and shareholder approvals, capitalization table, source-of-funds evidence, beneficial-owner information, and remittance confirmation.
