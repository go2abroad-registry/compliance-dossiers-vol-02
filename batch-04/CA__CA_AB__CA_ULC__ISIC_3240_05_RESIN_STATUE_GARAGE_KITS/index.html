Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), with the 'My Business Account' online portal. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act, using the same Business Number registration. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) separately administers export documentation and customs control.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity forming or owning an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) must first obtain an Alberta corporate registry account through Corporate Registry/Service Alberta. The ULC then obtains a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). The BN is the single CRA tax identifier. The taxpayer must add the required program accounts to the BN: GST/HST (RT account for GST/HST registrant), payroll (RP account if employees), customs/import-export (RM account if exporting or importing). No separate Alberta tax identification number is issued; Alberta corporate tax is filed under the BN on the Alberta Corporate Tax Return. Foreign entities without a Canadian address must appoint a resident agent or representative and may register directly with CRA, subject to the CRA's non-resident registration procedures.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

For Canadian income tax purposes, a ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation and no Canadian check-the-box election is available to reclassify it as a partnership or disregarded entity. For US federal income tax, the ULC may be eligible to file IRS Form 8832 and elect disregarded entity or partnership pass-through treatment, provided it meets the check-the-box eligibility rules. Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.2, 20.2 and related provisions) apply to cross-border payments and do not create an elective mechanism; taxpayers may only make factual determinations to avoid or adjust mismatched deduction/income outcomes. There is no other statutory entity classification election available under Alberta or Canadian federal tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in gross revenue from worldwide taxable supplies, or from Canadian taxable supplies for non-residents, in any four consecutive calendar quarters or over the immediate 12-month period; a person becomes a GST/HST registrant when their taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold. In Alberta, only the federal 5% GST applies; Alberta has no provincial sales tax or HST component.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for export goods under Division IV of the Excise Tax Act applies when the supplier sells and exports tangible personal property by delivering it to a non-resident outside Canada or to a destination outside Canada before use, or if the supply would have been zero-rated under the usual export rules. For services, zero-rating generally applies to a supply made to a non-resident person who is outside Canada at the time the supply is made, unless the service relates to Canadian real property, tangible personal property that is not exported, or another excepted case. The supplier must obtain documentary evidence that the goods were exported or the service was supplied to a non-resident recipient within the required period, retain that evidence in the books and records, and include the supplies on the GST/HST return for the applicable reporting period.

  • Customs Export Declaration prepared in the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS/Form B13A) or CESAR proof of report
  • Commercial invoice and packing list reflecting the resin statue goods and stated export destination
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or carrier-generated shipping document showing transport to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or confirmed bank transfer record evidencing payment and foreign currency repatriation

Not applicable. Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system, digital stamping mandate, government API clearance platform, or continuous transaction control system for GST/HST. Electronic invoicing is permitted and accepted by the CRA, but it is not mandatory for sales or exports. GST/HST registrants generally file returns and remit through CRA NETFILE / My Business Account on a monthly, quarterly, or fiscal-year basis; no tax authority pre-approval of each invoice is required.

The CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess GST/HST if the exporter fails to produce contemporaneous export evidence. A false statement in an export return or claim can trigger penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including significant monetary penalties calculated on the amount of tax sought to be evaded, plus interest and retrospective clawbacks. In cases of fraud or gross negligence, the CRA may assess penalties up to 25% of the improperly claimed amount, apply interest on arrears, and refer the matter to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada for criminal charges. Civil clawback reassessments are generally permitted for the normal four-year reassessment period, but the period may be extended indefinitely for misrepresentation or wilful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) operates under a fully convertible, free-floating exchange rate regime with no exchange controls. Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement and does not restrict current or capital account payments. Profit and dividend repatriation from an Alberta ULC is unimpeded by exchange-control licensing; the only structural cost is the statutory Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (generally 25% on dividends, interest, and royalties, subject to applicable tax treaty reductions, e.g., 15% under the Canada-US Tax Convention or the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement). A ULC is treated as a corporation for Canadian tax purposes and may also be subject to branch tax considerations if constituted as a discretionary pass-through for Canadian tax elections; this affects tax quantum but not convertibility or remittance legality.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender, mandatory repatriation, or conversion ratio requirements on export proceeds. An Alberta ULC may retain foreign currency receipts indefinitely in domestic or offshore operating accounts and convert at commercial discretion. Statutory reporting obligations are limited to FINTRAC international electronic funds transfer reports (for EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more), large cash transaction reports (CAD 10,000+), and cross-border currency movement declarations to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for physical currency of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CTF transaction surveillance and reporting requirements are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident shareholders should be effected as SWIFT MT103 payments (or Fedwire/CHIPS transfers from the United States) credited to the ULC's CAD operational account at a Schedule I bank. The remittance narrative must state 'Unrestricted Equity Contribution – Proceeds of Share Issuance' and reference the corporate registration number and board resolution date. Inward CAD transfers settle through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day finality; real-time settlement is available via the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for eligible low-value payments. FINTRAC international EFT reporting is mandatory for any inward transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; the bank will complete this filing, but the ULC must supply the ordering customer's full legal name, account details, and source-of-funds declaration. The board must contemporaneously record the injection in the capital account ledger and issue the corresponding shares to substantiate equity treatment and avoid re-characterization as shareholder debt subject to the Income Tax Act thin-capitalization rules (IT-109R).Supplier and trade settlements should be cleared as follows: (i) SWIFT MT103 gpi (Global Payments Innovation) for international bank-to-bank wires to resin and pigment suppliers, with full ISO 20022 remittance data citing the purchase order and invoice numbers; (ii) irrevocable usance or sight Commercial Letters of Credit (advised through the supplier's confirming bank) for large consignments exceeding CAD 25,000, minimizing prepayment risk; (iii) domestic Canadian raw material and utility disbursements via EFT through Payments Canada, Interac e-Transfer for amounts below CAD 20,000, or LVTS for high-value time-critical payments; (iv) USD-denominated disbursements via the ULC's USD clearing account settled through cross-border ACH (the US ACH network) or SWIFT to the US beneficiary bank; and (v) CNY-denominated supplier payments, if required, cleared via an offshore CNY clearing arrangement such as the Hong Kong RMB Clearing Bank or a Singapore RMB facility, compliant with the non-resident RMB conversion rules applicable to Canadian entities. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility as an ancillary collection layer, not as the primary treasury host. The ULC may lawfully maintain balances with offshore fintech payment service providers and FCA-regulated EMIs in the UK/EU and licensed US money transmitters (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Meridian) to optimize USD/EUR/CNY collection from Stripe and PayPal preorder rails. Legal constraints: an EMI is not a Schedule I/II Canadian bank under the Bank Act; funds are not insured by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC); balances are commingled with EMI operational accounts and carry counterparty and insolvency exposure. The primary operating account and all tax, payroll, and LVTS settlement functions must sit with a domestic chartered bank. Policy recommendation: maintain EMI balances below CAD 50,000 per institution and sweep to the chartered bank at least daily.
