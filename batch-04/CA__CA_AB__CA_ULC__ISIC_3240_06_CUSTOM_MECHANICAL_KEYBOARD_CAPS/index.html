Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Agence du revenu du Canada — national tax administration; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-taxes/business-number.html. Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered for the Province of Alberta by the CRA under the Provincial Tax Collection Agreement; Alberta Treasury Board and Finance administers only the corporate income tax of Alberta financial institutions, which is not applicable to this manufacturing ULC.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta ULC: (1) incorporate under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) through the Alberta Corporate Registry, maintain an Alberta registered office and appoint at least one director (Alberta imposes no director residency requirement, no minimum capital and no non-resident ownership approval); (2) obtain the Alberta Certificate of Incorporation and Corporate Access Number; (3) obtain the federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' online through Business Registration Online, or by phone, fax or mail; (4) activate on the BN the program accounts required for a custom-manufacturing exporter: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST) and, if employees are engaged, RP (payroll); (5) the CRA assigns a 9-digit BN with two-letter program identifiers (e.g., RC0001, RT0001); (6) file the GST/HST registration before commencing taxable activity and notify the CRA of changes of address, directors or business activity within the legislated notification periods.

Pass_Through_Eligible for U.S. federal income tax purposes because the unlimited-liability shareholder may elect under check-the-box to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership, creating a hybrid entity; the Canadian entity-level tax status is not altered by the U.S. election. — For a foreign-owned Alberta ULC: (1) incorporate under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) through the Alberta Corporate Registry, maintain an Alberta registered office and appoint at least one director (Alberta imposes no director residency requirement, no minimum capital and no non-resident ownership approval); (2) obtain the Alberta Certificate of Incorporation and Corporate Access Number; (3) obtain the federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' online through Business Registration Online, or by phone, fax or mail; (4) activate on the BN the program accounts required for a custom-manufacturing exporter: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST) and, if employees are engaged, RP (payroll); (5) the CRA assigns a 9-digit BN with two-letter program identifiers (e.g., RC0001, RT0001); (6) file the GST/HST registration before commencing taxable activity and notify the CRA of changes of address, directors or business activity within the legislated notification periods.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation for Canadian income tax purposes: an Alberta ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under ITA s. 89(1), resident in Canada and taxable on worldwide income at the federal general corporate rate plus Alberta provincial corporate income tax collected by the CRA. Pass_Through_Eligible for U.S. federal income tax purposes because the unlimited-liability shareholder may elect under check-the-box to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership, creating a hybrid entity; the Canadian entity-level tax status is not altered by the U.S. election.

Available election forms

(i) U.S. check-the-box election under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3 to classify the Alberta ULC as a disregarded entity (single member) or partnership (multi-member) for U.S. federal tax; (ii) fiscally transparent entity relief under Article IV(6) and (7) of the Canada-U.S. Income Tax Convention as amended by the Fifth Protocol, allowing income of the ULC to be treated as derived by its U.S. resident member where appropriate; (iii) Canadian hybrid-mismatch constraints under ITA ss. 18.2–18.4, with available elections to recognize or recharacterize hybrid mismatch payments for interest deductibility purposes; and (iv) GST/HST election under ETA s. 156 to treat supplies between closely related registrants as made for nil consideration, available where the ULC and a related registrant satisfy the closely-related test.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in a single calendar quarter or cumulatively over four consecutive calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory within 30 days of exceeding the threshold (ETA s. 240).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant zero-rates double-shot PBT plastic keycaps supplied for export under ETA s. 21 where the supplier exports the goods as part of the supply, or under ETA s. 22 where a non-resident purchaser exports the goods after acquisition; the goods must actually leave Canada within a reasonable time, the supply must be made to a non-resident, and the registrant must hold sufficient books and records to link the supply to physical export and to the foreign consideration received. Related design and digital-delivery services supplied to a non-resident may separately qualify for zero-rating under the ETA's non-resident services rule where the prescribed conditions (including the non-resident status of the recipient and the absence of Canadian real-property or Canadian-delivered tangible-property connection) are satisfied.

  • Canadian Export Declaration — CBSA B13A (or approved electronic export report / e-manifest) filed where the value of the exported keycaps exceeds CAD 2,000 or the goods are controlled or regulated
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list identifying the non-resident consignee outside Canada, the HS classification (HS 3926.90 or HS 8473.30 as applicable) and the stated export value
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Canada to the foreign destination, including the carrier-assigned tracking and delivery location
  • Carrier manifest, Canada export transaction report, or third-party logistics door-delivery record indicating the goods left Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent SWIFT/MT103 wire-conformation or payment-gateway settlement statement showing repatriation of the export proceeds to Canada

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoice acceptance for GST/HST purposes. Registrants with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1 million must file GST/HST returns and rebate applications electronically under ETA s. 238.02. Optional e-invoicing may follow the Pan-Canadian e-invoice standard (PEPPOL BIS) under the CRA's voluntary e-invoicing service; regardless of invoicing format, the ULC must retain full invoicing and export evidence for six years following the end of the relevant reporting period.

The CRA may audit export zero-rating claims within the 4-year normal reassessment period under ETA s. 298; if documentary proof of actual export is deficient, CRA reverses the zero-rating, assesses 5% GST on the supply, disallows related input tax credits and charges interest on the understated tax. A false statement made knowingly or in circumstances amounting to gross negligence triggers a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax understated under ETA s. 285, and wilful evasion is prosecutable under ETA s. 327 with substantial fines and imprisonment. Goods diverted back into Canada, or export proceeds not received from the foreign purchaser within the 180-day tax-control benchmark, cause retrospective clawback of the zero-rating and require the ULC to self-assess GST; note that Canada imposes no exchange-control statute fixing a 180-day repatriation deadline, but the 180-day benchmark is applied here as internal transfer-pricing and zero-rating substantiation policy.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. Non-residents may freely repatriate equity contributions and trade proceeds, but dividends and deemed distributions from an Alberta ULC are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, generally 25% unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty (e.g., 15% under Canada-China DTA). No prior administrative approval is required for profit remittance; the Ministry of Finance does not impose material quantitative limits on foreign exchange transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists. Export receipts from double-shot PBT keycap sales may be retained in any foreign currency. FinTRAC EFT and large cash transaction reporting obligations apply if any single transaction or series of transactions in a 24-hour period exceeds CAD 10,000, or if the physical cross-border movement of such receipts exceeds CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC); foreign exchange policy is coordinated with the Department of Finance Canada and OSFI; BOC does not operate exchange controls or license foreign-exchange dealers for internal convertibility purposes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Canada does not issue dedicated fintech EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licenses similar to the EEA regime. Offshore EMIs such as Wise and Revolut Business may provide workplace and float accounts only via a sponsor bank holding Canadian deposits, and they are not CDIC-insured. For an Alberta ULC requiring capital escrow and commercial-lending-style treasury clearing, these EMIs are viable as receiving or payout payment rails (notably in CAD/USD), but they are not appropriate for statutory capital escrow, ULC statutory filings, or fully segregated multi-currency clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The ULC shall receive the capital injection via SWIFT MT103. The inbound wire must identify the beneficiary bank under Field 57A and beneficiary party under Field 59. The Ordered Party field is the parent entity's issuing bank. In Field 70, the payment narrative must state '/EQIC/ EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION FOR ULC CORPORATE TREASURY' and the exact ULC certificate number to ensure no conversion to debt. As there is no Canadian FX control, no pre-approval is required from BOC, but the bank must report the transfer to FinTRAC if the value is CAD 100,000 or above under the EFT report requirement. For any trade-related capital contribution arising from unpaid share capital, the company must attach a notarized paid-in capital resolution to the bank signatory form.Direct trade settlements to Chinese keycap suppliers shall be routed via SWIFT MT103 (bank-to-bank) using the supplier's Chinese-issued USD or RMB account. For first-time suppliers with an invoice above USD 50,000, a Commercial Letter of Credit (form MT700) is recommended, backed by a pro-forma invoice, bill of lading, and inspection certificate; for established suppliers, D/P (Documents against Payment) via the sending bank's correspondent corridor is permitted. USD payments will clear over the U.S. correspondent Fedwire instead of Canada's Lynx real-time rail; CAD payments through Lynx are not economical for cross-border settlement.
