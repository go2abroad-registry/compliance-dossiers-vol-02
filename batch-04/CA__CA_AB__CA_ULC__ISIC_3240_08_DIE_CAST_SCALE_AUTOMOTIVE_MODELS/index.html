Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Ottawa. E-services portals: My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO) (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-registration-online.html). Alberta corporate income tax is assessed and collected by the CRA under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement (Alberta Corporate Tax Act, RSA 2000, c. A-15). Provincial incorporation registry: Alberta Corporate Registry (alberta.ca/corporate-registry).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the ULC under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta), RSA 2000, c. B-9, Part 8.1, via Alberta Corporate Registry; no minimum capital requirement; automatic domestic legal personality. Step 2: Obtain the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online through BRO, by fax, or by mail to the CRA; online BRO issuance is immediate for Canadian-resident corporations. Step 3: Open program accounts under the BN: GST/HST ('RT' prefix), payroll ('RP') if employees are hired, customs import-export ('RM'), and corporate income tax ('RC'); the CRA automatically establishes an RC account for incorporated taxpayers. Step 4: For a non-resident-owned ULC, the GST/HST account must be opened from the first calendar quarter in which total taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000, or earlier if voluntary registration is elected to recover input tax credits. Step 5: Retain the BN registration letter, CRA-issued access code for My Business Account, and display the GST/HST registration number on all commercial invoices and customs documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (hybrid). Canadian domestic default: Entity_Level_Taxation. For Canadian federal and Alberta income tax purposes, an Alberta ULC is a corporation deemed resident in Canada under subsection 250(4) of the Income Tax Act, RSC 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), taxable at entity level under Part I (federal 15% + Alberta 8% = 23% general combined rate; no small business deduction where controlled by a non-resident). For United States federal income tax, the ULC is an eligible entity that may be classified as a disregarded entity or partnership under Treasury Regulation §301.7701-3, unless it validly elects corporation status. Non-resident shareholders are taxed in Canada only through Part XIII withholding on distributions and are not within the Canadian territorial scope for active business income, except where a permanent establishment exists.

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic 'check-the-box' regime; the ULC is automatically a corporation for Canadian tax and cannot elect partnership status. For US purposes, the ULC may file Form 8832 (Entity Classification Election): default with a single US owner is disregarded entity; multiple owners default to partnership; corporate status requires an affirmative election. Canada-US Income Tax Convention Article IV(7) contains a treaty tie-breaker deeming the ULC resident only in the Contracting State where it is fully liable to tax, preventing double-dipping and dual-residence; the competent authorities may jointly terminate or modify the paragraph. Hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act sections 18.4-18.6 and 20.2) may deny or defer deductions for payments creating hybrid financial instrument mismatches or imported mismatches; Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory; generally reduced to 5% for dividends to a US parent owning at least 10% of voting shares under Article X of the Canada-US Treaty) applies to distributions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold. A Canadian-resident ULC is required to register for GST/HST once the total consideration for its taxable supplies (determined on a monthly, quarterly, or annual aggregate basis in the preceding four calendar quarters or the current quarter, excluding supplies of financial services, goodwill, and certain capital property) exceeds CAD 30,000. Because the ULC is resident in Canada, zero-rated export sales count toward the threshold. A non-resident supplier would only count taxable supplies made in Canada for the same threshold test.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of die-cast scale-model goods (1:18 scale car replicas) made in Canada is zero-rated under section 21 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the property is exported directly or indirectly by the supplier or by the recipient, with physical exportation occurring within a reasonable time. Section 22 of Schedule VI deems exportation to have occurred where the supplier has obtained and retained evidence satisfactory to the Minister of National Revenue. The zero-rating precondition requires: (a) the recipient is a non-resident of Canada (or the goods are delivered outside Canada), (b) the goods leave Canada in the same form and are not consumed or altered in Canada, and (c) the supplier retains the documentary bundle identified below. Related services supplied in respect of the exported goods (e.g., packaging, crating, export freight-forwarding services) are zero-rated under sections 7, 8, and 9 of Schedule VI where the services are performed in Canada directly in connection with the exported tangible property. Supplies delivered to Canadian-resident buyers or to Canadian fulfillment centers at which the goods are diverted back into the domestic market remain taxable at 5%.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed electronically through the Canada Export Reporting System (CERS), or the exporting carrier's low-value export reporting equivalent
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or courier/postal waybill with delivery confirmation) evidencing shipment from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice containing full description, HS classification (HS 9503.00 for scale model toys/collectibles), quantity, unit value, total consideration, and Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Canadian bank or the PayPal/SWIFT wire payment receipt demonstrating receipt of foreign-currency proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Freight forwarder's certificate of exportation or carrier's certification of physical delivery at the foreign destination (customs clearance documents abroad accepted as supplementary proof)
  • CN22/CN23 postal customs declarations or courier customs manifests for low-value parcel shipments

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or mandatory API-integrated clearance regime exists in Canada for B2B or B2C invoicing. CRA requires every GST/HST registrant to issue an invoice containing the prescribed particulars (registrant's BN and GST/HST registration number, invoice date, description of goods, consideration, and tax charged) under section 169(1) of the Excise Tax Act, regardless of whether the invoice is paper or electronic. Electronic invoices and books of account are accepted if legible, complete, and retrievable during the six-year retention period under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act. Canada is implementing a voluntary PEPPOL-based Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard for federal procurement and cross-border interoperability; there is no current mandate for real-time fiscal clearance or third-party e-invoicing stamping.

On audit, CRA will deny zero-rating if the documentary evidence is incomplete, if the goods did not in fact leave Canada, or if the supply was diverted to a Canadian consumer; the full consideration is then reassessed as taxable at 5% GST, and correspondingly claimed input tax credits on export-related purchases are clawed back with interest. Reassessment for GST/HST is generally limited to four years from the date of assessment under section 298 of the Excise Tax Act, with no limitation period for misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default. Civil penalties include: section 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act — greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax in issue for a false statement or omission, increased to 50% for gross negligence; section 280(1) failure-to-file penalties equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 1% of the net tax for each missed return; and daily compounding interest at the CRA prescribed rate. The Excise Tax Act general anti-avoidance rule (section 274) applies to artificial export-invoice routing or sham foreign-agent structures. For income tax, section 163(2) of the Income Tax Act imposes a 50% gross-negligence penalty on unreported export income, and the CRA can recharacterize unrepatriated export proceeds as taxable foreign accrual or deemed dividends. Note: the Excise Tax Act does not statutorily codify the 180-day F/X repatriation benchmark, but CRA export-verification practice expects documented receipt of foreign consideration within a commercially reasonable period; payment delays beyond 180 days materially heighten audit scrutiny and risk of zero-rating denial.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for current- and capital-account transactions. Canada has no exchange-control legislation restricting conversion or repatriation of capital, dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees. Profits earned by an Alberta ULC can be distributed to non-resident shareholders, but dividends and deemed dividends are subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax (generally 25% unless reduced by an income tax treaty). Capital contributions and equity injections are freely permitted without Bank of Canada approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no statutory conversion ratio or timeline. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts and may decide independently whether and when to convert. Receipts are subject to reporting for balance-of-payments and customs statistical purposes, but no regulatory directive requires repatriation or forced exchange.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); monetary policy and payment systems oversight. Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange control administration; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially regulates federally chartered banks, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/ATF compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Fully offshore EMIs are legally constrained in Canada: unless they register as a money-services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where applicable, obtain a payment-service provider authorization under the Retail Payment Activities Act, they cannot operate a Canadian-accessible account. Licensed Canadian payment fintechs (e.g., PayPal Canada, Wise Canada as an MSB) can be used as secondary collection/settlement channels but cannot replace a chartered bank for commercial letters of credit, large-value Swift wires, or secured liquidity management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions must be routed through SWIFT MT103 with field 50K identifying the ordering investor, field 59 identifying the ULC beneficiary account, and field 70 reading 'ULC EQUITY INJECTION' or 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' to sustain legal clarity. CAD-denominated injections settle on the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); USD injections are cleared via the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent (typically through Fedwire/CHIPS). Supporting documentation must include an executed subscription agreement, corporate board resolution, and updated capital ledger entries to avoid misclassification as trade proceeds.Suppliers are paid primarily via SWIFT MT103 for high-value one-off die-cast tooling and raw-material settlement. For recurring bulk purchases of zinc alloy, packaging, and tampo-printing consumables, irrevocable commercial letters of credit are opened using SWIFT MT700 (and MT707 for amendments) with the beneficiary's bank as advising/confirming bank. For immediate low-value disbursements, PayPal Business Payouts or a fintech MSB can repatriate funds to the corporate bank account, while USD payments can be made through the Canadian bank's U.S. dollar clearing rails.Deliverable outright FX forwards on USD/CAD and EUR/CAD executed with OSFI-regulated Canadian bank counterparties under an ISDA 1998 FX Agreement or short-form FX confirmation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or non-internationalized currencies, notably CNY and other Asian basket currencies, used when onshore convertibility is unavailable for the settlement date.Cross-currency swaps (XCCY basis swaps) on USD/CAD, CAD/CNY, and USD/CNY to finance working capital, align repayment flows, and hedge structural currency mismatch across Asian supplier terms and North American revenue.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility. Fully offshore EMIs are legally constrained in Canada: unless they register as a money-services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where applicable, obtain a payment-service provider authorization under the Retail Payment Activities Act, they cannot operate a Canadian-accessible account. Licensed Canadian payment fintechs (e.g., PayPal Canada, Wise Canada as an MSB) can be used as secondary collection/settlement channels but cannot replace a chartered bank for commercial letters of credit, large-value Swift wires, or secured liquidity management.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% (Alberta general corporate tax 8% + federal general corporate tax 15%; not available for the small business deduction if the ULC is controlled by non-residents, which is expected where a foreign owner uses a Canadian ULC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII dividend withholding tax on dividends paid by the Alberta ULC to a non-resident shareholder, unless the rate is reduced by an income tax treaty. A resident individual shareholder is not subject to Part XIII but taxed at ordinary income rates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For fiscal years, file Form T106 (reporting of non-arm's length transactions with non-residents) where the aggregate value of reportable transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000; prepare a Canadian Local File/Master File when the ULC engages in significant cross-border transactions, and file country-by-country report if group revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000; CRA may request contemporaneous documentation within three months of an audit.

Economic substance scope: Although Alberta/Canada has no offshore 'Economic Substance Regulations' in the style of the UAE, the Income Tax Act applies s.247 transfer-pricing, s.245 GAAR, and treaty limitation-on-benefits provisions that require commercial substance, actual employees, premises and risk management in Canada. A shell ULC with no substance that is disregarded by its U.S. parent is vulnerable to CRA recharacterization as a conduit, denial of treaty benefits, anti-hybrid rules (s.18.4/12.2), and Part XIII reassessment.
