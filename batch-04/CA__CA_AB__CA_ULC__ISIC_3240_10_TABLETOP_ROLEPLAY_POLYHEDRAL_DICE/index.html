Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), acting for the Minister of National Revenue. Primary federal registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html. Alberta's corporate registry is Service Alberta / Corporate Registry; Alberta corporate income tax is administered and collected by CRA through the federal T2 corporate return.

E-government filing portal

Following Alberta incorporation, the ULC must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN). Canadian-resident corporations normally apply online through Business Registration Online; non-resident-owned or newly formed Alberta ULCs may submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or, where the corporation has no Canadian address or is a non-resident registrant, Form RC2 'Request for a Business Number - Non-Resident'. The BN is the national taxpayer identifier; a separate GST/HST program account is created when the ULC registers for GST/HST. CRA imposes no minimum capital requirement for ULC formation, and non-resident ownership is permitted under Alberta law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (cross-border label): for Canadian CRA income tax purposes, an Alberta ULC is a taxable corporation resident in Canada and cannot be characterized as a partnership; however, it is not a per se corporation under the U.S. check-the-box rules, so a single-member ULC may be disregarded or treated as a partnership for U.S. federal tax purposes only.

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic 'check-the-box' election. An Alberta ULC may file IRS Form 8832 to elect U.S. classification as a corporation, partnership, or disregarded entity, but that U.S. election does not override Canadian entity-level taxation. Canada does not offer a unilateral election to cure hybrid mismatches; hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed through treaty tie-breaker and Canadian statutory rules, and the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention does not automatically reclassify a Canadian-resident ULC as fiscally transparent unless the relevant income is treated as such under both treaty and domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, measured in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (the 'small supplier' threshold). A Canadian-resident ULC exceeding CAD 30,000 in taxable revenues must register and charge GST. Non-residents carrying on business in Canada or soliciting taxable supplies through remote/digital channels may also be subject to registration at the same CAD 30,000 threshold, with specific rules for digital services and non-resident vendors.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant supplying tangible personal property (such as handcrafted epoxy resin polyhedral dice sets) may apply the 0% rate under Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act when the supplier ships the goods through a common carrier or exporter to a destination outside Canada, or delivers them to an authorized export agent and export actually occurs. The supplier must obtain and retain contemporaneous documentary proof of export, issue a zero-rated invoice, and file the GST/HST return reflecting the zero-rated supply. Incidental design/customization services provided to non-residents may also be zero-rated where they are physically delivered outside Canada or performed for a non-resident who is outside Canada.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Customs Export Declaration, Form B13A or electronic Canada Export Reporting System (CAED) record, where the export value or commodity requires CBSA export reporting; low-value parcel exports may be documented through the courier's or postal operator's electronic export manifest
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or recognized courier waybill identifying the seller, the overseas destination, and delivery terms such as FOB, EXW, or DAP
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the dice products, quantity, unit price, total export consideration, and foreign buyer
  • Proof of payment or foreign remittance, including bank transfer advice, credit card/PayPal/Kickstarter settlement reports, or equivalent payment records; Canada does not issue statutory 'inward remittance certificates', but CRA accepts equivalent documentary evidence of settlement in a foreign currency

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal e-invoicing, API transaction clearance, or digital tax stamping. CRA accepts standard electronic invoices and supports electronic records, but there is no national clearance portal. Exporters must retain GST/HST books, invoices, shipping records, and export declarations for six years from the end of the relevant fiscal year and must provide CRA with records in machine-readable format upon request.

CRA may retroactively disallow export zero-rating, reassess the supply as taxable at 5%, deny or claw back input tax credits and refunds, and impose interest and penalties if the exporter cannot substantiate export with complete documentary evidence. Under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, knowing or gross-negligent false statements in returns, invoices, or export documentation attract civil penalties equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the resulting tax understatement or false refund. Deliberate export fraud can be prosecuted as tax evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act, carrying fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years on indictment; the normal four-year reassessment limitation period does not apply where there is misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating, fully convertible Canadian dollar (CAD). Neither the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) nor the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2) imposes exchange controls, prior authorization, or repatriation licenses on current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance from an Alberta ULC to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted; the only statutory charge is Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (25% statutory, typically reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-U.S. Income Tax Convention), which the payer bank does not enforce as a treasury control. Goods, services, and crowdfunding settlements (Kickstarter/Stripe) may freely enter and leave Canada in CAD or foreign currency. The capital account is equally open for equity injections, shareholder loans, and intercompany trade payables, subject only to FINTRAC large-transaction reporting and OSFI prudential rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline exist in Canada. The wartime Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1952, and contemporary law does not obligate exporters to sell foreign currency to the Bank of Canada or to a chartered bank. Proceeds from exports of handcrafted dice (HS 9504.40, NAICS 339930) may be held indefinitely in CAD, USD, or foreign-currency accounts. The only reporting obligations are (a) FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction Reports for cash transactions of CAD 10,000 or more, (b) FINTRAC Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reports for physical movements of CAD 10,000+ (E677), and (c) customary SWIFT message archival at the clearing bank. No central-bank conversion license is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions is discharged by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision is discharged by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); economic sanctions administration and enforcement is shared by Global Affairs Canada and the Royal Canadian Mounted Police.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Viability is conditional: offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise Platform, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) are usable as secondary payout and FX-conversion rails, but not as the primary treasury account for a Canadian ULC. They are generally registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC rather than as Schedule I or II chartered banks; balances are not CDIC-insured; and they cannot issue documentary credits or guarantee instruments. Once the Retail Payment Activities Act is fully in force, a Platform Payment Service Provider with a FINTRAC MSB registration may clear CAD but remains outside the Payments Canada settlement system for SWIFT-based trade finance. Legal constraints include PCMLTFA MSB registration (sections 50A-50G), provincial consumer-protection licensing where applicable, and heightened account-reviews for high-velocity e-commerce flows. The recommendation is to operate the EMI as a re-currency bridge for Kickstarter/Stripe receipts only, with a same-day sweep to a tier-1 chartered bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from non-resident shareholders will be routed under either a subscription agreement (issuance of ULC shares) or a shareholder loan agreement. Comply with the following wire discipline: (a) remitter sends a SWIFT MT103 through the correspondent network with field 59 naming the ULC exactly and the beneficiary account sort/transit at the Alberta bank; (b) the ISO 20022 purpose code must be EQUI (equity) or INTR (intercompany loan), with a free-text REM field referencing 'ULC Subscription Agreement dated [date]' or the intercompany loan note; (c) all subscription proceeds will be credited to the Capital Escrow Account under a two-signature mandate, released only on resolution of closing conditions (board authorization, Alberta registration update, and UBO file completion); (d) aggregate credits equal to or above CAD 10,000 will trigger FINTRAC monitoring but require no affirmative client filing; (e) for U.S. shareholders, ensure a Form W-8BEN-E or W-9 is on file and that CRA Part XIII withholding is aligned with the treaty and section 212 exemptions; (f) no Bank of Canada authorization is required for the remittance.Trade disbursements consolidate into three clearing rails. First, consumer refunds and small raw-material reorders below CAD 10,000 are settled via ACH (U.S.), EFT (CAD), commercial cards, or Stripe/PayPal refund flows. Second, standard supplier settlements (epoxy resin, pigments, silicone, packaging) use a direct SWIFT MT103 with field 70 referencing the commercial invoice number and HS classification, combined with an intra-bank FX conversion at the same value date; two approvers must pre-authorize in the corporate banking portal. Third, capital purchases (e.g., 3D printers, industrial mixing equipment, bulk monomer above CAD 25,000) use an irrevocable documentary letter of credit issued by the Canadian bank, available by payment at sight or deferred payment under MT700, governed by UCP 600 and eUCP 2.0, with Incoterms DAP; the freight-forwarder documentation pack must include a commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, and certificate of origin. MT202 COV is used only for the cover transfer between correspondent banks. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability is conditional: offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise Platform, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) are usable as secondary payout and FX-conversion rails, but not as the primary treasury account for a Canadian ULC. They are generally registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC rather than as Schedule I or II chartered banks; balances are not CDIC-insured; and they cannot issue documentary credits or guarantee instruments. Once the Retail Payment Activities Act is fully in force, a Platform Payment Service Provider with a FINTRAC MSB registration may clear CAD but remains outside the Payments Canada settlement system for SWIFT-based trade finance. Legal constraints include PCMLTFA MSB registration (sections 50A-50G), provincial consumer-protection licensing where applicable, and heightened account-reviews for high-velocity e-commerce flows. The recommendation is to operate the EMI as a re-currency bridge for Kickstarter/Stripe receipts only, with a same-day sweep to a tier-1 chartered bank account.
