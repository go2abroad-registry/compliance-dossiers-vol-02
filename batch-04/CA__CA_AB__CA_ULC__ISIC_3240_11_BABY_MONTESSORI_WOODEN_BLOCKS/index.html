Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal GST/HST and income tax registration via CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services.html). Alberta corporate income tax (Alberta Corporate Tax Act) is administered by Alberta Finance and collected through the CRA under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement. Alberta has no provincial sales tax, so no provincial indirect tax registration is required. Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers export declarations via the CEDAR/ACF platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta ULC obtains its statutory tax identity as follows: (1) incorporate or extra-provincially register the ULC with the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number; (2) apply for a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), selecting the required program accounts: GST/HST (RT0001), corporation income tax (RC0001), payroll (RP0001), and customs/export (RD0001) where applicable; (3) for non-resident-owned ULCs, file Form RC1 with a non-resident declaration and supporting constitutional documents, and provide a copy of any U.S. Form 8832 check-the-box election when the ULC is held by a U.S. member; (4) enrol with the CBSA Export Reporting program to obtain the exporter identifier for electronic B13A filings; (5) display the BN on all invoices, GST/HST returns, and customs declarations. A ULC making only zero-rated export supplies may voluntarily register under RT0001 to recover input tax credits on Canadian inputs such as warehousing, freight, and design services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The Alberta ULC is a hybrid entity: for Canadian income tax it is a taxable Canadian corporation (ITA 89(1)), while for U.S. federal tax it is an eligible entity able to elect check-the-box treatment (U.S. Form 8832) as a disregarded entity of its U.S. shareholder. Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty Article IV(6) allows a fiscally transparent entity such as a disregarded ULC to claim reduced Canadian treaty rates through its U.S. resident member. Other relevant elections include: ITA 85(1) rollover election for capital property transferred into the ULC, ITA 87 amalgamation and ITA 88(1) wind-up elections, and the unlimited liability company deemed-dividend / return-of-capital characterization rules under ITA 248(1). No SIFT, partnership, or REIT flow-through election is available; the ULC remains within the federal and Alberta corporate tax nets.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) over any four consecutive calendar quarters; exceeding the threshold mandates GST/HST registration effective on the day of the excess. Voluntary registration below the threshold is available to claim input tax credits. Non-resident suppliers selling goods fulfilled in Canada through marketplace platforms must assess the deemed-supply GST/HST registration rules applicable to platform operators (e.g., Amazon.ca), independent of the small-supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI Part V, exports of tangible personal property (the Montessori wooden blocks) are zero-rated when the ULC obtains documentary evidence before filing its GST/HST return that the goods were exported as soon as reasonably possible after delivery. Goods sold to a non-resident for delivery in Canada are taxable at 5% unless delivered to a common carrier for export. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident person and performed entirely outside Canada (s.7.1), or performed in Canada exclusively for a non-resident recipient not present in Canada at the time of performance (s.7). FOB-Alberta wholesale sales to U.S. distributors, with the ULC as exporter of record, qualify at 0% provided shipping, payment, and title-transfer evidence is retained. Freight and marketing services supplied to foreign affiliates must satisfy the same non-resident and outside-Canada performance tests.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the ULC as shipper and the foreign buyer as consignee, showing the foreign destination port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank FX settlement evidence tracing payment from the foreign buyer through Stripe or Amazon Pay settlements into the ULC bank account

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or statutory B2B e-invoicing scheme for GST/HST. Registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE/RMS where the CRA e-filing mandates apply to larger registrants, and must retain accounting records in a readable format in Canada or accessible electronically for six years. A voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoice standard (Peppol/PINT) is available since 2023. For exports, electronic declarations must be filed with the CBSA through the CEDAR/ACF export reporting platform or via a licensed customs broker; no government invoice pre-clearance is required.

Export zero-rating is strictly documentary; if the ULC cannot prove actual export and foreign ownership, CRA reassesses the supply at 5% GST within the normal four-year reassessment window (ten years for misrepresentation), adds prescribed compound daily interest, and may deny corresponding input tax credits. False or misleading statements on export certificates trigger a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (ETA s.285(1)), and fraudulent schemes are referred to CRA criminal investigation or CBSA as indictable offences with imprisonment and forfeiture exposure. CRA export-integrity programs cross-match financial statements, payment-rail settlement reports, and B13A declarations; zero-rated sales without corresponding hard-currency repatriation within approximately 180 days of shipment, or with unexplained documentation gaps, are automatically profiled for audit, and any unrebutted deficiency results in full clawback with penalties and interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, tax obligations, contractual restrictions, and bank compliance review. There is no general prior approval requirement for inbound equity or shareholder loans, outbound dividends, interest, royalties, or trade payments. A Canadian Alberta unlimited liability corporation is a Canadian corporation for Canadian income-tax purposes; its ULC designation does not itself create pass-through treatment under Canadian tax law. Any US entity-classification election or cross-border hybrid treatment requires separate tax analysis. Dividends and other payments to non-residents may be subject to Canadian withholding tax, potentially reduced under an applicable tax treaty if eligibility and limitation-on-benefits requirements are satisfied. Interest, royalties, and service payments require characterization, transfer-pricing, withholding, and treaty review as applicable. Maintain board approvals, tax calculations, recipient residency and beneficial-ownership evidence, and supporting contracts for material remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no general statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or held in Canadian or foreign accounts, subject to applicable tax, sanctions, reporting, contractual, and bank requirements. The corporation should record export invoices, customs and shipping records, receipt dates, exchange rates, and any foreign-currency balances for accounting and tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Canada is Canada's central bank. Canada has no general exchange-control authority imposing routine restrictions on foreign-currency conversion or cross-border transfers. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions supervises federally regulated financial institutions, while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada administers applicable anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing reporting requirements. Alberta Treasury Board and Finance administers provincial financial matters but does not impose general foreign-exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment processing and foreign-exchange services, subject to provider eligibility, Canadian onboarding, and the provider's licensing and safeguarding model. Stripe may support eligible Canadian businesses for payment acceptance and settlement; Amazon Pay is a payment service rather than a substitute for a corporate deposit account. An offshore electronic-money institution account is not automatically a Canadian deposit account, may not have Canadian deposit insurance, and may not satisfy a bank's requirements for source-of-funds, local operating activity, or merchant settlement. Confirm the provider's Canadian regulatory status, permitted services, settlement currencies, account-holder legal name, and withdrawal route before relying on it for treasury. Keep operating liquidity at a Canadian financial institution and do not use payment-platform balances as a substitute for statutory or tax reserves.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general rate: federal Part I 15% + Alberta general corporate 8%. Where the ULC qualifies for the federal Manufacturing and Processing (M&P) profits deduction, the combined M&P rate is 21% (federal 13% + Alberta 8%). The 11% small business rate (federal 9% + Alberta 2%) is unavailable because a ULC controlled by non-resident shareholders is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC).
