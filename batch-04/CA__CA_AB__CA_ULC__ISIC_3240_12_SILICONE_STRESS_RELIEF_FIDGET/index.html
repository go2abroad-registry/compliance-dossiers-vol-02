Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — My Business Account portal, Business Registration Online (BRO), and GST/HST NETFILE electronic services; provincial counterpart in Alberta is the Corporate Registry (Alberta Connects), while Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (ATRA) administers no provincial sales tax for this jurisdiction.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts from the CRA: (1) file electronically via Business Registration Online or complete Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), providing legal name, operating name, foreign jurisdiction of incorporation, Alberta registered office address and mailing address, principal business activity (NAICS 339930 manufacture of toys/games), financial year-end, and estimated taxable supplies; (2) apply for a GST/HST program account (RT) either mandatorily where annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or voluntarily under s. 240(1) ETA; (3) if no Canadian presence, appoint a representative by executing Form RC391 (Authorizing or Cancelling a Representative) and a power of attorney; (4) for an Alberta ULC, incorporate/register through the Alberta Corporate Registry and then align the federal BN with the Alberta Corporate Access Number; no minimum capital requirement and no sectoral licence applies to compression molding of food-grade silicone fidgets.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: a Canadian ULC is a domestic corporation resident in its Alberta province for Canadian income tax purposes and therefore subject to entity-level corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act; however, because shareholders bear unlimited liability, the ULC is eligible for U.S. federal check-the-box pass-through treatment as a disregarded entity (single-member) or partnership (multi-member) for foreign owners; Alberta imposes no capital tax and the entity is not situated in a tax-exempt zone, although a non-resident without a permanent establishment in Canada may rely on treaty protection.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (1) U.S. Form 8832 Entity Classification Election (check-the-box) to disregard the Canadian ULC or treat it as a partnership for U.S. federal tax purposes; (2) voluntary GST/HST registration election under s. 240(1) of the Excise Tax Act with retroactive effect up to 30 days; (3) election to change GST/HST filing frequency (annual/quarterly/monthly) under ETA s. 243; (4) Quick Method of accounting election under ETA s. 227 where applicable; (5) election for timing of supply under ETA ss. 152/155; (6) treaty-based non-resident corporation election to be treated as a permanent establishment if a Canadian branch exists; Alberta offers no separate provincial sales tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of annual worldwide taxable supplies (ETA s. 240(1)); for non-resident suppliers, registration is triggered when taxable supplies of goods made in Canada and services performed in Canada exceed the threshold; small-supplier status is measured globally for associated persons, and voluntary registration is available once a BN is obtained.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods applies under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 20 and 24 of the ETA where the ULC, as exporter, supplies the goods and delivers them, or causes delivery by a common carrier, to an address outside Canada and retains proof of export within the prescribed period; estate and intangible supplies for use exclusively outside Canada qualify under Schedule VI s. 27. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI s. 44 unless they relate to real property situated in Canada, goods delivered to a Canadian address, or services rendered to an individual present in Canada at the time of performance. The zero-rated supply is a 'nil-rated' taxable supply allowing full input tax credit recovery; an unregistered foreign ULC may instead claim a non-resident GST/HST rebate for property acquired in Canada for export, and must maintain contemporaneous export evidence to avoid reassessment.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (Form B13 or ACI e-manifest for goods exceeding CAD 2,000 in value)
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Canada to a foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit advice evidencing repatriation of foreign-currency sale proceeds to the ULC's Canadian or offshore account)
  • Commercial Invoice with Incoterms stating the ULC as exporter of record and the foreign purchaser as importer of record
  • Packing List and Freight Forwarder's Certificate of Export

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance validation of invoices. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI/API where the registrant exceeds prescribed thresholds. Tax invoices must contain the prescribed particulars under ETA s. 221 and the Input Tax Credit Information (GST/HST) Regulations (SOR 2007-48), including supplier BN, invoice date, vendor and purchaser identity, and resultant tax. Books and records, including electronic export evidence, must be retained for six years; Alberta imposes no additional e-invoicing or clearance regime.

CRA conducts export integrity audits on zero-rated supplies to pipe foreign-source revenue; failure to substantiate export triggers reassessment of unreported 5% GST, disallowance of corresponding input tax credits and refunds, statutory interest, and penalties under ETA s. 280 (minimum CAD 250 and 25% of the amount sought, increased to 40% for repeat misconduct), ETA s. 285 (false statements attracting CAD 1,000 to CAD 25,000 fines), plus criminal evasion exposure under ETA s. 327 and Income Tax Act s. 239 (fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years). Erroneous refunds are clawed back retrospectively with compound interest; records must be maintained for six years, and the normal reassessment window of four years extends to eight years on misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. No exchange permits, approval screens, or repatriation authorizations apply to trade, dividends, interest, or capital movements. Legal-tender status is governed by the Currency Act; profit remittance is constrained only by Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory; 5%/15% treaty-reduced for qualifying US/Luxembourg corporate shareholders) and by Canadian thin-capitalization rules under s. 18(4) of the Income Tax Act, which cap deductible interest at a 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio for specified non-resident payees.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, surrender ratio, or mandatory conversion for export receipts. The ULC may hold USD or CNH proceeds indefinitely in foreign-currency bank accounts; for Canadian tax purposes, FX gains and losses are computed transaction-by-transaction under s. 39(2) of the Income Tax Act and reported in CAD. Physical transport of currency or monetary instruments across a Canadian border exceeding C$10,000 requires a Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report (CBSA Form E677) filed with FINTRAC/CBSA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - sole issuer of bank notes and supervisor of Payments Canada settlement systems. Canada operates a free-floating currency with no centralized foreign-exchange administration; FINTRAC enforces cross-border currency and monetary-instrument reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), while OSFI regulates Schedule I banks and the banking solvency framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are usable as auxiliary rails for Platform Pay settlements and small-value FX conversion, but each must be FINTRAC-registered as a Money Services Business and, if serving Canadian customers or the ULC, registered as a Payment Service Provider with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). Legal constraints prevent them from acting as the primary treasury bank; core liquidity must remain at a Schedule I bank for deposit insurance, SWIFT correspondent access, and regulatory clarity.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

ULC pass-through classification: because shareholders of an unlimited liability corporation bear unlimited liability and foreign shareholders may treat the entity as fiscally transparent, banks may struggle to identify the true beneficial owner versus the registered shareholder.

Defense evidence: Full organizational chart tracing from ultimate natural persons through each intermediate entity; FINTRAC-compliant identity evidence (passport, proof of address) for every 25%+ beneficial owner; signed unlimited-liability acknowledgment; and a written Canadian tax opinion confirming the ULC is a resident corporation while documenting the intended pass-through treatment for foreign tax purposes.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: CAD 500,000 related-party transactions; Master File: CAD 50 million consolidated group revenue

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) require demonstration of real economic activities and substance in Canada to support tax residency and claim benefits; non-compliance results in denial of tax benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Income Tax Act with fines up to $5,000,000 and imprisonment up to five years

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes, director liability for source deductions, and potential criminal charges
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 10 times the tax benefit or $1,000,000 per transaction; reassessment of arm's length prices

Director personal liability: Directors liable for corporate tax liabilities and potential personal penalties for reckless conduct
Failure to Withhold and Remit Taxes

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to the tax not withheld plus interest; additional fines up to $1,000,000

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unremitted amounts; possible imprisonment for fraudulent non-remittance
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Revocation of corporate status, penalties up to $1,000,000 per day for non-filing, and denial of tax benefits

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil leading to personal liability for taxes, director disqualification, and potential criminal liability
