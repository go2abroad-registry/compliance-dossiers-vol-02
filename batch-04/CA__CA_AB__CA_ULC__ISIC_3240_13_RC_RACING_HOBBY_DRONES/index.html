Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

An Alberta corporation must maintain a registered office in Alberta and an address for service that satisfies the Alberta Business Corporations Act and registry requirements. A commercial registered-office or virtual-office provider may be acceptable for statutory service if it maintains the required records, receives and promptly forwards service, and provides a genuine Alberta address. A mailbox-only, accommodation, or non-operational address is unsuitable. For FPV racing-drone retail, carbon-fiber milling, brushless-motor integration, inventory, product testing, importation, or customer returns, a virtual office does not establish sufficient operational substance and may be rejected by banks, payment processors, insurers, customs authorities, or tax authorities.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta); the directors may fix the consideration for share subscription. No escrow account or capital deposit is required unless the terms of the share subscription agreement expressly impose one. For a ULC, the unlimited liability clause must appear in the Articles and be reflected in the share certificate wording.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection approved by the local municipal fire department under the Alberta Fire Code; building permit and Certificate of Occupancy issued through the Safety Codes Act permitting process; workplace clearance from Alberta Occupational Health and Safety covering machine guarding, respirable dust control, noise exposure and lithium battery safety for the FPV drone assembly operation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; CRA My Business Account portal and GST/HST NETFILE; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA on behalf of Alberta Treasury Board and Finance.

E-government filing portal

An Alberta ULC incorporated in Canada obtains its statutory tax identifier by registering with the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request to Open a Business Number Program Account). A separate GST/HST program account (RT) is registered under the same BN. Non-resident entities carrying on taxable commercial activity in Canada must apply through the same RC1 process, provide corporate existence/officer information and a Canadian contact or authorized representative, and may be required to post security under the Excise Tax Act. No separate Alberta TIN exists; CRA BN is the single taxpayer identifier for federal and Alberta corporate tax and GST/HST.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation in Canada; the Alberta ULC is a corporation for Income Tax Act purposes, files a T2 corporate return and is not fiscally transparent in Canada. Pass_Through_Eligible only for foreign (e.g., U.S. IRS check-the-box) classification on Form 8832, not for Canadian domestic tax purposes. — An Alberta ULC incorporated in Canada obtains its statutory tax identifier by registering with the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request to Open a Business Number Program Account). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation in Canada; the Alberta ULC is a corporation for Income Tax Act purposes, files a T2 corporate return and is not fiscally transparent in Canada. Pass_Through_Eligible only for foreign (e.g., U.S. IRS check-the-box) classification on Form 8832, not for Canadian domestic tax purposes.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box or fiscal-transparency election for an Alberta ULC. A U.S. investor may make an IRS check-the-box election to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. tax purposes, but Canada continues to tax it at entity level; hybrid mismatch and treaty-shopping rules (Income Tax Act s.18.4/18.6, General Anti-Avoidance Rule, and Canada-U.S. treaty limitation-on-benefits) may disallow deductions or deny treaty relief on hybrid structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold based on total worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four calendar quarters; a person ceases to be a small supplier once CAD 30,000 is exceeded in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, at which point mandatory GST/HST registration applies. Non-residents carrying on commercial activity in Canada are subject to deemed registration rules and must register if taxable supplies exceed threshold or if they request registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of FPV racing drones and related tangible components are zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act at 0% GST/HST when the goods are sold for export, shipped directly to a destination outside Canada (by the supplier, purchaser or common carrier), exported in commercial quantities, and supported by satisfactory contemporaneous export evidence. Supplies of ancillary digital services/software to non-residents outside Canada may also qualify for zero-rating. Domestic Canadian supplies remain subject to 5% GST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A or authorized e-declaration record)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing movement of goods to the export destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign-exchange credit evidencing receipt and repatriation of export sale proceeds

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping. Registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed CRA particulars (supplier name, BN, invoice date, description, GST/HST paid/payable and rate) and retain copies; electronic invoices (PDF/XML/EDI) are accepted. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA portals, and CRA uses risk-based audit and data matching rather than pre-clearance stamps for compliance.

CRA and CBSA data cross-checks export documentation; if contemporaneous proof is insufficient or the supply was not actually exported, the zero-rating is reversed and 5% GST/HST is assessed. CRA may deny zero-rating where foreign sale proceeds are not received or repatriated within 180 days. The normal reassessment period is four years, extended without limitation in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Penalties include gross negligence penalties under section 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act of up to 50% of the net tax avoided, failure-to-remit penalties, interest, criminal prosecution for tax evasion, and, for a ULC, unlimited shareholder liability for unpaid corporate and GST/HST liabilities.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a convertible currency regime under the Bank of Canada Act. Capital account transactions are generally convertible for legitimate corporate purposes with no prior approval required for routine remittances. Current account transactions, including trade settlements, are fully convertible. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal, limited to standard tax filings and beneficial ownership disclosure; no significant regulatory barriers for routine FX flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the Bank of Canada. Repatriation of export receipts is permitted without conversion. All exports must be reported to Statistics Canada within 90 days of shipment for balance of payments purposes. No statutory conversion ratios or timelines apply; transactions occur at market rates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
