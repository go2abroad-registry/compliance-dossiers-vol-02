Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow account requirement under the ABCA for an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation. The sole subscriber may subscribe for one common share at a nominal CAD 0.01 per share fully paid in cash. Escrow is required only where a securities exemption or investor immigration capital guarantee so mandates; for ULC pass-through eligibility, share capital and subscription records must be kept current and supported by board documentation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety and Building Code certificates of compliance issued by the local Safety Codes Officer under Alberta's Safety Codes Act; structural load inspection for kiln, polishing benches, and storage racks; Alberta OHS workplace clearance for the production floor, including WHMIS/GHS SDS management, emergency response plan, and, where preferred, a Certificate of Recognition (COR) under the Partners in Injury Reduction program.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MBA) via canada.ca

E-government filing portal

For an Alberta ULC, foreign investors must first complete provincial incorporation through Alberta Corporate Registry (Alberta Registries) to obtain the certificate of incorporation and corporate access number. The corporation must then apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number', or by using the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, selecting the corporate income tax account (RT) and GST/HST account (RT). The applicant must submit the certificate of incorporation, articles, registered office address in Alberta, and non-resident shareholder information if applicable. A non-resident representative must attach a signed Form RC59 authorization. The CRA issues a nine-digit BN followed by a two-letter program code and four-digit reference number (e.g., 123456789 RT0001), which constitutes the statutory tax identification number for all federal and Alberta provincial tax accounts administered by the CRA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a domestic check-the-box election; an Alberta ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation and must file a T2 corporate income tax return on an entity-level basis for CRA purposes. However, an Alberta ULC is eligible for pass-through classification for U.S. federal tax purposes under Treasury Regulation § 301.7701-3 by filing IRS Form 8832, allowing it to be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership by its U.S. shareholders. Canadian hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act apply where such classification creates a deduction/non-inclusion mismatch, and may deny deductions or require inclusion adjustments. No other statutory entity-level classification election is available under the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of total gross worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; a person exceeding this small-supplier threshold must register for GST/HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate exports of tangible personal property, such as hard enamel lapel pins, where the goods are exported as part of the supply and the registrant retains documentary proof of export. Supplies of goods are zero-rated if the registrant ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada, including by common carrier or freight forwarder. Services supplied to a non-resident person are generally zero-rated unless the service relates to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada; services performed wholly outside Canada are zero-rated. Input tax credits attributable to zero-rated exports are fully refundable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or other transport document showing exported goods
  • Freight forwarder's certificate or carrier's proof-of-export document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank records evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time electronic fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for invoices. GST/HST registrants must issue tax invoices containing prescribed information under section 221 of the Excise Tax Act and retain records in Canada for six years. Returns and remittances must be filed electronically where annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 50,000. E-invoicing is voluntary and may be exchanged via PEPPOL-compatible systems; there is no CRA pre-clearance or API validation before issuance.

CRA audits will claw back zero-rated treatment and input tax credits if a registrant cannot produce adequate proof of export within the required period. The normal reassessment period is four years from the date of the return, extended to ten years where misrepresentation or fraud is shown. False statements or gross negligence in claiming export zero-rating attract a penalty under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act of 25% of the amount assessed, plus arrears interest. Wilful tax evasion may result in prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act with fines of up to CAD 250,000 and imprisonment for up to two years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free convertibility regime for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without prior approval or surrender obligations, subject solely to standard corporate filings, tax compliance, and FINTRAC anti-money laundering reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds is required. Receipts may be converted at prevailing market rates. Exporters must maintain supporting documentation for audit and comply with FINTRAC reporting thresholds where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
