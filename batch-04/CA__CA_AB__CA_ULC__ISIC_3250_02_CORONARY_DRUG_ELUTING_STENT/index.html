Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Alberta Treasury Board and Finance / Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (ATRA). CRA portals: Business Registration Online, My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE / Electronic Data Transfer; Alberta Corporate Access for provincial entity registry.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta ULC: (1) incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act and obtain the Alberta incorporation number and Corporate Access number; (2) obtain the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online, Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), or automatic CRA issuance after notification of incorporation; (3) register the applicable CRA program accounts: RC for corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, and RM for import/export; (4) for GST/HST registration, indicate the date of first taxable supply in Canada and expected annual Canadian taxable supplies, including zero-rated export values; (5) for customs purposes, obtain or link the RM account to CBSA’s electronic export reporting system. A non-resident ULC may need a Canadian contact or agent; no separate Alberta provincial sales tax registration exists because Alberta imposes no PST.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a foreign-owned Alberta ULC: (1) incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act and obtain the Alberta incorporation number and Corporate Access number; (2) obtain the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online, Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), or automatic CRA issuance after notification of incorporation; (3) register the applicable CRA program accounts: RC for corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, and RM for import/export; (4) for GST/HST registration, indicate the date of first taxable supply in Canada and expected annual Canadian taxable supplies, including zero-rated export values; (5) for customs purposes, obtain or link the RM account to CBSA’s electronic export reporting system. A non-resident ULC may need a Canadian contact or agent; no separate Alberta provincial sales tax registration exists because Alberta imposes no PST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a domestic check-the-box election. The Alberta ULC is Pass_Through_Eligible only for U.S. federal tax purposes through an IRS Form 8832 election to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity; the election does not change Canadian classification, and the ULC remains entity-level taxable in Canada. For GST/HST, qualifying closely related corporations may jointly elect under subsection 156(2) of the Excise Tax Act to make certain intercompany supplies for nil consideration. No hybrid mismatch election is available; Canadian hybrid mismatch rules apply primarily under Income Tax Act sections 18.4, 18.5, and 20.3.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada, measured over the preceding four calendar quarters or the current calendar quarter (small-supplier threshold). A non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada must register once the threshold is exceeded and may register voluntarily to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate an export sale of tangible personal property, including coronary drug-eluting stents, under section 1 of Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act if: the supply is a sale; the goods are exported in tangible form as soon as reasonably possible after the supply; shipping is by common carrier, courier, freight forwarder, Canada Post, or direct export by a non-resident carrier recipient; the goods are not significantly processed, altered, or used in Canada after the sale; and the supplier holds contemporaneous evidence of export such as the CBSA export declaration, bills of lading, carrier receipts, and bank wire documentation. The sale is then subject to GST/HST at 0%, and the supplier may claim input tax credits on inputs attributable to the exported goods. Services supplied from Alberta to a non-resident, such as offshore regulatory consulting or engineering services for stent production, are generally zero-rated under section 7 of Part V, Schedule VI unless the service relates to real or tangible personal property situated in Canada or is supplied to a GST/HST registrant in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CBSA eManifest export report) evidencing departure from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing a non-Canadian destination and consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire confirmation showing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice with product description, HS tariff classification, export value, and exporter/importer identifiers
  • Packing list, shipping certificate, and carrier receipt proving export was completed as soon as reasonably possible
  • CBSA release documentation and, where applicable, Health Canada export confirmation for the Class III medical device

No mandatory real-time tax clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada or Alberta. CRA accepts electronic invoices and electronic records; registrants must retain books and records for six years. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1.5 million, using GST/HST NETFILE or Electronic Data Transfer. Exporters must report export transactions electronically to CBSA through eManifest/CAED; Class III medical device exports also require appropriate CBSA release and Health Canada export/import documentation.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims closely. Missing, backdated, inconsistent, or non-contemporaneous export documentation may cause the supply to be reclassified as taxable in Canada, resulting in assessment of 5% GST on the gross invoice, denial or clawback of related input tax credits, and statutory interest under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act. Penalties include failure-to-remit and false-statement penalties under section 280, gross-negligence penalties under section 285 for false statements or omissions, and criminal enforcement under section 239 in fraudulent cases. The normal GST/HST reassessment period is four years, but it is unlimited where misrepresentation, fraud, or gross negligence is established; intercompany export pricing may also be challenged under Canadian transfer-pricing rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Capital account transactions, including equity injections and profit remittances, are subject to Bank of Canada oversight or channeling through authorized dealers; non-resident ownership in ULCs is permitted but profit repatriation requires tax reporting and potential withholding tax compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canadian exporters must repatriate foreign exchange proceeds within 90 days of receipt and surrender them to authorized foreign exchange dealers at market rates. Surrendered amounts must be reported to the Canada Revenue Agency; non-compliance may trigger penalties and reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility; electronic money issuers are subject to strict licensing under the Payment Card Networks Act and Bank of Canada regulations, restricting cross-border digital EMIs without specific approvals.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File if gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million. Additionally, transaction-specific thresholds: services > CAD 1 million, goods > CAD 5 million, and other transactions > CAD 1 million. Documentation must be prepared by the filing due date.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a separate ESR regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions require economic substance. For treaty benefits, the principal purpose test (PPT) under the Multilateral Instrument (MLI) applies. A ULC must demonstrate real business activity, employees, and decision-making in Canada to avoid treaty denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239: fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties: 50% gross negligence penalty on understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they failed to withhold or remit (s.227.1). Travel bans and asset freezes possible under criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income plus penalty of 10% of net adjustment (if adjustment > CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue). Interest on reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in or acquiesced to the misstatement; civil restitution and potential criminal charges for gross negligence.
Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: fines up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 10 years. FINTRAC administrative penalties up to CAD 1 million per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned if they knowingly direct or participate in money laundering; corporate veil pierced under criminal law.
