Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Conduct an Alberta name search (NUANS) and file Articles of Incorporation under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (ABCA) through the Alberta Corporate Registry, expressly including the 'unlimited liability' designation required for a ULC, to obtain the Alberta Corporation Access Number (CAN). Step 2: Register with the CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts'; the CRA issues the statutory 9-digit Business Number (BN), which functions as the national TIN and must be quoted on all tax filings, invoices, and customs declarations. Step 3: Open the required program accounts under the BN: RC for federal corporate income tax and Alberta corporate income tax (administered by CRA), RT for GST/HST, and RP for payroll if employees are engaged. Step 4: Complete GST/HST registration (RT account) at the same time; an exporter making only zero-rated supplies is not legally required to register but should register voluntarily to claim input tax credits on manufacturing inputs. Step 5: If the ULC has no Canadian place of business, appoint a Canadian agent for service of process and provide a Canadian address to CRA, as required for non-resident and ULC registrants. Step 6: Register as a CBSA client using the same BN and activate a CERS account to file electronic export declarations. Step 7: No separate provincial tax registration is required in Alberta; the T2 corporate income tax return filed with the CRA settles both federal Part I tax and Alberta provincial corporate tax under the Tax Collection Agreement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible CAD currency regime with no capital account restrictions for most legitimate corporate transactions. Current account items including profit remittances, dividends, and interest payments are freely convertible without prior approval. All cross-border FX transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 trigger mandatory FINTRAC reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. No prior regulatory approval is required for standard remittances provided tax filings and beneficial ownership documentation are current.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds applies under Canadian FX regulations. Resident entities must repatriate foreign currency receipts to a Canadian bank account, but no statutory timeline or fixed conversion ratio exists. Repatriation is strongly encouraged for resident-controlled entities. All FX receipts over CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC with supporting documentation including invoices, shipping records, and payment proofs.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Limited but viable through regulated platforms such as Wise Business or Mercury. Offshore digital EMIs require full Canadian corporate registration, FINTRAC compliance, and enhanced due diligence. Legal constraints include potential reclassification as an unlicensed money services business if structures bypass traditional banking rails.
