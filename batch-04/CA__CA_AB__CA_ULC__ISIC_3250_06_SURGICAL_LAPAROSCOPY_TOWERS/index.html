Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

An Alberta corporation must maintain a registered office in Alberta and a records office in Alberta as required by the Business Corporations Act. A commercial registered-office service may be acceptable if it provides a valid physical Alberta address, accepts service, maintains required records or access arrangements, and satisfies registry and bank requirements. A mailbox-only address is inadequate. For medical-device manufacturing, a virtual registered office does not replace the actual manufacturing, quality-system, licensing, importation, distribution, or establishment requirements applicable under the federal Food and Drugs Act and Medical Devices Regulations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, official business portal: CRA 'My Business Account' at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA under a tax collection agreement with Alberta Treasury Board and Finance; customs and export border controls are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) at https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca. Alberta has no provincial sales tax authority.

E-government filing portal

The Alberta ULC must first be incorporated or registered with the Alberta Corporate Registry; the foreign parent then obtains the statutory Canadian tax identifier by applying for a CRA Business Number (BN) through Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or CRA Business Registration On-line (BRO). The 9-digit BN with program account suffixes (RC for federal corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST, RM for payroll/import-export) serves as the TIN/VAT ID. A non-resident-owned ULC carrying on manufacturing activities, holding inventory, or maintaining employees in Alberta must register for the RC, RT, and RM program accounts, file T2 corporate income tax returns, and open an RM customs account with CBSA if it imports or exports goods. CRA will request the certificate of incorporation, Alberta registration extract, articles of amendment, and proof of registered head office address before issuing the BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No domestic Canadian check-the-box election exists under the Income Tax Act; an Alberta ULC is generally a taxable Canadian corporation and is assessed at the entity level. However, the ULC is eligible for pass-through treatment for U.S. federal tax purposes if all U.S. shareholders elect to treat it as a disregarded entity on IRS Form 8832. CRA does not recognize this election for Canadian tax, and will continue to treat the ULC as a separate person subject to Canadian corporate income tax, GST/HST, and Part XIII withholding tax on dividends. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA sections 18.2 and 18.3 may deny a deduction for a ULC interest, royalty, or management fee payment that is deductible in Canada but not included in the foreign owner's income solely because of the check-the-box classification, unless a 'fully taxed' exception or treaty limitation applies. A ULC may also be entitled to benefits under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention as a 'resident of Canada' if it is liable to tax in Canada on its worldwide income; no paper election is mandatory to claim treaty benefits, though Form NR303 can be filed to evidence residency for reduced withholding.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. A person, including a non-resident entity, making taxable supplies in Canada in the course of carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST if the person's annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000. The threshold is measured on a calendar-quarter-look-back and prospective basis; zero-rated exports count as taxable supplies for this purpose. Once the threshold is exceeded, registration under CRA GST/HST account (RT) is mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate the supply of surgical laparoscopy towers and related 4K endoscopic cameras, monitors, and trocars if the goods are delivered to a foreign hospital or distributor outside Canada and are exported by the supplier 'as soon after the supply is made as is reasonable' under section 21 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. The supply must be made to a non-resident and the goods must not be acquired for use, consumption, or supply in Canada. Optional software or configuration services exported with the original hardware may be zero-rated as a supply of movable property exported under section 9 (basic zero-rating) or as a supply to a non-resident under section 7, subject to the service being performed for a non-resident outside Canada for exclusive use outside Canada. The ULC must obtain and retain clear documentary evidence of shipment, destination, and foreign buyer status to support the 0% rate and the corresponding input tax credits (ITCs) for GST paid on raw materials, components, and overheads.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or electronic CERS Record)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing Canadian point of departure and foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice with full product description, HS classification, quantities, and value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT/TT transfer confirmation showing payment received in Canadian or foreign currency in a Canadian account
  • Packing List with serial numbers, weights, and unit counts
  • Signed Export Sales Contract or Purchase Order from the non-resident buyer
  • Forwarder's Cargo Receipt or Carrier Certificate of Export

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing or fiscal digital stamping regime currently exists in Canada. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records if they meet the GST/HST invoice requirements under section 221 of the Excise Tax Act (supplier identity, GST number, date, taxable amount, tax rate, and tax payable). Export declarations are filed electronically through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or the modernized CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal; there is no API-based real-time clearance or digital stamping for ordinary GST/HST invoices.

CRA may reassess a GST/HST return within four years of the filing date, but where a zero-rating claim is fraudulent or involves misrepresentation attributable to negligence, carelessness, or wilful default, reassessment is unrestricted. If the ULC fails to produce satisfactory evidence of export, CRA will recharacterise the supply as taxable at 5% GST, assess additional tax, interest, and penalties, and may deny the ITCs claimed. Gross negligence penalties under the Excise Tax Act can amount to 50% of the understated net tax; false statements or omissions can attract civil penalties up to 25% of the amount. Criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act can result in fines up to 200% of the evaded tax, imprisonment up to five years, or both. CBSA may also issue Corporate Administrative Monetary Penalties (CAMPs) for inaccurate export declarations, including omissions of mandated shipping particulars.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with full convertibility for current account transactions under the Foreign Exchange Act. Capital account transactions are generally open for corporate entities, though large or sensitive investments may require prior review by the Bank of Canada or OSFI. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted subject to withholding tax obligations and beneficial ownership reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the Bank of Canada is required. Exporters may retain foreign currency or convert to CAD at their discretion. All export transactions must be reported to CBSA and CRA with supporting commercial invoices, packing lists, and shipping documents for customs and tax compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border EMI operations via fintech platforms are restricted in Canada due to FINTRAC registration and OSFI oversight. Offshore digital EMIs require licensing through a Canadian bank or authorized partner, with strict AML controls and no unlicensed issuance permitted.
