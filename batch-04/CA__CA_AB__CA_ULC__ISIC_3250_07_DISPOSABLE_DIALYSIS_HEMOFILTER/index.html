Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official federal tax portal is CRA My Business Account (canada.ca/my-business-account) and CRA Represent a Client for external tax representatives. Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Canada-Alberta tax collection agreement, making the CRA the single competent authority for federal and provincial corporate income tax and GST/HST; the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) retains residual competence only for provincial fuel, tobacco, carbon, and special levy regimes and has no jurisdiction over the ULC's corporate income tax or GST/HST.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Alberta ULC by filing Articles of Incorporation through the Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES) under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation with a registered office in Alberta. Step 2: Register the ULC with the CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) using CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), Form RC1, or the Alberta integrated new-business registration channel; the BN is generally issued within one to three business days. Step 3: Open a GST/HST account under the same BN (Form RC1 Part I) when the ULC makes its first taxable supply in Canada or immediately upon exceeding the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold. Step 4: Open an Import/Export (RM) customs account with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) to obtain a Canadian customs client code for exporting hemodialyzers and importing polyethersulfone resin and PVC tubing. Step 5: Register for a CRA payroll (RP) account when hiring Alberta staff and obtain a Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) covering the manufacture, importation, and distribution of Class II-IV dialysis consumables. Step 6: File the first corporate income tax return (T2) within six months of fiscal year-end (mandatory electronic filing where gross revenue exceeds CAD 1,000,000) and report income to Alberta through the CRA-administered T2 system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (dual-status classification). Under Canadian domestic law, the Alberta ULC is a separate taxable corporation under the Income Tax Act (ITA) and is subject to entity-level Part I federal corporate income tax plus Alberta provincial corporate income tax; non-resident ownership is permitted, but the ULC is never fiscally transparent in Canada. Under U.S. federal tax classification, a Canadian ULC is not a per se corporation under U.S. Treas. Reg. Section 301.7701-2(b)(8), so a U.S. shareholder may file IRS Form 8832 to elect partnership or disregarded-entity treatment, achieving U.S. pass-through taxation of the Alberta dialysis manufacturing business while the entity remains a Canadian corporate taxpayer. Territorial scope: Canada taxes resident corporations on worldwide income with foreign tax credit relief under ITA s. 126; Alberta is a full-taxation province with no territorial regime, no exempt-zone status, and no provincial income-tax exemption applicable to this manufacturer.

Available election forms

Available statutory elections: (1) U.S. check-the-box election on IRS Form 8832 to classify the Alberta ULC as a partnership or disregarded entity for U.S. federal purposes, effective retroactively up to 75 days before filing and up to 12 months after filing; (2) GST/HST group registration election under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 273 allowing the ULC and related Canadian corporations to file consolidated GST returns; (3) ITA s. 261 functional-currency election allowing the ULC to compute Canadian taxable income in U.S. dollars where its books are maintained in USD; (4) ITA s. 216 election by non-resident owners to report net Canadian rental income on a reduced basis where applicable; (5) voluntary GST/HST registration election available before the small-supplier threshold so the ULC can recover input tax credits from the first taxable supply. Hybrid-mismatch compliance: Canada's hybrid rules (ITA s. 18.4 and s. 18.5) deny deductions on certain structured financial arrangements that exploit double-dip outcomes; no Canadian hybrid-mismatch election exists, so the ULC must align its IRS Form 8832 effective date with supply-chain cash flows and document that no intended double-dip deduction arises under the Canada-U.S. tax treaty.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1); a person must register for GST/HST once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the aggregate of the four preceding calendar quarters, with the same test applying to non-residents carrying on business in Canada. Voluntary registration from the first taxable supply is available and prudent for full input tax credit recovery; once registered, the ULC must remain registered for at least 12 months and deregistration requires an application supported by CRA consent under ETA s. 242.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating of exported tangible personal property under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 requires the ULC to (1) supply the hollow-fiber polyethersulfone dialyzers to a foreign purchaser, (2) export the goods as or as part of the supply by delivering possession outside Canada through the supplier, the purchaser, or a common carrier, and (3) obtain and retain CRA-recognized evidence of export, administratively accepted when obtained within 60 days of the supply and showing the goods left Canada within a reasonable time. Services: ETA Schedule VI, Part V also zero-rates certain services supplied to non-residents where the service is performed wholly outside Canada or performed in respect of exported goods, which covers offshore filter potting or assembly work conducted for a U.S. or other non-resident medical OEM. The ULC may not zero-rate supplies made to a Canadian resident intermediary, disposables sold for domestic Canadian consumption, or promotional samples, and must maintain arm's-length transfer-pricing documentation under ITA s. 247 where the foreign buyer is related. FX repatriation: Canadian tax and customs law does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; this dossier adopts the 180-day benchmark aligned with standard Swift LC and TT settlement terms because CRA and CBSA may question prolonged unpaid export receivables as evidence of a domestic supply or an unsubstantiated price adjustment.

Canada has no national mandatory e-invoicing, no real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based digital stamping, and no continuous-control clearance regime for GST/HST. Requirements in force: (A) mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account, or approved third-party software once annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1,500,000; (B) export shipments over CAD 2,000 must be reported electronically to the CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) using Form B13A (CUSDEC) or authorized broker software; (C) all invoices issued by the ULC must display the GST/HST registration number and Business Number; (D) electronic books and records must be retained in legible, retrievable format for six years under ETA s. 286. Quebec's phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing mandate (PEPPOL-based, effective for large suppliers from 2026-2027) does not bind an Alberta-based ULC absent Quebec counterparties; Alberta has no provincial e-invoicing statute.

CRA's GST/HST Export Verification Program (EVP) specifically targets medical-device exporters, including dialysis consumables manufacturers, and can reassess a zero-rated export and claw back the 5% GST together with associated input tax credits within the normal four-year assessment window under ETA s. 298, with the limitation period extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. A finding of insufficient export evidence reverses the zero rating entirely and triggers: statutory arrears interest; late-filing and late-remittance penalties equal to 5% of net tax plus 1% per complete month, capped at 12 months; a false-declaration or gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the tax sought to be avoided under the ETA penalty regime; and, upon conviction, fines of up to 200% of the tax and imprisonment of up to five years for wilful evasion under ETA s. 326. CRA audit teams also apply ITA s. 247 transfer-pricing verification to the export price of dialyzers sold to foreign distributors and scrutinize round-tripping of funds through related offshore entities. The ULC must therefore retain the CERS export record, bills of lading, commercial invoices, proof of delivery, and FX remittance evidence for at least six years under ETA s. 286 in a legible, electronically retrievable format.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both capital and current accounts, with no general restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders of an Alberta ULC. Payments on current account and capital transfers are freely permitted. Statutory withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) applies to certain outbound payments such as dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees, but withholding does not constitute exchange-control convertibility restriction. A non-resident shareholder of an Alberta ULC receiving branch-like pass-through allocations must consider withholding under subsection 216(1) on deemed Canadian-source income. Intercompany cross-border payments must satisfy transfer pricing documentation under section 247 to avoid recharacterization. There is no requirement to obtain Bank of Canada approval for profit remittance or foreign direct investment liquidation. For jurisdictions subject to Canadian sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act or the United Nations Act, payments may be blocked even if otherwise convertible.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for repatriation or conversion of export receipts into CAD. An Alberta ULC manufacturing dialysis consumables may retain export proceeds in foreign currency accounts with Canadian authorized foreign banks or hold such proceeds offshore, reporting the foreign property if the aggregate cost amount exceeds CAD 100,000 for Canadian income tax under section 233.3 and Form T1135. Proceeds must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency under the Customs Act for export reporting where required, but such reporting is statistical and compliance-related, not an FX surrender rule. Invoices in USD or EUR are permissible and settlement may be made through correspondent clearing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated bank prudential oversight. Under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act, the Canadian dollar (CAD) is the sole lawful currency, but no exchange control law or central bank approval requirement applies to cross-border fund transfers or currency conversion.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for transactional/trade-finance support; Low for primary operating account. Purpose-built Canadian fintechs such as a subsidiary of a regulated bank or a money service business registered with FINTRAC may offer cross-border payment services and multi-currency accounts. However, an offshore EMI licensed solely under an EEA passport or other foreign regime is generally not permissible as the principal treasury account for an Alberta ULC because it would not constitute a deposit-taking institution regulated in Canada, cannot provide a Canadian-branch or clearing routing capability, and may trigger unpaid supplier/consignment risks in North American healthcare procurement. Offshore EMIs may be used only for ancillary receivables concentration or vendor disbursement outside Canada, subject to FINTRAC MSB registration for Canadian-resident service providers and the prohibition on accepting deposits in the course of carrying on business in Canada under the Bank Act. An Alberta medical-device manufacturer must maintain a Canadian bank account for payroll, GST/HST remittance, CRA tax payments, and provincial health authority trade documentation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium for transactional/trade-finance support; Low for primary operating account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined federal and Alberta general corporate income tax rate (15% federal general rate + 8% Alberta general rate). An Alberta ULC is taxed as a Canadian resident corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) regardless of its unlimited liability and US check-the-box hybrid status.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends and deemed dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-residents. For an Alberta ULC, distributions to a US shareholder are treated as dividends for Canadian tax purposes and are therefore subject to Part XIII withholding at the statutory 25% rate unless treaty relief is successfully invoked.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due within 6 months after fiscal year-end. Final balance of tax payable due within 2 months after year-end (3 months for a CCPC claiming the Small Business Deduction). Instalments are due at the end of each fiscal quarter (or monthly for certain large corporations) in accordance with ITA s.157.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual fair market value of specified cross-border transactions with a non-arm's-length non-resident triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under ITA s.247(4). The documentation must be prepared before the T2 filing due date and retained for 6 years. Penalty of 10% of the net upward transfer-pricing adjustment applies where the aggregate net adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue for the year. Master File and Local File equivalents should be structured in alignment with CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R and OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the Gulf Cooperation Council type; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), the hybrid-mismatch recharacterization rules (ITA s.18.2 and s.18.3), and the 5th Protocol to the Canada-US Treaty collectively impose an economic-substance and business-purpose test. A ULC that is a shell with no real Canadian employees, premises, production activity, or decision-making functions, interposed solely to strip profits from the US parent, is vulnerable to GAAR reassessments, Part XIII deemed-dividend withholding, and penalty exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Indictable conviction under ITA s.239(1)(a): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years. Summary conviction under s.239(1)(b): fine up to CAD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years. CRA will also assess gross-negligence penalties, late-filing penalties, daily compounding interest, and may freeze bank accounts and seize assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders of an Alberta ULC face unlimited shareholder liability for corporate obligations; courts will pierce the veil for fraudulent conduct. CRA may impose travel bans via Canada Border Services Agency wanted-flags, issue arrest warrants, attach personal assets, and civilly recover the full tax deficiency from individuals who directed the evasion.
Gross Negligence and False Statements (ITA s.163(2))

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax or overstated refund; daily compounding interest at the CRA prescribed rate plus 4%; denial of the deduction in full; elevated CRA audit risk classification; and potential referral of the file to the Criminal Investigations Division where gross negligence crosses into willful blindness.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve the misstatement may be assessed personally under ITA s.227.1 (unremitted source deductions) and under equitable fraud doctrines. Because ULC shareholders carry unlimited liability, the corporate veil offers no protection; directors' personal residences and investment accounts are reachable to satisfy confirmed assessments.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Documentation Deficit

Statutory sanction: 10% penalty under ITA s.247(4) on the net upward transfer-pricing adjustment where the aggregate net adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue; full principal adjustment to taxable income; consequential gross-up adjustments; loss of correlative relief; and in aggravated cases criminal referral for profit-diversion schemes.

Director personal liability: While the s.247(4) penalty is corporate-level, directors who sign a knowingly unsupported T2 risk personal exposure under s.239(1) and s.163(2). The unlimited shareholder liability attribute of the ULC extends to the assessed tax deficiency and penalty amount, allowing CRA civil recovery against shareholders' personal wealth in Alberta.
Money Laundering, Shell Company Abuse, and FINTRAC AML Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code s.462.31 money laundering offences: imprisonment up to 10 years. FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and up to CAD 1,000,000 per contravention for entities. CRA may impose a 100% penalty on unreported foreign-source income and seize proceeds of crime; the Canada Border Services Agency may impose corporate-entity forfeiture and export bans.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and shareholders acting as instruments of the shell structure can be prosecuted personally for laundering. ULC unlimited shareholder liability exposes every shareholder to full satisfaction of FINTRAC penalties, tax deficiencies, and forfeiture orders. Travel bans, asset freezes, and personal bankruptcy proceedings are enforceable where the shell is found to be a fraudulent conveyance or a criminal enterprise.
