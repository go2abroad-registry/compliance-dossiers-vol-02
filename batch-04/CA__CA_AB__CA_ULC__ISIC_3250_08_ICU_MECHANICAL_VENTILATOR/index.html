Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; an Alberta ULC may incorporate with capital paid for in cash or property as determined by the incorporators; no escrow account is statutorily required.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain Alberta Building Code (ABC) compliance from municipal building safety authorities (e.g., City of Calgary or City of Edmonton Safety Codes Permit); a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate per the Alberta Fire Code; structural load assessments demonstrating that the facility can support heavy HVAC or turbine-testing equipment; workplace clearance and an OHS incident prevention plan from Alberta Occupational Health and Safety (Alberta Labour); and certification of electrical / mechanical systems by a licensed professional engineer. For production of life-support equipment, the facility must also secure Health Canada establishment licensing and relevant quality-system certificates (CSAZ/ISO 13485), all subject to random inspection by Health Canada and Alberta Municipal Affairs.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital; an Alberta ULC may incorporate with capital paid for in cash or property as determined by the incorporators; no escrow account is statutorily required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal corporate income tax and GST/HST administration; registration portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-registration-online.html). Provincial registry: Service Alberta / Alberta Corporate Registry (Registry One) for the ULC incorporation. Alberta corporate income tax is assessed and collected by the CRA under the Alberta-CRA Tax Collection Agreement.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the Alberta ULC under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) (RSA 2000, c. B-9, Part 2.1) via Alberta Corporate Registry; obtain the incorporation certificate and registry number. 2) Apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) and program accounts — RP (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RC (payroll), RM (import/export) — through Business Registration Online or Form RC1, authenticating with the Alberta registry number. 3) Register for GST/HST under ETA s. 240; the CRA assigns an RT program account and the BN must be shown on every GST/HST invoice. 4) Because the ULC is presumed foreign-controlled, it is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation; the general federal rate (15%) and Alberta rate (8%) apply to taxable Canadian-source income. 5) Separate non-tax statutory licensing (Health Canada Medical Device Licence / Establishment Licence under the Food and Drugs Act) must precede commercial sale; CRA tax registration does not constitute health regulatory approval.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Canadian domestic law, the Alberta ULC is a corporation subject to entity-level Part I income tax (federal 15% + Alberta 8%). The 'Pass_Through_Eligible' classification derives from U.S. federal 'check-the-box' eligibility under Treasury Reg. § 301.7701-3 (Form 8832): an Alberta ULC, having unlimited liability, is an eligible entity that may elect disregarded-entity or partnership status for U.S. federal tax purposes while remaining a Canadian resident corporation. Other elections: (a) ETA s. 156 election between closely related corporations to treat intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration (non-taxable, no input tax credits); (b) GST/HST Quick Method remittance election under ETA s. 227 where annual worldwide taxable supplies do not exceed CAD 400,000; (c) entity-classification election (Form 8832) is the principal mechanism to manage hybrid status, subject to Canada's hybrid mismatch rules enacted under BEPS Action 2 (2023 Budget Implementation Act) — no separate 'hybrid mismatch election' exists under the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1)(a): registration is mandatory once taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; registration must be completed within 29 days thereafter. Voluntary registration is available under ETA s. 240(4).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (including ICU mechanical ventilators) are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 where the goods are sold for delivery outside Canada and are exported 'by the supplier or at the supplier's direction'. Related post-sale services (warranty, technical support, calibration) may also be zero-rated under Part V, s. 7 where supplied to a non-resident and the benefit is realized outside Canada. The ULC must be a GST/HST registrant; zero-rated exports are taxable supplies at 0%, count toward the small-supplier threshold, and preserve the right to claim input tax credits on Canadian inputs. Domestic deliveries of ventilators within Canada are generally subject to 5% GST unless a specific Part II Schedule VI medical-device zero-rating applies; export zero-rating applies irrespective of the domestic classification. Canada does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure below represents the internal documentary retention and financing-control window applied by trade-finance banks and used by CRA as an evidentiary reference for traceable export proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A or CERS electronic export declaration; for U.S. land shipments, the reconciled export manifest/ACE record) naming the ULC as exporter and showing date and mode of export
  • Bill of Lading (ocean bill, airway bill, or truck waybill) naming the ULC as exporter or shipper; where a freight forwarder is named, a carrier certificate and CRA 'Exporter's Self-Declaration'
  • Commercial invoice for the exported goods plus the foreign destination customs entry or customs broker certification evidencing physical export
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT wire payment record or bank credit advice from the foreign buyer evidencing settlement in the invoiced currency
  • Packing list, customs value/weight summary, and the CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17.2 documentary checklist (including third-party exporter declarations where required)

No real-time fiscal clearance regime, government API pre-clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada. A GST/HST registrant must issue sales invoices containing prescribed information (ETA s. 223; simplified invoices under s. 224) and may issue electronic invoices with the recipient's consent and acceptable records (GST/HST Memorandum 12.10). Canada's federal and provincial procurement has adopted optional e-invoicing via the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (PEPPOL/PINT standard); buyer-driven B2B portals (EDI, Ariba, Coupa) are contractual, not statutory fiscal clearance obligations. The CRA requires invoices to show the supplier's BN/RT account and, for zero-rated exports, a statement that the supply is zero-rated.

CRA GST/HST reassessment window is normally four years (ETA s. 298) but becomes unlimited where a return contains misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud. Where export documentation is deficient, CRA may reassess the export revenue as taxable at 5% GST, deny related input tax credits, and assess prescribed arrears interest. Penalties: under ETA s. 280(2), knowingly making a false statement or gross negligence triggers a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 200% of the tax sought to be evaded; criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 327 carries fines and imprisonment (up to 5 years). Additional exposure for this ULC structure: ETA s. 323 makes directors jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST, and the unlimited liability attribute of the ULC allows creditors and CRA to pursue shareholders directly for corporate GST debts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current and capital account. There are no restrictions on non-resident equity ownership, dividend streaming, or profit remittance for an Alberta ULC. No physical repatriation or surrender requirements exist. Certain informational filings may apply (FATCA/CRS under Part XVIII of the Income Tax Act; CFIUS-style review under the Investment Canada Act), and the ULC's unlimited liability and tax-transparent status must be disclosed to the bank for risk-rating purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose mandatory export-proceeds surrender, conversion, or repatriation timelines. Exporters may retain foreign exchange revenues offshore or in domestic foreign-currency bank accounts. For all export/import transactions exceeding C$10,000, the money-transfer and reporting rules require record-keeping and, where applicable, FINTRAC reports; underlying commercial documents must support the Income Tax Act and customs disclosures.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Department of Finance Canada (Canada applies a free-floating exchange-rate regime with no exchange-control agency; the Bank of Canada issues currency and manages monetary policy, while cross-border reporting is enforceable through FINTRAC and Canada Border Services Agency).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to medium. No passport for EU/UK EMI licences into Canada. A fintech must be a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB) or hold a Canadian payment-provider arrangement with a Schedule I bank. Legal constraints include the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, the Retail Payment Activities Act (when in force), and bank-completion requirements. Such rails are suitable for collecting customer funds but not for capital-block escrow, government LCs, or trade settlements.
