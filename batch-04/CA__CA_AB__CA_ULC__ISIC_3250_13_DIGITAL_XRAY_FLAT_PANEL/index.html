Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account at cra-arc.gc.ca. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement via the T2 return and Alberta Schedule.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) in Alberta through the Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) with Articles of Incorporation, registered office requirement, no minimum capital, and unlimited shareholder liability. Step 2: Register with CRA for a 15-digit Business Number (BN: 9-digit number + 2-letter program identifier + 4-digit reference), using Form RC1, paper, or CRA Business Registration Online; obtain the RT (GST/HST) program account, RC (corporate income tax), and RP (payroll) accounts as applicable. Step 3: Apply the ETA small supplier test; register for GST/HST when taxable supplies made in Canada in the course of a commercial activity exceed CAD 30,000 worldwide for residents, or CAD 30,000 of Canadian-source supplies for a non-resident carrying on business in Canada; charge and remit 5% GST on taxable supplies, while exports are zero-rated. Step 4: File the annual T2 Corporation Income Tax Return within 6 months of fiscal year-end, including Alberta corporate income tax computed on the T2 Alberta Schedule (general rate 8%; small business deduction generally unavailable if the ULC is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation). Step 5: For a US-resident sole owner, file US Form 8832 for checked-the-box disregarded entity treatment for US federal tax purposes; Canadian Part I tax treatment as a corporation remains unchanged. Step 6: For exporters of medical radiology hardware, obtain Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) as a non-tax prerequisite and affix the CRA BN to all GST/HST invoices and export documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Canada applies entity-level taxation to the ULC as a corporation under ITA subsection 89(1); US check-the-box eligibility under Treasury Regulation 301.7701-3 permits disregarded entity or partnership flow-through treatment for a 100% US owner; Alberta applies the general 8% corporate income tax on federally determined taxable income; no territorial exemption or tax-exempt zone status applies).

Available election forms

(1) US check-the-box election under Treasury Regulation 301.7701-3 via Form 8832 to treat the Alberta ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for US federal income tax. (2) Canada-US Income Tax Convention Article IV(6) hybrid mismatch coordination rule, allocating treaty benefits consistent with each state's characterization of the ULC. (3) ITA subsection 84(2) deemed dividend election upon wind-up so distributions exceeding paid-up capital are treated as taxable dividends rather than proceeds of disposition. (4) ITA subsection 89(11) ULC non-resident shareholder adjustments, maintaining safe income and GRIP/LRIP accounts to characterize ordinary dividends for non-resident withholding tax. (5) Excise Tax Act subsection 156(2) closely related corporation election allowing zero-rated intercompany supplies and simplified GST/HST accounting. (6) Schedule VI section 7 election to zero-rate certain offshore services performed wholly outside Canada for non-residents.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold under ETA subsection 148(5)); for Canadian residents the threshold measures worldwide taxable supplies; for non-residents it measures taxable supplies made in Canada while carrying on business in Canada. A ULC whose worldwide or Canadian-source taxable revenues exceed CAD 30,000 in any preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in the current quarter must register for GST/HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under subsection 6(1) of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (ETA) applies to tangible personal property, including digital X-ray flat panel detectors, where the vendor ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada as part of a supply to a non-resident, the goods are exported in the commercial flow of trade, and the vendor retains required export evidence per CRA GST/HST Memorandum 14-5. The Alberta ULC must satisfy: (i) title passes in Canada to a non-resident purchaser; (ii) goods are exported promptly, normally within a commercially reasonable period not exceeding 60 days; (iii) a commercial invoice, bill of lading or air waybill, customs export declaration, and proof of foreign payment are retained; and (iv) the supply is reported as a zero-rated taxable supply on the GST/HST return, enabling full input tax credit recovery of GST paid on domestic inputs (amorphous silicon, cesium iodide deposition materials, substrate panels, and manufacturing overhead). Related services such as installation, calibration, remote diagnostics, and warranty provided to non-residents and performed wholly outside Canada may be zero-rated under section 7(a) of Schedule VI; services performed in Canada for foreign recipients, such as local repair of a detector, remain taxable at 5% GST.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or paper B13A form (mandatory under the Customs Act Export Reporting Regulations for controlled, regulated, or goods valued above CAD 2,000; serves as prima facie evidence of physical export)
  • Commercial invoice / statement of sale identifying the non-resident buyer, description of the DR detectors, export terms (Incoterms), and declared value (required by CRA GST/HST Memorandum 14-5)
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing dispatch from Alberta, Canada to the foreign destination and signed by the carrier
  • Freight forwarder's certificate of export or carrier's cargo manifest corroborating the movement of goods out of Canada
  • Foreign exchange settlement record, including bank SWIFT/MT103 credit advice or TT wire confirmation, evidencing remittance of export proceeds from the non-resident purchaser

None. Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government API validation, or digital stamping. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records under ETA subsection 223(1) and supports the voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol BIS 3.0 / five-corner model. Mandatory invoice content includes seller name, CRA BN, invoice date, description of goods, amount of GST charged, and buyer name or BN; invoices exceeding CAD 150 must show the recipient's GST/HST number. Records must be retained for six years for audit purposes.

CRA may reassess within 4 years (extended to 7 years for misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default) to deny zero-rating where export documentation is incomplete; denied zero-rating is reassessed at 5% GST on the full invoice value plus compound interest under ETA section 280. Penalties include: (a) failure to collect or remit GST - up to 25% of the unremitted amount plus interest (ETA subsection 280(1)); (b) false statements or omissions on returns - greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (ETA subsection 285(1)); (c) fraud or wilful evasion - indictable offence under ETA section 327 with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, plus a civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded; (d) clawback of previously claimed input tax credits with interest from the date the credit was taken. Directors and officers of the ULC may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST under ETA subsection 323. CRA also analyzes export patterns through commodity codes, freight terms, and settlement timelines; unexplained foreign exchange delays beyond a commercially reasonable period (180-day benchmark) may trigger an audit of the export evidence chain and treaty-based hybrid ownership arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible in both current and capital accounts. No exchange controls restrict repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, or capital on an arms-length basis. Profit remittance from Alberta by a ULC is subject to treaty-based non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (default 25% on dividends, reduced under applicable tax treaties) and must be substantiated by financial statements and, where applicable, a certificate of compliance. There is no separate convertibility or capital account clearance hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or holding-period applies to export proceeds in Canada. Export receipts may be retained in USD/EUR in a Canadian dollar or foreign currency bank account at an authorized bank. Documentary evidence for export includes the Canada Customs Invoice, Bill of Lading, and any applicable Electronic Export Information; medical X-ray flat panel detectors do not require export permits under normal circumstances, but items with military or high-end detection capability might require Controlled Goods Directorate authorization or an Export Control List license.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) as monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as bank prudential regulator; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/CFT supervisor; no dedicated foreign exchange control ministry — Canada administers FX under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act, with exchange controls effectively liberalized under the Current and Capital Account liberalization framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditionally viable for subsidiary treasury operations but not as a primary bank relationship. Canadian law treats non-bank fintechs as Money Services Businesses under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and, since 2024, as Retail Payment Activities Providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), requiring registration with the Bank of Canada. Offshore digital EMIs may operate in Canada through a licensed PSP or under a limited-purpose charter; they cannot accept deposits without a Schedule I bank licence. Feasibility rating: Medium for low-balance disbursement rails; Low for capital escrow and trade-finance operations because LC issuance and Canadian Routing Number clearing are restricted to domestic banks.
