UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Alberta corporations are principally governed by the Business Corporations Act, RSA 2000, c B-9, and Business Corporations Regulation, Alta Reg 118/2000. A private Alberta corporation must maintain a register of individuals with significant control under Business Corporations Act, ss 21.1-21.31. The regime generally captures individuals with 25% or more of voting shares or fair market value, or individuals with control in fact, subject to statutory details. The register is not a public beneficial-ownership registry; prescribed authorities may obtain access. Federal corporate transparency amendments are in the Canada Business Corporations Act and do not automatically apply to an Alberta corporation. The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, SC 2000, c 17, and regulations impose customer-identification, beneficial-owner, recordkeeping, and reporting duties on reporting entities, including relevant financial institutions and certain dealers and intermediaries; they do not impose a general FINTRAC filing duty on every operating corporation.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

An Alberta corporation must maintain a registered office in Alberta and a records office as required by the Business Corporations Act. A commercial registered-office service may be used if it satisfies statutory requirements and can reliably receive legal process and official notices. A mailbox-only address is unsuitable. For active pharmaceutical extraction and chemical processing, a virtual registered office does not replace required manufacturing premises, environmental approvals, occupational health and safety compliance, pharmaceutical establishment and product authorizations, or credible operational substance. Confirm applicable Health Canada, Alberta, municipal, environmental, and controlled-substance requirements before site selection and operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account portal (canada.ca); provincial corporate registry: Alberta Corporate Registry (Alberta Business Registry Online).

E-government filing portal

Incorporation of the Alberta ULC is first registered with the Alberta Corporate Registry under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) as an unlimited liability corporation (Alta. Reg. 209/2019). Thereafter, the ULC must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO). The BN serves as the universal statutory TIN for all federal tax accounts (corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, import/export). Account activation for GST/HST is completed simultaneously on Form RC1 or via BRO; the BN plus the two-letter program identifier (e.g., RT for GST/HST) constitutes the GST registration number. A registered office address in Alberta and resident agent are required; non-resident directors are permitted. Foreign entity must provide certificate of incorporation, registered office affidavit, and valid director identification for Alberta registry filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the Alberta ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under the Income Tax Act (Canada), taxed at the federal and Alberta provincial corporate rates; however, it is Pass_Through_Eligible at the foreign shareholder level via the Canada-US income tax treaty hybrid election, permitting the ULC to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity for US federal income tax purposes while remaining a corporation for Canadian purposes. No territorial scope election; residency under common-law central management and control. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Alberta ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under the Income Tax Act (Canada), taxed at the federal and Alberta provincial corporate rates; however, it is Pass_Through_Eligible at the foreign shareholder level via the Canada-US income tax treaty hybrid election, permitting the ULC to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity for US federal income tax purposes while remaining a corporation for Canadian purposes. No territorial scope election; residency under common-law central management and control. No special tax-exempt zone other than the Northern Territory Council of Yukon, Northwest Territories, Nunavut relief, which is not applicable.

Available election forms

The ULC may file US Form 8832 (Entity Classification Election) to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity for US federal tax purposes, relying on the Canada–US Tax Treaty (Article IV(7) hybrid mismatch provision). For Canadian purposes, the ULC may elect under subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act for treaty-protected rental income. Under the Excise Tax Act, the ULC may file an election with a closely related corporation to treat supplies as made for nil consideration (s. 156) or use the quick-method election for GST/HST (ss. 227–228). Alberta imposes its own corporate income tax but no separate corporate registration number; CRA BN captures Alberta Tax Account through the provincial Corporate Income Tax administration agreement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (the 'small supplier threshold' under ETA s. 148; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada that makes taxable supplies exceeding CAD 30,000 in any trailing four-calendar-quarter period must register; registration is mandatory for any person making taxable supplies in Canada regardless of threshold if engaged in commercial activity).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (including heparin sodium crude fractionation and anticoagulant active pharmaceutical ingredients) are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act provided: (i) the goods are sold by the ULC to a non-resident purchaser; (ii) the goods are exported directly outside Canada by the supplier or the purchaser's agent; (iii) the ULC retains evidence of export acceptable to CRA; and (iv) possession of the goods transfers outside Canada. For services performed on the exported heparin crude (e.g., toll manufacturing), zero-rating under Schedule VI, Parts V and VII applies where the service is performed for a non-resident and the goods are delivered outside Canada. The ULC must not claim zero-rating where the non-resident purchaser or the purchaser's agent retains possession of the goods in Canada, unless a subsequent export is documented.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic CAED / G7 system submission) with unique export reference number
  • Commercial Invoice (ERP-generated) describing the heparin sodium crude, tariff classification (HS 3001.90 / 3004.90), quantity, and sale price
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank swift wire confirmation of foreign-currency payment
  • Packing List and certificate of analysis (COA) for pharma-grade heparin fractionation
  • Export licence or Government of Canada Controlled Goods / Health Canada export permit if applicable (heparin API export)
  • Proof of delivery per International Commercial Terms (Incoterms) contract

Canada has no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or government-approved continuous transaction controls. The CRA accepts paper, PDF, and EDI invoices; a voluntary Peppol-based e-invoicing framework is being implemented through the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing initiative. GST/HST returns are self-assessed and filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, or through a registered EDI service provider, with remittance due one month after the reporting period end. Failing electronic filing for large taxpayers (annual taxable supplies > CAD 1.5 million) attracts filing penalties.

CRA may disallow the zero-rated export treatment and reassess the output tax at 5% GST plus penalty if documentary proof is missing within six years (normal reassessment period). Specific penalties under the Excise Tax Act include: s. 280 – failure to register (up to CAD 10,000), s. 280.1 – failure to file returns (1% of unreported tax + 25% of understatement), s. 285 – false statements and misrepresentations (25% of gross tax), and s. 285.1 – civil penalty for knowingly making false statements (up to CAD 25,000). Willful evasion may trigger criminal prosecution (ETA s. 327), fines up to CAD 250,000, and imprisonment up to 12 months; proceeds of crime are subject to forfeiture. CRA will claw back input tax credits previously claimed on exported goods with interest (s. 397, s. 280.2). Transfer pricing and foreign-exchange repatriation rules under s. 247 of the Income Tax Act may further adjust the export price to arm's-length value and impose a 10% penalty on reassessed amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains current account convertibility under the Exchange Control Act, permitting unrestricted conversion of current account transactions including trade proceeds and profit remittances. Capital account transactions require prior review or approval by the Department of Foreign Affairs, Trade and Development (DFATD) or are subject to Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) oversight. Profit remittance is generally approved subject to tax filing and beneficial ownership documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds to a designated bank is required. Exporters must report all foreign exchange transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 to the Bank of Canada via the Electronic Data Interchange Reporting System (EDIR) within 30 days of the transaction date. No statutory conversion ratios or surrender obligations apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High
