Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal corporate income tax, Goods and Services Tax (GST), and Alberta corporate income tax administered under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement; registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account at https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/gst-hst/business-registration-online.html; Alberta corporate incorporation is registered through the Alberta Corporate Registration System administered by Service Alberta.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta ULC: (1) incorporate under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) and obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number; (2) apply to CRA for a Business Number (BN) and related program accounts using Business Registration Online or Form RC1, selecting RC (corporate income), RT (GST/HST) and RZ (import/export) accounts; (3) confirm that the ULC is deemed resident in Canada under Income Tax Act s.250(4)(a) for Canadian tax; (4) register for GST/HST when the mandatory threshold is met, or voluntarily earlier if input tax credits on export-related inputs are needed; (5) register separately with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) and obtain the ability to file Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) data.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian domestic law has no general check-the-box election: an Alberta ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation and cannot elect pass-through status for CRA purposes. However, a ULC is hybrid-eligible in foreign tax systems, notably through the U.S. Form 8832 check-the-box election, which can cause a wholly-owned ULC to be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. income tax while remaining a corporation in Canada. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules in Income Tax Act ss.18.4 to 18.6 are designed to neutralize resulting deduction, double-dip, or imported mismatch benefits. Other narrow statutory elections include ITA s.216(4) (non-resident rental income from Canadian real property filed on a net basis) and ITA s.217 (certain non-resident employment income); none alter the entity-level corporate classification default.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – a person supplying taxable goods or services in Canada must register once worldwide taxable sales exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across the last four consecutive calendar quarters; exporters commonly register voluntarily below this threshold to claim input tax credits on inputs used in export production.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate exported tangible personal property, including multi-parameter bedside telemetry monitors, under Excise Tax Act s.16 and Schedule VI Part V where the supply is exported by the supplier, delivered to a carrier for shipment outside Canada, or supplied to a non-resident under the prescribed documentary conditions. The goods must not be consumed, used, or resold in Canada before export. Services performed wholly outside Canada, and services supplied in respect of exported goods, may be zero-rated under Schedule VI sections 7 and 8 where applicable. The supplier must retain commercial invoices and shipment evidence showing the foreign destination; zero-rating applies regardless of payment timing, but the exporter must demonstrate that the goods left Canada within a commercially reasonable period.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) transmitted via CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), including exporter identifier, HS/IEC code, value, destination country, and export control classification
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms 2020 terms of sale, complete product description, quantity, unit value, foreign buyer identity, and stated export destination
  • Bill of lading or air waybill, or a rail/truck/parcel transport record and carrier manifest evidencing actual shipment outside Canada
  • Packing list, container or consignment weight data, and any carrier-issued proof of export stamps or border-crossing documentation
  • Bank-issued inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 / TT wire credit confirmation evidencing repatriation of export proceeds where required for transfer pricing, customs valuation, or CRA audit purposes

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is imposed in Canada. CRA accepts electronic invoices and accounting records as substantiation. The Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and Peppol network are voluntary e-invoicing standards. For exports, CBSA requires electronic submission of Canadian Export Declaration data through CERS for declarable exports, generally CAD 2,000 or more to non-U.S. destinations and all controlled, regulated, or prohibited goods; this is customs reporting, not a GST/HST invoice clearance mechanism.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims against proof-of-export requirements; insufficient or inconsistent documentation may cause the supply to be recharacterized as taxable in Canada, resulting in reassessment of 5% GST plus interest. False export claims attract civil penalties under Excise Tax Act ss.280 and 281, generally the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the misstated net tax, plus administrative interest. Deliberate fraudulent schemes may lead to criminal prosecution with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. The normal GST/HST reassessment period is four years, extended for negligence or misrepresentation to seven years or more; exporters should maintain the complete documentation bundle for at least six years from the date the return is filed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and does not impose general exchange controls. Current-account payments are freely convertible; capital-account transactions are broadly unrestricted, although non-Canadian acquisitions of Canadian businesses above statutory thresholds require notification and a net-benefit review under the Investment Canada Act. Profit remittance is permitted without Bank of Canada approval, but dividends, interest, royalties, and similar Part XIII payments by a Canadian corporation to non-residents are generally subject to 25% withholding tax unless reduced under an applicable tax treaty (commonly 5-15% for dividends, 0-10% for arm's-length interest, and 0-10% for royalties depending on treaty). A CA_ULC is a Canadian corporation for income tax purposes and remains exposed to Part XIII withholding on distributions notwithstanding any foreign check-the-box transparency election.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists in Canada. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no prescribed statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds. Canadian exporters may retain foreign currency receivables in foreign-currency bank accounts in Canada or abroad. For customs and trade-statistics purposes, exports must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) when required by the Export Reporting Regulations, and commercial invoices must state the transaction value. For Corporate Tax Act purposes, the CRA requires books of account to be maintained in Canadian dollars and foreign exchange gains/losses are taxable or deductible under section 39 of the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (prudential regulation of banks); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (AML/KYC supervision); Department of Finance Canada (exchange-rate policy and Investment Canada Act administration).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms do not provide a direct substitute for a Canadian bank account because they lack direct access to Payments Canada clearing systems (Lynx and ACSS), SWIFT settlement relationships for CAD, and the trust placement required for Canadian trade finance. A ULC treasury should maintain its primary operating and capital accounts with a Canadian Schedule I bank or ATB Financial. An offshore EMI may be used only as an auxiliary collection/expense wallet if the EMI is appropriately licensed in its home jurisdiction, has satisfactory sanctions controls, and, where operating from Canada or soliciting Canadian clients, registers as a money services business with FINTRAC. Virtual IBANs issued by foreign EMIs are not acceptable as proof of Canadian settlement for statutory records.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or investor to the ULC's Canadian bank account with ISO purpose code 'INTC' for an intercompany shareholder loan and 'CAPI' for an equity subscription. The remittance must include the ULC full legal name, Alberta registration number, beneficiary account number, and a concise reference to the board/shareholder resolution. For capital injections, the bank file must hold the subscription agreement, board resolution, share register, and source-of-funds evidence. CAD receipts settle through Payments Canada Lynx for large-value transfers or ACSS for retail wires; USD/EUR receipts are converted at the receiving bank's spot rate unless a separate forward is in place. No foreign exchange control approval is required, but Investment Canada Act notification may apply if the injection is part of an acquisition of control of the Canadian business and value thresholds are exceeded.Use SWIFT MT103 for open-account trade payments and telegraphic transfers to component suppliers; use irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700/MT707 for secured international purchases; use documentary collections (DP/DA) for smaller calibration cash-flow sales; and use bank payment obligations under MT798 where supply-chain finance terms are available. CAD disbursements settle via Lynx or ACSS, USD through Fedwire/CHIPS, and EUR through TARGET2 using the beneficiary bank's correspondent network. The ULC must support each disbursement with invoices, purchase orders, bills of lading, packing lists, and Incoterms documentation.
