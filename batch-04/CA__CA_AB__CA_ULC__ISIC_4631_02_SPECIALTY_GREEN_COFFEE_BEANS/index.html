Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE at www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement (Alberta has no separate tax authority).

E-government filing portal

An Alberta ULC incorporated under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) obtains its Business Number (BN), corporate income tax account (RC), GST/HST account (RT) and payroll account (RP) automatically through CRA Corporation Integration upon incorporation. A foreign entity not incorporated in Canada (e.g., a non-resident shareholder establishing a Canadian branch or carrying on commercial activity in Alberta) must apply on Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with certified incorporation documents, a completed Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) entity information schedule, a copy of the foreign registration certificate, and a non-resident declaration; CRA issues the BN immediately upon verification, and GST/HST registration is effective from the application date. Importing into Canada as a non-resident makes GST/HST registration mandatory even below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (US federal check-the-box perspective: an Alberta ULC lacks limited liability within Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3 and therefore defaults to a partnership (multi-member) or disregarded entity (single-member), unless Form 8832 elects corporate treatment). For Canadian domestic tax purposes the same ULC is an Entity_Level_Taxation taxpayer — a Part I taxable corporation subject to the 15% federal rate and the 8% Alberta rate (23% combined), and it is the GST/HST registrant of record; the pass-through status is recognized only under foreign tax law and Canada-US Tax Treaty Article IV(7).

Available election forms

1) US Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3(c) 'check-the-box' election on IRS Form 8832 must be filed within 75 days of the desired effective date; default pass-through treatment arises without filing. 2) Election under Article IV(7) of the Canada-US Tax Convention: the US shareholder files the prescribed ULC treaty-election statement with the CRA and delivers a copy to the ULC as withholding agent, causing the ULC to be treated as fiscally transparent so that US-resident shareholders obtain reduced Part XIII withholding and treaty benefits. 3) GST/HST voluntary registration election (Excise Tax Act s. 240(3)) allows a small supplier to register early to recover input tax credits. 4) Hybrid mismatch elections and structural elections under Income Tax Act ss. 18.3-18.5 (effective 1 July 2022) do not create a standalone 'check-the-box' for Canadian purposes; taxpayers neutralize mismatches by fixing the entity's foreign classification or contractual allocations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1), measured on worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory on the transaction that exceeds the threshold. Non-resident importers must register (or self-assess at the border) even below CAD 30,000. Green coffee beans are zero-rated as basic groceries (Schedule VI, Part III), so the effective rate on the wholesale supply and on export sales is 0%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Two independent zero-rating bases apply to the specialty green coffee bean trade: (a) domestic zero-rating as basic groceries for human consumption under ETA Schedule VI, Part III (0% GST, full ITC entitlement); and (b) export zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 5 (goods shipped by the supplier to a destination outside Canada) and s. 6 (goods delivered to a non-resident recipient for export in the course of the recipient's business). Offshore services (e.g., storage, freight management) are zero-rated under Part V, s. 7 if performed wholly outside Canada. The registrant must prove actual export by retaining contemporaneous shipping and payment evidence.

  • Customs Commercial Invoice (vendor's invoice stating exporter, consignee, container number, INCOTERM, quantity, unit price in USD/CAD, and commodity HS code — e.g., HS 0901.11 for green arabica coffee)
  • Electronic B13A Export Declaration / Canadian Export Reporting System (ERS) filing confirmation for goods valued over CAD 2,000
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air waybill) evidencing foreign destination and the shipper-of-record
  • Container seal number and Customs transit/export documentation or the ERS 'Released' acknowledgment
  • Payment and FX settlement proof: SWIFT MT103 inbound remittance credit advice, documentary Letter of Credit settlement advice, or bank transfer confirmation (the functional equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate, which Canada does not issue)
  • Warehouse/receipt records and the purchase import documentation (Form B3 or EDI/ACI e-manifest) for traceability back to the originating export

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no tax-authority e-invoice stamping, and no API integration with the CRA for transaction-level invoicing in Canada. The CRA requires records to be retained in machine-readable form (ETA s. 286; Income Tax Act s. 230), accepts EDI/X12 and Peppol (Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework) on a voluntary basis, and mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE) for registrants with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1.5 million; paper filing remains available below that threshold. The GST/HST return (GST34) must be filed and the net tax remitted within one month after the end of the reporting period (monthly or quarterly), or within three months for annual filers.

The CRA may reassess within four years from the end of the reporting period (ETA s. 298) and longer in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or waiver. If the export documents do not establish physical export, CRA re-taxes the supply at 5% GST and disallows related input tax credits with arrears interest (s. 280). Gross negligence penalty: 50% of the net tax amount; false statements/omissions (s. 285): fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 12 months on summary conviction; tax evasion (ETA s. 327): penalties up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Records must be retained for six years from the last day of the period to which they relate; failure to produce export documentation shifts the burden to the registrant and triggers immediate clawback with interest. Export zero-rating claims are scrutinized via the CRA 'Exports and Trade' verification program when bill-to/ship-to addresses differ, when the goods return to Canada, or when settlement of the export invoice is not received within 180 days of the bill of lading date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility (IMF Article VIII) and imposes no exchange-control approval for inward or outward remittances. Green coffee import payments and profits can be freely settled in CAD/USD; however, an Alberta ULC's distributions to non-resident shareholders may attract Part XIII withholding tax (reduced under the Canada-US treaty to 5 per cent for qualifying corporate shareholders and 15 per cent for interest as applicable), so equity injections are preferable during capitalization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio; receipts from any re-export of green coffee may be retained indefinitely in USD or foreign-currency accounts. Reporting obligations are limited to CBSA export declarations, FINTRAC large-transaction reporting for CAD 10,000 or more, and CRS/FATCA account disclosures; no central-bank repatriation filing is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary, lender-of-last-resort and large-value settlement authority; foreign-exchange policy is administered under the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act, while FINTRAC supervises AML/CFT and money-services-business registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. Canada has no distinct EMI licence; foreign digital EMIs operated by licensed MSBs/FMSBs registered with FINTRAC can provide low-value supplier disbursements and cross-border FX conversion, but they are not direct participant banks in Payments Canada Lynx and their deposits lack CDIC coverage. For the Alberta ULC's trade-liquidity concentration, documentary credit issuance and regulatory clearability, Schedule I bank accounts remain the only fully viable clearing core.

Late filing penalty: 5% of balance owing plus 1% per full month (up to 12 months) for returns filed after the deadline; repeated failure penalty of 10% for second offense; interest on unpaid amounts at prescribed rates (currently 8% for corporations).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services; CAD 5 million for tangible goods, intangible property, and interest transactions with non-residents (per CRA's contemporaneous documentation requirements).

Economic substance scope: No specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in Canada, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) applies to disallow transactions lacking genuine economic substance. The CRA may recharacterize transactions if the main purpose is tax avoidance. For ULCs, the unlimited liability structure may be scrutinized for treaty shopping; substance must be demonstrated through real business activities, employees, and premises in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s. 227.1) if they fail to withhold or remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment disallowing tax benefits, plus penalties of 25% of the tax benefit (s. 163.2); potential treaty override and denial of treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to the sham arrangement; personal liability for tax benefits denied.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations; criminal charges for money laundering under the Criminal Code (s. 462.31) with imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 for AML violations; travel bans and imprisonment for willful blindness.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices, impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (s. 247(3)), plus interest; double taxation risk if not corrected.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the transfer pricing mispricing; personal liability for unpaid penalties.
