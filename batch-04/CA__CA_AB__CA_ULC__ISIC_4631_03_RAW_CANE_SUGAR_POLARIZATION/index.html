Competent corporate registry

Alberta Corporate Registry, Service Alberta (Registrar of Corporations) — physical service windows in Edmonton and Calgary and the online portal at www.alberta.ca/corporate-registry, with filings made directly by the incorporation agent or through an authorised Registry Agent

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the ABCA; the ULC must issue at least one share to each subscriber, and contributed capital may be credited as fully paid upon receipt. No escrow account is required under Alberta law; the directors may maintain corporate funds in a commercial Canadian bank account pending use in sugar trade transactions.

Funding & unlock path: Before physical occupation of any Alberta office or warehouse: (i) obtain a municipal business licence and building occupancy permit; (ii) obtain fire-safety certification from the local Safety Codes Officer under the Safety Codes Act and Alberta Fire Code; (iii) obtain structural load inspection sign-off for racked palletised sugar storage under the Alberta Building Code; and (iv) complete an Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) workplace hazard assessment and clearance under the Alberta OHS Act and OHS Code prior to deploying staff.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the ABCA; the ULC must issue at least one share to each subscriber, and contributed capital may be credited as fully paid upon receipt. No escrow account is required under Alberta law; the directors may maintain corporate funds in a commercial Canadian bank account pending use in sugar trade transactions.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — no Alberta industrial manufacturing licensing board applies to wholesale physical vessel shipment of raw cane sugar (ISIC 4631 / NAICS 424490). If the ULC later operates a physical warehouse or imports sugar as food for commercial resale, the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) Safe Food for Canadians licence and a municipal business licence may become conditionally applicable.

Category: Exempt — no provincial operating or manufacturing permit is required for wholesale commodity trading under Alberta law; an optional CFIA Safe Food for Canadians Act import licence applies only if the corporation takes title to sugar at the border for subsequent resale as food.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada); Business Registration Online platform via the Canada.ca business tax registration portal for Business Number (BN) and program account activation

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must: (1) determine whether it is required or eligible to register under the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act; (2) obtain a Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 or using the CRA's Business Registration Online; (3) create the applicable GST/HST program account (RT), payroll (RP), corporation income tax (RC), or import/export (RM) accounts; (4) provide proof of non-resident status, registered address in Canada, and, where a permanent establishment may arise, appoint a representative for service of process and tax notices; (5) for ULCs, file the Alberta/BC corporate registration with the provincial registry and then register with CRA using the corporate registry number; a non-resident may register voluntarily for GST/HST even if below the mandatory threshold to recover applicable input tax credits, subject to CRA administrative policies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must: (1) determine whether it is required or eligible to register under the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act; (2) obtain a Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 or using the CRA's Business Registration Online; (3) create the applicable GST/HST program account (RT), payroll (RP), corporation income tax (RC), or import/export (RM) accounts; (4) provide proof of non-resident status, registered address in Canada, and, where a permanent establishment may arise, appoint a representative for service of process and tax notices; (5) for ULCs, file the Alberta/BC corporate registration with the provincial registry and then register with CRA using the corporate registry number; a non-resident may register voluntarily for GST/HST even if below the mandatory threshold to recover applicable input tax credits, subject to CRA administrative policies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a domestic 'check-the-box' classification election; an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) is statutorily classified as a corporation and is taxable at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act and under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act. For foreign income tax purposes, an Alberta ULC may be classified as a flow-through or disregarded entity where the foreign jurisdiction's check-the-box rules apply, creating a hybrid entity. To the extent such hybrid arrangements are used, Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act sections 18.4, 18.5, 18.6, 20.1, and related provisions) disallow deduction-mismatch outcomes and require careful structuring of the ULC's financing, dividends, and royalty flows. A ULC may also elect under relevant federal or provincial rules to treat certain intercompany balances, equity contributions, or dividends as giving rise to a deemed remission or tax-step adjustments; the specific elections must be filed with CRA within prescribed timelines and substantiated in the entity's books and records.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 per calendar quarter (small supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act section 148); a non-resident supplier must register for GST/HST if it is carrying on a commercial activity in Canada and is not a small supplier. For B2B sales of goods supplied in Canada, including stored goods delivered to Canadian buyers, registration is required once the 30-day or quarterly threshold is exceeded; for pure export sales, registration may be voluntary but is generally advisable to support zero-rating documentation and input tax credit recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of raw cane centrifugal sugar may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act if the supplier (i) sells the goods under a written contract of sale, (ii) delivers the goods by transfer of title to an address outside Canada or otherwise to a rail car, truck, vessel, or container that is leaving Canada, and (iii) maintains evidence that the goods were exported before the buyer first used them or transferred them in Canada. Offshore services directly in support of the exported goods, including handling, demurrage, and shipping logistics, can also be zero-rated when performed for a non-resident person or in respect of goods delivered outside Canada. A rebate may be claimed by the exporter under section 252 of the Excise Tax Act for tax paid on goods subsequently exported. The ULC should obtain evidence of export within a reasonable period; CRA generally expects proof within two years from the taxation year of the supply, but best practice is to secure documentation immediately.

  • CBSA Commercial Export Declaration (e.g., Export Report via CITSW, with unique reference number)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Through Bill of Lading showing the sugar shipped from a Canadian port to a foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing raw cane centrifugal sugar, polarity/quality, quantity, and vessel marks

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) audits. The CRA accepts electronic tax invoices and records if maintained in a readable, structured format. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (pilot under Open Peppol) is voluntary; businesses may send Peppol-conformant invoices for B2B and B2G transactions. Export documentation for goods physically removed from Canada must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian International Trade Single Window (CITSW) or the ACI/Connected Cargo portal, using the Commercial Export Declaration (formerly Form B13) or appropriate e-transaction reports; vessel manifest data is transmitted to CBSA.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export supply within three years from the assessment date, extended to four years for a reporting-period return, or indefinitely in cases of gross negligence, misrepresentation, or fraud. If documentation is insufficient, CRA will restore the GST at 5% and may apply penalties under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act (10% of the net tax amount, minimum CAD 8,000, plus increasing penalties for repeat failures), a 10% gross negligence penalty and CAD 100 per day for failure to file, and possible prosecution for tax evasion or customs fraud; CBSA may also impose export penalties and administrative monetary penalties under the Customs Act for false export declarations. Inward remittance or FX repatriation evidence is critical in CRA audits; while not a statutory condition of zero-rating in Canada, the absence of bank documentation evidencing received or credited consideration will defeat good-faith claims and invite a full clawback of the input tax credits and refunds, together with interest and penalties.
