Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administrator for the Business Number (BN), GST/HST and federal corporate income tax; primary portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html). Provincial corporate income tax: Government of Alberta, Ministry of Finance — Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA), portal: https://www.alberta.ca/corporate-tax.aspx. Corporate registry: Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta ULC must (1) complete Alberta incorporation or extra-provincial registration under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) and obtain a registered office; (2) request a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) with program accounts via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts'; (3) activate the GST/HST program account (RT) and the corporate income tax program account (RC), while confirming zero-rated export status; (4) file the initial Alberta corporate tax registration through the integrated federal-provincial system, with Alberta TRA referencing the same BN; (5) for non-resident directors, provide the Alberta registry filing and registered office consent. Registration is immediate upon CRA confirmation; GST/HST registration becomes effective on the date requested, generally the incorporation date.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Under Canadian domestic income tax the ULC defaults to Entity_Level_Taxation as a taxable corporation; pass-through eligibility is exercisable for foreign members via the ULC hybrid status — US Check-the-Box election and Canada-US Income Tax Convention Art. IV(7) fiscally transparent treatment). Territorial_Scope does not apply because Canada taxes worldwide business income of resident corporations with foreign tax credits.

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic 'check-the-box' election. Available mechanisms: (a) US Check-the-Box election on IRS Form 8832 to classify the Canadian ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for US federal tax; (b) treaty look-through election under Canada-US Income Tax Convention (1980, as amended) Art. IV(7), allowing US members to take the ULC's Canadian-source items into account as fiscally transparent for treaty purposes; (c) 'lazy partnership' / elective deemed-partnership arrangement under ITA s.96 used in cross-border fund and US parent structures; (d) ITA s.216(1) election for non-resident taxpayers with Canadian rental or timber income; (e) Alberta election to align provincial corporate taxable income with federal allocation; (f) GST/HST joint election under ETA s.273 for closely related corporations to simplify ITC recovery.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold under ETA s.148(1), measured on taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters (CAD 30,000 on worldwide gross revenue for non-resident registrants). Voluntary registration is strongly recommended for an exporting ULC to claim input tax credits (ITCs) on domestic operating expenses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property (dried goji berries / herbal botanicals) exported by the GST-registered Alberta ULC are zero-rated at 0% GST provided: (1) the goods are shipped 'in a continuous journey' and removed from Canada within 60 days after delivery to the exporter (or within 2 years where no consideration is payable until export); (2) legal title passes to a non-resident purchaser; (3) the ULC retains documentary evidence of export (ETA ss.9, 143; Schedule VI, Part V); (4) the supply qualifies as a sale, hire-purchase, or consignment under ETA rules; (5) ancillary logistics services supplied to non-residents and performed outside Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s.7. Health Canada/CFIA export permits may be required for herbal botanical commodities; such regulatory approval does not affect GST zero-rating qualification.

  • Commercial Invoice (English/French) with HS 1211.90 or HS 0813 classification, INCOTERMS, and ULC exporter legal name/BN
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or IATA Air Waybill evidencing continuous movement from Canada to the non-resident buyer

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or CRA API-based clearance system for GST/HST. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE where the registrant's total annual revenue exceeds CAD 1 million; all tax payments above CAD 10,000 must be remitted electronically. For B2G (federal government) contracts, suppliers must issue e-invoices through the PEPPOL network under Canada's e-Invoicing for Federal Suppliers mandate. Export invoices may be issued in standard XML/EIUS or commercial format; no fiscal clearance stamp is required for zero-rated export supplies.

CRA may audit zero-rated export claims and any unsupported export documentation results in clawback of ITCs and GST refunds with interest. Civil penalties: up to CAD 100,000 under ETA s.281.1 for false statements or misrepresentations; gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the net tax claimed under ETA s.285(1); criminal tax evasion under ITA s.239 and ETA s.327(1) carries fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years per count. Retrospective reassessment periods are normally 4 years (ETA s.298(4)) but extend to 10 years where willful misrepresentation or fraud is established. Scheme-based 'shell game' or DTAR adjustments under ETA ss.118(2.1)-(2.2) may apply where diversion of taxable activities to non-residents is found. ULCs are also subject to statutory record retention of 6 years and must produce CERS/B13, transport documents, and proof of payment to sustain zero-rating on audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts; Canada has assumed IMF Article VIII obligations. The Canadian dollar floats freely with no surrender, conversion, or pre-approval requirements. Remittances of profits to foreign shareholders are permitted without regulatory license, subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% (treaty-reduced, e.g., 5–15% for beneficial owners under an Alberta ULC structure), deemed-dividend rules for non-arm's-length advances, and applicable FINTRAC reporting. No exchange control approval is required for either equity contributions or outbound dividend flows; however, banks assess source-of-funds documentation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Canada; 0% of export proceeds must be converted to CAD. Receipts may be held indefinitely in USD, EUR, or CNH foreign-currency operating accounts maintained by a Canadian chartered bank. Statutory obligations are limited to (i) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer and large cash transaction reporting at the CAD 10,000 threshold; (ii) CBSA Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Report for physical movements equal to or above CAD 10,000; and (iii) documented export declarations and certificates under the CFIA Safe Food for Canadians Act (SFCA) for the dried goji and herbal botanical product line.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting jointly with the Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate policy; prudential and compliance competencies are vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering and cross-border transfer monitoring.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium (ancillary support only). In Canada, non-bank fintech and electronic money institutions must be registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) or foreign MSBs with FINTRAC and hold a licence under Alberta's Money Services Act; they may neither accept deposits nor extend credit under the Bank Act. For wholesale B2B invoices above CAD 25,000 and for documentary letters of credit or trade-finance leverage, digital EMIs are not an acceptable sole treasury rail; they are viable only for receivable collections below CAD 10,000 per transaction. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Medium (ancillary support only). In Canada, non-bank fintech and electronic money institutions must be registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) or foreign MSBs with FINTRAC and hold a licence under Alberta's Money Services Act; they may neither accept deposits nor extend credit under the Bank Act. For wholesale B2B invoices above CAD 25,000 and for documentary letters of credit or trade-finance leverage, digital EMIs are not an acceptable sole treasury rail; they are viable only for receivable collections below CAD 10,000 per transaction. The defensible blueprint is a hybrid structure: tier-1 Schedule I banking as the primary clearing fabric, with FINTRAC-registered EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) used only for micro-remittance, supplier onboarding, and final-mile disbursement.
