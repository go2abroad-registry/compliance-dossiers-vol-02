Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canada domestic) combined with Pass_Through_Eligible (U.S. check-the-box): the ULC is a corporation resident in Canada taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act at the federal 15% general rate plus Alberta 8% non-CCPC rate (23% combined), but its ULC share capital is treated as quasi-debt (nil paid-up capital), making ULC distributions deductible in computing income and outside Part XIII dividend withholding; for U.S. federal tax, the ULC is an eligible entity and a single-member ULC is a disregarded entity (branch/PE treatment) — a hybrid entity status subject to the BEPS hybrid rules in ITA s. 18.4–18.7 and Canada-U.S. Treaty Art. 4(7).

Available election forms

1) U.S. IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election: single-member ULC elects disregarded entity status; multi-member ULC elects partnership; ULC is not on the per se corporation list. 2) Optional Part I 'fiscally transparent entity' alignment: the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention (1980, as amended by the 5th Protocol) Art. 4(7) governs income attribution to the U.S. shareholder; treaty benefits are applied on the basis of the residency of the person taxable on the income. 3) No Canadian equivalent of check-the-box exists — the ULC cannot elect flow-through status for Canadian tax; however, the CRA's administrative treatment of ULC shares as indebtedness allows deductible distributions. 4) For hybrid mismatches, no election is available; Canadian domestic hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4–18.7, effective for fiscal years beginning after 30 June 2022) deny deductions for payments made under structured arrangements to the extent a deduction is also claimed in a foreign jurisdiction. 5) ULC paid-up capital is nil by statute, so subsection 84(1) dividend mechanics are largely inapplicable; capital distributions are treated as repayment of deemed debt.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold, ETA s. 240); ULC must register within 29 days after the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration available and advisable to recover import GST; cross-border e-commerce vendors of qualifying goods must register under the 2021 non-resident GST framework at the same CAD 30,000 threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V: (i) s. 1(a) — supply by way of sale to non-resident where the ULC exports the goods, or the goods are shipped by the ULC's common carrier outside Canada; (ii) s. 1(b) — supply to a non-resident who ships the goods to a destination outside Canada; (iii) s. 1(c) — goods delivered to a carrier for export as part of the sale. The matcha must leave Canada 'as soon as practical' and must not be consumed or diverted into the Canadian market. Wholesale exports of culinary/ceremonial matcha to U.S. or overseas buyers qualify; related offshore services (logistics, brokerage) rendered to non-residents in respect of exported goods are also zero-rated under s. 7(a) if the services relate to the exported goods and are performed in Canada. Domestic supply of unsweetened/unflavoured matcha to Canadian retailers may additionally qualify as zero-rated basic groceries (Schedule VI, Part III, s. 1(a)); any sweetened or beverage-mix variants are taxable at 5%. Export zero-rating generates a claimable input tax credit position (refund) on the GST/HST return.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed electronically in the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) within 15 days of export, bearing the 9-digit BN and the CERS E-number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or Cargo Control Document for truck/rail) naming a destination outside Canada and showing the ULC as exporter/shipper
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List referencing the E-number, BNs, harmonized code (HS 0902.10 for green tea/matcha), and the non-resident purchaser
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank wire confirmation (Swift/TT) evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in a Canadian financial institution from the overseas buyer
  • Non-Resident Purchase Order and counterparty onboarding file (KYC/AML standard checks, OFAC/UN sanctions screening) evidencing the foreign arm's-length purchaser

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory digital stamping, and no API-based e-invoicing clearance. Mandatory electronic filing (GST/HST NETFILE or EFile) applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies of CAD 1.5 million or more; GST/HST payments of CAD 10,000 or more must be remitted electronically. Voluntary PAN-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (PEPPOL-based) endorsed by the federal government in 2024; CRA is piloting structured e-invoice data usage, and registrants should retain invoice-level data in a format compatible with future e-invoicing adoption.

CRA can reassess a zero-rated export as taxable within the normal 4-year limitation period (ETA s. 298(1)), and without time limit where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud arises (ETA s. 298(2)), clawing back 5% GST plus compounding daily interest at the CRA prescribed rate (approximately 8–9% per annum in 2024–2025). Where zero-rating is denied because the documentation bundle is incomplete, the ULC loses its input tax credit refund and is assessed for the unremitted GST on the full invoice value. Civil penalty under ETA s. 285 for false statements or omissions knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence: greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; repeated or intentional abuse can trigger criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 (tax evasion) with fines and imprisonment. CRA's export audit protocol requires the ULC to retain every document in the export bundle for six years (ETA s. 286(2)) and to corroborate the physical movement of goods with carrier waybills and CBSA exit confirmation; bank-instrument and bill-and-hold structures without third-party carrier evidence are treated as domestic supplies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible without Central Bank notification or licensing. There are no de facto allocation windows, queue systems, black-market premiums, or exit tax on capital remittance. The only material frictions are: (i) treaty-based withholding taxes under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention (Art. X: 5% on dividends to a US corporate parent owning at least 10% of voting shares; otherwise 25%); (ii) thin capitalization limits for interest paid to specified non-resident shareholders (debt-to-equity cap of 1.5:1); and (iii) GST/HST application on imported matcha, which is recoverable as an input tax credit for the ULC when registered for GST/HST.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender exists in Canada: export proceeds and any matcha re-sale receipts in USD, JPY, or other currencies may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts. There is no mandatory conversion timeline, no exporter-clearance certification, and no Central Bank repatriation report. However, two operational thresholds apply: (i) every international wire (inbound or outbound) of CAD 10,000 or more is reported by the bank to FINTRAC as an Electronic Funds Transfer report; and (ii) any physical export shipment valued above CAD 2,000 must be declared through the Canada Border Services Agency's Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) - matcha powder is not a controlled good, but a valid CBSA Business Number and customs broker authorization are required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole central bank and monetary authority; it administers no exchange-control approval regime. Transaction-level AML supervision is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), while prudential conduct of Deposit-Taking Institutions is supervised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Payments Canada operates the Lynx high-value RTGS system used for CAD wholesale clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary payment rail; Low as a sole treasury platform. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Ebury) can legally debit a Canadian ULC's account for JPY vendor payouts, but they are not authorized to provide documentary letters of credit, banker's acceptances, or formal trade guarantees. Real-name use requires the EMI to be registered as a money services business with FINTRAC (pre-Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act), and, where participating in the Retail Payment Activities Act registration regime, to hold an operational license issued by the Bank of Canada. The statutory constraint is that no substitute clearing of CAD via Lynx is possible for such EMIs; all CAD settlement must be executed through a Schedule I chartered bank. The blueprint therefore confines EMI rails to supplier disbursements under USD 25,000; all procurement over that amount, and the capital escrow mandate, remains at Tier 1 banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The US parent executes a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the ULC's CAD operational or blocked Capital Escrow account at the Tier 1 Canadian bank. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION / ULC EQUITY INJECTION'; benefit account number and ULC legal name must match the corporate registry record. The Canadian bank reports the transfer to FINTRAC as an EFT if it equals or exceeds CAD 10,000, and records the transaction under the client's Balance-of-Payments reporting profile; no Central Bank approval is needed. Under the Investment Canada Act, establishment of a new Canadian business by a WTO investor requires a notification to Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) only if the enterprise value threshold is surpassed (CAD 1.9+ billion for 2025) - a USD 500,000 matcha-trading ULC is far below the threshold, though a voluntary 'New Business Notification' is advisable if a longer acquisition trajectory or real-estate footprint is contemplated. GST/HST does not apply to an equity subscription; shareholder loans (if used instead of equity) are exempt financial supplies. The funds are released from the escrow/blocked ledger upon completion of the Alberta share issuance and filing of the updated Shareholder Register, with an ordinary board resolution attached.Matcha procurement payments to Japanese suppliers flow over three clearing rails. (i) Primary: SWIFT MT103 gpi telegraphic transfers in JPY from the ULC's CAD/USD account via the Canadian bank's FX desk, with the JPY settled over the BOJ-NET system; all MT103 messages carry a structured remittance reference matching the invoice number, ensuring 'straight-through processing' into the Japanese supplier's account at MUFG Bank or Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation. (ii) Documentary: for initial or high-value procurement (aggregate profile above USD 50,000 per shipment), the bank issues an irrevocable, unconfirmed import Letter of Credit under UCP600, advised through a Japanese confirming bank, payable at sight in JPY against presentation of the commercial invoice, packing list, phytosanitary/certificate of analysis, and bill of lading; this L/C instrument protects the ULC while building supplier trust. (iii) Fallback Settlement: CAD wires to the supplier's Canada-based proxy account (if the Japanese seller maintains a Toronto or Vancouver concentration account) are permitted but disfavored due to FX spread. All EFTs of CAD 10,000 or above are subject to mandatory FINTRAC reporting, and every commercial import is supported by a CBSA entry (Form B3-3) with the ULC's Importer Business Number, so that the trade finance and customs records reconcile arithmetically.Outright FX Forwards (CAD/JPY and USD/JPY): vanilla deliverable forwards up to 12 months, requiring an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex with the Tier 1 dealer bank; fully permissible for commercial hedge of matcha purchases, no Bank of Canada consent requiredFX Swaps (Spot-Forward combinations): permitted for rolling supplier payment dates and for matching the US parent's USD injection against the ULC's JPY disbursement calendarCross-Currency Swaps (USD/CAD and CAD/JPY): permissible for the US parent-to-ULC intercompany loan conversion, converting USD shareholder advances into fixed-CAD obligations and eliminating remittance basis mismatch over multi-year trade cyclesNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDF): legally permissible in Canada but operationally unnecessary for CAD/JPY - both currencies are fully deliverable; an NDF would raise credit costs and is recommended only for exotic third-currency transits (e.g., settlement via a HK or EUR clearing hub)

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium as a supplementary payment rail; Low as a sole treasury platform. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Ebury) can legally debit a Canadian ULC's account for JPY vendor payouts, but they are not authorized to provide documentary letters of credit, banker's acceptances, or formal trade guarantees. Real-name use requires the EMI to be registered as a money services business with FINTRAC (pre-Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act), and, where participating in the Retail Payment Activities Act registration regime, to hold an operational license issued by the Bank of Canada. The statutory constraint is that no substitute clearing of CAD via Lynx is possible for such EMIs; all CAD settlement must be executed through a Schedule I chartered bank. The blueprint therefore confines EMI rails to supplier disbursements under USD 25,000; all procurement over that amount, and the capital escrow mandate, remains at Tier 1 banks.
