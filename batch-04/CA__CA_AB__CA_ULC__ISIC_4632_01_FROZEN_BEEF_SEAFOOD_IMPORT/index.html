Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/my-business-account.html)

E-government filing portal

For an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC), after Alberta incorporation is complete, the company must register for a federal Business Number (BN) with the CRA. This is achieved by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or by applying online through CRA's Register online for a business number service. The BN becomes the base for a separate GST/HST account (RT program), which the importer must open for GST/HST reporting. Non-resident entities without Canadian incorporation can use a representative and file Form RC1 with supporting foreign registration documents; the CRA will assign a BN and GST/HST account number, subject to proof of foreign business identity and, where applicable, a Canadian representative agreement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a 'check-the-box' election analogous to the United States. For federal income tax, an Alberta ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act. The ULC may be treated as fiscally transparent for certain foreign tax purposes (e.g., under the Canada-US tax treaty for US federal tax), but such foreign classification does not alter Canadian domestic taxation. No hybrid mismatch or partnership elections are available to convert the ULC's Canadian tax character; Canadian tax law applies the same entity-level treatment irrespective of the unlimited liability attribute.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) made in Canada; however, any person importing goods into Canada for commercial purposes may be required to register to account for GST on imported goods even if below the threshold, and voluntary registration is advisable to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating on exports is available when the supplier transfers ownership of goods and delivers them to a foreign address, or delivers them to a common carrier or exporter for immediate export; the supply must be substantiated as an export under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act. Services supplied to non-residents are generally zero-rated when the service is not rendered in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. For an importer re-exporting frozen beef or seafood, the initial import will be taxable under GST Division III, and the subsequent export supply can be zero-rated upon provision of adequate export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) electronic declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical export and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer evidence showing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and marine insurance certificate
  • Transaction and payment records (SWIFT MT103 or letter of credit drawdown documentation)

Canada does not currently operate a national real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-based clearance system for GST/HST invoices. Registered businesses must issue invoices containing prescribed information under section 223 of the Excise Tax Act. Registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1 million are required to file GST/HST returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or an XML production system through CRA approved software. Standard reciprocal exchange of commercial e-invoices is permitted but not mandated.

CRA may reassess within four years, or seven years in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. False zero-rating claims are subject to penalties under subsection 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act, gross negligence penalties equal to 50% of the net tax, and mandatory interest. Fraudulently claimed refunds can be wholly clawed back, together with monetary penalties and potential prosecution in tax court or under the Criminal Code. Businesses must preserve export evidence for six years and maintain a complete audit trail of both the import GST payment and the subsequent export sale to sustain the zero-rated treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully floating exchange-rate regime with unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility, consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely deliverable both onshore and offshore; there is no exchange-licensing authority, quota system, or central-bank approval for capital movements. Profit remittance from an Alberta ULC to non-resident shareholders is subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory rate, reducible under an applicable income tax treaty), with no convertibility or repatriation permit required. Material compliance hurdles are limited to (i) the Investment Canada Act notification and national-security review for establishment of a new Canadian business by a non-Canadian, (ii) FINTRAC reporting of electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more and enhanced beneficial-ownership disclosure, and (iii) anti-hybrid-mismatch and GAAR exposure if the ULC's pass-through election is used to create a deductible/disregarded mismatch in cross-border structures

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished statutory exchange controls in 1951 and the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) imposes no obligation to surrender, convert, or repatriate export or trading proceeds. A Canadian entity may hold, spend, or lend received foreign currency without restriction. The only operational duties are statistical: Statistics Canada's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) collects balance-of-payments data on direct-investment and trade transactions, and FINTRAC requires an electronic funds transfer report for every wire of C$10,000 or more within a 24-hour window

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank regulation, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering surveillance, and Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) for Investment Canada Act administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for use as a primary treasury or escrow rail; MEDIUM for use as a limited operational payment channel only. Legal constraints: a non-resident electronic money institution cannot accept deposits in Canada because the Bank Act reserves the deposit-taking function for Schedule I/II banks and authorized federal credit unions; foreign money-service businesses (MSBs) must either be registered with FINTRAC as a foreign MSB or operate through a FINTRAC-registered Canadian agent; access to the Lynx RTGS and retail payment clearing infrastructures is limited to Payments Canada members; and the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime will further condition the lawfulness of PSP services. Cross-border EMIs such as Wise Platform or Airwallex may support low-value supplier disbursements but cannot lawfully serve as the escrow clearing hub for large frozen-commodity import settlements or replace a regulated Canadian bank for documentary credit operations

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from non-resident shareholders is routed through a designated CAD/USD capital escrow account held at a Schedule I OSFI-regulated bank. The injection must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'Equity Contribution / Share Capital Subscription' and reference to the ULC subscriber resolution, using the ISO 20022 remittance field to state 'ISIC 4632 - Wholesale Import of Frozen Meat and Pelagic Fish'. Prior to credit, the bank verifies the Investment Canada Act status: the ULC must file the Notification of Acquisition of Control or Establishment of a New Canadian Business with ISED within 30 days of implementation, and a pre-clearance national-security review may be required regardless of threshold. For sums above US$100,000, the bank will require the full KYC/KYB package and source-of-funds evidence. FINTRAC travel-rule data (originator name, address, and account) must be transmitted on every wire; all EFTs of C$10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC within the statutory filing window. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for controlled transactions with any non-resident where the aggregate gross value of controlled transactions exceeds CAD 5,000,000 in a taxation year; Master File and Local File required for Canadian members of MNE groups with consolidated group revenue equal to or exceeding CAD 800,000,000; Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) filed at the same revenue threshold; penalty trigger where a transfer pricing adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10.00% of gross revenue.

Economic substance scope: No named Economic Substance Regulation exists in Canadian federal or Alberta law, but economic substance is statutorily enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245 of the Income Tax Act), which disallows avoidance transactions lacking a bona fide business purpose, and through the Canada-US treaty Limitation-on-Benefits article requiring substantive business activity, public-listing status, or 90% qualified-ownership plus a 50% base-erosion test; the ULC remains a taxable Canadian corporation that cannot accept hybrid-status deductions in Canada; treaty benefits are denied where the entity is a conduit, shell, or lacks real economic capacity, premises, personnel, and cold-chain trading substance in Alberta.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing (Section 239 Income Tax Act)

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction, a fine of not less than 50.00% and not more than 200.00% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, the same fine plus imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty under section 163(2) of 50.00% of the understated tax; revocation of business licences and securities of the frozen-food import entry under the Customs Act.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable under section 227.1 for unremitted Part XIII withholding tax and unpaid corporate tax where due diligence is not exercisable; CRA may issue a section 226 departure-restriction notice requiring security before the director leaves Canada; criminal conviction triggers travel restrictions, enforcement of restitution orders, and seizure of personal and corporate assets.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Sham Import Invoicing

Statutory sanction: 10.00% transfer pricing penalty on the net adjustment under section 247(3) where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10.00% of gross revenue; reassessment of arm's-length profits; interest accrual from the original filing deadline; secondary T106 late-filing penalties up to CAD 24,000 per form; Customs valuation penalties for fraudulent invoices under the Customs Act.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII amounts and penalties where they authorized or acquiesced in the fraudulent invoicing; criminal charges can attach to signing officers; CRA enforcement includes garnishment, liens, and section 226 departure interdiction for tax debts.
Shell Company / Treaty-Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial of all tax benefits, including dividend withholding relief and losses; 15.00% GAAR penalty where the CRA applies the avoidance-transaction penalty provisions of the Income Tax Act; denial of Canada-US treaty benefits under the Limitation-on-Benefits Article, creating exposure to the full 25.00% Part XIII withholding rate; Alberta corporate registry dissolution and loss of ULC capacity.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unremitted withholding tax and for corporate tax where funds have been distributed before CRA assessment; civil restitution orders in favour of the Crown; travel bans are available in respect of unpaid assessments where the individual is scheduled to depart Canada; foreign criminal assistance mechanisms facilitate extradition in aggravated fraud cases.
AML / Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for corporate violators and CAD 100,000 for individuals; suspension of import licences; seizure of undeclared monetary instruments under the Customs Act; criminal money-laundering penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; freezing orders and forfeiture of corruptly acquired cold-chain assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers personally subject to regulatory enforcement for failure to report suspicious transactions; personal liability for large-cash-transaction penalties where the director exercised control; conviction triggers imprisonment, post-conviction travel restrictions, disgorgement of profits, and permanent disqualification from federally regulated insured activities.
