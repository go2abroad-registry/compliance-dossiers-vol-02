Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Alberta ULC; no escrow account is required by the Business Corporations Act (Alberta). Subscribers may pay capital into the corporation's operational bank account upon issuance, and any escrow arrangement will only be imposed by contract or as a condition of a licensed alcohol wholesaler bond facility.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via the My Business Account portal; customs and excise administration through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA); corporate income tax for Alberta is collected federally under the Canada–Alberta tax collection agreement; provincial alcohol licensing and bonded distribution oversight rests with the Alberta Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis (AGLC).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta ULC: (1) incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act and obtain Alberta corporate registration documents and access number; (2) register the ULC for a CRA Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online or Form RC1/RC1A for non-resident entities, opening a corporate income tax account, GST/HST account, and import/export account as applicable; (3) apply to AGLC for the required wine/spirits license and bonded warehouse or bonded transfer authorization; (4) obtain CBSA import/export program accounts for customs transactions; and (5) maintain a complete administrative file combining Alberta corporate records, CRA BN, AGLC license, and CBSA authorization for audit readiness.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under U.S. federal tax rules, the Alberta ULC may file IRS Form 8832 to make a check-the-box election, permitting classification as a corporation, partnership, or disregarded entity for U.S. purposes; absent the election, the ULC defaults to corporation classification. For Canadian Income Tax Act purposes no domestic check-the-box election exists, so the ULC remains a taxable corporation. Available related elections include the GST/HST closely related corporation election under section 211.1 of the Excise Tax Act, a non-resident s.216 election for Canadian rental income, periodic non-resident withholding elections on corporate distributions, and Canada–U.S. treaty tie-breaker/residency relief mechanisms. Hybrid mismatch anti-abuse rules under the Income Tax Act may recharacterize deductible payments where the ULC's U.S. check-the-box treatment creates a hybrid outcome.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For GST/HST, the small-supplier threshold is CAD 30,000 in annual taxable supplies made in Canada; non-residents making taxable supplies in the course of carrying on business in Canada are generally subject to the same threshold, with mandatory registration for persons making taxable supplies through a Canadian permanent establishment or where taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000. No threshold applies for excise duty accounts, CBSA import/export program accounts, or AGLC liquor licensing, which are required regardless of revenue once bonded wine/spirits trading is conducted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating is available for supplies of goods exported from Canada and for services performed on exported movable property under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Wine and spirits stored in a CBSA excise bonded warehouse qualify for zero-rating when removed for export and substantiated by contemporaneous shipping and payment records. Excise duty is not payable on alcohol released solely for export where the removal is made under customs control; duty-paid wine/spirits later exported can qualify for duty remission or refund under the Excise Act, 2001. The exporter must retain complete proof of export, foreign destination, ownership transfer, and actual receipt of foreign proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / truck waybill showing delivery to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and bonded warehouse removal/transfer document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice or SWIFT MT103 confirming foreign payment
  • AGLC or CBSA bonded warehouse authorization and excise removal declaration

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping system comparable to Latin American VAT models. Registered suppliers must issue tax invoices showing the supplier's BN, invoice date, description, consideration, and GST charged, and GST/HST returns must be filed electronically once a person is registered. Customs clearance and export reporting use CBSA electronic data interchange, including eManifest and the Canadian Export Declaration system, and bonded wine/spirits removals are tracked through CBSA excise bonded warehouse records. AGLC may require electronic reporting of sales and volumes for duty/markup reconciliation, but there is no continuous transaction-control clearance mandate.

CRA may reassess GST/HST zero-rating within four years for ordinary assessments and without limitation for misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default. If export substantiation fails, the zero-rated treatment is reversed, 5% GST is assessed on the sale value, and the taxpayer is liable for unpaid tax, compounded daily arrears interest, and penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including up to 25% of the amount sought for false statements or omissions. CRA's Export Suspect Screening and CBSA verification programs apply heightened scrutiny to bonded alcohol exporters with missing or inconsistent documentation. For excise duty, fraudulent export claims can trigger administrative penalties, seizure of goods, criminal prosecution, and, for GST/HST offences, fines up to CAD 100,000 on summary conviction or up to CAD 1,000,000 on indictment plus imprisonment. Alcohol licence and bonded status can be suspended or revoked by AGLC/CBSA for non-compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations under the IMF and maintains full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Profit repatriation is permitted without exchange control approval, subject to withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act and reporting thresholds under the Currency Exchange Reporting Act. Bonded trade revenues in CAD/USD/EUR may be converted freely; no central bank authorization is required for cross-border transfers related to trade or equity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirements for export receipts in Canada. Export proceeds may be held in foreign currency accounts, reinvested, or remitted freely. However, customs declarations and Canadian Export Reporting System filings must be accurate, and transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 require FINTRAC reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (not a direct exchange control authority); federal banking supervision is conducted by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money laundering oversight by FINTRAC. Canada maintains no generalized foreign exchange approval regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary collection/disbursement, but not as a substitute for a tier-1 corporate bank account. Canadian non-bank EMI / payment service providers are regulated under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which imposes registration and risk-management obligations. However, such FIs generally cannot hold inherited or mandatory escrow funds and may not offer commercial credit or LC facilities; therefore unlicensed offshore EMIs are not viable for bonded liquor inventory settlements or statutory capital verification.
