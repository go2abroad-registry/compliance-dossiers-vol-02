Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has no general exchange controls and the Canadian dollar is generally freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. A Canadian corporation may generally receive, hold, convert, and remit foreign currency, dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, loan proceeds, and repayment amounts, subject to corporate-law solvency requirements, applicable withholding tax, transfer-pricing rules, thin-capitalization rules, beneficial-ownership verification, sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, lender covenants, and documented corporate purpose. Canada does not generally require prior central-bank approval for foreign investment, capital contributions, intercompany loans, or profit repatriation. Dividend declarations by an Alberta unlimited liability corporation must comply with applicable corporate-law solvency tests and the entity's governing documents. The ULC is not automatically fiscally transparent for Canadian tax purposes; Canadian tax classification and any foreign tax treatment must be separately confirmed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, convert export receipts into Canadian dollars, or convert a prescribed percentage of export proceeds. No general statutory conversion deadline applies. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign-currency accounts, subject to contractual banking terms and accounting, tax, customs, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering obligations. Export transactions must be supported by commercial records, customs documentation, invoices, shipping records, and proof of delivery where relevant. Foreign-currency balances and transactions must be recorded under applicable Canadian accounting and tax rules. Banks may request transaction-purpose, beneficiary, source-of-funds, and trade-document evidence and may impose internal risk-based controls.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. The Department of Finance Canada administers federal financial-sector policy, while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing compliance. The Canada Border Services Agency, Canada Revenue Agency, and provincial liquor authority regulate relevant trade, tax, excise, and alcohol activities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment initiation, foreign-exchange execution, expense management, and collection support; low as a substitute for a full Canadian operating bank. An offshore electronic-money institution generally cannot accept deposits or conduct deposit-taking in Canada without the required Canadian authorization. A foreign payment service provider may serve the corporation cross-border subject to its home-jurisdiction authorization, Canadian payment-services requirements where applicable, contractual corridor restrictions, sanctions screening, settlement-bank controls, and FINTRAC registration obligations if it conducts regulated services in Canada. Canadian payment service providers may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act and Bank of Canada supervision when the statutory regime applies. Offshore EMI balances may lack Canadian deposit insurance, Canadian creditor protection, and reliable access to domestic clearing. Use a Canadian bank for payroll, tax, liquor-related payments, recurring local debits, and primary liquidity; use an EMI only after confirming licensing, safeguarding, settlement, data, insolvency, and account-closure protections.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined effective headline corporate income tax rate on active business income (15% federal Part I general rate plus 8% Alberta general corporate rate).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, subject to reduction under an applicable tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for the filing of the T106 return; a full contemporaneous transfer-pricing report is expected whenever related-party cross-border transactions occur, and CRA routinely requests it even below that threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate Economic Substance Regulations, but the CRA applies the GAAR and substance-over-form principles. The treaty entitlement of a ULC is ring-fenced by the OECD principal purpose test; an Alberta ULC that lacks employees, premises, inventory management and commercial planning will be recharacterised as a shell and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the understated tax under ITA s. 163(2), plus compounded daily interest; criminal liability under ITA s. 239(1) carries a fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: A director or officer who directs or participates in evasion is personally exposed to criminal fines, imprisonment, and travel restrictions; civilly, CRA may apply s. 160 to transfers of assets to non-arm's-length parties.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR applies to disregard the arrangement, reallocate income, deny treaty benefits and impose normal tax at 23% combined, with late-payment interest and penalties; administrative penalty exposure up to 50% on gross misstatement.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors may be jointly assessed for unremitted Part XIII withholding under s. 227.1 if the artificial payments were made out of the Canadian entity; they may be ordered to make restitution in CRA civil proceedings.
Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC penalties: up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations per failure; conviction for money laundering under the Criminal Code carries imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be charged as parties to the offence where they actively set up or oversee compensatory anti-money-laundering bypass mechanisms; the Crown may seek forfeiture of corporate assets.
Transfer Pricing Gross Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s. 247(3), a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the transfer-pricing adjustment is imposed; the underlying corporate tax is reduced or eliminated only if the adjustment is reversed; no offsetting deduction is available.

Director personal liability: Directors signing inaccurate T106 certificates or knowingly approving false intercompany pricing are not personally liable for the corporation's tax debt under s. 227.1 absent s. 160 in rem arrangements, but they face criminal exposure for fraudulent tax returns and professional disciplinary action.
