Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for an Alberta ULC. Shares may be issued with no par value or with a nominal par value as stated in the articles. No escrow account or government capital reserve is required for a general commodity-trading ULC; subscription consideration must be paid into the corporate bank account and reflected in the share register.

Funding & unlock path: Occupancy and fire safety certificate issued by the local fire authority or municipal code enforcement under the Alberta Fire Code; structural load clearance for warehouses storing bagged milk powder; registration with the Workers' Compensation Board Alberta (WCB) and compliance with the Occupational Health and Safety Act and Occupational Health and Safety Code for workplace safety.

Export and customs authority: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-resident shareholder of an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) must obtain a federal Canadian Business Number (BN) through the CRA Business Registration Online service or by filing Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts. The ULC must first be incorporated or extra-provincially registered in Alberta through the Alberta Corporate Registry and must maintain a valid corporate registry number. Useful program accounts for cross-border dairy commodity trade include the GST/HST account (program code RT) and the import/export account (program code RM). Non-resident entities without a physical Canadian presence must appoint a Canadian agent or provide a Canadian address for service, and may be required to file a non-resident GST/HST registration. Supporting documents include the certificate of incorporation or extra-provincial registration, director/officer identification, and any applicable dairy import/export licences.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian Income Tax Act); Pass_Through_Eligible (for U.S. federal check-the-box classification of an Alberta ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership where all members have unlimited liability).

Available election forms

No domestic Canadian check-the-box election exists for an Alberta ULC; it remains a corporation for Canadian income tax and GST/HST purposes. U.S. members may file IRS Form 8832 (Entity Classification Election) to elect pass-through or disregarded status under Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3. An Alberta ULC may also engage hybrid-mismatch considerations: Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules under Income Tax Act subsections 18.4, 18.5, and 18.6 can deny deductions where payments are subject to different tax characterization in Canada and another jurisdiction. For GST/HST purposes, voluntary registration is available under Excise Tax Act subsection 240(3).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in or from Canada over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters, computed on worldwide taxable revenues before expenses; mandatory registration applies to non-residents who make taxable supplies in Canada and are not small suppliers, as well as to certain non-residents supplying digital services or physical goods into Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of raw milk powder exported from Canada qualify for zero-rating under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier sells commercial goods and delivers them outside Canada or exports them in accordance with the prescribed export provisions, with documentary evidence proving the goods left Canada. Related services, such as freight, insurance, and services performed directly on exported goods, may also be zero-rated. A GST/HST registered exporter must retain records sufficient to identify the export transaction, including shipping documents and customs declarations, within the normal CRA six-year record retention period.

  • Canada customs export declaration (CERS/Electronic Export Report) where mandatory; Form B13A for non-CERS filers
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, rail, or truck) or Cargo Control Document evidencing export from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract for the raw milk powder
  • Certificate of origin and/or CFIA export certification for dairy commodities where required by the destination country or Canada
  • Bank inward remittance certificate / SWIFT wire or TT credit document confirming foreign currency settlement
  • CBSA export transaction number or customs broker acknowledgement

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. GST/HST invoices must contain the prescribed disclosures (seller name, Business Number, GST/HST registration number, invoice date, description, and tax payable). CRA accepts electronic invoices and automated EDI/XML exchange. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants using GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST DEPOSIT. Customs export reporting is electronic: exporters must submit export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) and CARM within 15 days after goods are exported where reporting is required.

CRA may disallow zero-rating on audit, reassess GST/HST at 5% on the full export consideration, charge daily interest, and impose penalties for false statements or omissions under Excise Tax Act section 285.1 (greater of C$250 and 25% of the net tax) and for failure to collect or remit under section 280. Repeated or grossly negligent export claims can attract criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code, with fines up to C$500,000 and imprisonment. CBSA may also assess administrative monetary penalties (AMPS) of up to C$25,000 per infraction for export reporting non-compliance or misrepresentations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange controls restricting inbound or outbound wire transfers, trade settlements, capital injections, or profit remittances for a dairy commodity trading company. Profit remittance hurdles are exclusively tax-related: dividends, interest, royalties, and certain fees paid by the Canadian ULC to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (generally 25%), subject to treaty reduction where the beneficial owner qualifies. For an Alberta ULC with a U.S. parent and hybrid classification, treaty-based withholding relief may be challenged by limitation-on-benefits and BEPS hybrid mismatch rules; however, no FX permit or exchange-control approval is required. Alberta imposes no provincial currency restriction or remittance approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: not applicable. Canada has no mandatory surrender timeline for export receipts. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. Export proceeds from whole and skimmed milk powder sales may be retained in foreign currency or converted to CAD at the applicable market rate. Banks require underlying transaction documentation under AML/CFT obligations, and cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC by the bank. Export declarations under the Customs Act may be required for statistical and trade compliance, but these do not create FX surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange administration. Exchange controls are not exercised by any governmental authority; AML/CFT supervision is performed by FINTRAC and prudential bank supervision by OSFI.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Foreign digital EMIs and non-resident payment service providers are not authorized to take Canadian deposits or issue documentary letters of credit. They may operate as money services businesses registered with FINTRAC and can support ancillary foreign-currency payments and collection, but they cannot replace a Canadian Schedule I bank for commercial LC issuance, trade-document handling, or a capital escrow account. Legal constraints require that any EMI activity with Canadian clients comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and provincial money services business requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or shareholder to the Alberta ULC operating account at the Canadian bank. The remittance field should clearly specify the purpose, for example 'Share subscription CA_ULC / ISIC 4633'. Canada does not prescribe local purpose codes, but a precise SWIFT Field 70 description avoids recharacterization. For any cross-border EFT of CAD 10,000 or more, the Canadian bank must report to FINTRAC. The bank may request a source-of-funds package including board resolutions, executed share subscription or loan agreements, capitalization table, and source account statements. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% (15% Federal general corporate income tax + 8% Alberta provincial corporate income tax); the 11% small business rate is unavailable because the ULC is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (ITA) for dividends paid by a Canadian-resident ULC to a non-resident shareholder. Under the Canada-US Tax Treaty (Article X): 5% where the beneficial owner is a US corporation that owns at least 10% of the voting stock of the ULC, and 15% for all other US-resident beneficial owners.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

File within 6 months of the fiscal year-end (e.g., a December 31 year-end must be filed by June 30 of the following year). Tax balance is due 2 months after the fiscal year-end for a non-CCPC ULC (3 months for a CCPC). Quarterly instalments are required when estimated federal-provincial tax exceeds CAD 3,000.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 12 months after the fiscal year-end for T1134 (6 months for T1135 alongside the T2).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 reporting threshold: CAD 1,000,000 of aggregate cross-border non-arm's-length transactions per related non-resident in a taxation year. Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is required for all non-arm's-length cross-border transactions regardless of amount; the 10% transfer-pricing penalty under subsection 247(3) applies only where the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue. OECD-aligned Local File and Master File documentation should be prepared when the entity is within an MNE group meeting the country-by-country (CbC) reporting threshold of EUR 750,000,000 consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute; substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule (section 245 ITA), the transfer-pricing rules (section 247 ITA), anti-conduit financing rules, and the treaty limitation-on-benefits clause. The CRA will disregard an Alberta ULC that lacks real employees, premises, risk-bearing capacity, and board-level control in Canada, re-characterizing it as a mere conduit and denying treaty-based withholding reductions plus applying GAAR penalties.
