Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), head office Ottawa; self-service portals: CRA My Business Account, Business Registration Online, GST/HST NETFILE, and Represent a Client. Albertan corporate income tax is collected by CRA on behalf of Alberta (Alberta Corporate Tax Act) through the federal T2 system.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation for Canadian tax purposes: the ULC is a resident corporation (domestic law status under ABCA) filing T2 corporate returns, subject to Part I federal tax (15% general) plus Alberta corporate tax (8%), for a combined 23% general rate; the small business deduction is unavailable because non-resident control prevents CCPC status. Pass_Through_Eligible for U.S. federal tax purposes only: single-member ULC may elect disregarded-entity (check-the-box) treatment via IRS Form 8832, with the U.S. shareholder currently including Canadian active income. Territorial_Scope: worldwide income taxation with foreign tax credits (no territorial exemption). Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable.

Available election forms

There is no Canadian check-the-box: the ULC cannot elect transparent treatment in Canada. Available elections: (a) U.S. Form 8832 check-the-box election (DRE vs. corporation); (b) hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4-18.6, effective for payments after June 30, 2022) may recharacterize share-based or interest payments as deemed dividends or deny deductions for ULC structures; no election avoids this where the foreign holder is tax-transparent; (c) GST/HST s. 156 election to treat closely related corporations as a single entity; (d) GST/HST small-supplier and zero-rating elections (Form GST166 if claiming exemptions); (e) standard Canadian elections on the T2 (e.g., consolidated capital dividend account is N/A; section 219(1) Part I election for non-resident corporations not applicable to an Alberta ULC).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD small-supplier threshold (worldwide and Canadian taxable supplies; where non-resident, Canadian-source supplies including sale of goods delivered in Canada by the vendor are counted). Registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration available below the threshold. B2B apparel wholesale supplies of exported goods are zero-rated and do not erode the threshold; supplies to Canadian retailers count at 5%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment arises under Excise Tax Act s. 22 (tangible personal property exported by the supplier in the course of commercial activity), s. 23 and Schedule VI, Part V for supplies to non-residents. For off-price wholesale liquidation: (1) sales of finished garments physically shipped outside Canada qualify at 0% if the vendor retains proof of export; (2) supplies to non-resident buyers of goods destined for delivery outside Canada qualify if the goods are not consumed in Canada; (3) wholesale services (e.g., warehousing, inspection, liquidation agency) performed for non-residents qualify under s. 7 of Schedule VI unless the services relate to Canadian real estate or goods consumed in Canada. GST payable on any local Canadian retail sales would be 5% (Alberta), recoverable by registered business purchasers as input tax credits; export-related GST refunds (net remittances) are claimed on the GST/HST return.

  • CBSA export documentation: Canadian Export Declaration (CED) filed via Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for goods requiring export reporting (e.g., controlled goods or shipments above prescribed thresholds); ACI/eManifest data, commercial invoice and packing list.
  • Transport evidence: original or electronic Bill of Lading (ocean/air/truck/rail), Cargo Control Document (CCD), waybill, or carrier certificate confirming outbound movement of the finished garments to the non-resident buyer.
  • Consideration and FX evidence: inward remittance certificate-equivalent (SWIFT MT103 confirmation, bank credit advice, or foreign exchange record), underlying purchase order/contract, and (if applicable) CUSMA/certificate of origin documents corroborating the export sale.

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or government digital stamping of invoices. E-invoicing is voluntary (Pan-Canadian Peppol/PINT framework being phased in). GST/HST returns are subject to mandatory NETFILE e-filing for large filers; CRA accepts EDI (EDIFACT/X12) and PDF/paper for commercial invoices. Tax records must be kept in Canada or be accessible electronically for CRA inspection (6-year retention rule).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a clean floating exchange-rate regime with full current-account and capital-account convertibility consistent with the Currency Act and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. There are no exchange controls, no capital-export permits, no profit-remittance gatekeeping, and no restriction on dividend, interest, or capital outflows to non-resident shareholders of an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC). The only operational convertibility hurdles are transactional: FINTRAC/monitoring of every international electronic funds transfer at or above CAD 10,000, CRS/FATCA self-certification on the shareholder, and the bank's internal source-of-funds diligence. A ULC electing pass-through eligibility does not alter convertibility; it only shifts the point of Canadian tax reporting for non-resident owners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no requirement to credit export earnings to a domestic account. An Alberta ULC may lawfully retain USD, EUR, CNY, or other export receipts in offshore or onshore foreign-currency clearing accounts and may net trade receivables within intercompany structures, subject to transfer-pricing documentation and FINTRAC reporting of any inbound transfer of CAD 10,000 or more. Export Development Canada (EDC) provides credit insurance and guarantees but performs no FX-surrender administration.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and settlement agent for the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); prudential bank supervision is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (Canada); cross-border AML/CFT surveillance and wire-reporting is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). No exchange-control ministry or foreign-investment screening body applies currency licensing to ordinary trade flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM - Circumscribed and Secondary. Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) now requires registration of payment service providers, and only RPAA-registered PSPs or Payments Canada members can access domestic clearing rails (ACSS/LVTS). Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, and similar) are viable only as an ancillary FX-conversion and small-value settlement layer through a sponsor bank or a FINTRAC-registered foreign money-services business (MSB); they do not provide direct LVTS access, guaranteed CAD settlement, or reliable Documentary LC execution. For an off-price apparel liquidation ULC routing Swift_Wire and ACH_Direct payments, the treasury architecture should be Tier-1 Schedule I bank as primary clearing counterparty with one RPAA/FINTRAC-compliant EMI for minor outbound vendor payments and cross-currency float optimization. A non-licensed offshore EMI operating without Canadian registration creates sanctions non-compliance risk and is not viable as a principal settlement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity from a non-resident shareholder must be a SWIFT MT103 wire to the ULC's USD or CAD settlement account at a Tier-1 Schedule I bank, carrying purpose code 'C' (BPM6 direct investment - equity capital injection) and a reference line identifying the shareholder, the ULC, and the subscription agreement. The bank requires: an executed board resolution authorizing the capital call; a notarized Subscription Agreement; source-of-funds evidence from the remitting bank; CRS/FATCA self-certification of the shareholder; and confirmation that the funds are not derived from a high-risk or sanctioned source. FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer reporting is triggered at CAD 10,000 or above. CAD-domiciled injections clear via ACSS (1-3 business days) or LVTS (T+0 real-time); USD injections clear via the bank's US correspondent through Fedwire or CHIPS and are then internally swept to the CAD operating account at the Bank of Canada daily reference rate. A voluntary Capital Escrow Account may be used to demonstrate committed equity to trade counterparties but is not statutory because Alberta imposes no minimum-capital requirement.For off-price apparel trade settlement, the clearing hierarchy is: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer for supplier payments up to USD 100,000, using USD correspondent clearing through Fedwire/CHIPS with T+1 execution; (ii) Irrevocable confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit issued via MT700 for bulk production in Bangladesh, Vietnam, India, or China, with MT754 advising negotiation, MT707 for amendments, and MT799 for pre-advice and risk participation; and (iii) ACH_Direct (ACSS credit-push) or LVTS T+0 for domestic CAD payments to freight forwarders, customs brokers for CBSA duties, and Alberta warehouse operators. Every disbursement must mirror the purchase-order lifecycle: PO, pro-forma invoice, LC/MT103, transport documents, customs entry, and reconciliation, which jointly prevent split-payment flags and sanctions interdiction failures. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM - Circumscribed and Secondary. Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) now requires registration of payment service providers, and only RPAA-registered PSPs or Payments Canada members can access domestic clearing rails (ACSS/LVTS). Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, and similar) are viable only as an ancillary FX-conversion and small-value settlement layer through a sponsor bank or a FINTRAC-registered foreign money-services business (MSB); they do not provide direct LVTS access, guaranteed CAD settlement, or reliable Documentary LC execution. For an off-price apparel liquidation ULC routing Swift_Wire and ACH_Direct payments, the treasury architecture should be Tier-1 Schedule I bank as primary clearing counterparty with one RPAA/FINTRAC-compliant EMI for minor outbound vendor payments and cross-currency float optimization. A non-licensed offshore EMI operating without Canadian registration creates sanctions non-compliance risk and is not viable as a principal settlement rail.
