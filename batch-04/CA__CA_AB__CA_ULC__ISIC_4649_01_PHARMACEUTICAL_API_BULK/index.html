Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via the GST/HST NETFILE portal and My Business Account (MyBA); corporate income tax administered federally by the CRA under the Tax Collection Agreements, and Alberta provincial corporate tax is also administered by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; customs and export controls are governed by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity wholesale-distributing APIs into or from Canada must first obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN/15-character program account). The procedure is: (i) apply through the CRA's 'Business Registration Online' (BRO) service or interface with CRA call centre; (ii) open a GST/HST program account by filing Form RC1 'Request for Business Number Registration or Disclosure', or through the non-resident registration application; (iii) where the ULC is incorporated in Alberta, the Alberta corporate registry number must be cited and the Alberta Corporate Income Tax account (CRA account under Alberta Tax Administration Agreement) must be opened; (iv) if the entity is treated as a partnership or disregarded entity for US tax purposes, the ULC may additionally need to provide Form 8832, CRA information returns, and treaty residency/beneficial ownership documents; (v) maintain a permanent place of business or Canadian agent for service if required by the registrations, and report all changes through MyBA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: An Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) is eligible for check-the-box transparency under US federal tax principles while remaining a corporation for Canadian income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act. For Canadian tax purposes the ULC is an entity-level taxpayer resident in Alberta by incorporation. Its Canadian tax scope is worldwide income for residents, with foreign tax credits available; Alberta imposes a provincial tax rate of 8% on taxable income allocated to Alberta. Non-resident shareholders carrying on business indirectly through the ULC may be subject to Canadian tax on treaty-protected business profits only if a permanent establishment exists; GST/HST registration is required for supplies made in Canada by the partnership, joint venture, or disregarded entity structure where threshold tests are met.

Available election forms

Available federal elections include the CRA Section 216(1) election for non-residents with rental or timber income, the deemed resident election under Section 216.1, and the partnership or bare trust elections under the Income Tax Act. For hybrid entities such as a ULC, the Canada Revenue Agency may apply General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and hybrid mismatch provisions under sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act; eligible taxpayers may make such prescribed elections and disclose reportable transactions under section 237.3. A ULC may also be separately elected under IRS Form 8832 to be a disregarded entity or per se corporation for US check-the-box purposes, and such election must be consistently reported in cross-border tax filings and be compliant with treaty limitation-on-benefits clauses.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies, determined on a calendar-quarter basis; a non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada must register and collect GST/HST if the worldwide taxable supply revenue exceeds CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the preceding four-quarter period, unless the supplies are exclusively zero-rated exports, and Canada permits a non-resident to be voluntarily registered in order to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating eligibility under the Excise Tax Act applies to (i) goods sold by a non-resident, supplied in Canada, and exported by the supplier under section 22 as part of continuous export transaction documentation; (ii) supplies of goods to a foreign exporter when exported within prescribed time limits; (iii) supplies of prescription drugs and pharmaceutical and biological products that qualify under section 1 of Part III of Schedule VI, including active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) where the product is a drug for human or animal use and is sold under a prescription or for use in a prescription or clinical trial; and (iv) services performed on goods destined for export, provided the services are physically performed outside Canada or on exported goods. To claim 0% GST on an API wholesale export, the supplier must establish the goods were shipped out of Canada, delivered to a non-resident purchaser, and not acquired for consumption or use in Canada before export. A valid proof of export is legally required and must be obtained before the filing deadline for the reporting period, with a maximum two-year extension period under the CRA's two-year rule only where the evidence is obtained before the return is filed.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A or approved electronic equivalent), containing the exporter name, BN, description of pharmaceutical API, Harmonized System classification 2933/2934/2942, quantity and value
  • Commercial invoice referencing the ULC and foreign purchaser, stating the terms of sale (Incoterms 2020) and confirming export destination
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill (or equivalent transport document) evidencing movement from Canada to the foreign buyer
  • Customs or border crossing examination / port clearance proof where export occurs through a Canadian border point with a licensed customs broker or self-filing corporation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT bank wire / letter of credit advising documenting receipt of foreign currency payment from the non-resident purchaser
  • Delivery and warehousing evidence showing the goods left Canada to a foreign customs territory
  • Pharmaceutical import license of the destination country, if applicable, to substantiate legitimate export of APIs
  • Foreign customs entry or importer-of-record declaration for the destination market where commercially available

Canada does not mandate a central real-time Government clearance model or digital stamping platform for GST/HST invoices. However, GST/HST registrants must issue an electronic or paper invoice containing the registrant name, BN/GST/HST registration number, date, description, total consideration, and tax charged. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million, which may be done through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or approved third-party software; CRA has introduced a voluntary 'e-invoice' interoperability framework based on the Peppol standard for business-to-business procurement, but no fiscal clearance stamping obligation exists. Export invoices must carry the notation 'Zero-rated export' and preserve the underlying export evidential records.

CRA will disallow zero-rating and reassess the GST/HST at 5% with interest if the export documentation is deficient, if export does not occur within the prescribed period, or if the transaction lacks commercial substance. Normal reassessment periods run four years from the date of filing, being extended under misrepresentation, fraud, or gross negligence. Penalties include: intentional or gross negligence penalties under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the net tax amount; false statements or omissions penalties under subsection 281(1); repeated non-compliance penalties; and criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act, punishable by fine to the maximum of CAD 1 million plus imprisonment on indictment. For hybrid structures, CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to deny refund claims and impose offsetting penalties; refunds obtained by false zero-rating invoices are subject to full clawback plus compound interest, denial of input tax credits, and potential cross-border referral to tax treaty competent-authority procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency and no exchange-control law. Current-account and capital-account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, profit repatriation) are fully convertible without central-bank permit. Remittances from the Alberta ULC are unrestricted for FX, but Canadian Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., 5%-15% under the Canada-U.S. treaty) applies to dividends, interest, and royalties; the ULC entity-classification rules and anti-hybrid regime require a cross-border tax opinion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion timeline, or minimum conversion ratio applies to Canadian export receipts; the Alberta API exporter may hold, reinvest, or convert foreign currency freely. Remaining obligations are FINTRAC reporting for cross-border cash or transfers of C$10,000 or more, Export and Import Permits Act screening for controlled pharmaceutical goods, and Statistics Canada merchandise-trade declarations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), policy-coordinated with the Department of Finance Canada (Ministère des Finances Canada); prudential supervision of chartered banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF regulation by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).
