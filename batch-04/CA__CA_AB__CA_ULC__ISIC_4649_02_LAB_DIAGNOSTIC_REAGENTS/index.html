Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a general check-the-box election for Canadian corporations. An Alberta ULC is generally a Canadian corporation taxable under the federal Income Tax Act and applicable Alberta corporate tax rules, regardless of its unlimited-liability status. Where eligible under United States classification regulations, the ULC may separately file a United States Form 8832 election to be treated as a disregarded entity, partnership or corporation for United States federal tax purposes. That election does not change its Canadian corporate status or Canadian tax filing obligations. Cross-border hybrid-entity, foreign-affiliate, treaty-benefit, withholding and hybrid-mismatch rules must be reviewed separately.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, generally measured over a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, subject to the Excise Tax Act rules and exclusions. A person may register voluntarily below the threshold. Certain non-residents and persons making specified supplies may have registration obligations independent of the ordinary small-supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are generally zero-rated where the supplier exports the goods or the statutory export conditions are satisfied, including prescribed documentation and evidence that the goods were delivered or made available for export outside Canada. Goods supplied to a non-resident may qualify where the non-resident exports the goods under the applicable Excise Tax Act rules; merely selling to a non-resident or receiving foreign currency is insufficient. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated only where a specific place-of-supply rule applies, commonly where the service is supplied to a non-resident and is not excluded by rules for services connected with real property, tangible personal property, litigation, personal services, or services performed in Canada. Export zero-rating does not eliminate the need to charge GST on domestic supplies and does not itself guarantee an input tax credit refund.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the Canadian supplier, purchaser, goods, value, currency, terms and destination
  • CBSA export declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), where required by the goods, destination, value and applicable reporting exemption
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier manifest or equivalent carrier evidence
  • Packing list and purchase order or sales contract
  • Proof of delivery or proof that the goods were exported from Canada, including carrier tracking, customs exit evidence or foreign import documentation where available
  • Evidence supporting the GST zero-rating treatment and reconciliation to the accounting records
  • Inward remittance evidence may support commercial and audit substantiation but is not a general Canadian statutory condition for export zero-rating

Canada has no general nationwide real-time GST invoice-clearance or mandatory continuous transaction control system. GST registrants must issue invoices or other prescribed documentation containing required information, including supplier identity, GST registration number where applicable, date, description, consideration, and GST amount or applicable calculation. Electronic invoices are generally acceptable if integrity, accessibility and record-retention requirements are satisfied. CRA books and records must normally be retained for at least six years from the end of the relevant year, subject to longer periods or unresolved audit requirements. Export declarations are filed electronically through CBSA CERS when required; this is a customs reporting requirement, not a GST invoice-clearance mandate.

Canada has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline and no general requirement that export proceeds be received in Canada within 180 days to preserve GST zero-rating. CRA may reassess GST where export conditions are not met, documentation is inadequate, goods were not exported, services were not eligible, or zero-rating was claimed on domestic supplies. The reassessment may include GST collectible, denial or repayment of related input tax credits or refunds, arrears interest and applicable penalties. Gross-negligence penalties can generally equal 50% of the understated tax or the overstated refund or credit attributable to the conduct; other penalties may apply for false statements, failure to file, failure to remit or failure to maintain records. Fraudulent conduct may attract prosecution and fines or imprisonment under applicable federal statutes. Incorrect customs declarations may trigger CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalties System sanctions, seizure or forfeiture, reassessment of duties and taxes, and interest. A zero-rated export refund is therefore subject to retrospective audit and clawback based on the statutory limitation periods and any extended period applicable to misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII status for both current account and capital account transactions. Canada maintains no exchange control regime: the former Exchange Control Act and Currency, Foreign Exchange and Gold Regulations were repealed, and no central bank permission, exchange control license, or investment authorization is required for foreign exchange conversion, dividend remittances, or capital repatriation. A non-resident shareholder's receipt of profit from an Alberta ULC is nevertheless subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, typically reduced to 5%-15% under an applicable tax treaty) and to Form NR4 remittance by the payer; this is a tax compliance, not a currency convertibility, hurdle. Practical frictions arise primarily from reporting thresholds under the PCMLTFA, including mandatory FINTRAC reporting of all international electronic funds transfers (EFTs) of CAD 10,000 or more, and from Canadian bank de-risking policies toward non-resident-owned entities lacking a physical Canadian nexus.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada imposes no statutory foreign currency surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline for repatriating export proceeds from wholesale distribution of diagnostic ELISA reagents. Export revenue may be retained in USD or other currencies indefinitely at the entity's discretion. The only statutory constraints are: (i) filing of the Canadian Export Declaration (formerly B13A) through the Canadian Export Reporting System with Statistics Canada/CBSA for any commercial export exceeding a declared value of CAD 2,000 per shipment; (ii) compliance with the Export Control List and Export Permits Act for controlled or dual-use reagents/chemicals, administered by Global Affairs Canada (where applicable, determine whether the specific ELISA reagent falls under Group 1 or 2 controlled items); and (iii) FINTRAC reporting of all cross-border physical currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the exclusive central bank and monetary authority for the Canadian dollar; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated deposit-taking institutions and insurance companies; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and foreign exchange transaction reporting obligations. Trade sanctions and export controls are jointly administered by Global Affairs Canada and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for cross-border payment facilitation, Low for substitute deposit/settlement banking. Under the Bank Act (Canada), only a chartered bank or an authorized foreign bank branch may accept deposits in Canada. Offshore digital EMIs and neo-banks (e.g., Wise, Currencycloud, Airwallex) may be registered with FINTRAC as money services businesses (MSBs) and thus legally transmit funds on behalf of a Canadian-domiciled ULC; however, they cannot offer a full Canadian-dollar operating account with deposit insurance (CDIC) or serve as the ULC's primary treasury account. A foreign EMI may be used as a supplementary multi-currency collection and vendor-payout rail for USD/EUR clearing, provided the ULC maintains a primary settlement account in Canada. Any EMI that opens a local CAD account must register as an MSB with FINTRAC, appoint a Canadian agent, implement the Travel Rule on outgoing EFTs, and comply with provincial money-lending/transmitter licensing in Alberta; absence of an Alberta registered office will trigger bank refusal in most cases.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan funding from a non-resident parent must be wired in compliance with the PCMLTFA Travel Rule, using a SWIFT MT103/MT202 via the parent's bank to the ULC's Canadian Schedule I settlement bank. The receiving bank requires: (i) full originator name and physical address; (ii) beneficiary name 'CAULC Corp.' (or exact registered legal name), account number, and receiving bank BIC; (iii) a transaction purpose code that is consistent with the Alberta Capital Escrow Account designation; and (iv) release of funds from the capital escrow account only after the corporate registration certificate, Notice of Articles, and tax ID (Australian-style Canada Revenue Agency BN, where applicable) are verified. Funds must arrive in CAD or be converted immediately via the bank's wholesale FX desk. Where a non-resident shareholder injects capital in kind (reduce price on inventory), the receiving bank may require a Commercial Invoice and a director resolution to reference 'capital contribution in kind'. No exchange-control approval is required, but the bank will file an International Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC for any EFT to or from Canada exceeding CAD 10,000. Remittance of a profit back to the parent will require a Part XIII withholding tax remittance (NR4) if dividends or interest are paid.Outbound trade settlements to overseas suppliers of diagnostic ELISA reagents should be executed using SWIFT MT103 (straightforward payment) for open account supplier payments and Commercial Letters of Credit (irrevocable, confirmed, at-sight or usance) for strategic or first-time purchases. For CAD-denominated supplier contracts, the ULC is cleared through Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system (formerly LVTS) for high-value transfers, while routine payments may use the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) or the Real-Time Rail (RTR). For USD/EUR obligations, the Canadian bank will convert CAD to the invoice currency using its intraday spot desk, and the MT103 is routed through the bank's United States or European correspondent (e.g., BMO New York, RBC New York, CIBC New York). Trade settlements should be documented with purchase orders, invoices, bills of lading, and proof of delivery to support the underlying trade purpose and to allow the bank to classify payments as 'TRADE' rather than 'financing' in the AML monitoring system.
