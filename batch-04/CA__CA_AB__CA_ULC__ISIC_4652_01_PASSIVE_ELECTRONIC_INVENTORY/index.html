Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital requirement and no escrow or trust account rule for an Alberta private ULC; the articles may authorize an unlimited number of shares without par value, and shares may be issued for property, past services, or a promissory note under ABCA s. 25, subject to a directors' solvency declaration.

Funding & unlock path: Alberta Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) Act and OHS Code compliance for the warehouse operation; fire safety certification per the National Fire Code (Alberta edition) covering egress, extinguishers, and alarm systems; structural load inspection and engineering certificate for high-density racking under the Alberta Building Code; WCB-Alberta workers' compensation registration; employers with more than 20 workers must establish a joint occupational health and safety committee.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital requirement and no escrow or trust account rule for an Alberta private ULC; the articles may authorize an unlimited number of shares without par value, and shares may be issued for property, past services, or a promissory note under ABCA s. 25, subject to a directors' solvency declaration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — My Business Account portal; Business Registration Online; also Alberta Corporate Registry for corporate law registration and CBSA for customs accounts/export declarations.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta ULC should first incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act and obtain an Alberta corporation number. For federal tax identity, apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or Form RC1. Add the required program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll if employees). For cross-border goods movement, obtain a CBSA import/export account and register with the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) to file export declarations. The BN, not the SIN, is the statutory TIN/VAT registration identifier for businesses in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The Alberta ULC is a corporation for Canadian tax purposes, but it is 'pass-through eligible' for U.S. federal tax because a ULC may be made a disregarded entity/partnership for U.S. shareholders under the U.S. check-the-box rules via Treasury Form 8832. Canadian tax law has no parallel corporate pass-through election; for CRA the ULC remains an entity-level taxpayer. U.S. check-the-box on the ULC can create hybrid mismatch considerations, and distributions may be treated as dividends or deemed dividends for Canadian withholding tax, subject to treaty relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies made by the person, subject to the small-supplier rules under section 123 of the Excise Tax Act; a commercial distributor of passive electronic components will normally exceed this threshold and must register for GST/HST and charge 5% GST on taxable Canadian supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of tangible personal property (including multilayer ceramic capacitors and resistors) is zero-rated under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier establishes the property is exported from Canada as part of the sale, delivered outside Canada to the recipient or to a common carrier for export, and the export occurs within prescribed time limits. Services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated where not performed in connection with Canadian real estate or goods. A ULC distributing passive electronic inventory must maintain contemporaneous evidence that title and risk of loss pass for export; domestic deliveries within Alberta and other Canadian provinces are taxable at 5% GST (or applicable HST in participating provinces).

  • Customs Export Declaration: CBSA B13A report via Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), or equivalent highway/AOI (air/rail/marine) export declaration
  • Transport document: signed Bill of Lading, air waybill, or CUSMA/customs carrier document evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Commercial invoice/packing list with Incoterms, identification of the non-resident buyer, and Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 bank credit evidence of foreign currency repatriation

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory electronic-invoicing clearance, and no digital stamping requirement. GST/HST returns are filed periodically (monthly/quarterly/annually) through CRA My Business Account or EDI; e-invoices are accepted if they accurately record the supply, but there is no CRA API pre-clearance or electronic invoice authorization step.

CRA will treat an apparent export zero-rating claim as taxable at 5% GST if the export documentation bundle is incomplete or the goods do not leave Canada, and may reassess within the normal four-year period, with no limitation period in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Penalties include interest, gross-negligence/civil penalties for false statements (subject to 25% of the tax amount under ETA), and criminal tax-evasion fines of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks include denial of input tax credits/refunds and mandatory repayment of any GST/HST refunds claimed based on invalid zero-rated exports.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a convertible currency regime under the Foreign Exchange Act for current account transactions. Capital account transactions, including equity investments and portfolio flows, require prior Bank of Canada approval. Profit remittances, dividends, and interest payments are freely convertible without restriction when underlying transactions comply with exchange control rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies under Canadian law. Export receipts convert freely into CAD at market rates with no statutory timeline or conversion ratio. Importers retain foreign currency proceeds up to 180 days for import payments.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50 million gross revenue, or CAD 5 million in cross-border transactions, or CAD 1 million in intangible property transactions (per CRA guidelines)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions require economic substance for transactions to be respected. A ULC must demonstrate real business activity and substance to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution may lead to fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if they failed to exercise due diligence; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize transactions, and impose transfer pricing adjustments with penalties up to 10% of the adjustment amount.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if the company is deemed a sham; potential for criminal charges under the Income Tax Act.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities; criminal penalties include imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly or negligently fail to implement compliance programs; travel bans may be issued.
