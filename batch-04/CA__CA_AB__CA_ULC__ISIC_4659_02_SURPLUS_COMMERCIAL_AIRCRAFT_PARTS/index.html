Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal and Alberta corporate income tax and GST/HST administration (T2 Corporate Internet Filing, GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account); Alberta Treasury Board & Finance (ATBF) is nominal provincial authority but Alberta corporate tax is collected by CRA under the Tax Collection Agreement; registration of legal entity: Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Ontario-equivalent is Alberta Corporate Registry/RegistryX). No separate provincial sales tax authority in Alberta.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (RSA 2000, c B-9), the ULC files Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) to obtain its 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) and associated program accounts: RC (GST/HST), RT (T2 corporate income tax), RP (payroll). The BN is the statutory tax identification number; no separate TIN/VAT ID exists. Non-resident parent/shareholders may be added by share transfer without re-registration; the ULC remains a Canadian resident corporation. For a foreign entity branch (non-ULC), the non-resident obtains a CRA BN for non-residents and registers extra-provincially under Alberta's Business Corporations Act/Part 21 of the Companies Act (OOP registration), but for this CA_AB ULC structure the standard domestic RC1 procedure applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Canada): ULC is deemed a corporation and a taxable Canadian corporation under ITA s. 83.4; files T2 and pays federal (15% general) and Alberta (8%) corporate income tax on worldwide income. Pass_Through_Eligible (US federal): disregarded-entity check-the-box election available on IRS Form 8832, creating hybrid-entity status; Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4, 18.6) apply. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canada): ULC is deemed a corporation and a taxable Canadian corporation under ITA s. 83.4; files T2 and pays federal (15% general) and Alberta (8%) corporate income tax on worldwide income. Pass_Through_Eligible (US federal): disregarded-entity check-the-box election available on IRS Form 8832, creating hybrid-entity status; Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4, 18.6) apply. Territorial_Scope: not applicable — Canada taxes residents on worldwide income with foreign tax credits.

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election. Key elections: (i) US IRS Form 8832 check-the-box to treat the Alberta ULC as disregarded/pass-through for US federal tax (hybrid entity) — must be filed within 75 days of formation or 12 months after any change; (ii) ITA s. 83.4(2) deeming provision confirming corporate status of ULC (no separate election needed); (iii) ETA Schedule IX Form GST20 election for closely related corporations (for GST pooling/organization); (iv) ITA s. 216(4) election for non-resident-owned rental property (not typically used by ULC trading company); (v) ITA s. 219 non-resident branch tax election (inapplicable to AB-resident ULC); (vi) Hybrid mismatch neutralization: careful application of ITA ss. 18.4/18.6 and US Reg. 1.1503/transition rules to avoid deduction/non-inclusion mismatches, plus s. 17.1 associated timing for unpaid amounts.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold: a person (including a non-resident) must be registered for GST/HST once worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated and excluding exempt supplies) exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter for a start-up; once registered, the Alberta ULC charges 5% GST on domestic taxable supplies and 0% on exported goods/eligible offshore services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supply of the surplus commercial aircraft rotables and avionics parts is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V (Goods) if the ULC exports the goods as part of the supply: (a) ships/delivers them to a destination outside Canada (s. 5), (b) transfers possession to a common carrier/forwarding agent for export before consideration is due (s. 6), or (c) the goods are supplied for export with continued possession outside Canada (ss. 11-14). Related services provided to a non-resident (who is not registered and not carrying on business in Canada) are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V (Services), s. 7; services in respect of the exported goods (repair, warehousing, freight logistics) are zero-rated under Part V, s. 12 (services to goods for export) etc. The ULC must satisfy the two-year evidence retention rule: proof of export (e.g., commercial invoice, air waybill, freight manifest) must be obtained no later than 2 years after the supply; otherwise the supply is re-characterized as taxable at 5%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs B13A form or ACI eManifest for US-bound goods) — proof that the goods were exported from Canada and reported under the Customs Act for non-US destinations
  • Bill of Lading / original Air Waybill (Master and House) issued by a licensed carrier evidencing the aircraft parts left Canada and were consigned to the foreign air carrier/MRO
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 transfer receipt confirming foreign currency payment and FX repatriation into the ULC's Canadian bank account within the 180-day deadline

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or CRA-API clearance exists in Canada. Voluntary PAN-Canadian e-Invoicing launched by the CRA using the PEPPOL framework (optional). Mandatory electronic filing: GST/HST registrants with taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1 million per year must file using GST/HST NETFILE or EDI; all T2 corporate returns must be filed electronically (EFILE/TEI) for corporations with gross revenue > CAD 1 million; electronic payment through the CRA's My Payment required for large remitters. The ULC must retain invoices (electronic or paper) meeting ETA s. 223(1) disclosure rules; foreign-language invoices require French/English translation on request.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current account convertibility under IMF Article VIII and the Bank of Canada Act. Capital account transactions require prior review by the Department of Finance for certain portfolio investments. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted subject to FINTRAC reporting thresholds of CAD 10,000. Key hurdles include mandatory PCMLTFA compliance, enhanced due diligence for aviation-sector clients, and prohibition on unlicensed cross-border money transmission.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada. Exporters must file Automated Export Reporting System (AERS) declarations with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for applicable shipments. Repatriation of foreign exchange receipts is required but occurs freely through licensed banks. All FX transactions exceeding reporting thresholds must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility rating. Offshore digital EMIs are prohibited for aviation-parts distribution due to FINTRAC licensing requirements and Bank of Canada foreign exchange oversight. Legal constraints mandate use of traditional correspondent banking channels and prohibit unlicensed cross-border money transmission services.
