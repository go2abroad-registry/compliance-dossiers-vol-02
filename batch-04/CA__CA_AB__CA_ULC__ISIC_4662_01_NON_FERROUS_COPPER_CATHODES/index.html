Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal tax administration for corporate income tax and GST/HST; CRA portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (canada.ca). Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered by the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA), Alberta Treasury Board and Finance, under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act. Alberta ULC incorporation is filed with the Alberta Corporate Registry (Alberta Registries), which coordinates CRA Business Number issuance. Export controls and export declarations are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

S1: Incorporate the Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) through the Alberta Corporate Registry; obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, Corporate Access Number (CAN), registered office address in Alberta and Alberta registered agent. S2: Obtain the federal Business Number (BN) automatically via the registry-CRA integration, or file Form RC1 Request for a Business Number / use CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). S3: Activate CRA program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll if the ULC employs Canadian staff); all copper trading and invoicing activity should reference the RT account. S4: For foreign-controlled or non-resident-owned structures, provide the CRA with certified constating documents, proof of non-resident ownership, and a Canadian agent or representative for service; a non-resident GST/HST registrant without a permanent establishment in Canada may be required to post security. S5: Register for Alberta corporate income tax with the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (the T2 corporate return serves as the federal-provincial filing vehicle for the combined federal and Alberta corporate taxes). S6: Retain the BN certificate, program account confirmation letters and registration dates for counterparty KYC/AML due diligence on LME warrant transfers and documentary letter of credit trade lines.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (default); Pass_Through_Eligible via subsection 104(2) Income Tax Act (Canada) election. An Alberta ULC is by default a Canadian resident corporation and a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act, subject to federal corporate income tax at 15% plus Alberta corporate income tax at 8%, and to GST/HST registration as a single legal person. The entity is not in a territorial or tax-exempt zone regime. The default corporate classification can be displaced for Canadian tax purposes only by filing the subsection 104(2) ITA election, which deems the ULC not to be a corporation and deems each member to be a partner of a partnership, yielding full pass-through taxation and fiscal transparency.

Available election forms

Subsection 104(2) Income Tax Act (Canada) election: the only mechanism available to convert the Alberta ULC's default entity-level taxation into pass-through treatment. The election is filed with the CRA by written notice (accompanied by the ULC board resolution and unanimous shareholder consent; no prescribed CRA form is assigned) and takes effect for the taxation year designated in the election. Upon a valid election, the ULC is deemed not to be a corporation, each member is deemed to be a partner of a partnership, and the members report their respective shares of income and are jointly and severally liable for GST/HST obligations; partnerships, not the ULC, become the GST/HST registrant. The election, once made, cannot be revoked without CRA consent. Hybrid mismatch and Canada-US tax treaty transparency rules (including treaty article IV(6) fiscally transparent entity provisions and BEPS Action 2) must be evaluated because the election may cause the ULC to be treated consistently or inconsistently with its US federal classification; no US Form 8832 check-the-box election has any effect on Canadian tax classification, and Canada does not offer any comparable check-the-box regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada maintains no exchange-control approvals or licensing for ordinary cross-border payments, profit repatriation, or equity injections. Dividend and interest outflows remain subject to Canadian withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act and applicable tax treaties; no central bank permission is required for remittances. Anti-money-laundering and sanctions compliance (Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Act, FINTRAC) and international tax disclosure (FATCA/CRS, Part XVIII.1) apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Export receipts are not subject to any statutory surrender, conversion, or mandatory repatriation timeline. Settlement must be supported by normal commercial documentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs entry summary). Export transactions are reportable to Statistics Canada under the Canadian Export Reporting framework and to the CBSA for customs clearance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign-exchange statistics and trade reporting administered through Statistics Canada and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium for use as a primary treasury account; offshore electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are not a recognized deposit-taking class in Canada. Any money-service business operating or soliciting clients in Canada must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and comply with Canadian AML/CFT obligations. Unregistered offshore EMIs are legally constrained and will likely be rejected by Canadian chartered banks for correspondent settlement, particularly for LME warrant transfers and documentary LCs. An MSB-registered Canadian fintech may support ancillary payment aggregation but lacks the balance sheet capacity and SWIFT correspondent network necessary for high-value base-metals settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections must be effected as SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with a clear payment message referencing 'equity contribution' or 'additional paid-in capital' and the company's legal name. The underlying remittance should include a certified board resolution authorizing the issuance of shares; Canadian banks verify the remitter's identity and source of funds, and may require a copy of the share subscription agreement, proof of no issuance to a prohibited person, and notification under Part XVIII.1 (FATCA/CRS) where a non-resident 25% or greater beneficial holder exists.
