Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Alberta ULC; no escrow or capital deposit account is required by law. Shares may be subscribed at par value or deferred subscription terms, provided consideration is paid in cash, property, or past services as resolved by the board.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Alberta Fire Code (Safety Codes Act, RSA 2000, c S-1) with local municipal fire inspector sign-off; structural load inspection for bulk silos, transfer equipment, and marine terminal interfaces; occupational health and safety clearance under the Alberta OHS Act, RSA 2000, c O-2, and the OHS Code, including WHMIS/GHS safety data sheets for cement dust, crystalline silica exposure controls, and alkaline pH PPE requirements; transportation of dangerous goods compliance for vessel and rail transfers.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Alberta ULC; no escrow or capital deposit account is required by law. Shares may be subscribed at par value or deferred subscription terms, provided consideration is paid in cash, property, or past services as resolved by the board.

Competent tax authority

Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered by the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA), but Alberta does not impose a provincial sales tax.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned ULC carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST registration if not a small supplier or if required for non-resident sellers. Apply online through the CRA Business Registration service or submit Form RC1, Request for a Business Number, to the CRA tax services office. For an out-of-province or non-resident entity, a representative must be authorized via Form RC59, and a Canadian contact address may be required. Provincial corporate tax registration is administered by Alberta TRA through the Corporate Tax Registration form, linked to the same CRA BN.

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A ULC may be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. federal tax purposes through a U.S. check-the-box election on IRS Form 8832; for Canadian income tax purposes, a ULC remains a corporation liable to income tax under the Income Tax Act. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in world-wide taxable supplies during any calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters; non-resident persons making taxable supplies of real property or delivering goods in Canada may also have mandatory registration obligations regardless of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported by the supplier are zero-rated for GST/HST under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Zero-rating applies where the goods are delivered to a non-resident person and not further processed in Canada, or are exported in accordance with regulations. Offshore services relating to goods exported, and services supplied to non-residents that qualify under the 'service to a non-resident' rules, may also be zero-rated when not performed in connection with Canadian real property or goods destined for Canadian consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) Summary
  • Bill of Lading or Waybill evidencing movement from Canada to international destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment settlement from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List and shipping documents
  • CRA GST/HST export evidence records (retained for six years)

Canada does not have a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for GST/HST. Registered businesses must meet CRA record-keeping requirements and retain invoicing and customs documentation in electronic or paper form. CRA supports electronic data interchange and API-based filing through GST/HST NETFILE for registered businesses, but there is no government pre-approval of invoice serial numbers or fiscal stamping.

CRA may reassess GST/HST within four years, or later in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. False zero-rating claims or missing export documentation result in denial of zero rating, resulting in GST/HST payable at 5% plus interest. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include the gross negligence penalty of up to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax, and a fine or imprisonment under s. 327 for wilful evasion. Recovered input tax credits can be clawed back with arrears interest. CRA audit flags include repeated zero-rated sales to related foreign entities without evidence of physical export, delayed FX repatriation, and absence of shipping documents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with a free-floating CAD. Both current-account items (clinker/cement trade, ocean freight, port charges) and capital-account items (equity injections, intercompany loans, shareholder advances, profit repatriation) are fully convertible without exchange-control approval, quota, surrender requirement, or license. Profit remittance is unrestricted by FX law, but a Part XIII Canadian withholding tax applies to dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-resident shareholders; for a ULC with a US parent, the Canada-US treaty typically reduces dividend withholding to 5% (at least 10% ownership) and interest/royalties to 0-10% depending on character. Practical hurdles are limited to bank Source-of-Funds (SOF) verification, FINTRAC reporting thresholds, and Canadian sanctions (Special Economic Measures Act, United Nations Act). No minimum holding period exists for trade-related FX conversion; CAD/USD spot settles T+1 via Lynx-correspondent clearings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for receivables from Portland clinker (HS 2507) or cement (HS 2523) exports. Proceeds may be held in CAD, USD, or other freely convertible currencies. However, for Canadian T2 corporate income tax and financial-statement purposes, foreign-currency amounts must be translated to CAD under ITA section 261 using the Bank of Canada exchange rate, and Canadian banks must automatically report all incoming international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC as Large Electronic Funds Transfer Reports.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) for bank and clearing-system supervision; FINTRAC as the financial intelligence unit under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for cross-border electronic fund transfer (EFT) reporting; Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate and monetary policy; CBSA for physical cross-border currency declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility for ancillary low-value disbursements (supplier pre-payments, demurrage fees), but NOT viable as the primary treasury or escrow account. Canada has no distinct 'EMI' license; non-bank payment service providers must register as money services businesses (MSB/FMSB) with FINTRAC and, from September 2025, obtain a Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) license supervised by the Bank of Canada. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, OFX, Veem) may serve the ULC through a Canadian partner bank or as a registered foreign MSB, but they cannot take deposits, provide overdrafts, issue commercial letters of credit, or participate directly in Lynx (Canada's RTGS). Using an offshore EMI for clinker trade settlements also triggers heightened EDD by the Tier-1 relationship bank and may compromise the ULC's ability to obtain UCP600 LC issuance and discounting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan wires from the non-resident parent must be routed by SWIFT MT103 to the ULC's Tier-1 CAD/USD account at RBC, TD, BMO, CIBC, Scotiabank, or ATB Financial with a payment narrative such as 'intercompany equity contribution' or 'capital subscription' (Canada has no statutory purpose-code schedule, but the bank's customer profile requires a clear SOF narrative). Supporting boarding documents: ULC board resolution recording the injection, capital contribution schedule, Alberta Articles/ledger update, and Bank-of-Canada noon-rate translation into CAD for T2 booking. Funds are typically held 24-72 hours for source-of-funds verification, then credited same-day via Lynx (CAD RTGS) or via Fedwire to the Canadian bank's USD correspondent. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined non-resident general corporate rate (15% Canadian federal Part I + 8% Alberta provincial general rate). For an Alberta ULC, the federal and provincial rates apply on a combined return; unlimited liability does not alter Canadian corporate residence or reduce the statutory rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-residents. Under the Canada–U.S. Income Tax Convention, the rate is 5% for a U.S. beneficial owner that is a corporation owning at least 10% of the ULC's voting shares and 15% for portfolio investors; the ULC is not fiscally transparent for Canadian tax purposes and therefore cannot avoid dividend WHT by reason of a U.S. check-the-box election.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is expected where the aggregate amount of non-arm's length cross-border transactions with non-residents exceeds C$1,000,000 in a fiscal year. A Master File should be prepared where the multinational group generates global consolidated revenue above C$100,000,000; a Form T822(C) Country-by-Country notification is separately required where group revenue meets the applicable OECD threshold. Canada does not require the documents to be filed with the T2, but they must be ready for CRA inspection within 3 months of a written request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime; instead, CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), the tax-treaty Limitation on Benefits and Principal Purpose Test, and the common-law substance-over-form doctrine. An Alberta ULC used solely as an invoicing conduit without genuine commercial activity, employees, physical premises, or control over vessel logistics will have treaty benefits denied, Part XIII withholding assessed on deemed dividend flows, and possible criminal referral for artificial offshore profit stripping.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Failure to File

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s.239(1), a person evading tax is liable to a fine of between 50% and 200% of the tax evaded, and imprisonment for up to 5 years. A civil false-statement penalty under s.163(2) is 50% of the misstated amount; late-filing penalties under s.162 also apply.

Director personal liability: A director who participates in the evasion may be convicted as a party to the offence and sentenced to imprisonment; the director can be jointly assessed under s.160/227.1 for unremitted withholding taxes, and upon conviction a court may impose restitution, asset freezing, and travel restrictions pending extradition to Canada.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA adjustments under ITA s.247, plus interest and a s.247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the gross upward transfer-pricing adjustment when the taxpayer fails to meet documentation standards. Repeated or gross negligence can elevate the penalty or lead to a criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers signing the T2 may be assessed for the 50% civil penalty under s.163(2) for knowingly making false statements; criminal liability for participation is possible, and indemnity from the corporation may be nullified in cases of fraudulent misrepresentation.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may invoke GAAR (ITA s.245) to deny treaty benefits by recharacterizing the underlying transaction and assessing ordinary Part XIII withholding taxes plus interest and penalties. The Alberta Business Corporations Act may administratively strike the ULC from the registry for failure to maintain a real corporate presence; authorities may also deny reciprocal recognition of the ULC's legal existence.

Director personal liability: Where the veil of incorporation is pierced by reason of fraud or sham, the U.S. shareholders and Canadian directors may be held directly liable for corporate tax debts, withholding taxes, and damages. Directors can be named personally in CRA collection proceedings and may face travel bans through the Canada Civil Remedies process.
AML and Payment Compliance (Swift LC / TT Wire)

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties: up to C$500,000 for corporations per violation; criminal penalties on conviction including fines and imprisonment up to 5 years; re-export licenses or Marine Cargo Insurance may be revoked; orders suspending business operations can be issued.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and compliance officers can be subject to personal fines up to C$500,000 for willful non-compliance; for serious OCG involvement, the director may be extradited and prosecuted under the Criminal Code with imprisonment up to 10 years, and personal assets can be traced under mutual legal assistance treaties with the U.S.
