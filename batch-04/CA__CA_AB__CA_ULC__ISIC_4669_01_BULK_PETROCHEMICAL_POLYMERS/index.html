Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or blocked-account requirement under Alberta law. A nominal subscription of 100 common shares at CAD 1.00 total is sufficient, and subscribed funds remain freely usable working capital in a Canadian bank account.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - My Business Account portal; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under a tax collection agreement with the Province of Alberta.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta ULC, apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using form RC1, either through the CRA's 'Register a Business with CRA' online service or by mail/fax. The same BN is used to enroll for GST/HST, payroll deductions, corporate income tax, and Alberta provincial corporate income tax. Non-resident ULCs must provide information regarding Canadian directors and place of business; a Canadian resident agent may be required for service. Registration for GST/HST is mandatory immediately if the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier, or if it solicits business in Canada; otherwise registration may be voluntary to claim input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The Alberta ULC is taxed as a corporation under the Canadian Income Tax Act; no Canadian 'check-the-box' or entity classification election exists for ULCs. For U.S. federal tax purposes only, the ULC may be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership if the owner files a Form 8832/late check-the-box election, but such U.S. classification does not alter Canadian CRA entity-level taxation. No hybrid mismatch election is available under Canadian domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies in the last four consecutive calendar quarters (or in a single calendar quarter for a new business), unless the entity is a tax-recovery registrant or is already required to register due to non-resident solicitation. Charities and public institutions have a CAD 50,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of virgin polyethylene/polypropylene granules is zero-rated as a commercial product if the supplier transfers possession or ownership of the goods to a non-resident and the goods are shipped by the supplier or by the non-resident purchaser to a destination outside Canada, or exported under CRA-approved export programs for distribution. The supplier must retain contemporaneous evidence showing that the goods were actually delivered outside Canada within the required time and not subsequently consumed in Canada.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration submitted electronically through CBSA eManifest / Canadian Export Reporting System (SES)
  • Bill of Lading or other shipping document evidencing export from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order
  • Proof of settlement / foreign currency receipt (SWIFT MT103, bank advice) where the sale price is in USD
  • Cargo or container tracking report showing arrival at foreign port

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-clearance of invoices exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants must issue a valid tax invoice containing prescribed information (supplier name, BN, GST registration number, taxable supplies, tax rate and amount). The CRA requires electronic records to be retained in a readable format for six years and may perform real-time access audits to assess evidence of export.

The CRA may disallow any zero-rating claim and reassess GST at 5% if the exporter cannot provide sufficient documentary proof of export; interest and penalties apply from the original filing date. Knowingly making false statements with intent to evade tax under the Excise Tax Act may attract civil penalties of up to 25% of the tax sought, and tax evasion charges can lead to fines of up to 200% of the tax avoided and imprisonment. Exporters should maintain a six-year documentary retention period to mitigate clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar and foreign currencies are fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII obligations. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted; the only compliance hurdles are Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (subject to treaty relief) and standard corporate documentation supporting the distribution. There is no requirement for ministerial FX approval or evidence of genuine trade for conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not mandate surrender, conversion, repatriation, or minimum holding timelines for export receipts. A ULC may retain export proceeds in foreign currency. Cross-border currency and monetary instrument movement in excess of CAD 10,000 must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency / FINTRAC, otherwise no statutory conversion ratio or surrender timeline exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange surveillance and integrity oversight are shared with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange administration and imposes no exchange control licensing regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low. A non-resident or unlicensed electronic money institution cannot serve as a primary treasury rail for this ULC because it lacks Canadian MSB registration under FINTRAC, Retail Payment Activities Act licensing, SWIFT correspondent access, LC issuance powers, and LVTS clearing membership. Canadian-licensed fintech platforms may support secondary expense payments, but for bulk petrochemical polymer settlements and documentary credits, Tier 1 bank rails remain legally and operationally necessary.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory local file threshold is prescribed by statute; however, CRA requires contemporaneous documentation to be available for any non-arm's-length cross-border transaction. Penalty risk begins when the net transfer pricing adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 1 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in certain offshore jurisdictions. The CRA uses the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s. 245 to disregard transactions without genuine business purpose, and treaty benefits require the ULC to have real officers, offices, bank accounts, and commercial decisions in Alberta.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Invoicing & Revenue Concealment

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s. 239(2) (evasion), max fine of CAD 100,000 and up to 5 years imprisonment (indictable); summary conviction fine of CAD 25,000 to CAD 100,000 and up to 2 years imprisonment. Civil penalties include gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax plus compound interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under s. 227.1 for withheld source deductions; if they directed or participated in the fraudulent scheme, they may be jointly and severally liable for the full corporate tax arrears, face asset seizure orders, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Export Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: The CRA recharacterizes the margin under s. 247(2); a transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the gross adjustment applies if the net adjustment exceeds the threshold (lesser of CAD 5 million or 10% gross revenue). Interest and gross negligence penalties can be applied concurrently.

Director personal liability: Directors who knew or authorized the mispricing are subject to a penalty of 10% of the adjustment under s. 247.3, and can be named as third parties in proceedings with asset freezes; criminal referral may follow if documentation was fabricating.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245) denies the transaction benefit: dividends may be recharacterized as taxable paid-up capital, treaty benefits denied, and Part XIII WHT is reassessed at 25%; a GAAR penalty of 50% of the tax benefit applies. The entity may also be de-listed as a resident for DTA purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for gross negligence penalties if they caused the entity to advance the sham structure; CRA may extend liability to personal assets, including bank accounts and real estate, and issue a certificate of arrest for travel outside Canada.
AML & Payment Fanning via Trade Finance

Statutory sanction: Failure to report suspicious transactions can trigger fines up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 5 million for financial entities, per FINTRAC and Financial Institution Statutes. For a medium-risk petrochemical trader, false structuring and trade-based money laundering may attract additional criminal charges.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined or sentenced for misrepresentation to FINTRAC; personal liability extends to civil forfeiture proceedings under the Alberta Civil Forfeiture Act if the funds originated from criminal activity.
