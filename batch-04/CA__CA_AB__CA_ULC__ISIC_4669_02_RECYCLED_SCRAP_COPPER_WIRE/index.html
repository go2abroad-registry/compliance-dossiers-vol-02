Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; registration via Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and account administration through My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs/export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) within the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) platform. The Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) handles ULC incorporation records.

E-government filing portal

An Alberta ULC obtains its federal Business Number (BN) and the required program accounts (RC for corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST, RM for import/export) from the CRA. Procedure: (1) incorporate or continue the ULC under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) via the Alberta Corporate Registry and file the initial Alberta annual return; (2) register the federal BN online through the BRO portal or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'; (3) activate the RT (GST/HST) program account, which is mandatory once CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada is exceeded; a non-resident-owned ULC must be prepared to post security (surety bond, irrevocable letter of credit, or cash) under ETA s. 241(1) where the Minister requires; (4) activate the RM import/export program account, enroll in CERS/CARM, and either obtain approved-exporter status or authorize a licensed customs broker/freight forwarder for electronic export declarations; (5) file the T2 corporate income tax return within six months after fiscal year-end and identify non-resident ownership schedules. Independently, the scrap-metal dealing activity requires a dealer licence under Alberta's Scrap Metals Dealers and Recyclers Act before commercial operations may commence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — For Canadian federal and Alberta income tax purposes, the Alberta ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation subject to entity-level Part I corporate income tax and Alberta corporate income tax (Alberta general rate 8%; small-business rate 2% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income; no Alberta capital tax or provincial sales tax). For US federal tax purposes, the Alberta ULC is not a per se corporation and is eligible, by election, for pass-through classification (disregarded entity or partnership), producing a cross-border hybrid entity.

Available election forms

(a) US Form 8832 check-the-box entity classification election, the controlling hybrid election, allowing a single-member ULC to be treated as a disregarded entity and a multi-member ULC as a partnership for US federal tax purposes; (b) Canada-US Tax Treaty Article IV(6) fiscally transparent entity treatment, permitting the US owner to claim treaty benefits on a look-through basis, subject to the hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss. 94.3 and 18.6; (c) ETA s. 156 election for supplies of scrap or logistics services between the ULC and a closely related Canadian corporation to be treated as made for nil consideration; (d) ETA s. 150 election to align the GST/HST reporting period with the ULC's fiscal year.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a fully convertible, free-floating currency; Canada observes IMF Article VIII and maintains no restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions and no capital controls. The Alberta ULC may freely remit dividends, royalties, management and franchise fees, interest on shareholder loans, and trade settlements to foreign parties without central-bank approval. Repatriation barriers are exclusively tax-driven under the Income Tax Act (Canada): non-resident withholding tax on dividends is normally 25%, treaty-reducible to 5% for a 10% corporate shareholder or 15% for portfolio holdings under the Canada-U.S. Income Tax Convention, with further optimization available if the ULC is treated as disregarded or flow-through for U.S. federal tax purposes. Intercompany pricing and thin capitalization must respect section 247 and the relevant Part XIII evidentiary requirements; cross-border wires of CAD 10,000 or more are informationally reportable to FINTRAC, but no exchange licence, quota, parity band, or conversion-ratio regime applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None statutory. Canada imposes no mandatory sale or surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion into CAD, and no deadline for repatriation of foreign-currency receipts. The ULC may retain shredded-copper-scrap sale proceeds in USD or other foreign currency in a Canadian bank foreign-currency account, convert them at wholesale market rates at any time, or apply them directly to pay foreign suppliers, smelters, and freight costs. The controlling administrative requirements are informational: FINTRAC large-value transaction records for every wire of CAD 10,000 or more; CBSA cross-border currency and monetary-instrument declarations at the CAD 10,000 threshold; B13A export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System covering the consignment value and net mass; and the Statistics Canada export reporting questionnaire. No statutory conversion ratio exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act, is the central monetary, exchange-rate, and foreign-reserve authority; exchange-rate policy and the Exchange Fund Account rest with the Minister of Finance, with the Bank of Canada acting as fiscal agent. Banking safety-and-soundness and licensed-institution supervision is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and cross-border transaction surveillance is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); customs and export controls are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) jointly with Global Affairs Canada. Canada has no separate central foreign-exchange control authority equivalent to a State Administration of Foreign Exchange.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for a secondary liquidity and FX-receivables channel; Low-Medium for the primary treasury spine. A fintech or electronic-money institution serving Canada must be registered with FINTRAC as a money service business and, if providing retail payment services, must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act, and must clear through a licensed Canadian bank or an appropriately licensed foreign bank with a Canadian nexus. The Alberta ULC may lawfully open an EMI account (for example Wise, Airwallex, or similar digital platforms) for small receipts and for optimized FX spread at volumes below roughly CAD 500,000 per month. However, an EMI account is not a deposit account under the Bank Act, is not CDIC-insured, cannot serve as the issuing, confirming, or advising bank for a documentary letter of credit, and cannot lawfully hold a segregated capital-escrow balance with trust-like protection. EMI rails also tend to trigger enhanced due-diligence queries where the underlying settlement is physical scrap, and they generally do not provide a complete SWIFT gpi UETR trail or a direct correspondent-banking relationship with a trade-finance desk. The blueprint therefore mandates that the ULC maintain its primary operational, escrow, documentary-credit, and FX-hedging accounts at a Tier-1 Schedule I bank, with an EMI used only under a board-approved ancillary treasury policy and with funds moved through the same-day LVTS or ACH network to avoid breaching FINTRAC and bank concentration thresholds.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Sector-based red flag: scrap and waste trading is classified Medium-Risk Enhanced because of high value-to-weight ratios, shredder and teardown ambiguity, mixed-material provenance, and the use of cash-heavy second-hand markets.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23.0% combined standard combined rate (15% federal general corporate rate + 8% Alberta provincial flat rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable Canadian dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder (including any deemed dividend arising on a ULC redemption/repurchase of shares). The rate is applicable on the gross dividend amount unless a treaty reduces it.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the taxation year end. Example: a December 31 year-end files by June 30 of the following year. The balance of tax payable is due no later than 3 months after the tax year-end for all corporations other than CCPCs (CCPCs must pay within 3 months, but certain related corporations and small CCPCs may be eligible for the 6-month payment extension for amounts in excess of the prior-year instalment threshold).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory minimum transaction threshold requiring contemporaneous TP documentation under Canadian law (ITA s.247(3)). The Canada Revenue Agency expects all taxpayers engaged in cross-border non-arm's length transactions to prepare complete and accurate documentation, sufficient to earn a penalty waiver, reflecting 10 comparable years; a T106 return is mandatory for any particular transaction exceeding CAD 50,000. Canada's TCP 'Local File'/'Master File' framework applies to taxpayers with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 50 million or cross-border related-party transactions exceeding CAD 1 million, equivalent to CAD 1,000,000 (as per CRA administrative practice mirroring G20 final report; no explicit SEP). For smaller companies, CRA uses a materiality threshold of CAD 50,000 per transaction and public companies must file a Schedule 88.

Economic substance scope: Although Canada does not have a general Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code similar to the UAE, the principles of economic substance are embedded in Canadian income tax law through the concept of 'carrying on business', the 'sham doctrine', 'ineffective transactions' under GAAR (s.245 ITA), and the non-resident trust attribution rules (s.94). An Alberta ULC used solely to invoice scrap copper purchases/sales with no commercial activity will be reassessed under the 'agency/permanent establishment' provisions and is exposed to the misapplication of the treaty's 'business profits' article. CRA will apply a substance-over-form test requiring actual management, personnel, and asset in Canada. For the ULC to claim treaty benefits, it must pass a 'purpose' test under the Canada–US treaty's limitation-on-benefits provisions in Article 29: the Preamble and Ownership/Base Erosion tests require the ULC to have a genuine business in Alberta; a shell ULC with no trading, storage, or contractual enforcement functions is a red flag. ERP on 'commercial circular metals trading' requires the ULC to maintain physical stock records, scale weight certificates, supplier contracts, and negotiate terms from Alberta.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239(1), a fine of CAD 4,000 to 20,000 plus 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. Under s.163(2), civil penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 10% of the understatement (50% if the misstatement was made knowingly; the penalty increases to 50% of tax sought to be evaded where fraud). Full reassessment limitations extended to any time in case of fraud or misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors of substantial gross negligence with CRA issuance of an assessment can be jointly liable under the wire of having participated in the negligence; the CRA will apply 'knowingly, or under circumstances of gross negligence' standard. Criminal prosecution can pierce the corporate veil and cause the director to be personally restored for fines, unremitted source withholding, and Part XIII tax; travel bans under the Extradition act for foreign directors absent from Canada.
Transfer Pricing Penalty and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.247(3), the Canadian taxpayer is subject to a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the amount of net transfer pricing adjustment in excess of the lesser of CAD 50,000 and 10% of the gross adjustments. Where documentation is missing, the penalty may be imposed without the net adjustment threshold. Adjustments also trigger resourcing and potential double taxation unless a corresponding adjustment is obtained under the treaty MAP process.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for transfer pricing penalties, but if the company is insolvent, CRA can claim for the corporate tax debt under the statutory 'directors' liability' rules (s.227.1 ITA) for unremitted Part XIII withholding tax and GST/HST. Directors who deliberately signed false T106 declarations are subject to indictable fraud under s.380 Criminal Code and s.495 ITA for complicity.
Shell Company / Sham / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under s.245 reassessment ignoring the sale agency and eliminating the ULC's gross profit; penalties and interest restored. The company's deduction of expenses is denied; the transaction is reconstructed as if the ULC never existed; non-resident is taxed under force-of-attraction rules. A GAAR penalty of 25% of the transaction benefit (under s.245(5)) applies to the planning. If misrepresentation is found, the Criminal Code prohibits any false document with intent to defraud under s.380 — count as an indictable offence.

Director personal liability: Judicial piercing of the corporate veil where the ULC is an agent or simplicitic instrument will make the non-resident parent directly responsible for Canadian tax under the 'thin capitalization' and 'agency' rules. Directors are personally liable for withholding taxes and for any GST/HST remitted amounts; travel restrictions and asset freezes can follow from a recovery action. For abusive domestic disguised AG, CRA may also issue an immediate 'Canada Revenue Act' assessment and apply a 25% gross-negligence penalty 25% of tax.
AML / Money Laundering for Physical Commodity Trading

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), failure to report suspicious transactions is a summary offence punishable by fine CAD 250,000 and up to 5 years imprisonment for individuals; for corporations, penalties of up to CAD 500,000 per violation. CRA-completed shell company with no commercial substance triggers Qualified Income Tax fraud and the Minister of Public Safety can issue a Certificate of Permanent Realization prohibiting export of gold or copper. Enforcement agencies may require retroactive risk assessment, penalties from Fintrac, and referral to the RCMP International Financial Crimes Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally charged with money laundering under s.462.31 of the Criminal Code if they directed a transaction involving property derived from a designated offence; maximum imprisonment of 10 years. Directors who fail to apply AML safeguards (due diligence, travel risk, and compliance) are exposed to Fintrac's individual penalties of up to CAD 500,000. A travel ban may be issued pending criminal investigation; corporate dissolution is not a shield while assets are in the hands of the de facto controller.
Withholding / Part XIII and T106 Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: An assessment under Part XIII imposes the full 25% withholding tax on the gross amount paid or credited; plus interest at the prescribed rate. A penalty for failure to withhold is 10% of the tax withheld (or 20% day). For T106 late filing, a flat CAD 25 daily penalty up to CAD 2,500, plus CAD 500 for a first inaccurate filing and CAD 1,000 for each subsequent incorrect T106. For a dedicated ECI group, non-compliance can amount to a 'willful non-filing' under s.238, a strict liability offence leading to fine and potential imprisonment for officers.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for the corporation's failure to remit source deductions and Part XIII withholding tax under s.227.1 ITA without any burden to prove due diligence. The corporate veil does not prevent CRA from issuing a Notice of Personal Liability, and the 'due diligence' defence is limited. If the dividend was paid while the corporation was insolvent, a fraudulent conveyance claim may be made against the recipient.
