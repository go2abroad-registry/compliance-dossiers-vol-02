Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

An Alberta corporation must maintain a registered office and records address in Alberta and must have the required registered office, records office and agent arrangements under the Alberta Business Corporations Act. For an Amazon FBA cross-border retail business, maintain evidence of actual management, inventory and logistics arrangements, supplier contracts, customer-support capability, accounting records, Canadian tax registrations where applicable and authorized personnel. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); principal portal is CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and GST/HST NETFILE; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement, with Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration retaining authority over provincial non-income tax programs.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) before completing GST/HST and payroll registration. The standard route is to file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' online through CRA My Business Account or by paper, selecting the required program accounts, including GST/HST, import/export, and corporate income tax where applicable. For an Alberta ULC, incorporation is first completed through the Alberta Corporate Registry, after which the registry-issued corporate access number is used to open the CRA corporate account. Supporting documentation for foreign entities includes registered office information, director and beneficial-owner identification, constitutional documents, proof of establishment in the foreign jurisdiction, and, where applicable, a partnership or corporate authorization under provincial corporate law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — for foreign, particularly United States, tax purposes the Alberta ULC may be eligible for check-the-box transparency as a disregarded entity; however, under the Canadian Income Tax Act the ULC is a resident Canadian corporation owing Part I tax on its worldwide income. Unlimited shareholder liability is a corporate law feature, not a Canadian tax-classification election; a ULC remains fully subject to Canadian entity-level corporate income tax unless relief is available under an applicable tax treaty or specific statutory provision.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a domestic check-the-box election; entity classification is determined by statute and legal form. A US parent may make a separate entity-classification election under United States Treasury Regulation §301.7701-3 for US federal tax purposes, but Canadian tax authorities do not automatically follow that election. Available Canadian tax elections relevant to the ULC structure include the income-tax rollover elections under subsection 85(1), the GST/HST Quick Method for eligible suppliers, and the GST/HST related-party election provisions. Canada also applies statutory hybrid mismatch rules in Part I of the Income Tax Act, which may disallow deductions or recharacterize payments where the ULC or a related hybrid arrangement produces a double deduction or deduction/non-inclusion mismatch.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully floating exchange-rate regime and maintains unrestricted convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions under IMF Article VIII. There is no exchange-control approval requirement for inbound capital injections, foreign direct investment, profit repatriation, dividends, or repayment of shareholder loans. The sole practical hurdles are AML/KYC verification, financial-sanctions screening, mandatory FINTRAC reporting of international electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more, and, where applicable, Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on dividends/interest/royalties, reduced under an applicable tax treaty. Canadian-chartered banks may lawfully hold USD and other foreign-currency accounts, and proceeds from Amazon FBA sales may be retained in foreign currency without any surrender obligation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion of export receipts into CAD. There is no statutory deadline for repatriating export proceeds, and a Canadian exporter may leave funds in offshore or onshore foreign-currency accounts. However, all exports must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency through the Canadian Export Reporting System (Form B13A) when the goods are physically exported, and financial institutions must report every international electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more, or any smaller transfer that is suspected, to FINTRAC. For the marketplace-based FBA seller, export reporting is generally handled by the exporter of record for cross-border shipments, while the seller must maintain transactional documentation to support the stated value of goods in the Amazon disbursement stream.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); for prudential bank supervision, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); for AML/CFT surveillance of money services businesses and international wires, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada has no standalone foreign exchange control administration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from the ultimate parent or shareholders into the Canadian ULC must be wired in the ULC's legal name via a corporate account at a Schedule I Canadian bank using SWIFT MT103. The remitting bank should complete the wire with the full corporate beneficiary name, the ULC's bank account number, and the bank's SWIFT BIC, and the receiving bank should be instructed to apply the funds as 'Direct Investment – Capital Contribution' in the bank's purpose-of-payment classification. In practice, Canada does not prescribe a statutory purpose code, but the bank's wire-transfer form should state 'Investment – Capital Call' and the ULC must provide the capital-call notice, board resolution, shareholder subscription agreement or shareholder loan agreement, source-of-funds evidence, and updated corporate registry extract before the funds are credited. For USD inbound transfers, clearing will occur during the bank's US correspondent or through Fedwire/CHIPS, and for CAD inbound transfers the receiving bank may settle via Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system. All transfers are subject to name screening against OFAC, UNSC, and Canadian sanctions lists, and any international wire of C$10,000 or more must be reported by the financial institution to FINTRAC.Outbound disbursements to overseas Amazon FBA suppliers and logistics providers should be settled using the following clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct open-account telegraphic transfers; (2) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) for high-value transactions requiring documentary compliance; (3) Payments Canada's Lynx RTGS for same-day domestic CAD payments; (4) Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for low-value Canadian-dollar EFT batches; and (5) the fintech EMI's local payment rails for small-value same-currency supplier payments in destination markets. For USD outbound wires, clearing should be executed through the Canadian bank's US correspondent account, CHIPS, or Fedwire. All outbound payment instructions should include the supplier's invoice number, purchase order reference, product harmonised-system commodity code, and a precise purpose description to satisfy FINTRAC reporting, tax withholding review, and anti-sanctions screening. Once the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration/supervisory regime is fully phased in, payment service providers offering Canadian-dollar payment accounts will require Bank of Canada oversight. EMI balances are not CDIC-insured; therefore daily float should be minimised and sweeps to an insured Schedule I bank account should occur each business day.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair-market value of reportable cross-border transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents triggers mandatory Form T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. Country-by-Country Reporting obligations apply to Canadian ultimate parent entities of MNE groups whose consolidated revenue is at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime similar to offshore financial centers; instead, the CRA and Canadian courts apply substance-over-form principles under the general anti-avoidance rule (s.245) and treaty-shopping case law. The ULC must demonstrate real commercial substance in Alberta — including substantive management, active employees, board minutes, operational decision-making, books and records, and risk exposure — or treaty benefits, deductions, and Part XIII rate relief may be denied and the entity recharacterized as a conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Gross Negligence

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239: imprisonment for up to 5 years, fines up to 200% of the evaded tax, and forfeiture of the tax benefit. Civil gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) is 50% of the understated tax plus arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ss.227.1 and 323 for unremitted source deductions, Part XIII tax, GST/HST, and T2 balances if they knew a T2 return was a misrepresentation; CRA may issue director assessment, asset seizure, and a criminal travel ban where evasion charges are laid.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under GAAR (s.245) to disallow treaty exemptions, apply the 25% Part XIII withholding on deemed dividends, add s.162(7) penalties for false information returns, and impose interest from the original return due date. The ULC could be struck off if it is proven to be a sham without genuine commercial operations.

Director personal liability: Personal liability attaches to directors who sign false statutory declarations; the unlimited liability feature of the ULC permits creditors and the CRA to recover corporate tax debts directly from shareholders/directors; courts may impose restitution orders, extraterritorial travel bans, or imprisonment for contempt in fraudulent sham prosecutions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: CRA may reallocate income under s.247(2), deny deductions, and impose a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net upward adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000, maximum CAD 25,000), plus interest and arrears. Excessive interest on shareholder loans is recharacterized as a deemed dividend subject to 25% non-resident withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or authorize false transfer-pricing documentation are personally liable under s.247(5); the CRA may pursue civil enforcement against personal assets, and criminal charges can generate travel restrictions upon court order.
AML/CFT Red Flags (Proceeds of Crime)

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative monetary penalties may reach CAD 100,000 per violation for corporations and CAD 20,000 per officer/director; severe cases can trigger criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code with asset freezing and forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors with direct knowledge or reckless participation in money laundering face up to 10 years imprisonment; personal bank accounts and travel documents may be frozen; criminal forfeiture orders can be entered against director-owned property despite ULC corporate separation.
