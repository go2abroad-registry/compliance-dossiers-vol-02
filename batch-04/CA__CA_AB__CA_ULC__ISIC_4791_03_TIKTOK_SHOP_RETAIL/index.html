Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Alberta Corporate Registry, Service Alberta and Red Tape Reduction, 4th Floor, 10155-102 Street, Edmonton, AB; filings accepted online through the Corporate Registry system or accredited service providers.

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act (Alberta), RSA 2000, c B-9, as amended: ss 5-12 (incorporation and articles), s 15.1 (election to be an Unlimited Liability Corporation), s 101 (registered office), s 113 (directors), s 24 (share consideration). The Canada Business Corporations Act is not applicable; the ULC is a provincial entity.

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (Form 1) filed with the Registrar under the ABCA, together with Form 2 (Notice of Registered Office) and Form 3 (Notice of Directors).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or deposit requirement for incorporation; shares may be issued without par value for consideration fixed by the board per ABCA s 24.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, acting as the sole federal tax collection authority for income tax and GST/HST; Alberta corporate income tax is assessed federally under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; corporate law filings are made with the Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta); CRA portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html (My Business Account).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the unlimited liability corporation (ULC) with Alberta Corporate Registry (or extra-provincially register a BC/NS ULC), obtaining the Alberta organization number. Step 2: Register with CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 to obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) and program accounts, including RT0001 (corporate income tax) and RT0001/RZ identity for GST/HST. Step 3: For non-resident-owned ULCs, use the CRA non-resident simplified GST/HST registration process; non-resident shareholders are not issued separate Canadian TINs, and the ULC's BN is the sole statutory tax identifier. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a provincial ULC is statutorily a 'taxable Canadian corporation' under Income Tax Act s. 89(1), subject to Part I corporate income tax (combined 23% general corporate rate: 15% federal + 8% Alberta) and to full GST/HST registration, filing, and remittance obligations; however, the ULC structure is Pass_Through_Eligible only upon a valid ITA s. 114.1 election, as described below.

Available election forms

Available: (i) Form RC232 election under Income Tax Act subsection 114.1(1), deeming the ULC to be a partnership for Canadian tax purposes where foreign members are taxed on a look-through basis; (ii) United States check-the-box election on Form 8832 under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3, classifying the Canadian ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. federal tax purposes; (iii) domestic hybrid-mismatch rules and Canada-U.S. Treaty limitation-on-benefits provisions operate to prevent double non-taxation from abusive use of the ULC election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding 12 calendar months (general small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 240(2) and s. 242.1); non-resident vendors and digital platform operators (including TikTok Shop as marketplace/distribution platform) become mandatory GST/HST registrants when supplies into Canada reach or exceed C$30,000 in a calendar year, with the simplified non-resident GST/HST regime available regardless of Canadian presence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act where: (i) the ULC is GST/HST-registered; (ii) physical goods sold through TikTok Shop are exported, or made available to the non-resident buyer outside Canada, and are removed from Canada promptly under a genuine contract of sale; (iii) the ULC maintains contemporaneous evidence of export; and (iv) services supplied to non-residents (including digital platform facilitation or offshore advertising) are not made in the course of a GST/HST taxable supply in Canada and are for consumption exclusively outside Canada. Supplies of digital content or goods delivered to TikTok Shop consumers within Canada are standard-rated at 5% and must be included in the GST/HST return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System/CES) for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 or controlled goods
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing export from Canada to the destination country
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank foreign-exchange receipt) evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds
  • TikTok Shop seller payout statements and platform transaction reports identifying buyer jurisdiction and shipment tracking

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based invoice authorization, and no digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Pan-Canadian e-invoice standard 'Riel' (Peppol BIS-based XML); CRA does not pre-clear invoices. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants with annual taxable revenues exceeding C$1 million; all returns must otherwise be filed electronically via CRA's My Business Account or approved third-party software, and payments must be remitted electronically within the statutory due date (generally one month after the end of the monthly/quarterly reporting period).

CRA's digital-economy and non-resident audit teams treat marketplace sellers as high-visibility; zero-rating claims are systematically matched against customs declarations, transport documents, and platform payout data. Failure to repatriate FX proceeds within 180 days is treated as a red flag that the export may be fictitious, triggering GAAR and retrospective clawback. On disqualification, CRA may reassess within four years (or seven years on waiver), disallow the 0% rate, recover the net GST/HST with interest, and impose penalties under ETA s. 285 equal to the greater of C$250 and 25% of the tax amount; criminal prosecution is available under ETA s. 239 and ITA s. 239(1) with fines up to C$100,000 and imprisonment up to five years; all export records and FX remittance evidence must be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement, and there are no exchange control approvals, surrender requirements, or central bank permits required for inbound equity subscriptions, intercompany loans, or outbound trade settlements. Profit remittance and dividend distributions from an Alberta ULC to a non-resident parent or shareholder are freely permitted, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under sections 212 and 215 of the Income Tax Act (Canada). For a ULC that is treated as a taxable Canadian corporation, dividends paid to a U.S. shareholder may qualify for a reduced withholding tax rate under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention if the recipient provides a valid IRS Form W-8BEN or W-8BEN-E certificate of residency. Alberta imposes no separate provincial exchange control or capital repatriation restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada does not impose statutory export proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion into Canadian dollars, or prescribed repatriation timelines. An Alberta exporter may lawfully hold export proceeds in foreign currency accounts at Canadian authorized foreign currency dealers. For tax purposes, the exporter must report foreign currency gains and losses under subsection 39(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada), accrue foreign interest under subsection 12(1)(c), and preserve books, records, export invoices, bank settlement advices, and FX contracts for at least six years pursuant to subsection 230(1) of the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and, for financial-sector conduct and AML supervision, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign exchange control administration; the Bank of Canada operates the national monetary framework while the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act provide the legal basis for the Canadian dollar as legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as a supplementary payment and settlement rail, Low as the primary treasury relationship. A foreign electronic money institution (EMI) or fintech such as Wise Inc. may operate in Canada as a money services business (MSB), requiring registration with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. It cannot accept Canadian deposits or carry on banking activities without a Bank Act authorization, and it cannot directly clear through Payments Canada. Wise B2B is viable for cross-border supplier disbursements and TikTok Shop payout aggregation only in tandem with a Tier-1 Canadian bank account. The Alberta ULC must maintain a Canadian-dollar operating account and a USD foreign-currency sub-account at a Schedule I bank for treasury concentration; fintech rails are used strictly for corridor optimization, not as a liquidity custody layer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital injections from non-resident shareholders should be executed by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the investor's bank to the ULC's Canadian-dollar operating account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The Beneficiary field must identify the ULC legal name; the Remittance Information field must state 'Share Capital Subscription' or 'Paid-up Capital Injection' together with the Alberta Corporate Registry number and the date of the directors' resolution authorizing issuance. Canada requires no exchange control permit; however, if the foreign investor acquires control of an existing Alberta business or the transaction meets the monetary thresholds in the Investment Canada Act, a post-closing investment notification or application for review may be required. The supporting verification package should include board resolutions, share subscription agreements, share certificate ledger, source-of-funds letters from the remitting bank, and Form T106 (if any intercompany cross-border loans or transfers are made). For shareholder loans, a written demand loan agreement with arm's length interest rate is required to avoid deemed interest and Canadian withholding tax consequences.The primary clearing rails for outbound trade disbursements are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct wire settlements to supplier bank accounts in USD, EUR, or CNY, relying on the ULC's foreign currency sub-account and its bank's correspondent network; (ii) documentary letters of credit issued by a Canadian commercial bank subject to UCP 600 for bulk inventory purchases from PRC-based suppliers; (iii) Payments Canada Automated Clearing Settlement System for domestic CAD payables; and (iv) commercial cross-border payout rails such as Wise B2B for low-value supplier payments whose settlement requirements fit fintech corridor rules. Each wire instruction should include the invoice number, purchase order reference, and ISIC/NAICS business descriptor. For supplier disbursements involving Chinese renminbi, the Canadian bank may require a non-deliverable forward or CNH account arrangement because CAD/CNY convertibility of direct CNY funds is subject to corresponding bank availability. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% (15% federal general rate + 8% Alberta general corporate rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by an Alberta ULC to a non-resident person is 25%. Treaty relief may reduce the rate depending on the parent’s jurisdiction and ownership percentage (e.g., Canada–China DTA: 15% general, 5% for beneficial owner holding ≥10% of voting shares; Canada–US DTA: 15% general, 5% for beneficial corporate owner holding ≥10% voting rights).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed no later than 6 months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. Balance of tax due is generally due the later of 2 months after year-end or 3 months after year-end for eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations; for non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada, the filing deadline remains 6 months after fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing records (equivalent to a Local File) are mandatory in Canada when the aggregate value of all reportable transactions (purchase, sales, services, interest, royalty) between the Alberta ULC and non-resident non-arm’s length persons in a fiscal year exceeds C$1,000,000. A Master File is required where the Canadian corporation belongs to a multinational enterprise group with consolidated group revenue of at least C$750 million; Country-by-Country reporting follows the same group threshold.

Economic substance scope: Alberta and federal Canada do not apply the UAE/ Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, the Income Tax Act socio-economic anti-avoidance rules (GAAR, s.245) and substance-over-form doctrine effectively require the ULC to maintain meaningful physical and economic presence in Alberta (e.g., real employees, office/warehouse or supply-chain functions, inventory risk, independent board decisions). An artificial transaction without economic substance will be recharacterized, and a ULC used solely as a dormant pass-through shell will be denied treaty benefits and PTEP (post-bound rate) under the substance-based test in s.94.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1) — criminal fine not less than 100% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment for up to 5 years. Civil sanctions under s.163(2) impose a gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the amount of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize, approve, or acquiesce to the filing of a false return may be prosecuted personally for the offence (s.239(1.1)). The court may order a travel ban, cease- and-desist, restitution, and imprisonment of the responsible officer; civil liability under s.160 can also reach the director as a transferee.
Shell Company / Artificial Avoidance / GAAR

Statutory sanction: s.245(2) GAAR denying tax-benefit recharacterization (the transaction will be taxed as if the substantive step had not occurred), additional fines/interest, and transfer-pricing penalties under s.247(9). CRA can also reclassify the ULC as a conduit and impose Part XIII withholding on deemed dividends (s.84).

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to s.160 debtor-liability, an administrative assessment as a transferor, and in egregious situations, criminal liability for participation in an avoidance transaction under s.239. Courts may issue injunctions and travel bans and may convert the ULC’s unlimited liability into a personal guarantee of the director (under AB Business Corporations Act an ULC’s shareholders are jointly and severally liable, although directors are not automatically, but dishonesty triggers officer-complicity liability).
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA will re-determine the price under s.247(2); a proportionate penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment is imposed unless the taxpayer has made bona fide, documented, contemporaneous efforts to establish arm’s-length pricing (s.247(9)). The adjustment also accrues arrears interest and may trigger a gross-negligence penalty if concealment is found.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the mispricing are jointly and severally liable for penalties assessed under s.247(9) and may be named in a civil action for restitution. Repeated or fraudulent mispricing can subject officers to criminal tax evasion charges (s.239) and personal tax liens on their property.
Failure to File Information Returns / AML & FINTRAC Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under s.162(7), penalties of C$25 per day (up to C$2,500) for each unreported transaction; under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), a corporation can face administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 per violation, and failure to register as an MSB attracts a separate penalty and stop-re work order.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for FINTRAC penalties, but directors who direct, participate in, or approve non-filing are treated as operating the business under the PCMLTFA and may be personally assessed. A court order may freeze the director’s personal assets as a travel ban until unfiled returns are produced; entrepreneurial disregard of filing obligations can trigger a custody order for contempt of court and imprisonment.
