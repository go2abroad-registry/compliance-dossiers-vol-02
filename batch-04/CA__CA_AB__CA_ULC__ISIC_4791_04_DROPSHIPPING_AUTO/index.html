Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an Alberta ULC. Shares may be issued for lawful consideration as set by the directors; no escrow deposit or paid-up capital account is required for a non-distributing (private) unlimited liability corporation.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for a purely virtual dropshipping operation. If the company later leases office or warehouse space, Alberta OHS Code, fire safety inspection, municipal building permits, and structural load certification will be mandatory before occupancy.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) — official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Alberta provincial registry — Service Alberta Corporate Registry, portal: https://www.alberta.ca/corporate-registry.aspx; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA under a tax-collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Alberta ULC: (1) incorporate through Service Alberta Corporate Registry and obtain the Alberta corporate access number; (2) apply to CRA on Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain CRA Program Accounts) or through Business Registration Online; (3) CRA issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN) as the statutory tax identifier; (4) the same BN is used for the corporate income tax account ('RP') and, where required, the GST/HST account ('RT'), payroll ('RC'), and other CRA program accounts. A foreign resident without a Canadian Social Insurance Number may submit the RC1 by mail or fax with certified copies of incorporation documents and officer identification.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (an Alberta ULC is eligible for U.S. check-the-box pass-through/disregarded classification; under Canadian Income Tax Act Part I the entity remains a separate taxable corporation on its worldwide income, and no Canadian partnership pass-through is available). — For a non-resident-owned Alberta ULC: (1) incorporate through Service Alberta Corporate Registry and obtain the Alberta corporate access number; (2) apply to CRA on Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain CRA Program Accounts) or through Business Registration Online; (3) CRA issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN) as the statutory tax identifier; (4) the same BN is used for the corporate income tax account ('RP') and, where required, the GST/HST account ('RT'), payroll ('RC'), and other CRA program accounts. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (an Alberta ULC is eligible for U.S. check-the-box pass-through/disregarded classification; under Canadian Income Tax Act Part I the entity remains a separate taxable corporation on its worldwide income, and no Canadian partnership pass-through is available).

Available election forms

U.S. check-the-box election under IRS Form 8832 can be made to treat the Canadian ULC as a corporation, partnership, or disregarded entity for U.S. federal tax purposes; no similar election exists under Canadian domestic law. Any hybrid mismatch benefit is subject to Canadian anti-hybrid rules and U.S. GILTI, BEAT, and CFC limitations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD of consideration from taxable supplies made in Canada in the current calendar quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory once the supplier ceases to be a 'small supplier' under the Excise Tax Act. Voluntary registration is available below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act when the goods are physically exported by or on behalf of the supplier, ownership passes outside Canada, and contemporaneous commercial and customs documentation proves export. Services and intangible personal property supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated if the service or intangible is supplied wholly outside Canada or is not for use in Canada, subject to statutory exclusions for real property in Canada, tangible personal property ordinarily located in Canada, and services rendered to individuals physically present in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or approved electronic export declaration where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading / Waybill (original or electronic carrier document showing destination outside Canada)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire-transfer record evidencing payment from the export buyer within 180 days

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, CRA API stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants must issue complete electronic invoices and retain records; GST/HST returns are filed electronically via NETFILE. For exports, CBSA receives electronic export/cargo data (ACI/eManifest) but does not approve commercial invoices before shipment.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims under the GST/HST and may disallow the 0% rate if the exporter fails to maintain or produce the required documentary bundle, causing a retroactive assessment of 5% GST/HST plus daily compound interest. The normal four-year reassessment period is extended in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Civil penalties include false-statement and gross-negligence penalties; CRA may claw back refunds already paid and recommend criminal prosecution for wilful evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains fully free capital and current account convertibility under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and Canada's obligations as an OECD member and IMF Article VIII participant. There is no exchange-control licensing requirement, no restriction on conversion of CAD, and no prior authorization for dividend, profit, or capital repatriation. Cross-border payments remain subject to FINTRAC reporting thresholds and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). An Alberta ULC may remit profits by eligible dividend or return of capital, subject to applicable non-resident withholding tax treaty relief.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender regime. There is no mandatory timeline for repatriation of export receipts, no compulsory conversion ratio into CAD, and no requirement to maintain export proceeds in a designated account. Exporters may retain foreign currency in freely held accounts and convert as a treasury decision.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada for currency policy and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated banking supervision; anti-money-laundering supervision is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI services have Low viability as a primary treasury rail in Canada because Canadian-dollar clearing ultimately requires a Canadian payment services partner or money services business (MSB) registration under FINTRAC. An unregistered offshore EMI cannot independently access Canadian clearing and settlement systems, and banks will generally reject settlement flows from entities without a Canadian regulatory footprint. Feasibility may rise to Medium if the EMI partners with a regulated Canadian MSB or a Schedule I bank and maintains transparent originator information on all SWIFT payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to the Alberta ULC should be routed by SWIFT International Wire with a clear payment narrative referencing the corporate resolution and the purpose of the remittance. Recommended labels include 'capital contribution', 'shareholder loan', or 'working capital advance' under a written funding agreement. Because Canada has no exchange control approval system, no prior central bank filing is required, but the receiving bank will apply enhanced verification, and all international wires over CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC. Foreign-currency inbound transfers can be converted to CAD freely at commercial FX rates.Outbound trade disbursements are typically cleared by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for high-frequency, low-value supplier and logistics payments, and by commercial documentary letters of credit for bulk inventory purchases. The ULC may freely choose settlement currency. All outbound SWIFT transactions over CAD 10,000 trigger FINTRAC EFT reporting, and sanctions screening obligations must be satisfied before any payment.
