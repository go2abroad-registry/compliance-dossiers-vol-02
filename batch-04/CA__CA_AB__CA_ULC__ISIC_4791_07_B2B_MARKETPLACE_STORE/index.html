Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal corporate income tax and GST/HST; Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the Canada-Alberta tax collection agreement. Primary portal: CRA My Business Account (canada.ca/my-cra-account); provincial tax-specific contact: Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration, Alberta Finance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign ULC must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 or through the CRA's online Register a Business service. The entity must provide its jurisdiction of incorporation, Alberta corporate registry number, registered office address, full English legal name, principal business activity (NAICS/ISIC 425120/4791), and the responsible officer's identity. CRA assigns the 9-digit BN with an RT program account for GST/HST; the same BN covers federal and Alberta corporate income tax accounts. Non-residents should use the CRA non-resident business number registration path and supply their foreign tax identification number and permanent establishment information.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (foreign check-the-box classification; for Canadian Income Tax Act purposes the Alberta ULC remains a corporation taxable on worldwide income and is not treated as a partnership or sole proprietorship) — A foreign ULC must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 or through the CRA's online Register a Business service. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (foreign check-the-box classification; for Canadian Income Tax Act purposes the Alberta ULC remains a corporation taxable on worldwide income and is not treated as a partnership or sole proprietorship)

Available election forms

No Canadian domestic check-the-box or partnership election exists for a ULC. Cross-border pass-through status is achieved through an election available under foreign law, e.g., U.S. Form 8832 entity classification election to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership, where permitted by relevant treaty and foreign domestic law. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules, ITA ss. 18.4 to 18.6, do not provide elective treatments; they apply mandatory ordering rules to deny deductions or require inclusions where a hybrid mismatch would otherwise arise.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 worldwide taxable supply threshold under the small supplier rule: registration is mandatory once total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. For non-resident digital/B2B platform operators supplying specified services to Canadian non-registrants, the same CAD 30,000 registration threshold generally applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V when the supplier obtains evidence that the goods were exported by commercial shipment or delivered to a common carrier for delivery outside Canada. Services supplied to a non-resident are generally zero-rated under Part V if the service does not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada. For an electronic B2B wholesale platform, service fees charged to non-resident sellers in connection with the export of goods may qualify as zero-rated offshore services if the underlying supply is not a taxable Canadian supply; intermediary or agency services connected to a taxable Canadian supply may remain subject to 5% GST.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canada Customs Coding Form or CBSA export filing)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and export packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank remittance or export proceeds evidence)
  • Proof of delivery to a destination outside Canada (signed delivery receipt / logistics tracking)
  • Container or sea cargo manifest / electronic export report

No real-time CRA pre-clearance, digital stamp, or fiscal clearance mandate applies in Canada. Electronic invoices and credit notes are accepted if complete, legible, and contain the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act, including the supplier's BN, GST/HST registration number, date, and tax amount. CRA does not mandate PEPPOL, but e-invoicing through business platforms or electronic filing through GST/HST NETFILE is permitted. Records, including source data and e-invoice files, must be retained for six years; on audit, CRA may require format-readable access to systems and transactional data.

CRA may deny zero-rating, claw back input tax credits and refunds, and reassess within the normal four-year period; where misrepresentation, negligence, or fraud is established, the reassessment limitation period is open-ended. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a false statement penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of any net tax understatement or refund overstatement, gross negligence penalties, and interest. Intentional evasion may lead to criminal prosecution and director/shareholder liability for unremitted GST/HST. If the required export evidence is incomplete or fails audit, the 0% rate is reversed to 5% GST with interest from the original reporting period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating, fully convertible currency under the Currency Act with no restrictions on current or capital account transactions for residents or non-residents; profit remittances, dividend distributions, and capital repatriations from an Alberta ULC are unrestricted, subject only to standard withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act (e.g., Part XIII non-resident withholding) and documentary compliance with banking purpose codes; no prior regulatory approval or exchange control permit is required for cross-border transfers denominated in CAD, USD, or any other freely convertible currency

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation of export receipts; exporters and B2B platform operators may retain foreign currency balances in offshore or domestic accounts at their discretion; the only compliance obligations are FINTRAC reporting of transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 or equivalent (large cash transaction reports and electronic funds transfer reports) and accurate record-keeping for tax and customs valuation purposes under the Customs Act and Excise Tax Act

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated deposit-taking institutions; foreign exchange policy coordination resides with the Department of Finance Canada, while FINTRAC administers anti-money laundering surveillance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (conditional). Offshore digital EMI platforms and fintech payment providers (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe) can operate in Canada only through licensed money service business (MSB) registrations with FINTRAC and, where applicable, provincial money lender licensing; these providers are not authorized to take deposits in Canada and cannot serve as the sole banking rail; as ancillary payment and FX settlement vehicles they are viable for B2B marketplace receivable collection, payout disbursement, and cross-border vendor settlement, provided the principal operating and escrow accounts remain with a Schedule I bank; EMIs lacking FINTRAC MSB registration or operating solely under a foreign EMI license (e.g., UK FCA e-money license) face legal constraints in clearing CAD-denominated payments and may trigger enhanced due diligence by correspondent banks; recommended structure is a dual-rail architecture: primary bank accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank for legal settlement, with a registered MSB fintech layer for efficiency in low-value recurring B2B transactions
