Competent corporate registry

Alberta Corporate Registry — Companies Branch, Service Alberta (Ministry of Service Alberta and Red Tape Reduction), Registrar of Corporations, Edmonton, Alberta; registered through the official online corporate registry portal (corporate-registry.service.alberta.ca).

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act (Alberta), RSA 2000, c B-9, as amended — Part 1 and ss. 12 to 19 (articles of incorporation and statutory filing protocol), s. 102 (registered office and records office), ss. 105 and 110 (first directors and board governance), together with the provincial corporate name regulations; the unlimited liability corporation (ULC) designation is reflected in the articles of incorporation and adherence to the unlimited-liability regime.

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (Form 1 under the Business Corporations Act) filed electronically through the Alberta Corporate Registry, culminating in the issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/register-your-business.html) and federal Business Number (BN) system; provincial tax administration for Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under a tax collection agreement with the Government of Alberta.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Alberta ULC must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and, where applicable, a GST/HST account by using CRA Business Registration Online or by completing and submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the Alberta Certificate of Incorporation or registration documents. A non-resident registrant without a Canadian establishment may register under the streamlined non-resident procedure using Form GST20 (Request for Registration for the Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax for Non-Resident Individuals, Trusts and Partnerships), and the CRA may require security or a bond under subsection 240(2.1) of the Excise Tax Act where the non-resident does not have a place of business in Canada. The BN (9 digits) serves as the TIN for corporation tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: For Canadian federal and Alberta income tax purposes the ULC is a resident taxable corporation and is taxed at the entity level (Entity_Level_Taxation), but it is eligible for disregarded-entity or partnership classification under the U.S. check-the-box rules (Treasury Reg. § 301.7701-3) because Alberta ULCs are not limited companies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: For Canadian federal and Alberta income tax purposes the ULC is a resident taxable corporation and is taxed at the entity level (Entity_Level_Taxation), but it is eligible for disregarded-entity or partnership classification under the U.S. check-the-box rules (Treasury Reg. § 301.7701-3) because Alberta ULCs are not limited companies. The entity therefore carries dual-status classification: entity-level taxation domestically and elective transparency for U.S. tax purposes.

Available election forms

(1) U.S. check-the-box election on IRS Form 8832 to treat the Alberta ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership rather than a corporation, which is recognized by the CRA for Canadian tax reporting of the resulting foreign tax attributes; (2) GST/HST streamlined registration election for non-residents under Form GST20, subject to CRA security requirements; (3) group election under Section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for nil-consideration supplies between closely related Canadian corporations, if applicable; (4) no domestic Canadian check-the-box election exists, and hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.4 et seq. of the Income Tax Act (Canada) apply to any resulting deductibility/inclusion mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada over any four consecutive calendar quarters; however, a non-resident person that carries on business in Canada and makes taxable supplies in Canada is required to register unless the person is a small supplier, and the CRA may compel registration and require security for a non-resident registrant that does not have a fixed establishment or place of business in Canada (ETA subsections 240(1), 240(2) and 240(2.1)).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property (subscription box merchandise) is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 of the Excise Tax Act where the recipient obtains the property outside Canada or the property is exported as soon as reasonably possible after the supply, and the supplier retains documentation establishing that the goods were exported within 30 days of delivery or within 60 days where the supplier holds conclusive export evidence; services performed on exported goods, and some services supplied to a non-resident recipient, may also qualify under ETA Schedule VI Part V sections 7-10 if the service relates to exported tangible personal property.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA ACI eManifest / B13A or equivalent electronic export declaration filed with the Canada Border Services Agency)
  • Commercial Invoice and packing slip
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill showing Canada as origin and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign currency receipt evidence (payment from non-resident card processor in FX)
  • Proof of foreign delivery (carrier tracking history, foreign customs clearance record, or signed delivery confirmation)

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping regime under the ETA. CRA operates a voluntary e-invoicing framework aligned with the Peppol network through certified Digital Service Providers; GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed tax invoices containing the supplier's BN, transaction date, a description of the subscription box goods, GST charged (or a clear zero-rated indicator), and the registrant's GST/HST account number, and are required to retain invoices and supporting export evidence in a readable and accessible format.

Where the required proof-of-export documentation is not obtained in a timely manner, the supply is retroactively re-characterized as taxable in Canada, and the CRA may assess unreported GST, disallow input tax credits, and apply interest on the net tax deficiency; intentional or grossly negligent conduct attracts a civil false-statement penalty equal to 50% of the understated net tax under ETA section 285.1, a minimum CAD 250 penalty under ETA section 280 for knowingly making a false statement or evading collection, summary-conviction fines up to CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 12 months under ETA subsection 280(1), and potential director liability for unreported GST/HST under ETA section 323.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains fully liberalized current and capital account convertibility. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely in global markets with no exchange control framework, no prior approval thresholds for foreign investment (except sector-specific national security reviews), and no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend repatriation, or capital repatriation. For an Alberta ULC, dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are permitted; the only applicable requirement is strict compliance with Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (currently 25% on gross dividends, treaty-reduced as applicable) and transfer pricing documentation. Remittances can be executed in CAD, USD, or any freely convertible currency at the commercial rate offered by Schedule I banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, mandatory conversion timeline, or repatriation timeline exists for export proceeds in Canada. Export receipts can be retained indefinitely in foreign currency, sold forward, or converted at the exporter’s commercial discretion. Every exporter must simply maintain adequate books and records for foreign currency transactions under the Income Tax Act (Canada), and the CRA may require the use of the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate for tax reporting purposes, but there is no regulatory obligation to convert export settlements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined federal (15%) and Alberta (8%) general corporate rate; a ULC eligible for and filing the partnership election is fiscally transparent and pays no entity-level corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents. Under the Canada-US Income Tax Treaty, the rate is reduced to 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for a corporate shareholder that owns at least 10% of the voting stock. A partnership-elected ULC does not pay dividends; instead, distributions to non-resident members follow partnership allocation rules and are not subject to Part XIII on distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within six months after each taxation year-end; for a December 31 year-end, the deadline is June 30. Quarterly tax instalments are due on the 15th day of the month following each calendar quarter if the corporation's net tax exceeds CAD 3,000.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar fiscal period the T5013 must be filed by March 31; for a non-calendar fiscal period, by the 15th day of the fifth month after the end of the fiscal period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate controlled transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents in a taxation year; this threshold triggers the mandatory Form T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. Canadian practice follows OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, and CRA expects Master File and Local File documentation to the extent that controlled transactions exceed this de minimis threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate annual Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) filing, but economic substance remains essential: CRA may apply the general anti-avoidance rule (section 245) to disregard tax-motivated transactions lacking commercial substance, and the Canada-US Treaty Limitation-on-Benefits clause requires substantive business operations, not a paper ULC shell. A subscription box ULC must demonstrate real employees, premises, inventory risk, and strategic decision-making in Canada to prevent treaty-shopping reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act subsection 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Civil reassessment, gross-negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax, plus compound daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize or participate in evasion face personal fines, imprisonment, and civil restitution. In a ULC, shareholders also have unlimited liability for the company's tax debts; CRA may assert director liability for unremitted GST/HST and source deductions under subsection 227.1.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: CRA can invalidate the arrangement under the sham doctrine or section 245 GAAR, recharacterize the ULC's income as taxable dividends, deny foreign tax credits, apply subsection 163(2) penalties and interest, and impose Part XIII withholding tax on distributions that were previously treated as tax-transparent. Foreign courts may also deny treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham or substance-less ULC may be assessed personally for the corporation's unpaid tax liabilities; travel bans can be issued under CRA enforcement procedures for large assessments; directors face civil suits from minority shareholders or creditors, and the unlimited liability feature permits CRA to pierce the veil and pursue shareholders directly.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 500,000 per violation for non-compliance with registration, compliance program, record keeping, or transaction reporting requirements. Criminal money laundering is punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years and fines at the discretion of the court.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly participate in, or willfully fail to prevent, AML non-compliance can be prosecuted personally; the court may impose personal fines, issue compliance orders, and asset freezes. ULC shareholders remain liable for any resulting corporate penalties, and a conviction may lead to immigration inadmissibility and travel restrictions.
