Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST portal: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA on behalf of Alberta Finance under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity selling taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and a separate GST/HST account. Registration is made electronically through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) using Form RC1 (General Application) or Form RC460 (Non-Resident Corporation Application), supported by certified incorporation documents, registered alien address or Canadian agent, director/officer details, and the intended date of commercial activity. If the entity is not a small supplier, registration is mandatory before making taxable supplies. For an Alberta ULC, no separate provincial sales tax account is required because Alberta has no provincial sales tax; Alberta corporate tax registration is consolidated with the federal BN under the Alberta Corporate Income Tax Return (AT1).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity selling taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and a separate GST/HST account. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election exists; an unlimited liability corporation (ULC) is always a taxable Canadian corporation subject to entity-level federal and Alberta corporate income tax. For U.S. federal tax purposes, the ULC may be classified as a disregarded entity or partnership under IRC Section 7701/IRS Form 8832, but that foreign election has no effect on Canadian domestic taxation. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act (including CRA folio treatments under the OECD BEPS framework) may apply to Canadian-resident entities and cross-border hybrid instruments, but there is no elective regime for the ULC to opt into pass-through taxation in Canada.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies made in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act Section 148). Non-resident vendors of digital/e-commerce services, including online resale platforms, must register for the simplified non-resident GST/HST regime once taxable Canadian supplies exceed CAD 30,000; a single platform or marketplace may be liable for collection on behalf of third-party sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the goods are supplied by way of sale, possession passes, and the goods are delivered outside Canada by the vendor or the vendor's agent within specified time frames. Services are zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident person and not directly related to Canadian real property or Canadian tangible property, and the service is not performed in Canada for a non-resident who is present in Canada at the time. For online luxury consignment and secondhand goods, the vendor must prove physical exportation of the goods, maintain contracts with non-resident customers, and show safe delivery outside Canada. Digital services and performance obligations delivered electronically may qualify for zero-rating if the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not enjoyed in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Declaration via CBSA, forms B13A or Canada Border Services Agency electronic reporting)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill showing foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice with export indication
  • Proof of payment / Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer record evidencing foreign currency settlement
  • Packing list and insurance documentation (where applicable)

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-connected e-invoicing for GST/HST. Taxpayers may issue electronic invoices; the CRA requires full, supporting records in English or French, including sales, purchases, disbursement, and tax data. Large businesses may use EDI or XML-based e-invoicing voluntarily. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or through financial institution php services; no clearance from CRA is required before issuing an invoice.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export within a 4-year normal reassessment period, extendable to 10 years in cases of fraud, neglect, or misrepresentation. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include: (i) failure to register penalties of up to 15% of the unremitted GST; (ii) false statements or omissions penalties of 10% of the tax paid or claimed, limited to the amount involved; (iii) gross negligence penalties up to 50% of the underestimated tax liability or overclaimed rebate; and (iv) interest at daily compounded prescribed rates on all unpaid GST/HST liabilities. Where CRA determines that claimed zero-rating was fraudulent or unsupported, the full GST at 5% is clawed back from the date of supply, plus interest and penalties, and criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act or Criminal Code may be pursued for deliberate falsification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD) on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Neither the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, nor the Income Tax Act imposes registration, licensing, or approval requirements for inward equity injections, dividend distributions, or repatriation of capital. Profit remittance encounters no exchange-control hurdle; the sole fiscal friction is Part XIII withholding tax of 25% on dividends paid to non-residents, potentially reduced to 5%-15% under an applicable bilateral income tax treaty (e.g., the Canada-United States convention), which is administered through bank documentation such as NR301 or W-8BEN-E forms rather than through FX administration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export receipts. A Canadian entity may retain foreign-currency receivables indefinitely in domestic foreign-currency accounts or external bank accounts, and may freely elect the settlement currency. The only obligation is statistical: cross-border transactions above prescribed thresholds must be reported to Statistics Canada through the balance-of-payments survey routed via the entity's bank; this reporting is informational only and does not restrict timing, amount, or currency of settlement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), which operates a free-floating exchange-rate regime and administers no exchange-control licensing; prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money-laundering oversight of all cross-border payment and remittance activity is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. A foreign-regulated fintech EMI (e.g., Wise or Airwallex) may be used only as an auxiliary payment corridor, never as the primary treasury or escrow settlement account. Any EMI carrying on remittance, FX-dealing, or money-transfer business in Canada must separately hold a FINTRAC Money Service Business (MSB) registration and maintain a Canadian AML/ATF compliance program; provincial regimes (e.g., Ontario MSB licensing) may add further conditions. For a Medium-Risk-Enhanced luxury-resale entity, EMI throughput limits and source-of-funds freezes on high-ticket items are common, and foreign EMIs cannot deliver licensed escrow, commercial letters of credit, or CAD/USD clearing at Schedule I standards. Recommended structure: a Schedule I Canadian bank account as the treasury spine, with one FINTRAC-registered EMI used solely for low-value cross-border payouts and foreign-currency receivables collection, subject to the ULC's own due diligence on the EMI's FINTRAC/MSB status and sanctions compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from non-resident shareholders settle by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV for aggregated interbank transfers) into the ULC's Capital Escrow Account, with field 50K identifying the remitting shareholder and remittance information citing ISO 20022 category purpose code INTC (intercompany payment) together with the narrative 'inward equity capital contribution for common share subscription'. Canada imposes no exchange-control approval or pre-registration requirement. The bank will require (i) a directors' resolution authorizing the share issuance, (ii) the subscription or share-purchase agreement and an updated share register evidencing the funds as equity rather than shareholder debt, and (iii) a funds-received confirmation tagged under the Statistics Canada balance-of-payments classification for inward foreign direct investment (equity). To preserve the ULC's tax-transparent status, escrowed capital must remain segregated from operating cash; disbursement into the operational account is permitted only against documented trade payables and permitted operating expenses. On exit, capital repatriation and dividend distributions remit freely by SWIFT, subject to Part XIII withholding documentation and treaty relief forms.Trade settlements run primarily over SWIFT MT103 single-credit transfers and MT202 COV for high-value interbank payments, each carrying the underlying commercial invoice and contract reference in the remittance information fields. For high-ticket luxury inventory purchases, a Commercial Letter of Credit governed by UCP 600 (or an ISP 98 standby for performance) may be opened in favour of the supplier, payable against presentation of the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or waybill, and an independent authentication report. Because CAD is freely convertible, settlement can occur in CAD, USD, EUR, or the supplier's chosen currency without FX approval. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate tax rate in Alberta (15% federal Part I general rate plus 8% Alberta general corporate rate).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. The balance of tax due is generally payable within two months after the fiscal year-end, with an extension to three months for certain CCPCs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 is mandatory where reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with a single non-resident exceed C$1,000,000 in a taxation year; a Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of C$750,000,000 or more. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared and retained by the Alberta ULC for all material controlled transactions.

Economic substance scope: Alberta has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations, but Canadian courts and CRA will disregard artificial corporate structures under the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in Income Tax Act s.245, the sham doctrine, and treaty abuse rules under the OECD Multilateral Instrument. Real economic substance must include active board decisions in Canada, salaried employees, inventory management, contract negotiation, and consignment platform operations; a ULC with minimal employees or assets is vulnerable to recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; criminal tax evasion under ITA s.239 punishable by a fine equal to 50%-200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years; CRA may reassess without a normal limitation period where misrepresentation is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize or participate in evasion are personally liable for criminal fines and imprisonment; CRA can assert director liability under s.227.1 for unremitted source deductions; courts may impose asset freezes, restitution orders, and travel restrictions pending criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(2) allows CRA to adjust taxable income to arm's-length amounts; ITA s.247(3) imposes a penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where contemporaneous documentation is inadequate or transactions are not arm's length; statutory interest accrues on reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false documentation or incomplete T106 filings may be subject to ITA s.163.2 penalties for participation in a false statement; their personal assets can be used to satisfy judgments where they are privy to deliberate gross negligence.
Shell Company / Artificial Structure / GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA s.245 permits CRA to deny the tax benefit, recharacterize transactions, and reassess; if the arrangement involves a tax treaty benefit, the Principal Purpose Test under the Multilateral Instrument may remove treaty access; CRA penalties and interest attach to the re-determined income.

Director personal liability: In a ULC, shareholders have unlimited liability for the corporation's debts; directors can be personally assessed for unpaid corporate tax withholdings and for knowingly signing returns based on sham documentation; civil fraudulent conveyance actions may pierce any remaining protections.
Anti-Money Laundering / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Failure to comply with Canada's Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Financing Act can result in administrative monetary penalties; money laundering under the Criminal Code is indictable with imprisonment up to ten years; CRA and FINTRAC can freeze accounts and seize assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct or permit evasive AML practices are exposed to criminal prosecution, personal fines, imprisonment, and court-ordered restitution; departure bans and asset freezes may be imposed pending criminal proceedings.
