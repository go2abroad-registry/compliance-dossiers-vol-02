Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) with e-service portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-portal.html). New business registration is also available via the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal. The corporate income tax of Alberta is administered by the CRA under a federal-provincial collection agreement with Alberta Treasury Board and Finance for provincial corporate tax purposes.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the ULC under the Alberta Business Corporations Act through the Alberta Corporate Registry (CORES system), obtaining the incorporation number, articles of incorporation and certificate; no minimum capital is required and non-resident ownership is permitted. Step 2: Obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO), by telephone, or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with certified English or French corporate documents to the CRA International and Non-Resident Taxation Services Office. Step 3: Open program accounts within the BN: RT (GST/HST), RC (corporate income tax), RP (payroll) and RM (import/export). Step 4: Register for GST/HST once the ULC is not a small supplier (taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters), or voluntarily register to recover input tax credits; a non-resident ULC must provide a Canadian address for service and may appoint an authorized GST/HST representative under Excise Tax Act section 273. Step 5: For the Alberta corporate income tax account, no separate provincial registration is required because CRA collects the Alberta tax on behalf of Alberta; the ULC must still comply with Alberta annual return filings at the Corporate Registry.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: For Canadian domestic tax purposes, the ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation resident in Canada subject to entity-level federal Part I tax and Alberta provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income. However, due to its hybrid nature, it is eligible for pass-through treatment at the shareholder level for US federal tax purposes (disregarded entity or partnership by analogy), and Alberta partnership/ULL rules permit unlimited liability flow-through to members. For GST/HST purposes, the ULC is a separate registrant and cannot disregard the GST/HST account.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (1) US federal check-the-box election on Form 8832 to treat the Canadian ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership, which is recognized for Canada-US treaty purposes with respect to US shareholders; (2) Excise Tax Act section 273 election appointing a GST/HST representative for non-resident registrants; (3) Excise Tax Act section 156 election to have supplies between a registrant and a closely related corporation deemed made for nil consideration, enabling GST/HST pooling for related Cosmetics DTC entities; (4) Quick Method of Accounting election (GST/HST) for small suppliers and the Streamlined Accounting Method for sales through a third-party fulfillment center; (5) hybrid mismatch planning where the ULC remains a corporation for CRA purposes but is flow-through for US purposes, subject to applicable CFC and subpart F rules; (6) treaty claims under the Canada-US Tax Treaty for reduced withholding and effective permanent establishment thresholds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 taxable supplies in any single calendar quarter or over the four immediately preceding consecutive calendar quarters (the small supplier threshold). Voluntary registration is available for non-residents and e-commerce exporters to recover GST/HST input tax credits; registration is effective from the date of the application.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The online sale of cosmetics and personal care products shipped from Alberta to a foreign customer qualifies for zero-rating under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 (supply of tangible personal property exported by the supplier) and section 13 (delivery to a common carrier for export), provided the ULC obtains and retains documentary proof of export before filing the relevant GST/HST return or within a reasonable period thereafter. Services supplied entirely outside Canada, including digital beauty consultations and subscription content that is delivered and enjoyed offshore, are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7. The goods must physically leave Canada within a reasonable time under the sale contract; supplies delivered to a Canadian freight forwarder, a Canadian fulfillment hub or a Canadian residential address do not qualify. On a zero-rated export, the ULC recovers the 5% GST embedded in its Canadian purchases through the input tax credit mechanism, resulting in a perfectly refunded export.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A or ACI eManifest export reporting submitted to the Canada Border Services Agency)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / courier manifest (DHL, FedEx, UPS) evidencing the physical shipment from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the cosmetics, quantities, value, and foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, PayPal/Shopify Payments settlement report, or bank statement proving payment in foreign currency and repatriation of funds
  • Customer purchase order or contract containing international delivery terms (Incoterms, e.g., FOB, EXW, CIF) and destination address outside Canada

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time tax clearance, government e-invoicing authorization, API-fiscal stamping or digital clearance regime for B2B or B2C GST/HST invoices. A voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the PEPPOL standard is being adopted from 2023 onwards, with participation mandatory only for certain suppliers to the federal government. Electronic GST/HST return filing (NETFILE) is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable revenues exceeding CAD 1 million; smaller registrants may file electronically voluntarily. The ULC must retain and produce, upon CRA request, a standard GST/HST invoice (BN/RT number, supplier and recipient name, transaction date, description, GST amount, and unique invoice identifier) for a minimum of six years. Payment service providers (Shopify Payments, PayPal) are not required to perform tax pre-clearance on each transaction, but CRA may issue a compliance demand for transaction-level data.

The CRA conducts targeted audits of e-commerce exporters, including beauty DTC retailers, to verify zero-rated export claims. If the mandatory customs documentation bundle is missing, incomplete, or internally inconsistent, CRA will reassess the supply as taxable at 5% GST, disallow the related input tax credits, and charge interest compounded daily on the net tax owing. Penalty exposure includes: (a) ETA s. 280(1) penalty of CAD 100 plus 25% of the tax sought to be evaded for false statements or omissions on a return; (b) ETA s. 285(1) gross negligence penalty of 25% of the net tax understatement; (c) administrative monetary penalties under ETA s. 280.1 for failure to provide required information; and (d) criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 for knowingly evading GST/HST, with fines payable to the Crown and imprisonment of up to five years. The normal GST/HST reassessment period is four years, but where fraud, neglect, carelessness or wilful default is established, reassessment is unlimited in time. Failure to repatriate export sales proceeds within the 180-day benchmark will not automatically invalidate a zero-rating claim under ETA, but CRA will treat it as a serious indicator of unreported taxable Canadian supply and will extend the audit window and apply retrospective interest and penalties on any clawed-back refund.

Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid balance plus 1% per full month (max 12 months) for repeat failures; late payments accrue daily compounded prescribed-rate interest (currently ~8-9%); failure to file Schedule 8/T106 attracts an additional penalty of CAD 100 per day (min CAD 1,000) and potential gross negligence penalties under s. 163(2) of up to 50% of understated tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 gross transaction value per non-arm's length non-resident counterparty triggers mandatory T106 reporting and contemporaneous TP documentation (Local/Master File benchmark study must be prepared within 3 months after fiscal year-end to avoid s. 247(3) penalties).

Economic substance scope: Canada applies GAAR (s. 245 Income Tax Act) and treaty Limitation on Benefits clauses. A ULC retailing cosmetics online must demonstrate Canadian substance: physical inventory storage in Alberta, centralized management of returns/logistics, employee decision-making on pricing and suppliers, board meetings in Canada, and substantive e-commerce contracts. Mere flow-through of funds through a ULC without Alberta-based operational substance risks treaty denial on dividends and GAAR reclassification. No separate ESR filing exists in Canada, but substance documentation is required to defend the corporate residence and beneficial-ownership claims.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Underreporting

Statutory sanction: CRA civil penalty under s. 163(2) of 50% of the tax evaded, plus interest and full reassessment; criminal prosecution under s. 239 ITA for tax evasion: fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; seizure and freeze of bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors personally assessed under s. 227.1 ITA for corporate withholding taxes and GST/HST if the ULC fails to remit; criminal fines can be imposed on the directing mind; travel bans and exit controls under CRA collections; civil restitution and director disqualification in Alberta.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance / Unremitted Part XIII

Statutory sanction: Part XIII tax becomes immediately payable, plus late-planning penalties of 10% of the unremitted amount, daily compounding interest, and potential 50% gross negligence penalty if the omission is considered intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for the full unremitted Part XIII tax, interest, and penalties; CRA may issue a director liability assessment and enforce against personal assets; willful evasion may lead to criminal charges and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure / Inadequate Documentation

Statutory sanction: s. 247(3) applies a 10% penalty on the net transfer pricing adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000) if the taxpayer failed to make reasonable documentation efforts; unreported T106 filings attract CAD 100/day penalties; the underlying tax is re-assessed with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors not automatically joint and severally liable for TP penalties under s. 247, but gross negligence or false statements are subject to s. 163(2)/(3) penalties and director criminal liability under s. 239; CRA may pursue negligent directors in cases of egregious foreign profit shifting.
Shell Entity / Artificial Tax Avoidance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: GAAR under s. 245 disallows the tax benefit and recharacterizes the distribution to the foreign parent; a further misrepresentation penalty of 25% of the benefit may apply where a transaction is attributed to a misrepresentation; the CRA can reassess within 3 to 10 years, disregarding the normal limitation period.

Director personal liability: Directors of a foreign parent that caused the ULC to be a sham may be treated as directing minds; Canadian courts can pierce the ULC's unlimited liability shield, impose restitution for tax avoided, issue travel bans against directors, and in criminal tax evasion cases impose imprisonment up to 5 years.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering (FINTRAC)

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for entities; failure to report a suspicious transaction (STR) leads to penalties of up to CAD 2,000,000 per occurrence for third parties such as banks; criminal offence of laundering under Criminal Code s. 462.31 attracts up to 10 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who directed, authorized, or assented to AML breaches face personal fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 6 months (summary) or 5 years (indictable) under PCMLTFA; asset forfeiture orders and travel bans apply to directors involved in cross-border laundering flows.
