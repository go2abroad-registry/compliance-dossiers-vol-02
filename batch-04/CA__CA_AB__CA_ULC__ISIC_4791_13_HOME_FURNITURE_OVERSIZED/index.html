Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) — federal income tax and GST/HST administration; business portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA). Alberta corporate income tax is also administered by the CRA under the Alberta Tax Administration Agreement, while customs assessment and import GST enforcement are handled by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

After Alberta incorporation via the Alberta Corporate Registry (Registry Connect), the ULC obtains a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN). Domestic applicants file Form RC1 (Business Number Registration) through BRO; non-resident applicants use Form RC464 (Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Registration for Non-Residents), filed with CRA's International and Large Business Directorate by mail or courier. The BN is the sole statutory identifier; program accounts are appended as suffixes: RT for GST/HST, RC for corporate income tax, RP for payroll, and RM for import/export. CRA can set the GST/HST registration date retrospectively to the day taxable supplies first exceeded CAD 30,000, and registered entities must display their BN (RT account) on all invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (default): an Alberta ULC is deemed resident in Canada under ITA s. 250(4)(c) and is taxed under Part I on worldwide income. Pass_Through_Eligible: the ULC may elect under ITA s. 34.1 to be treated as a partnership (deemed not to be a corporation), flowing income out to shareholders. Territorial_Scope: residence-based full taxation with foreign tax credits and treaty relief; non-resident shareholders of a ULC face Part XIII withholding and certain dispositions may attract branch tax analysis. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (default): an Alberta ULC is deemed resident in Canada under ITA s. 250(4)(c) and is taxed under Part I on worldwide income. Pass_Through_Eligible: the ULC may elect under ITA s. 34.1 to be treated as a partnership (deemed not to be a corporation), flowing income out to shareholders. Territorial_Scope: residence-based full taxation with foreign tax credits and treaty relief; non-resident shareholders of a ULC face Part XIII withholding and certain dispositions may attract branch tax analysis. Tax_Exempt_Zone: none for ordinary Alberta e-commerce; Alberta has no provincial sales tax, but the federal 5% GST applies and no income-tax holiday zones are available for this activity.

Available election forms

Alberta ULC s. 34.1 election: the eligible unlimited liability company, jointly with all shareholders, may file a written election with the Minister to be deemed not to be a corporation and to have its business carried on by a partnership composed of its shareholders (pass-through); for GST/HST purposes, the election deems the shareholders to be registrants carrying on the business with joint and several liability. There is no general US-style check-the-box regime in Canadian tax law; however, for US tax purposes the ULC may be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity on Form 8832, and planners must align the US check-the-box result with the ITA s. 34.1 election to avoid hybrid mismatches. The hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.4) and the excess interest and financing expenses limitation rules (ITA ss. 18.2/18.3) further constrain deduction-stuffing in cross-border ULC financing structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: GST/HST registration becomes mandatory when taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or in the aggregate over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; however, under the July 1, 2021 platform-economy rules, marketplace facilitators and non-resident sellers using Canadian fulfillment warehouses are deemed suppliers and must register and collect GST/HST without benefit of the small-supplier exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated (0% GST/HST) treatment applies to the export supply of oversized furniture and household goods under ETA Schedule VI, Part V where the sale is made by a registrant, the goods are delivered to a carrier or freight forwarder for shipment outside Canada, they leave Canada in substantially the same condition, and the supplier retains satisfactory documentary evidence of export; ETA Schedule VI, Part VII separately zero-rates services performed on goods that are being exported (e.g., white-glove preparation, packing, and drayage to the US border). Supplies delivered to a Canadian address, or goods exported by the buyer rather than the seller, are not zero-rated and remain taxable at 5%.

No mandatory real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime applies in Canada. GST/HST invoices require at minimum the supplier name and BN (RT account), recipient name, date, description of supply, consideration, and tax amount (ETA s. 223). E-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable through CRA's Peppol/PINT-based pilot (2024-2026). Import fiscal clearance is electronic through the CBSA Single Window Initiative and the CARM digital platform, with import GST accounted on the customs self-assessment; exporters must file the Canada Export Declaration electronically. Registrants must retain books and records, including underlying export data, for six years, and CRA may require machine-readable formats.

CRA's GST/HST export verification and refund-integrity programs examine export documentation transaction-by-transaction; fabricated or manipulated evidence results in denial of the 0% rate, reassessment and clawback of any refund with arrears interest, civil penalties under ETA ss. 280-285 including false-statement and culpable-conduct penalties, and gross-negligence penalties under ITA s. 163(2); the four-year normal reassessment period is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation is established, and criminal prosecution for evasion (ETA s. 331, Criminal Code fraud) may follow. Because the entity is an Alberta unlimited liability corporation, each shareholder is jointly and severally personally liable for the ULC's GST/HST debts and clawback amounts, including those arising from invalid export zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account transactions with no general restrictions imposed under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, or the International Economic and Financial Sanctions regime. There is no requirement for central bank approval for routine cross-border capital transfers, dividends, or profit remittances, provided the transactions are supported by appropriate documentary evidence and comply with anti-money laundering (AML) and know-your-client (KYC) requirements under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). ULC distributions to non-resident shareholders may be characterized as dividends or deemed taxable dividends by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and are subject to withholding tax under the Canada Income Tax Act, typically reduced to 5% or 0% under an applicable tax treaty if the shareholder is a tax resident of a treaty jurisdiction and provides a valid residency certificate. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal but prudent advance pricing and transfer-pricing documentation are recommended to satisfy CRA's Part XIII withholding and section 247 compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada maintains no statutory requirement for exporters to surrender, sell, or convert export proceeds into CAD within any prescribed timeline. The Income Tax Act and the Customs Act do not impose mandatory conversion ratios on export receipts. Exporters may maintain foreign currency banking accounts with any Canadian banking entity or designated financial institution. All inbound payments must be reported under the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) thresholds and, for statistical purposes, to Statistics Canada via the International Trade Ministerial Reporting Program if transactions exceed CAD 1 million per month. There are no exchange control restrictions on repatriating export proceeds, and hedging instruments are fully accessible.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and Global Affairs Canada for trade-related sanctions export controls; the Department of Finance Canada oversees the Exchange Fund Account and the Canadian dollar exchange rate regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) and fintech payment partners have limited viability for onboarding a Canadian ULC as a primary banking partner due to Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) implemented in November 2024, which mandates Bureau de la concurrence registration with the Bank of Canada (BoC) for any payment service provider operating in or targeting the Canadian market. Offshore EMIs that lack BoC RPAA registration, FINTRAC MSB registration, and physical presence in Canada are legally constrained from processing domestic or cross-border payments directly for a Canadian entity. A practical approach is to use a top-tier Canadian Schedule I bank as the core deposit and clearing bank, and separately onboard a licensed Canadian PSP (e.g., Stripe Canada Ltd., Payoneer Inc. if FINTRAC-registered) solely for e-commerce card settlement and BNPL payment acceptance. The PSP must hold a BoC RPAA registration or operate under an exemption. The ULC must maintain ultimate control over treasury cash flows; funds should be swept continuously to the Schedule I bank clearing account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections into the Alberta ULC should be wired via SWIFT to the designated Tier-1 domestic bank account using the bank's SWIFT BIC and a unique client attribution ID (CA/AB/ULC-style reference). The sender must indicate the appropriate purpose message in the remittance details: either 'Equity injection - capital contribution for general corporate and working capital' (purpose code under the IMF Balance of Payments Manual 6, typically 'D614' for equity capital for direct investment in an enterprise) or 'Loan - shareholder subordinated debt' (code 'D613' for intercompany financing). The bank will request a board resolution approving the capital injection, a shareholder contribution agreement or promissory note for shareholder loans, and a completed FINTRAC 'Large Cash Transaction Report' trigger if payment is over CAD 10,000 or in cash; wire transfers over CAD 30,000 are automatically subjected to FINTRAC catch-all reporting. The ULC must ensure that the incoming wire is not accidentally marked as 'proceeds of trade' for legal goods, which could trigger different anti-money-laundering classification; instead, choose 'equity investment - corporate investment' as legal phraseology. For funds originating from outside Canada, the settlement must clear through a correspondent bank of the remitting institution; a cross-border wire template (SWIFT MT103) with full beneficiary address and account number is mandatory. Verification steps include a written board resolution dated within the last 30 days, financial declarations of the investing entity, and if over CAD 25,000, a Certificate of Compliance under the CRA non-resident entity requirements.For trade settlements related to cross-border procurement of furniture and freight services, the primary clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103 message for high-value and mid-value transfers routed through the ULC's Schedule I Canadian bank correspondent network. For transactions above CAD 10,000, outbound MT103 must include the full legal name of the beneficiary, the beneficiary's account number (IBAN or domestic account), the beneficiary bank's SWIFT BIC, and the corresponding purpose code (e.g., 'EP' for general trade of goods, 'RD' for repayment of loans). A commercial documentary irrevocable letter of credit (L/C) is permissible for high-value supplier purchases, conforming to UCP 600, and can be issued in favour of the foreign manufacturer or freight agent. The cleared L/C reduces direct exposure but adds documentary costs and requires a bank quarantine of the remittance until presentation of compliant documents (invoice, bill of lading, packing list, certificate of origin).
