Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

An Alberta corporation must maintain a registered office in Alberta and a records office capable of receiving legal documents and maintaining statutory records. A virtual-office arrangement may be acceptable for the statutory registered office if it provides a genuine physical Alberta address, reliable service of process, document forwarding, and access to corporate records as required. A post-office box alone is insufficient. For an electronic fast-fashion apparel retailer, a virtual registered office does not by itself establish operational substance, management presence, inventory capability, consumer-law compliance, employment presence, GST/HST registration position, or provincial tax nexus. The company should maintain commercially credible evidence of management, banking, payment processing, supplier arrangements, fulfillment, returns, privacy compliance, product safety, and customer-service operations. Payment processors and banks may reject nominee-only or mailbox-only structures.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html); GST/HST NETFILE, GST/HST REGFILE, and Represent a Client portals. Alberta corporate income tax is administered federally by CRA under the Alberta Tax Administration Agreement; the Provincial Tax and Revenue Administration is not the filing counterparty for corporate income tax or GST/HST.

        
E-government filing portal
For a foreign-owned Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC): (1) Incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (ABCA) and obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number from the Corporate Registry via Corporate Online; (2) apply to CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or through the online BN Registration service; the ULC is a deemed resident corporation for Canadian income tax purposes and must obtain its own BN distinct from any parent; (3) separately activate the GST/HST account on the same BN by indicating the GST/HST program on Form RC1 or by subsequent online election; supply the Alberta registered office address and, if no Canadian presence, complete Form RC151 (GST/HST Application for Non-Resident Registrant) and appoint a Canadian representative or maintain a Canadian address for records; (4) receive confirmation of the 9-digit BN and 15-character program-specific reference number (RT for GST/HST); (5) file the GST/HST return under ETA s. 238 and remit net tax via CRA My Payment or financial institution. Foreign entities cannot obtain a 'TIN' separate from the BN; the BN is the statutory TIN and must appear on all invoices (ETA s. 223(1)).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible - Under Canadian domestic tax law the ULC is a separate taxpayer and a corporation taxed at entity level under ITA s. 134.1; however, under Article IV(7) of the Canada-U.S. Income Tax Convention and IRS Rev. Rul. 98-37, the ULC is fiscally transparent and eligible for U.S. 'check-the-box' pass-through treatment. For Canadian GST/HST purposes, the ULC is a distinct registrant and not eligible for pass-through consolidation absent a GST/HST group election with Canadian-resident members.

        
Available election forms
IRS Form 8832 Check-the-Box election to treat the Canadian ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. federal tax purposes (maximum 75 days retroactive effect); GST/HST group registration election under ETA s. 156 (consolidation of related Canadian registrants); GST/HST annual fiscal-year election under ETA s. 243; no Canadian equivalent to the U.S. check-the-box, and the ULC has no Canadian 'partnership' election; hybrid mismatch elections under the Multilateral Instrument (MLI) and domestic hybrid mismatch rules (ETA and ITA) may require the parent jurisdiction to align characterization.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
5%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CAD 30,000 - the small-supplier threshold based on total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in any four consecutive calendar quarters, excluding supplies of a capital nature. Once exceeded, registration is mandatory within 29 days of the day following the trigger quarter. Non-resident retailers without permanent establishment in Canada may be required to register under the normal foreign-supplier regime, and marketplace platforms (including operators facilitating sales of fast-fashion apparel) are deemed to supply the goods and must collect GST/HST on covered sales regardless of the underlying vendor's small-supplier status.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Export of tangible personal property (including fast-fashion apparel and footwear sold to non-residents and delivered to a destination outside Canada) is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V (sections 1-10), provided the vendor obtains and retains documentary proof of export within a reasonable time, generally within 60 days of the sale or earlier of delivery; services performed for non-resident recipients and not performed in Canada are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VII, but digital supply of services to Canadian-resident consumers remains subject to GST/HST. The vendor must establish the goods left Canada by direct or indirect export and that possession transferred to a non-resident purchaser. Exports via a Canadian fulfillment warehouse destined for foreign delivery may qualify only with proper customs evidence in the exporter of record records; the ULC must not claim zero-rating on goods delivered in Canada, including goods later forwarded internationally by the purchaser.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital invoice validation exists at the federal or Alberta level; CRA does not operate an e-invoicing clearance hub. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records provided they are complete, legible, and support audit verification; a point-of-sale receipt or electronic invoice must show the BN, transaction date, vendor identity, and GST amount. CRA may reassess GST/HST returns within four years of the original assessment, with the limitation period extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or willful omission; where zero-rating is not substantiated, CRA imposes GST at 5% on the consideration, plus net tax interest and penalty (ETA s. 239.1). False statements on applications, invoices, or export documentation attract penalties of CAD 10,000 per count (ETA s. 285(1)) and the greater of false-claim penalties under ETA s. 285(3) equal to 25% of the amount or CAD 100,000; gross negligence penalties under ITA s. 163.2 may also be asserted on income tax. Suspicious export refund claims can trigger CRA verification holds, pre-refund audits, documentation interviews with carriers, and cross-border data exchanges with foreign customs authorities; clawback is retroactive to the supply date and accelerated with interest accruing at prescribed rates compounded daily. Criminal prosecution may be referred to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada for willful evasion.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Canadian dollar is freely floating with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. Canada maintains no exchange controls, no central bank approval for inbound equity, and no general profit-remittance barriers beyond standard corporate solvency and board-approval requirements. The Income Tax Act (Canada) imposes Part XIII withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees paid by a resident corporation to non-residents (generally 25% absent treaty relief); under the Canada-US Tax Treaty (1980), the withholding rate on dividends is reduced to 5% when the US shareholder owns at least 10% of the voting shares of the Alberta ULC. Capital contributions to the ULC in the form of paid-up capital or contributed surplus are not subject to Part XIII withholding and are recorded on CRA Schedule 50 for paid-up capital reconciliation.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; the Alberta ULC may lawfully retain foreign currency in offshore or onshore accounts. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) requires electronic export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System for goods shipped from Canada valued at or above CAD 2,000. For treasury purposes, foreign-source income and foreign affiliate interests must be reported to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) through Form T1134 (return relating to controlled and not-controlled foreign affiliates) and Form T1135 (foreign income verification statement) when ownership or holdings meet the statutory thresholds.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign exchange oversight coordinated by the Department of Finance Canada, prudential banking regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Non-resident shareholders transmit CAD or USD by SWIFT MT103 wire with Field 70 remittance information stating 'Shareholder Capital Contribution' and, for direct investment equity, purpose code 1220. The funds are screened against OFAC, OSFI, UN, and EU sanctions lists and credited to the BMO Capital Escrow Account; upon receipt of the executed shareholder resolution, updated corporate register, and signed CRA Schedule 50 paid-up-capital reconciliation, the funds are transferred through Lynx (Payments Canada real-time settlement) to the operational account. No Part XIII withholding or GST/HST applies to equity contributions; the transaction must be booked as contributed surplus or paid-up capital rather than shareholder debt to avoid thin-capitalization rules and the recharacterization of interest as a deemed dividend.Fast-fashion inventory supplier settlements use SWIFT MT103 open-account transfers supported by commercial invoices, packing lists, and bills of lading; purchase orders exceeding CAD 100,000 are routed through commercial letters of credit (L/C) advised by the RBC or TD trade finance desk to the supplier's confirming bank. Domestic payables clear through Payments Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR) for sub-CAD 1,000 disbursements and Lynx for high-value instructions. Cross-border payments to Chinese fabric and garment suppliers may be settled in RMB through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) via correspondent banks or in USD through New York correspondent clearing, subject to the bank's country-limits policy. Klarna and Shopify Payments merchant settlements are received by ACH or direct deposit into the CIBC operating account in CAD, with automatic conversion of USD proceeds at the bank's daily corporate FX rate.Deliverable FX forward contracts in CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/CNY executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Canadian Schedule I bank, subject to CSA Derivatives Reporting Regulations requiring trade reporting to a recognized trade repository.Non-deliverable FX forward (NDF) contracts for restricted or non-deliverable currencies in the sourcing corridor (e.g., certain Asian emerging-market currencies), documented under an ISDA Novation and Credit Support Annex with mark-to-market collateral posting.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps) for CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/CNY notional conversion on tenors of up to 12 months, documented under the ISDA Master Agreement and designated for hedge accounting under IFRS 9 where applicable.FX options (vanilla calls, puts, and collars) to hedge procurement cost and revenue volatility, with premiums settled in CAD and positions marked to market under the mark-to-market rules of the Income Tax Act subsection 142.6(1) and reported to the bank's treasury risk committee.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital electronic money institutions such as Wise Business, Payoneer, and Airwallex are classified in Canada as money services businesses (MSBs) rather than as Schedule I banks; they must register with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, are not members of Payments Canada, and do not provide Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) coverage. They are legally viable for ancillary cross-border collections, supplier payouts, and FX conversion, but the primary treasury function (capital escrow, Lynx clearing, credit facilities, hedging, and trade finance) must be maintained with a domestic Schedule I bank to achieve regulatory and counterparty integrity.
