Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada. Single federal tax authority for corporate income tax, GST/HST, and Alberta's provincial corporate income tax (which the CRA administers under a collection agreement with Alberta; Alberta levies no provincial sales tax). Operational portal: CRA My Business Account at canada.ca/cra-my-business-account; GST/HST NETFILE; corporate T2 e-file mandate applies. Ancillary registry: Alberta Corporate Registry for ULC incorporation, extra-provincial registration, and annual registry returns.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the ULC in Alberta (or import a BC/other provincial ULC into Alberta) through the Alberta Corporate Registry; the Business Number (BN) of 9 digits is automatically assigned by the CRA via integrated registration. Step 2 — Activate the GST/HST program account (RM prefix) online through My Business Account or by filing Form RC1A; the CRA issues the 15-character GST/HST registration number. Step 3 — For a ULC incorporated outside Alberta, file for extra-provincial registration with the Alberta Corporate Registry, obtain the Alberta Corporate Access/registry file number, and file the Alberta annual return. Step 4 — Register with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for an exporter account (for U.S./foreign export clearance and B13A declarations). Step 5 — File the T2 corporate income tax return (mandatory e-file) covering both federal Part I tax and Alberta corporate income tax (Alberta schedules embedded in T2); keep trade name, registered office, and directors updated with both the CRA and Alberta Registry. Step 6 — For U.S.-parented ULCs, the U.S. owner may concurrently file IRS Form 8832 (check-the-box) and obtain a U.S. EIN, but Canadian registration remains that of a separate taxable corporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the ULC is a separate person and a taxable Canadian corporation under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 248(1); it is not fiscally transparent in Canada. Taxed at federal Part I general rate of 15% and Alberta corporate income tax of 8% (23% combined) on worldwide income unless a treaty/foreign tax credit applies; the small-business rate (9% federal + 2% Alberta on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income) is available only if the ULC is a Canadian-controlled private corporation. For GST/HST, the ULC is a separate registrant person. 'Pass_Through_Eligible' applies solely on the U.S. federal tax side under check-the-box, creating hybrid entity exposure that Canada's hybrid mismatch rules seek to neutralize. Dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory rate, reduced to 5% for a U.S.-resident corporate shareholder holding 10% or more of voting shares under the Canada-U.S. tax treaty). Alberta is a non-participating province: no PST, no sub-provincial VAT, but Alberta corporate income tax is a separate provincial levy administered by the CRA.

Available election forms

(i) U.S. check-the-box election (IRS Form 8832) to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership — the defining election for a ULC owned by U.S. residents; in Canada the entity remains taxable at the entity level. (ii) Hybrid mismatch documentation and remedial timing elections under Canada's BEPS Action 2 rules, which may deny the ULC's deduction for payments to a fiscal-transparent U.S. owner (deduction-without-inclusion) unless the U.S. owner includes the corresponding income; a discretionary CRA interpretive ruling can be requested. (iii) GST/HST election under s. 156 of the Excise Tax Act (Form GST25) to leave GST uncharged on taxable supplies of auto parts between the ULC and closely related registered corporations. (iv) Quick Method of accounting election (Form GST74) where annual global taxable supplies do not exceed CAD 400,000, allowing simplified net-tax remittance for the e-commerce retail arm. (v) Fiscal-period election to adopt any 12-month fiscal year (non-calendar permitted) for T2 purposes. (vi) Election to file consolidated? Not available; Alberta income tax is fully harmonized into the federal T2 return with no separate provincial election.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility under IMF Article VIII for both current and capital account; the Canadian dollar is a freely floating currency. No prior government approval is required for capital injections, loan repayments, dividend distributions, profit remittances, or liquidation proceeds. The only material remittance friction is Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, generally 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty, and standard bank source-of-funds verification; this is not an FX-control restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose mandatory surrender, conversion ratios, or statutory repatriation timelines for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency; however, for Canadian income tax purposes they must be expressed in Canadian dollars using Bank of Canada exchange rates. Financial institutions must report international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC and retain supporting records for five years.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada is the monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions regulates banks; FINTRAC administers AML/EFT transaction reporting. Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration and no exchange-control approval body for current or capital account transactions.
