Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; portal: CRA My Business Account. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA on behalf of Alberta Treasury Board and Finance; entity formation is filed through the Alberta Corporate Registry; Alberta does not operate a separate provincial sales tax agency.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta ULC carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 or through CRA online registration. The BN is used as the single tax identifier for GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll, import/export, and other program accounts. Alberta does not issue a separate tax identification number for corporate income tax; the BN plus the Alberta corporate registry account constitutes the provincial tax identity.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign-owned Alberta ULC carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 or through CRA online registration. The BN is used as the single tax identifier for GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll, import/export, and other program accounts. Alberta does not issue a separate tax identification number for corporate income tax; the BN plus the Alberta corporate registry account constitutes the provincial tax identity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

For Canadian income tax purposes, a ULC is a corporation and is not itself a pass-through entity. However, a ULC formed in Alberta is eligible to be treated as a disregarded/flow-through entity for U.S. tax purposes under U.S. Treasury Regulation Section 301.7701-3 via IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election. Canadian hybrid mismatch provisions under Income Tax Act sections 18.4, 18.5, 95(5.1), 113.2, and the general anti-avoidance rule under section 245 govern inappropriate hybrid mismatch benefits; no analogous check-the-box election exists under the Excise Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST registration is mandatory once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold). A non-resident ULC making taxable supplies in Canada is subject to the same threshold; registration may be optional below threshold but is advisable for input tax credit recovery and export zero-rating claims.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of tangible personal property exported by the supplier or delivered to a common carrier for transportation outside Canada, provided the goods leave Canada as part of a continuous export transaction under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Offshore services supplied to non-residents, or services performed entirely outside Canada, may qualify under sections 6, 7, 8, and 10 of Part V of Schedule VI. The supplier must retain evidence of export and must be a GST registrant to claim the refund of input tax credits attributable to such zero-rated exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or CBSA e-Manifest RMD)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada has no mandatory real-time invoice clearance system, no fiscal stamping, and no government-approved digital tax clearance engine. GST/HST registrants are required to file returns electronically through CRA My Business Account or GST/HST NETFILE; e-invoicing is voluntary and may follow PEPPOL interoperability standards where adopted commercially.

CRA and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) scrutinize export files for documentary completeness, commercial bona fides, and timing of export. If export zero-rating is unsupported, the GST becomes payable, the corresponding input tax credits are clawed back on reassessment, and interest runs from the original filing date. Penalties under Excise Tax Act section 280 for false statements, omission, participation, or assent to evasion can reach the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus daily civil penalties and criminal liability where fraud is established. Reassessment periods are extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime. Canada maintains an open capital account with no licensing requirement for current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance from an Alberta Unlimited Liability Company to non-resident shareholders is lawful, subject to income tax filings and Part XIII non-resident withholding where applicable, plus compliance with sanctions administered by Global Affairs Canada and with FINTRAC reporting thresholds. There is no general administrative hurdle to repatriation, although banks may require updated beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds documentation before releasing large wires.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio in Canada. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted to Canadian dollars at market rates. Reporting becomes relevant only for Canadian residents with foreign property or foreign accounts exceeding CAD 100,000 (Form T1133) and for FINTRAC electronic-fund-transfer reporting on transactions of CAD 10,000 or more within a 24-hour period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions for prudential bank supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada for anti-money-laundering and electronic-fund-transfer reporting; Canada has no standalone foreign-exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech electronic-money institutions and cross-border payment platforms are rated Medium-High as supplementary settlement layers, but Low as the sole banking backbone for the Alberta ULC. Platforms such as Wise Platform, Airwallex, and Stripe operating accounts can reduce FX cost and accelerate supplier disbursements, yet they are not OSFI-regulated deposit-taking institutions, do not carry CDIC deposit insurance, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and must be either FINTRAC-registered Canadian money services businesses or operate through a Canadian partner bank. The ULC should use a Schedule I bank as the primary treasury vault and capital escrow holder, and treat EMIs strictly as operational disbursement sub-ledgers.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23.0% aggregate federal-plus-Alberta combined corporate income tax rate (federal general rate 15% after the 13% General Rate Reduction; Alberta provincial general rate 8%). A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) would pay 11.0% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income, but a ULC controlled by non-residents is generally ineligible for the small business deduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act; Alberta imposes no additional provincial withholding tax. Under the Canada-US Tax Treaty (Fifth Protocol), the rate is reduced to 5% for a US corporate shareholder that beneficially owns at least 10% of the voting shares of the ULC and meets Limitation-on-Benefits, and to 15% for individual or non-corporate US shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate fair market value of reportable cross-border non-arm's-length transactions triggers mandatory contemporaneous documentation duty under s. 247(4) (complete and accurate description of transactions, participation in pricing, and comparability analysis). The transfer pricing penalty threshold is reached when the net transfer pricing adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue. Country-by-Country reporting and the OECD Master File/Local File are mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750,000,000 (approximately CAD 1,100,000,000); a single online retail ULC is unlikely to meet this threshold but must still maintain a proportionate Local File.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to the UAE, but economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 of the Income Tax Act), the treaty Limitation-on-Benefits clause (Article XXIX A of the Canada-US Treaty), and the 'liable to tax' test for treaty residence. A ULC must demonstrate a real fixed place of business in Alberta, active board management meetings in Alberta, substantive procurement and fulfillment functions, actual personnel and payroll, and a genuine business purpose for the unlimited liability structure; otherwise CRA may recharacterize entities under GAAR, deny treaty benefits, and impose the 25% GAAR penalty on the tax benefit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Blindness

Statutory sanction: Criminal indictment under s. 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine between 50% and 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2) of 50% of understated tax; Appeals and enforcement reassessment at full 23.0% combined rate plus compound arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Because an Alberta ULC has unlimited shareholder liability, both directors and shareholders are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts; CRA may seize personal bank accounts and real estate, register garnishments, invoke travel bans through the national enforcement program, and obtain personal restitution orders, with additional imprisonment for fraudulent misrepresentation.
Failure to Remit Part XIII Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Full liability for the unremitted tax, daily compounding arrears interest, a 10% non-remittance penalty, and the denial of treaty relief where the procedural NR301/Form 6166 documentation is incomplete.

Director personal liability: Directors' liability under s. 227.1 extends to principal, interest, and penalties; the unlimited liability nature of the ULC exposes shareholders personally; CRA can issue director liability assessments and pursue personal bankruptcy enforcement.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, reassessment at full federal-plus-Alberta rates, compounding interest, and potential double taxation if the foreign jurisdiction refuses a corresponding adjustment; GAAR penalty of 25% of the tax benefit if the pricing structure is found to be an avoidance transaction.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who knowingly signed false T106 filings are jointly liable for penalties under s. 247 and may be prosecuted for false statements under s. 239; CRA may pierce the corporate form of the ULC because the unlimited liability clause attaches directly to shareholders.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under Article XXIX A; recharacterization of transactions under GAAR with loss of deductions; denial of the 10% provincial abatement; 25% GAAR penalty on the full tax benefit; CRA may also require the ULC to file Canadian withholding on payments to the foreign parent as deemed dividends.

Director personal liability: CRA and Alberta courts treat the unlimited liability clause as a pre-existing waiver of limited liability; directors face personal civil restitution for the corporate tax deficiency, travel restrictions, asset freezes, and criminal sanctions for contempt or false filings.
Money Laundering / FINTRAC Reporting Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 per violation; criminal penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; CRA may forfeit the instrumentalities and proceeds of the offence.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for corporate fines under s. 48 of the PCMLTFA; shareholders of the ULC are additionally exposed through the unlimited liability clause; enforcement actions can include personal asset seizure and international travel restrictions.
Failure to File and Instalment Default

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of 5% plus 1% per month for up to 12 months; late instalment interest and penalties; mandatory nil-filing penalties for non-resident information returns; disqualification from Alberta Crown procurement where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for unremitted corporate tax, interest, and penalties under s. 227.1; the Crown may obtain a judicial restitution order against both the ULC and its shareholders and may deny travel passports for outstanding enforcement debts.
