Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first incorporate the Alberta ULC under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) and then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and Program Accounts. Registration may be completed electronically via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), by mailing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or by telephone. Non-resident corporations must provide the registered office address in Alberta, full legal name, directors/owners, the date of incorporation, the corporate access code from Alberta Corporate Registry, and a designation of a deemed resident or authorized representative if applicable. A GST/HST program account is added separately once taxable supplies in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold or upon voluntary registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (for U.S. federal tax check-the-box purposes); nevertheless, CRA treats the Alberta ULC as a taxable Canadian corporation and it remits federal income tax at entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act.

Available election forms

No domestic Canadian check-the-box regime exists. For U.S. tax mapping, the ULC may file IRS Form 8832 to elect disregarded-entity or partnership classification; for Canadian GST/HST, a non-resident supplier can voluntarily register under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 240(1) once threshold is met. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s. 18.4 apply to cross-border arrangements to deny inappropriate deductions. The Alberta ULC structure is commonly used with a U.S. parent to permit U.S. hybrid classification while remaining a Canadian taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; small supplier exception under ETA s. 240(1).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of tangible personal property supplied by a GST/HST registrant where the property is exported by the supplier or the recipient in commercial quantities as part of the sale, with proof of export retained (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1; services zero-rated under s. 20). For B2B industrial MRO catalog e-commerce, the sale is zero-rated only if the goods are delivered from a Canadian location to a foreign destination or delivered to a common carrier for export, within the prescribed time limits and quantities, and the supplier holds contemporaneous evidence of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Form B13A or electronic Canadian Export Declaration via CBSA)
  • Transport document (ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or highway manifest) evidencing movement out of Canada
  • Commercial invoice describing the goods, quantities, sale price, and foreign purchaser identification
  • CBSA cargo control document or foreign customs entry record evidencing arrival in the destination country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/SWIFT remittance confirmation where required by CRA

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API pre-authorization, or digital stamping regime is in operation in Canada. GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed tax information under ETA s. 169 and the Input Tax Credit Information (GST/HST) Regulations. Filers with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1 million must file GST/HST returns electronically through CRA’s file transfer service, which supports XML/API transmission. There is no pre-clearance or manual approval of individual invoices before issuance.

CRA may deny zero-rating and claw back any input tax credits previously claimed where export documentation is inadequate, and may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period. Penalties include failure-to-file penalties for prescribed export declarations, late-filing penalties of 5% plus 1% per month compound interest under ETA s. 280, and false-statement penalties of 50% of the wrongly claimed refund under ETA ss. 285.1–285.2. Deliberate fraudulent export zero-rating schemes attract full reassessment, interest, penalties, criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327, and potential seizure of goods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating, fully convertible currency; Canada complies with Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted, with no central-bank allocation, no prior exchange-control license, and no repatriation authorization applicable to inbound FDI, share subscriptions, trade settlements, or profit remittances. Dividends, interest, and royalties may be remitted in any convertible currency, subject solely to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)); the default rate is 25%, treaty-reduced, and a U.S.-parented Alberta ULC must additionally address the deemed-dividend rules in subsections 84(2)/84(3) of the Act and the limitation-on-benefits article of the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention. No statutory escrow or blockage period applies to outbound profit remittance; only solvency tests under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) (RSA 2000, c. B-9) constrain distributions. A ULC may elect a USD functional currency for Canadian tax reporting under section 261 of the Income Tax Act, materially simplifying cross-border treasury measurement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory holding period for export receipts. A ULC may retain 100% of export collections in CAD or in foreign-currency deposit accounts at Canadian or offshore banks without Bank of Canada authorization. For income recognition, export receipts are translated at the Bank of Canada noon spot rate for the taxation year, or under the section 261 functional-currency election where the ULC's audited financial statements are prepared in USD. FINTRAC reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C., 2000, c. 17) apply only to large-value or sentinel electronic fund transfers, not to currency conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), constituted under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), with prudential bank supervision exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C., 1991, c. 46). Canada maintains no foreign-exchange control agency; the statutory exchange-control apparatus was abolished in 1951. Balance-of-payments and trade-transaction data are collected by Statistics Canada and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for statistical, customs, and reporting purposes only, and no ministerial approval is required for cross-border fund movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Foreign digital EMIs holding UK FCA or EU e-money authorizations cannot passport into Canada and are not permitted to open deposit accounts, hold Canadian client funds, or clear CAD payments unless they (i) register as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C., 2021, c. 23), and (ii) register as a money services business with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer may be used only as auxiliary FX/collection gateways that settle through a sponsor Schedule I Canadian bank; they are not a lawful substitute for the ULC's primary corporate operating account and do not satisfy Canadian clearing-rail access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route non-resident shareholder funds via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire/CHIPS for USD) to the ULC's CAD/USD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. Field 70 must state 'Share capital subscription – issuance of ULC shares' and reference the Alberta Corporate Registry number; field 57 must carry the beneficiary bank's institution/transit number and field 59 the ULC account name and number. No exchange-control approval or FDI filing is required, and no statutory escrow applies; however, wires above C$10,000 trigger the bank's FINTRAC large-value EFT report. If the subscriber is a U.S. member of a check-the-box disregarded ULC, clearly label the remittance as equity rather than intercompany debt in field 70 to prevent later Part XIII withholding characterization as deemed interest or a deemed dividend.Supplier payments and trade settlements use SWIFT MT103 with field 70 purpose code 'Industrial MRO goods – trade settlement'. For high-value purchase orders, issue irrevocable documentary letters of credit governed by UCP600, payable at sight and confirmed by the beneficiary's bank, through the banks' trade desks (BMO and Scotiabank are preferred). Domestic CAD disbursements clear same-day through Payments Canada high-value rails (LVTS, with the ongoing migration to the Lynx real-time gross settlement system), while USD disbursements clear via the Schedule I bank's U.S. correspondent over Fedwire or CHIPS.
