E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — An Alberta ULC is a separate legal person subject to Canadian entity-level income tax (federal 38% Part I rate less 13% abatement, yielding a 15% general federal effective rate, plus Alberta 8% general corporate rate; combined approximately 23% before the small business deduction). For U.S. federal tax purposes, the ULC is an eligible entity under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3 because its members have unlimited liability; the default classification is disregarded (single member) or partnership (multi-member), permitting pass-through treatment and access to the Canada-U.S. Treaty fiscally transparent entity rule. Territorial scope: worldwide income with foreign tax credits; no tax-exempt zone applies. Alberta has no provincial sales tax, so the 5% GST is the only indirect tax on local supplies.

Available election forms

U.S. check-the-box election: IRS Form 8832 (due within 75 days of formation) may override the default partnership/disregarded status and elect corporate classification; there is no Canadian federal counterpart election to reclassify a corporation as transparent. Available material elections: GST/HST joint election under ETA s.273 for closely related entities; ETA s.211.1 election for foreign supplier reporting; ITA s.216(1) election on deemed Canadian rental income; ITA s.216.1 election for Canadian resource property income; ITA s.115.1 election to file for treaty-protected Canadian real property gains; ITA s.85 rollover election for property transfers into the ULC; amalgamation election under ITA s.87; and ETA nil-consideration election for inter-corporate supplies. Hybrid mismatch scrutiny applies to ULC pass-through elections under the MLI, GILTI/CFC rules, and ITA s.247 transfer pricing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold: mandatory registration when worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. No separate Alberta sales tax registration is required because Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax. Non-resident online sellers may use CRA simplified GST/HST registration (GST494).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under ETA Schedule VI, Part V: (i) goods (portable battery generators, HS 8504.40 / 8507; solar panels, HS 8541.43) sold to a non-resident buyer are zero-rated if ownership transfers to the buyer and the goods are exported from Canada within 60 days of transfer of ownership (supplier-export) or within 60 days of delivery to the recipient (recipient-export), in accordance with the Export and Exclusion of Property Regulations SOR/88-252; (ii) ancillary services to exported goods (e.g., remote monitoring software, warranties) supplied to a non-resident, non-registrant recipient are zero-rated under s.7 of Part V where they do not relate to Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada and are not rendered to an individual present in Canada; (iii) the ULC must retain sufficient evidence of physical export and settlement; no GST is charged on qualifying export sales, and full input tax credits are recoverable on related inputs, including GST paid at importation on inventory.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic filing through CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CES)) covering portable power generators and solar panels
  • Commercial sales invoice and packing list stating HS classification (HS 8504.40 / HS 8507 for battery generators; HS 8541.43 for solar modules), Incoterms and destination
  • Transport documentation: Master/House Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) or courier shipment records naming the ULC as exporter and the non-resident buyer as consignee
  • CBSA proof-of-export evidence: carrier certificate, border exit stamp, freight forwarder confirmation, or CBSA Proof of Export report

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime. The CRA operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing Service (Peppol BIS 3.0 interoperability pilot, 2023 onward). Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1 million; electronic NETFILE/GST/HST remittance is standard for all registrants. Export declarations must be filed electronically via the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) where the value of exported goods is CAD 2,000 or more, with Form B13A used only in fallback. GST/HST tax invoices must display the supplier BN, date, parties, description, tax amount and rate, and records must be retained for six years.

Invalid zero-rating claims trigger CRA reassessment of the supply at 5% GST, daily compound interest at the CRA prescribed rate, and, where false statements or omissions are made knowingly, a gross negligence penalty under ETA s.280 equal to 25% of net tax (50% for a repeat violation), plus criminal liability under ETA s.327 (on summary conviction, up to CAD 50,000 fine and/or 6 months imprisonment; on indictment, up to CAD 500,000 and/or 5 years). The normal four-year GST/HST reassessment period is suspended where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud, and clawback of related input tax credits and refunds applies. For export receivables outstanding beyond 180 days, CRA may impute interest income under ITA s.80.4 and s.247 for related-party balances and recharacterize overdue amounts as deemed loans or shareholder benefits under ITA s.15(2); unrelated-party bad debts are governed by ETA and ITA bad debt recovery rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts with no profit-remittance licensing, registration, or prior approval requirement. The former Exchange Control Act lapsed in 1951 and was never re-enacted. Dividend remittances from the Alberta ULC to a non-resident shareholder attract Part XIII withholding (statutory 25%; reduced to 5% under Article X(2) of the Canada-US Tax Convention (1980, as amended) where the beneficial owner is a corporation holding at least 10% of the voting stock, claimed via Form NR301). Interest on intercompany shareholder loans is generally exempt from Canadian withholding under the treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline applies to export proceeds in Canada. The ULC may invoice in USD or CNH, retain foreign-currency deposits indefinitely, and net supplier payments offshore. Only reporting duties attach: FINTRAC EFTR disclosure for any wire of CAD 10,000 or more and CRA Form T1135 (Foreign Income Verification Statement) where specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000 aggregate cost.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2) is the sole monetary and currency-issuing authority; Canada maintains no separate Foreign Exchange Administration. OSFI licenses deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); FINTRAC enforces cross-border wire monitoring and EFT reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17).

They are not substitutes for a Tier-1 bank: they lack CDIC deposit insurance, commercial LC issuance, and interbank FX credit lines; the Tier-1 bank account must remain the principal liquidity anchor.
