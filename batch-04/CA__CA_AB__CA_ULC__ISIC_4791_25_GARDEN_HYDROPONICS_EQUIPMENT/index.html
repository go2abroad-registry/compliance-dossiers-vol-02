Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal income tax and GST/HST administration; Alberta provincial corporate income tax administered separately by the Government of Alberta, Treasury Board and Finance, operating through Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (ATRA); federal business portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-business.html; Alberta corporate registry: Alberta Corporate Registry.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation under the Alberta Business Corporations Act, the Alberta Corporate Registry files registration data electronically with the CRA, and the CRA automatically issues the nine-digit Business Number (BN) with a corporate income tax program account (RC suffix) to the ULC. If automatic issuance does not occur, the ULC must file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA. The ULC, being incorporated in Canada, is resident in Canada under section 250(4)(a) of the Income Tax Act and is, for Canadian domestic tax, a separate Part I taxpayer notwithstanding any U.S. federal pass-through or disregarded-entity election. The ULC must register for the GST/HST program account (RT suffix) when taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or earlier by voluntary registration; payroll (RP) and customs import/export (RM) program accounts must be added as operations require. Non-resident direct or indirect ownership is permitted, no minimum capital is required, and no business fitness condition applies. Annual compliance requires the federal T2 corporate income tax return and the Alberta AT1 corporate tax return even where the ULC is classified as pass-through for U.S. purposes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Canada maintains no general foreign exchange control restrictions on current account transactions, capital account transactions, or profit remittances. Dividends, interest, royalties, and loan repayments may be remitted abroad without central bank approval, subject only to withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and applicable income tax treaties. For a CA_AB ULC, non-resident shareholders may receive distributions without exchange control clearance, but compliance with subsection 84(2) deemed dividend rules is essential to avoid unintended taxable distributions. Capital contributions in foreign currency are freely creditable to the ULC's bank account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not require exporters to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or to convert foreign currency receipts into CAD within any statutory timeline. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency bank accounts, used for foreign-currency trade settlements, or converted at commercial rates. FINTRAC reporting thresholds apply only under the PCMLTFA, including electronic funds transfer reports for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange market supervision is decentralized and conducted through the Bank of Canada and major financial institutions; Canada has no separate foreign exchange control agency because the Canadian dollar is a freely convertible currency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Feasible with limitations. A fully offshore EMI (not licensed as a Canadian MSB or FMSB) cannot legally provide payment services in Canada without FINTRAC registration. Canadian-licensed MSBs and payment processors (including Stripe, PayPal, Wise, and Payoneer) are viable for merchant settlement and cross-border disbursements. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol for foreign shareholder capital injection to a CA_AB ULC: (1) Use a Canadian schedule I bank account or authorized foreign bank branch. (2) Instruct the remitting bank to send a SWIFT MT103 with a clear purpose code using ISO 20022 Purpose Code 'EQUI' (equity financing) or 'CAPP' (capital payment) and narrative 'Share capital contribution to Alberta ULC'. (3) Provide the bank with the ULC's corporate registration number and business number. (4) Ensure the remitter's bank completes all customer due diligence on the beneficial owner. (5) The receiving bank will verify the ULC's board resolution authorizing receipt. (6) A ULC-specific consideration: remittances characterized as shareholder loans must be documented with a written loan agreement and comply with Canada Revenue Agency section 15(2) or debt-equity rules; equity contributions should be recorded in the share register and share certificates. (7) Filing of Form T106 (if non-arm's length) may be required. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Form T106 is triggered for reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions when aggregate transaction value exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the taxation year. CRA's administrative practice also expects contemporaneous OECD-compliant Local File and Master File documentation for large entities; Country-by-Country reporting is required for Canadian-headquartered MNE groups with consolidated annual revenue exceeding CAD 900,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), the broader GAAR (including s.246 in Alberta), and the transfer-pricing arm's-length standard require real economic substance. A ULC that is merely a pass-through treasury center, owned by a US parent and holding online sales receivables without employees, premises, or management, will be challenged. The CRA may disregard a 'sham' agency agreement, reassess the US parent on the Canadian-source income, and deny treaty benefits under the prevention-of-treaty-abuse clause (Article 29 of the Canada-US DTA).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Non-Reporting

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1): on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 8 years. Civil reassessment plus gross-negligence penalties under s.163(2) of 50% of the false amount.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed or acquiesced to the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid Part I tax, GST/HST, and payroll source deductions under ITA s.227.1(1) and s.323. CRA may issue director's liability certificates and restrict departure (administered by the Canada Border Services Agency for departure interception and travel bans).
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under ITA s.247(2) that disallows the non-arm's-length transfer price; 10% penalty under s.247(3)(b) on the net upward adjustment (unless reasonable efforts documented); interest compounding. The CRA may also apply GAAR to deny the tax benefit and reallocate income to the Canadian sales function.

Director personal liability: Alberta's Business Corporations Act (s. 123(4) and s. 263) permits creditor and Crown claims against directors of an unlimited liability company to pierce the veil if the ULC is used for fraudulent amalgamation. Directors may face personal civil restitution for unpaid corporate tax to CRA, Director of Public Prosecutions charges for fraud, and a 10-year ban under the Bankruptcy Insolvency Act if the shell is used to conceal assets.
Transfer-Pricing Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: ITA s.162(7) penalty: CAD 100 per day, up to CAD 2,500, for each incomplete T106 (including late filing); failure to provide CRA with access to accounting records can trigger a court-imposed penalty of CAD 100 per day per transaction; net TP adjustment attracts a 10% penalty (s.247(3)).

Director personal liability: Directors who commit the corporation to unsubstantiated intercompany pricing may be personally assessed as trustees over the TP penalty if the corporation becomes insolvent; under s.159(d) of the Tax Court of Canada Act, directors can be examined with respect to the ULC's books, and an Alberta court may not grant discharge for director misconduct.
Money Laundering / Shell Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC enforcement: failure to report large virtual-currency/electronic funds transfers over CAD 10,000 attracts fines up to CAD 500,000 and/or imprisonment; Criminal Code of Canada s.462.31 (laundering proceeds of crime) carries imprisonment up to 10 years on indictment; Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act s.74.1 penalties can be levied on the ULC.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability under the Purchase and Sale of Business Act for receiving tainted assets; statutory director liability for unpaid provincial tax under Alberta's Employment Pension Plans Act and Consumer Protection Act can compel restitution. Civil forfeiture proceedings under the Criminal Code apply to corporate property and personal assets used to launder funds.
