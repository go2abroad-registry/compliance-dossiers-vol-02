Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the ULC is a corporation resident in Canada and is an entity-level taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (no Canadian statute treats it as a partnership or trust), but its unlimited liability share structure renders it an eligible entity under U.S. Treas. Reg. 301.7701-3, permitting a check-the-box election to achieve pass-through or disregarded-entity treatment for U.S. federal tax. For GST/HST, the ULC is a resident registrable person under ETA s. 123(1) and s. 240. Alberta does not impose a provincial sales tax, HST, or corporation capital tax; therefore the indirect tax scope is limited to the federal 5% GST.

Available election forms

(i) U.S. check-the-box election on IRS Form 8832 to classify the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. federal tax; (ii) Income Tax Act s. 88(2.2) election on a wind-up of an unlimited liability corporation, deeming the corporation not to be a corporation so that shareholders absorb the ULC's losses and deductible expenses (the statutory ULC loss pass-through); (iii) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election with a closely related corporation and s. 167.1 group election to zero-rate or simplify intercompany GST/HST supplies; (iv) Income Tax Act s. 216 election by a non-resident shareholder to compute Canadian-source rental income on a net basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - mandatory registration threshold measured on the total worldwide consideration for taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made by the person and its associates over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters under ETA s. 123(1) ('small supplier') and s. 148; for a non-resident, the threshold applies to Canadian-source taxable supplies only. The cold storage registrant in Alberta becomes a mandatory registrant once gross taxable supply consideration exceeds CAD 30,000 in the four-quarter look-back, and voluntary registration for non-residents with Canadian permanent establishments is available under ETA ss. 240 and 241.1.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is claimable by the ULC for supplies meeting the export tests of ETA Schedule VI, Part V. Goods: ss. 5-7 (s. 5, supply to a non-resident of goods delivered outside Canada; s. 6, goods exported as part of a commercial transaction; s. 7, goods placed in the possession of a common carrier for export), provided the non-resident recipient is not registered under ETA s. 240. Services: s. 9, a supply of a service to a non-resident in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada that is destined for export - this is the operative rule for temperature-controlled storage, handling, and cold-chain logistics services rendered to perishable pharmaceuticals subsequently exported; the services must be performed before export and the goods must be exported by or on behalf of the non-resident within a reasonable time (CRA generally expects direct exports to leave Canada within 60 days). The documentary and evidentiary standards are set out in CRA GST/HST Memoranda 4-5-1 and 4-5-2.

  • CBSA Canada Export Declaration electronically filed through the CARM portal for shipments exceeding CAD 2,500
  • Commercial invoice identifying the shipper and consignee, Incoterms rules, HS classification, and declared value
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or air waybill (air) evidencing final delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward foreign remittance certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 or FX receipt) demonstrating receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer within the 180-day repatriation window
  • Executed warehousing/logistics agreement naming the non-resident recipient and confirming that the recipient is not registered for GST/HST
  • Time-temperature chain-of-custody logs and pharma quality records substantiating the cold-chain through the point of export

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, invoice stamping, or tax-authority e-invoicing API regime. The binding digital requirements are: (a) ETA s. 286 - books and records, including computerized records, must be retained for six years in a readable and accessible electronic format, with foreign-resident records producible to CRA on demand; (b) ETA s. 169(1)(a) and CRA GST/HST Memorandum 8-6 - a registrant must issue and retain invoices containing the prescribed particulars (BN and GST registration number, consideration, tax amount) and must quote its GST registration number on zero-rated export invoices; (c) GST/HST NETFILE and mandatory electronic payment/remittance channels apply to registrants satisfying CRA thresholds; (d) the CBSA CARM (Assessment and Revenue Management) single-window portal mandates electronic filing of Canada Export Declarations and related cargo documentation through its API-enabled online interfaces. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is voluntary, with adoption recommended for interoperability but not yet statutory.

CRA export verification audits apply a strict substantiation standard: where export documentation is incomplete, internally inconsistent, or the goods are diverted to the domestic market, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST, the related input tax credits and refunds are retroactively clawed back, and arrears interest runs from the original remittance due date. Penalties include: (a) ETA s. 285.1 culpable-conduct penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the overstated refund or understated net tax; (b) ETA ss. 280-282 prosecution for wilful evasion and false statements, with fines up to CAD 250,000 and imprisonment, and higher statutory amounts for corporate offenders; (c) ETA s. 298(1)-(3) extended or unlimited reassessment periods where misrepresentation, negligence, or fraud is established; (d) ITA s. 163(2) a 50% gross-negligence penalty on any understated Part I tax; and (e) ITA s. 247 transfer-pricing documentation penalties where cross-border warehousing charges are not arm's length. Although Canada imposes no codified export-proceeds repatriation deadline, intercompany export receivables remaining outstanding beyond 180 days attract CRA transfer-pricing scrutiny and may trigger functional income reallocation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. No exchange-control permit, central bank approval, or capital repatriation licensing regime applies to dividend distributions, capital redemptions, or shareholder loan repayments. Effective constraints are indirect: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5% on dividends for a U.S. parent under the Canada-U.S. Treaty); (ii) section 18(4) thin-capitalization limitation of interest deduction where debt-to-equity exceeds 1.5:1 for Canadian-resident corporations; (iii) Investment Canada Act (ICA) notification obligation for a non-Canadian establishing a new Canadian business, including a ULC; and (iv) FINTRAC reporting of every cross-border wire transfer equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000. Profit remittance from a ULC is freely permitted; no surrender, conversion quota, or substantive F/X approval exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate foreign currency earnings. A Canadian ULC may retain USD or other foreign currency in domestic or foreign bank accounts indefinitely. Tax consequence management applies: unrealized foreign exchange gains on capital account become taxable upon realization; business-level foreign currency gains are included in income. Banking compliance requires the account's stated business purpose to include 'global cold-chain trade settlement' and periodic FINTRAC reports for large cross-border receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as apex monetary and FX reserve manager; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of all Schedule I and Schedule II banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/CTF financial intelligence unit; Payments Canada as operator of Lynx (high-value settlement), ACSS (retail ACH), and the new Real-Time Rail (RTR); Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration administered by the Bank of Canada for all payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, as supplementary payment rail only. Legal constraints under the Retail Payment Activities Act require any foreign EMI (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Qonto) offering services to Canadian residents or clearing CAD to register with the Bank of Canada as a retail payment service provider and with FINTRAC as a money service business (MSB). Unregistered offshore EMIs cannot legally clear CAD, cannot hold a Canadian settlement account at Payments Canada, and will be blocked by Tier 1 compliance gatekeepers. A compliant EMI may be used for niche vendor payouts or multi-currency sub-accounts, but the ULC's primary treasury must remain in a Schedule I bank to avail itself of Lynx finality and efficient FX pricing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity injections must be executed via SWIFT MT103 with Field 70 (Remittance Information) reading 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION / CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – CRA ACCOUNT [federal corporate account number]'; where the bank's portal permits, select the purpose code 'D1 – Direct Investment' or 'Capital Contribution' to align with OSFI reporting standards. U.S.-source USD capital should clear via Fedwire/CHIPS to the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent, then be converted into CAD at interbank mid-benchmark pricing and credited to the ULC's operational account. No Bank of Canada or Foreign Exchange authorization is required; however, the ULC or its non-Canadian parent must file Investment Canada Act Form 1 notification with Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada within 30 days of establishing the new Canadian business. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous local file documentation is triggered when aggregate cross-border non-arm's-length transactions exceed CAD 1 million per fiscal year; master file documentation is expected for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above CAD 50 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the Income Tax Act GAAR (s. 245) and common-law substance-over-form doctrine apply: transactions must be supported by real employees, refrigeration assets, commercial risk, and substantive managerial activity in Alberta. An ULC with no physical warehouse, no personnel, and no actual cold-chain operations will be recharacterized as a sham and denied treaty and deduction benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Gross Negligence

Statutory sanction: Under the Canadian Criminal Code and Income Tax Act, criminal tax evasion carries fines up to CAD 5 million and imprisonment up to 5 years; the CRA will additionally reassess and apply a 50% gross-negligence penalty on unreported tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unremitted tax withheld under s. 227.1; civil piercing of the corporate veil allows the CRA to pursue directors' personal assets, issue travel bans, and seek imprisonment where the ULC is found to be a façade for non-resident shareholders
Shell Company and Fictitious Non-Resident Ownership Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty withholding reductions and loss deductions; CRA may impose a GAAR penalty of 50% of the disclaimed benefit; the Alberta registry may dissolve the company for non-compliance with its registered-office and annual-return obligations; fines and penalties up to CAD 1 million can be assessed

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for unpaid corporate tax, remittance penalties, and interest if the ULC is an instrumentality of the non-resident shareholder; personal civil liability, director-disqualification orders, and travel bans are enforceable
AML and Beneficial Ownership Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative penalties up to CAD 100,000 per violation, and criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for willful non-compliance; the Alberta securities and company registries may administratively strike the ULC for false UBO declarations

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly assist in laundering proceeds from cold-storage or pharmaceutical minimum-payment transactions are subject to personal criminal liability, forfeiture of personal property, and extradition/travel restrictions; civil liability extends commensurate with fraudulent or negligent conduct
