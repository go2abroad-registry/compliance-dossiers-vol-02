Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or deposit requirement under Alberta law. The unlimited liability shares must be fully issued and may be paid in money, property, or services; the directors must fix a fair value for non-cash consideration, and shareholders retain statutory unlimited liability for the Corporation's debts irrespective of paid-up capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and structural certification under the Alberta Fire Code (AR 29/2006) and the Safety Codes Act (RSA 2000, c S-1): obtain municipal development and building permits, fire-resistance ratings for racked storage, structural load inspection, exit and alarm commissioning, and a fire marshal occupancy clearance. Operational clearance must also be obtained under the Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) Code (AR 87/2009), including WHMIS/GHS workplace labelling, forklift and pedestrian traffic safety, emergency response plan, lighting levels, and first aid audit.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account at canada.ca; customs and bonded warehouse licensing: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the CARM (Assessment and Revenue Management) portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Confirm the Alberta ULC is a Canadian resident corporation for domestic tax purposes and must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) through BRO or Form RC1. Step 2: Open the GST/HST program account (RT) electronically; registration is mandatory once taxable supplies in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (ETA s. 148) and may not be backdated more than 30 days. Step 3: The non-resident US parent does not require a separate Canadian tax ID unless it is itself carrying on business in Canada through a permanent establishment; treaty relief is claimed under Article VII and Article IV(6) of the Canada-US Tax Treaty. Step 4: Obtain the CBSA customs bonded warehouse licence (Customs Act s. 91) and link it to the ULC's BN, including the importer/exporter (RM) program account and any carrier or customs broker accounts. Step 5: Register for payroll (RP) and corporate income tax (RC) accounts if the ULC has staff and Canadian-source income; file Part XIV branch tax returns (T2 plus branch tax) for foreign-owned ULCs. Step 6: File NR4 and Form NR301 where Part XIII withholding applies to cross-border payments. Step 7: Retain books, export declarations, and bonded warehouse ledgers for at least six years (ETA s. 286; ITA s. 230(4)).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: Under Canadian domestic law, the Alberta ULC is a separate taxpayer subject to entity-level Part I corporate income tax (federal 15% general rate; Alberta 8% provincial rate on the same taxable base) and Part XIV branch tax of 25% (treaty-reduced) on after-tax profits repatriated to the US parent. Canada does not recognize check-the-box pass-through treatment domestically; the pass-through eligibility arises only under US federal tax law via IRC § 7701 (Form 8832), and is given effect in Canada solely through the fiscally transparent provisions of Article IV(6) and Article XXVII(7) of the Canada-US Tax Treaty (Fifth Protocol). Territorial scope: the ULC is resident in Canada and taxed on worldwide income; non-resident branch operations are taxable on Canadian-source business income attributable to the permanent establishment (the bonded depot). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: Under Canadian domestic law, the Alberta ULC is a separate taxpayer subject to entity-level Part I corporate income tax (federal 15% general rate; Alberta 8% provincial rate on the same taxable base) and Part XIV branch tax of 25% (treaty-reduced) on after-tax profits repatriated to the US parent. Canada does not recognize check-the-box pass-through treatment domestically; the pass-through eligibility arises only under US federal tax law via IRC § 7701 (Form 8832), and is given effect in Canada solely through the fiscally transparent provisions of Article IV(6) and Article XXVII(7) of the Canada-US Tax Treaty (Fifth Protocol). Territorial scope: the ULC is resident in Canada and taxed on worldwide income; non-resident branch operations are taxable on Canadian-source business income attributable to the permanent establishment (the bonded depot). No tax-exempt zone status applies at the federal or Alberta level for income tax; the bonded warehouse is a customs duty and GST suspension regime, not an income tax holiday.

Available election forms

(1) US check-the-box election under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3 via Form 8832 (disregarded entity or partnership) for US federal purposes. (2) Canada-US Treaty relief under Article IV(6) (fiscally transparent entities) to avoid double taxation of US-resident members. (3) GST/HST elections: ETA s. 177 (closely related corporations election), ETA s. 179/180 (zero-rating election for exporter's goods), and ETA s. 211.1 (financial services election). (4) Branch tax planning election under ITA s. 219.2 / Form NR217 to reduce Part XIV tax through reinvestment of Canadian earnings. (5) Transfer pricing advance pricing arrangement (APA) under ITA s. 247 for cross-border intercompany services. (6) Qualified donee/GST rebate elections are not applicable to this commercial scope.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD (small supplier threshold, ETA s. 148(1)) based on taxable supplies made in Canada over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter. A non-resident carrying on business through a permanent establishment is required to register upon exceeding the threshold; a CBSA-licensed bonded warehouse operator must register for GST even below threshold where it makes taxable supplies of storage, handling, and logistics services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST applies to: (1) supplies of goods exported by the supplier, including goods withdrawn directly from a CBSA-licensed bonded warehouse for export, under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 1, 5, and 19; (2) goods imported into Canada, placed in a customs bonded warehouse, and subsequently exported without release into the domestic market; (3) services supplied to a non-resident person not registered for GST (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7), including storage and handling services supplied at the bonded depot to non-resident principals; (4) transportation services for exported goods (s. 3); and (5) goods delivered to a common carrier for export. The supplier must demonstrate that possession of the goods passed to the purchaser in Canada solely for export and that the goods were exported within a reasonable period (typically 60-180 days) to satisfy the continuity-of-export rule. Goods released from the bonded warehouse for home consumption are taxable at 5% GST at release, with an input tax credit available to registered commercial operators.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration filed via CERS (Canadian Export Reporting System) or Automated Export Declaration (AED) for low-value shipments
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill / Cargo Control Document evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and purchaser's signed purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 wire transfer or documentary credit proving export payment to the ULC
  • Bonded warehouse ledger and CARM release documentation (Form B3-3 equivalent) showing entry into and removal from the customs bonded warehouse
  • Packing list and any Export Control List permits for controlled goods

Canada does not currently impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or nationwide mandatory e-invoicing mandate for GST/HST (status as of 2025); e-invoicing is voluntary through the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL framework, with provincial pilots in Quebec and Ontario. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns (NETFILE/Internet File Transfer), customs accounting and duty/GST assessment through the CBSA Single Window Initiative (SWI) and CARM portal, and export reporting via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) using B13A. Bonded warehouse operators must transmit electronic goods-movement records and obtain CBSA pre-authorization for warehouse operations. GST on goods deposited in a customs bonded warehouse is suspended until release for home consumption, at which point GST is assessed through CARM; storage and handling services supplied to non-resident principals are generally zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export supplies within four years (ETA s. 298), and without time limit where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default is established (ETA s. 298(3)); retrospective clawback of the 0% rate results in 5% GST, statutory interest, and penalties when export documentation is deficient or goods are diverted to the domestic market. Civil penalties: ETA s. 280(1) gross negligence penalty of 25% of the unremitted tax; s. 285(2) failure-to-file penalties; s. 286(1) failure to provide information; s. 329 successor liability; director liability under ETA s. 323(1). Criminal sanctions: ETA s. 327(1) false statements or evasion - summary conviction fine up to CAD 25,000 and/or 12 months imprisonment; indictment fine up to CAD 100,000 and/or 5 years imprisonment. Export carousel / GST 'straw man' schemes are investigated by CRA's GST/HST Criminal Investigations Program, and CBSA may revoke bonded warehouse licences under Customs Act s. 102 for compliance failures. FX repatriation: Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day hard deadline for export proceeds; the 180-day benchmark in this dossier reflects the ETA reasonable-time export rule and standard open-account documentary terms. Intercompany export receivables are nonetheless subject to ITA s. 247 transfer pricing at arm's-length value, and Part XIV branch tax (25%, treaty-reduced to 5% or 15%) applies to after-tax branch profits of the foreign-controlled Alberta ULC.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no surrender, approval or licensing requirements for importing/exporting capital or for remitting dividends, loan repayments or branch profits. Profit remittance from an Alberta ULC faces no FX-control hurdle, but Part XIII withholding tax may apply (generally up to 25% domestic rate, reduced by tax treaty to typically 5% for treaty-protected portfolio dividends and 0-5% for certain treaty-qualifying direct corporate ownership structures). Cross-border wire transfers or telegraphic transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 require FINTRAC reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio or timeline. An Alberta free-trade-zone bonded depot may hold export/foreign-currency receipts in domestic or foreign currency accounts indefinitely; banks will apply normal record-keeping, source-of-funds verification and FINTRAC large-transaction reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Canada's central bank); Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency or commission. The relevant regulatory perimeter is set by OSFI, FINTRAC and Payments Canada. No exchange-control approvals exist under federal or Alberta law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, as an ancillary payment rail only. Offshore digital EMIs are legally constrained: they cannot accept deposits or provide banking services in Canada unless authorized under the Bank Act or applicable provincial credit union/corporation statutes. A fintech EMI may register with FINTRAC as a money services business for remittance execution, or be licensed under the Retail Payment Activities Act when it is fully in force, but it cannot replace a Tier 1 Canadian bank as the principal clearing and settlement bank for the bonded depot.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity from the non-resident shareholder must come by SWIFT MT103 with the invoice/reference field describing the payment as an equity contribution or purchase of ULC shares. The Canadian receiving bank should receive: (i) the exact legal name of the ULC, (ii) the beneficiary account number, (iii) the corporate resolution approving issuance of shares or contributed surplus, and (iv) a remitter confirmation showing the registered shareholder as the source of funds. A copy of the executed share-subscription agreement, a valuation letter (if requested), and source-of-wealth documentation for the injecting shareholder must be lodged before the bank releases funds for treasury use. The CAD is fully convertible and no currency surrender is required; funds can remain in a USD multi-currency account if the depot's liabilities are USD-denominated. FINTRAC reporting applies to any cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more.The primary clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border supplier payments and foreign customs/tax payments; (2) irrevocable commercial documentary letters of credit (confirmed where required) issued/advised by the Tier 1 relationship bank for trade flows; (3) Payments Canada LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) for same-day CAD clearing to domestic logistics providers and customs brokers; (4) EFT/ACSS for routine low-value domestic disbursements; and (5) the bank's internal multi-currency global wire platform for USD/EUR/GBP settlements. Every instruction must reference the underlying commercial invoice and the bonded-depot contract ID; outgoing cross-border electronic transfers above CAD 10,000 must be filed with FINTRAC by the bank.Deliverable FX forward contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP, CAD/CNH and other freely convertible pairs) executed with the Tier 1 Canadian bank or an authorized swap dealer.FX currency options (call/put strategies) for hedging trade receivables and payables denominated in foreign currency.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps or cross-currency swaps for long-term debt and capital expenditure financing.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permissible where the settlement currency is not freely deliverable or is subject to regulatory restrictions on offshore trading; the NDF will be documented separately from the CAD/USD streaming market.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium, as an ancillary payment rail only. Offshore digital EMIs are legally constrained: they cannot accept deposits or provide banking services in Canada unless authorized under the Bank Act or applicable provincial credit union/corporation statutes. A fintech EMI may register with FINTRAC as a money services business for remittance execution, or be licensed under the Retail Payment Activities Act when it is fully in force, but it cannot replace a Tier 1 Canadian bank as the principal clearing and settlement bank for the bonded depot.
