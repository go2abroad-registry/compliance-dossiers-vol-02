Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online portal at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency; Alberta Ministry of Finance for provincial fuel tax/licensing (no separate provincial corporate income tax administration).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta ULC is incorporated under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (or continued into Alberta) and receives an Alberta corporate registry number. The ULC must then apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account using Form RC1 or the CRA Business Registration Online portal. If the ULC will export or import goods, it must also obtain a CRA customs account. A non-resident without a Canadian permanent establishment that does not carry on business in Canada may voluntarily register for GST/HST; mandatory registration applies only if the non-resident carries on commercial activity exceeding the small-supplier threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No Canadian pass-through election is available: an Alberta ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under the Income Tax Act (Canada). A foreign parent may file U.S. Form 8832 check-the-box to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. tax purposes only, which does not alter Canadian entity-level taxation. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules and treaty anti-avoidance provisions must be reviewed for cross-border financing and charter structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — gross annual worldwide taxable supplies (small-supplier threshold over four consecutive calendar quarters) for mandatory GST/HST registration; non-residents carrying on business in Canada are treated on the same threshold basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada by the supplier are zero-rated under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act. Services, including the charter or leasing of offshore supply vessels, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident not registered for GST/HST and the service is performed or consumed wholly outside Canada — e.g., offshore platform support services on a continental shelf beyond Canadian territorial waters. The ULC must retain contemporaneous evidence of the contractual destination, route/navigation logs, and non-resident status of the charterer.

  • Canada Export Declaration (B13A) / CBSA electronic export report
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing vessel movement out of Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or bank transfer proof of foreign payment

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is mandatory in Canada. CRA accepts electronic invoices provided the invoice discloses the vendor's BN, transaction details, GST/HST calculated, and other prescribed particulars. Voluntary Peppol-based e-invoicing adoption is in progress but not yet statutory. Export clearance is effected through the CBSA electronic reporting systems.

CRA may reassess within four years (unlimited on misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect) to retroactively deny zero-rating and assess unpaid GST plus interest. False or misleading statements attract a penalty under s.285.1 of the Excise Tax Act equal to 25% of the tax understated (minimum CAD 100). Wilful evasion may lead to penalties of 100% of the tax and criminal prosecution under s.281.1, with substantial fines and imprisonment. Claimants must maintain complete export proof; failures of documentation or physical export inevitably trigger clawback and penalty exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, with no general approval requirement for inbound equity, shareholder loans, dividends, interest, loan principal, or arm's-length commercial payments. Banks may require source-of-funds evidence, transaction purpose, beneficial-ownership information, sanctions screening, and underlying contracts. Payments remain subject to Canadian sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules, tax withholding, corporate-law solvency requirements, lender covenants, and any applicable Investment Canada Act review or notification. A Canadian ULC's tax classification and cross-border treatment depend on the relevant foreign jurisdiction; U.S. check-the-box treatment, if relevant, requires separate analysis and does not change Canadian legal or tax status. Dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, and other cross-border payments may attract Canadian withholding tax unless domestic law or a treaty reduces it and procedural conditions are met. ULC distributions may have atypical treaty consequences; confirm recipient residence, beneficial ownership, characterization, and treaty eligibility before payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory deadline requiring exporters to repatriate export receipts and no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign-currency proceeds. Receipts may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted voluntarily. Banks may request invoices, charter agreements, bills of lading, vessel and counterparty information, and payment-purpose details. Canadian banks and other reporting entities have statutory AML, sanctions, and applicable transaction-reporting obligations; these are not export-proceeds surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Canada is Canada's central bank. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control authority imposing routine currency conversion, capital-account approval, or export-proceeds surrender requirements. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions supervises federally regulated banks; FINTRAC administers applicable anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing compliance and reporting. These functions do not constitute general exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payments through a properly regulated Canadian financial institution or payment service provider; Low as a substitute for a Canadian corporate deposit account where the provider is an offshore electronic-money institution. Canada does not confer Canadian deposit-taking status on an offshore EMI merely because it serves Canadian customers. Verify Canadian registration or authorization applicable to the provider's activities, account safeguarding and insolvency treatment, permitted Canadian-dollar clearing access, foreign-exchange execution terms, sanctions screening, and any restrictions on client-money or escrow services. Retail payment providers may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act and Bank of Canada oversight where applicable; that regime is distinct from bank deposit insurance and does not itself authorize deposit-taking. Use a Canadian bank for operating balances, payroll, tax payments, and controlled escrow unless counsel confirms the alternative's legal status and protections.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate non-arm's-length transactions with each non-resident counterparty triggers T106/Local File documentation; CbC report and Master File trigger at group consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million (approximately C$1.1 billion).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations regime; however, CRA applies GAAR, source-residency principles, and treaty anti-abuse rules (LOB/beneficial ownership) requiring that the ULC maintain genuine board decisions, operational management, employees, accounting records, and physical presence in Alberta for vessel-leasing activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False or Omitted Filing

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239 serious tax evasion: on indictment, fine equal to 50%-200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) is 50% of understated tax; all interest and penalties compound.

Director personal liability: Directors/officers who directed or acquiesced in the false return are personally exposed under s.239(2); CRA can seek civil restitution, freezing orders, travel bans, and extradition under the Canada-US MLA treaty.
Part XIII Withholding and Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: Payer liability for the full unreported withholdable amount under s.227(8); 10% failure-to-withhold penalty absent due diligence; interest on unpaid tax; possible prosecution for failure to remit.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 for the corporation's unremitted Part XIII withholdings; CRA may issue personal director assessments, garnish personal assets, and impose cross-border departure restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment to arm's-length price under s.247(2); penalty of 10% of net transfer-pricing adjustment under s.247(3); late-filing penalties; interest; reduction or denial of cross-border deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for the TP penalty, but wilful participation in false documentation or fraud triggers joint civil liability and criminal referral; personal exposure may exceed corporate equity because the ULC's shareholder liability is unlimited.
Shell Company / GAAR / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR under s.245 disallows the tax benefit and imposes the 25% tax-benefit penalty; treaty relief is denied under beneficial ownership / LOB provisions; CRA may also apply sham/offshore recharacterization and reassess prior years.

Director personal liability: Directors who accept passive roles while controlling the ULC from abroad may be held as shadow directors; because the entity is an unlimited liability company, shareholders and directors risk direct creditor and tax authority recourse beyond paid-up capital.
AML/Sanctions Non-Compliance in Maritime Escrow Payments

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative penalties up to C$1,000,000 per violation for enterprises and C$500,000 for individuals; Criminal Code money-laundering offence punishable by up to 10 years imprisonment; UN and Special Economic Measures Act sanctions enforcement.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who knowingly facilitate prohibited financial dealing face criminal prosecution; ULC shareholders may be personally liable for fines and costs due to the unlimited-liability corporate form.
