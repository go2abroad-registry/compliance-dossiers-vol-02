Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada; registration and filing portals are Business Registration Online, GST/HST NETFILE, and My Business Account. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the Alberta Tax Collection Agreement, so no separate Alberta tax authority account is required.

E-government filing portal

An Alberta ULC must first complete Alberta incorporation. CRA then issues the federal Business Number (BN), a 9-digit statutory TIN. A foreign-owned ULC applies through Form RC1 or RC1-W for non-resident applicants, or through Business Registration Online, supplying the certificate or articles of incorporation, registered-office address, director information, ISIC 5222 business description, and Canadian agent contact details. The applicant selects program accounts: RC for corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, and RM for import or export accounts. GST/HST registration is effected by adding an RT program account, and the GST/HST ID is the BN followed by RT0001. If the ULC is a small supplier, RT registration is not required until the threshold is exceeded. Alberta corporate income tax does not require a separate account; CRA computes it on the T2 corporate income tax return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic check-the-box or elective pass-through regime; for Canadian Income Tax Act purposes, the Alberta ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under subsection 250(4) and is subject to Part I tax at the entity level. The pass-through eligibility of the ULC is a foreign classification feature: under Treasury Regulation section 301.7701-3, the ULC is a foreign eligible entity and, because shareholders do not have limited liability, it is classified by default as a partnership or disregarded entity unless Form 8832 is filed to elect classification as an association taxable as a corporation. Hybrid mismatch rules in sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act may restrict deductions arising from such hybrid arrangements. No GST/HST entity classification election is available; GST/HST status follows the supply activity and not the ULC share structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; exceeding this small-supplier threshold creates a mandatory GST/HST registration obligation for the Alberta ULC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI Part V. For the crew-recruitment agency, a supply to a non-resident shipowner or foreign manning company of seafarer sourcing, vetting, placement, or payroll-administration services is zero-rated if the service is performed in Canada and is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. If the service is performed entirely outside Canada, it is outside the scope of GST/HST rather than zero-rated. The supplier must be a GST/HST registrant, the recipient must be a non-resident, and the performance must be documented. Input tax credits attributable to zero-rated exports remain fully recoverable, so the business can claim a net refund of Canadian GST paid on inputs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canada Border Services Agency export report or E-export transaction number) for goods; not applicable to pure service supplies
  • Contract or purchase order with the non-resident shipowner or manning company clearly identifying the offshore crew services and place of performance
  • Commercial invoice describing the zero-rated services and showing no GST/HST charged
  • Bill of lading or equivalent vessel and crew deployment evidence when the service relates to an ocean-going ship
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT credit, or bank payment advice proving receipt of export proceeds from outside Canada

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government e-invoicing clearance for GST/HST. Registrants must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE and remit payments by approved electronic banking channels; invoices and supporting records must be retained in Canada, generally for six years, and CRA may request commercial documents, contracts, and bank statements to verify zero-rated exports.

CRA audits export claims under the GST/HST memoranda and may deny zero-rating if the documentary bundle is incomplete or the recipient or place of performance is not established. The denial exposes the ULC to a retroactive assessment of 5% GST/HST on the full consideration, compound daily interest, and, in cases of false statements or gross negligence, civil penalties and possible criminal prosecution. The normal reassessment window is four years from the filing due date; it becomes unlimited if the claim was made under misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a freely convertible floating currency with no general exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. However, cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by financial intermediaries under PCMLTFA Part I. Non-resident dividend remittances are subject to Part XIII withholding tax of 25%, reduced by treaty. Profit repatriation by a ULC is unrestricted subject to corporate solvency tests and adequate supporting documentation demonstrating arm's-length transactions and economic substance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules applicable to Canadian-resident corporations. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency. Canadian resident exporters must report earnings to the Canada Revenue Agency, and cross-border business transactions over CAD 10,000 in value must be reported for FINTRAC purposes, but no FX conversion is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is conducted under the authority of the Minister of Finance, with anti-money laundering supervision by FINTRAC and payment system oversight by the Bank of Canada under the Payment, Clearing and Settlement Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Payoneer, WorldFirst, Airwallex) are feasible as supplementary cross-border collection and crew-disbursement rails, but their primary use is Medium viability. They must not replace a Canadian chartered bank account because unlicensed EMI operations are not eligible for CDIC deposit insurance, are not directly supervised by OSFI, and may be constrained under the Retail Payment Activities Act if they cause clearing on Canadian rails. The ULC should maintain a domestic Schedule-I bank account as the treasury spine and use EMI accounts only for niche currency corridors or settlement in jurisdictions with restricted banking access.

Inbound capital injections from foreign shareholders into the Alberta ULC are executed over SWIFT with an MT103 using purpose code 'B1' (Capital Subscription) or 'C11' depending on the bank's tag 23B mapping. Route via a Canadian correspondent in Toronto/Vancouver and clear the instruction as 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION / CAPITAL INJECTION'. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate rate (Federal Base Part I rate 15% under ITA s.123(1)(a) plus Alberta general corporate income tax 8%). Applicable to a non-resident-owned Alberta ULC that is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (ITA s.212(2)) on dividends paid by the Alberta ULC to its non-resident shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline: within six months after the fiscal year-end (ITA s.150(1)). Balance-due day: two months after the fiscal year-end for a non-CCPC ULC (ITA s.157(1)(a)); three months where the corporation is a CCPC with taxable income within the small-business limit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no statutory revenue threshold for transfer-pricing documentation: each cross-border transaction with a non-resident related party is subject to contemporaneous documentation under ITA s.247(4) (prepared at or before the filing deadline for the ULC's taxation year). The s.247(3) penalty applies only where the aggregate transfer-pricing adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 and 10% of the ULC's gross revenue for the year. Canada's Country-by-Country reporting threshold is C$750 million (or EUR 750 million) in consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent; below that threshold, CRA nonetheless expects OECD-compliant Local File/Master File standard documentation as articulated in Information Circular IC 87-2R.

Economic substance scope: UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are not in force in Canada or Alberta. The ULC remains a fully taxable Canadian corporation regardless of its U.S. 'disregarded entity' tax election. The relevant substance-requirements are the GAAR (ITA s.245), the section 247 transfer-pricing recharacterization, statutory anti-hybrid and anti-conduit rules, and the principle that a ULC must maintain real Canadian substance (Alberta registered office, active crew-placement management, Canadian bank accounts, Canadian directors' meetings, payroll and payroll records maintained in Canada) to support treaty benefits and avoid a sham or factual-authority reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal tax evasion - marine crew payroll and placement-fee fraud

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1): on indictment, a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment for up to 5 years; on summary conviction, C$2,000 to C$100,000 fine and up to 2 years imprisonment. Civil gross-negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax. The normal four-year reassessment window is suspended indefinitely for misrepresentation (ITA s.152(4)(a)(i)).

Director personal liability: Directors who directed or acquiesced in the fraudulent filings are personally exposed in the ULC's unlimited-liability structure; the CRA can pursue director assets directly. Criminal arrest warrants, seizure of personal bank accounts, travel restrictions and bail conditions barring international departures (including for non-resident U.S. directors) apply; upon conviction, imprisonment to 5 years is available.
Shell-company and sham substance abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR (ITA s.245(2)) denies the tax benefit; the CRA may re-derive the ULC's taxable income under s.152(7), assess 50% gross-negligence penalties under s.163(2), and disallow refunds. In flagrant cases the ULC's files may be referred to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada for s.239 charges.

Director personal liability: Because a ULC has no capital shield, shareholders and directors face direct contractual and CRA claims; courts may treat deliberate asset stripping as a fraudulent conveyance and order civil restitution, injunctions freezing personal assets, and, for non-resident directors, removal under immigration enforcement measures.
Failure to remit Part XIII withholding on dividends and Treaty form abuse

Statutory sanction: ITA s.227(8): penalty of 10% of the unwithheld amount (20% where the failure continues) plus prescribed daily interest. Knowingly failing to remit is criminal under s.227(9): up to 200% of the tax and 5 years imprisonment. The ULC is also assessed for the withheld amounts as principal payer.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for the full unremitted Part XIII tax, interest and penalty. The CRA can issue civil execution against director assets without first exhausting the ULC; travel monitoring and restraint orders are available in enforcement proceedings where evasion is alleged.
Transfer pricing mispricing of crewing-management fees

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(2) re-determination of the arm's-length price and income; s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the aggregate adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue and contemporaneous documentation is absent. Recharacterized amounts may be treated as disguised dividends subject to Part XIII 25% withholding (or 5% under treaty).

Director personal liability: Directors who approve falsified intercompany contracts may face gross-negligence penalties; indemnity from the ULC is unenforceable in the ULC's unlimited-liability structure, and personal liability can be pursued through s.227.1 for associated withholding failures or through civil actions for breach of fiduciary duty.
