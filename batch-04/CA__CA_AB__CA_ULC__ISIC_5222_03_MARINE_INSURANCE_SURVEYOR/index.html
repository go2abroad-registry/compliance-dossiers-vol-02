Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — sole federal tax administrator for federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, and payroll; provincial corporate income tax for Alberta is administered by the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement and is filed through the CRA T2 return. Online portals: CRA 'My Business Account' and 'Business Registration Online' (BRO).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC): (i) incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act through the Alberta Corporate Registry; (ii) obtain a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' with the CRA or by using CRA Business Registration Online, completing Schedule A to open program accounts (RP/RM for corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST, RC for payroll); (iii) non-resident corporations without a Canadian establishment must register through CRA's Non-Resident Client Service using the same RC1/non-resident online module and must file a T2 Corporate Income Tax Return with non-resident schedules even if no Canadian tax is payable; (iv) GST/HST registration is compulsory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or where the non-resident makes specified taxable supplies in Canada; (v) Alberta provincial corporate tax is then assessed automatically by TRA through the CRA T2 process without a separate registration.

For U.S. federal purposes, the ULC may be classified as a disregarded entity or partnership under U.S. check-the-box rules, creating a hybrid entity; the CRA has an administrative position denying Canada-U.S. treaty benefits to such hybrid ULC structures where the income is not 'derived by' the U.S. resident member. — For a foreign-owned Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC): (i) incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act through the Alberta Corporate Registry; (ii) obtain a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' with the CRA or by using CRA Business Registration Online, completing Schedule A to open program accounts (RP/RM for corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST, RC for payroll); (iii) non-resident corporations without a Canadian establishment must register through CRA's Non-Resident Client Service using the same RC1/non-resident online module and must file a T2 Corporate Income Tax Return with non-resident schedules even if no Canadian tax is payable; (iv) GST/HST registration is compulsory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or where the non-resident makes specified taxable supplies in Canada; (v) Alberta provincial corporate tax is then assessed automatically by TRA through the CRA T2 process without a separate registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Alberta ULC is a corporation for Canadian federal income tax purposes and is not fiscally transparent: if its central management and control is in Canada it is a resident corporation taxed on worldwide income; otherwise it is a non-resident corporation taxable only on Canadian-source income (Part I tax, Part XIII withholding on passive payments, and Part XIV branch tax where applicable). For U.S. federal purposes, the ULC may be classified as a disregarded entity or partnership under U.S. check-the-box rules, creating a hybrid entity; the CRA has an administrative position denying Canada-U.S. treaty benefits to such hybrid ULC structures where the income is not 'derived by' the U.S. resident member.

Available election forms

There is no Canadian check-the-box or equivalent entity-classification election. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) U.S. IRC § 301.7701-3 election via IRS Form 8832 by the ULC's U.S. owner to characterize the ULC as disregarded, partnership, or corporation for U.S. purposes; (ii) Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 216 election permitting a non-resident to file on a net taxable basis for Canadian rents and timber royalties rather than Part XIII gross-basis withholding; (iii) ITA s. 217 election for optional net-basis taxation of certain Part XIII payments; (iv) hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4, 20.1, and 92.1 apply statutorily without election to deny deductions or recharacterize payments arising from hybrid entity arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of annual worldwide taxable supplies (small-supplier threshold), with mandatory registration for any person, including non-residents, carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada once the threshold is exceeded; prescribed non-resident suppliers of services relating to Canadian-situated goods or digital services may be required to register without a Canadian establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Excise Tax Act (ETA) Schedule VI is available for: (i) tangible personal property that is exported from Canada as part of the supply (Part V rules for goods); (ii) services supplied to a non-resident where the service does not relate to real property located in Canada and does not relate to tangible personal property located in Canada at the time the service is performed. For a marine hull/cargo surveyor, inspection and survey services performed inside Canada on a vessel or cargo physically located in Canadian territory are taxable at 5% GST even when billed to a non-resident underwriter; zero-rating applies to services performed outside Canada, digital survey analytics/report deliverables supplied to non-residents, and services in respect of property located outside Canada, provided the supplier retains contemporaneous proof of the non-resident status of the recipient and, where applicable, evidence of export.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or CBSA ACI/eManifest export report for goods physically exported from Canada
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Cargo Manifest evidencing delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice and signed survey contract identifying the non-resident underwriter and the precise scope and situs of the marine survey services
  • Bank remittance advice or SWIFT wire confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign-currency funds from the non-resident counterparty

No federal mandate for real-time electronic clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) verification. Canada has no API-based fiscal clearance regime; the CRA accepts paper, PDF, EDI, and Peppol-standard electronic invoices. Taxpayers must retain complete, auditable supporting records for at least six years. Voluntary Peppol interoperability is being adopted in some sectors, and Quebec has announced phased mandatory Peppol e-invoicing for large enterprises by 2026–2028, but this does not apply to an Alberta GST-nexus filer's invoicing regime.

The CRA may retroactively deny zero-rating, reassess GST/HST at the full 5% rate with compound daily interest at the prescribed rate, and impose civil penalties including the ETA s. 285 false-statement penalty of up to 25% of the net tax that would have been payable where a zero-rated supply is misclassified or supported by false or forged export documentation. The ordinary reassessment window is four years; it is unlimited where there is misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect in filing. Criminal prosecution under ETA and the Criminal Code of Canada can result in fines and imprisonment. Failure to repatriate foreign-currency proceeds within the 180-day commercial-substantiation benchmark (or within ordinary invoice terms) creates heightened audit risk of recharacterization of the supply as taxable in Canada and may attract transfer-pricing adjustment of the transaction price, although no statutory 180-day repatriation deadline exists under Canadian GST/HST law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII and imposes no exchange controls, no currency surrender requirements, and no licensing for cross-border capital movements. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is legal tender under the Currency Act, and marine insurance survey fee receipts, premium-sharing sums, and intercompany payments may be freely converted and remitted. Profit remittance from an Alberta ULC to a foreign shareholder is unrestricted at the banking level; however, dividends and deemed distributions to non-resident shareholders attract Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (25% statutory rate), reducible under an applicable tax treaty (a 10%-or-greater U.S. corporate parent generally qualifies for 5% withholding under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention). Because a ULC can elect pass-through treatment under ITA s. 248(1), remittances must be documented with board resolutions clearly distinguishing nontaxable returns of capital from taxable dividend distributions to prevent withholding leakage and bank-level documentation refusals.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for export proceeds repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for foreign-currency earnings. Marine survey fees invoiced in USD, EUR, or GBP may be retained offshore or held in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks. The sole compliance overlay is FINTRAC reporting: incoming or outgoing international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more per transaction are automatically reported by the financial intermediary, and the ULC must not structure payments to evade this threshold.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada maintains no standalone foreign exchange administration; exchange-control oversight is distributed among the Department of Finance Canada (monetary and exchange-rate policy), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (prudential supervision of banks, swaps, and derivatives), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (AML/CFT transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise Business (Wise Payments Ltd.) and comparable cross-border fintech EMIs (e.g., Payoneer) operate in Canada as FINTRAC-registered money services businesses rather than deposit-taking banks. Feasibility rating for this Alberta ULC marine survey operation: HIGH for operational disbursement execution, including settlement of sub-CAD 50,000 invoices, contractor payments in USD/EUR, and collection of foreign-currency survey fees through multi-currency account rails; LOW for capital escrow or underwriter-claim trust balances, because Alberta escrow practice and Schedule I lender undertakings require a CDIC-insured deposit-taking institution. Legal constraint: funds held through a fintech EMI are pooled in omnibus bank accounts and do not attract Canadian bank deposit protection; accordingly, the ULC must maintain its primary operating and custodian relationship with a Schedule I bank and use an EMI solely as a low-value payment-execution rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the foreign parent initiates a SWIFT MT103 (Category 601 or 699 financial transfer) to the ULC's CAD or USD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank; for amounts above the remitting bank's value threshold, an MT202 cover may accompany the MT103 detail. The wire instruction must cite purpose code CAPL (capital contribution) and state 'Contribution to stated capital of ULC - no commercial invoice'; trade-related RV/TRADE codes must be avoided to prevent customs and tax review. For intercompany loans, the remittance must reference a written cross-border loan agreement, apply an arm's-length interest rate, and cite purpose code BONL; the payment narrative must identify the Alberta corporate registry number and the named beneficiary ULC. CAD-leg settlement clears through Payments Canada Large Value Transfer System or the new Settlement Optimization Interface; USD-leg settlement clears Fedwire or CHIPS through the Schedule I bank's U.S. correspondent. The receiving bank will hold the credit pending upload of the capital-contribution board resolution and a source-of-funds letter from the remitting institution; FINTRAC international electronic funds transfer reporting is automatic above CAD 10,000.
