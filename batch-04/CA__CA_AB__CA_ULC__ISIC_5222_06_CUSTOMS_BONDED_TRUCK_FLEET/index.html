Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Alberta law permits a registered office at a physical address within Alberta at which statutory records may be inspected and process served (ABCA s 20); third-party virtual-office providers are acceptable only if they maintain an actual Alberta street address and licensed registry representation. However, an ISIC 5222 GPS-sealed bonded road-feeder fleet cannot operate with mere virtual substance: the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) requires the corporation to hold and maintain a customs bonded carrier license, a physical dispatcher/terminal facility in Alberta, security bonds, and documented in-transit control procedures under the Customs Act, RSC 1985, c 1 (2nd Supp), Part V. A virtual office is therefore acceptable only as the statutory registered address, not as commercial substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada observes IMF Article VIII full current and capital account convertibility. The Canadian dollar is freely floating and internationally accepted. No foreign-exchange approval is required for equity injections, trade settlements, debt servicing, or profit remittances by an Alberta ULC. Dividend distributions to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, potentially reduced by tax treaty, but are not blocked or licensed by any foreign-exchange authority. Non-Canadian investors establishing or acquiring a Canadian business should also verify the Investment Canada Act notification/review obligation; this is an investment-screening requirement, not a currency-control restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada does not impose statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory repatriation timelines, or minimum conversion ratios. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted at the client's discretion. Financial institutions must report completed international electronic funds transfers (IEFTs) and large cash/currency transactions of C$10,000 or more to FINTRAC, and must maintain underlying trade documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority, LVTS oversight, and Retail Payment Activities Act registration); FINTRAC (AML/CFT and international wire/cross-border transaction reporting); OSFI (prudential supervision of bank FX risk). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration at the federal level.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail; Medium/Low as an ancillary payout rail. An offshore fintech or electronic money institution is not an authorized deposit-taking institution under the Canadian Bank Act and cannot provide LVTS or Canadian clearing access. Such an EMI may operate in Canada only if registered with FINTRAC as a money services business and, where applicable, recognized under the Retail Payment Activities Act (administered by the Bank of Canada). The ULC should use a Schedule I Canadian bank as the master clearing counterparty; an EMI may serve only as a supplementary payment channel for niche cross-border payouts, with balances maintained at very low levels.
