Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to NVOCC ocean freight forwarding services where the goods are physically exported from Canada (Schedule VI s.23) or where the service is provided to a non-resident and performed wholly outside Canada (Schedule VII). Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating, fully convertible current and capital account regime. No exchange control approvals are required for repatriation of profits, dividends, interest, royalties, or loan repayments. Profit remittance from an Alberta ULC to non-resident shareholders is generally subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (default 25% for non-treaty residents, reducible to 5% or 15% under tax treaties). A ULC may elect to be treated as a corporation for Canadian tax purposes; non-resident ownership is unrestricted. Capital outbound transfers must also comply with FINTRAC reporting thresholds, sanctions screening, and anti-money-laundering declaration requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion obligation exists for export proceeds. Canadian residents may hold and deploy export receipts in any eligible currency. NVOCC service receipts denominated in USD or other foreign currencies may be credited to approved foreign-currency deposit accounts at Canadian chartered banks or held offshore, subject to standard reporting, record-keeping, and anti-money-laundering obligations. No foreign exchange transaction tax or repatriation timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the competent AML/CFT supervisor; Ministry of Finance for capital controls and economic sanctions administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium feasibility for non-resident electronic money institutions (EMIs) and offshore payment fintechs. Canadian bank authorization protects 'bank' terminology; non-regulated fintechs cannot offer deposit accounts or settle NVOCC commercial letters of credit. EMI accounts may be used only as auxiliary disbursement rails where underlying funds flow through a Canadian-regulated account. The ULC should rely primarily on Schedule I Canadian banks and may use licensed foreign money-services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC for FX conversion; pure offshore EMIs are legally constrained for holding client freight monies because they are not Canadian deposit-taking institutions and cannot issue enforceable deposit receipts in Canada.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate income tax rate: 15% federal and 8% Alberta. A qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation may generally access an 11% combined small-business rate on up to CAD 500,000 of eligible active business income: 9% federal and 2% Alberta. A non-resident-owned ULC is generally not a CCPC and normally cannot access the CCPC small-business rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends paid or credited to a non-resident, subject to treaty reduction. Under the Canada-United States treaty, dividends are generally reduced to 5% where the beneficial owner is a qualifying corporate shareholder meeting the ownership threshold and 15% in other qualifying cases. Other treaties commonly provide 5% or 10% for qualifying corporate ownership and 15% or 25% otherwise.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 and AT1 are generally due within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. The federal and Alberta tax balances are generally due two months after year-end; a qualifying CCPC may generally have three months for the federal balance and, subject to Alberta conditions, the Alberta balance. Instalments are generally due monthly, with limited quarterly options for eligible corporations.

Deadline: · Frequency:

GST/HST returns are generally due one month after the end of the reporting period, subject to annual-filer rules and special deadlines. GST/HST balances are generally due with the return. Corporate tax instalments are due during the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no general statutory revenue threshold that eliminates contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions. T106 reporting generally applies when aggregate reportable transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents exceed CAD 1 million, subject to statutory exceptions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to a Canadian-resident ultimate parent or surrogate parent of an MNE group meeting the EUR 750 million consolidated-revenue threshold. Canada does not generally impose an OECD-style mandatory Local File/Master File filing requirement for every taxpayer, but contemporaneous documentation must support the arm's-length result.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone economic-substance regulation equivalent to the UAE, Cayman Islands or certain other ESR regimes. Substance remains relevant under the Income Tax Act through the general anti-avoidance rule, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty anti-abuse provisions, foreign-affiliate rules, thin-capitalization rules and principal-purpose analysis. The ULC should maintain Alberta management, decision-making, personnel, premises, operating systems, customer and carrier contracts, risk controls, capitalization and records proportionate to its NVOCC and freight-forwarding functions. A contractual commission or pass-through label does not override the actual functions, assets and risks performed in Canada.
