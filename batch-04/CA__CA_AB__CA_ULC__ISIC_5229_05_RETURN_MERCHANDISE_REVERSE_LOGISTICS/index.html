Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Alberta requires the registered office of every Alberta ULC to be a physical address in Alberta at which documents may be served during normal business hours. A post office box is strictly prohibited. A virtual office service that provides a real physical street address, a dedicated office number, and mail handling may be acceptable for registered office purposes. However, for tax residency and Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) registration under the Excise Tax Act, and to preserve the pass-through benefits of a ULC, the entity must establish sufficient commercial substance, including ordinary business operations, books and records, and appropriately held meetings. A 'mailbox-only' virtual office is not sufficient for Canada Revenue Agency purposes, and may trigger audit or reassessment risk.

None - Alberta imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement for a ULC; shares may be issued for money, property, or past services, and subscribed capital may be nominal (e.g., CAD $1.00 per share).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — the national tax administration for the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST), operating through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, My Business Account, and the GST/HST NETFILE system; Alberta corporate income tax is assessed and collected by the CRA on behalf of Alberta under a federal-provincial administration agreement with Alberta Treasury Board and Finance. Portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; incorporation registry: Alberta Corporate Registry.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (AB ULC): (1) incorporate under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) via the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain the Alberta Corporation Number; (2) apply to the CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts RC (corporation income tax) and RT (GST/HST) electronically through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by paper Form RC1, with Form RC348 for non-resident GST/HST registration and proof of a Canadian financial institution account; (3) if the ULC is intended to be treated as a flow-through partnership for Canadian tax purposes (no subsection 216(1) Income Tax Act election), the ULC must file a T5013 Partnership Information Return, and each non-resident shareholder must obtain its own BN, report Canadian-source income, and rely on applicable tax treaty permanent-establishment protection; (4) for U.S. shareholders, file IRS Form 8832 (check-the-box) to align U.S. federal classification with the Canadian flow-through treatment.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a foreign-owned Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (AB ULC): (1) incorporate under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) via the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain the Alberta Corporation Number; (2) apply to the CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts RC (corporation income tax) and RT (GST/HST) electronically through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by paper Form RC1, with Form RC348 for non-resident GST/HST registration and proof of a Canadian financial institution account; (3) if the ULC is intended to be treated as a flow-through partnership for Canadian tax purposes (no subsection 216(1) Income Tax Act election), the ULC must file a T5013 Partnership Information Return, and each non-resident shareholder must obtain its own BN, report Canadian-source income, and rely on applicable tax treaty permanent-establishment protection; (4) for U.S. shareholders, file IRS Form 8832 (check-the-box) to align U.S. federal classification with the Canadian flow-through treatment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under subsection 216(1) of the Canadian Income Tax Act, an Alberta ULC may irrevocably elect to be deemed a corporation for Canadian income tax purposes; absent that election, the CRA administratively treats the ULC as a partnership for Canadian tax purposes, with each shareholder's share of income and loss flowing through and no entity-level tax, provided the relevant conditions are satisfied. Complementary elections address single-tier treatment for corporate groups; Canadian hybrid mismatch arrangement rules apply to cross-border flow-through structures, and for U.S. federal tax purposes, an IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election may be used to achieve disregarded-entity or partnership status that mirrors the Canadian classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD — small supplier threshold under subsection 240(1) of the Excise Tax Act, measured by total worldwide taxable supplies (before GST/HST) in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or the current calendar quarter; a reverse logistics ULC exceeding the threshold must register for GST/HST, and non-resident ULCs making taxable supplies in Canada are also registrable; voluntary registration is advisable to claim input tax credits on inbound freight, warehousing, grading equipment, and Canadian operating costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For an Alberta ULC conducting cross-border returned-goods reverse logistics and quality grading, zero-rating eligibility arises as follows: (a) supplies of tangible personal property exported by or on behalf of the supplier are zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the goods are shipped or delivered to a destination outside Canada; (b) services supplied to a non-resident person may be zero-rated under section 3 of Part V of Schedule VI unless the service is performed on tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, relates to Canadian real property, or relates to intangibles used in Canada; (c) repair, maintenance, cleaning, or restoration services performed on returned goods situated in Canada for a non-resident owner may be zero-rated under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI where the goods are not further processed in Canada and are exported by the non-resident owner; (d) quality-grading and re-packaging services falling outside the enumerated zero-rated categories and performed while goods remain in Canada generally attract 5% GST; however, the GST is recoverable as an input tax credit by a GST-registered recipient, and services performed in a customs bonded warehouse for goods in transit may be zero-rated; all zero-rating claims require documentary evidence of export and supporting payment records to be maintained for CRA audit.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CESR) or an authorized service provider, evidencing the physical export from Canada
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) naming the Alberta ULC as exporter and confirming delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent SWIFT/ACH foreign-exchange advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer within the repatriation window (FINTRAC record-keeping requirement)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and quality-grading report describing the goods and confirming the non-resident owner's title
  • Original CBSA import entry (Form B3 / Customs Commercial Entry in the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management system) demonstrating lawful prior importation and identity of the returned goods being re-exported

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing clearance regime. GST/HST invoices may be issued electronically if they contain the prescribed information under section 169 of the Excise Tax Act and the Information Returns Regulations (supplier name, BN/RT program account, invoice date, description, consideration, GST rate, and tax amount). The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework based on Peppol PINT specifications is voluntary, and the CRA requires that all GST/HST records be retained for six years at a place of business in Canada or be retrievable electronically on demand.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST, input tax credits, and zero-rated export treatment within the normal four-year limitation period under subsection 298(1) of the Excise Tax Act, with the period extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default. False or unsupported zero-rating claims attract a civil penalty under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the tax that would have been payable or the refund claimed, plus arrears interest; late or deficient remittances carry failure penalties under section 280; and wilful evasion or deliberate falsification of export records is an offence under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act punishable by fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, confiscation of proceeds, and imprisonment, with parallel Criminal Code exposure. Where the ULC operates as a flow-through entity, assessments and clawbacks may be issued directly against unlimited-liability shareholders, and recaptured amounts include input tax credits previously claimed, GST/HST deemed self-supply on goods diverted to domestic distribution, zero-rating reversals, and associated penalties on both the ULC and its non-resident principals; tax treaties do not limit GST/HST assessments because indirect taxes fall outside their scope.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized current and capital account environment. There are no exchange controls under the Currency Act or the Bank of Canada Act; the Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible. Profit remittance and dividend distributions are not subject to exchange approval, but non-resident withholding tax may apply under the Income Tax Act to dividends, interest, rents, and royalties, subject to treaty relief. Capital repatriation of share redemption or liquidation proceeds is unrestricted once applicable Canadian tax clearance is obtained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose statutory surrender or conversion timelines for export proceeds. Foreign currency receipts may be retained in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks; however, reporting obligations may arise under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and FATF/CRS information exchange regimes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as bank prudential regulator; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/CFT supervisor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Medium. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of all specified non-arm's length transactions with non-residents in a taxation year; taxpayers above this threshold must prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation and file Form T1066.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate Economic Substance Regulations comparable to those in the UAE; however, section 245 of the ITA (general anti-avoidance rule) and section 247 (transfer pricing) operate as functional substitutes. An Alberta ULC conducting cross-border reverse logistics is required to demonstrate real operational substance, including a physical facility, commercial decision-making, personnel, and inventory-handling capability in Alberta. Without such substance, CRA may recharacterize the arrangement, collapse transfer-pricing efficiency, and treat the ULC as a conduit for Canadian-source profit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under section 163(2) of the ITA equal to 50% of the understated tax; criminal conviction under section 239(1) imposes a fine not exceeding 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 2 years; interest is compounded daily on all assessed amounts.

Director personal liability: A director who personally participated in, directed, or acquiesced to the false filing may be subject to criminal fines and imprisonment; the director may also be jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding taxes and other trust monies under section 227.1, with the CRA permitted to levy bank accounts and initiate cross-border asset seizure proceedings.
Failure to File T2 and T1066

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalty of 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month up to 12 months; repeat failure escalates to 10% plus 2% per month up to 20 months; T1066 filing failure is penalized at CAD 100 per day, minimum CAD 1,000, maximum CAD 25,000; interest accrues from the original balance-due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately orchestrate non-filing may be named as participants in an evasion under section 239 and may personally face prosecution; where the corporation has failed to remit source deductions, directors face civil liability under section 227.1, and issuance of a CRA compliance order may trigger travel restrictions or denial of passports.
Transfer-Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess the transactions at the arm's length price, deny deductions, apply compound interest, and impose a 10% penalty under section 247(12) on the net transfer-pricing adjustment where documentation is incomplete or inaccurate; valuations involving intangibles or grading technology may be further recharacterized.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or direct transfer-pricing invoices that misvalue services can be jointly and severally assessed for the 10% penalty; the same adjustments may produce deemed dividends and Part XIII withholding liability, for which directors who failed to withhold are personally responsible.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Violation / Surplus Stripping

Statutory sanction: Part XIII tax is payable as if no withholding occurred, plus compound interest; failure-to-withhold penalties under section 227(8) equal 10% of the amount not deducted, or 20% where the failure is knowingly made; section 212.1 may recharacterize a non-arm's length transfer of shares to a Canadian corporation as a dividend and assess tax on the resulting surplus.

Director personal liability: Directors of the ULC are personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes under section 227.1 if they knew of the failure or failed to exercise care; personal recovery may include garnishment, seizure of personal assets, and restrictions on international travel under federal tax debt collection and criminal enforcement powers.
Artificial Shell Company and General Anti-Avoidance Rule

Statutory sanction: CRA may invoke the general anti-avoidance rule under section 245 to disregard or recharacterize the arrangement, deny the tax benefit, reassess the ULC on the Canadian-source income, and charge interest; third-party penalties under section 163.2 may apply to advisors or promoters who knowingly sold the structure; CRA may also apply the sham doctrine and the substance-over-form principle.

Director personal liability: When the corporate veil is pierced, directors may be held personally accountable for restitution of the diverted funds, all reassessed Part XIII taxes, interest, and penalties; participation in a sham scheme to evade tax is a criminal offense carrying up to 2 years' imprisonment and fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded.
