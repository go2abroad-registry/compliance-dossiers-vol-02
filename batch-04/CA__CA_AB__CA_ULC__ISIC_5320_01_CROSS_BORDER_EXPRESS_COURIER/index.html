Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), business-facing portal 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO) at Canada.ca; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement; customs and import/export control: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate a ULC under the Alberta Business Corporations Act or register the existing BC ULC extra-provincially in Alberta to obtain an Alberta Corporate Registry number. 2. Apply for the 15-digit CRA Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1. 3. Activate CRA program accounts: RC0001 (federal and Alberta Corporation Income Tax), RT0001 (GST/HST), RP0001 (payroll, if employees are engaged) and RM0001 (CBSA Import/Export account). 4. For express courier operations, enrol with CBSA as an approved eManifest/courier carrier and establish a CARM client profile for duty and GST accounting. 5. File annual T2 Corporation Income Tax Return for the ULC (Canadian fiscal period) even where the ULC is treated as a disregarded entity in the United States. 6. Remit corporate tax instalments to CRA; non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII gross-basis withholding, with US-resident shareholders eligible for treaty relief.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

U.S. check-the-box election under Treas. Reg. 301.7701-3 (Form 8832) to treat the Canadian ULC as a disregarded entity/pass-through for U.S. federal tax purposes, whilst it remains a legal Canadian corporation taxed at entity level (opaque) under the Income Tax Act; this deliberate hybrid position is governed by the Canada-US treaty tie-breaker and the OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch rules implemented in ITA ss. 18.2-18.3 (deductibility disallowances for hybrid payments made after 30 June 2022). Additional statutory mechanics: GST/HST registration election as a 'registrant' under ETA s. 240(2); voluntary 'small supplier' registration; election for closely related corporations to deem supplies made for nil consideration (ETA s. 156(2)); and Alberta tax harmonisation (no separate provincial PST election; Alberta maintains a single federal GST layer).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, tested on the aggregate consideration of all taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies such as export courier services) sourced to Canada in the current and preceding four calendar quarters; registration is mandatory once exceeded, with voluntary registration available to below-threshold businesses that carry on commercial activity in Canada (ETA ss. 148 and 240).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (effective 0%) is available to the GST/HST registrant on (a) taxable goods delivered to a non-resident outside Canada or to a common carrier for export and exported as soon as reasonably practicable (ETA Sch. VI Part V, s. 1), and (b) cross-border courier and transportation services that convey goods from a place in Canada to a place outside Canada (ETA Sch. VI Part VI). The supply consideration is excluded from GST/HST and the attributable input tax credits become refundable, producing a net GST refund position for the export-heavy express courier business. Eligibility requires the ULC to establish a foreign consignee/destination, actual departure of goods, contemporaneous commercial and transport documentation, and export within the normal time frame; no GST collection is made on zero-rated supplies, while domestic courier legs within Canada remain taxable at 5%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic CADEX equivalent) or CBSA-accepted consolidated manifest release as proof of exit
  • Commercial invoice or Canada Customs Invoice (CI1) with harmonised-system classification, declared value, currency and Incoterms
  • Air waybill / bill of lading / courier master waybill showing a foreign destination address, shipment date and carrier details
  • Inward remittance certificate: SWIFT/MT103, bank settlement advice, or other verifiable foreign-currency receipt tracing the export proceeds
  • Destination-country customs entry, delivery confirmation or signed proof-of-delivery from the consignee evidencing completion of export

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous-transaction-control e-invoicing mandate. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or CRA EDI/XML channels where the registrant has annual revenue exceeding CAD 1 million, and remittances above CAD 10,000 require electronic payment. Cross-border express courier shipments are cleared through CBSA eManifest (advance commercial information), the Single Window Initiative, and the CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) client accounting platform; courier releases are secured by approved electronic manifests and consolidated entry prior to arrival. All GST/HST records, invoices, export evidence and clearance manifests must be retained for six years.

CRA maintains targeted audit programs for zero-rated export and courier commodity flows ('export scams'). Deficient or fabricated export evidence triggers reassessment of GST/HST at 5% on gross consideration, denial of attributable input tax credits, and extension of the normal four-year limitation to ten years for misrepresentation (ETA s. 298(2); ITA s. 152(4)(a)(ii)). Civil penalties: ETA s. 285.1 false-statement penalty of CAD 250 plus 25% of the net-tax misstatement, escalated to 50% where the statement was made knowingly or in circumstances of gross negligence; ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty at 50% of the misstatement. Criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239(1) carries fines of 50%-200% of evaded amounts and imprisonment up to five years, and may result in CBSA suspending or revoking courier/customs release privileges and CRA disqualifying directors. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; however, unrepatriated receivables beyond the 180-day benchmark may be recharacterised as shareholder loans under ITA s. 15(2) (with Part XIII withholding under s. 212) or adjusted under the transfer-pricing rules in s. 247, producing immediate income inclusions and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates under a hybrid regime of current-account convertibility for residents and non-residents on trade and service transactions, with capital-account transactions subject to reporting thresholds administered by the Bank of Canada. Profit remittances by Canadian-resident corporations are permitted without prior approval, subject to tax filing obligations and FINTRAC AML reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirement applies to service exports such as cross-border express courier operations. Proceeds may be retained offshore; however, all receipts must be reported to FINTRAC within prescribed timelines and supported by commercial documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore EMI issuance is materially constrained by Canadian AML/KYC obligations and FATF compliance; domestic Canadian fintech platforms or licensed money-service businesses are the only viable channels for cross-border courier settlements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% general corporate rate (15% federal basic rate as reduced by general corporate income tax reduction and provincial abatement, plus 8% Alberta corporate branch tax rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on every dividend paid or credited by the ULC to a non-resident person; Alberta does not impose a separate provincial dividend withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Fiscal year-end plus 6 months for filing the T2 return; final instalment and balance remitted to CRA by the last day of the month in which the statutory payment date falls.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 100 — Revenue Gross revenue from time-definite international courier pickup, linehaul, customs clearance, and final-mile delivery, stated per Canadian GAAP/IFRS and including fuel surcharges, OVP charges, and revenue from non-resident affiliates only if attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment or reported allocable income. A non-resident ULC carrying on business in Alberta such that it has a permanent establishment there must report only Canadian-source business revenue and allocate branch-net profit on the basis of the T2 return; worldwide revenue must not be included to the extent not attributable to the Canadian PE. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax for the first day plus 1% per full month for up to 12 months; a second or subsequent failure within three years increases the first-tier penalty to 10% and each month to 2% for up to 20 months. CRA interest compounds daily on arrears and penalties at the prescribed quarterly rate (currently 8% for corporate underpayments as of 2025).
Schedule 1 — Canadian Small Business Deduction Computation of active business income eligible for the federal small business deduction; for an express courier ULC, the activity is active if it employs more than 5 full-time employees and the ULC is not a specified financial institution. The small business deduction is unavailable to non-resident-owned investment corporations and to corporations controlled, directly or indirectly, by non-residents unless they are otherwise qualified CCPCs; attributing ownership and control from foreign parent companies will negate or reduce the schedule. Schedule 21 — Corporation Income Tax Rate Reconciliation Reconciliation of federal provincial tax rates, including the 15% federal general rate and 8% Alberta rate, and any reduction for manufacturing and processing profits or small business deductions. For a non-resident corporation, the Alberta branch tax treatment under the Income Tax Act Part XIV may be reported; however, a treaty-protected PE with no branch tax rate reduction must complete the rate reconciliation carefully. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax for the first day plus 1% per full month for up to 12 months; a second or subsequent failure within three years increases the first-tier penalty to 10% and each month to 2% for up to 20 months. CRA interest compounds daily on arrears and penalties at the prescribed quarterly rate (currently 8% for corporate underpayments as of 2025).
Form T106 — Non-Arm’s Length Transactions Annual information return for each transaction or arrangement between the ULC and a non-resident related corporation, including management fees, interline transportation charges, aircraft or vehicle leasing, route rights, royalty for software, and allocation of back-office costs. The form is mandatory where the total arm’s-length value of reportable non-arm’s-length transactions exceeds CAD 1 million in a taxation year; a non-resident ULC must file T106 if it transacts with its foreign parent or affiliates. Failure to file triggers strict penalties. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax for the first day plus 1% per full month for up to 12 months; a second or subsequent failure within three years increases the first-tier penalty to 10% and each month to 2% for up to 20 months. Non-filing or late filing of Form T106 is CAD 100 per day up to CAD 25,000. False or omitted information can attract the accuracy penalty of 50% of the understated tax under subsection 163(2). CRA interest compounds daily on arrears and penalties at the prescribed quarterly rate (currently 8% for corporate underpayments as of 2025).
