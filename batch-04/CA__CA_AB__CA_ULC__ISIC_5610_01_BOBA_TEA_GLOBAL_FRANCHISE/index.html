Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Alberta ULC; the Corporation may be incorporated with any number of no-par value common shares and no escrow account or capital deposit is required. The founding shareholder's contributed capital is recorded in common shares and contributed surplus to support franchise royalty collection and syrup supply chain operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada. Registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration-online; My Business Account (MyBA) at canada.ca/my-cra-business-account for GST/HST and federal corporate income tax accounts. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the provincial tax collection agreement; Alberta does not issue a separate corporate tax licence.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta ULC must first be incorporated or continued under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) and, if required, extra-provincially registered. For tax purposes, apply to CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) via BRO or, for non-resident applicants that cannot use BRO, file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts' with CRA, attaching the certificate of incorporation, company summary, directors' register and/or appointment details, and a signed declaration of the controlling officer. The BN-RT account (GST/HST account) is the statutory VAT/TIN equivalent; no separate 'TIN' number is issued by Alberta for corporate income tax. The applicant must also register for CRA payroll account (BN-PE) and Alberta workers' compensation coverage if the ULC employs staff in Alberta. CRA will assign a 9-digit BN plus two-letter program identifier; GST/HST registration is obtained by checking the RT box on BN application or calling CRA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (under Canadian domestic income tax, the Alberta ULC is a corporation and is taxed as a separate person; no Canadian pass-through election is recognized. The same entity may be 'Pass_Through_Eligible' for foreign classification purposes, e.g., under U.S. check-the-box, but that election does not affect its Canadian tax status).

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election under the Income Tax Act. The ULC remains a corporation for all Canadian federal and provincial income tax purposes. For foreign purposes, the entity may file IRS Form 8832 or local equivalent to elect pass-through/disregarded status, but CRA tax attributes are computed at entity level. Domestic elections relevant to the structure include the GST/HST group election under Excise Tax Act s. 240 to treat related Canadian residents as one registrant, and the ETA quick-method accounting election if turnover qualifies. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are attacked under Income Tax Act ss. 18.2 and 18.3; no domestic statutory 'hybrid election' is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada in a single calendar quarter, or C$30,000 over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (excluding capital property and financial services). A ULC carrying on commercial activity in Canada and exceeding the threshold must register immediately; a non-resident ULC supplying zero-rated exports generally can register voluntarily to recover associated input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% GST applies to goods exported by or on behalf of the supplier when the goods are shipped to a destination outside Canada and exported within a reasonable time; and to certain supplies of services and intangible property (including a franchise license or royalty stream) made to a non-resident franchisee where the supply is not excluded by Schedule VI, Parts V and VI of the Excise Tax Act. Excluded services include services rendered in Canada to an individual present in Canada, services relating to real property situated in Canada, and services in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Franchise royalties from a non-resident should be supported by a written licence/franchise agreement demonstrating the principal purpose of the payment is for use outside Canada and that no service is performed in Canada for the benefit of the non-resident that would remove zero-rating.

  • CBSA Form B13A Export Declaration (or electronic CBSA declaration) for controlled/prohibited goods or goods with a value exceeding the CBSA reporting threshold
  • Commercial invoice with HS classification, country of origin, export value, and statement of GST zero-rating
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or carrier/forwarder delivery confirmation showing ultimate foreign destination and export date
  • Freight/courier movement evidence (tracking records, ports/airports of exit, export permits if applicable)
  • Proof of foreign payment and remittance (SWIFT/Wire confirmations, banking settlement advices, franchisee royalty statements, and any inward remittance certificate or foreign exchange conversion record)

Canada does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance, mandated API clearance, or digital-stamping system for GST/HST. CRA requires prescribed invoice content set out in ETA s. 221(1) (e.g., supplier name and GST/HST account number, date, description, amount, and GST rate). E-filing is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding C$1.5 million, who must file via GST/HST NETFILE, an approved service provider, or an electronic return scheme through MyBA. All GST/HST returns and registrations are administered through CRA's electronic gateway; paper filing remains available for smaller registrants in limited circumstances.

CRA and CBSA coordinate export-data audits. If a zero-rated export is documented improperly or is fraudulent, CRA may retroactively claw back the 5% GST (i.e., reassess the supply at 5%), deny input tax credits related to the supply, and charge compounded daily arrears interest. Penalties include an ETA failure-to-file/remit penalty of 5% plus 1% per full month of outstanding tax (maximum 12 months), a net tax misstatement penalty, and an ITA gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the misstated amount if made knowingly or under gross negligence. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent GST evasion may lead to fines and imprisonment; non-resident franchisees' 180-day repatriation windows and transfer-pricing documentation are also scrutinized as potential adjustments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar is a free-floating currency and no prior Bank of Canada approval is required for inbound equity, loans, trade payments, royalty remittances, dividends, or capital repatriation. A non-resident contribution into an Alberta ULC is treated as direct investment for balance-of-payments purposes. Compliance hurdles are limited to: (a) Investment Canada Act notification/review if a non-Canadian acquires control of a Canadian business; (b) Part XIII CRA withholding tax on outbound interest, royalties, and dividends unless reduced under an applicable income tax treaty and substantiated via NR301/NR302 forms; (c) FINTRAC reporting of cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; and (d) transfer-pricing documentation under Section 247 of the Income Tax Act for cross-border transactions with non-arm's length parties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion rules for export proceeds. Export receipts, including overseas franchise royalties and syrup supply payments, may be retained in foreign currency or converted to CAD at commercial rates. Financial institutions must submit cross-border wire transfer reports to FINTRAC for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more, and the ULC should maintain original sales invoices, franchise agreements, and bank credit advisories to evidence the underlying economic purpose of each receipt.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank supervision, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT cross-border wire monitoring, and the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for tax and non-resident reporting. Canada has no dedicated exchange-licensing authority because there is no general exchange-control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. An offshore digital EMI may be used for cross-border collection of small-value franchise fees and syrup payments, but it cannot act as a Canadian chartered bank, cannot accept deposits in the legal sense, and must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC before providing remittance, FX dealing, or payment processing services to persons ordinarily resident in Canada. A registered EMI is also subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) framework, which requires operational risk and safeguarding controls. The ULC should maintain a Tier 1 Canadian bank account as the final settlement and clearing account, with the EMI used only as a collection conduit, never as the treasury master account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions) must be filed when the value of reportable transactions with a non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000; Master File and Local File documentation required under OECD guidelines for large cross-border operations; Country-by-Country Reporting applies only to consolidated groups exceeding CAD 750,000,000 in revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act, recharacterizing transactions that lack economic substance and frustrate the object, spirit, or purpose of the tax regime. The CRA may also invoke doctrines such as sham transactions, accommodation arrangements, and economic reality to pierce artificial corporate structures that are mere shells or conduits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statement

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239: fines of CAD 10,000 to CAD 100,000 or up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment for up to five years; gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated amount under s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in or authorized false statements can be held personally liable for the corporation's tax liability; the corporate veil may be pierced where there is willful blindness to tax fraud.
Paying or Authorizing Dividend Without Withholding

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent is personally liable for the unwithheld tax, interest, and penalties under Part XIII (s.215); failure to withhold carries a 10% penalty plus daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII amounts and associated penalties under s.227.1, even if the ULC is dissolved.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under s.247(2) plus a penalty equal to 10% of the net upward adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; interest on reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be exposed to liability if they knowingly sign misstated intercompany pricing or fail to maintain contemporaneous documentation; piercing of the corporate veil may occur if the ULC is deemed an instrument of passive misconduct.
Artificial Shell / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization may disallow the tax benefit and reallocate income; gross negligence penalty may be imposed for misrepresentation; unclaimed property, corporate law misuse, and anti-money-laundering sanctions may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders may face civil liability for fraudulent conveyance, travel bans in cross-border investigations, and imprisonment for international tax fraud where the corporate form is deliberately abused.
