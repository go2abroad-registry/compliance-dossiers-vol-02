Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Canadian ULC is generally a corporation for Canadian federal and Alberta income tax purposes and is not a Canadian pass-through entity. Canada does not provide a general check-the-box election to disregard a Canadian corporation. A ULC may be eligible to elect disregarded-entity or partnership treatment under United States entity-classification rules if it meets the U.S. eligibility requirements; that election does not change its Canadian classification. Cross-border hybrid treatment can trigger treaty, withholding, foreign tax credit, and hybrid-mismatch consequences. Confirm the ULC's formation statute and applicable U.S. rules before relying on an election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies worldwide in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, subject to the detailed small-supplier rules and exceptions. A person exceeding the threshold in a single quarter generally ceases to be a small supplier on the supply that causes the threshold to be exceeded; different timing applies when the threshold is exceeded over four consecutive quarters. Non-residents carrying on business in Canada may have registration obligations that depend on their status, activities, and the special rules for non-residents. Assess the facts rather than treating CAD 30,000 as a universal exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying exports of goods may be zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act where the statutory export conditions are met and the supplier retains evidence supporting export and the purchaser and delivery facts. Exported services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer is foreign or pays from abroad; the specific service-export provisions, exclusions, recipient status, and place-of-supply rules must be satisfied. Restaurant meals physically supplied and consumed in Alberta are ordinarily taxable at 5% GST, including meals purchased by non-residents. Zero-rating is generally not the applicable treatment for meals served in Canada. A GST registrant may claim eligible input tax credits, subject to documentation and statutory restrictions; refunds are governed by the ordinary GST return rules.

  • Canadian export declaration or applicable export reporting record, where required under customs and export-control rules
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier record, or other carrier proof identifying the goods and destination

Canada has no general federal real-time GST invoice-clearance, mandatory e-invoicing API, or digital tax-stamping regime for ordinary restaurant transactions. GST registrants must keep records and issue compliant invoices or receipts containing prescribed information when required, including for input tax credit support. Electronic records and invoices are generally permitted if statutory record-keeping and accessibility requirements are met. Separate provincial, public-procurement, payment-network, or sector-specific requirements may apply.

Canada has no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. The CRA may audit zero-rated supplies and deny the treatment or assess unremitted GST, interest, and applicable penalties if the statutory conditions or supporting records are absent. False statements, omissions, or gross negligence may attract penalties under the Excise Tax Act; knowing tax evasion or fraud may result in prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an Article VIII member of the IMF and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the Canadian dollar. There are no general exchange-control approvals for profit remittances, dividends, capital repatriation, or trade payments. Inbound foreign investment remains subject to the Investment Canada Act net-benefit notification and review thresholds. Cross-border remittances are monitored by FINTRAC, and non-resident dividend payments are subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, typically 25% absent treaty relief (reduced to 5% for significant shareholdings and 15% for portfolio holdings under the Canada-U.S. treaty; A ULC status may engage specific tax treaty classification issues).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement exists. Exporters may retain and use foreign currency freely. All outgoing or incoming cross-border wire transactions of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC, and financial institutions must keep prescribed records under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. No mandatory conversion timeline or conversion ratio applies to export receipts; settlement currency is at the commercial discretion of the parties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, with prudential regulation of financial institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and cross-border transaction reporting by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not maintain a separate foreign-exchange-control agency; monetary and exchange-rate policy rests with the Bank of Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate for payment facilitation but low for deposit-taking treasury functions. Canada does not license foreign 'Electronic Money Institutions' under a dedicated EMI regime. Cross-border fintechs may operate only as money-service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC or, if deposit-like activity is intended, as federally regulated Schedule I or II banks. Offshore EMIs can provide settlement accounts than exact interoperability with Canadian AFT/ACSS rails, but such institutions cannot accept Canadian retail deposits and would require explicit MSB or bank licensing to clear CAD. For an Alberta ULC, such platforms are viable only for inward/outward remittance and minor PKF trade settlement, not as primary operating bank accounts.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined (15% Canadian federal general corporate rate + 8% Alberta general corporate rate); an Alberta unlimited liability corporation (ULC) remains a full Canadian taxpayer and does not achieve domestic pass-through treatment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (ITA 212(2)); Alberta does not impose a separate state-level dividend withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return is due no later than six months after the fiscal year-end; the balance of tax payable is due two months after the fiscal year-end for general corporations, and three months after year-end for qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations.

Schedule 1 - Federal tax calculation Computes Part I federal tax after the general rate reduction, small business deduction (where eligible), investment tax credits, foreign tax credits and other federal credits. Schedule 4 - Corporation losses Tracks non-capital loss balances, carrybacks and carryforwards, capital losses, restrictions on loss utilization after acquisition of control, and specified future tax consequences. Preserve the 20-year carryforward of non-capital losses; an offshore parent's acquisition of control may trigger loss restriction rules (subsection 111(5)/111.1) and require annual continuity schedules. Late or omitted T2 filings and remittances trigger federal and Alberta penalties: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month of delay for up to 12 months under ITA 162(1), a 10% repeat penalty, prescribed arrears interest, and separate late-filing penalties for information schedules such as T106 and Form NR4.
Schedule 9 / Alberta corporate tax calculation Calculates Alberta corporate allocation, the Alberta 8% general rate or 2% small business rate, Alberta tax credits and the resulting provincial instalment/balance. Alberta taxes the ULC's attributable income; do not apply the Alberta small-business rate if the foreign controlling shareholder or a group of non-resident shareholders prevents Canadian-controlled private corporation status. Late or omitted T2 filings and remittances trigger federal and Alberta penalties: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month of delay for up to 12 months under ITA 162(1), a 10% repeat penalty, prescribed arrears interest, and separate late-filing penalties for information schedules such as T106 and Form NR4.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions; this threshold triggers the T106 Information Return and supports CRA's contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation requirement; prudent taxpayers maintain Local/Master-File style documentation regardless of dollar value.

Economic substance scope: Alberta has no separate offshore Economic Substance Regulations; Canada instead applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), the beneficial-ownership doctrine and permanent establishment analysis to prevent treaty shopping and shell-company avoidance. An Alberta ULC operating a hotpot restaurant must maintain real employees, physical premises, bank accounts, board minutes, operational risk and business decision records; a hollow ULC with only a nominal registered agent and no Canadian activity will not carry treaty benefits and may have income reattributed to the foreign shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under ITA s.239; fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to five years, plus civil gross-negligence penalties under s.163(2) and prescribed arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and shareholders of the ULC who instructed or participated in the fraud can be convicted personally; CRA may assess directors under the corporate veil-piercing provisions, obtain civil restitution, asset-freezing orders, departure/restraint orders, and criminal imprisonment.
Unremitted Source Deductions and Part XIII Cross-Border Withholding

Statutory sanction: Assessments under ITA s.227 include the unremitted amount, 10% penalty (10% additional penalty on second failure), prescribed interest, and criminal prosecution for wilful non-remittance carrying imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Under ITA s.227.1, a director of the ULC is jointly and severally liable for the unremitted sums unless the director exercised the degree of care, diligence and skill reasonably necessary; liability is not extinguished by incorporation or bankruptcy.
