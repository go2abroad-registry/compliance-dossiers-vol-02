Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal corporate income tax and GST/HST administration; Business Number and program accounts are managed through CRA My Business Account portal. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA on behalf of the Government of Alberta under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) supervises customs bonded inflight catering kitchens and aircraft stores removal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Unlimited Liability Corporation under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (RSA 2000, c. B-9) via the Alberta Corporate Registry. Step 2: Submit CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts) to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and the GST/HST program account (RT suffix). Step 3: The Alberta corporate income tax account (CD suffix) is created simultaneously under the BN; Alberta tax is reported on the single T2 corporate income tax return under the federal-provincial combination. Step 4: Non-resident shareholders require no Canadian TIN; the ULC is the registrant taxpayer for both income tax and GST/HST. Step 5: Where the kitchen operates as a bonded facility, apply to CBSA for a ships' stores / sufferance-warehouse licence and obtain an aircraft stores account for duty-and-tax-relieved receipt of imported food inputs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — An Alberta ULC is taxed as a corporation at entity level in Canada (federal 15% + Alberta 8% combined effective rate, subject to available deductions); it is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation where owned by non-residents, so the small business deduction is unavailable. The pass-through characteristic is effective only for U.S. federal tax purposes via an IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election and, where applicable, under Article IV(6) of the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention. For GST/HST, the ULC is a separate registrant and all supplies are treated at the entity level.

Available election forms

1) IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election to classify the Alberta ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. federal tax purposes under Treas. Reg. section 301.7701-3. 2) Fiscal transparency claim under Article IV(6) of the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention (Fifth Protocol) permitting U.S. resident shareholders to obtain treaty benefits on flow-through income of the ULC. 3) GST/HST election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act (Form GST25) for closely related corporations and the joint venture election under section 273 (Form GST20) to streamline GST administration between the caterer and the airline operator. 4) No Canadian check-the-box mechanism exists for Canadian income tax; Income Tax Act hybrid mismatch rules (sections 12(3.1)-12(3.4) and 18.4-18.6) operate as disallowance rules and provide no elective relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small supplier threshold based on worldwide taxable supplies (excluding financial services and capital property) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and the current quarter. Registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted; zero-rated export catering revenues count as taxable supplies for threshold measurement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 (tangible personal property exported by the supplier) for inflight meals and catering supplies delivered at a Canadian airport to a non-resident international airline and consumed outside Canada. CRA also recognizes aircraft stores relief for international carriers under the Customs Act and the Ships' Stores Regulations. To qualify: (i) the airline must be a non-resident or an international carrier operating scheduled international flights; (ii) the meal load must be attributable to an identified flight departing Canada to a destination outside Canada, including the United States; (iii) the goods must be physically loaded onto the departing aircraft at the ramp; and (iv) meals supplied for purely domestic Canadian flights remain taxable at 5% GST.

  • Itemized catering invoice and purchase order referencing airline name, flight number, departure date, and international destination
  • Aircraft catering load sheet / flight manifest signed by the airline's station agent confirming physical ramp delivery
  • CBSA bonded warehouse removal record (aircraft stores declaration) and, where applicable, B13A export declaration or ACI export eManifest for bonded inventory
  • Non-resident airline's written confirmation of the international flight itinerary and statement that meals are consumed outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory pre-clearance API regime. CRA administers GST/HST on a self-assessment basis: registrants must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE (large filers may use batch XML machine-to-machine transmission through certified software) and remit net tax monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on revenue. Invoice content must satisfy the Input Tax Credit Information Requirements under the ITC Information Requirements Regulation (SOR/2007-275) and ETA section 169. A voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol interoperability standard is being phased in by CRA but is not statutory. Bonded inflight kitchens must maintain CBSA-approved inventory records and digital lot-level tracking of imported food inputs and aircraft stores removals.

If export documentation fails substantiation, CRA reclassifies the supplies as taxable at 5% GST, disallows input tax credits on the related inputs, and reassesses net tax with daily compound arrears interest. The normal reassessment window is four years; it extends to seven years for neglect and is unlimited for misrepresentation or fraud. False-statement penalties under ETA section 285 equal 25% of the tax sought to be evaded, and gross-negligence penalties under ITA section 163.2 may apply in parallel income tax audits. For bonded stores, CBSA may seize goods, issue Administrative Monetary Penalties under the Customs Act (up to CAD 5,500 per false statement), and demand duties and taxes on unexplained inventory shortages. Criminal prosecution under ETA section 328 or ITA section 239 can result in fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment. The 180-day inward remittance benchmark is not a statutory Canadian deadline; however, prolonged non-receipt of export proceeds will be treated as evidence that no bona fide export occurred, and a GST/HST refund or rebate claim on the related ITCs is time-barred after two years under ETA section 297.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Current-account items (trade, services, dividends, interest, management fees) and capital-account items (equity injections, shareholder loans, portfolio flows) are freely convertible without prior central-bank approval. Profit and capital repatriation are subject only to ordinary corporate formalities, commercial reasonableness, and tax compliance, not exchange-control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no surrender, conversion, or holding-period requirement on export proceeds. Catering-export receipts may be retained in CAD, USD, or other currencies. FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting requirements apply for cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, but there is no statutory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC); no separate FX-licensing authority exists, as exchange controls are not implemented in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury and capital custodianship; Medium for ancillary low-value disbursements. Foreign EMIs cannot accept Canadian deposits or function as Schedule-I banks. They must register as money service businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act to provide payment services in Canada. The Alberta ULC should maintain its primary operating and capital escrow accounts with a regulated domestic bank, using EMIs only for petty vendor settlements where dual-control and audit trail can be maintained.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined net corporate income tax rate (15% federal general rate after federal-provincial abatement and general rate reduction; 8% Alberta general corporate rate).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (s. 212(2)) on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident shareholders, before any tax treaty relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed no later than six months after the end of the corporation's taxation year. The balance of assessed taxes is due two months after year-end for a non-CCPC and three months after year-end for a CCPC eligible for the small business deduction; instalments are required monthly or quarterly for larger corporations.

Deadline: · Frequency:

T106 must be filed by the corporate T2 due date, i.e., six months after the taxation year-end. Transfer-pricing documentation must be prepared contemporaneously on or before the filing date and made available to the CRA on request within a reasonable time.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$750,000,000 group consolidated revenue triggers Canada's Country-by-Country Report and Master File obligations under the OECD/BEPS framework. There is no statutory C$ de minimis for Local File/transfer-pricing documentation; the CRA requires contemporaneous documentation for every controlled transaction. Documentation penalties apply where the transfer-pricing adjustment exceeds the greater of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue. For a mid-sized ULC catering operation, maintain full Local File covering CUP, resale price, cost-plus, or TNMM analyses for all cross-border meal purchases and service transactions.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but economic substance is enforced through the common law, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245), and treaty limitation-on-benefits provisions. A ULC with no real office, no employees in Alberta, and board decisions taken exclusively outside Canada is not recognized for treaty relief and may be recharacterized by the CRA as a shell. The CRA may assert that central management and control is exercised abroad and that the ULC lacks Canadian treaty residency, or apply GAAR to deny benefits. For a ULC used as a tax-transparent US check-the-box entity, Canadian corporate residency and permanent-establishment substance must be independently maintained.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under s. 163(2) of 50% of the tax understated. Criminal sanction under s. 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fines from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years on indictment; up to two years on summary conviction. Additional charges under s. 380 Criminal Code may apply for fraud on revenues.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and agents who authorize or participate in evasion are personally liable for criminal fines and restitution; CRA may seek a travel ban under s. 226 where the assessed individual poses a flight risk. For a ULC, every shareholder has statutory joint and several unlimited liability for the corporation's debts, so the corporate veil is already partially lifted by Alberta law.
Failure to Withhold Part XIII / Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: Under s. 227(8) of the Income Tax Act, penalty of 10% of any amount not withheld or remitted; s. 227(9) raises the penalty to 20% for gross negligence. The unremitted amount itself remains collectible with statutory interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholding; the CRA may assess a director personally without first exhausting corporate recovery. In a ULC, shareholders also face unlimited personal liability for the corporation's debts, including tax withholdings.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of the aggregate transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the greater of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue. CRA may extend the reassessment period for misrepresentations under s. 152(5), and GAAR may apply with s. 245 penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly tolerate false T106 disclosures or who instruct the use of non-arm's-length charges can be assessed for participation in the avoidance arrangement. The unlimited shareholder liability of a ULC makes each shareholder personally exposed for the resulting federal and provincial tax debts.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to recharacterize the arrangement and deny treaty benefits, resulting in reassessment of additional tax plus interest and penalties. The foreign parent may also be charged under s. 239 where the shell is used to conceal taxable Canadian-source income.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders who knowingly maintain a shell with no substance may be personally liable for fraudulent preference, civil conspiracy, or wrongful distribution under provincial corporate law. CRA can impose travel bans under s. 226(1) where significant tax liability is owed and the individual is leaving Canada. Because the ULC has no limited-liability shield, shareholders' personal assets remain reachable.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence of laundering proceeds of crime under s. 462.31 Criminal Code: fines up to C$250,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Under PCMLTFA, relevant reporting entities that fail to report suspicious transactions may face civil penalties of up to C$100,000 (individuals) and C$500,000 (entities). False ownership declarations are subject to corporate registry penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly hide ownership or facilitate money laundering face asset freezing, confiscation, and personal criminal liability. Foreign principals may be ineligible for admission or removed from Canada. In a ULC, shareholders are personally and jointly liable with the corporation for all debts, penalties, and regulation-related claims without the need to pierce the corporate veil.
