Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate or landlord-provided compliance under the Alberta Fire Code for the office occupancy; ordinary building code clearance for business occupancy requiring no structural load re-inspection; workplace hazard clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Alberta) covering ergonomics, electrical safety, and emergency egress for employees. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and corporation income tax; Alberta provincial corporate income tax is also administered by the CRA under a tax collection agreement. Registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account; Alberta Corporate Registry for ULC incorporation.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) at the Alberta Corporate Registry and obtain the Alberta registration certificate and corporation number; (2) open a CRA tax account package by completing Form RC1 or through Business Registration Online, requesting a nine-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) and activating the Corporation Income Tax account, GST/HST account, and any required payroll account; (3) for non-resident control, appoint an authorized accountant/representative and provide certified corporate documents, registered office address, and particulars of directors; (4) within 30 days of becoming a non-small supplier, register for GST/HST using the BN; (5) retain the GST/HST account number and confirm applicability of normal or quarterly/annual filing thresholds.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — (1) Incorporate the Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) at the Alberta Corporate Registry and obtain the Alberta registration certificate and corporation number; (2) open a CRA tax account package by completing Form RC1 or through Business Registration Online, requesting a nine-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) and activating the Corporation Income Tax account, GST/HST account, and any required payroll account; (3) for non-resident control, appoint an authorized accountant/representative and provide certified corporate documents, registered office address, and particulars of directors; (4) within 30 days of becoming a non-small supplier, register for GST/HST using the BN; (5) retain the GST/HST account number and confirm applicability of normal or quarterly/annual filing thresholds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a domestic 'check-the-box' election: the Alberta ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation subject to entity-level Part I income tax. However, because the ULC has unlimited shareholder liability, it is eligible for classification elections under foreign tax law (e.g., U.S. Form 8832 to be disregarded or taxed as a partnership), subject to the home jurisdiction's per-se rules. Canadian hybrid-mismatch provisions (Income Tax Act sections 18.2, 18.4, 18.6 and 18.7) operate as mandatory anti-hybrid disallowances, not elections; they deny or defer deductions for payments that are deductible in Canada without corresponding ordinary income inclusion in the counterparty jurisdiction, and can apply to imported hybrid and branch mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — total taxable supplies made in Canada by the person and associated entities in a calendar quarter and in the four preceding calendar quarters; for non-resident digital platform operators, deemed supplies made or facilitated in Canada count toward this threshold and require registration once exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: zero-rating applies under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act when goods are exported by the vendor or delivered to a non-resident recipient and removed from Canada as part of a continuous export transaction. For services and intangible personal property, supplies made to a non-resident person are generally zero-rated (Schedule VI, Part V, section 7) unless the service relates to real property located in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or is rendered to an individual present in Canada. The digital magazine portal can zero-rate subscriptions, digital content licensing, and sponsored media placements delivered to foreign subscribers and foreign advertisers provided the recipient is a non-resident person, the supply is not consumed in Canada, and contemporaneous proof of non-residency and export/delivery is retained. Zero-rated exporters also claim input tax credits in the normal GST/HST return to obtain refunds for GST/HST paid on inputs.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or approved EDI/CBSA electronic equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Canadian bank foreign-exchange settlement proof
  • Commercial invoice and signed sales contract
  • CBSA cargo control documentation and broker export report
  • Proof of non-resident recipient status (tax ID, registered address, contract terms)
  • Digital delivery logs, IP geolocation records and platform usage evidence for online magazine and advertising services

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based pre-approval, or digital stamping of invoices exists under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act. Registrants must issue sufficient supporting invoices (including GST/HST registration number, amount of tax charged, and total consideration), file GST/HST returns electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or a registered service provider, remit electronically when the net tax exceeds CAD 10,000, and retain all electronic records in a readable format in Canada. Voluntary e-invoicing can follow the PEPPOL/Common e-Invoicing framework, but CRA does not currently clear invoices in real time.

CRA and CBSA scrutinize zero-rating claims for missing or inconsistent export evidence. If substantiation is inadequate or fraudulent, CRA will retroactively claw back zero-rating, reassess the supply as taxable, collect unpaid GST/HST plus compound daily interest, and may impose penalties for false statements or omissions of up to 25% of the associated federal amount, and gross-negligence penalties of up to 50% of the tax understated. The normal four-year reassessment period is extended to ten years where misrepresentation, neglect or fraud is established, and wilful evasion attracts criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act. Export proceeds should be repatriated within the 180-day standard; failure without commercial justification may be used to challenge the genuineness of the export and the entitlement to zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital account. No prior central-bank approval is required for inbound equity injection, intercompany lending, dividend repatriation, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance is subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, generally treaty-reduced; 15% for US portfolio shareholders and 5% for a US corporate parent holding at least 10% voting shares under the Canada-US Tax Treaty). The Alberta ULC structure is specifically recognized under Treaty Article IV(6) for US-resident shareholders, which can mitigate treaty-access friction, but a bank will require the W-8BEN-E/treaty position memorandum before processing foreign-currency conversions or cross-border distributions. Canada imposes no capital-flow management taxes, no anti-speculation Tobin-style levies, and no netting or maturity restrictions on derivative hedging by non-financial corporates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds. A ULC may retain SaaS/digital-advertising export revenue in CAD, USD, or any foreign currency at a CDIC-insured Canadian bank or in a properly disclosed offshore treasury account, with no BoC or OSFI approval and no conversion levy. FINTRAC reporting obligations apply only to cash and virtual-currency transactions above CAD 10,000, not to export-proceeds wiring generally.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) in conjunction with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). The BoC issues the Canadian dollar (CAD), manages the Exchange Fund Account, and acts as the monetary and foreign-reserve authority. OSFI regulates all federally incorporated Schedule I/II banks; the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) insures eligible CAD deposits. Canada has no standalone Foreign Exchange Administration or exchange-control ministry; Payments Canada owns and operates Lynx (high-value CAD settlement), the Real-Time Rail (RTR), and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM with material legal constraints. Offshore digital EMIs (UK-FCA-authorised or EU-EMI licensed) cannot act as the primary treasury bank for a Canadian ULC because they lack direct membership in Payments Canada (Lynx/RTR/ACSS) and do not provide CDIC deposit insurance. They may only function as a settlement annex for USD collections via a Canadian correspondent. Domestic fintechs (e.g., Stripe Treasury-linked banking-as-a-service, Nuvei CFS, Wealthsimple Cash) are viable solely when balances are routed to and held at a CDIC-insured Schedule I bank. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA, effective September 2024, supervised by the Bank of Canada), payment service providers must register with the BoC; the ULC treasury itself does not require RPAA registration, but any fintech intermediary used for Stripe/AdSense payouts must be RPAA-compliant.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 (ISO 20022 pacs.008) from the foreign parent to the ULC's Canadian account. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must cite the ULC's corporate registration number and the phrase 'Share capital contribution — ULC [legal name]' or 'Intercompany loan — ULC [legal name]'. Canada operates no exchange-control purpose-code regime, but the receiving Schedule I bank will apply FINTRAC large-transaction-record checks for cash/virtual-currency only above CAD 10,000 and retain originator information per the Wire Transfer Rule. The Investment Canada Act review is not triggered for a de novo digital media ULC (only acquisitions of control of a Canadian business above the WTO threshold, approx. CAD 1.7 billion in 2024, require post-closing notification). The ULC must preserve the SWIFT confirmation, the subscribed-share certificate, and the board resolution authorizing the contribution to satisfy any bank EDD request.Outbound trade and operating disbursements (content-licensing, contractor payments, advertising technology, royalties) settle via SWIFT MT103 (gpi) with field 70 referencing the invoice number; CAD high-value legs clear over Payments Canada Lynx, while low-value batch payouts clear via ACSS or RTR. USD disbursements are cleared through the bank's US-chartered clearing account on Fedwire/CHIPS or through the Canadian USD Settlement Service. Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (MT700/MT720) remain available where content-sourcing or print-on-demand suppliers require documentary protection, but for a digital magazine portal, open-account MT103 clean payments are the dominant rail.
