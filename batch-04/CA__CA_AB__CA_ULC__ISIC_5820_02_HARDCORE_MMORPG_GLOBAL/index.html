Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Primary portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Alberta corporate income tax is administered federally by the CRA under a federal-provincial tax-collection agreement; there is no separate Alberta corporate tax authority. Alberta Corporate Registry handles corporate law filings only and is not a tax authority.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta ULC must obtain a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Procedure: (1) register via Business Registration Online (BRO), by paper Form RC1, or through a CRA-authorized representative; (2) the BN automatically activates a Corporation Income Tax account (RC) — mandatory for every corporation resident in Canada, with T2 Corporation Income Tax Return due within six months after fiscal year-end and tax balances due within two months (or three months for a Canadian-controlled private corporation eligible for the small business deduction); (3) open a GST/HST account (RT) once taxable worldwide revenues exceed the CAD $30,000 registration threshold; (4) open a payroll deductions account (RP) when employees are engaged; (5) complete Alberta corporate law incorporation and initial notice filings with the Alberta Corporate Registry, obtaining the Alberta corporate access number, which is separate from the CRA BN. Non-resident officers or directors may appoint a Canadian authorized representative using Form RC59/RC461 and must provide the ULC's foreign mailing address. If the ULC is fiscally transparent under US check-the-box rules, the US shareholder should file Form 8832 and notify the CRA of the resulting classification to align Canadian and US filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Alberta ULC is mandatorily a corporation and a resident of Canada for Canadian Income Tax Act Part I and Alberta corporate tax purposes (a taxable Canadian corporation), but it is eligible for pass-through treatment at the shareholder level for US federal tax purposes: a single-member ULC may be treated as a disregarded entity and a multi-member ULC as a partnership under US check-the-box rules unless an affirmative corporate-classification election is made. For GST/HST, the ULC is a separate person from its shareholders; no fiscal transparency or consolidation override is permitted under the Excise Tax Act.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (1) US IRC Form 8832 check-the-box election to classify the ULC as an association taxable as a corporation, as a partnership, or as a disregarded entity; (2) Canada-US Tax Convention Article IV(6) election, filed by written notification to the CRA competent authority, under which a Canadian unlimited liability company that is fiscally transparent for US tax purposes may be treated as a resident of Canada for all purposes of the Convention, thereby preserving treaty benefits on cross-border payments; (3) Excise Tax Act section 239 small-supplier relief for non-resident digital-service suppliers before mandatory GST/HST registration; (4) Income Tax Act section 216 election for non-resident passive income and services where applicable; (5) allocation of the CAD $500,000 small-business deduction limit among associated corporations; and (6) hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.2, 18.3 and 18.4, which are mandatory rather than elective and disallow deductions to the extent of the resulting US-Canadian hybrid mismatch.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in total annual worldwide taxable supplies. Mandatory registration is triggered when taxable supplies exceed CAD $30,000 either in a single calendar quarter or over 12 consecutive months; registration must be applied for within 29 days after the month or quarter of the excess. Alberta uses the identical federal threshold because no provincial component applies. Small suppliers below the threshold may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST/HST applies to the ULC's MMORPG digital exports under ETA Schedule VI, Part V (sections 21, 22 and 23) where: the recipient is a non-resident not present in Canada at the time of supply; the digital content is delivered to a destination outside Canada; and the services are performed wholly outside Canada. In-app virtual currency, consumables, and game entitlements sold through Apple App Store and Google Play are zero-rated only with documented proof that each end-customer is a non-resident, that the download and gameplay occur offshore, and that the platform remittances constitute foreign-source consideration. Sales to Canadian consumers remain taxable at 5% GST. Effective July 1, 2021, the ETA digital-economy marketplace rules (sections 18.1 and 18.2) may deem the online platform operator, rather than the ULC, to be the supplier of digital services to Canadian consumers; the ULC must map the contractual billing flow to confirm whether it or Apple/Google is the deemed supplier of the Canadian portion, and whether zero-rating applies to offshore B2B channel sales.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency electronic export declaration / B13A) — required for tangible media shipments; for pure digital MMORPG downloads, retain a digital export record instead
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export of promotional or collector-box media, with server-side download logs and IP geolocation records substituting for digital deliveries
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank credit advices, and FX conversion records evidencing offshore consideration received from Apple and Google
  • Apple App Store and Google Play consolidated monthly payout statements showing gross sales, store-level apportionment, country-level download counts, and net settlement amounts
  • Subscriber licensing agreements, terms-of-service acceptance records, and digital delivery confirmations demonstrating non-resident end-user status
  • Server access logs, IP geolocation reports, and content-delivery-network delivery data evidencing offshore enjoyment of digital content
  • Zero-rated tax invoices, B2B recipients' value-added tax registration numbers, and online-marketplace supporting schedules for any zero-rated self-assessment

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of individual e-invoices. E-invoicing is permitted, and the Pan-Canadian Peppol interoperability framework and the federal e-invoicing service are available on an opt-in basis. The ULC's practical compliance duties are: (1) issue GST/HST tax invoices with the prescribed information for B2B supply chains under ETA sections 169 and 170; (2) rely on simplified invoices and net settlement reports for Apple App Store and Google Play consumer transactions; (3) file GST/HST returns electronically via Form GST34 using GST/HST NETFILE or CRA My Business Account on a monthly, quarterly, or annual basis depending on annual revenue; and (4) retain electronic source records, including platform payout statements, subscriber IP logs, purchase transaction files, and currency conversion records, for six years under ETA section 286 and Income Tax Act section 230.

CRA audit scrutiny of zero-rated digital exports is elevated: the supplier bears the burden of proving export entitlement, and failure to maintain the contemporaneous documentation bundle triggers reassessment of the supply at 5% GST with compound daily interest. Fraudulent or grossly negligent zero-rating claims attract: a 30% gross-negligence penalty under ETA section 285(1); wilful-evasion penalties up to 200% of the net tax sought to be evaded; summary conviction fines up to CAD $25,000 and imprisonment up to two years, or indictment fines up to CAD $100,000 and imprisonment up to five years; and an eight-year limitation-period extension for misrepresentation under ETA section 298. Retrospective clawback also cancels any input tax credits claimed on related inputs and permits reopening of prior returns, recharacterization of offshore platform receipts as Canadian-source royalties or service revenue under transfer-pricing rules (Income Tax Act section 247), and imposition of associated hybrid-mismatch disallowances. Directors of a ULC may be assessed jointly and severally for unremitted or fraudulently claim-adjusted GST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. No exchange control legislation, approval requirements, or profit-remittance hurdles apply. The Alberta ULC may repatriate profits, pay dividends, return capital, and service foreign debt in CAD or any freely convertible currency, subject only to withholding tax under the Income Tax Act and reporting obligations under exchange control statistics reporting to Statistics Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, forced conversion, or prescribed timeline for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency domestically or abroad. Reporting burdens apply only where a single transaction exceeds C$10,000 or where patterns of transactions trigger FINTRAC large-value reporting; no proportionality ratio or conversion mandate exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); money laundering supervision by Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium. Canada does not issue a dedicated electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or Payoneer may operate in Canada only as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC or as authorized foreign money remitters under provincial remittance regimes. They may support USD/CAD conversions for MMORPG payouts from Apple and Google, but they are not deposit-taking institutions; their use should be strictly limited to settlement accelerators rather than primary treasury accounts. Primary liquidity must rest with federally regulated chartered banks due to improved protection under the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) and OSFI liquidity standards.
