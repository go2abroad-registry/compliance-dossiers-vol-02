Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — administered through the CRA 'My Business Account' portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO); provincial registry counterpart: Alberta Corporate Registry / Alberta Registries.

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian-resident ULC: (1) incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act and obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number; (2) apply to CRA for the nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO), Form RC1, or telephone; the GST/HST (RT) program account is issued simultaneously, while the corporate income tax (RC) account is opened automatically upon incorporation; (3) where foreign shareholders exist, file Form RC372 for the non-resident account and the first T2 corporate return in the ULC's initial taxation year; (4) register for GST/HST under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act when making taxable supplies in Canada and when the 'small supplier' exemption (C$30,000 threshold) is exceeded; since 1 July 2021, a non-resident digital platform or supplier must also consider the simplified (STAR) registration regime for supplies of digital services to Canadian consumers; (5) open a CRA payroll (RP) account only if Canadian employees are engaged.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — notwithstanding this hybrid classification, the Alberta ULC is an entity-level taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on worldwide income; pass-through features arise through: (i) the subsection 39(11) election allowing non-resident shareholders to treat ULC shares as partnership interests on disposition; (ii) US 'check-the-box' transparency for US shareholders; and (iii) unlimited shareholder liability, which enables treaty-based flow-through claims in certain cross-border contexts.

Available election forms

(a) Income Tax Act subsection 39(11) election — ULC shares deemed partnership interests for computing non-resident shareholders' gains and losses on disposition; (b) ITA section 216 election for net-rental income of non-resident owners; (c) Excise Tax Act section 156 election (Form GST25) to deem supplies among closely related corporations to be made for nil consideration; (d) ETA section 211.1 election for non-resident registrants; (e) US Form 8832 'check-the-box' classification election affecting the home-jurisdiction characterisation of the Canadian ULC; (f) capital cost allowance class elections at the corporate level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — measured on total worldwide taxable supplies in the current calendar quarter and the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters ('small supplier' threshold under ETA s.148); digital platform operators and non-resident suppliers of digital services to Canadian consumers apply the same C$30,000 threshold under the modified July 2021 regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a taxable supply of intangible personal property (e.g., PC/console game licences and Steam/PlayStation digital content) made by a GST/HST registrant to a non-resident person not registered under Part IX of the ETA, provided the supply is for the non-resident's consumption or use outside Canada (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.25.1). For an individual purchaser, the ordinary country of residence governs; for a corporate purchaser, the jurisdiction where the corporation is resident or has a fixed establishment governs under the s.26.1 place-of-supply rules. Revenue routed through the Canadian ULC from foreign platform payout rails (Steam Valve, PlayStation Store) is zero-rated where the platform is the non-resident recipient and the underlying customers are non-resident; contemporaneous documentation must evidence the foreign destination and the purchaser's non-resident status.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (B13A or CADEX electronic filing) — applicable only where physical encoded media or server-hosting hardware crosses the border; not required for cloud or digital delivery
  • Commercial invoice or digital sale contract containing foreign purchaser identifiers, place-of-supply attestation, and payment terms
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (physical-media distribution only)
  • Steam Valve / PlayStation Store payout statements evidencing foreign-platform settlement and foreign customer jurisdictions
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Canadian deposit-taking institution for FX conversions above regulatory reporting thresholds
  • Transfer-pricing documentation (OECD Chapter V / CRA policy) demonstrating arm's-length consideration for the publishing intangibles

None — Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) when annual taxable turnover exceeds C$1.5M; the voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (PEPPOL BIS 3.0 / Business Payments in Canada) is available; zero-rated export records must be retained for six years from the end of the taxation year in either paper or electronic format.

CRA will disallow zero-rating and reassess uncollected GST/HST at 5% (plus any applicable provincial component) where the exporter cannot substantiate that the digital supply was made to a non-resident and consumed outside Canada. The normal reassessment period is 4 years for corporations (3 years for individuals), extending to 7 years or indefinitely for misrepresentation, neglect, or non-arm's length transactions. Penalties: 50% gross-negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) / ETA s.285 of the understated tax; C$100 to C$25,000 per instance for false statements and omissions (ETA s.280); criminal prosecution under ITA s.239 / ETA s.327 carries fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Interest at the prescribed rate compounds daily from the original filing due date. Reassessment clawback is retrospective with no forgiveness window for fraudulent export zero-rating claims; transfer-pricing penalties (ITA s.247(3)) add 10% of the net adjustment. Canada imposes no specific statutory FX repatriation deadline for zero-rated exports; the 180-day convention reflects standard Canadian export-credit terms and CRA transfer-pricing expectations for foreign receivables, beyond which documentation of continued foreign use is required.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility as an IMF Article VIII member. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely and there are no exchange controls restricting capital inflows, outflows, dividend remittances, or royalty distributions for Alberta ULCs. The only cross-border impediments are tax-driven, namely Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally 25% for non-treaty beneficiaries, often reduced by treaty, with Canada-US treaty rates typically 15% on dividends and 0-10% on royalties). No convertibility approval is required for digital gaming revenue repatriated from Steam or PlayStation storefronts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation, no statutory conversion timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio exists in Canada. Export receipts from video game publishing may be retained in CAD, USD, EUR, or any foreign currency indefinitely. Reporting obligations that apply are (i) FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting for transactions at or above CAD $10,000 or the equivalent in foreign currency, and (ii) Statistics Canada trade-in-services reporting, but neither requires repatriation or FX conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Department of Finance Canada. Canada has no standalone foreign-exchange control agency; the Bank of Canada is the monetary authority and the future registrar for the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) payment service provider framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) can be used for multi-currency collections from Steam, PlayStation, and other platforms, but under Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and Retail Payment Activities Regulations (in force September 2024), any payment service provider serving Canadian entities must register with the Bank of Canada and maintain a Canadian presence. The EMI must also be a registered money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC. Foreign EMIs cannot access LVTS ACSS settlement rails and do not offer true Schedule I Canadian bank guarantees. Legal constraint: reliance on an unregistered offshore EMI may create AML/KYC deficiencies at the tier-1 bank. Recommended posture: EMI as secondary collection-wallet, tier-1 bank as primary treasury spine.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Preferred rail is SWIFT MT103 for non-USD jurisdictions and ABA/Fedwire routing for USD originating in the United States. Payment purpose code: equity injection or subscription for shares, identified on MT103 field 70 as EQUITY CONTRIBUTION CA_ULC SHARE SUBSCRIPTION. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined federal-provincial general corporate rate (15% federal Part I + 8% Alberta provincial). Where the ULC qualifies as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), the Small Business Deduction applies at 11% combined (9% federal + 2% Alberta) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income; however, a ULC held by US non-residents is generally not CCPC-eligible and is taxed at 23%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid or credited by a resident Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 mandatory where aggregate annual non-arm's length cross-border transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000, or where intercompany loans/advances exceed CAD 1,000,000 in carrying value at any point in the year. Local File and Master File compliance under CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R and OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines triggers when the Canadian taxpayer's gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million, or the multinational group's assets exceed CAD 50 million; Country-by-Country Report (Form RC4649) required for groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada operates no EP-based Economic Substance Regulation equivalent to the UAE or Cayman regimes; however, substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 I.T.A.), the transfer-pricing recharacterization rule (s.247(2) I.T.A.), treaty beneficial-ownership and Limitation-on-Benefits provisions under Canada-US Treaty Article XXIX A, and BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules. The Alberta ULC must show real decision-making, operational staff, physical premises, and risk-bearing capital in Alberta; purely passive mailbox ULCs are disregarded by the CRA on audit. The ULC's inherent unlimited liability under the Alberta Business Corporations Act s.15(1)-(2) provides a strong commercial substance indicator, and GST/HST registration and Canadian payroll demonstrate further residency and substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Under s.239(1) I.T.A.: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment for up to 5 years, or both; civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) I.T.A.: 50% of the understated federal and provincial tax; the CRA Criminal Investigations Program may impose restraint orders on all corporate and personal assets.

Director personal liability: All directors and officers who participated in the fraudulent scheme face joint and several criminal liability; s.227.1 I.T.A. imposes civil joint and several liability for unremitted amounts; as a ULC, shareholders are personally liable for the corporation's debts under ABCA s.15(2), allowing the CRA and creditors to pierce the corporate veil without court application.
Unpaid Part XIII Withholding on Dividends, Royalties, and Interest

Statutory sanction: Failure to withhold or remit is subject to a 10% penalty under s.227(8) I.T.A., daily compound interest on the arrears, and immediate demand for the full unremitted amount; the CRA may issue a s.225.2 jeopardy order freezing bank accounts and garnishing Steam/PlayStation payouts.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and severally liable for the entire unremitted Part XIII tax plus interest and penalties under s.227.1(1) I.T.A.; CRA issues director liability letters directly to US-resident directors without requiring a Canadian collection judgment; unpaid director liability can trigger cross-border enforcement and travel restrictions under reciprocal US-Canada tax collection agreements.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Shell Abuse (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: s.247(2) I.T.A. permits the CRA to recharacterize or adjust the transaction to an arm's-length amount; s.247(3) imposes a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000); s.245 I.T.A. GAAR denies deductions where the transaction lacks bona fide substance; failure to file T106 attracts daily penalties and an additional 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) for deficient documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the intercompany pricing are subject to s.227.1 for associated withholding deficiencies; in a GAAR or sham finding, the unlimited liability nature of the ULC renders shareholders personally liable for the tax deficiency and professional fees; additionally, CRA may use s.160 I.T.A. to assess transferee liability against shareholders who received diverted corporate assets.
AML and Payment-Rail Compliance Failures

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for a corporate entity under s.73 of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; failure to register as a money services business where the ULC converts digital-store proceeds or performs currency exchange carries a fine and 5 years' imprisonment; the CRA may reallocate the diverted income to the controlling shareholder and apply a 50% negligence penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers face criminal liability under Criminal Code s.462.31 for laundering proceeds of designated offences; FINTRAC can impose separate AMLs of up to CAD 500,000 on individual directors who direct payment-rail diversion; the Alberta ULC's unlimited liability regime additionally exposes every shareholder to third-party restitution claims, civil forfeiture, and prohibition from serving as a director of any Canadian corporation for up to 10 years.
