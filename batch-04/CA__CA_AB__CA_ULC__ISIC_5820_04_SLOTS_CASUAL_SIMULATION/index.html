Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The ULC may claim 0% GST/HST on qualifying export supplies: (i) tangible goods physically exported from Canada with required customs documentation; and (ii) digital supplies of software licences, virtual slots/social casino content, and related services supplied to a non-resident recipient outside Canada, provided the supply is not for use or consumption in Canada. The exporter must demonstrate the recipient's non-resident status through contracts, payment routing, intellectual-property delivery data, and app-store/platform sales evidence. Zero-rated export supplies allow full input tax credit recovery and net GST/HST refund claims.

Canada does not operate a national real-time fiscal clearance regime, digital stamping system, or mandatory continuous transaction control (CTC) API e-invoicing mandate. GST/HST returns are self-filed electronically through NETFILE. Invoices must include the information prescribed by the Excise Tax Act; electronic invoices and digital records are acceptable. CRA may perform transaction-level analytics and document matching; Apple IAP and Google Play payment reconciliations are not tax clearances and do not replace statutory invoicing and record-keeping obligations. Quebec's separate QST electronic invoicing and filing requirements do not apply in Alberta.

CRA audits export zero-rating by tracing the full contract-to-payment-to-delivery chain, especially for digital gaming exports. Unrepatriated export proceeds beyond the 180-day benchmark are an audit red flag and may lead to recharacterization of the supply as taxable Canadian consumption, with penalty and arrears interest assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada imposes no exchange controls and grants full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the Canadian dollar (CAD). No approval, permit, prior declaration, or licensing requirement applies to inward investment layers, dividend or branch-profit remittances, royalty and management-fee payments, or repayment of shareholder loans. Profit remittance hurdles are exclusively tax and reporting oriented: non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax of 25% statutorily (frequently reduced to 5-15% under the Canada-United States Income Tax Convention and other tax treaties) on dividends, interest, royalties, rents, and management fees, with mandated NR4 returns and remittances to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Cross-border funds movement is subject to FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting by financial institutions for each international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour period, and to sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act, the United Nations Act, and Global Affairs Canada consolidated sanctions lists. A transfer may be blocked, delayed, or rejected where the counterparty, currency route, intermediary bank, or ultimate beneficiary is sanctioned. Alberta imposes no provincial currency controls, remittance restrictions, capital controls, or separate exchange-approval body.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has not required export-proceeds surrender since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations in 1951. There is no statutory holding period, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to channel export receipts through the domestic banking system. Receipts from simulated social-casino revenue through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing may be retained in USD or converted to CAD at the corporate treasury's discretion. For goods exports, a B13A export declaration is required through the Canadian Export Reporting System for controlled, regulated, or high-value goods purely for customs and statistical purposes, not for FX surrender; services and royalty flows are captured through Statistics Canada survey requirements. FINTRAC EFTR obligations apply to the financial institution, not to the exporter, for each international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more within a 24-hour period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). The Minister of Finance and the Department of Finance Canada retain residual exchange-control policy authority under the Currency Act, the Financial Administration Act, the Special Economic Measures Act, and the United Nations Act. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control permit office, consistent with its IMF Article VIII liberalised open-currency status.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low (conditionally viable as an offshore treasury-concentration vehicle, not as a Canadian payment rail). Canada does not issue electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences under the UK or EU model; payment services are regulated under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), administered by the Bank of Canada, and a payment service provider that operates in or targets Canada must be registered with the Bank of Canada. An offshore digital EMI (for example, an FCA-authorised UK EMI or an EU e-money institution) cannot lawfully provide payment services to Canadian residents or to the ULC's Canadian treasury operations without RPAA registration, and Canadian tier-1 banks will generally decline to credit or settle accounts for an unregistered offshore EMI. The EMI may nevertheless serve as an offshore USD concentration node where Apple/Google marketplace payouts are collected and then remitted to the ULC's Schedule I operating account, provided that: (i) no Canadian end-user payment services are rendered by the EMI; (ii) balances are not held as client money in a manner that triggers Canadian insolvency, securities, or banking regulation; (iii) the board expressly accepts the absence of Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protection and the lack of Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudential oversight; and (iv) every onward transfer carries a clear purpose code and full audit trail. Conservative treasury practice is to hold all marketplace proceeds directly in a USD or CAD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank and use offshore EMIs only for non-Canadian developer and advertising settlements where bank correspondent access is constrained.

Conservative treasury practice is to hold all marketplace proceeds directly in a USD or CAD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank and use offshore EMIs only for non-Canadian developer and advertising settlements where bank correspondent access is constrained.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not prescribe a universal absolute C$ threshold for Local File/Master File preparation; however, CRA administrative guidance requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation where the aggregate fair market value of non-arm's-length non-resident transactions exceeds C$1,000,000 in a taxation year. A Country-by-Country Report is mandatory where the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds €750,000,000, converted to Canadian dollars at the applicable exchange rate.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not legislate an explicit Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the manner of the UAE; nevertheless, the GAAR, the s.247 reasonableness test, and the Canada-US treaty's limitation-on-benefits provision require the Alberta ULC to maintain real commercial substance—dedicated offices, payroll, active decision-making, and actual execution of game publishing activities. A nominally incorporated ULC holding digital sls IP and booking IAP revenue without genuine activity may attract recharacterization and loss of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239 imposes, upon conviction, a fine of between 100% and 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment up to 5 years; the CRA may separately assess a gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax and transfer-pricing penalties of 10% of the net adjustment. Civil penalties and compound interest accrue without limitation.

Director personal liability: Directors can be jointly and severally assessed for corporate unremitted source deductions under s.227.1 and may be subject to travel bans, asset seizure, civil restitution, and up to 5 years' imprisonment; piercing the corporate veil of an Alberta ULC is exceptionally easy because shareholders have unlimited liability for ULC debts.
Shell Company / Artificial Tax Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under s.245 to resettle tax, deny treaty withholding reductions, whole apportion income to the real business, and impose 10% transfer-pricing penalties on understated amounts; administrative monetary penalties can reach C$25,000 and criminal sanctions apply for willful avoidance.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the ULC to be treated as a sham may be personally liable for the corporation's tax under the 'legal personality' and unlimited liability provisions of the Alberta Business Corporations Act; directors also face involuntary billing assessments, court-ordered restitution, and potential clawback of improperly transferred assets.
AML / Regulatory Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed gaming in Alberta can trigger AGLC administrative penalties up to C$100,000 and revocation; the Criminal Code and Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) expose the corporation to fines of up to C$2,000,000, asset seizure, and prosecution of officer; failure to file Currency Transaction Reports and terrorist financing reports separately attracts penalties under the PCMLTFA.

Director personal liability: Under the PCMLTFA, directors and senior officers can be convicted personally where they were knowingly involved, incurring fines up to C$1,000,000, imprisonment up to 5 years, director disqualification, and restitution orders; in an unlimited liability company, the individual shareholders of the ULC are also directly liable for regulatory debts if the entity is unable to pay.
