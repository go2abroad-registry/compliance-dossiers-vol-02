Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; business portal: CRA 'My Business Account' via canada.ca

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian incorporated ULC, obtain the statutory Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 with CRA, either through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service or by mail. The ULC must also open a separate GST/HST program account and, as applicable, a corporate income tax account. A non-resident foreign entity not yet incorporated in Canada may register for a BN and HST account using the same Form RC1, supported by proof of Canadian nexus, a registered address for service, and corporate constitutional documents; a non-resident may be required to apply via the CRA international tax services office. The BN is the sole federal tax identifier for income tax, GST/HST, and customs obligations in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime for domestic tax purposes; a ULC is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act. ULC hybrid status may be relevant for U.S. federal tax classification, for which Form 8832 may be used for U.S. purposes. Canadian statutory elections applicable to the enterprise include: (i) GST/HST election under subsection 156(1) for closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for no consideration; (ii) joint GST/HST election for qualifying non-resident registrants; (iii) section 150(1) fiscal period election; and (iv) rollover elections under section 85 to transfer eligible property on a tax-deferred basis. Hybrid mismatch elections and agreement filings may be required under sections 18.4 to 18.6 of the Income Tax Act where a ULC is treated as a partnership or disregarded entity in a foreign jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (worldwide) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, measured for small supplier determination; the same threshold applies to non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property qualifies for the 0% GST/HST export rate where the supplier sells the property and, as part of the supply agreement, ships or delivers it outside Canada to an address specified by the buyer, whether by common carrier, courier, or the supplier's own transport, and contemporaneous documentary evidence of export is kept. Services that are for the benefit of a non-resident person and performed wholly or primarily outside Canada may be zero-rated, provided the supply is not a supply of a service made to a person who is resident in Canada at the time the service is performed. Platform tournament access, matchmaking, spectator streaming, and prize-fund administration services supplied to non-resident esports organizations or overseas users will qualify for zero-rating if the 'place of supply' rules under section 142.2 are satisfied; subscription and fee-based platform services consumed within Canada are subject to 5% GST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (via Canada Border Services Agency, including secure export reporting under the Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill / outbound courier manifest showing the goods or equipment left Canada
  • Importer's purchase order or contract evidencing the destination of goods or services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank ledger evidencing foreign currency receipt for the exported supply
  • Proof of payment received (wire transfer/payment gateway statement) matching the export sale
  • Complete GST/HST zero-rating evidence log including client name, non-resident status evidence, contract, and service-rendered records

No federal mandate for mandatory electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance/API stamping for GST/HST. CRA accepts electronic records provided they contain the mandatory invoice data. A voluntary e-invoicing framework exists, and PEPPOL-aligned formats may be used for supply-chain efficiency, but CRA does not operate a digital stamping or clearance gateway. Quebec's provincial electronic invoicing rules (for Quebec QST) do not apply to an Alberta-based platform.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rated supply and clawback the GST/HST benefit if the required export documentation is incomplete, if the non-resident status of the purchaser is not established, or if the goods/services are not in fact exported or supplied outside Canada within the relevant time. Penalties under subsection 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act apply to false or omissive statements; a knowingly false statement incurs a penalty of 25% of the amount of tax that would have been evaded, plus arrears interest. Criminal prosecution may expose officers, directors, and agents to fines and imprisonment for tax evasion. Additionally, the CRA may deny future zero-rating allowances and audit multiple fiscal years where a systemic export-zero-rating pattern is found, and a taxpayer may be held jointly and severally liable with its directors for unremitted GST/HST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a comprehensively free and open currency regime under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Both current account (trade in goods and services, royalties, and licensing fees) and capital account transactions (direct investment, portfolio flows, loans) are fully convertible into CAD and foreign currencies without exchange controls, quota restrictions, or discriminatory multiple-currency practices. Profit remittances, dividends, and branch repatriation amounts are freely transferable, subject only to general anti-money laundering (AML) reporting thresholds (e.g., FINTRAC reports for ≥ CAD 10,000) and to the corporate law solvency tests of the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincial equivalents. No prior administrative authorization is required for repatriation; only a ULC's unlimited liability feature and provincial tax filings may in practice lead to additional legal comfort letters for cross-border wiring.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose the surrender, conversion, or custodial requirement of export proceeds. Esports platform tournament fees, subscriptions, prize pools held in escrow, and sponsorship revenues may be settled in any invoiced currency. However, for accounting and tax harmonization, the functional currency of the Alberta ULC will be CAD; material non-CAD export receipts are marked-to-market under IFRS and section 95F of the Income Tax Act, and any realized FX gains are taxable as business income. No statutory timeline exists for repatriating export earnings into Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in partnership with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for banking oversight, and the Department of Finance Canada for overall international financial policy. Currency transaction reporting is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate feasibility with legal constraints. Canada does not issue European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licences. A non-Canadian EMI may not obtain a passport directly; it must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and hold a money transmitter licence in each province where it operates (most substantially via Alberta's Money Services Business Act registration). In practice, an Alberta ULC may use well-regulated offshore digital banking platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, or an EU-licensed EMI) only as a settlement gateway for foreign currency receipts and contractor payouts, provided the EMI's flow is backed downstream by a Canadian Schedule I bank account. Legal constraints include FINTRAC-data residency, mandatory record keeping of large virtual currency transactions, and the prohibition of using an EMI as the sole treasury account because the ULC's statutory records and income-tax remittance accounts must be accessible to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and provincial enforcement authorities.
