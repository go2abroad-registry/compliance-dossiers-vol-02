Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE; corporate registration and provincial administrative interface through Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta)

E-government filing portal

An Alberta ULC is created through Alberta Corporate Registry, after which the CRA issues a nine-character Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (RT) on registration. Foreign shareholders may hold 100% of the ULC without minimum capital or residency requirements. Non-resident corporations seeking a BN/GST/HST account must file Form RC1 and provide identity documentation; a registered GST/HST account is mandatory once taxable worldwide revenues in Canada exceed the small supplier threshold or when making taxable supplies of digital services to Canadian consumers. The BN/RT must appear on invoices and GST/HST filings under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

For Canadian domestic tax, an Alberta ULC remains an entity-level taxable corporation; 'Pass_Through_Eligible' reflects U.S. check-the-box flexibility (Form 8832) under which the ULC may be classified as a disregarded entity for U.S. tax purposes. Canada does not offer a domestic check-the-box election, but closely related Canadian corporations may elect under ETA s.156 to zero-rate intercompany supplies. Hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss.18.2 and 18.4 restrict cross-border deduction duplications and other BEPS Action 2 outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 (small supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s.148, measured on worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four calendar quarters; non-resident digital service providers supplying specified digital products or services to Canadian consumers may be required to register under ETA s.240.1 once the same threshold is exceeded)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act to tangible goods exported by the supplier or through common carrier/mail to a non-resident destination, and to services or intangible supplies, including drama streaming rights, digital downloads, distribution royalties, and offshore advertising, supplied to a non-resident who acquires them for use wholly outside Canada. The supplier must demonstrate with contemporaneous records that the recipient is non-resident and that the supply is not made available to Canadian consumers. Subscriptions or over-the-top streaming services delivered to Canadian end-users are taxable at 5% GST in Alberta.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration (B13A/AED or equivalent via the Canadian Export Reporting System) where goods are physically exported
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing dispatch from Canada
  • Commercial invoice describing the zero-rated export and the non-resident purchaser
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment record demonstrating receipt of foreign currency or clearance from the non-resident
  • Export contract, purchase order, and non-resident identity evidence establishing offshore use and ownership of the exported product or service rights

Canada does not operate a mandatory national real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital stamping regime for GST/HST. Filing is electronic via GST/HST NETFILE; electronic invoices are accepted if readable and complete. Businesses and digital platforms must retain full transactional records, including customer identity, supply description, place of consumption, and evidence of zero-rating, to support export treatment.

CRA audits scrutinize the authenticity of zero-rated export claims, examining physical export movement, non-resident status, contracts, platform sales data, royalty flows, and inward remittance timing. If documentation fails to establish entitlement, the CRA may claw back the unremitted GST/HST, impose interest, and apply penalties under ETA s.280 for false statements (5%/10%/25%) and s.285.1 for gross negligence (50%). Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating can lead to criminal prosecution. Inward remittance delays beyond 180 days or incomplete FX repatriation evidence heighten audit suspicion and may result in denial of the zero rating and reassessment of prior claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction. The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions; there are no exchange controls, approval requirements, or quotas on foreign-currency accounts, inbound equity injections, or outbound capital repatriation. Profit remittance from an Alberta ULC is unrestricted, but dividends, interest, and royalties to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory rate), reduced by treaty, e.g., 5% on dividends to a qualifying US corporate shareholder under the Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty, including ULC-specific provisions of the Fifth Protocol available to US members.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline. The ULC may retain all streaming-app subscription export receipts (Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, Stripe) in CAD or foreign currency. Applicable obligations are limited to: (i) FINTRAC reporting of electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) Statistics Canada international services surveys when selected; and (iii) ordinary Income Tax Act treatment of foreign exchange gains and losses.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank). Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration; OSFI regulates deposit-taking institutions, FINTRAC administers AML/CFT and large EFT reporting, and the Minister of Finance retains residual powers under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with legal constraints. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., UK, EU, or Singapore-headquartered) may be used for ancillary cross-border payouts only if it is registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) or remains strictly offshore with no solicitation of Canadian clients. Unlicensed foreign EMIs cannot lawfully provide money-transfer or deposit services to a Canadian-resident ULC without breaching the Bank Act and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. FINTRAC-registered PSPs and EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, and Stripe are viable as supporting rails for mass creator payouts and app-store collection, but the primary treasury and settlement hub should be a Schedule I Canadian bank account to preserve multi-currency clearing, correspondent-banking relationships, ISDA counterparty capacity, and robust CDIC-insured deposit protection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder wires paid-up capital via SWIFT MT103 for the customer credit portion, with the remitting bank using MT202 for the underlying interbank movement. The wire must identify the ULC by full legal name and Alberta corporate-access number; the purpose-of-payment field must state Share Capital Subscription / Equity Contribution with the subscription reference. USD wires clear through the Canadian bank's US correspondent account; CAD wires clear directly through Payments Canada Lynx. The ULC must obtain board and shareholder resolutions, issue and file the share-allotment return with the Alberta Corporate Registry, and deliver the share certificate to the bank. For non-arm's length investors, Form T106 must be filed with the CRA; beneficial-ownership records under Alberta's Corporate Interests Transparency Act must be updated. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA practice requires contemporaneous documentation (Local File and Form T106) when the aggregate value of non-arm's-length transactions exceeds CAD 1 million per related party or class of transaction; Master File is expected for multinational groups with global consolidated revenue above CAD 1 billion, consistent with OECD BEPS Action 13.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) and the CRA's substance-over-form doctrine require genuine production, staffing, physical presence, custody of IP, and operational decision-making in Alberta to maintain tax advantages. A transparent ULC lacking these elements invites reassessment and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of misrepresented net income under ITA s.163(2); criminal conviction under s.239(1) carries a fine of 50–200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years (indictment); interest on arrears compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions under ITA s.227.1; willful participation in filing false returns triggers criminal complicity under s.242(1), with travel bans, garnishable assets, and up to 2 years imprisonment for summary conviction.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable income under ITA s.247(2); penalty under s.247(3) equal to 10% of the net adjustment amount where the taxpayer was grossly negligent or failed to make reasonable efforts and the penalty exceeds CAD 5,000; plus future tax interest.

Director personal liability: No automatic directorial liability, but a director who knowingly authorizes mispriced intercompany contracts may be assessed for penalties under s.225.1 if the corporation fails to pay; piercing of the corporate veil occurs for fraudulent conveyance where dividends were paid with intent to defeat tax claims.
Shell Company / General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: CRA may ignore the acquisition or arrangement and deny the tax benefit under ITA s.245(2); if a misrepresentation exists, a 50% GAAR penalty applies under s.245(4.1); the corporation may also be reclassified as not a resident of Canada for treaty purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors who allowed the shell arrangement and caused non-payment of taxes may have the corporate veil pierced under equitable restitution; travel restrictions and personal liability up to CAD 250,000 per director where deliberate misrepresentation occurs.
Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code laundering offenses under s.462.31 with imprisonment up to 10 years; PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 per contravention; assets subject to seizure and restraint.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted as laundering participants if they knowingly use unreported payment flows or omit beneficial ownership; they personally face asset forfeiture, travel bans, and restitution orders under s.462.34; revocation of corporate registrations for repeated non-filing.
