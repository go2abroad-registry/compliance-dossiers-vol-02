Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, Ottawa, ON — federal authority for GST/HST under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. E-15 and for federal corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.). Alberta's provincial corporate income tax (8% general rate) is administered by CRA under a federal-provincial collection agreement on behalf of Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration. Primary digital portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO); non-resident-oriented filings are routed through the CRA International Tax Services Office, 2204 Walkley Road, Ottawa, ON K1A 0A8.

E-government filing portal

The Alberta ULC (incorporated after March 2021 under the Business Corporations Act, RSA 2000, c B-9, as amended for unlimited liability companies) is legally resident in Canada pursuant to ITA s. 250(4)(c). Step 1: Apply through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), or file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' by fax or mail to the CRA International Tax Services Office, attaching certified Alberta Certificate of Status, articles of incorporation, and authorized-person identification. Step 2: CRA issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN) together with program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RC (T2 corporate income tax), RP (payroll if employees), and RD (CBSA import/export if physical goods are ever bundled). Step 3: GST/HST registration becomes effective on the earlier of the date of the first taxable supply made in Canada or the date the registrant ceases to be a small supplier (C$30,000 threshold); a Canadian-incorporated foreign-owned ULC is NOT eligible for the Simplified GST/HST Regime for non-resident digital-services suppliers (effective July 1, 2021), so standard registration rules apply. Step 4: Execute CRA electronic remittance enrolment (My Payment / pre-authorized debit) and e-filing mandates; T2 corporate income tax returns must be filed electronically once gross revenue exceeds C$1,000,000. Step 5: Maintain all books and records in Canada, or elsewhere where CRA's approval condition is satisfied, with full disclosure of worldwide related-party ownership.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Canadian tax characterization: the ULC is a company taxed at entity level as a Canadian-resident corporation (federal 15% basic rate plus Alberta 8% general rate; not a Canadian-controlled private corporation if majority non-resident-owned, so the C$500,000 small-business 2% active-business rate is generally unavailable). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Canadian tax characterization: the ULC is a company taxed at entity level as a Canadian-resident corporation (federal 15% basic rate plus Alberta 8% general rate; not a Canadian-controlled private corporation if majority non-resident-owned, so the C$500,000 small-business 2% active-business rate is generally unavailable). Foreign shareholders receiving distributions are subject to Part XIII withholding at 25%, generally reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention. US tax characterization: a Canadian ULC is an 'eligible entity' (not a 'per se' corporation); it defaults to disregarded-entity status when 100% owned by a single US shareholder, or partnership status when multi-member, unless an affirmative Form 8832 check-the-box election selects corporate classification. The resulting profile is entity-level Canadian taxation with US flow-through eligibility — the classic ULC hybrid structure.

Available election forms

(a) US check-the-box election under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3 using Form 8832 to elect corporation, partnership, or disregarded-entity status; Article IV of the Canada-US Treaty contains ULC-specific flow-through rules that may deny or restrict treaty benefits where a Canadian company is fiscally transparent in the United States. (b) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations to treat intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration and/or to file a single combined GST/HST return for the corporate group. (c) ITA s. 85 rollover election to defer capital gains on property transfers into the ULC; ITA s. 5907 surplus elections for structuring outbound foreign-affiliate earnings. (d) Hybrid-mismatch elections and traps: ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 (interest/royalty deductibility) and s. 94B (dividends) deny Canadian deductions where the corresponding payment is not reasonably included in US taxable income because of the ULC's flow-through status; prudent capital structure design is required. (e) GST/HST zero-rating of exported digital services is status-based (ETA Schedule VI, Part V), not election-based.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1)(a), measured on the worldwide taxable supplies of the ULC plus any associated persons, in the current calendar quarter and in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters. Once the threshold is exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory under ETA s. 240, effective on the first day of the month following the calendar quarter of exceedance. The ULC must collect 5% GST on taxable B2B and B2C supplies made in Canada, including AI avatar/video-generation SaaS subscriptions sold through Stripe, Paddle, or other payment rails to Canadian recipients; zero-rated export supplies do not count toward the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported digital services are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA, principally paragraph 7(a) (supply made to a non-resident person who is not registered under the ETA, not acting as a Canadian agent, and not acquiring the service for consumption in Canada) and paragraph 7(b) (services supplied to non-resident persons that relate to real property or tangible personal property located outside Canada). For the ULC's AI avatar/video-generation SaaS, zero-rating applies to: (i) B2B subscriptions by foreign-incorporated customers without a Canadian establishment that are not GST/HST registered; (ii) B2C consumers whose normal place of residence is outside Canada; and (iii) supplies settled through Stripe/Paddle where the contract, IP-delivery records, geolocation of access, and server logs confirm performance and delivery outside Canada. Canadian-resident customers are taxable at 5% GST in Alberta. Input tax credits (ITCs) on Canadian development, hosting, and operating overheads are fully recoverable to the extent the ULC makes zero-rated exports. Where Paddle operates as merchant of record, consumption tax duties may contractually shift to Paddle, but the ULC remains the legal supplier and must preserve its own zero-rating substantiation file.

  • CBSA Form B13A Export Declaration (filed electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System; required only where physical goods such as hardware-embedded avatar devices are exported, not for pure digital SaaS)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / carrier transport record evidencing physical export from Canada (for hybrid digital-plus-hardware products)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, Stripe/Paddle settlement statement, or bank credit advice evidencing foreign-currency consideration repatriated into the ULC's Canadian bank account
  • Executed SaaS subscription agreement, statement of work, or license order form identifying the non-resident customer and delivery jurisdiction
  • IP delivery and usage logs: deployment records, server-geolocation verification, IP-address geolocation of logins, and output-delivery metadata demonstrating that AI avatar video content was accessed outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice and GST/HST e-filing acknowledgment (CRA NETFILE confirmation) evidencing the claimed zero-rated export fields on the return

Canada has NO real-time fiscal clearance regime, no CRA API-based digital stamping, and no South-American-style electronic invoice authorization as of 2025. Mandatory electronic filing does apply: GST/HST registrants with annual gross revenue above C$1,500,000 must file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE, EFILE, or CRA My Business Account); all issuers of invoices must maintain the prescribed contents of ETA s. 223(1) — supplier legal name, Business Number, date, description, tax rate and amount — and for zero-rated exported SaaS, the invoice must clearly indicate zero-rated status. CRA is aligned with the voluntary Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework built on the Peppol/PINT open standard; adoption does not yet trigger CRA clearance or mandatory stamping. Place-of-supply rules under Schedule IX of the ETA deem digital services (AI video-generation and virtual-avatar SaaS) to be supplied in Canada where the recipient's normal place of residence or billing address is in Canada; payments through Stripe/Paddle should be routed through integrated tax engines that validate the CRA BN/RT account for B2B customers.

CRA audit scrutiny of zero-rated digital exports is elevated, particularly for hybrid ULC structures. The normal reassessment window is four years from the date the GST/HST return is required to be filed (ETA s. 298(1)); the limitation does not apply where there is misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, nor in cases of fraud. Penalties for non-compliance: failure to register attracts a penalty of the greater of C$250 and 1% of the consideration for taxable supplies (ETA s. 280); failure to remit attracts a 3% penalty on the unremitted amount (ETA s. 280.1); false statements or omissions in any return attract a penalty of the greater of C$250 and 25% of the net tax understated (ETA s. 285). Retrospective clawbacks typically disallow the zero-rating, reassess 5% GST on the full invoiced export value, disallow input tax credits, and add compound daily interest at the CRA prescribed rate (approximately 8%-9% for 2025). Wilful evasion is a criminal offence under ETA s. 327(1) with fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment for up to five years. In addition, because the entity is an Alberta ULC with unlimited shareholder liability, and because the US-check-the-box parent may be a disregarded-entity owner, CRA can pursue the ULC shareholders personally for reassessed GST/HST, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is fully convertible on both current and capital account transactions, having accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. No prior exchange-control approval is required for inbound capital injections, outbound trade settlements, dividend remittances, or repatriation of profits. Profit remittance from an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (CA_ULC) to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted; however, dividends and deemed-dividend distributions are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), at 25% statutory rate, reduced to 15% under the Canada-US Tax Treaty and further to 5% where the US recipient is a corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the voting stock. The ULC's unlimited-liability feature does not block distribution but may affect bank credit underwriting and treaty eligibility, requiring careful characterization of the entity as a 'person' entitled to treaty benefits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement for exporters to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign-currency receipts into CAD. There is no mandatory timeline for repatriation of export revenue and no minimum conversion ratio. A ULC may retain foreign currency in resident or offshore operating accounts; the only reporting obligations arise under FINTRAC's cross-border currency declaration rules (disclosure of physical currency or monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000 upon crossing the border), the Income Tax Act's T106/T1134 foreign-reporting requirements for transactions with non-residents, and standard corporate tax filing for eligible business income subject to the CAD 600,000 federal small-business deduction limit and Alberta provincial small-business rate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acting in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no centralized foreign-exchange control authority; the Bank of Canada operates a freely floating CAD exchange rate and does not administer currency surrender or conversion approvals.

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as supplemental liquidity rail, Low as primary settlement infrastructure. Canada does not recognize the European EMI license; digital cross-border providers such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, not as deposit-taking institutions. Capital contributions from non-resident shareholders into the CA_ULC operational account at a Tier-1 bank must be executed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank settlement) using the participating bank's international wire instructions, denominated in CAD or a fully convertible major currency. The remitting bank must state one of the following ISO 20022 purpose codes as applicable: 'CUST' (Customer Capital Contribution), 'EQUI' (Equity Injection), or 'COST' (Contribution in Cash), and in the narrative field the standard code 'B7' (Share Capital) per the Bank of Canada's CFIC (Canadian Financial Instruments Code) annex. Verification steps: (1) pre-notification of the deposit with a scanned copy of the board resolution authorizing issuance of ULC shares; (2) source-of-funds questionnaire completed by the remitting shareholder; (3) confirmation of creditor/debtor names matching the ULC's business number and corporate name; (4) post-receipt reconciliation against the share register and filing of the ISC report; (5) registration of the share issuance in the Alberta Corporate Registry within the statutory filing period.
