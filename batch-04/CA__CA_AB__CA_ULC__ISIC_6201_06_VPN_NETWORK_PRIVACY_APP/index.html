Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Alberta ULC. Shares may be issued without par value. No statutory escrow account is required. The corporation must authorize and issue shares in accordance with its articles, share terms, directors' resolutions, subscription agreements, and applicable solvency requirements.

Funding & unlock path: No sector-specific civil-defence clearance is ordinarily required for an office-based SaaS business. Structural-load or engineering inspections are generally conditional on leased-space alterations, server-room construction, elevated equipment loads, generators, or other physical installations.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, CRA My Business Account portal; Alberta Treasury Board and Finance – Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA/ATRA), including Alberta Corporate Income Tax administration under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act. Alberta does not levy a provincial sales tax or HST, so GST/HST registration is exclusively with CRA at the federal level.

E-government filing portal

An Alberta ULC must first complete Alberta incorporation through the Alberta Corporate Registry, which issues a Corporation Access Number. The ULC must then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) as the federal statutory TIN by using Business Registration Online or filing Form RC1; if the ULC is treated as a non-resident applicant for GST/HST purposes, Form RC59 and an authorized representative arrangement may apply. The BN is the taxpayer identification number for GST/HST, federal corporate income tax, payroll, and import/export program accounts. For Alberta corporate income tax, the ULC must separately register with the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration and file Alberta corporate tax returns using the corporation's Alberta account details, while also maintaining the CRA BN. The foreign parent or foreign shareholders must provide registered office information, director identification, and contemporaneous books and records; no minimum share capital is required for an Alberta ULC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a domestic check-the-box regime. An Alberta ULC remains a separate taxable corporation under the federal Income Tax Act and Alberta corporate income tax law, and no Canadian election can make the ULC fiscally transparent. The 'Pass_Through_Eligible' classification is relevant primarily for U.S. federal tax purposes: a Canadian ULC may be classified as a disregarded entity or may elect corporate status by timely filing IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election, with corresponding hybrid mismatch, transfer pricing, and treaty limitation-on-benefits consequences. Hybrid mismatch rules may restrict deductions where a payment is deductible in Canada and excluded or deducted again in the shareholder's jurisdiction because of divergent ULC classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, made in Canada in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; once the threshold is exceeded, mandatory registration applies. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold, subject to CRA conditions and the requirement to remain registered for at least one year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For tangible goods, 0% GST/HST applies when the supplier exports the goods under a commercial export arrangement or delivers them to a licensed exporter for export, and proof of export is retained. For VPN network privacy software, digital encryption services, and other intangible personal property or services, zero-rating applies when the supply is made to a non-resident person and the service or intangible property is not supplied for use, consumption, or enjoyment in Canada. The supplier must show by contract, subscription terms, payment routing, IP address and geo-location logs, subscriber records, and delivery documentation that the recipient is non-Canadian and that the VPN service is not used in Canada. A supply made to a Canadian resident or to a non-resident individual physically present in Canada for consumption in Canada is subject to 5% GST.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (B13A) or equivalent customs export declaration for goods
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill showing the exporter, consignee, and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank wire receipt, or payment settlement record evidencing payment from the non-resident buyer
  • GST/HST invoice inscribed with the zero-rating eligibility and the supplier's BN and GST/HST account number
  • Executed service agreement, software licence, or subscription agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and territorial use rights
  • Subscriber and VPN network logs demonstrating provision of the service only outside Canada

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no API transaction stamping, and no digital stamping requirement for GST/HST invoices. E-invoicing and PEPPOL participation are voluntary. GST/HST invoices may be issued electronically or on paper if they include the supplier name, BN and GST/HST registration number, invoice date, recipient, description of supply, consideration, and applicable tax rate or amount. Registered vendors must retain full records capable of supporting zero-rating and may be required to provide electronic records to CRA in an accessible format.

CRA will deny zero-rating and recalculate GST/HST at 5% if the export documentation is incomplete, if the VPN service is shown to be supplied to Canadian consumers, or if the non-resident recipient resupplies or uses the service in Canada. CRA may reassess beyond the normal four-year limitation period where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default exists, claw back input tax credits, and add statutory interest and civil penalties, including penalties for false statements, failure to remit, and gross negligence. Criminal prosecution may also be pursued in egregious cases. A 180-day documentation and payment reconciliation policy is prudent because CRA expects sufficient banking and foreign-exchange records to establish that the export price was actually paid by the non-resident recipient within a reasonable commercial period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Foreign equity injection, profit repatriation, dividends, interest, royalties, and trade settlements require no exchange-control approval. Frictions are tax-driven only: 25% statutory Part XIII withholding on dividends, interest, and royalties; treaty-reduced rates under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention (15%/5% dividends; 0-10% interest for arm's length counterparties); potential recharacterization of ULC share distributions as deemed interest for qualifying foreign shareholders; Form T106 reporting for non-arm's length cross-border payments; and Investment Canada Act notification for non-Canadian establishment of a business. VPN software exports are not subject to any capital-movement permit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline for export proceeds or SaaS subscription receipts. Foreign currency may be retained onshore or offshore indefinitely and freely converted through any Schedule I bank or FINTRAC-registered money services business.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary and retail payment supervision authority; OSFI supervises federally regulated banks and deposit-taking institutions; FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Canada maintains no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration; exchange control is absent at the federal level.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, limited to payment-acceptance and payout side-rails. Canada does not grant EU-style EMI licences; offshore digital EMIs must register as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC and, if providing retail payment services, with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (in force November 1, 2024). They cannot take deposits, hold escrow, or act as the ULC's primary clearing bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory de minimis; CRA requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for all cross-border non-arm's-length transactions, with the administrative threshold for formal disclosure set at CAD 1 million per category of transactions; consolidated international groups must prepare CbC/Master File documentation when global revenue is at least CAD 750 million

Economic substance scope: No specific Economic Substance Regulations are enacted in Canada as for OFC regimes; however, the Canadian general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) at ITA s.245 disregards transactions that lack genuine economic substance and are primarily tax-motivated, and the OECD BEPS MLI, plus the treaty LOB clauses, can deny benefits to a VPN/network privacy ULC that does not carry on adequate real business activities, employment, and risk-bearing functions in Alberta

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Omission of Canadian-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under ITA s.239 carries a fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA may levy civil penalties and gross negligence charges (10-50% of the understatement) plus compounded interest

Director personal liability: Director/shareholder officers can be held jointly and severally for unpaid source deductions and GST/HST under ITA s.227.1; where fraud is proven, the corporate veil is pierced, directors face direct civil restitution, travel bans on leaving Canada, and potential incarceration
Shell Company / Essence Without Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA can reassess, deny deduction of charges, invoke GAAR to disregard the arrangement, and impose a 15% GAAR penalty on the tax benefit plus recaptured tax and interest; treaty benefits may be denied under the principal purpose test where the ULC structure is a conduit

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize artificial transfer-pricing schemes without adjusting arm's-length returns are exposed to the 10% transfer-pricing penalty under ITA s.247(3) and, upon deliberate participation, to anti-avoidance sanctions and personal liability for the corporate tax shortfall
Transfer Pricing Manipulation via Non-Arm's-Length Intellectual Property Royalties

Statutory sanction: Transfer price adjustment to arm's-length reaching from CRA under s.247; if the adjustment exceeds the lesser of 10% of gross revenue and CAD 5 million, a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment applies; the taxpayer must also pay interest and may be unable to rely on treaty relief without valid substance

Director personal liability: Officers who sign knowingly false or incomplete T106 declarations are subject to penalties up to CAD 24,000 and gross-negligence misconduct penalties; they can be held personally liable for the corporate remittance shortfall to the Crown where the overvaluation is part of a fraudulent stripping arrangement
Money Laundering / AML Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million per violation per entity; under the Criminal Code, money-laundering offences carry imprisonment up to 10 years; a total ban on the ULC distributable profits would not apply, but the entity continues as a separate legal person only if remedial filings are made

Director personal liability: Senior officers and directors who knew of, authorized, or were wilfully blind to unflagged suspicious transactions are personally indictable; forfeiture of personal assets, monetary fines, and imprisonment up to 10 years are possible; travel bans and immigration restrictions may be enforced through the FATF frameworks
