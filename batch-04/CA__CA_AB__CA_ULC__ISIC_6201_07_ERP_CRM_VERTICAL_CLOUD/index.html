E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canada); pass-through eligibility exists only as an elective U.S. federal tax attribute of the Alberta ULC under the U.S. check-the-box rules.

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act (ITA), an Alberta ULC is a 'corporation' under s. 248(1), deemed resident under s. 250(4)(a), and taxed at the entity level on worldwide net income (federal Part I general rate plus Alberta 8% general corporate rate, no provincial sales tax). There is no Canadian 'check-the-box' election. For U.S. purposes, the ULC is a permissive 'foreign eligible entity' under Treasury Reg. § 301.7701-3; U.S. shareholders may elect disregarded/partnership treatment on Form 8832, creating a hybrid entity. Canada's BEPS Action 2 anti-hybrid rules (ITA ss. 18.2, 18.4 and Part VI.1 'excessive eligibility' provisions) may deny deductions for certain hybrid mismatch payments, and ITA s. 111.9 restricts dual-consolidated-loss utilization in cross-border ULC structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (net of zero-rated exports, financial services, and non-taxable items) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters — the ETA small-supplier threshold (ETA s. 148). Non-resident suppliers of cloud software and other 'specified supplies' to Canadian consumers apply the same CAD 30,000 worldwide threshold under the 1 July 2021 simplified registration regime. Associated corporations must aggregate supplies under ETA s. 156.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Vertical ERP/CRM cloud subscriptions qualify for 0% GST as (i) services supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST, where the service is not performed in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada (Sched. VI, Part V, s. 7 ETA), or (ii) intangible personal property (software licences, cloud right-to-use) supplied to a non-resident for consumption wholly outside Canada (Sched. VI, Part V, s. 10 ETA). B2B supplies to Canadian GST/HST registrants remain taxable at 5% (the customer claims an input tax credit); only genuine offshore foreign customers qualify for zero-rating. The exporter must substantiate non-residency (foreign registry documentation, foreign billing address, IP geolocation records), absence of Canadian use or benefit, and offshore service delivery; platform monitoring and IP/DNS metadata are recommended as primary evidence, with CRA Form GST 334 (Certificate of Non-Residence) as best-practice corroboration.

  • GST/HST-compliant commercial invoice (ETA s. 223) expressly quoting '0% GST — Sched. VI, Part V, ss. 7/10 ETA'
  • Executed cloud subscription/licence agreement with non-resident covenants, offshore consumption clause, and designated foreign delivery/jurisdiction terms
  • Non-resident customer declaration (CRA Form GST 334 Certificate of Non-Residence, or equivalent) confirming non-registration for GST/HST and absence of Canadian establishment
  • Incoming foreign-currency remittance evidence: SWIFT MT103, Stripe Billing settlement report, or Direct Debit credit advice matched to the applicable invoice

None mandatory. Canada and Alberta currently impose no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API invoice-authorization regime. The CRA promotes voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing interoperability through the PEPPOL network (BIS 3.0 documents). GST/HST registrants must issue standard paper or electronic invoices meeting ETA s. 223 content requirements (supplier name, BN, GST/HST registration number, invoice date, tax rate and amount or a clear '0%' zero-rating indicator) and must retain all records, including electronic subscription and payment-platform data, for at least six (6) years.

Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day field is the operational audit-documentation benchmark for demonstrating a bona fide export transaction. Where CRA denies zero-rating on inadequate documentation, the supply is re-taxed at 5% with compound daily interest under ETA s. 280 and any associated input tax credits are clawed back. Gross negligence penalties equal 25% of the reassessed tax for false statements (ETA s. 285(1)). Criminal evasion sanctions range from a minimum fine of CAD 4,000 up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment up to five (5) years (ETA s. 327). The normal reassessment limitation period is four (4) years after filing, extended to ten (10) years where misrepresentation or omission is established (ETA s. 298). Circular payment flows, offshore shell counterparties, or persistent late repatriation will elevate audit scrutiny of export zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII current-account convertibility and maintains an open capital account. There are no federal or Alberta exchange controls preventing conversion of CAD into USD/EUR or reverse. Profit repatriation is not restricted by FX legislation, but cross-border remittances of dividends, interest, royalties, or management fees are subject to withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada). An Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) may distribute proceeds freely through a domestic Canadian bank account, provided the bank records the tax residency and underlying board authorization for payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio exists in Canada. Export receipts, including cloud and software-as-a-service service fees, may be retained in foreign currency in authorized bank accounts. Under OSFI reporting guidelines banks report material cross-border balances and CBSA may require transaction-level reporting for merchandise but not service exports; an Alberta ULC is not obligated to convert foreign currency receipts into CAD, and no treasury timeline is imposed for conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange control oversight is distributed among the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), with monetary authority vested solely in the Bank of Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a settlement and collection channel, not as a primary banking substitute. Under the Bank Act, only federally incorporated chartered banks may accept deposits in Canada; digital EMIs incorporated abroad may operate through FINTRAC-registered money-services-business licensing and the Retail Payment Activities Act final supervisory framework, but they cannot hold ULC treasury funds as bank deposits. Wise, Stripe Treasury, and similar EMIs are viable for low-value merchant remittances, payroll, and vendor distributions, provided settlement flows are swept daily to a Canadian Tier 1 bank account to preserve clearing integrity and regulatory oversight.

Annual unanimous shareholder resolutions (waiver of auditor, approval of financial statements, confirmation of share transfers and board composition) Signed resolutions minuted within 60 days after the fiscal year end and in all events before the annual-return due date; share transfers evidenced in the register of members effective on the transfer date. $0 USD An unwaived year makes a compulsory independent audit mandatory; missing or defective minutes create director and officer liability exposure, weaken shareholder authorization of dividends and distributions, and are flagged in due diligence.
