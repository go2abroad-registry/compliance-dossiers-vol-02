Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll, and Alberta corporate income tax under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement; Alberta counterpart is Alberta Treasury Board and Finance. Primary portal: CRA My Business Account (MRA) / GST/HST Business Portal, plus Represent a Client services.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a Canada Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA. The nine-digit BN is the sole national tax identifier; no Alberta-specific TIN exists. Non-residents must identify the jurisdiction of residence and may be required to designate an agent for service in Canada. The GST/HST program account (RM) is activated on the same BN; registration for the corporate income tax account (RT) and payroll account (RP) follows the ordinary corporate procedures. For an Alberta ULC, the constituent registration under the Alberta Business Corporations Act must also be completed with Alberta Corporate Registry, but that is not a tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation for Canadian domestic tax: an Alberta ULC is a 'Canadian corporation' resident in Canada and is subject to federal and provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income; no Canadian domestic check-the-box regime exists. Pass_Through_Eligible for U.S. federal tax: the ULC may make a 'check-the-box' election on IRS Form 8832 to be treated as a disregarded entity (single shareholder) or partnership (multiple shareholders). Territorial scope for non-resident shareholders and branch operations: Canadian-source income of such entities is subject to tax; a ULC is not a Tax_Exempt_Zone entity.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a domestic election to reclassify a ULC as a partnership or disregarded entity; Canadian law treats the ULC as a corporation at all times. The principal available election is the U.S. IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election (or default classification rules under Treasury Reg. §301.7701-3) which allows the ULC to be treated as a flow-through for U.S. federal income tax purposes. In a cross-border hybrid context, the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention and hybrid mismatch rules may reclassify an entity for treaty purposes, and an entity-level election or co-ordination statement may be required to avoid double non-taxation. For Alberta corporate tax purposes, no provincial classification election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) over any consecutive four-quarter period; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. A non-resident whose only Canadian activity is the supply of cloud cybersecurity services to non-residents is generally outside this threshold, but once the threshold is met or the person is carrying on a commercial activity in Canada, registration becomes compulsory. Voluntary registration is available to non-residents before the threshold is reached, frequently used to recover input tax credits on Canadian development expenses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of cloud vulnerability scanning and penetration testing services (classified as an intangible personal property/service) is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when supplied to a non-resident who is not registered under the ETA for that supply, and the supply is not made in connection with Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or services provided to an individual present in Canada. For IPP-based SaaS, the zero-rating condition further requires that the IPP be not for use in Canada and not be delivered or made available in Canada to any person. The supplier must demonstrate, through contractual terms, IP-usage clauses, and technical logs, that the recipient's use and enjoyment occur outside Canada. A GST/HST zero-rated supply is reported on a GST/HST return and may create a net refund where input tax credits exceed output tax on zero-rated supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Export Declaration or CERS/B13A) for any tangible goods bundled with the SaaS; for pure cloud services, a non-resident service contract with IP location clauses replaces the customs declaration.
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill for carrier-based exports of hardware/appliances; for SaaS-only, a server log / network access report showing non-resident delivery and no domestic consumption is the evidentiary substitute.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT payment confirmation from a Canadian bank evidencing receipt of convertible foreign currency and reconciliation to the invoiced amount, supporting FX repatriation and avoiding later tax-qualified foreign income recharacterization.

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoice stamping, or digital stamping. CRA permits electronic invoicing and electronic record retention, provided the invoice contains all prescribed ETA information (supplier BN, name, date, description of supply, consideration, GST/HST rate, and amount of tax). The CRA publishes specifications for receiving invoices electronically. There is no mandatory API-based real-time clearance; however, in Quebec, large enterprises may be required to use the RPCI/IBS e-invoicing framework, which does not apply to Alberta ULCs. Adoption of voluntary PEPPOL-BIS/PINT standards is increasing for B2B e-invoicing but is not statutory in Alberta.

CRA can reassess a zero-rated export supply at 5% GST/HST if the documentary evidence fails to prove non-resident receipt or if the facts show use or enjoyment in Canada. The clawback includes the unremitted tax, compound interest under ETA s.280, and penalties under ETA s.285 (false or misleading statements: 25% of the amount assessed), ETA s.280 (failure to remit: 5% general penalty), and late-filing penalties under ETA s.281 (5% plus 1% per month up to 12 months). In fraudulent export schemes, criminal tax evasion charges may be brought under ETA s.327.1(1), with fines up to 200% of the tax attempted to be evaded and imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks generally follow a four-year limitation window, extended to eight years or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible on both the current account and the capital account. There are no exchange-control approvals, no current-account conversion quotas, no limits on profit remittance, and no restriction on the repatriation of invested capital. Dividends, interest, management fees, royalties, and proceeds from liquidation of an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation may be transferred abroad without Bank of Canada authorization. The only relevant friction is Canadian taxation, principally withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act at the statutory 25% rate, reducible by applicable tax treaty (for example, 5% or 15% on non-resident dividend distributions under the Canada-United States Tax Convention). The underlying cybersecurity SaaS subscription income constitutes a cross-border current-account receipt freely creditworthy to a Canadian operating account. No de facto convertibility restriction exists, although banks may apply standard sanctions and source-of-funds checks before releasing international wire transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no regulatory timeline for repatriating export or service-proceeds. Export receipts from cloud vulnerability scanning and penetration-testing SaaS may be received in USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency, retained indefinitely in foreign-currency bank accounts, converted at negotiated commercial rates, or used directly for Syrian? No, used directly for global disbursements without requiring notification to the Bank of Canada. No Canadian exporter is required to surrender foreign currency to the central bank. Foreign-currency accounts maintained at Canadian banks are lawful and may be used for treasury operations. The only obligations that may attach to such receipts are ordinary income tax characterization, transfer-pricing rules if the ULC and its non-resident parent transact at non-arm's-length terms, and FINTRAC reporting for transactions above CAD 10,000 in a twenty-four-hour period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), functioning as the central monetary authority and supervisor of retail payment systems, together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation of federally incorporated banks and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF supervision. Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control bureau; the Minister of Finance, through the Investment Review Division under the Investment Canada Act, exercises limited screening over certain inbound investments that are not otherwise exempt or below notification thresholds.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. A non-resident electronic-money institution (EMI) is not recognized as a bank under the Bank Act and therefore cannot be used as the primary settlement custodian for the Alberta ULC. A foreign EMI may be used as a payment gateway or collection facilitator, but the ULC's own treasury cash must reside at a CDIC-member Canadian Schedule I bank or an authorized foreign bank branch. Retail payment services provided to Canadians may trigger registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which is administered by the Bank of Canada and phased in through 2024-2025. If the EMI is a non-bank that merely routes subscription receipts into the ULC's Canadian bank account, it should be contractually documented as a payment facilitator and not as the master settlement account. Legal constraints include absence of CDIC deposit insurance for EMI pooled accounts, FINTRAC registration/reporting obligations on money-services activities, sanctions screening requirements, and the need to ensure that the EMI does not hold the ULC's funds in a manner that would create unauthorized money-service business risk for the Canadian entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routes are via SWIFT MT103 international wire transfer from the non-resident parent or investor through its foreign bank to the ULC's Canadian settlement account at a Schedule I bank. Canada does not mandate a specific central-bank purpose code, but the remittance information field (Field 70) should expressly identify the commercial nature, for example 'Capital subscription - Alberta ULC shares' or 'Shareholder loan - intercompany treasury'. The Canadian receiving bank will apply standard AML verification, sanctions screening, and beneficial-ownership confirmation before credit. Supporting contractual documentation should be held in treasury files and produced on request, including executed subscription agreement, loan agreement, board resolutions, and source-of-funds records. If the capital injection creates a new Canadian business owned by a non-Canadian, an Investment Canada Act notification or review threshold analysis should be performed. After the inbound SWIFT credit reaches the Canadian bank's correspondent, the domestic CAD leg is cleared on the Lynx high-value payment system; if the injected amount is in USD, funds are held in a USD settlement account or converted by a bank-treasury FX trade.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements and vendor expense payments should use the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for international supplier invoices, dividend/royalty remittances, and professional-fee payments, with the ULC as ordering customer and the foreign recipient as beneficiary; (ii) Commercial Letter of Credit (documentary LC) or documentary collections when the transaction involves physical deliverables or contracted trade milestones, issued endorsed by the Canadian relationship bank; (iii) domestic electronic payments through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for Canadian payroll, tax, and vendor disbursements; and (iv) major corporate card or virtual-card rails for low-value operational expenses. There is no exchange-control permission required before sending outbound wires, and no financing restriction. The bank will debit the CAD or foreign-currency account and, if required, execute a spot FX trade through the same relationship treasury desk. Legal constraints include absence of CDIC deposit insurance for EMI pooled accounts, FINTRAC registration/reporting obligations on money-services activities, sanctions screening requirements, and the need to ensure that the EMI does not hold the ULC's funds in a manner that would create unauthorized money-service business risk for the Canadian entity.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate rate (federal 15% + Alberta 8%) for an Alberta ULC not entitled to the Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) small business deduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends paid by the Canadian-resident ULC to a non-resident shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation under subsection 247(4) of the Income Tax Act for transactions between the ULC and non-arm's length non-residents. Form T106 must be filed when aggregate cross-border non-arm's length transactions exceed C$1,000,000 in fair market value. The Canadian transfer pricing penalty threshold for formal documentation is generally triggered when the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue; the Master File and Country-by-Country Report filing threshold for the U.S. parent group is C$1,000,000,000 of consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate statutory 'Economic Substance Regulations' comparable to certain Gulf cooperation council jurisdictions. Instead, substance is enforced through the sham doctrine, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in section 245 of the Income Tax Act, the acquisition-of-control loss restriction rules, and the limitation-of-benefits provisions of the Canada–U.S. treaty. CRA will disregard an Alberta ULC that has no physical office, no employees, no board minutes, no bank account, and no real management function in Alberta; the entity is treated as a pure conduit and income may be allocated to the actual beneficial owner. A ULC used in cybersecurity SaaS must demonstrate genuine Canadian business activity, including contract negotiation, delivery of penetration testing, hosting of vulnerability data, and technical risk management.
