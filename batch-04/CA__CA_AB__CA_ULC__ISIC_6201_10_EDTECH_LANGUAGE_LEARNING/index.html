Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Alberta ULC; subscribers may record nominal consideration (e.g., CAD 1,000) in the Share Subscription List, receivable in a Canadian-dollar corporate bank account prior to share issuance; no escrow or statutory holdback is required.

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety or structural inspection is triggered for a virtual headquarters; if the ULC occupies a physical office, Alberta Fire Code (Alta Reg 55/2019) inspection and municipal occupancy permit apply, and workers' compensation coverage registration with WCB-Alberta is mandatory upon hiring employees.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Alberta ULC; subscribers may record nominal consideration (e.g., CAD 1,000) in the Share Subscription List, receivable in a Canadian-dollar corporate bank account prior to share issuance; no escrow or statutory holdback is required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; digital portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MyBA), GST/HST NETFILE, T2 Corporation Internet Filing. Provincial corporate registry: Alberta Corporate Registry (Corporate Online Service) under Service Alberta.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the ULC under Part 5.1 of the Business Corporations Act (Alberta), RSA 2000, c B-9 (Alberta ULC regime effective 2021) and obtain the Alberta Certificate of Incorporation. Step 2: Register with the CRA for a Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1, electing the Corporation Income Tax account and the GST/HST account (and Import/Export account only if tangible goods are eventually exported). Step 3: The BN is issued instantly online; the GST/HST account becomes effective as of the quarter in which worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, with mandatory registration within 30 days thereafter. Step 4: Non-resident (e.g., U.S.) shareholders must deliver Form W-8BEN-E and a valid U.S. TIN; the ULC should retain the Article XXIX(8) treaty election documentation for the fiscally transparent election file. Step 5: File annual T2 corporate returns and periodic GST/HST returns electronically via CRA's MyBA portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The Alberta ULC is a 'taxable Canadian corporation' for Canadian income tax purposes, but is uniquely eligible under Article XXIX(8) of the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention (Fifth Protocol) to elect fiscally transparent (partnership/disregarded entity) treatment for U.S. tax purposes; U.S. shareholders may file Form 8832 (Check-the-Box) to effect the U.S. classification. For GST/HST, the ULC may execute a joint election under Excise Tax Act s. 156 with an affiliated corporation to treat inter-corporate supplies as made for nil consideration. Alberta does not provide a separate entity-classification check-the-box regime; provincial corporate income tax follows federal entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in aggregate worldwide taxable supplies in any single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, Excise Tax Act s. 148); once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration within 30 days

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of subscription-based digital language-learning software and automated tutoring services to non-resident persons are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act: s. 7 (supply of services to a non-resident otherwise than in Canada in respect of real property in Canada or tangible personal property in Canada) and s. 10 (supply of intangible personal property to a non-resident not for use in Canada). Where sales are made through Apple App Store (Apple_IAP) or Google Play Billing, the digital economy marketplace rules (effective July 1, 2021) deem the non-resident platform operator to be the supplier of the underlying subscription to Canadian consumers, so the ULC's platform-originated revenue stream is not subject to GST/HST collection by the ULC; a supply from the ULC to the non-resident platform operator may itself be zero-rated as a service/intangible supplied to a non-resident.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) - required only if physical educational goods are exported; not required for pure digital subscription services
  • Commercial invoice / order record identifying the non-resident customer and the digital subscription delivered
  • Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Console financial reports showing country-level sales, payment settlement amounts, and any platform-remitted GST/HST
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT records evidencing receipt of foreign funds
  • Non-resident customer attestation or IP geolocation / subscriber onboarding logs establishing non-residency (B2B: signed NRA self-declaration)
  • GST/HST return and zero-rated supply worksheet demonstrating the export classification of the relevant revenue

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping requirement. The CRA administers the voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (Peppol band); GST/HST returns and T2 returns must be filed electronically by most registrants, with mandatory electronic filing for GST/HST registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1,000,000 and for corporations whose gross revenue exceeds CAD 1,000,000. Invoices may be issued electronically (PDF/XML) without government pre-approval.

CRA may reassess retroactively for four years under Excise Tax Act s. 298(1); the limitation period is unlimited where a return contains misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud (ETA s. 298(6)). Fraudulent zero-rating exposes the registrant to a civil penalty under ETA s. 280.1(1) equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded, plus interest and retroactive clawback of input tax credits and refunds. Income tax gross-negligence penalties (ITA s. 163(2)) of 50% of the understated tax may also apply. Criminal prosecution for tax fraud exceeding CAD 1,000,000 under Criminal Code s. 380(1)(a) carries a maximum penalty of 14 years' imprisonment, fines at the court's discretion, and CRA collection measures including refund set-off, bank freezing, and asset garnishment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current account and capital account items. No quantitative restrictions, licensing, or prior approval requirements apply to CAD conversion, foreign-currency holding, or cross-border capital movements. Profit repatriation from a Canadian ULC to non-resident members is unrestricted under exchange-control law but is subject to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, potentially reduced under an applicable income tax treaty). EdTech subscription revenue constitutes an export of digital services that is zero-rated for GST/HST purposes; no services-sector foreign-ownership restriction applies. The ULC may freely maintain CAD and USD operating accounts and settle contractual obligations in either currency at market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada does not impose surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timelines on export proceeds received in foreign currency. An Alberta ULC may retain USD or other foreign currency indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts. However, FINTRAC reporting obligations apply to (i) electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more entering or leaving Canada, and (ii) physical cross-border currency movements above the same threshold. The corporate group must also file Form T1135 (Foreign Income Verification Statement) if the ULC holds specified foreign property with an aggregate cost amount exceeding CAD 100,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada maintains a liberalized, fully floating foreign-exchange regime; there is no dedicated FX exchange-control authority. FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) is the financial intelligence unit responsible for AML/ATF reporting and large cross-border transaction surveillance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High as a supporting disbursement rail only, not as the primary treasury. Wise (Wise Canada Inc.), Payoneer, and Airwallex are FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) operating through partner chartered banks; they are not Schedule I banks, are not CDIC members, and their balances do not carry deposit insurance. Legally they may open CAD/USD virtual accounts for the ULC and provide local clearing in foreign markets (e.g., USD, EUR, GBP), but the core capital-escrow and liquidity-settlement function must remain at a Canadian chartered bank to satisfy the underwriters and corporate-governance expectations of the ULC. Use case: contractor payroll in emerging markets and optimization of Apple/Google payout receipts.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate (after 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction) plus 8% Alberta general corporate rate = 23% combined headline rate for a non-CCPC ULC.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Under the Canada–US DTA, reduced to 5% for a US recipient corporation that owns at least 10% of the ULC's voting shares and 15% for a US individual receiving dividend income. Treaty reductions under Canada-UK and Canada-Netherlands DTAs may also apply.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than 6 months after the ULC's fiscal year-end. Final tax balance due is 2 months after year-end for a non-CCPC (default for a ULC controlled by non-residents), or 3 months after year-end for a qualifying CCPC. No extension is available for late filing unless a CRA waiver on corporate dissolution or unreasonable hardship is approved.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA administrative contemporaneous documentation threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for non-arm's length transactions with a non-resident related party (aggregated by transaction category). Loan and financing transactions are documented from CAD 500,000. A complete Local File / Master File becomes mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 1,500,000,000 (CDN equivalent of €750M) under the Country-by-Country Reporting rules. Documentation must be produced and provided to CRA within 30 days of written demand.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose a standalone Economic Substances Regulation regime analogous to offshore jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245 of the Income Tax Act) and the Canada-US DTA Limitation on Benefits Article XXIX A require demonstrable business substance. A ULC must hold active employees, business premises, material capital at risk, and actual management decision-making in Canada to sustain treaty benefits and to avoid GAAR reassessment. CRA may disregard the separate legal personality of a shell ULC and allocate all income to the controlling shareholder where the corporation is a mere conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False T2 Return

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1): on summary conviction, a fine between 50% and 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine between 100% and 200% and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax, plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed, participated in, or acquiesced in the fraud are personally liable under criminal law, face fine and imprisonment, and may be subject to a travel ban under s.242(2) of the Income Tax Act. The corporate veil is pierced where the ULC is used as an alter ego; directors may be ordered to make personal restitution and disgorgement.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment, plus reassessment of omitted taxable income and daily interest. The penalty is not relievable where the taxpayer has not made reasonable efforts in documentation. Negative adjustments are prohibited for taxpayers that fail to provide adequate documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for withheld payroll/GST under s.227.1, but for pure transfer-pricing reassessment the liability remains at the corporate level. A director who knowingly enters into a pricing arrangement that strips profits without business rationale may be personally charged under criminal fraud provisions and may be subjected to travel restrictions.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under s.245 nullifying the entire tax benefit; denial of treaty benefits under Article XXIX A LOB; 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2); additional interest charges. In flagrant cases, the CRA may apply s.18(4) thin-capitalization rules to deny interest deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate conduit arrangements may be held personally liable for the resulting tax benefit; Canadian courts may lift the corporate veil and impose personal ownership of the ULC's income. A lifelong travel ban and seizure of personal assets are possible in fraudulent versions of the same scheme.
AML / Unlicensed Remittance

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA s.82: failure to register is a summary offence with corporate fines up to CAD 1 million. FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1 million per contravention. Where the payment rail is only Apple/Google IAP and the ULC does not interact with end users' funds, MSB registration is not required; any conversion of funds for a third party triggers registration.

Director personal liability: Under s.75 PCMLTFA, directors and officers are personally liable for the corporation's contravention if they knew or ought to have known of the matter and failed to exercise due care. If the unreported flows form part of a money laundering offense, directors face imprisonment and confiscation orders.
