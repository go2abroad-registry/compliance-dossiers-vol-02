Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portal: CRA My Business Account; registration via Business Registration Online (BRO). For Alberta corporate income tax, the CRA administers under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement; the Alberta provincial tax authority is Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta ULC must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST account and Canada Revenue Agency program accounts. Apply via CRA Business Registration Online or file Form RC1. Supporting documentation: certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, registered office information, director/officer identification, foreign entity registration documents, and proof of authority for the individual completing the application. If no Canadian director or physical presence, the CRA may require a representative or agent. No separate Alberta corporate tax registration because Alberta corporate income tax is collected federally, but the entity must file a provincial corporate tax return in respect of income allocated to Alberta.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign-owned Alberta ULC must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST account and Canada Revenue Agency program accounts. Apply via CRA Business Registration Online or file Form RC1. Supporting documentation: certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, registered office information, director/officer identification, foreign entity registration documents, and proof of authority for the individual completing the application. If no Canadian director or physical presence, the CRA may require a representative or agent. No separate Alberta corporate tax registration because Alberta corporate income tax is collected federally, but the entity must file a provincial corporate tax return in respect of income allocated to Alberta.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

An Alberta ULC is generally taxed as a corporation under the federal Income Tax Act; however, under subsection 34(2) of the Income Tax Act, a ULC may elect to be treated as a partnership for Canadian tax purposes if all members are resident in Canada or are certain non-resident corporations (per the statutory conditions). This election is available only to Canadian resident member corporations/partnerships, and is generally unavailable for a ULC with U.S. individual shareholders. For U.S. federal tax purposes, an Alberta ULC is a per se corporation under U.S. Treasury Regulations § 301.7701-2(b)(8)(i)(A), so it cannot make a check-the-box election to be disregarded. No other check-the-box mechanism exists under Canadian law. Hybrid mismatch rules (sections 18.4 and 18.5 of the Income Tax Act) may apply to deny deductions on payments giving rise to hybrid financial instrument or hybrid transfer benefits; no affirmative election prevents these rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; only supplies made in Canada count toward the threshold for non-residents carrying on business in Canada. A non-resident supplying digital services to Canadian customers must register if the threshold is met, unless the supply is zero-rated and the non-resident is not carrying on business in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate a supply of a service under Section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and is not present in Canada at the time the service is supplied, and the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. For software-based financial portfolio tracking services delivered over the cloud, zero-rating applies if the customer is a non-resident individual or unregistered non-resident business and the service does not relate to Canadian-situated property. The supplier must obtain documentary evidence of non-residency, including IP geolocation, billing address, payment card country, and customer attestation where available.

  • Customs export declaration (e.g., Canadian Export Reporting System/CERS) only if tangible goods are exported; for pure digital services, no customs export declaration is required
  • Commercial invoice or service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and Canadian supplier
  • Evidence of payment inflow, including SWIFT/MT103 or payment processor statements from Stripe/Apple
  • Non-resident status evidence: customer attestation, certificate of residence, or jurisdiction-specific tax residency certificate
  • IP address/geolocation logs and billing address evidencing the customer is outside Canada
  • GST/HST registration status confirmation that the recipient is not registered under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act

No real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime is currently mandated for GST/HST in Canada. E-invoicing is permitted and must satisfy CRA record-keeping requirements under the Excise Tax Act, including supplier name, GST/HST registration number, invoice date, description of supply, amount, and tax charged. Voluntary e-invoicing standards (e.g., Peppol) are accepted. No fiscal device or pre-approval is required.

The CRA may reassess within the normal four-year reassessment period (or longer in cases of misrepresentation or fraud) to deny zero-rating, requiring remittance of 5% GST on the revenue, plus interest and penalties. Penalties under sections 280 and 285 of the Excise Tax Act may apply where a registrant knowingly makes false statements: the amount can be up to 25% of the tax sought, plus fines and imprisonment on conviction. Input tax credits claimed on expenses attributable to the zero-rated supply may be clawed back if the zero-rating is overturned. The CRA scrutinizes cross-border digital services using payment processor data; failure to substantiate non-resident recipient status or failure to remit within the statutory period can result in assessment, collection actions, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized currency régime consistent with IMF Article VIII. Both current account payments (e.g., royalties, dividends, service fees) and capital account transfers (e.g., equity injections, loan repayments, derivative settlements) are freely convertible into CAD and outbound foreign currency without prior exchange-control approval. Profit remittance from an Alberta ULC is subject solely to Canadian taxation, primarily Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends (statutorily 25%, reduced under applicable income tax treaties) and, where applicable, branch tax or withholding on deemed receipts. No regulatory exchange-rate restriction or conversion limit applies to the repatriation of business profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose statutory export proceeds surrender timelines or mandatory conversion ratios. Receipts denominated in foreign currency may be retained, converted, or repatriated freely under the floating exchange rate régime. Reporting obligations apply only under FINTRAC for cross-border electronic fund transfers (EFTs) of CAD $10,000 or more within a 24-hour period, and under Statistics Canada’s International Transactions Reporting System for balance-of-payments purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canadian foreign exchange is market-determined under the Bank of Canada Act and Currency Act. Supervisory oversight for banks and FX dealers is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while transaction monitoring and cross-border reporting are enforced by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as deposit-taking banking entities under the Bank Act; they fall outside the Canadian regulatory perimeter and cannot provide insured or fully regulated clearing facilities for a ULC treasury. A foreign EMI may operate in relation to Canada only as an unregulated payment provider, creating material settlement risk, FX custody risk, and FINTRAC-reporting gaps. For strategic treasury purposes, such providers are acceptable only as ancillary collection channels for operating revenue; all liquidity, hedging, and capital movements must be executed through OSFI-regulated Schedule I banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan from a non-resident parent is executed via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the ULC’s Schedule I operating account. The remittance instruction must carry a permitted purpose code (e.g., 'G220 - Other investment in Canadian equity' for capital contributions, or 'G110 - Foreign direct investment in Canada - Equity' as applicable under Statistics Canada’s classification). Verification by the receiving bank will include the corporate resolution authorizing the transaction, signed subscription agreement, and a UBO certificate demonstrating that the source of funds is lawful. For amounts equal to or exceeding CAD $10,000, the bank must issue an EFT report to FINTRAC. All funds are received in CAD or USD and immediately credited to the ULC’s operational liquidity pool; no conversion restriction applies.Trade settlements for software royalties, external R&D services, and intercompany cost-sharing are cleared through SWIFT MT103 over the LVTS or the US-based Fedwire/CHIPS networks, depending on currency. For USD disbursements, the ULC should maintain a corresponding USD account at the Canadian bank for cheaper us-$ clearing. Commercial letters of credit are not required for intangible digital services; however, where goods-based hardware reselling exists, an irrevocable Documentary Credit may be opened at the financing bank. All outbound wires must include a compliant purpose code (e.g., '6810 - Software license fees', '4200 - Royalties', '2530 - Professional services'). FINTRAC EFT reporting thresholds and OFSI sanctions screening apply to beneficiary banks.
