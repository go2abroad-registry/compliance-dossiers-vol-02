Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC), with business registration portal 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO); certain provincial corporate registration and tax filings with Alberta Corporate Registry and Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration are coordinated through CRA.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishes an Alberta ULC via incorporation or extra-provincial registration with Alberta Corporate Registry. It must then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN), which serves as the statutory TIN, and any program accounts (RT for GST/HST, RC for payroll, RP for non-resident taxes, RD for customs). Applications are made through BRO or by filing Form RC1/RC186 with the CRA (or by using the Alberta Online Incorporation service which transmits initial BN data). Required supporting information includes the certificates of incorporation, registered office address, names of directors/officers, Canadian mailing address or representative, and estimated revenue; for non-residents, a valid non-resident corporation account may be opened and a section 150(2)(A) treaty waiver assessed where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No Canadian federal or Alberta election allows a ULC to be taxed as a partnership or sole proprietorship; it remains a corporation subject to Part I corporate tax and Alberta corporate income tax. Cross-border owners may however elect under U.S. check-the-box rules (IRS Form 8832) to treat the ULC as disregarded or partnership for U.S. tax, and the Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty's hybrid-entity provisions may modify treaty eligibility. Canada has no election to be treated as a tax-exempt zone entity or an Investment Trust. Hybrid mismatch elections are generally not available domestically.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (taxable supplies made in Canada, including 'specified supplies' of digital intangibles/services to Canadian recipients, in a current calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is met).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of an exported good is zero-rated under Part V Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, if the supplier maintains satisfactory evidence that the goods were exported. For intangible personal property like a fitness/health app or biometric tracker data, a supply to a non-resident person is zero-rated when the supply does not require the supplier to enjoy/consume the property in Canada, is not in connection with Canadian property/services, and is not made to a Canadian resident. The app's exportation of digital telemetry must be supported by contracts, platform download/use records, non-resident client evidence and payment flows. Pure digital subscriptions sold to non-residents may qualify for zero-rating when the place of supply is outside Canada.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System proof) or CBSA acknowledgment for goods
  • Commercial invoice and signed sales agreement / digital supply contract
  • Export bill of lading / air waybill / shipper's export declaration
  • Evidence of foreign payment and inward remittance certificate / bank SWIFT confirmation
  • Non-resident registration and place-of-supply evidence (IP address country logs, foreign billing address, app-store country storefront records)
  • CRA GST/HST zero-rated export substantiation schedule (internal reconciliation of invoices to export evidence)

Canada does not mandate real-time tax clearance, digital stamping, or CRA invoice pre-approval. Electronic invoices with digital signatures are recognized. Registered businesses must report through GST/HST NETFILE or EDI and retain full point-of-sale/API transaction logs. CRA online services provide the GST/HST program account remittance API and create/update digital registration information; non-resident digital suppliers use the simplified GST/HST registration portal.

CRA may reassess any zero-rated supply as taxable (GST/HST at 5% plus provincial component where applicable) if documentary proof is missing, payment does not originate from a non-resident, or the app is found to be used in Canada. Civil gross-negligence penalties of 50% of understated net tax can be imposed along with a false-statement penalty of 25% of the net tax evaded (minimum CAD 5,000), and criminal sanctions exist for intentional evasion. Retrospective reassessment generally covers four years from the filing date, but extends to ten years where there is misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible with an open capital account. Current-account items (subscription revenue from Apple IAP / Google Play, licensing fees, cloud compute outflows) are freely convertible without prior approval. Capital-account transactions (shareholder capital injections, intercompany loans, dividends, repatriation of proceeds) are unrestricted with respect to conversion and remittance, subject only to: (i) Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents (statutory 25%, generally reduced to 5%–15% under the Canada–US Tax Treaty for a corporate shareholder of an Alberta ULC); (ii) CRS/FATCA reporting; and (iii) FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) obligations for transactions exceeding CAD $10,000. No in-Canada retention ratio, no prior-exchange-approval requirement, and no profit-remittance licence apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory remittance timeline for export or digital-services revenue. Export proceeds and app-store settlement funds may be retained in any currency and any jurisdiction at the exporter's discretion. Compliance obligations are limited to: (i) FINTRAC reporting by the Canadian bank for incoming/outgoing EFTs of CAD $10,000 or more; (ii) record retention for six years under subsection 230(a) of the Canadian Income Tax Act; and (iii) transfer-pricing documentation under Section 247 of the Income Tax Act for transactions with non-resident related parties such as a US parent.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy and oversight are jointly administered through the Federal Department of Finance (policy lead), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI, prudential supervision of financial institutions), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC, AML/CFT transaction reporting), all operating under the Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated central foreign-exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, subject to strict conditions. Canada does not recognise European EMI licences; foreign digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) may be used only as ancillary payout rails and cannot serve as the primary clearing bank for a Canadian ULC. A Canadian-domiciled fintech provider must register with FINTRAC as a Money Service Business (MSB) and, if covered, as a Payment Service Provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada. Use of any foreign EMI requires that the ULC maintain its primary liquidity and capital-escrow accounts at a Schedule I domestic bank, and that the EMI's Canadian MSB registration be verified on the FINTRAC registry before onboarding.
