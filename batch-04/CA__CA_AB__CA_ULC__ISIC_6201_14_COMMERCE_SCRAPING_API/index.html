Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portal: CRA Business Registration Online and CRA My Business Account at canada.ca; Alberta incorporation portal: Alberta Corporate Registry.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation through the Alberta Corporate Registry; there is no minimum capital requirement and non-resident ownership is permitted. Step 2: Apply for a Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts. Step 3: The BN is the statutory Canadian tax identifier; it will normally be assigned with RT (GST/HST) and TP (corporate income tax) program accounts. Step 4: The AB ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation and must file T2 corporate income tax returns; if taxable supplies made in Canada exceed the small-supplier threshold, it must register, collect, and remit GST/HST using the RT account. Step 5: For foreign owners, no separate Canadian TIN is required; foreign ownership is disclosed on the T2 return and dividend payments may be subject to Canadian withholding tax. The commercial web scraping and proxy API gateway business requires no additional national tax or statutory licence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible for U.S. federal check-the-box purposes; however, for Canadian income tax purposes the AB ULC is an entity-level taxpayer, i.e. a taxable Canadian corporation, not a partnership or disregarded entity. Canada applies residence-based worldwide taxation, and the ULC's unlimited liability and non-resident ownership do not create CRA-recognised tax transparency.

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic check-the-box election. The AB ULC may elect for U.S. federal tax classification under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3 by filing IRS Form 8832 to be treated as a corporation, partnership, or disregarded entity; a single-member ULC may become disregarded for U.S. tax while remaining a Canadian taxpayer. No Canadian hybrid mismatch election is available; CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule and treaty anti-avoidance principles where hybrid arrangements are used. Under GST/HST there is no election to override the place-of-supply rules, although voluntary registration is permitted for small suppliers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; GST/HST registration is generally mandatory for a business whose total taxable supplies made in Canada, calculated in the current and immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, exceed CAD 30,000. A non-resident may also register voluntarily; once registered, GST at 5% applies to taxable supplies made in Canada unless the supply is zero-rated or exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A commercial scraping / proxy API service supplied to a non-resident customer is eligible for 0% GST if the supply is a service made to a non-resident person under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Data and API services delivered over the internet to foreign clients are generally zero-rated even if the service provider's servers are in Canada. Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated if sold to a non-resident and exported as soon as feasibly possible. The AB ULC may claim input tax credits and, where applicable, refunds for GST paid on Canadian inputs attributable to zero-rated exports.

  • Executed API subscription or SaaS agreement identifying the non-resident customer and describing the data/API service supplied
  • Commercial invoices on ULC letterhead showing the CRA Business Number, the words zero-rated export, and no GST charged
  • Evidence of non-resident status of the customer, such as foreign registration, foreign tax identification number, registered foreign address, and confirmation of no permanent establishment in Canada
  • Payment evidence: bank inward remittance, SWIFT confirmation, Stripe payout statement, or cryptocurrency transaction hash and conversion record
  • Delivery and usage logs evidencing API access and data destination endpoints outside Canada
  • Where physical goods are involved, a Canada Border Services Agency export declaration or B13A and a bill of lading
  • Where applicable, an inward remittance certificate or equivalent bank statement supporting satisfaction of the 180-day repatriation control

Not applicable as a real-time fiscal clearance regime: Canada does not require government invoice stamping, a clearance API, or mandatory e-invoicing for GST/HST. Invoices may be kept electronically under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act; the PEPPOL e-invoice network is voluntary. GST/HST returns are transmitted electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or certified tax preparation software, with no invoice-level API approval. The enterprise should maintain audit-proof transactional records for Stripe and cryptocurrency payment rails, including ledger records, foreign client identifiers, and zero-rating evidence.

CRA may reassess export zero-rating within the normal four-year reassessment period or later in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. If the exporter cannot substantiate a supply to a non-resident, or the commercial scraping API is found to be supplied for Canadian consumption, CRA can claw back the 0% rate and assess 5% GST on the full consideration, deny associated input tax credits, and charge daily compound interest. Gross-negligence and false-statement penalties may apply under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, and deliberate evasion is prosecutable under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act. Transfer-pricing and general anti-avoidance rules may reattribute income to the Canadian ULC where artificial offshore invoicing is used. The 180-day payment parameter is a compliance pipeline documentation control and does not override the substantive CRA evidentiary requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada accepts IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains full convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar is free-floating and there are no exchange-control restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation. Cross-border remittances remain subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (generally 25% absent a treaty, with appropriate treaty relief available), plus FINTRAC reporting for electronic funds transfers at or above CAD 10,000. CRS, FATCA, and AML/KYC requirements must be satisfied by the receiving bank, but there are no currency convertibility or registration hurdles for ordinary trade or investment flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion timeline, or minimum conversion ratio. Exporters may invoice in any currency, retain revenue in permitted foreign-currency accounts with an authorized financial institution, and convert to CAD at market rates. There is no statutory deadline for repatriation of export receipts. Reporting obligations are limited to general anti-money-laundering declarations (FINTRAC EFT reports for CAD 10,000 or above) and the usual CRA corporate income and foreign-asset reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority, together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank regulation and the Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate and international financial policy coordination.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium as a supplementary collection tool, Low as a primary banking rail. Foreign electronic-money institutions and fintech EMI platforms are not banks in Canada and cannot accept retail deposits except through an OSFI-regulated institution. An offshore EMI can be used for low-value receivable aggregation and FX conversion only where settlement balances remain in a Canadian Schedule I bank, the EMI is registered with FINTRAC as a money-services business or operates under a valid provincial MSB licence, and the platform can evidence sanctions screening and transaction audit trails. The Alberta ULC must not leave operational liquidity stored on a passive EMI ledger; all net funds must circulate through the principal Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder advances must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 from the investor’s bank to the ULC’s operational or capital-escrow account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The payment message should include the investor legal name, the ULC beneficiary name and account number, and a clear corporate-purpose reference such as 'ULC capital contribution / share subscription.' The receiving bank will require a board resolution, subscription agreement, share register update, and source-of-funds proof; wire values of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC. If the investor originates in USDC/crypto, funds must first be passed through a FINTRAC-registered exchange or licensed MSB that converts to CAD, or be received by the bank-approved VASP corridor; direct on-chain transfer to the bank account is generally not permissible.For external vendors, suppliers, and licensing partners, use SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for cross-border fiat settlements; use MT700/SWIFT commercial letters of credit only when underlying merchandise transactions require presentation of shipping and compliance documents. For CAD disbursements within Canada, settle via LVTS or EFT to an operational account with automated positive pay reconciliation using invoice references. USD payments from Canada may be cleared through CHIPS, Fedwire, or the Canadian bank’s US-dollar clearing network.
