Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC); shares may be issued for nominal or unpaid consideration and no escrow account is required. Liability exposure is governed by the unlimited liability characteristic: shareholders remain jointly and severally liable for the corporation's debts, liabilities, and obligations to the extent provided under the ABCA and the Articles.

Funding & unlock path: Occupancy permit and fire safety certification issued under the Alberta Safety Codes Act, including fire extinguisher placement and emergency exit compliance; structural load clearance for the leased office premises; workplace occupational health and safety clearance under the Alberta Occupational Health and Safety Act, RSA 2000, c O-2 and the OHS Code for computer-based office operations, including ergonomic and electrical safety standards.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC); shares may be issued for nominal or unpaid consideration and no escrow account is required. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; CRA My Business Account portal at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Alberta corporate income tax is collected by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain an Alberta corporate access number or extra-provincial registration through the Alberta Corporate Registries (CORES). It then applies to the CRA via Form RC1 or online My Business Account, providing incorporation documents, Canadian registered office or agent address, director particulars, and principal business activity. The CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with an RT (GST/HST) account and, where applicable, an RP payroll account. Non-residents must designate a Canadian representative for service of notice and satisfy CRA non-resident registration validation protocols.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

An Alberta ULC is by default a taxable Canadian corporation under the Income Tax Act (Canada) but is pass-through eligible: (i) subsection 96(2.1) Income Tax Act election may treat a foreign-owned ULC as fiscally transparent where the shareholder jurisdiction also treats it as transparent; (ii) US check-the-box election on Form 8832 may classify the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership; (iii) GST/HST closely related corporation election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act (Form GST25) taxes intra-group supplies at nil consideration; (iv) GST/HST group registration election under section 239 (Form GST10) consolidates filing; hybrid mismatch positions must be documented to prevent double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold measured by worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies over the preceding four calendar quarters; mandatory registration applies upon exceedance, elective below threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of SaaS or computer software to a non-resident who is not a GST/HST registrant is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (sections 7 and 23), provided the supply is not made to a resident of Canada, is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, and is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada. For PropTech real estate SaaS, the underlying property management or rental screening services must relate to real property located outside Canada for zero-rating to apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA export declaration, where physical goods are involved)
  • Bill of Lading or House Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign bank wire confirmation evidencing payment from the non-resident
  • Commercial invoice and signed SaaS subscription or software licensing agreement
  • Non-resident status and nil-registration declaration obtained from the recipient

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate. GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE or EDI where required by the CRA, and must retain books and records, including electronic records, in a prescribed machine-readable format. Alberta has no provincial e-invoicing regime; voluntary adoption of PEPPOL or other business software partner e-invoicing standards is permitted.

CRA can reassess and claw back ITCs and zero-rating refunds within the normal four-year reassessment period, with no limitation period where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a false statement or omission penalty (section 285(1)) of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated, a gross negligence penalty (section 285(2)) of the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the net tax understated, and compound interest under section 280. The exporter bears the burden of proving the non-resident status of the recipient, the foreign situs of the service, and receipt of funds within the documented period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized currency regime for both current and capital accounts. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible and there is no general foreign exchange control approval requirement for cross-border remittances, profit repatriation, dividend payments, or capital investment. The only residual statutory powers are emergency reserve powers under the Currency Act and repealed Foreign Exchange Control Regulations; none are currently active. Profit remittances from an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) to non-resident shareholders are subject to normal Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding and tax treaty considerations, but no FX conversion approval or central bank license is needed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender obligations and no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign currency receipts. Exporters and service providers such as PropTech/SaaS businesses may retain export revenues in foreign currency accounts or convert them at their discretion. For Canadian tax reporting under the Income Tax Act, the ULC must account for foreign exchange gains and losses pursuant to section 261 and maintain an appropriate functional currency election if substantial CAD reporting is not practical.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking oversight and FINTRAC for AML compliance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMI or fintech-only banking providers are not a legally independent substitute for a tier-1 Canadian bank account. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), any cross-border payment service operating into Canada must hold a Canadian Money Services Business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC or operate solely through a bank-registered agent. Offshore EMIs may be used as supplementary settlement rails (e.g., digital wallets for SaaS receipts), but all capital escrow, FX conversion, and trade settlement for the Alberta ULC must route through a Canadian scheduled bank or authorized provincial credit union. Direct integration with Stripe Billing and ACH Direct is viable as a payment acceptance adjunct to the primary bank clearing account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Unlimited liability structure combined with non-resident shareholders

Root cause: Unlimited liability structure combined with non-resident shareholders

Defense evidence: Provide Alberta Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, Articles of Incorporation, full beneficial ownership register, specimen resolution authorizing account opening, passport copies for all directors and UBOs, and explanatory disclosure of the ULC's pass-through eligibility.
Client/tenant deposit flows for a PropTech rental screening platform

Root cause: Client/tenant deposit flows for a PropTech rental screening platform

Defense evidence: Submit the institutional platform license exemption assessment, operational flow-of-funds diagram, master service agreement template, privacy and data protection policy demonstrating no borrowing, lending, or deposit-taking activity, and a lawyer's opinion confirming the entity does not constitute a bank or money services business under Canadian law.
Cross-border capital injection and intercompany disbursements from a related parent

Root cause: Cross-border capital injection and intercompany disbursements from a related parent

Defense evidence: Provide intercompany loan or share subscription agreement, board resolution supporting the capital contribution, audited or reviewed financial statements evidencing the funding's commercial purpose, tax residency certificates of parent entities, and a source-of-wealth statement covering the ultimate funding source.
High-risk foreign correspondent bank jurisdiction appearing in the payment chain

Root cause: High-risk foreign correspondent bank jurisdiction appearing in the payment chain

Defense evidence: Maintain an optimized routing map using only Canadian and OECD-listed correspondent banks, include a sanctions self-certification, provide copies of the ULC's own OFAC/CSIS screening policy, and produce the bank's electronic KYC prior notice record for any wire involving heightened-risk jurisdictions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfers into the Alberta ULC capital escrow account must use SWIFT MT103 with explicit narrative referencing the capital subscription or shareholder loan. Recommended ISO 20022/World Bank BOP purpose code: 'G11' (Direct Investment) or equivalent 'Debt settlement / intercompany loan' category for loan injections. The Canadian correspondent bank should attach a Certificate of Funds Origin and the ULC must file any required CRA Reporting of Unlisted Disposition or AML Suspicious Transaction Report if the amount exceeds CAD 10,000. Retain the corporate resolution, capital call schedule, and ULC shareholder register as trailing documentation to prove the commercial and non-money laundering purpose.Trade settlements for software licensing fees, rent screening vendor payments, and international subcontractors should be executed via SWIFT MT103 through the tier-1 Canadian bank with automated dual-signatory control. For high-value or recurring disbursements (threshold > CAD 250,000), a Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP600 (SWIFT MT700) may be issued, supported by standby LC in favour of the ULC.
