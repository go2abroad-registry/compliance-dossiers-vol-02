Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) portal; provincial registry: Alberta Corporate Registry (Alberta King's Printer), accessible via ALIS or third-party service providers.

E-government filing portal

For an Alberta ULC, incorporate through the Alberta Corporate Registry (or Corporations Canada if federal extra-provincial registration is desired) to obtain a legal entity certificate. Subsequently, the entity must register with the CRA through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (or by paper form RC1) to obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN), a Corporate Income Tax account (RT), and a GST/HST account (RT). The BN is issued immediately upon successful online application; paper applications typically take 2–4 weeks. Non-resident directors or officers must provide a Canadian address for service and certified identification. Once incorporated, the ULC has a separate legal personality and is a resident of Canada for tax purposes; therefore, the BN number is the statutory TIN for all federal tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (For Canadian income tax purposes, the ULC is an entity-level taxable corporation subject to Part I corporate income tax; however, due to its unlimited liability character, it may be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership for US federal tax purposes if a check-the-box election is made. The entity structure is Pass_Through_Eligible only in the cross-border sense, not under domestic Canadian rules).

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under Canadian domestic law. The entity’s classification for Canadian tax is determined solely by corporate legal form. For US federal tax purposes, the ULC may file IRS Form 8832 to elect to be classified as a corporation, partnership, or disregarded entity. Canada also provides a section 216 ITA election for non-residents on Canadian rental income, and GST/HST elections for closely related corporations (e.g., section 156 election), but these do not alter the entity’s tax classification for income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 (annual gross worldwide revenue of a small supplier from taxable supplies made in Canada; registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, regardless of whether the supply is a digital service or goods).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of services are zero-rated under Section 9, Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act where the recipient is a non-resident of Canada and the supply is not in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property, and is not a service performed on or in relation to an individual in Canada. For the fleet route optimization and real-time telematics SaaS, the supply of cloud-based software services to foreign logistics operators (non-resident customers) qualifies for 0% GST/HST provided the customer is not a Canadian resident, the service is rendered outside Canada, and no basis exists for zero-rating denial under the place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (not applicable to pure services; required only if the supply includes physical goods – e.g., telematics hardware)
  • Bill of Lading (for goods shipments; for SaaS, a service agreement and server access log is the equivalent evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or SWIFT/bank wire receipt from a non-resident account)
  • Written service agreement demonstrating non-resident customer status and offshore performance
  • Commercial invoice showing 0% GST/HST, referencing Section 9 of Schedule VI
  • Telematics platform usage logs or IP geolocation records evidencing the customer’s foreign location

No government-mandated real-time fiscal clearance, API-based clearance, or digital stamping system exists in Canada. CRA accepts standard electronic invoices (B2B/B2C) provided they contain the prescribed information (supplier name and BN, invoice date, description of supply, GST/HST charged, and consideration). Registrants must retain invoices in an auditable format (e.g., PDF, CSV) and report GST/HST on a periodic return; there is no statutory requirement for a third-party digital clearinghouse.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export within the normal four-year limitation period (or longer for misrepresentation) and require payment of any underpaid GST/HST plus interest and penalties. Under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act, penalties can reach up to 8% of the tax amount, and for willful or careless misstatements, penalties up to $25,000 and potential criminal charges under section 327 (including imprisonment). Refund claims lacking proper substantiation are denied and clawed back with retroactive interest. CRA’s audit focus includes cross-border digital service transactions; funds repatriated outside the 180-day window are scrutinized as indicators of no genuine export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Canada abolished exchange controls upon repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1952; the Canadian dollar trades freely without fixed parity, and there are no capital-account or current-account restrictions on CAD conversion, profit repatriation, service royalties, interest, or intercompany loan repayment. The Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation may open and operate CAD, USD, EUR, and other foreign-currency accounts without Bank of Canada approval. Practical remittance frictions are tax-led rather than FX-led: Part XIII withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5-15% under the Canada-U.S. Convention); Part XIV branch tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5% on the first CAD 500,000 of taxable income) where a U.S. shareholder treats the ULC as a disregarded entity and the ULC carries on business in Canada; and CRS/FATCA reporting. No convertibility certificate, foreign-investment exchange approval, or capital-import permit exists for a software export business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export receipts; the ULC may retain export proceeds in CAD or foreign currency and may net them against payables in any currency. Residual obligations are statutory reporting only: (i) disclosure to the Canada Border Services Agency of any cross-border movement of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more under section 12 of the PCMLTFA and the Cross-border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting Regulations; (ii) bank-side FINTRAC reporting of electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and large cash transactions; and (iii) CRA record retention proving the export character of software subscription income. Because fleet route optimization and telematics SaaS (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 488490) constitute services rather than physical goods, Customs Act export declarations and Export Control List licensing do not apply to the software.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Foreign-exchange policy and reserve administration are vested in the Minister of Finance and executed through the Exchange Fund Account, for which the Bank of Canada acts as fiscal agent. Prudential regulation of Schedule I banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46). Anti-money-laundering supervision, large-transaction reporting, and client due diligence are enforced by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). International economic sanctions are administered by Global Affairs Canada and enforced by the Canada Border Services Agency and the RCMP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional Medium as a secondary payments and FX collection rail; Not Viable as the primary treasury bank. Canada has no European-style EMI licence; cross-border digital payment providers operating into Canada must be registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and must satisfy provincial money-services requirements where applicable. Such entities (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) may lawfully process cross-border collections, vendor disbursements, and currency conversion for the ULC, but they may not accept deposits in the bank-supervisory sense, are not members of Payments Canada, are not covered by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation, and cannot process payroll, GST/HST remittances, or CRA tax payments. The blueprint requires a Schedule I chartered-bank primary account with fintech rails layered on for collection efficiency and FX segmentation.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier
