Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Alberta Corporate Registry for provincial incorporation registration.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (ULC), file the initial return with the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain the Alberta incorporation number; the corporation must then apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, simultaneously requesting (a) the GST/HST program account (RT), (b) the corporate income tax program account (RC), and (c) the payroll program account (RP) if the ULC has Canadian employees. A non-resident-owned ULC should designate a Canadian resident representative for service and complete the CRA non-resident ownership records. CRA issues the BN immediately online; the RT account must be opened before the first taxable supply, subject to the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For Canadian federal and Alberta income tax purposes, a ULC is a domestic corporation taxed at the entity level (ITA s. 89(1)); however, the ULC is eligible for U.S. check-the-box classification as a disregarded entity/partnership, while CRA continues to treat the ULC as a taxable Canadian corporation, creating a hybrid entity structure (CRA administrative position; ITA s. 18.4(3) hybrid mismatch rules apply to foreign branch results). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — For Canadian federal and Alberta income tax purposes, a ULC is a domestic corporation taxed at the entity level (ITA s. 89(1)); however, the ULC is eligible for U.S. check-the-box classification as a disregarded entity/partnership, while CRA continues to treat the ULC as a taxable Canadian corporation, creating a hybrid entity structure (CRA administrative position; ITA s. 18.4(3) hybrid mismatch rules apply to foreign branch results). Alberta does not impose a separate provincial corporate income tax administration; the provincial rate of 8% is assessed federally under the Tax Collection Agreement, resulting in a combined Alberta general corporate rate of 23% (15% federal + 8% Alberta).

Available election forms

(i) U.S. Treasury Reg. §301.7701-3 check-the-box election to classify the ULC as a corporation or as a disregarded entity/partnership for U.S. federal tax purposes; (ii) ITA s. 216(4) net-basis election for non-resident recipients of Canadian-source rents and timber royalties; (iii) ITA s. 219 branch tax election (inapplicable to a Canadian-incorporated ULC but relevant to its Canadian branch of foreign operations); (iv) ETA s. 225(2)/s. 232 election to treat closely related corporations as a single person for GST/HST purposes; (v) GST/HST Quick Method election for eligible small suppliers; (vi) CRA election to file consolidated GST/HST returns for associated entities; (vii) foreign affiliate and underlying foreign affiliate acknowledgment filings where the ULC holds controlled foreign subsidiaries.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA s. 148(1): a ULC must register for GST/HST once its worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter, or exceed CAD 30,000 in the last four consecutive calendar quarters with supplies arising in more than one quarter. EMR/SaaS licenses supplied to Canadian medical clinics are taxable at 5% GST in Alberta; supplies to non-registered non-residents qualify for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 21.1 (supply of intangible personal property, including software licenses and SaaS) where the ULC supplies EMR software or related services to a non-resident recipient who (a) is not registered for GST/HST, (b) is not acquiring the supply for consumption, use, or supply in Canada, and (c) the supply is not excluded by s. 21.1(2) for online 'made available' software where the recipient is in Canada. Services directly related to exported goods or provided to non-residents (Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 7 and 23) are zero-rated where the export test is satisfied. Refund mechanics: input tax credits (ITCs) for GST paid on Canadian inputs are claimed on the GST/HST return; a non-resident purchaser of Canadian services or intangibles may claim a rebate under ETA s. 252 using Form GST106 within two years of the supply, achieving 100% recovery where the property is acquired for export and not for use in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A / Canadian Export Declaration) — required only for physical goods exports; digital SaaS exports are evidenced by supply contracts, electronic delivery logs, and server access records as a substitute for customs declarations.
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill evidencing physical movement of goods — maintained where exported hardware or EMR appliances accompany the software; for pure SaaS delivery, service delivery logs, IP address records, and cloud infrastructure attestations substantiate the cross-border supply.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign exchange/remittance record from a Canadian chartered bank, or SWIFT MT103 / FX settlement confirmation) evidencing payment from the non-resident recipient into the ULC's Canadian bank account within the documentation window.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada (no CFE-style mandate). E-invoicing is voluntary via the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL network. Mandatory requirements: (a) GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or Business Gateway API (XML) and monthly filing is mandated once taxable revenue exceeds CAD 1.5M; (b) invoices must show the supplier's BN, the date, and the GST/HST registration number and must be issued within 15 days of the supply; (c) all books and records must be retained for six years in English or French; (d) the simplified GST/HST regime for non-resident digital platform operators (ETA Part IX, s. 179.1) applies to the ULC only if it operates a platform; direct B2B EMR SaaS licensing remains under the ordinary GST/HST rules with place-of-supply rules under ETA s. 142.1.

CRA export zero-rating audits focus on the non-resident status of the recipient, the place of use or consumption of the software, and payment provenance. Legal consequences of false or unsupported export zero-rating claims include: (a) ETA s. 285.1(1) penalty of 25% of the net tax understated for false statements; (b) ITA s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; (c) criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1) and ETA s. 327.2 with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment; (d) retrospective clawback of input tax credits with compounding daily interest under ETA s. 280 and ITA s. 161; (e) extension of the normal four-year reassessment window (ETA s. 298; ITA s. 152(4)) to any period in the case of misrepresentation or fraud; (f) administrative penalties of up to CAD 25,000 under ETA s. 285.1(1.3) for third-party promoters of false export claims; and (g) for a ULC, the unlimited liability of shareholders does not shield foreign owners from CRA collection actions following a zero-rating clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has no exchange-control legislation; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts, consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations, and no capital-inflow or profit-repatriation licensing is required. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends (statutory 25%; treaty-reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US treaty). ULC-specific watch items: Alberta ULCs are flow-through eligible; deemed-dividend rules under ITA ss. 84(2)/(3) apply on paid-up-capital reductions and wind-up; and a hybrid-mismatch/check-the-box review is mandatory for any US shareholder of the ULC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation, statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada. Regulatory compliance obligations instead consist of FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer (EFT) reporting on all cross-border wires of CAD 10,000 or more, FATCA/CRS account due diligence, and maintenance of the Alberta corporate beneficial-ownership register for the ULC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision and cross-border transfer surveillance by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); payment-system oversight by Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium - ancillary wallets only. Legal constraints: (1) a non-resident electronic-money institution or fintech soliciting or serving Canadian-domiciled clients must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and comply with PCMLTFA record-keeping and client-identification requirements; (2) most offshore EMIs are not Payments Canada members, so any CAD EFT/ACH settlement ultimately requires a Schedule I bank sponsor; (3) foreign EMIs do not provide CDIC deposit coverage or OSFI-regulated custody, making them unsuitable for the ULC capital escrow function; (4) the ULC must not concentrate operating liquidity at an unregulated EMI. Recommended use: low-balance disbursement wallets for contractor payouts and cross-border USD card spend.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident investors wire CAD or USD directly to the ULC-named capital escrow account at a Schedule I bank using SWIFT MT103. Field 50: remitter name and tax-ID; field 59: ULC legal name and account; purpose/reference field: 'INV-4 - equity injection for direct investment' (Bank of Canada S000 investment class). US-origin wires route via Fedwire to the Canadian bank's US correspondent (JP Morgan for RBC/TD, BMO Harris for BMO, CIBC US for CIBC) and are then booked in CAD via Lynx or the Real-Time Rail. All wires of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC EFT reporting by the receiving bank, so the ULC must be able to evidence board authorization, the share-subscription agreement, and the non-loan treatment of the payment. If the ULC has made an Article XXIX-B check-the-box election for US federal tax purposes, the bank will also require a valid IRS W-8BEN-E and FATCA certification before booking the inward credit.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for international vendor licensing and USD payments with field 70 purpose code 'PURCHASE OF SOFTWARE LICENSE/FEES'; (2) Payments Canada Lynx (formerly LVTS) for CAD high-value transfers exceeding CAD 100,000; (3) Real-Time Rail and EFT/ACH for CAD operating disbursements below CAD 100,000; (4) commercial letters of credit (UCP600, MT700) reserved for occasional hardware or data-centre equipment imports - not expected for a pure SaaS subscription model; (5) cross-border payroll via ACH or SEPA when contracting Canadian and US developers. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium - ancillary wallets only. Legal constraints: (1) a non-resident electronic-money institution or fintech soliciting or serving Canadian-domiciled clients must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and comply with PCMLTFA record-keeping and client-identification requirements; (2) most offshore EMIs are not Payments Canada members, so any CAD EFT/ACH settlement ultimately requires a Schedule I bank sponsor; (3) foreign EMIs do not provide CDIC deposit coverage or OSFI-regulated custody, making them unsuitable for the ULC capital escrow function; (4) the ULC must not concentrate operating liquidity at an unregulated EMI. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods, CAD 500,000 for services, CAD 250,000 for interest, royalties, and other transactions with non-residents

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form doctrine. For treaty benefits, the beneficial ownership test and economic substance are required; a ULC must demonstrate real business activity, employees, and decision-making in Canada to avoid treaty denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239) with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties of 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under s. 227.1 if they fail to withhold or remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment for fraud.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000) if documentation is inadequate, plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the mispricing; potential criminal charges for tax evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reassessment of income, and application of GAAR with penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; potential dissolution of the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if they authorized the sham structure; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal charges for money laundering under the Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned for up to 10 years if they knowingly facilitate money laundering; civil forfeiture of assets.
