Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for an Alberta ULC under the ABCA or related regulations. No escrow account or capital deposit rule applies; the initial subscription may validly be a nominal CAD 1.00 paid-up share allotment. Shareholder unlimited liability provides the creditor protection mechanism.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada and its GST/HST registration and filing portal, including the Business Registration Online (BRO) service and My Business Account.

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ALC/ULC), the corporation must first complete provincial incorporation filings with Alberta Corporate Registry. The CRA Business Number (BN) is then procured by filing Form RC1 Request for a Business Number, or through CRA Business Registration Online, specifying the required GST/HST account. A ULC may use a Canadian registered agent or office address; non-resident officers and directors are permitted, but the corporation must provide a Canadian address for service. CRA generally issues a 9-digit Business Number plus a 2-letter program account suffix (e.g., RT for GST/HST) within 1 to 15 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a newly incorporated Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ALC/ULC), the corporation must first complete provincial incorporation filings with Alberta Corporate Registry. The CRA Business Number (BN) is then procured by filing Form RC1 Request for a Business Number, or through CRA Business Registration Online, specifying the required GST/HST account. A ULC may use a Canadian registered agent or office address; non-resident officers and directors are permitted, but the corporation must provide a Canadian address for service. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian federal income tax does not provide a domestic 'check-the-box' election. For Alberta ULCs, tax classification for Canadian purposes remains a taxable Canadian corporation subject to Part I income tax. However, when an Alberta ULC is owned by a U.S. parent, it may be elected under U.S. Treasury Regulation § 301.7701-3 to be taxed as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. federal income tax purposes. Canada does not recognize this U.S. check-the-box classification for Canadian tax purposes. Canadian hybrid mismatch rules apply to arrangements involving hybrid entities under Income Tax Act ss. 18.4 and 18.5, as well as to the payment of dividends in respect of hybrid securities. A ULC can also make corporate elections in prescribed form for tax purposes, including section 216 elections for non-resident landlords and election under subsection 146(1) for certain retirement arrangements if applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — The small supplier threshold. A non-resident or domestic person becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant once worldwide taxable supplies (revenue from carrying on commercial activity in Canada) exceed CAD 30,000 in either a single calendar quarter or in the four most recent consecutive calendar quarters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment under the Excise Tax Act applies to: (1) goods supplied by way of sale and physically delivered directly to a destination outside Canada by the supplier or the purchaser, or shipped or delivered out of Canada in the prescribed manner; (2) services supplied to a non-resident person, provided the service is not, in whole or in part, performed in relation to real or tangible personal property located in Canada at the time the service is performed, and is not made in connection with a transaction in respect of which the recipient is an agent or in respect of goods acquired in Canada by a person who is not a non-resident at the time of acquisition. For multi-channel helpdesk SaaS and conversational bot cloud services supplied to non-resident clients, the service is zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not Canadian-use-related, provided the supplier does not satisfy the 'significant assistance' test. The supplier must maintain adequate documentation evidencing exportation and non-resident status of the recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or tax authority digital stamping/sealing of invoices. There is no mandatory national e-invoice clearance platform for B2B invoices. CRA permits electronic invoices provided the invoice contains all prescribed information and complete records are retained. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or authorized third-party software; large registrants are required to use electronic filing. Alberta has no provincial digital transaction control system.

CRA may reassess exported supplies to taxable status where documentary requirements are not satisfied, and may deny zero-rating retroactively. Penalties for false statements or omissions under the Excise Tax Act include gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the net tax adjustment, plus arrears interest; criminal penalties apply where fraudulent intent is proven. For income tax, unremitted or repatriated export proceeds are not directly time-limited, but the standard 180-day deadline operating assumption in this dossier reflects the tax-regime substantiation standard. CRA audit scrutiny of zero-rated export claims focuses on proof of physical export, non-resident recipient status, actual provision of the service, and adequate ledger records. Any default or clawback may also trigger reassessment within the normal four-year limitation period, extended to seven years or indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, meaning there are no exchange controls, surrender requirements, or mandatory conversion ratios imposed on current account software subscription revenues or capital account transactions; the Alberta ULC may remit dividends, interest, royalties, and service fees freely through Canadian clearing; the only practical profit-remittance hurdles are (i) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposing a 25% statutory non-resident withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties, reducible by treaty (dividends usually 5-15%; interest 0-15% depending on treaty and arm's-length status), (ii) Part I corporate income tax on ULC earnings at federal and Alberta provincial rates with no distribution requirement, (iii) FINTRAC wire-reporting thresholds of CAD 10,000, and (iv) entity-classification mismatches between Canada and the home jurisdiction when a ULC is treated as a deemed corporation for Canadian tax purposes but transparent in the shareholder's jurisdiction; convertibility is unaffected by these tax and reporting obligations; non-resident ownership of a ULC is permitted without exchange control approval, though a non-resident controlling investment may trigger notification or review thresholds under the Investment Canada Act if enterprise value thresholds are crossed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no forced conversion ratio for export receivables, including ISIC 6201 cross-border SaaS and customer-support helpdesk services; export earnings may be retained in any currency in domestic or offshore accounts indefinitely; only non-surrender obligations exist: FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports for cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, statutory record-keeping for international transactions, Statistics Canada balance-of-payments survey reporting for firms above reporting thresholds, and CRA functional-currency reporting rules under subsection 261(2) if the books are kept in a foreign currency; no Central Bank-approved exporter certificate, forex repatriation certificate, or exchange-rate conversion procedure applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential supervisor of Schedule I banks, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF regulator, and the Department of Finance Canada as policy authority; Canada has no separate Foreign Exchange Administration or exchange-control licensing bureau; FX policy is implemented through the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and applicable sanctions legislation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a peripheral treasury node; LOW as the primary bank. A Canadian fintech, payment service provider, or digital bank (e.g., an entity registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada and as a money services business with FINTRAC, such as Wise Business or a processor-integrated banking partner) can legally support an Alberta ULC for FX conversion, foreign contractor payouts, and cross-border disbursements; however, offshore digital EMIs holding Canadian money are not banks and cannot provide CDIC-insured CAD deposits, cannot access Payments Canada clearing directly, and may be viewed as high-risk by correspondent banks. The ULC treasury blueprint must therefore maintain a Tier-1 Canadian bank as the master operating and escrow account, and use fintech/EMI rails only for targeted outbound payments and FX execution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods transactions; CAD 500,000 for services transactions (per CRA guidance for non-arm's length cross-border transactions).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the 'sham' doctrine apply. A ULC must demonstrate genuine business purpose and economic substance to avoid reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the tax evaded (gross negligence penalty) plus interest; criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act can lead to fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they failed to withhold or remit (director's liability under Section 227.1). Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for criminal tax evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under GAAR, deny treaty benefits, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax benefit (plus interest).

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in a sham arrangement; potential for civil fraud claims and travel bans.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations; criminal penalties for money laundering under the Criminal Code (up to 10 years imprisonment).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly failed to comply; travel bans and asset freezes possible under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.
