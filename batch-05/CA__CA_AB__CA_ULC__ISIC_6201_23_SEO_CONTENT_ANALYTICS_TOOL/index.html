Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual offices with a physical street address in the Province of Alberta (e.g., Calgary or Edmonton) are generally acceptable providing the registered address is a real, physical street address where the registered office is capable of being attended during business hours and not a mere post office box. However, for an unlimited liability corporation engaged in MarTech SaaS with no local employees, the Alberta Business Corporations Act requires a registered office in Alberta. Virtual office services that offer mail forwarding and telephone answering are acceptable for compliance filing purposes, but the ULC must exercise actual commercial substance through its board of directors (which may be incorporated virtually) and through the maintenance of books and records at a physical location accessible to the Registry. The Canada Revenue Agency ('CRA') may challenge virtual-only substance for HST/GST accounts or transfer pricing purposes if the ULC has active foreign shareholders; therefore a documented local director or agent is mandatory.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - National portal: Canada.ca/Canada Revenue Agency Business Registration Online (CRA My Business Account)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident ULC carrying on business in Alberta or earning Canadian-source income must register for the CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account via the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or by completing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts). The ULC must first incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act or equivalent and obtain provincial incorporation documents. For GST/HST, registration is mandatory if the ULC makes taxable supplies in Canada and worldwide taxable revenue exceeds CAD 30,000, or voluntarily to claim input tax credits. The CRA will issue a 9-digit BN plus an RW account (GST/HST) and possibly an RC (payroll) account if employees are resident in Canada. Corporate income tax account (RT) is required for filing T2; however, a ULC is typically treated as a disregarded entity for Canadian income tax purposes if it elects under section 216 of the Income Tax Act, and the shareholder (often a US person) must file a Canadian T2 return on behalf of the ULC. The TIN/GST/HST registration can be completed electronically by an authorized representative with a CRA My Business Account credential.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident ULC carrying on business in Alberta or earning Canadian-source income must register for the CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account via the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or by completing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts). The ULC must first incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act or equivalent and obtain provincial incorporation documents. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada), a ULC may elect to be treated as a corporation for Canadian income tax purposes (section 216 election), which overrides the default look-through treatment and creates a corporate tax entity. Alternatively, absent the election, the ULC is fiscally transparent and the shareholder is directly taxable on Canadian-source income, similar to a partnership for tax purposes. Also available: check-the-box election under US Treasury Regulations section 301.7701-3 for US federal tax purposes, but for Canadian tax the section 216 election is the applicable statutory mechanism. Hybrid mismatch election rules under the Income Tax Act (including section 128.2 and Part VI.1) may apply to avoid double non-taxation where the ULC is treated as transparent in one jurisdiction and opaque in another. Alberta corporate tax registration with Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) via Corporate Tax Program is also required for the ULC if it is not a section 216 elected corporation only in limited circumstances; provincial allocation applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) in any single calendar quarter, or within the previous four consecutive calendar quarters; small supplier threshold. For non-resident ULCs carrying on business in Canada, the same threshold applies. Voluntarily registerable if carrying on a commercial activity in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For a ULC supplying SEO data crawler and keyword analytics tool as a SaaS/MarTech analytics service to non-resident clients, the supply is an 'exported supply of intangible personal property' that may be zero-rated under section 23 (exported services) or schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act. The service must be supplied to a non-resident that is not registered for GST/HST, and the service must be performed for the non-resident's benefit; services that relate to real property in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or an individual present in Canada are excluded from zero-rating. Because the customer is a non-resident and consumes the SEO analytics tool outside Canada, the supply qualifies for zero-rating if the recipient has no GST/HST registration and the IP/service is not delivered to a Canadian location. For cloud-based SaaS, zero-rating is generally available under the 'exported service' rules in section 23 of the Excise Tax Act, provided the ULC keeps evidence of the non-resident status of the recipient. Where payment Rails Stripe/Paddle act as merchant of record and remit VAT, the ULC must still validate the underlying evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CUS-XXX non-commercial): not applicable for intangible SaaS; if goods embedded in physical media exported: Canada Customs Coding Form B13A / Automated Commercial Declaration (ACD) via CBSA
  • CRA-Form-issued detailed commercial invoice showing 'Zero-rated supply - non-resident recipient' and the GST/HST number (RW) if required
  • Non-resident status evidence: name, address, jurisdiction, and GST/HST registration status of the purchaser (e.g., vendor's own AI-generated attestation from Stripe/Paddle or a valid W-8 or equivalent statement)
  • Bill of Lading or delivery confirmation (only for physical goods; for SaaS use the server-based delivery log and user access records)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT confirmation of payment received from foreign buyer in foreign currency (CAD equivalent conversion)

Canada does not mandate a government-approved real-time e-invoicing clearance system analogous to Mexico or Italy. However, the CRA requires that all GST/HST registrants issue sales invoices containing prescribed information (vendor name, BN, date, description, tax amount). Electronic invoices and records are acceptable if readable and retainable. For digital service/reporting and analytics tool revenues, the CRA applies the 'place of supply' rules for GST/HST; the supply of intangible personal property (SaaS) is deemed made in Canada if the recipient is a Canadian-resident individual or the business address is in Canada. There is no requirement for digital stamping or API-based fiscal clearance. However, the CRA has implemented the 'GST/HST Electronic Filing and Remittance' mandate: registrants with taxable supplies over CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE (or EDI via My Business Account). The ULC must also file an annual information return for non-resident distributors? No, that is Quebec QST specifics. Alberta has no provincial sales tax, so only the 5% federal GST applies; no Quebec QST or provincial PST applies in Alberta. The CRA GST/HST return period is monthly or annually depending on threshold, and the ULC must use the CRA My Payment service to remit.

Under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, the CRA may reassess within 4 years (or later in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud). For fraudulent zero-rating, the ULC and its directors are jointly and severally liable for uncollected GST/HST, interest, and penalties. Penalties under section 280 (gross negligence) of up to 25% of the amount of tax that was misstated, and criminal prosecution under section 327 for tax evasion (fine up to CAD 200,000 plus imprisonment for up to 2 years). Retrospective clawback of input tax credits claimed on inputs used to make zero-rated supplies may apply if the zero-rating is disallowed, and intercompany clawback under section 216(4) if the ULC's election is invalid; also, if the CRA invokes the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, the tax benefits from the ULC structure (including refunds or nil net tax) may be denied and penalties of 25% of the benefit and 50% of the benefit if not disclosed and misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both the current and capital accounts; Canada accepted IMF Article VIII obligations in 1951 and operates with no foreign-exchange permits, approval queues, or post-transaction capital controls. Profit remittance for an Alberta ULC is unrestricted: dividends, share redemptions, and intercompany repayments may be wired abroad without prior approval, subject only to (i) Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory; 5% under Article X(2) of the Canada-US Tax Treaty when the US parent owns at least 10% of the ULC's voting stock, with Article XXIX(8) confirming the ULC is not fiscally transparent), (ii) FINTRAC EFTR/STR reporting for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more (or equivalent), and (iii) standard documentary CDD. There are no capital-export quotas, minimum holding periods, or forced-repatriation constraints.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation quota on export proceeds for ISIC 6201 / NAICS 541511 software and SEO analytics services. The ULC may retain USD or EUR settlement funds offshore indefinitely, or in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian banks, without exchange-control permission. The only compliance hooks are FINTRAC cross-border currency reporting (Form E667 for physical cash over CAD 10,000), EFTR electronic transfer reporting (CAD 10,000 threshold), and ordinary corporate income recognition of accrued export receivables. Export Development Canada (EDC) facilitation is optional, not statutory.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act, is the central monetary and currency-issuing authority; the Minister of Finance retains residual powers over foreign exchange administration under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52). Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration or exchange-control bureau. FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administers the operative cross-border control regime under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) prudentially regulates the federally incorporated Schedule I banks that execute CAD cash and FX operations.
