Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC) - the sole federal tax administrator for GST/HST and federal corporate income tax, operating via Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MyBA), and GST/HST NETFILE/e-FILE portals. Alberta corporate income tax is also administered by the CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement; Alberta Treasury Board and Finance sets provincial rates only. Entity incorporation is registered with the Alberta Corporate Registry (Alberta Corporate System).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the ULC under the Alberta Business Corporations Act via the Alberta Corporate Registry; the corporate name must include 'Unlimited Liability Company' or the abbreviation 'ULC'. Step 2: Upon incorporation, the Corporate Registry transmits data to the CRA, which automatically issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a federal corporate income tax program account (program code RC); verify or update the BN through BRO or MyBA. Step 3: Register a separate GST/HST program account (program code RT) with the CRA once taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters, or elect voluntary registration earlier to recover input tax credits. Step 4: Open a payroll deductions program account (program code RP) if employees will be hired. Step 5: Enroll for electronic services via MyBA/Represent a Client, including GST/HST NETFILE and pre-authorized debit, because GST/HST payments above CAD 10,000 must be remitted electronically. Non-resident shareholders must provide the registered office with a Canadian agent for service; the foreign parent LLC itself is not required to register unless it carries on business in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Default: Entity_Level_Taxation - an Alberta ULC is a corporation under s.248(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada), taxable on worldwide income at the 15% federal general rate plus Alberta's 8% provincial general corporate rate (2% Alberta small-business rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income if eligible). Pass_Through_Eligible - a Canadian-resident ULC may elect under s.96(1) ITA to be deemed a partnership for income tax purposes, allowing income, losses, and credits to flow through to shareholders, including non-resident US LLC members; if elected, the ULC is generally still treated as a corporation for Part IX GST/HST unless a parallel s.273 Excise Tax Act election is made. Territorial_Scope: Canada taxes resident corporations on worldwide income; non-resident shareholders of an unelected ULC face Part XIII withholding on deemed dividends arising from shareholder benefits and s.15(1) or s.212(13.1) recharacterization in cross-border finance structures. No Tax_Exempt_Zone status is available in Alberta.

Available election forms

(i) Subsection 96(1) ITA election for a ULC to be treated as a partnership - the functional Canadian equivalent of a check-the-box pass-through election; filed with the CRA using the prescribed prescribed election form and all shareholder consents. (ii) Excise Tax Act s.273 election to align the ULC's GST/HST treatment with its partnership characterization for income tax. (iii) Hybrid mismatch integrity rules under ITA s.18.2 to s.18.4 may recharacterize payments made under hybrid arrangements, including a ULC with US LLC shareholders, and s.17(1) ITA imposes imputed interest on outstanding balances owed by non-resident related parties unless settled within approximately 180 days.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small-supplier threshold measured over any four consecutive calendar quarters and over any single calendar quarter. A supplier whose taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 ceases to be a small supplier and must register for GST/HST without delay. A ULC may voluntarily register below this threshold to claim input tax credits. Additional registration rules apply only to non-resident digital-economy suppliers of B2C services; the ULC exporting B2B anti-cheat middleware remains under the ordinary threshold regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The anti-cheat middleware qualifies for 0% GST/HST zero-rating under three paths of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: (a) supplies of goods actually exported by the supplier under s.1 - generally inapplicable to cloud-delivered kernel software; (b) supplies of services made to a non-resident person under s.7, provided the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST and the service does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed - the main route for gaming-security integrity middleware provided to foreign game developers and publishers; and (c) supplies of intangible personal property (software licenses, API access rights) under s.10, zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST and the copyright or other intangible is not used in Canada, subject to the substantially-all (90%+) place-of-use test and apportionment. The ULC may claim and retain full input tax credits on the zero-rated supply chain and must not collect GST on qualifying exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration transmitted via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for physical goods exports above CAD 2,000; not strictly required for pure cloud-delivered middleware
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (or an electronic delivery manifest proving foreign destination or server deployment outside Canada)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - under Canadian practice, the equivalent is a SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing receipt of the foreign customer's payment in the ULC's Canadian bank account
  • Executed software license or SaaS anti-cheat subscription agreement naming a non-resident counterparty and specifying the foreign target territory
  • Commercial invoice addressed to the non-resident customer marked 'zero-rated export supply' with GST shown at nil
  • Internal IT deployment records showing the anti-cheat engine kernel is licensed for use outside Canada during the relevant reporting period

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API invoice-approval system. The CRA mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns for all registrants with taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5M and electronic remittance for payments over CAD 10,000. The Pan-Canadian B2B e-Invoicing Standard (Peppol/CIUS-PC) is being phased in voluntarily from 2024 to 2026; it is not yet mandatory, but the CRA will treat Peppol-PN invoices and delivery manifest records as best-practice evidence in audits of export zero-rating.

The CRA has a 4-year reassessment window under s.298 of the Excise Tax Act, extended without time limit where misrepresentation, neglect, wilful default, or fraud is established. If the ULC fails to substantiate the non-resident recipient or foreign use, the CRA will re-impose 5% GST on the supplies, charge daily compound arrears interest, and may also: (i) apply the gross-negligence false-statement penalty under ETA s.280 equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax avoided; (ii) apply the failure-to-register penalty under ETA s.280.1 cited as the greater of CAD 250 and 1% of taxable supplies; (iii) claw back input tax credits claimed on the zero-rated chain; (iv) reassess intercompany profit allocations and imputed interest under ITA s.17 and s.247 when the 180-day repatriation benchmark is exceeded; and (v) pursue criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ETA s.327 to s.329, with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment. For a ULC with a valid s.96(1) partnership election, the denial of zero-rating may also trigger Part XIII withholding on deemed distributions to non-resident shareholders.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account in a floating exchange-rate environment. There are no exchange-control approvals, import licences, or currency-swap mandates for software exporters. Profit remittances and capital repatriation from the Alberta ULC are unrestricted in law, subject only to standard tax withholding (Part XIII on dividends, interest, and royalties), CRS/FATCA self-disclosure, and FINTRAC reporting for wire transfers above CAD 10,000 (or any suspicious transaction threshold).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes zero statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements. Licence fees and subscription revenues from the Gaming Anti-Cheat Engine may be retained in CAD, USD, EUR, or other trade currencies in a Canadian-regulated bank account without forced conversion. Sole statutory requirements are proper invoicing, taxable income reporting to the Canada Revenue Agency, and FINTRAC electronic funds-transfer monitoring where a single wire exceeds CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority. Canada maintains no standalone foreign-exchange administration; specific currency and cross-border reporting duties are delegated to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium only as a supplementary settlement wallet, not as the principal treasury layer. Canadian banking law does not licence foreign fintech EMIs as deposit-taking institutions; therefore an offshore EMI cannot hold, open, or present legal authority to advise on a true Alberta clearing account. EMIs (UK FCA or EU-authorised) may, however, provide an embedded multi-currency acceptance rail for Stripe payouts and low-ticket purchases from remote players. Legal constraints: the ULC must maintain its main operating and statutory payroll account with a Canadian bank or regulated Canadian credit union; all EMI flows at aggregation must be disclosed to the Canadian bank during KYC and reconciled via direct bank-to-bank upstream accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are routed by SWIFT wire from the parent entity to the ULC's designated RBC or BMO capital escrow account. The SWIFT remittance instruction must state 'SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION / CA-AB ULC' and reference the Alberta registry entity number. While Canadian banks do not use a mandatory Harmonized System purpose code, the bank will internally tag the transfer as a capital import under FINTRAC's large wire report. To avoid CRA reassessment, the corporate record must maintain a board resolution, a subscription agreement under the Alberta Business Corporations Act, and an intercompany capital account entry in CAD.
