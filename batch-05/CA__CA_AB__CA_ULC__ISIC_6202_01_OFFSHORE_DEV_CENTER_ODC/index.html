Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portals: CRA Business Registration Online and My Business Account. Company law registration is with Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Alberta ULC: (1) incorporate under the Alberta Business Corporations Act through the Alberta Corporate Registry, obtaining the Alberta registration number and certificate of incorporation; (2) apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online or Form RC1, requesting the corporate income tax account (program account RC0001), GST/HST account (RT0001) and, if employees are engaged in Canada, payroll account (RP0001); (3) provide the Alberta registered office address and, if no Canadian residential address, appoint a Canadian representative or agent for service of documents; (4) upon approval, CRA issues a nine-digit BN with program suffixes; no separate Alberta provincial corporate tax registration is required because Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Canada-Alberta tax collection agreement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a domestic check-the-box election. An Alberta ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under the Income Tax Act and files a T2 corporation income tax return. For cross-border owners, the ULC is eligible for a U.S. federal check-the-box election on IRS Form 8832: a 100% U.S. owner may elect disregarded entity status, and a multi-owner ULC may elect partnership status. The election is filed with the IRS only and does not alter CRA treatment. Canadian hybrid mismatch rules, including Income Tax Act section 18.4 and related anti-avoidance provisions, may restrict deductions or treaty benefits involving hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies made in Canada by a non-resident, or worldwide by a Canadian-resident corporation, over the last four consecutive calendar quarters (the CRA small supplier threshold). Once exceeded, the ULC must register for GST/HST and charge, collect, and remit 5% GST on taxable Canadian supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Canada qualify for 0% GST under Schedule VI, Part V, Excise Tax Act, provided the exporter maintains documentary proof of export. Services supplied by the Alberta ULC to a non-resident person are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 unless the service is supplied in connection with real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or is a personal service rendered to an individual present in Canada. Offshore software development and maintenance services to foreign clients generally qualify when the recipient is non-resident and not registered for GST/HST, the services are performed and consumed outside Canada, and the ULC retains a written contract, delivery evidence, and remittance records. Zero-rated exporters may claim input tax credits on their GST/HST returns and receive refunds of input tax credits where applicable.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate exists in Canada. CRA does not operate an API-based invoice clearance system. Electronic invoicing using PEPPOL, EDI, or PDF/e-invoice formats is permitted. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by registrants above CRA thresholds, and books and records must be retained for six years; CRA may inspect electronic systems and reassess on the basis of invoice, bank, or reconciliation mismatches.

Wilful evasion or fraud may result in criminal prosecution, expanded lookback periods, and retrospective clawbacks of zero-rated treatment with no limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control approvals, conversion licenses, or capital import/export restrictions. The Canadian dollar floats freely. Profit remittances from the ULC are unrestricted and are not subject to exchange control; the only friction is Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties (25% statutory rate, generally reduced under an applicable tax treaty). Repatriation of equity and dissolution proceeds is likewise unencumbered by FX regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished statutory exchange controls in 1951 under the Currency Act. There is no mandatory surrender, no conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for export receipts. Proceeds from software services exports may be held in any currency, invested, or converted at will. Only statistical and AML reporting obligations apply: the financial institution files a FINTRAC electronic funds transfer report for cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and Statistics Canada may collect Balance of Payments survey data on export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange control administration is not centralized - the Department of Finance Canada administers the Exchange Fund Account, with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) providing prudential bank supervision and FINTRAC providing AML/payment surveillance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Viable only as secondary disbursement rails for low-value operational transfers (contractor payouts, travel expense, micro-vendor payments) in the CAD 1,000 to 25,000 band through Wise B2B and Payoneer. These entities are FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses in Canada and must hold the necessary provincial money-services licensing under Alberta's Money Services Act. They are not legally authorized to accept deposits as banks; they cannot provide notional cash-pooling, credit facilities, or commercial letters of credit, and therefore are not viable as the primary treasury or capital-holding accounts for the ULC. Any use must be subordinated to a Schedule I bank operating account and fully visible in the corporate ledger.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% (15% federal + 8% Alberta provincial)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Six months after the fiscal year-end (e.g., if FYE Dec 31, deadline is June 30 of the following year).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods transactions; CAD 500,000 for services, intangibles, and other transactions (aggregate per year).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but requires economic substance for treaty benefits under the Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clause in tax treaties. The CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) to deny tax benefits from transactions lacking commercial substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the tax evaded (civil), plus interest; criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239) with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s. 227.1) if they failed to withhold or remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for criminal evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess to deny treaty benefits, apply GAAR to recharacterize transactions, and impose penalties up to 25% of the tax benefit (s. 163.2).

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for penalties if they knowingly participated in the scheme; potential criminal charges for tax evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for money laundering (up to CAD 500,000 fine and 10 years imprisonment).

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 for AML violations; travel bans and imprisonment for willful non-compliance.
