Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — My Business Account online portal; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA for the Alberta Ministry of Treasury Board and Finance, and Alberta corporate registry filings are processed by Alberta Corporate Registry / Service Alberta.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta ULC, the company must first complete Alberta incorporation or extra-provincial registration with the Alberta Corporate Registry to obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number. The corporation must then register with the CRA using Business Registration Online or Form RC1 to obtain the single 9-digit Business Number (BN) and open a GST/HST account (RT account). A payroll or import/export account is opened only if the business has Canadian employees or physical imports/exports. Non-resident vendors of specified digital services providing taxable supplies in Canada must complete the CRA Form GST25, 'GST/HST Simplified Application for Non-Resident Registration', if the simplified regime applies. The BN is immediate and must be shown on all returns, invoices, customs releases, and related tax documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

For Canadian Income Tax Act purposes, the Alberta ULC is a resident Canadian corporation subject to Part I entity-level corporate income tax and Alberta corporate tax. For U.S. federal tax purposes, the ULC is an eligible entity under Treasury Regulations §301.7701-3 and may file Form 8832 (check-the-box election) to be classified as a partnership, a disregarded entity, or an association taxable as a corporation. Canada does not provide a domestic check-the-box election for full provincial or federal flow-through treatment; therefore hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act s. 94.1 et seq., BEPS Action 2) and the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention Article IV residence tie-breakers must be considered before relying on fiscal transparency, and the ULC must maintain contemporaneous documentation of the elected classification for both Canadian and U.S. filing purposes.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies, including remote digital services supplied to persons in Canada, measured over any 12-month period; once this threshold is exceeded, registration is mandatory under the normal GST/HST regime or the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration, unless the supplier makes only zero-rated supplies.

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, invoice stamping, or transaction-level tax API regime. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account (subscriptions, in-app purchases, royalties, platform commissions) and capital-account flows (equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend repatriation). No Central Bank approval, exchange-control permit, or capital-account registration is required. The operative constraints are fiscal rather than monetary: Part XIII withholding tax at 25% statutory (typically 5-15% under the Canada-US, Canada-UK, and Canada-Netherlands treaties) on dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents, and FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting by financial intermediaries for transfers at or above CAD 10,000. The ULC must demonstrate treaty beneficial-ownership substance and maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for related-party treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation window. Foreign-currency receivables may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, or other accounts held at a Canadian Schedule I bank. For income-tax purposes, foreign-currency revenue must be converted to CAD at the Bank of Canada daily-average or noon exchange rate applicable to the taxation year. Cross-border EFTs exceeding CAD 10,000 are subject to bank-side FINTRAC reporting; there is no Central Bank approval for maintaining foreign-currency balances.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada operates a free-floating currency regime under IMF Article VIII and has no separate foreign exchange administration. Prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision, EFT reporting, and money-services-business registration are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); payment-clearing infrastructure (Lynx high-value and ACSS retail systems) is governed by Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary corporate-treasury venue; MEDIUM as an ancillary collection and creator-payout subnet. Canada has no statutory electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence; non-resident EMIs (such as Wise, Airwallex, MultiPass, and Currencycloud platforms) must either partner with a Canadian Schedule I/II bank or register with FINTRAC as a money-services business (MSB) to serve Canadian legal entities or clear CAD-denominated payments through Payments Canada. An unregistered offshore EMI cannot access Lynx/ACSS rails, cannot provide guaranteed CAD settlement for local creators, and exposes the ULC to cross-border EFT reporting complexity and source-of-funds audit friction. The structural recommendation is to keep legal-entity cash, CRA tax payments, payroll, and capital escrow exclusively with Tier-1 Canadian Schedule I banks, and to permit offshore EMIs only for routing USD/EUR creator payouts at or below CAD 10,000-equivalent per transaction.
