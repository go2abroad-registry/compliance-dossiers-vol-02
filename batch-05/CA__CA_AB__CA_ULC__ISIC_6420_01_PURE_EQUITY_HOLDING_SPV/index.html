Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal income tax and GST/HST; CRA collects Alberta corporate income tax on behalf of the Province under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act via the tax collection agreement. Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (TRA) administers provincial non-income taxes. CRA digital portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MyBA), GST/HST NETFILE.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the ULC under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) at the Alberta Corporate Registry (registered agent filing). Step 2: obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; a non-resident parent is disclosed, but the ULC is a Canadian-resident corporation. Step 3: activate program accounts on the BN — RP (corporate income tax, automatic), RT (GST/HST, only if registrant), RC (payroll, if employees). Step 4: file annual T2 Corporation Income Tax Return within 6 months of fiscal year-end and remit any Alberta/federal tax within 2 months of year-end. Step 5: file Form T1134 (information return re foreign affiliates) and T106 (cross-border reportable transactions) if applicable. A foreign entity with no Canadian presence deriving only Canadian dividends needs a BN only for Part XIII withholding compliance, using the same RC1/Non-Resident Registration route.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — under the Income Tax Act (Canada), an Alberta ULC is a corporation resident in Canada, taxed on worldwide income (federal general rate 15% plus Alberta general rate 8%, combined 23%). The ULC is not fiscally transparent in Canada. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — under the Income Tax Act (Canada), an Alberta ULC is a corporation resident in Canada, taxed on worldwide income (federal general rate 15% plus Alberta general rate 8%, combined 23%). The ULC is not fiscally transparent in Canada. Pass_Through_Eligible status applies only for U.S. federal tax purposes (IRS Form 8832 check-the-box), creating a hybrid entity; this does not alter Canadian entity-level taxation.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime. Key ULC-relevant mechanics: (i) U.S. shareholders may file IRS Form 8832 to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. tax purposes, subject to Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty Article IV(6)(b) fiscally-transparent provisions; (ii) intercorporate dividend deduction under ITA s. 112(1) (statutory, no election) because the ULC is a Canadian corporation; (iii) foreign affiliate surplus election/mechanics under ITA s. 113(1) with Form T1134 reporting; (iv) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident rental income and s. 217 employment income (not applicable to pure holding SPV); (v) anti-hybrid mismatch restrictions in ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5, 95 and s. 162(12), which may deny deductions for interest/royalty flows to hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — cumulative worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) made in any single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters triggers mandatory GST/HST registration (Excise Tax Act s. 240). Dividend income and share-sale gains are exempt financial services, so a pure equity holding SPV without fee income is generally not a registrant.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7 when the supplier (or an agent) exports goods to a destination outside Canada, with continuous-journey evidence and proof of export. Services: zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident person not present in Canada, unless the non-resident is a GST/HST registrant, the service is performed for an individual in Canada, or the service relates to Canadian real property. A pure equity holding SPV's dividend income is an exempt financial service (Schedule V Part VII), not zero-rated, so GST/HST does not apply to dividend flows.

  • Customs export declaration (Canada B13A/CAED; ACE/AES where goods transit the United States)
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill / CMR carrier evidence of export
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/bank statement evidencing export proceeds repatriation into Canada
  • Underlying contracts, purchase orders, insurance certificates, and CRA books-and-records retention under ETA s. 286 (six years)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control/API mandate exists in Canada or Alberta. CRA requires mandatory electronic filing (GST/HST NETFILE) for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,000,000, and mandatory T2 e-filing for corporations with gross revenue over CAD 1,000,000. Peppol-based Pan-Canadian e-invoicing is voluntary (CRA 2024–2025 pilot), with no statutory clearance obligation.

CRA reassessment windows: 3 years for income tax (ITA s. 152(4)) and 4 years for GST/HST (ETA s. 298), extended up to 10 years or indefinitely for misrepresentation or fraud. Penalties: ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence misrepresentation — 25% of understated tax; ETA s. 280 failure to file/remit — 1% of net tax plus 25% per month (maximum 12 months, minimum CAD 100); ETA s. 285 false statements — greater of CAD 100 and 25% of tax; ETA s. 327 criminal evasion — fines up to 250% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; ITA ss. 238/239 criminal evasion — 50%–200% fines and imprisonment up to 5 years. Improper export zero-rating is reversed on audit: the supply becomes taxable, ITCs are denied, interest (ETA s. 296) and penalties apply. The 180-day repatriation benchmark is an evidentiary best practice for proving payment/receipt in export substantiation, not a statutory deadline under the ETA or ITA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a floating, fully convertible currency. Canada maintains no exchange-control restrictions applicable to current-account or capital-account transactions, and no separate approval is required for dividend remittances, equity injections, or intercompany debt servicing by an Alberta ULC. Any withholding tax exposure arises under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (sections 212 and 215), not under FX regulations. A pure equity-holding SPV may freely repatriate profits, repay shareholder loans, and purchase foreign currency from a Schedule I/II Canadian bank at commercial rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose statutory surrender timelines or mandatory conversion ratios on export receipts. A Canadian exporter/SPV may hold foreign currency in Canadian or offshore accounts. Reporting obligations are limited to FINTRAC large cash/electronic funds transfer reports (CAD 10,000), CRA foreign income/asset reporting (Form T1135 where applicable), and, for goods exports, Export and Import Permits Act compliance where controlled goods are involved.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); with the Department of Finance Canada as the federal FX policy authority, OSFI as prudential bank regulator, and FINTRAC as AML/ATF supervisor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury rail. A non-Canadian electronic money institution (EMI) is not authorized under the Bank Act to take deposits or operate as a bank in Canada. Such an entity may only provide ancillary payment services if registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and compliant with provincial consumer-protection laws; it cannot substitute for a Canadian bank account for capital escrow, ULC statutory filings, or high-value clearing. For cross-border disbursements, a FINTRAC-registered MSB can be used as a secondary payout rail, but primary liquidity must remain in a Canadian prudence-regulated bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the inbound equity capital injection via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident parent's bank to the ULC's Canadian-dollar account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. Field 50K must identify the originating shareholder/remitter; field 59 must show the ULC's legal name and account number; field 70 should state 'ULC share subscription - equity contribution - [Alberta corporate access number]' to avoid misclassification as revenue. Canadian banks do not apply a single statutory purpose code, but internal corporate banking codes such as 'TRF/INV' are commonly used for inward investment. Canadian banks require a board resolution, share subscription agreement and source-of-funds evidence before accepting the inward credit if the amount exceeds FINTRAC's reportable threshold; where the parent is in a restricted/high-risk jurisdiction, pre-approval and the completion of enhanced due diligence should occur before the wire is sent.
