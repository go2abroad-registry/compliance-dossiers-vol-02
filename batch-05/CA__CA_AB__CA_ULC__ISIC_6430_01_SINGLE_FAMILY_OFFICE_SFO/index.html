Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal portal: My Business Account (canada.ca). Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement; Alberta does not maintain a separate corporate tax registration authority.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Alberta ULC must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or the Business Registration Online (BRO) service. The application must provide the Alberta certificate of incorporation, foreign registration documentation, registered office address in Alberta, directors, ultimate beneficial owners, and intended Canadian activities. CRA issues a nine-digit BN and assigns program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), and any other applicable withholding accounts. Registration is mandatory before carrying on business or making taxable supplies in Canada; a non-resident owner may hold all shares and the ULC may be registered immediately.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a domestic 'check-the-box' election. Under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention (Article IV(7)) and U.S. Treasury Regulation §301.7701-3, an Alberta ULC may be treated as fiscally transparent or disregarded for U.S. federal income tax purposes: a single-member ULC wholly owned by a U.S. person may be disregarded as a separate entity, and a multi-member ULC may be classified as a partnership. Such treaty-based classification can be applied consistently with U.S. tax elections, but the ULC remains an entity-level taxpayer under Canadian domestic corporate income tax law unless a specific statutory provision (e.g., section 216 election for Canadian rental income) applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide Canadian taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made by a person carrying on business in Canada; the small-supplier threshold is the CAD 30,000 cumulative amount over the current quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters. Exempt financial services are excluded from taxable supply threshold. Non-resident suppliers of digital services to Canadian consumers have a separate CAD 30,000 registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the goods are exported by the supplier in accordance with the contract of sale and the supplier obtains sufficient documentary evidence of export (e.g., customs export declaration, bill of lading, freight waybill) at the time of supply. A supply of a service to a non-resident is zero-rated if the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, is not a personal service performed for an individual present in Canada, and is not a service performed in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada unless the property is to be exported by or on behalf of the non-resident. A single-family office serving a non-resident principal must document the non-resident's identity, the offshore nature of the service, and the absence of a Canadian benefit recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or approved electronic export report / Cargo Report) where applicable
  • Bill of Lading or Waybill issued by a carrier showing Canadian exit and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent foreign-currency bank record confirming payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice with exporter and buyer names, Incoterms, description, and value
  • Carrier certificate or customs broker export-filing receipt, where applicable

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval, XML schema, or digital stamping mandate for invoices. GST/HST registrants may issue paper or electronic invoices containing prescribed content (supplier's BN, date, description, amount, and tax amount). Registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 10 million must file GST/HST returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE or EDI; however, there is no Continuous Transaction Controls (CTC) or electronic invoice clearance requirement. Records must be preserved in auditable electronic or PDF form.

CRA may disallow zero-rating and reassess within the normal four-year reassessment period (unlimited where misrepresentation is due to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud), recovering unremitted GST/HST plus compound interest. A false statement or omission in a GST/HST return may trigger a penalty equal to 50% of the tax at issue under Excise Tax Act s.285.1; repeated failure to collect or remit taxes may attract a penalty equal to the amount not remitted. Knowingly contravening the Excise Tax Act can lead to criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment. Retroactive clawback of input tax credits and re-characterization of export supplies as taxable are common where evidence of physical export or non-resident service recipient status is deficient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) holds IMF Article VIII status and is fully convertible; Canada maintains an open capital account with no general restrictions on current-account payments, capital movements, profit repatriation, or foreign direct investment. The principal remittance hurdle is Part XIII withholding tax on distributions from an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) to non-resident shareholders: the statutory 25% is treaty-reduced (e.g., 15% or 5% under the Canada-US Treaty where the shareholder is the beneficial owner), but post-5th Protocol Alberta ULCs are not qualifying persons and cannot themselves claim treaty relief; a parallel taxable Canadian corporation or ULC-specific treaty protection analysis is therefore required. Interest, royalties, rents, and management fees paid to non-residents are also subject to Part XIII withholding and must satisfy arm's-length transfer-pricing standards. Alberta's ULC income is computed under the Income Tax Act (Canada) as a corporation, but for Canadian federal tax purposes a ULC may elect treatment and its US owner may, in certain cross-border structures, treat it as a disregarded flow-through; any such characterization must be aligned with the Canada Revenue Agency administrative positions and the Canada-US Treaty limitation-on-benefits rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose statutory surrender of export receipts, mandatory conversion ratios, or time-limited repatriation obligations on foreign currency proceeds. An Alberta ULC may retain export proceeds or investment sale proceeds in any foreign currency domestically or offshore, subject to (i) FINTRAC reporting of single cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, (ii) the CRA functional-currency election rules where the ULC maintains CAD or a qualifying foreign currency books of account, and (iii) CBCA/ABCA corporate record-keeping requirements. Treasury policy should nonetheless consolidate foreign currency positions through a Canadian Schedule I bank to reduce settlement risk.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary and currency authority for Canada; prudential supervision of federally chartered banks and trust companies is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (Canada); anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing supervision is vested in the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); no dedicated foreign-exchange control ministry exists because comprehensive exchange controls were abolished in Canada effective December 1, 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail, Medium as an ancillary disbursement rail. Canada does not confer EU-style EMI passporting; a foreign fintech or digital EMI must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under section 37(1) of the PCMLTFA and, depending on provincial activity, hold a money-lender or payment-agent authorization. Regulated licenses such as Wise Payments and Payoneer can provide small-value cross-border rails but cannot lawfully provide capital escrow, securities custody, or settlement-grade corporate banking services for a single-family office ULC managing high-value family assets.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for inbound capital uses a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the family trust or shareholder to the ULC's Canadian account, with payment narrative explicitly tagged 'Shareholder Equity Contribution', 'Paid-in Capital - ULC', or 'Shareholder Loan - [Principal Name]'. For US-origin funds, Fedwire or ACH automatic transfers settle into the Canadian bank's New York correspondent before CAD conversion via the bank's FX desk. For securities-related inflows, broker-to-custodian free-of-payment transfers through DTC in the United States and CDS in Canada preserve the beneficial ownership chain. FINTRAC cross-border reporting applies at CAD 10,000 or more; banks will require the ULC resolution authorizing the injection, board minutes, updated Alberta corporate registry, source-of-wealth declaration, and a comfort letter from the family office or trust protector. Purpose code mapping for intercompany capital movements is commonly recorded as 'Equity participation', 'Loans between related enterprises', 'Investment in Canadian securities', or 'Treasury funding' on the bank's payment instruction; prompt submission of the underlying funding agreement eliminates most compliance holds.Outbound treasury disbursements are settled through SWIFT MT103 for operating payments, MT202 COV for interbank and correspondent transfers, and Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) for trade settlement requiring documentary compliance. Canadian-dollar high-value obligations clear same-day through Lynx, the national high-value payment system; cross-currency obligations settle through CLS where both currencies are CLS-eligible, eliminating settlement risk. Securities and family-asset disbursements use CDS for Canadian instruments and Euroclear, Clearstream, or DTC for cross-border portfolios, with free-of-payment or delivery-versus-payment instructions confirmed by the custody bank. Every ULC must appoint an auditor at each annual shareholders' meeting unless all shareholders unanimously resolve to waive auditor appointment for that year. A certified audit is therefore mandatory if any shareholder declines the waiver, if the corporation is a reporting issuer, or if a lender, custodian, or family-office governance policy requires an audit; otherwise the requirement may be waived annually by unanimous written consent.
