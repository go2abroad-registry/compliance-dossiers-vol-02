Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) under the Excise Tax Act, Part IX (GST/HST) and the Income Tax Act; Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Alberta Tax Collection Agreement. The operational portal is CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity or a Canadian-registered Unlimited Liability Company (ULC) in Alberta must first complete Alberta corporate registry formalities (certificate of incorporation or extra-provincial registration). The entity then obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1, electronically through Business Registration Online, or by written application. A separate GST/HST account must be registered if annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000. The CRA BN serves as the statutory tax identifier (TIN) for federal and provincial corporate tax filings and for GST/HST filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Domestically, an Alberta ULC is treated as a taxable corporation under the Canadian Income Tax Act; no Canadian check-the-box partnership election exists. For foreign tax classification purposes (notably under US tax law), an electing Canadian ULC may be treated as a pass-through or disregarded entity. For GST/HST purposes, joint elections may be available under section 150 (closely related persons) and section 156 (inter-corporation supplies), and small registrants may elect the Quick Method of Accounting. Hybrid mismatch rules are addressed through Canada's earnings-stripping and interest-deduction limitations; no separate elective hybrid mismatch regime applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered supplier may zero-rate the GST/HST on exports of tangible goods where possession passes to the recipient outside Canada and the supplier has retained documentary evidence of export. Services, including certain financial services in the factoring and supply-chain-finance context, may be zero-rated when the supply is made to a non-resident recipient and is not used or consumed in Canada. Factoring transactions are generally exempt financial services, but a zero-rated supply to a non-resident may arise where the factoring service is performed outside Canada and all consideration flows from a non-resident.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (B13 / Canadian Export Reporting System or authorized portal)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement out of Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank wire verification evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and factoring/assignment agreement evidencing the transaction nature
  • Insurance, packing list, or transport records supporting the export transaction where applicable
  • CRA form or electronic record documenting the zero-rated export supply and the supplier's GST/HST account details

Canada does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government-continuous transaction controls. Registered suppliers may issue electronic invoices (EDI, XML/PDF) provided the records meet CRA legibility and retention requirements. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or on a prescribed schedule. Canada is progressing toward a Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework interoperable with PEPPOL, but e-invoice clearance remains voluntary and not a precondition for claiming input tax credits or refunds.

CRA and CBSA coordinate export verification and may reassess any zero-rated supply where documentary evidence does not prove export. Erroneous or fraudulent zero-rating can trigger repayment of claimed input tax credits and refunds, plus statutory interest from the date the refund was received. Penalties include fines under section 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act for false statements, gross negligence penalties equal to 50% of the additional tax payable, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion. The ordinary reassessment period is four years after the filing date; fraud or willful evasion permits reassessment without a time limitation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime with no general restrictions on capital account or current account transactions. The Canadian dollar trades freely in interbank and over-the-counter markets. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, management fees, royalties, and repayment of intercompany debt are lawful transfers that may be effected through any Schedule I bank or authorized foreign bank branch; however, cross-border remittances to non-residents may trigger Part XIII Canadian withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, and outbound payments must satisfy the solvency and creditor-protection tests applicable to an unlimited liability company under Alberta corporate law. There is no Canadian currency surrender requirement for individuals, corporates, or trade finance institutions, and no obligation to sell foreign exchange to the central bank. A Canadian ULC engaged in trade supply chain factoring may maintain multi-currency bank accounts domestically and with foreign correspondent banks, subject only to bank AML/KYC limits and financial instruments regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for the surrender or mandatorily required conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may lawfully retain export receipts in foreign currency in resident or non-resident accounts, may convert to Canadian dollars at any time through authorized foreign exchange dealers, and may use such proceeds for cross-border trade settlements. Factoring companies receiving foreign-currency receivable collections from Canadian exporters or foreign obligors are not required to convert those proceeds into CAD or to repatriate them within any prescribed period. All such flows must nonetheless be reported through the regular banking system, be traceable under FINTRAC record-keeping rules, and comply with economic sanctions and trade control laws administered by Global Affairs Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); exchange policy oversight is also exercised by the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the Financial Administration Act; there is no standalone exchange-control administration equivalent to a Foreign Exchange Administration in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with material legal constraints. Canada does not grant banking charters to electronic money institutions as stand-alone deposit takers; offshore EMI and neobank platforms cannot offer Canadian-dollar transactional accounts unless they partner with a federally regulated Schedule I bank or an authorized foreign bank branch. An offshore digital EMI may be used as a payment initiation tool or as a virtual account overlay, but underlying funds must clear through a Canadian bank account in the legal name of the CA_ULC. The CA_ULC must be registered as a money services business in Canada under FINTRAC if it is involved in money transmission, foreign exchange dealing, or virtual currency dealing, unless a statutory exemption for authorized factoring licence holders applies. Foreign fintechs not licensed in Alberta cannot act as the primary clearing rail for trade settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to the CA_ULC should be routed via SWIFT MT103 or structured as a genuine equity or debt transaction. The recommended purpose code for direct investment in the capital of the Canadian company is 'S10 Investment in Canada' where the correspondent-bank manual uses the OECD purpose codes, or the transmitting bank's equivalent code for cross-border intercompany financing; debt contributions and shareholder loans should use a code reflecting intercompany financing or repayment of loans depending on the remitter's internal classification. Each inbound wire must be pre-advised to the receiving Canadian bank with a completion of the bank's foreign exchange and tax identification forms, including the ULC's Business Number, the ultimate remitter name and address, and the stated business activity in the recipient field. The settlement account must be an operational or capital escrow account registered to the CA_ULC. The bank will verify the funds against OFAC/UN/Canadian sanctions lists and may require the non-resident investor to complete a Canadian Suspicious Transaction Report threshold review. Verification should include a scanning receipt of the signed investment agreement, the subscriber resolution, and confirmation that the wire is not an unlicensed deposit under Canadian law. For any cross-border share subscription, the completion must be recorded in the Alberta corporate register and reported, where relevant, to Statistics Canada's International Transactions in Services tool.Outbound trade disbursements from the factoring ULC should be settled through SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers to non-resident suppliers or OEM sellers, MT202 COV for interbank cover payments where required, and commercial letters of credit issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank advised through a foreign confirming bank for purchases of trade receivables or supplier financing. For factoring disbursements to exporters, the standard method is a direct SWIFT MT103 payment to the exporter's bank, with the field 70 remittance information referencing the invoice number, the factoring agreement, and the underlying purchase order. Commercial LCs are appropriate when the trade receivable itself is collateralized by a documentary title or when the factoring arrangement includes a receivables purchase from a supplier requiring irrevocable payment terms. For intra-group treasury settlement between ULC and foreign affiliates, MT103 with clear purpose codes and an internal service-level agreement is acceptable. All outbound flows must be executed only to accounts not on sanctions lists and must be supported by underlying documentary evidence retained for seven years under FINTRAC and Canada Revenue Agency policies.
