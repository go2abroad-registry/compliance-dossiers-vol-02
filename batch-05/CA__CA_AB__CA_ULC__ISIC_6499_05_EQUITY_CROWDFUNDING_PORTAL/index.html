Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), Represent a Client, GST/HST NETFILE, and My Business Account at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency. Alberta-specific authorities: Alberta Corporate Registries (Alberta Corporate Account Registry - ACAR) for incorporation and Alberta Treasury Board and Finance for the 8% Alberta corporate income tax (2% small business rate on the first CAD 500,000) and for the absence of any Alberta provincial sales tax.

E-government filing portal

A ULC obtains the statutory 9-digit Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account through (i) one-stop electronic application via Business Registration Online (BRO), (ii) paper Form RC1 - Request for a Business Number - filed with the Sudbury Tax Centre, or (iii) an authorized representative acting through Represent a Client. Mandatory GST/HST registration arises when the portal's Canadian-sourced taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act ss. 240(1), 241, 242); voluntary registration is available to a non-resident that carries on a business in Canada, makes a taxable supply in Canada, or holds an interest in Canadian intangibles, and permits full input tax credit recovery. The BN is the single federal/provincial TIN; Alberta issues no separate provincial tax identifier. Concurrently, the ULC must maintain its Alberta corporate record and file any required T2 corporate return or, after a partnership election, T5013 partnership information return.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. A ULC is by default an incorporated taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (entity-level taxation at federal and Alberta provincial rates), but may elect under ABCA s. 39 to be deemed not to be a corporation, with each shareholder deemed a partner and the portal's fee income flowing through on a current basis. Territorial scope: resident shareholders are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident shareholders are taxed only on Canadian-source income attributable to the Alberta permanent establishment (ITA ss. 2(3), 115). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A ULC is by default an incorporated taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (entity-level taxation at federal and Alberta provincial rates), but may elect under ABCA s. 39 to be deemed not to be a corporation, with each shareholder deemed a partner and the portal's fee income flowing through on a current basis. Territorial scope: resident shareholders are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident shareholders are taxed only on Canadian-source income attributable to the Alberta permanent establishment (ITA ss. 2(3), 115). Tax-exempt zone: none; no Canadian special economic zone status applies to Alberta fintech or crowdfunding operations.

Available election forms

(1) ABCA s. 39 partnership election - requires unanimous shareholder consent and that all shareholders be resident in Canada at the time of the election, filed with CRA; converts the ULC into a deemed partnership for income tax purposes, with reporting migrating from T2 to T5013. (2) ETA s. 273 election - filed with CRA to deem the ULC and its shareholders a single GST/HST partnership registrant, mirroring the income-tax pass-through. (3) US check-the-box - a US shareholder may file IRC Form 8832 or Form 8858 to treat the Canadian ULC as a partnership or disregarded entity for US federal tax purposes, producing a hybrid entity profile that must be reconciled with Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4(3), 94B and OECD BEPS Action 2). (4) Default no-election - the ULC remains a corporation in Canada while flowing through to a sole US member, enabling consolidated US federal filing and Canadian corporate allocation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - worldwide and Canadian-sourced taxable supply threshold for small supplier status, measured per calendar quarter and trailing four quarters; excludes exempt financial services supplies for threshold purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The portal's service fees to a non-resident, non-registered person are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 9 provided the service is not rendered to an individual present in Canada, does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and is not supplied to a non-resident GST/HST registrant. Fees charged to Canadian-resident issuers are generally subject to 5% GST; fees that constitute the arranging of an equity issuance may be exempt as a financial service under Schedule V, Part VII, s. 1, so substance and documentation determine taxability. Refund mechanics: zero-rated outputs permit full input tax credit recovery on the GST/HST return, and any excess net ITC refund is paid under ETA s. 170 within 30 days of filing.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CES electronic filing (paper form G13/B13A) with the Canada Border Services Agency where tangible goods are exported; for services, replaced by FINTRAC EFT report for cross-border payment flows
  • Commercial invoice / Canada Customs Invoice (Form B5-2) accompanied by the signed portal service agreement and fee schedule with the non-resident issuer
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (goods-only route); for services, substitute with time-stamped platform logs, KYC files, and the foreign issuer's certificate of residence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a Canadian chartered bank or authorized foreign-exchange dealer evidencing USD/CAD settlement through the banking channel

Canada imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls. CRA relies on post-audit verification of electronic records under ETA s. 286 and voluntary adoption of the PEPPOL e-invoicing framework in the B2G segment. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE; remittances above CAD 25,000 must be made by EFT or wire. A crowdfunding portal must retain machine-readable digital ledgers covering issuer contracts, escrow statements, investor KYC files, and fee schedules for CRA inspection within 30 days of demand.

CRA may reassess zero-rated claims within 4 years (ETA s. 298), extended to 7 years for misrepresentation and without limitation for fraud; if contemporaneous export evidence is absent, the 0% rate is clawed back to 5% of the full fee, GST is assessed as collectible, daily compound interest accrues under ETA s. 280 (prescribed rate plus 4%), and gross-negligence penalties apply under ETA s. 285 (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of net tax sought), with additional false-statement penalties under ETA s. 327.1. Criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ETA s. 327 may impose fines of 50%-200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; equity crowdfunding portals also face Alberta Securities Commission exempt-market-dealer audits, FINTRAC money-services-business examinations, and securities licence sanctions that can cascade into reassessment and licence revocation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange rate regime; there is no foreign-exchange control law, no central bank conversion permit, and no capital-flow approval mechanism. Current-account transactions (trade in goods/services, dividends, interest, royalties, management fees) and capital-account transactions (equity subscriptions, intercompany loans, portfolio investment) may be executed freely in any currency. Profit remittance by an Alberta ULC is unrestricted, subject only to the corporate solvency tests under the Alberta Business Corporations Act, to withholding tax on payments to non-residents under Income Tax Act Part XIII, and to applicable treaty relief under the Canada-US Tax Convention. All cross-border electronic funds transfers at or above CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC by the financial intermediary; non-resident investments of controlling scale may trigger Investment Canada Act notification or review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts or other foreign-currency earnings. A ULC may retain export and crowdfunding revenues indefinitely in CAD, USD, EUR, or any other currency in domestic or offshore accounts, and may convert at commercial rates through any authorized bank or registered foreign exchange dealer. The only binding obligations are FINTRAC reporting of cross-border electronic funds transfers and currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more, and the maintenance of complete wire-transfer records for any transfer exceeding CAD 1,000, plus compliance with federal sanctions and OSFI anti-money-laundering guidance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - central monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) - federal prudential regulator of banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) - AML/KYC and cross-border transaction reporting authority; Alberta Treasury Board and Finance - provincial financial services oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility with significant legal constraints. Canada does not recognize the EU/UK EMI passport, and offshore digital EMIs cannot hold client funds, clear CAD, or access domestic payment rails without either (i) entering into a regulated sponsorship arrangement with a Schedule I Canadian bank or (ii) obtaining FINTRAC registration as a money services business and, where applicable, Bank of Canada authorization under the Retail Payment Activities Act. For escrow-grade crowdfunding funds, securities laws in Alberta compel custody with a Canadian financial institution or a registered securities firm; an offshore EMI is therefore legally disqualified from serving as primary treasury custodian and may only provide auxiliary disbursement or FX conversion support.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident institutional investors inject equity capital into the ULC via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wires bearing the explicit purpose reference 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION - CA AB ULC' together with the share subscription agreement and allotment certificate; no Bank of Canada approval or exchange-control permit is required. Canadian banks treat such receipts as capital-account inflows and may request the underlying transaction documents for KYC/EDD completion. For retail equity-crowdfunding investors, inbound funds must be routed exclusively into the segregated capital escrow account through Payments Canada Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) ACH credits or direct bank wires, never into the ULC operating account, in accordance with Alberta securities custody requirements. FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting triggers at CAD 10,000; all incoming payments must include the remitter's legal name, account number, and regulated-bank originator information.Outbound trade settlements are executed through (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border supplier and invoice payments; (ii) commercial letters of credit and standby LCs governed by UCP 600 where trade finance is extended to startups and portfolio companies; (iii) Lynx (the Canadian real-time gross settlement system replacing LVTS) for high-value CAD domestic disbursements; and (iv) USD clearing via the Canadian bank's US correspondent network over CHIPS or Fedwire for US-dollar payables.
