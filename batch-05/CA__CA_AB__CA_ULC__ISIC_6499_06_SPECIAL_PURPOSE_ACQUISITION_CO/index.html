Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); registration channel is Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration and ongoing account administration is via the CRA 'My Business Account' portal; export-related customs liaison is the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) using the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS); Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement, and Alberta imposes no provincial sales tax or GST/HST addition.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign parent forming or acquiring an Alberta Unlimited Liability Company (ULC) must: (1) incorporate or convert under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) using the statutory designation 'Unlimited Liability Company' (Alberta ULC regime brought into force under the 2022 amendments); (2) obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) through Business Registration Online or the one-stop corporate registration service, opening the RC (corporate income tax) and RT (GST/HST) program accounts; (3) open the RT account compulsorily once the ULC ceases to be a small supplier (worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the current or four preceding calendar quarters), or where the ULC solicits business from Canadian residents, or where the CRA requires security under section 296 of the Excise Tax Act; (4) file a T2 corporation income tax return if the ULC is resident in Canada (central management and control in Alberta) or carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment, together with international disclosure schedules (T1134, T1135, Schedule 57) and, where applicable, Form RC312 for reportable transactions; (5) complete the Alberta Corporate Registry annual return (Service Alberta); and (6) for a SPAC holding escrow/trust assets, maintain the GST/HST account for reporting of exempt financial supplies and file net-tax returns with supporting custodial trust statements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Canadian domestic income tax law the Alberta ULC is an entity-level Part I taxpayer, but the ULC is hybrid-eligible through several election mechanisms: (i) U.S. shareholders may execute a U.S. check-the-box election on Form 8832 to treat the Canadian ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. federal tax purposes while Canadian law continues to tax the ULC as a corporation, creating the classic hybrid flow-through bridge; (ii) the Canada-U.S. Income Tax Convention (1980, as amended by the 1995 and 2007 Protocols), particularly its Article IV treatment of fiscally transparent entities and unlimited liability companies, permits treaty characterization of the ULC's income as that of its U.S. shareholders to the extent those shareholders are liable for the ULC's debts, with related limitation-on-benefits and permanent establishment consequences; (iii) Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act subsections 18.4(2)-(4) and section 20.2, plus the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) regime of section 18.2 for taxation years beginning after September 30, 2023) self-disallow deductions to the extent arbitrage arises from the ULC's pass-through status; and (iv) Alberta statutory conversion elections allow an existing Alberta corporation to convert into an Alberta ULC by filing articles of amendment, and a ULC may elect, under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta), the terms of its unlimited liability share attributes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold under subsection 240(1) and section 148 of the Excise Tax Act: a person whose worldwide value of taxable supplies (including associated persons' supplies) does not exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter and in the four preceding calendar quarters is not required to register; for a cross-border SPAC, issuance of shares or debentures and holding of escrow trust securities are exempt financial supplies under Schedule V, Part VII and do not count toward the threshold, but the ULC must register if it solicits business in Canada, carries on a commercial activity in Canada, or is otherwise deemed registered under the anti-avoidance rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier exports the goods 'as soon after the sale as is reasonable under the circumstances' and retains the prescribed documentary evidence of export (Schedule VI, section 9). Offshore services and intangible supplies made to non-residents are zero-rated under sections 7 to 22 of Part V, Schedule VI where the supply is performed wholly outside Canada, relates to real property situated outside Canada, or applies to goods situated outside Canada at the time the risk passes. A SPAC's core activities — issuance of its listed units or shares, settlement of warrants/derivatives, and investment of escrow proceeds — are GST/HST-exempt financial services under Schedule V, Part VII rather than zero-rated supplies; however, separately contracted management, advisory, or acquisition-target services supplied to a non-resident sponsor or a non-resident acquisition vehicle may qualify for zero-rating if the recipient is non-resident and the services are not in respect of Canadian-situated property or goods. The exporter must be a registered supplier holding an RT program account before it can invoice zero-rated supplies or claim export refunds.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (Form B13A / A1) filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or the CBSA EDI portal where the value of the exported goods equals or exceeds CAD 2,000 or the goods are controlled or prohibited
  • Commercial invoice (with the supplier's Business Number, buyer's name and address, complete description, quantity, and value of the exported goods, and the export date)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (ocean bill, air waybill, rail waybill, or parcel/courier receipt evidencing movement out of Canada)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from the Canadian financial institution receiving the export proceeds, referencing the ULC's account and the foreign payer/SWIFT wire
  • Custodial trust account statements and contractual evidence identifying the non-resident beneficiary, the escrow trustee, and the source and character of the funds in the SPAC trust
  • Foreign-currency conversion records, foreign cusip/ISIN records, and any applicable export permits or certificates, retained for six years under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act and sections 87 to 91 of the related regulations

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no continuous transaction controls (CTC), and no government-imposed digital stamping of invoices. GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed commercial invoices under section 3 of the GST/HST Regulations (SOR/91-30), retain books and records for six years, and file periodic net-tax returns electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or the 'My Business Account' API; monthly filing is required for registrants whose total annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million and quarterly filing for smaller registrants, with payments due one month after the end of the reporting period; the CRA may require a non-resident registrant to post security under subsection 296(2), and all electronic records must be provided in machine-readable format on CRA demand.

CRA's GST/HST export verification and refund programs test whether goods were actually exported and whether books and records substantiate every link in the export chain before a refund or zero-rating is accepted. The four-year limitation period under subsections 298(1) to (4) of the Excise Tax Act applies to GST/HST refund reassessments, extended to seven years for non-residents with a permanent establishment, and becomes unlimited where misrepresentation, wilful default, or fraud is established under subsection 298(6). Civil penalties: 50% of the falsely claimed refund or credit under subsection 285(1) (minimum CAD 250); wilful evasion under subsection 280(1) yields a penalty of at least CAD 25,000 and up to 200% of the amount claimed, plus daily compounded interest; criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to five years under subsection 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act and section 239 of the Income Tax Act. Under the Income Tax Act, gross negligence penalties of 50% of the misstated tax apply under subsection 163(2), and the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in subsection 245(2) may deny treaty, hybrid, or export benefits where a ULC SPAC arrangement is adjudged abusive; mandatory disclosure of reportable/notifiable transactions under section 237.3 (Form RC312) carries separate penalties of CAD 5,000 to CAD 10,000, while tax-shelter non-registration penalties and promoter penalties (25%/100% of gross proceeds) may apply to abusive flow-through or export schemes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains free convertibility for current account transactions under IMF Article VIII. Capital account liberalization permits corporate profit remittances with no material convertibility barriers for ULC entities. FINTRAC reporting applies to FX transactions exceeding CAD 10,000; non-resident ownership in ULCs is permitted subject to beneficial ownership verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender of export proceeds required. Exporters may retain foreign currency in offshore accounts. Banks may request supporting documentation for transactions over CAD 10,000; all FX dealings must comply with FINTRAC reporting obligations.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% (15% federal general corporate rate + 8% Alberta provincial general rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends paid to non-residents under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act; reduced to 15% generally, and to 5% for a corporate beneficial owner holding not less than 10% of voting stock, under the Canada–U.S. Tax Convention; other DTA rates vary.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of non-arm's-length transactions with a non-resident taxpayer in a taxation year triggers the mandatory T106 and contemporaneous documentation requirement; separate Country-by-Country reporting applies where the MNE group consolidated revenue is C$750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; instead, economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA), the corporate-residence test based on central management and control, and the doctrine of sham. A ULC SPAC must maintain substantive governance, trust documentation, investment decisions, acquisition activities, and commercial records; a purely artificial use of the ULC to convert income into capital or treaty-protected distributions is recharacterized and penalized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion conviction under s.239(1) ITA: fine between 50% and 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment up to five years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2): up to 50% of the understated tax payable.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers participating in or authorizing the false return may be jointly convicted under s.239; civil restitution, director's liability certificates, seizure and sale of personal assets, cross-border travel bans, and extradition to Canada are possible.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under s.247(2) to apply arm's-length pricing; s.247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the gross adjustment where reasonable efforts and documentation are absent; interest accrues on all reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unpaid corporate Part XIII withholdings and GST if the mispriced payments are characterized as deemed dividends or benefits; quantum of the directors' liability can extend to the full unreported withholding amount.
Shell Company / GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial of the tax benefit under s.245(2) plus reassessment; third-party promoter penalty under Part XVIII; gross-negligence penalty up to 50% where the arrangement nables trust or capital treatment.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the ULC's unlimited liability veil and impose joint and several liability on directors for dividends declared while the corporation was insolvent; personal asset tracing and judgment enforcement follow.
Failure to Report Cross-Border Transactions

Statutory sanction: Penalty for failure to file T106: not less than C$1,000 and not more than C$25,000 per year, calculated at C$100 per day of default; CbCR non-compliance attracts separate tiered penalties and cross-notification to foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors of the ULC who approve or cause the non-filing are exposed to culpable reprimand, director's responsibility for Part XIII and GST amounts, and inability to obtain a tax clearance certificate for cross-border asset transfers.
Dividend Stripping / Unreported Part XIII Withholding

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full Part XIII amount as a deemed dividend under s.84(2) or s.214(1), together with interest and penalties; s.160 transferor liability applies where assets are transferred to a non-resident for less than fair market value.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the ULC's failure to withhold and remit Part XIII tax under s.227.1; any fraudulent distribution to the non-resident shareholder permits the CRA to pierce the corporate veil and pursue personal assets.
AML/CFT and Trust-Escrow Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, typically up to C$1,000,000 or more per violation for a corporation; criminal sanctions include fines and imprisonment of officers.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize unreported cross-border wire movements can be held personally criminally liable, subjected to travel bans, asset forfeiture, and disqualification from professional fiduciaries; senior compliance officers face personal regulatory penalties for AML compliance failures.
