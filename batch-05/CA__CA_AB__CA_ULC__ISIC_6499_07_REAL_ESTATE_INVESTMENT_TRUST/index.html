Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account; provincial corporate registry administered by Service Alberta / Alberta Corporate Registry.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) under the Alberta Business Corporations Act through the Alberta Corporate Registry, obtaining the Alberta Corporate Access Number. Step 2: Register with the CRA via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts: RC0001 (Corporate Income Tax), RT0001 (GST/HST), RP0001 (Payroll, if applicable), and RM0001 (Import/Export, if applicable). Step 3: If the ULC has no Canadian establishment and is treated as a non-resident for registration purposes, file Form RC4380 (Application to Register a Non-Resident Business). The ULC remains a Canadian tax resident corporation for federal Income Tax Act purposes and must file a T2 return annually within six months of year-end irrespective of foreign ownership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

An Alberta ULC may elect under s. 39(13) of the Alberta Corporate Tax Act to be treated as a partnership for Alberta provincial corporate income tax purposes, flowing Alberta taxable income and losses through to shareholders; this election does not alter federal classification, under which the ULC remains a taxable corporation subject to entity-level Part I tax (combined federal 15% + Alberta 8% general corporate rate). For U.S. federal tax purposes, the Canadian ULC is not a per se corporation and may check the box via Form 8832 as a partnership or disregarded entity, subject to Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4–18.6, 20.2 and 94) and treaty Limitation-on-Benefits provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold under s. 148 of the Excise Tax Act (worldwide taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters). A non-resident or Canadian ULC making taxable supplies of real property situated in Canada, or otherwise carrying on commercial activity in Canada, must register once the threshold is exceeded; non-registered non-resident vendors selling taxable real property may trigger purchaser GST withholding and self-assessment obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 of the Excise Tax Act where the goods are shipped and satisfactory documentary evidence of export exists (CRA administrative practice generally expects export within 60 days of transfer or shipment). Services are zero-rated under s. 7 where supplied to a non-resident and performed wholly outside Canada, or where the non-resident recipient is not a GST/HST registrant and the service does not relate to Canadian real property or tangible goods situated in Canada. For this Alberta ULC/REIT vehicle: residential rents are exempt (no GST/HST and no ITC recovery), commercial rents are taxable at 5% with full input tax credit recovery, and distributions or dividends to holders are not consideration for a supply and fall outside the GST/HST net; genuine foreign-head-office management, advisory, or financing services to non-residents may qualify for zero-rating only if the statutory conditions are met. Real property situated in Canada cannot be 'exported' and its sale is a taxable supply of real property unless it is used residential housing (exempt). While no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline exists in Canadian tax law, CRA expects contemporaneous evidence of foreign-currency receipt within a reasonable commercial period (pipeline standard: 180 days); absence of timely inward payment undermines export substantiation.

  • Form B13A Export Declaration (filed through CERS/CUSDEC for goods exceeding CAD 2,000 or as otherwise prescribed)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean / Air / Truck / Intermodal)
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (if treaty preference is claimed)
  • Carrier Certificate of Export / Proof of Delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT / foreign-currency payment records

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance mandate, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. The CRA accepts XML, EDI, PDF, and Peppol-based e-invoices; the voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (Peppol BIS 3.0) is operational for B2B interoperability. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE and My Business Account API channels.

CRA export-audit protocols require contemporaneous export documentation and foreign-use evidence; unsupported zero-rating claims are re-characterized as taxable domestic supplies and GST/HST is reassessed at 5% with daily compound arrears interest under s. 280 of the Excise Tax Act. False statements or omissions attract penalties under ETA s. 285 (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax amount); income-tax gross negligence penalties under ITA s. 163(2) equal 50% of the understated tax; fraud and willful evasion under ETA s. 327 / ITA s. 239 may result in fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. The standard 4-year reassessment limitation period for both GST/HST and corporate income tax is indefinitely extended where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established, permitting full retrospective clawback of improperly claimed export refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada imposes no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no prior monetary authority approval on capital injection, dividend distribution, profit remittance, or portfolio flows. The principal legal constraint for a non-resident-owned Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) operating as a real estate investment trust is Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) on distributions to non-resident unitholders, subject to any applicable tax treaty. A ULC's unlimited-liability pass-through characterization should be supported by independent Canadian tax and corporate law opinions to prevent mischaracterization of trust distributions for withholding and bank KYC purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline for export proceeds surrender or any mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters and trust entities may retain, convert, or hedge foreign currency proceeds freely. Reporting obligations that may apply in relation to cross-border funds include FINTRAC large-cross-border-currency-transaction reports (CAD 10,000 or equivalent within 24 hours), electronic funds transfer reporting thresholds, and mandatory beneficial-ownership records, but these are not exchange-control surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally chartered banks under the Bank Act (Canada); no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration exists because Canada maintains a fully liberalized capital-account regime

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with structural constraints. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not authorized deposit-taking institutions in Canada and generally cannot offer Canadian personal or corporate deposit accounts. A cross-border EMI may engage in payment facilitation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act as a money services business, or under the Retail Payment Activities Act as a retail payment service provider, but such entities must be registered and typically require a Canadian Schedule I bank sponsor for settlement. For an Alberta ULC/REIT, a foreign EMI is viable only as a narrow disbursement channel (e.g., unitholder payout aggregation) and must not hold capital escrow balances, real estate purchase funds, or tax withholdings. Primary cash management and all inbound capital injections should be routed through tier-1 domestic commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from foreign members or unitholders should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the Alberta ULC's Schedule I bank account. Canadian-dollar settlement is effected through Lynx, the Bank of Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement system, for large payments; ordinary amounts settle through Payments Canada's retail electronic funds transfer rails. SWIFT Field 70 should state 'Capital contribution to CA-AB ULC REIT' or 'Subscription for units in REIT ULC', and the transaction narrative should clearly identify the source of funds and preclude misclassification as a loan or commercial sale. Canada does not maintain an IBAN system; correspondent instructions must use the Canadian clearing code (institution number and transit number) plus SWIFT BIC of the beneficiary bank. For USD inbound capital, the wire may be sent via the Canadian bank's US clearing correspondent (Fedwire routing number) or via SWIFT MT103, with the beneficiary account number and a statement that the credit is a capital contribution, not a trade receivable. The bank may request underlying subscription agreements, board resolutions, and unitholder identity documents for regulatory reporting.Outbound Canadian-dollar disbursements for operating expenses, property management, construction, and unitholder distributions are cleared via Payments Canada retail EFT (ACH equivalent) or Lynx for time-critical large-value payments. Cross-border USD or other multi-currency trade disbursements use SWIFT MT103 for supplier payments, service fees, and distributions, with commercial letters of credit (documentary credits governed by UCP600, SWIFT MT700/MT710 issuance, and MT730 acknowledgements) for real estate acquisition, construction contracts, or goods-related cross-border transactions requiring title and inspection documents. Dividend and REIT distribution wires to unitholders in the United States may be routed through cross-border correspondent networks, US Fedwire/CHIPS for same-day settlement, or US ACH for retail unitholder payouts, subject to Canadian withholding tax and tax information reporting.
