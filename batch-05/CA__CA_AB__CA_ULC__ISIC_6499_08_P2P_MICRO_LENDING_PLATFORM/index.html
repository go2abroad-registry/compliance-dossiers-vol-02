Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE. Provincial tax: Alberta Treasury Board and Finance (Tax and Revenue Administration), which is administered by the CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement; therefore Alberta corporate income tax is filed with the single federal T2 return.

E-government filing portal

Step 2: Register with the CRA through Business Registration Online or Form RC1 to obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN). Step 3: Activate program accounts: RC0001 for corporation income tax, RT0001 for GST/HST, and RP0001 for payroll. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian income tax): the Alberta ULC is a deemed resident corporation under Income Tax Act subsection 250(4) and is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income at the 15% federal Part I general corporate rate plus the 8% Alberta general corporate rate. Territorial_Scope: Canada applies worldwide taxation with foreign tax credit relief; no tax-exempt zone applies to this entity in Alberta. Pass_Through_Eligible (foreign classification): the ULC is an eligible entity under US Treasury Regulation section 301.7701-3 and may elect pass-through treatment on IRS Form 8832, creating hybrid status subject to the Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (ITA sections 18.2 to 18.31, effective for fiscal periods beginning on or after July 1, 2022) and the fiscally transparent entity rule in Article IV(7) of the Canada-US tax treaty.

Available election forms

US check-the-box election (IRS Form 8832) for foreign pass-through classification of the ULC; ITA subsection 85(1) rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of property to the ULC; ITA section 216 election for non-resident corporations receiving Canadian rental income to file on a net basis in lieu of Part XIII withholding; GST/HST elections under Excise Tax Act sections 156 and 157 for closely related corporations (combined filing and nil-consideration elections); documented reliance on the hybrid mismatch carve-outs under ITA section 18.21 for ULC structures where foreign tax follows entity-level treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold). Non-residents carrying on business in Canada must register above the threshold; registration is voluntary below it, and once registered, GST must be charged on all taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V when goods are exported in accordance with the Export and Import of Goods regulations and proof of export under CRA Memorandum 7-2-1 is retained. Services: zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 applies when the recipient is a non-resident and the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada or to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. Financial services inherent to the P2P micro-lending platform are exempt supplies under Schedule V, Part VII, and are neither zero-rated nor subject to GST; no input tax credits are recoverable on exempt supplies. Only separate, non-financial technology or data services supplied to non-residents may qualify for zero-rating under section 7.

  • Bill of Lading or Waybill evidencing Canadian port of loading and foreign destination

No real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by registrants with annual gross revenue above CAD 300,000, using GST/HST NETFILE, XML/JSON APIs, or My Business Account; T2 corporate returns must be e-filed by corporations with gross revenue above CAD 1 million. Canada is implementing the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (PEPPOL-based) with phased federal B2G procurement mandates; B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary.

CRA reassessment authority is four years, unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Deficient export evidence or diversion of exported goods or services back to Canada triggers clawback of zero-rated treatment and assessment of 5% GST on the full consideration, plus prescribed-rate arrears interest and late-remittance penalties of 6% of unremitted amounts. Civil false-statement penalties apply at the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (ETA section 285); gross negligence and criminal tax evasion (ETA section 327) carry fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Director liability (ETA section 323) and, uniquely for the ULC, Alberta statutory shareholder unlimited liability expose executives and shareholders personally to CRA collection. Export proceeds not repatriated within the 180-day benchmark are treated as high-priority audit red flags and may trigger transfer-pricing and foreign-accrual recharacterization; the governing GST requirement is contemporaneous third-party evidence of export and payment.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains no exchange-control regime; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and Canada fully honours IMF Article VIII current-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar floats freely, and there are no restrictions or approvals required for capital-account transactions, non-resident investment, or profit remittances. For the Alberta ULC, inbound equity capital, loan funding, and interest repayments are freely transferable in CAD or foreign currency; the only frictions are (i) FINTRAC reportable-transaction and electronic-funds-transfer reporting for amounts at or above CAD 10,000, (ii) the Investment Canada Act net-benefit review if a foreign investor acquires control of a Canadian business above statutory thresholds, and (iii) Canadian non-resident dividend withholding tax (statutory 25% base; bilaterally reduced, e.g., 5–15%) where remittances are structured as dividends — the ULC's pass-through eligibility together with return-of-capital distributions materially reduces such leakage.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation obligations for export receipts or cross-border service income in Canada. A Canadian entity may retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds, whether in domestic foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks or offshore. The only compliance hooks are the Income Tax Act foreign-currency translation rule (translation at the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate for computing Canadian taxable income), FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting for any single cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, and the general bank AML/KYC documentary requirements. For the P2P platform, loan principal collections and interest income in USD or other currencies may be accumulated and swept without any surrender or conversion ratio applying.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy is administered under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act, with the Bank of Canada holding the sole statutory authority to intervene in Canadian-dollar exchange markets; operational AML/CFT surveillance of cross-border value transfers is vested in the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary banking substrate; medium as a supplementary settlement rail. Under the Bank Act, only authorized banks may accept on-demand deposits to the public's use; a foreign electronic money institution (EMI) cannot hold Canadian-dollar deposits for Canadian businesses. A non-resident EMI must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC before offering transfer or payout services into Canada, must appoint a Canadian compliance officer, and is subject to the PCMLTFA compliance regime. Because the P2P micro-lending platform is classified as high risk, an unregulated offshore EMI should never hold the ULC's principal settlement funds; the correct architecture is a Schedule I bank as the vault and operating layer, with either an FINTRAC-registered EMI or a licensed Canadian payment processor as the alternative-rail layer for small-value disbursements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% aggregate (15% federal general + 8% Alberta general); 11% aggregate CCPC small-business rate on first CAD 500,000 of active business income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends paid or credited by a Canadian-resident ULC to non-resident shareholders (s. 212(1) and (2) ITA), including deemed dividends on a reduction of paid-up capital and on redemption of shares.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline is no later than 6 months after the corporation's fiscal period-end (s. 150(1) ITA). A December 31 year-end return is due June 30. Instalments: monthly payments (12 per year) are required where federal/provincial Part I tax payable for either the current or preceding year exceeds CAD 3,000; the first instalment is due on the last day of the first month after the fiscal period begins.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory date as the federal T2 — the Alberta return is due no later than 6 months after fiscal year-end; instalments are aligned with federal monthly instalment cycle

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Canadian TP documentation is administratively required once the total annual value of transactions with any one non-arm's-length non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000 for goods/services and CAD 5,000,000 for intangible property and financing arrangements; s. 247(4) penalty relief mandates that records/documents be prepared before the T2 filing deadline and the CRA must be provided access within 3 months of written demand.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not codify an ESR law, but CRA applies the s. 245 general anti-avoidance rule, the hybrid-mismatch rules (ss. 18.2/18.6), thin-capitalization limits (s. 18(4): 1.5:1 debt-to-equity for non-resident shareholders after 2017), and the Canada-US LOB clause to strip any tax benefit lacking economic substance. An Alberta ULC used merely as a check-the-box conduit — no Alberta staff, no underwriting algorithm maintenance, no physical decision-making — will fail GAAR and treaty benefits, triggering recharacterization and Part XIII reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s. 239(1) ITA: a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment for up to 2 years, plus forfeitures; civil s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax and CRA interest compounding daily. FINTRAC referral escalates cases to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unremitted Part XIII and payroll source-withholding debts under s. 227.1 ITA unless they exercised the degree of care/diligence of a reasonably prudent person; courts will pierce the ULC's veil as a matter of Alberta law, and those convicted face civil restitution, asset freezes, departure-clearance certificates under s. 226 restricting international travel, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalization Abuses

Statutory sanction: An upward adjustment under s. 247(2) plus a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the adjustment amount (s. 247(3)); denied interest deductions under s. 18(4)/ss. 18.2/18.6; recharacterization of hybrid royalties as dividends subject to 25% Part XIII WHT; double-counting penalties under the GAAR 25% tax-benefit penalty (s. 245(5)).

Director personal liability: The CRA can assess penalties against the ULC and asses directors as co-conspirators for 'representative' liability; Alberta courts may impose personal restitution where the director knowingly signed false transfer-pricing certificates; persistent failure can support a criminal tax-evasion referral and travel-blocading Ministerial certificates.
AML / FINTRAC and Lending-Licence Failure

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per contravention for individuals and higher for entities; knowing contraventions constitute an indictable offence punishable by a fine of up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years for officers/directors; FINTRAC registration may be suspended, and Alberta's consumer-protection regulator may revoke the licence, effectively killing the platform.

Director personal liability: AML regime exposes directors personally: senior officers are statutorily responsible for ensuring a compliant compliance program; failure to appoint a Compliance Officer with reporting authority is an automatic violation; personal criminal fines, imprisonment, and BC/AB civil asset-recovery proceedings may follow.
