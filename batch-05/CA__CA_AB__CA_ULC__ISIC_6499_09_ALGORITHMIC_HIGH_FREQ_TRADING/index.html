Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portals: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO). Alberta corporate income tax is administered federally by the CRA under the Alberta Tax Collection Agreement (Alberta Corporate Tax Act, RSA 2000, c A-15); there is no separate Alberta corporate tax registration.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the unlimited liability company (ULC) under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta), RSA 2000, c B-9, s 15, filing articles that impose unlimited shareholder liability; obtain the Alberta Corporate Access Number. Step 2: Complete a single registration with the CRA via BRO (online), by phone at 1-800-959-5525, or by filing Form RC1, to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) which is the statutory TIN for federal Part I tax, Alberta corporate tax, and GST/HST; open program accounts for GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM) only if required. Step 3: Foreign-controlled ULCs must satisfy the ABCA Canadian-residency requirement for directors (at least 25% of the board, minimum one, must be resident Canadians). Step 4: Elect the fiscal year-end and file Form T2 within six months of year-end; a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Alberta with a permanent establishment there must file a T2 and may claim relief under the Canada-US Tax Convention.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (domestic Income Tax Act: an Alberta ULC is a resident corporation taxed on worldwide income under Part I, with provincial income allocated to Alberta at the 8% general rate). Pass_Through_Eligible solely for US-resident members under Article IV(6) of the Canada-US Tax Convention (fiscally transparent look-through entity) and under US check-the-box principles because shareholders bear unlimited liability; proprietary algorithm high-frequency trading income generally constitutes a specified investment business, precluding the small business deduction.

Available election forms

Available elections: (1) Canada-US Treaty Article IV(6) look-through election, implemented by notice of reliance appended to the ULC T2 return, with a supporting US Form 8832 entity classification election (the ULC is a foreign eligible entity with default partnership or disregarded-entity treatment for US purposes); (2) ETA s 156 election for closely related corporations to treat intercompany supplies of the HFT group as made for nil consideration for GST/HST; (3) selected listed financial institution special attribution method election under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act, where the ULC is a listed financial institution; (4) no provincial check-the-box analog exists, and hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss 18.4, 18.5, 20.1) operate automatically without election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters of worldwide taxable supplies (ETA s 148 small-supplier threshold). Exempt financial supplies do not count toward the threshold; however, a listed financial institution, including a securities dealer whose principal business is trading in financial instruments such as an HFT market-making ULC, cannot be a small supplier and must account for GST/HST under the special attribution method for selected listed financial institutions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% rating under ETA Schedule VI, Part V where the supplier sells and physically exports the goods with delivery outside Canada within the prescribed time. Services: Schedule VI, Part V zero-rates services supplied to non-resident persons for consumption wholly outside Canada, excluding services relating to Canadian real property or personal services rendered in Canada; financial services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part VII. For proprietary HFT, principal trading gains and market-making spreads are exempt or outside the GST/HST net; execution rebates and platform fees are zero-rated only when supplied to non-resident counterparties evidenced by offshore-delivery and non-resident-status records.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A or G13 EDI acceptance record)
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill / Cargo Control Document evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice and exchange settlement statements (SWIFT MT103 / DvP settlement advice) evidencing non-resident consideration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice proving receipt of export proceeds

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-based e-invoicing regime exists in Canada; the CRA is progressively adopting the Pan-Canadian Peppol BIS 3.0 e-invoicing framework on a voluntary basis only. Tax invoices must contain the prescribed content under ETA s 169(1) (supplier name and BN, date, recipient identity, description, consideration, and GST rate). Registrants with annual taxable supplies of at least CAD 1,000,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically (NETFILE) and remit electronically where mandated.

CRA may reassess and claw back zero-rated refunds with arrears interest (ETA s 296) and impose a 25% false-statement or gross-negligence penalty on the refund improperly claimed (ETA s 280); ITA s 163(2) imposes a 50% penalty on understated tax; criminal evasion is punishable by fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment (ETA s 327; ITA s 239); the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s 245) and Treaty Article 29(9) principal-purpose limitation apply to artificial ULC look-through structures; directors are jointly and severally liable for collected or unremitted GST/HST (ETA s 323). Although the ETA prescribes no codified 180-day repatriation rule, CRA auditors apply the 180-day benchmark as an evidentiary presumption in export zero-rating and refund audits.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full, unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital accounts; the Canadian dollar is a freely floated G10 currency and no advance central-bank approval is required for dividends, interest, management fees, capital returns, or liquidation proceeds. Profit-remittance hurdles are exclusively fiscal and administrative: a general 25% Part XIII (Income Tax Act) withholding tax on payments to non-residents, treaty-reduced to 5% for dividends paid to a qualifying US corporate parent holding at least 10% of the ULC, and to 0% for arm's-length interest under the Canada-US Treaty, each requiring a completed Form NR301 or W-8BEN-E; CRS/FATCA reporting; and transfer-pricing documentation (Form T106) for non-arm's-length intercompany funding. Alberta imposes no provincial exchange-control overlay, and non-resident ownership of an unlimited liability corporation is expressly permitted. A material nuance is that the ULC remains a Canadian corporation subject to the full Income Tax Act even when a US shareholder elects 'disregarded entity' treatment for US purposes, so the bank's withholding, reporting, and CRS records must cite the ULC's Canadian CRA business number rather than the US EIN alone.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio: a resident exporter, including a proprietary algorithmic high-frequency trading entity earning securities-related income from non-resident counterparties, may retain, invest, and repatriate 100% of export receipts in any currency. The only binding obligations are (i) FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting for every single incoming or outgoing wire equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000; (ii) FINTRAC large-cash-transaction reporting and the declaration of physical import/export of currency at CAD 10,000; and (iii) Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting used exclusively for macroeconomic statistics. For the Alberta ULC, USD-denominated market-making proceeds held in a USD sub-ledger are free balances; the de facto 'surrender discipline' is the entity's internal treasury policy on converting USD to CAD for tax instalments, operating disbursements, and margin calls, not any statutory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority and holds the settlement accounts underlying the Lynx high-value payment system; Canada, however, operates no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration. Exchange controls were fully abolished in 1951, leaving a liberalised regime in which the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially supervises banks, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces cross-border transactional reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium as a supporting disbursement channel, Low as the primary clearing hub. Offshore digital EMIs (UK FCA-authorised, EU PSD2 institutions, and US fintech banks) cannot obtain Lynx or CDSX settlement membership and therefore cannot clear CAD market-making cash legs, margined derivatives, or delivery-versus-payment obligations. As a secondary rail, an overseas EMI may handle small-value vendor, colocation, and director disbursements, but the legal constraints are material: the EMI must either hold FINTRAC money-services-business registration or avoid touching Canadian-source funds; every EFT at or above CAD 10,000 triggers FINTRAC reporting and increases de-risking pressure from the ULC's principal Schedule I bank; and the ULC's AML/ATF programme must contractually oblige the EMI to apply sanctions and PEP screening with audit rights. The core treasury and clearing account must remain at an OSFI-regulated Schedule I bank.
