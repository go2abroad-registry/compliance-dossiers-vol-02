Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST Program and Income Tax Directorate; electronic account administration and filing via the CRA My Business Account (MyBA) portal and Represent a Client (RaC) service for authorized third-party representatives.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta), the Alberta corporate registry automatically provisions a federal Business Number (BN), a 9-digit CRA statutory tax identifier with program account suffixes. The ULC must activate its GST/HST program account (suffix 'RT') through MyBA or by filing Form RC1A; where no BN was auto-generated, the corporation must apply via Form RC1 (Business Number Registration) or the CRA Online Business Registration service. A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business or making taxable supplies in Canada must procure a BN by filing Form RC1 or registering online before filing GST/HST returns, claiming refunds, or remitting payroll source deductions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Alberta ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under the federal Income Tax Act, but it is eligible for fiscally transparent pass-through treatment at the shareholder level where its US owners file an IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election treating the ULC as a disregarded entity; subsection 250(5.1) of the Income Tax Act nonetheless deems the ULC resident in Canada for Canadian tax purposes unless the enumerated foreign-entity exception is satisfied.

Available election forms

Available elections: (i) US federal check-the-box election on Form 8832 to treat the Canadian ULC as a disregarded entity; (ii) hybrid-mismatch ordering rules under subsections 18.4 and 250(5.1) of the Income Tax Act governing fiscally transparent entities; (iii) GST/HST joint election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for supplies between closely related corporations; (iv) exported financial services zero-rating rules under section 23 and Schedule VI, Part VI of the Excise Tax Act for financing services supplied to non-resident borrowers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the Excise Tax Act small-supplier threshold: an Alberta-resident ULC is not required to register for GST/HST until its worldwide taxable supplies (including financing revenue, discounts, and related fees) exceed CAD 30,000 in a trailing four-quarter period, after which registration is compulsory and GST must be collected on taxable Canadian supplies. For non-residents, the 2021 digital-economy measures impose mandatory simplified-regime registration once Canadian-source supplies of digital services or related intangible property exceed CAD 30,000 in a 12-month period; non-residents carrying on business in Canada remain subject to the general nexus rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero percent GST applies to: (a) tangible goods removed from Canada under section 22 of the Excise Tax Act and Schedule VI, Part I, provided the supplier retains proof of export; (b) services supplied to a non-resident person where the service is not performed in respect of Canadian real property, tangible personal property located in Canada, or for consumption in Canada (section 23, Schedule VI, Part V); and (c) exported financial services supplied by a financial institution to a non-resident person or for use wholly outside Canada (section 23, Schedule VI, Part VI). For the ULC's revenue-based financing fund, interest, yield, discounts, and revenue-share payments sourced from non-resident digital-brand borrowers qualify for zero-rating only where the financing is advanced from Canada, the borrower is a non-resident, and the economic use of the financial service occurs outside Alberta and Canada; financing extended to Canadian-resident borrowers remains exempt as a domestic financial service and is input-tax-credit restricted rather than zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration transmitted via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for controlled goods or non-US exports valued over CAD 2,000
  • Commercial invoice describing the exported goods or the underlying financing contract and transaction schedule for zero-rated services
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Proof of Export with carrier signature and date of departure from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire and remittance records evidencing collection of export proceeds into the ULC's Canadian financial institution account within the 180-day repatriation benchmark
  • Executed financing agreements, revenue-share ledgers, and borrower non-residency certificates substantiating the offshore supply for financial-service zero-rating
  • GST/HST return schedules and line 101 / Schedule B adjustments computing the attributable zero-rated export proportion and any input tax credit apportionment

Canada maintains no mandatory continuous transaction control, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime; the CRA does not operate a pre-invoice API clearance gateway. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million (via GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or MyBA), and all supporting e-invoice records must be retained digitally for six years. Voluntary B2B e-invoicing interoperability is emerging through the Pan-Canadian Peppol infrastructure and CRA data-exchange audit standards.

CRA audits every zero-rated claim against the documented export trail; absent presentable export evidence, the zero-rating is retrospectively clawed back, GST/HST is reassessed at 5%, prescribed daily compounded interest accrues, and associated input tax credits are denied. The ordinary four-year reassessment window is extended indefinitely for misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. Civil penalties under the Excise Tax Act include section 282 failure-to-file penalties of 5% of net tax, section 285 failure-to-remit penalties of 5% (first default) and 10% (subsequent defaults), section 285.1 gross-negligence false-statement penalties of 50% of the tax understated (minimum CAD 500), and section 327 criminal fines on indictment up to CAD 100,000 with imprisonment up to five years (fines doubled for repeat offenders). Non-arm's-length financing terms may additionally be adjusted under section 247 of the Income Tax Act with corresponding transfer-pricing penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating, fully convertible Canadian dollar regime and has no modern exchange-control law comparable to SAfE/FEMA-style capital controls. Both current-account items (royalties, financing fees, interest, service payments) and capital-account items (equity injections, loan advances, redemption proceeds, dividend remittances) may flow in and out of an Alberta ULC without central bank approval and without a statutory current-account/capital-account distinction. The practical convertibility hurdles are: (i) FINTRAC transactional and Suspicious Transaction Report thresholds at CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) Investment Canada Act notification for the establishment of a new Canadian business by a WTO investor; (iii) Income Tax Act Part XIII non-resident withholding taxes on remittances of dividends, interest and royalties (Canada-US Convention treaty rates: dividends 5% for a 10%+ corporate voting shareholder, otherwise 15%; royalties generally 10%, with treaty-reduced 0% for specified copyright royalties); and (iv) FATCA/CRS registration and annual reporting where the ULC is characterized as a reporting Canadian financial institution. Profit remittance is lawful and stranded-cash risk is low if the ULC maintains a USD account and files T2 returns with contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for intra-group royalty flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriating export proceeds, no mandatory surrender of foreign currency, and no conversion ratio for export, royalty or financing receivables. An Alberta ULC may lawfully hold USD, GBP, EUR and other currencies in onshore or offshore foreign-currency accounts without converting receipts to CAD. The only binding compliance boundary is the PCMLTFA cross-border currency declaration and reporting regime for physical cash or near-cash instruments of CAD 10,000 or more, and the electronic funds transfer reporting obligation imposed on Canadian banks and money services businesses for EFTs at or above CAD 10,000. Treasury policy should nonetheless set an internal 30-day concentration-and-sweep rule for USD receivables to reduce counterparty exposure and optimize same-day conversion pricing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); the Minister of Finance (Department of Finance Canada) administers the Exchange Fund Account and the statutory currency framework, principally the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act and the Exchange Fund Account Act; prudential licensing and supervision of deposit-taking financial institutions is performed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering, financial-transaction and cross-border FX surveillance is performed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High with binding legal constraints. Foreign digital EMIs (e.g., Wise for Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury, Global Payments) may lawfully serve only as a collection or FX pass-through conduit for the Alberta ULC; they cannot provide genuine Canadian clearing/chequing capabilities unless registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) and, for retail payment services, authorized under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) within the OSFI-administered payment-service-provider regime. Legal constraint one: EMIs structured as US LLCs or UK e-money institutions often decline Canadian ULCs because of the ULC's unlimited-liability character, so the sole shareholder must execute a guarantee, or the EMI contract should be novated to a Canadian-registered subsidiary. Legal constraint two: disbursements of financing proceeds to portfolio merchants must originate from a Schedule I bank to preserve a pristine SWIFT audit trail, avoid shadow-banking characterization, and satisfy enhanced due diligence expectations. Recommended architecture: maintain the primary operational and disbursement account at RBC, TD or BMO; use the EMI exclusively as a segregated USD receivable-collection pass-through under a Canadian-law payment facilitation agreement.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium-High with binding legal constraints. Foreign digital EMIs (e.g., Wise for Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury, Global Payments) may lawfully serve only as a collection or FX pass-through conduit for the Alberta ULC; they cannot provide genuine Canadian clearing/chequing capabilities unless registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) and, for retail payment services, authorized under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) within the OSFI-administered payment-service-provider regime. Legal constraint one: EMIs structured as US LLCs or UK e-money institutions often decline Canadian ULCs because of the ULC's unlimited-liability character, so the sole shareholder must execute a guarantee, or the EMI contract should be novated to a Canadian-registered subsidiary. Legal constraint two: disbursements of financing proceeds to portfolio merchants must originate from a Schedule I bank to preserve a pristine SWIFT audit trail, avoid shadow-banking characterization, and satisfy enhanced due diligence expectations. Recommended architecture: maintain the primary operational and disbursement account at RBC, TD or BMO; use the EMI exclusively as a segregated USD receivable-collection pass-through under a Canadian-law payment facilitation agreement.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Canada federal basic Part I rate 15% after General Rate Reduction; Alberta provincial corporate rate 8%; combined effective headline rate 23% for general active business income. The 38.08% gross federal rate is abated by 10% federal abatement and reduced by the 13% General Rate Reduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For China-source dividends paid to the Canadian ULC: Chinese EIT withholding is 10% and the Canada-China DTA also provides 10%, so no source reduction is available. For Canada-source dividends paid to Chinese shareholders: Canadian Part XIII statutory rate is 25%, reduced to 10% under DTA Article 10 for a Chinese corporate beneficial owner holding at least 25% of the voting power; otherwise 15%.
