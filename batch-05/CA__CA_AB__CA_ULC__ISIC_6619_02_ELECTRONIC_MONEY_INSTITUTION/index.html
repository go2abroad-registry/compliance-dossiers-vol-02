Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible current and capital account. There are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on the repatriation of capital or profits for non-residents. Profit remittance by a Canadian Unlimited Liability Company (ULC) to a foreign shareholder is treated as a dividend distribution; Part XIII non-resident withholding tax may apply unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty. The Canadian dollar is a floating currency with deep onshore and offshore markets. Electronic money institutions face no special FX convertibility hurdles beyond standard reporting and AML obligations for large cross-border transfers and suspicious transaction reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or conversion ratio exists in Canada. Export receipts may be held in foreign currency, converted freely, or routed through any authorized financial intermediary. Reporting obligations apply under the Currency Exchange and Cross-Border Currency Reporting provisions of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for every incoming or outgoing cross-border transfer exceeding CAD 10,000, and for large cash transactions (CAD 10,000+).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision and bank licensing also engage the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while FINTRAC supervises AML/CFT compliance for money services businesses and electronic money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI service providers and virtual IBAN platforms can facilitate settlement but cannot independently serve as the primary clearing bank for a Canadian-licensed electronic money institution. Feasibility rating: Medium. A licensed Canadian EMI must maintain a settlement deposit account at a domestic Schedule I bank or through a designated clearing agency. Offshore EMIs may be used for treasury optimization and cross-border payout distribution; however, they are subject to significant legal constraints: they are not authorized to hold Canadian client funds, they cannot provide Canadian IBANs, and any arrangements must ensure client funds remain at the domestic clearing bank in accordance with the Payment Card Networks Act and provincial money services licensing regimes. The federal Retail Payments Activities Act will also apply once fully proclaimed, requiring registration and compliance for any payments service provider with a substantive Canadian presence.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate income tax for an Alberta ULC (15% federal Part I rate plus 8% Alberta provincial corporate rate). The ULC is a Canadian-resident corporation taxed under Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 2(1) on worldwide income; no preferential rate applies because a US-parent ULC is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (no Small Business Deduction).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII tax under ITA s. 212(2) on dividends and deemed dividends (s. 84(2), s. 84(3), s. 214(3)) paid by the Alberta ULC to any non-resident shareholder, unless reduced by treaty. No Part XIII tax is imposed on dividends to a Canadian-resident corporate shareholder because such dividends are deductible under s. 112.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

T2 return due no later than 6 months after fiscal year-end (ITA s. 150(1)); balance of Part I tax due within 2 months after year-end for a non-CCPC ULC (3 months for a CCPC claiming the small business limit) (ITA s. 157); late instalments accrue interest from the instalment due date; T106/T1134 information returns due at the same time as the T2.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Complete contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Policy (Master File + Local File) and Form T106 are statutorily required where (a) gross revenue exceeds CAD 25 million and (b) aggregate non-arm's-length transactions with related non-residents exceed CAD 5 million in the taxation year (ITA s. 233.1; CRA IC-87-2R; OECD-aligned TPTP). Local File documents the Alberta ULC's functional profile, wallet-float intercompany financing, and royalty payments; Master File covers the US parent's global value chain.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) comparable to UAE/Cayman regimes, but substantive activity is enforced through: (1) corporation residence based on central management and control (de Beers principle) — a ULC whose board decisions, reserve custody, and AML functions are all performed by the US parent risks CRA reassessment as a non-resident 'sham'; (2) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA) denies tax benefits from transactions without economic substance, with a 25% penalty on the tax benefit; (3) the hybrid-mismatch rules (s. 18.4-18.6) limit double-deduction arbitrage from the US check-the-box election; (4) Financial institution and payment services licenses under the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act mandate local management substance in Canada; (5) the CRA may apply the GAAR to strip treaty benefits where the Alberta ULC lacks payroll, premises, and real local decision-making.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Indictable offence under ITA s. 239: imprisonment up to 5 years and fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded; summary conviction: up to 2 years less a day and similar fines; civil gross negligence penalty: 50% of the additional tax (s. 163(2)); interest accrues at the prescribed rate from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false T2 returns face criminal liability under s. 239; directors are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for all unremitted Part XIII withholdings and interest/penalties; CRA may issue a director's liability assessment even after corporate bankruptcy; criminal courts commonly impose travel restrictions, pass seizures, and bail conditions on directors pending tax evasion trials.
Shell Company / Sham and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply the sham doctrine to disregard the ULC and assess the US parent directly on Canadian-source business income; GAAR (s. 245) imposes a 25% penalty on the tax benefit plus interest; where the US parent lends funds into the ULC, s. 212.3 foreign-affiliate-dumping rules deem a dividend equal to the outstanding loan, subject to 25% Part XIII tax; s. 84(2) can characterize share redemptions as deemed dividends rather than capital returns.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize the sham structure are personally liable under s. 160(1) for assets transferred below fair value; directors can be named in GAAR penalty assessments where they exercised control over the tax-planning scheme; continued non-cooperation risks criminal search warrants under s. 231.5 and personal travel bans pending the criminal process; CRA publishes serious non-compliance names in its offshore-compliance enforcement disclosures.
AML / FINTRAC and Retail Payment Licensing Violation

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 768,688 for a first violation and up to CAD 1,537,375 for a repeat violation (per breach); Retail Payment Activities Act penalties: up to CAD 1,000,000 per contravention for a corporation; money laundering under Criminal Code s. 462.31: imprisonment up to 10 years plus discretionary fines and forfeiture of wallet reserves and corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Directors/officers convicted of money laundering offences face imprisonment up to 10 years and personal fines up to CAD 1,000,000 (first offence) or CAD 2,000,000 (repeat); FINTRAC may issue a compliance order prohibiting an individual from managing an MSB; CRA may invoke s. 231.6 to compel production of foreign bank records and s. 231.7 for search warrants; criminal courts can impose interim travel bans and require surrender of passports during AML investigations.
Transfer Pricing and T106/Information Return Default

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing penalty under s. 247(3): 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment (no cap), applied to the full adjustment; failure to file T106/T1134: CAD 100 per day up to a CAD 2,500 maximum per return; the normal 3-year reassessment window is extended by 3 years (7 years total) where T106 is not filed; CRA can also impose the 50% gross-negligence penalty if the TP deficiency is egregious, plus prescribed-rate interest from the assessment date.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false TP documentation are exposed to s. 239 criminal liability for false statements; unpaid Part XIII on deemed dividends triggered by TP adjustments (s. 214(3)) becomes a director-liability amount under s. 227.1, with 10% penalty when withholding is not remitted; CRA can obtain s. 231.6 orders against directors personally to compel production of US parent board minutes and transfer-pricing models; officers refusing to cooperate may face contempt proceedings and travel bans during the investigation.
