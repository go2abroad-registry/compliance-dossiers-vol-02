Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account portal at canada.ca; Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration for provincial tax programs (corporate income tax and GST/HST are also administered by CRA under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation obtains a statutory Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1. The BN serves as the TIN. The entity then activates program accounts for corporate income tax (account RC), GST/HST (account RT), and, if applicable, payroll (account RP). Non-resident applicants should provide certified constituent documents, a registered office address in Alberta, and director identification; CRA may apply a 1-2 week processing cycle with immediate BN issuance for online non-resident registrations where documents are in order.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign-owned Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation obtains a statutory Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1. The BN serves as the TIN. The entity then activates program accounts for corporate income tax (account RC), GST/HST (account RT), and, if applicable, payroll (account RP). Non-resident applicants should provide certified constituent documents, a registered office address in Alberta, and director identification; CRA may apply a 1-2 week processing cycle with immediate BN issuance for online non-resident registrations where documents are in order.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election exists; domestically the Alberta ULC is taxable at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. For U.S. tax purposes, the foreign member may file Form 8832 to classify the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership. Under the OECD hybrid mismatch framework, an election under Income Tax Act subsection 18.15(7) is available to prevent deduction/non-inclusion mismatches. For indirect tax, an election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act can allow closely related corporations to have single GST/HST reporting when conditions are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); registration is mandatory above this threshold and may also be required for non-residents carrying on business in Canada even below threshold, subject to CRA attestation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% GST applies where the goods are exported by the supplier or under the supplier's direction to a non-resident, and documentation proves export within the prescribed time frame. For services, a supply to a non-resident is zero-rated where the service is not rendered to an individual present in Canada and is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, subject to specific exceptions. Note: cross-border remittance and money transmission services are generally exempt financial services under Part VI of Schedule IX, not zero-rated, so GST recovery turns on input tax credits and associated exempt-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign exchange transaction record

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API clearance mandate exists in Canada. CRA requires GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million to file GST/HST returns electronically via My Business Account or EDI. Invoices must comply with the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act and the Electronic Commerce and Electronic Payment Regulations. CRA clearance is periodic and audit-based rather than real-time.

CRA may reassess within 4 years, or beyond 4 years in the case of misrepresentation or willful default. For fraudulent export zero-rating, CRA can deny the zero-rating, claw back refunds and input tax credits, impose civil penalties of 25% of the relevant amount under sections 280 and 281 of the Excise Tax Act, apply a gross negligence penalty up to 50%, charge arrears interest, and pursue criminal prosecution under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions across Alberta. No exchange-control approval, quota, or allocation procedures apply to equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend remittances, or trade settlements. Profit and capital repatriation is restricted solely by tax law, not FX law: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%; typically reduced to 15% dividends and 0% to 10% interest or royalties under applicable treaties, including the Canada-U.S. and Canada-U.K. conventions, relevant to the CA-AB ULC structure) must be withheld and remitted to the Canada Revenue Agency. The ULC remittance business operates under a floating-rate regime, with CAD clearing through the Bank of Canada-operated Lynx RTGS system and the Payments Canada ACSS batch system; no conversion certificate is required for export or remittance receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose statutory export-proceeds surrender timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation deadlines; the CA-AB ULC may retain all foreign-currency receipts in foreign-denominated accounts at authorized Canadian banks or foreign EMIs. The only applicable obligations are FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting (international transfers of CAD 10,000 or more within a 24-hour period) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and statistical balance-of-payments surveys conducted by Statistics Canada; neither obligation requires surrender or conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), supported by the Department of Finance Canada for currency and international financial policy coordination; no separate foreign-exchange control administration exists for Alberta because Canada accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and abolished exchange controls in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High as auxiliary payout rail, Low as primary settlement bank. Foreign offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Corpay cross-border offerings) cannot hold Canadian client remittance funds without becoming a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA, in force November 1, 2024, with Bank of Canada registration required by November 2025) or operating through a FINTRAC-registered domestic MSB agent. The CA-AB ULC may use a foreign EMI account for disbursement-side liquidity and to convert CAD into USD, EUR, or local payout currencies; however, statutory trust-protection principles require segregated client funds to be held in a Canadian-regulated financial institution. Use of an offshore EMI without RPAA registration creates regulatory non-compliance under both the FINTRAC regime and the Bank of Canada supervision framework; a registered Canadian PSP or Tier-1 bank settlement account remains mandatory as the principal liquidity anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident parent capital injection is routed as a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer (or MT202 COV where the ordering bank uses a correspondent cover) to the ULC's designated CAD capital escrow account at a Tier-1 Alberta bank. Field 50K must show the parent or UBO; field 57A must show the beneficiary bank BIC; field 70 must carry the OECD purpose code EQTS for equity subscription or LOAN for intercompany credit, with the invoice or share-certificate reference appended; field 71A should be SHA. No Bank of Canada or FINTRAC authorization is required, but incoming international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more must be captured under the ULC FINTRAC EFT-reporting regime and reported within the statutory window. The injection is matched to the ULC share register, and the funds settle via Lynx (large-value RTGS) or ACSS; CRS classification and Part XIII character (equity versus debt) must be determined before the wire to prevent incorrect tax stamping of the remittance.Outbound disbursement rails: (1) domestic CAD trade settlements via Payments Canada ACSS batch clearing or Lynx real-time settlement for large-value transactions; (2) cross-border remittance payouts via SWIFT MT103 with ISO purpose codes SALA (wages), REMR (personal remittance), or EDUO (education), settled through SWIFT gpi for track-and-trace and field 70 payment-detail transparency; (3) commercial import letters of credit (sight or usance) issued under UCP600 rules with the ULC as applicant, payment effected through the issuing bank's MT700 series and the negotiating bank's reimbursement claim; (4) for cash-pickup and e-wallet corridors, the ULC executes a prefunding MT103 or memo-posted transfer to the destination-country payout bank's Nostro account, or uses card-based clearing rails (Visa Direct or Mastercard Send) subject to network settlement schedules; (5) foreign-currency payouts are netted through the ULC FX conversion account and closed by the Canadian bank's automated sweeping facilities.
