Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act, acting as the federal tax administration and, for Alberta corporate income tax purposes, under the federal–provincial Tax Collection Agreement; primary digital portal is CRA My Business Account (accessible via the Canada Revenue Agency web portal).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA, together with applicable program accounts: RT (Corporate Income Tax), RP (Payroll), and GST/HST. The process is initiated by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' online, by mail, or by telephone; alternatively, the incorporation filing through the Alberta Corporate Registrar may be linked to CRA records via the Business Registration Online service. The BN serves as the statutory tax identification for GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll, and import-export accounts; the ULC must thereafter file a T2 corporate income tax return and remit GST/HST using that BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (specifically for U.S. federal check-the-box classification of a Canadian ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership); for Canadian domestic income tax purposes, the Alberta ULC is an Entity_Level_Taxation taxpayer treated as a corporation under the Canadian Income Tax Act and is subject to worldwide federal and provincial corporate income tax, modified by treaty relief and applicable dividend-taxation rules.

Available election forms

For foreign shareholders, the Alberta ULC may file U.S. Internal Revenue Service Form 8832 to elect pass-through (disregarded entity or partnership) classification for U.S. federal tax purposes. In Canada, no check-the-box regime exists; however, specified Canadian elections and protections apply, including the 'entity classification safe harbour' under the Canada-U.S. tax treaty and hybrid mismatch rules contained in sections 18.4, 18.5, and 18.6 of the Canadian Income Tax Act, which disallow deductions on certain structured arrangements involving hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (taxable supplies, including worldwide taxable supplies of goods and services made in Canada, measured over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or the current calendar quarter for small-supplier determination); below this threshold registration is generally optional.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of supplies for GST/HST purposes is available under Schedules VI and VII of the Excise Tax Act: (i) tangible personal property exported by the supplier (or by an agent/consignee) within prescribed time frames; (ii) services supplied to a non-resident where the services are performed wholly outside Canada and the intangible/benefit is enjoyed outside Canada; (iii) exempt/non-taxable supplies of financial and legal services connected to foreign litigation funding where those services otherwise qualify as zero-rated exports. For an Alberta ULC engaged in cross-border commercial litigation, zero-rating will be recognized only where documentary proof shows the service was supplied to a non-resident client for use or benefit entirely outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA ACI/Export eManifest or B3 Canada Customs Coding Form; substitute documentary proof of export for services/intangibles)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Purchase or Currency Conversion Record

Canada does not mandate real-time CRA clearance, digital fiscal stamping, or continuous transaction controls for GST/HST invoices. Electronic invoices are permitted and legally valid under the Excise Tax Act provided they contain the required prescribed information (supplier name/BN, invoice date, tax status, tax amount, and description). GST/HST returns must be transmitted electronically in prescribed circumstances using CRA NETFILE or a registered filer transmission link.

CRA export-investigation and refund-audit practice requires strict documentary substantiation; failure to prove export within the statutory period results in forced GST/HST reassessment at 5%, denial of input tax credits, and interest retroactively charged. A false or misleading statement supporting a zero-rated export claim triggers penalties under Excise Tax Act s. 280 (up to 25% of the net tax understated) and, where gross negligence is established, further penalties plus potential criminal prosecution under s. 327; the CRA also has a four-year reassessment window that extends indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud is established, with clawback of previously claimed refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized onshore current- and capital-account convertibility regime under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. The Canadian dollar is freely floating, and no administrative approval is required for dividend repatriation, loan servicing, or capital inflows. Profit remittance hurdles are fiscal rather than exchange-control based: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends applies at a statutory 25% rate, generally reduced under an applicable income tax treaty (e.g., 5% under the Canada-United States treaty for substantive corporate shareholders). An Alberta ULC is eligible for flow-through tax treatment, but distributions remain subject to standard wire clearing and FINTRAC reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists: Canada imposes zero mandatory conversion ratios and no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds or litigation fee receipts. Exporters and professional-service chambers may retain foreign-currency balances indefinitely. However, all incoming international wires, including FX conversions of cross-border fee receivables, are subject to FINTRAC reporting: a Large Transaction Report (CAD 10,000 or more) and a Cross-Border Electronic Funds Transfer Report (CAD 10,000 or more within a 24-hour period) when processed through a Canadian financial intermediary.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential banking supervision is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision and cross-border wire transaction reporting are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no separate central exchange-control agency and lists no IMF Article XIV restrictions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Low for trust funds, Medium for operational expense flows. The Bank Act restricts deposit-taking to authorized banks, federal and provincial credit unions, and licensed trust companies operating in Canada. Offshore digital banking and electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business) are not authorized to hold IOLTA/client trust funds under the Law Society of Alberta Trust Safety Rules, which require client trust money to be maintained in a Canadian chartered bank, a branch of a foreign bank authorized under the Bank Act, or a licensed Alberta credit union or trust institution. Fintech EMIs therefore serve only as ancillary settlement wallets for operational non-client expenditures and cannot act as the primary treasury rail for litigation fee disbursements or client settlement monies.

Compliant protocol: the shareholder or venture-capital sponsor originates a SWIFT MT103 from the home-country bank to the ULC's Schedule I Canadian account. The MT103 narrative must state 'Equity contribution - subscription for ULC shares, non-resident direct investment into Alberta ULC', cite IMF BPM6 category 'Equity other than investment fund shares - Direct investment' in the purpose-of-payment field, and include the ULC's exact registered name and address. The Canadian receiving bank must file the FINTRAC Large Transaction Report for CAD 10,000 or more. The corporate secretary must record the share issuance, file the applicable Alberta share-ownership returns, and annex CRA Schedule 50 (T2) shareholder information. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 500,000 for local file; CAD 50 million for master file

Economic substance scope: No comprehensive Economic Substance Regime in Canada; General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and foreign affiliate rules apply to prevent artificial structures lacking economic substance

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction: fines up to $1 million and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties for gross negligence

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes, penalties and interest; potential criminal charges and director disqualification
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of transaction value; income reassessment and interest charges

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for penalties and interest where willful misconduct
Failure to Withhold Taxes on Dividends

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to the withheld tax amount plus interest; possible criminal liability

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for the tax, penalties and interest
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Penalty taxes, potential revocation of corporate license and GAAR recharacterization

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; directors personally liable for taxes, fines and restitution
