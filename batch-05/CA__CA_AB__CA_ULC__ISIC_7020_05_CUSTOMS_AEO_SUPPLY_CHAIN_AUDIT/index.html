Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); electronic service portals: My Business Account, Represent a Client, and Business Registration Online (BRO). Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act on behalf of Alberta Treasury Board and Finance; customs and AEO-related filings are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal and the Canadian International Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation must: (1) incorporate or extra-provincially register under the Alberta Business Corporations Act (RSA 2000, c B-9) via the Alberta Corporate Registry, obtaining a Alberta corporation number; (2) apply to the CRA for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN), the statutory tax identification number, using Form RC1 or the online Business Registration Online service; (3) open a GST/HST program account by appending the 'RT' program identifier to the BN; (4) obtain an 'RM' customs program account with the CBSA for import/export reporting; (5) open an 'RP' payroll program account if employees are engaged; and (6) register for Alberta corporate income tax, which is filed through the CRA under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (domestic Canadian income tax: the ULC is a taxable corporation under the federal Income Tax Act and the Alberta Corporate Tax Act); Pass_Through_Eligible (hybrid status: the ULC's unlimited shareholder liability permits classification as a disregarded entity or partnership for US federal tax purposes under a US check-the-box election).

Available election forms

US Treasury Reg. SS 301.7701-3 check-the-box election on Form 8832 may treat the Alberta ULC as a disregarded entity for US federal tax purposes, triggering Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 12.3, 18.2, 18.3, 19.1) for taxation years beginning after 30 June 2022. Domestic elections include: section 85(1) rollover election when transferring property to the ULC on incorporation; Excise Tax Act section 273 election between closely related corporations to make taxable supplies at nil consideration; GST/HST Quick Method election and annual/quarterly filing-period elections; and the election to treat the ULC as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) where Canadian-resident control thresholds are met for the small business deduction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies (including zero-rated exported services) made in Canada within a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary GST/HST registration is immediately available to claim input tax credits on acquisition costs and to zero-rate export supplies of AEO supply-chain consulting and audit services

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of AEO certification advisory and supply-chain security audit services (ISIC 7020 / NAICS 541614) is zero-rated under section 7 of Part V of Schedule IX to the Excise Tax Act where the recipient is a non-resident, the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant, the service is not supplied in connection with real or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and the service is not performed while the recipient is physically in Canada. Zero-rating substantiation requires an executed engagement contract, a commercial invoice denominated in foreign currency, evidence of the client's non-resident status (foreign tax ID or corporate registry extract), workpapers evidencing delivery of the audit outside Canada, and bank records showing receipt of funds from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form G13 or e-manifest filed through the Canadian International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement (where goods are part of the audited supply chain)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency payment)
  • Signed engagement contract and commercial invoice identifying the non-resident recipient and the specific AEO audit deliverables

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API validation, or digital stamping for GST/HST invoices. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and operates under the Pan-Canadian B2B E-Invoicing Framework (Peppol standard, administered by the Digital Governance Standards Institute, launched 2023). CRA record-keeping rules require electronic ledger and invoice data to be retained in a complete, machine-readable, and accessible format for six years. For customs transactions, the CBSA CARM portal and the Canadian International Trade Single Window are the mandatory electronic channels for filing G13 export declarations and e-manifest data.

CRA may reassess GST/HST within the normal four-year limitation period and without limitation where misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default is established (Excise Tax Act s. 298(4); Income Tax Act s. 152(4)). Fraudulent or documentation-deficient export zero-rating claims expose the registrant to: (i) retroactive clawback of input tax credits and refunds; (ii) re-assessment of the supply at 5% GST with compound daily arrears interest at prescribed rates; (iii) a false-statement penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded under ETA s. 285; (iv) a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated federal and Alberta income tax under ITA s. 163(2); (v) criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 with fines up to CAD 100,000 on indictment and imprisonment up to five years; and (vi) CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalties under the Customs Act for inaccurate G13 export declarations. The 180-day inward-remittance benchmark is the internal strict-substantiation window adopted for this dossier; Canadian law imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, so the benchmark operates as a best-practice evidentiary cutoff to defend the zero-rating claim and AEO compliance posture.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under Canada's acceptance of IMF Article VIII obligations, with no distinction between capital and current account. Share subscriptions, intercompany advances, consulting revenues, service fees, dividends, interest, and royalties may be converted and remitted without Bank of Canada consent. For the Alberta ULC (CA_ULC), the operative hurdles are (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5% or 15% for dividends and generally 10% for royalties under the Canada-US Convention), (ii) FINTRAC reporting obligations for every cross-border electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, and (iii) Investment Canada Act notification or review for a non-Canadian shareholder establishing or acquiring a Canadian business. The unlimited-liability/pass-through status of the ULC creates no currency-law restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender obligation, no conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for repatriating export proceeds; the mandatory conversion quota is effectively 0%. Since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951, the applicable disciplines are reporting and record-keeping: an authorized customs broker or exporter must file the CBSA export declaration (B13A / G7 electronic export reporting) for goods leaving Canada, the ULC's bank must file a FINTRAC cross-border EFT report for any related receipt of CAD 10,000 or more, and export documents must be retained for six years. In the AEO supply-chain audit context, export proceeds must be reconciled with shipping and customs security records, but no proceeds are required to be converted into CAD or held in Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (French: Banque du Canada). Canada operates a statutorily unconstrained exchange system; the Bank of Canada issues currency and manages monetary policy, while cross-border banking supervision is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). No foreign-exchange administration issues convertibility or remittance permits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity funding to the Alberta ULC requires no exchange-control licence or central-bank purpose code. The compliant routing is: (i) the parent transmits a SWIFT MT103 (with MT202 cover where necessary) to the ULC's Schedule I bank account, using tag 70 narrative 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / SHARE SUBSCRIPTION — CA-AB ULC' and avoiding any false goods description; (ii) if the transfer is funded in USD, the receiving bank performs the CAD conversion at its daily dealing rate; (iii) the bank must file a FINTRAC cross-border EFT report because the amount typically exceeds CAD 10,000; (iv) the ULC must verify the sender against its expected-source registry and retain the wire advice and board resolution in the AML file; and (v) if the shareholder is a non-Canadian establishing a new Canadian business, the ULC should file the Investment Canada Act notification within 30 days of implementation. Intercompany loans must be evidenced by a written loan agreement, with arm's length interest to avoid Part XIII withholding or transfer-pricing adjustment.The AEO audit service model relies on non-goods payments. Standard clearing instructions are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for all cross-border vendor, subcontractor, licensing, and dividend payments; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) only for procurement of security equipment or goods with title movement, where the AEO security audit trail requires documentary compliance; (iii) domestic CAD disbursements via Payments Canada ACSS batch (FTS) for values below CAD 25 million and via Lynx for high-value transfers; (iv) USD payments through the bank's U.S. correspondent network or its U.S.-cleared ACH channel; and (v) none of the payment rails require prior government approval, but all cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more are automatically reported to FINTRAC by the originating bank. A Canadian-registered Money Service Business, such as Wise, holds a FINTRAC MSB registration and can execute low-value cross-border payments and FX conversions, but it is not a Bank Act deposit-taking institution, does not participate in Payments Canada's Lynx or ACSS clearing, is not CDIC-insured, and cannot hold the ULC's escrowed capital or tax balances. The compliance-safe use is limited to outbound vendor disbursements not exceeding CAD 10,000 per transaction, with the bank as the governing clearing source.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined federal and Alberta general corporate income tax rate (federal 15% plus Alberta 8%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII on gross dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, unless a tax treaty reduces the rate. Under the Canada-US treaty, the rate is generally 5% for a corporate beneficial owner holding at least 10% of the voting shares and 15% for shareholdings below 10%. Other Canadian DTAs commonly provide 10% to 15% portfolio dividend relief. Alberta does not impose a separate provincial dividend withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not prescribe a separate OECD-style local/master file monetary threshold. Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation must be prepared and retained for all reportable transactions under ITA s.247(4). Form T106 must be filed when the aggregate annual value of transactions with each non-arm's-length non-resident exceeds C$100,000, and when loan or advance balances exceed C$1,000,000. The ULC must maintain a written analysis using the best method, accurate comparability data, and a defensible intercompany pricing conclusion before the T2 filing date.

Economic substance scope: Alberta and Canada do not operate an economic substance regulation regime comparable to offshore or Gulf jurisdictions. Instead, Canadian tax law applies the general anti-avoidance rule under ITA s.245, sham and agency doctrines, and the limitation-on-benefits provisions of tax treaties. An Alberta ULC must demonstrate real physical presence, substantive AEO audit staff, operating contracts, and strategic-management decisions in Alberta to support treaty entitlement and to defeat any characterization as a conduit or shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under ITA s.239 may result in a fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and up to five years' imprisonment for each count. Civilly, CRA may impose a 50% gross-negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2), plus interest and restitution. Criminal court may order forfeiture of assets traced to evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed, assented to, or acquiesced in the evasion can be indicted as parties to the offence. Conviction may result in imprisonment, asset seizure, and travel restrictions or court-ordered travel bans. Civil restitution may be ordered jointly and severally, effectively piercing the corporate veil.
Failure to Withhold or Remit Part XIII Tax

Statutory sanction: The payor remains liable for the full unremitted Part XIII tax, statutory interest, and penalties. CRA may also assess a 10% late-remittance penalty in addition to daily compound interest, and may collect by garnishing bank accounts and filing security against the ULC's assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for unremitted withholding tax under ITA s.227.1. CRA can issue a director liability assessment demanding personal payment; failure to pay may result in civil seizure of personal assets, court-ordered departure prohibitions, and criminal prosecution if the failure is part of a deliberate scheme.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA may re-assess the ULC's taxable income using the arm's length price. A transfer-pricing penalty under ITA s.247(3) can be imposed equal to 10% of the net adjustment amount unless the taxpayer made reasonable efforts to establish arm's length pricing and prepared contemporaneous documentation. Separate administrative penalties apply for non-filing of T106.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or signed the mispriced intercompany invoices may be liable to account for the resulting corporate tax deficiency through gross-negligence penalties. Where the ULC was used as a mere instrument of tax avoidance, the corporate veil may be pierced and personal assets applied to satisfy the deficiency; criminal transfer-pricing fraud can also trigger imprisonment.
Shell Company / GAAR / Sham

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule under ITA s.245 may deny all or part of the tax benefit. The sham doctrine may result in the underlying transactions being recharacterized, CRA assessing unreported income, and the statutory reassessment period being extended beyond the normal three-year limitation where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect or wilful default. A 50% gross-negligence penalty may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders cannot rely on the ULC's separate personality when the company is a facade or conduit. Courts will pierce the veil to impose civil restitution on the individuals who controlled the shell. Criminal liability for sham transactions may result in imprisonment, seizure of bank accounts, and travel bans pending trial.
AML / FINTRAC / Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Admin monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA may reach C$500,000 per entity and higher for repeat or egregious violations. Criminal money laundering convictions carry imprisonment up to ten years, and funds or property involved may be frozen and forfeited. FINTRAC non-compliance penalties are assessed separately from Revenue Canada tax penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct, authorize, or participate in foreign-exchange structuring or sanctions evasion are personally exposed to criminal charges. The corporate veil does not protect against money-laundering offences; conviction may result in long imprisonment, loss of professional standing, and post-sentence travel bans.
