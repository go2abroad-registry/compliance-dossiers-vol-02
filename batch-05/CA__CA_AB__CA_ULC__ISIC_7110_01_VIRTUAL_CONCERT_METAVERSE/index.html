Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions; no capital controls restrict inbound equity subscription, outbound dividend remittance, or repatriation of invested capital for an Alberta ULC. Greenfield entity formation and de minimis capital injections are exempt from Investment Canada Act review (only control-acquisition thresholds trigger notification/review). Profit remittance is untrammelled but subject to Part XIII withholding tax: dividends 25% statutory (reduced to 5% under the Canada-US Tax Convention for a US-incorporated shareholder owning at least 10% of voting shares, provided treaty-entitlement and ULC conduit conditions are documented); interest on arm's-length non-participating debt is exempt; royalties 25% statutory with treaty-reduced rates as applicable. No CAD retention, repatriation deadline, or conversion margin applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no restriction on retention of foreign-currency export proceeds; virtual performance hosting and digital ticketing receipts qualify as services exports and may be retained indefinitely in USD or other foreign currency accounts at any Schedule I Canadian bank or offshore. The only obligations are PCMLTFA record-keeping and FINTRAP electronic funds transfer (EFT) reporting for cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more per instruction, plus standard anti-money-laundering client identification on the originating and receiving accounts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central monetary authority and Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) oversight body; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of Schedule I banks under the Bank Act (Canada); FINTRAP as AML/CTF intelligence unit under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration; exchange controls were abolished in 1951 and Canada has maintained IMF Article VIII status since 1952. Foreign-exchange dealing in CAD is limited to Schedule I/II banks and authorized FX dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: MEDIUM as a settlement supplement, LOW as a primary treasury rail. Canada has no statutory 'EMI' licensing category; offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) operate only as FINTRAP-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and cannot accept deposits, issue Canadian bank accounts, or provide credit under the Bank Act. A Canadian ULC may use a FINTRAP-registered PSP (e.g., Stripe Canada) for ticket-payout rails and a securities-registered crypto platform for USDC conversion, but the capital escrow, USD/CAD FX forwards, and Lynx RTGS clearing must sit at a Schedule I bank. Contractual constraint: the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime with the Bank of Canada is being phased in over 2025-2026; all PSP agreements must contain an RPAA-compliance representation and data-residency covenant.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: investor remits via SWIFT MT103 in USD to the ULC's segregated USD capital escrow account at a Schedule I bank, with field 70 narrative stating 'capital contribution - subscription for Alberta ULC common shares' and balance-of-payments classification as equity capital. The receiving bank screens against UN/OSFI/OFAC lists; any EFT of CAD 10,000 or more triggers FINTRAP reporting. Funds are then either (i) retained in a USD sub-account and converted to CAD at maturity via a deliverable USD/CAD FX forward, or (ii) converted immediately at the bank's daily rate with settlement through Lynx RTGS (the Canadian high-value payment system operated by Payments Canada). Verification evidence comprises SWIFT MT103 confirmation, FX deal ticket, board resolution, and updated shareholder register; keeping a CAD-denominated operational ledger for downstream project spend eliminates re-FX leakage on payouts.Clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border artist, vendor, and technology-licence royalty payments from the CAD operational account, with the Canadian paying bank performing Part XIII non-resident withholding tax remittance and NR4 information-return filing when the payee is a non-resident; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600, advised/confirmed by the Schedule I bank) for high-value content-licensing and metaverse-infrastructure procurements; (iii) Lynx RTGS same-day CAD wires for domestic Canadian counterparties; (iv) Stripe/PSP payout rails (ACH, SEPA, card-rail) for ticket proceeds to artists in local currency; (v) USDC-ticketing flows are converted by a securities-registered crypto platform and swept as CAD into the operational bank account daily to eliminate open crypto-asset exposure.Deliverable OTC FX Forwards (USD/CAD) executed with a Schedule I bank under ISDA 2002/CSA, maturities 1-24 monthsFX Swaps (tom-next / spot-next rollovers) for settlement-date and funding-date mismatches on ticket revenueCross-Currency Swaps (USD/CAD, fixed-fixed or fixed-floating) for matched long-dated deployment of capital-escrow balancesVanilla Currency Options (USD/CAD calls and puts) for contingent hedging of variable virtual-concert ticket revenuesCLS-Eligible Spot and Forward Settlement through CLS Bank where both counterparties are CLS membersNDF (Non-Deliverable Forward) - not applicable to the core CAD exposure because CAD is fully convertible and deliverable; permitted only as an overlay hedge for a receivable leg denominated in a non-convertible currency payable by a foreign metaverse licensee
