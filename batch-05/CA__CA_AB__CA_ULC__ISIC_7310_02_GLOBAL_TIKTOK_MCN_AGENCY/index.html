Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses.html

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Canada/ULC branch or corporation must first complete federal incorporation or extra-provincial registration with Corporations Canada (or the Alberta Corporate Registry for AB-ULC), then obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) via online registration or Form RC1. The BN serves as the GST/HST registration number, corporate income tax account, payroll, and import/export account. Non-resident corporations that carry on business in Canada and have Canadian-source revenues must apply for a BN before commencing activities. There is no separate TIN; the 9-digit BN plus two-letter program account code (e.g., RT for GST/HST) is used. ULCs with unlimited liability are treated as corporations under Canadian tax law and are subject to the same BN process. Registration must be completed within 30 days of becoming a registrant. Supporting documents include certificate of incorporation, articles, proof of registered office address, and a completed Form RC464 for non-resident businesses if applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (ULC is taxable as a corporation under the Canadian Income Tax Act; despite its unlimited liability, it is not a pass-through entity for Canadian federal/provincial corporate income tax purposes. For U.S. federal tax purposes, the ULC may be treated as an entity eligible for check-the-box classification, but this does not alter its Canadian tax status.)

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election exists; Canadian tax classification follows legal form. A ULC is mandatorily a corporate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act. However, an Alberta ULC may make an election under section 216(1) to file on a net basis as a non-resident if carrying on business in Canada. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Canada applies OECD hybrid mismatch rules per Section 815, which may deny deductions for payments involving hybrid entities, such as a ULC deemed to be a disregarded entity in another jurisdiction. The ULC's unlimited liability does not afford any Canadian tax transparency election. A ULC can also elect under subsection 256.1(1) to treat a wholly owned subsidiary as a branch for tax purposes in limited inter-corporate scenarios, but this is not analogous to check-the-box.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated and certain financial services) triggers mandatory GST/HST registration. An entity may voluntarily register before exceeding the threshold; non-resident corporations carrying on a business in Canada and earning GST taxable income must register regardless of threshold if not a 'small supplier'. For non-resident digital economy suppliers, including MCN/advertising services, the threshold applies to Canadian-source digital supplies unless an election is made under the simplified non-resident registration framework (Form GST494).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST) applies to exports of goods from Canada (provided the goods are physically removed from Canada within a reasonable time and will not be resold in Canada) and to certain services performed on exported goods. For advertising and MCN agency services supplied to non-residents, zero-rating generally applies under section 23(1) of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the service is delivered to a recipient who is a non-resident of Canada and is not required to be used in Canada. Advertising services are zero-rated when supplied to non-residents except where the supply relates to advertising to a Canadian audience or is performed in a manner that would have been part of the advertiser's taxable business in Canada. For TikTok creator agency services, the key test is whether the service is supplied to a non-resident (e.g., TikTok entity) for use and consumption exclusively outside Canada; if any element is used in Canada, the supply may be taxable at 5%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CAED) filed through CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System (CES), required for exports of goods with a value exceeding CAD 2,000 or controlled/prohibited goods; for services no customs declaration is filed but commercial invoices and service agreements are required.
  • Bill of Lading (through ocean, air, or rail carrier; for digital services a signed contract and delivery record / server access logs are acceptable in lieu of transport documents)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/Fintech payment proof (e.g., Wise B2B confirmation or TikTok Creator Payout summary) evidencing funds received from outside Canada in the exporter's Canadian account

Canada does not mandate a real-time domestic e-invoicing clearance/OCR system comparable to Latin American countries. GST/HST registrants must issue periodic prescribed information in a retrievable electronic or paper format. CRA accepts electronic invoices for GST/HST purposes if the invoice contains all required information (vendor BN, date, tax rate, tax amount, amount). Digital stamping or tax authority clearance is not required. Reporting is generally done through tax returns filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE; mandatory electronic filing applies to most registrants with annual revenues above CAD 10,000. While there is no pre-clearance API, CRA's Business Gateway API permits machine-to-machine filing of GST/HST returns and remittances, and participants in the GST/HST post-audit environment are expected to maintain structured audit files (GIFI).

CRA and CBSA may jointly audit zero-rated export claims. Where documentation fails to establish physical export or direct foreign consumption, CRA will reassess the supply as taxable at 5%, apply interest and penalty under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act (net failure penalty of up to 6% of unpaid tax, plus a repeated-failure penalty of 8% and 4% per year compounding). Additionally, intentional misrepresentation for zero-rating may lead to a 13% civil penalty under section 285.1, and repeated serious violations constitute GST evasion subject to criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act or Excise Tax Act with fines of up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for intentional falsification of records. Exporters must retain all substantiation in English or French for six years from the end of the audit period. If export rehearing proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days due to foreign-exchange controls, the exporter may be required to accrue revenue in Canadian dollars and may face a 10% surtax on unremitted income under ITA section 17, unless a deferral election is obtained.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully and freely convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime; there are no capital-account controls on inbound equity injections, dividend repatriation, or liquidation proceeds. Current-account transactions, including creator payouts and cross-border service fees, are unrestricted. Profit remittance is governed solely by tax at the point of outflow: 25% Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) on dividends paid to non-residents, typically reduced under an applicable treaty (e.g., 5% under the Canada–US Treaty for a corporate owner of at least 10% voting shares). No regulatory authorization is required for remittance; only FINTRAC cross-border electronic-funds-transfer reporting applies at CAD $10,000 or above.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation period for export receipts or foreign-currency service revenues. The Alberta ULC may retain USD and other foreign currency in resident or non-resident banking accounts indefinitely. The only applicable obligations are Canadian income-tax recognition of foreign-exchange gains and losses under sections 39 and 95 of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and standard financial-reporting rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); foreign-investment screening under the Investment Canada Act administered by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High as a settlement adjunct; Medium as a primary treasury bank. Wise Business (Wise Canada Inc., registered as a Money Service Business with FINTRAC) and Payoneer provide multi-currency account equivalents and low-cost local-rail creator payouts using SWIFT-equivalent routing. Legal constraints: any non-bank EMI must be FINTRAC-registered; EMIs do not carry Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation protection, offer limited credit and FX-hedging product suites, and are generally unsuitable for holding the ULC's capital-escrow subscription account. Recommended use: sub-CAD $10,000 creator payouts and volatile-currency disbursements only.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No general statutory audit applies to a non-reporting private corporation under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta). A mandatory independent audit attaches only if: (i) the corporation is a reporting issuer or offers securities to the public; (ii) it operates as a bank, trust company, insurance company, or other regulated financial institution; (iii) shareholders holding at least 5% of the issued voting shares demand an audit at an annual meeting; or (iv) the articles or a unanimous shareholders agreement require an audit. A private ULC creator-MCN agency is therefore ordinarily subject only to a review or agreed-upon-procedures engagement unless a covenant or shareholder resolution raises the standard.
