Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and federal/Alberta corporate income tax administration; Alberta corporate income tax is collected by CRA under the Tax Collection Agreement. Portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) via canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) before registering for GST/HST and corporate income tax. Procedure: (1) file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' electronically through CRA Business Registration Online or by paper; (2) attach the Alberta incorporation/attestation certificate, registered office information, directors and authorized persons, non-resident owner details, and estimated fiscal year-end; (3) simultaneously elect program accounts for GST/HST (RT) and corporation income tax (RC); (4) if there are no Canadian resident officers or physical presence, appoint an agent under Form RC59 'Business Number Information' or Form RC84(PA) for non-residents, and file a completed Form RC1 with copies of foreign registration documents; (5) CRA issues a 9-digit BN plus program account suffixes (e.g., BN/RT0001 for GST/HST). The ULC must also register with Alberta's registry if it needs to maintain extra-provincial status, although Alberta does not impose a separate provincial tax registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign-owned Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) before registering for GST/HST and corporate income tax. Procedure: (1) file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' electronically through CRA Business Registration Online or by paper; (2) attach the Alberta incorporation/attestation certificate, registered office information, directors and authorized persons, non-resident owner details, and estimated fiscal year-end; (3) simultaneously elect program accounts for GST/HST (RT) and corporation income tax (RC); (4) if there are no Canadian resident officers or physical presence, appoint an agent under Form RC59 'Business Number Information' or Form RC84(PA) for non-residents, and file a completed Form RC1 with copies of foreign registration documents; (5) CRA issues a 9-digit BN plus program account suffixes (e.g., BN/RT0001 for GST/HST). The ULC must also register with Alberta's registry if it needs to maintain extra-provincial status, although Alberta does not impose a separate provincial tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Canadian domestic tax law the ULC is an ordinary corporation resident in Canada and is subject to entity-level tax; Canada has no check-the-box election. However, for U.S. federal tax purposes, a Canadian ULC is an eligible entity that can file IRS Form 8832 to elect classification as a disregarded entity or partnership, thereby achieving pass-through treatment. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act sections 18.4–18.7, 94, and related provisions) and the OECD BEPS Action 2 framework may recharacterize certain payments or deny deductions where the ULC's U.S. pass-through election creates a hybrid financial instrument or hybrid payee mismatch. The Canadian shareholder/branch rules do not permit a domestic fiscally transparent election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — mandatory GST/HST registration applies when worldwide taxable revenues from supplies made in Canada (and, where applicable, deemed supplies made in Canada) exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident PR firm must monitor both the annual rolling four-quarter volume and the single-quarter spike rule.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of exported goods are zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the goods are exported in accordance with the prescribed documentary and physical evidence rules. Public-relations, media-relations, and crisis-communication services supplied to a non-resident client are zero-rated under paragraph 7(a), Part V, Schedule VI where the recipient is a non-resident and the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada and is not a supply of services rendered to tangible personal property located in Canada at the time the service is performed. To claim 0% GST/HST, the ULC must retain contemporaneous contracts, invoices, proof of non-resident status, and evidence that the services were performed/consumed outside Canada or in furtherance of the non-resident's business outside Canada.

  • Commercial invoice and detailed description of PR services or exported goods
  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or equivalent electronic export report/e-manifest (Canada Border Services Agency R-coded release) for goods
  • Bill of Lading or transport document (marine bill of lading, airway bill, truck/courier waybill) evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Proof of export and delivery, including customs broker confirmation or CBP export data where applicable
  • Inward remittance certificate, bank confirmation, SWIFT MT103, or foreign-exchange payment evidence showing receipt of consideration from the non-resident client within the prescribed period

Canada does not operate a national real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime. GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed invoices under section 223 of the Excise Tax Act showing the seller's Business Number, invoice date, description of supply, total consideration, and GST/HST charged. Electronic invoices and e-records are accepted, but CRA does not require prior approval or API filing. CRA requires electronic GST/HST return filing in specified circumstances and for tax preparers with more than a de minimis filing volume; Alberta has no additional provincial fiscal clearance requirement.

CRA may deny the 0% rate and reassess GST/HST, interest, and penalties if the ULC fails to maintain adequate export documentation or if the services are found to be consumed/rendered in Canada. If a false statement, misrepresentation, or wilful evasion is established, the CRA may apply the penalty under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act of 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded (or CAD 100 if greater), plus interest and a four-year limitation extension (unlimited in fraud cases). Criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment. In an unlimited liability corporation, shareholders also face statutory legacy liability, and CRA may pursue them personally for unpaid GST/HST and penalties. A zero-rating claim based on manufactured or sham export transactions gives rise to retrospective clawback, gross negligence penalties under the Income Tax Act, and potential criminal tax-evasion charges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a freely convertible currency under IMF Article VIII status. There is no general exchange-control statute, no current-account approval requirement, and no capital-account authorization framework for most cross-border payments. Profit remittances by dividend, management fee, or repayment of intercompany debt do not require Bank of Canada or FINTRAC prior approval. Dividend repatriation by an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) is, however, subject to corporate formalities (board resolution, solvency analysis) and, where applicable, Code subsection 212(2) or 215(1) withholding taxes plus the potential consequences of electing under subsection 216.1. Foreign ownership of a Canadian ULC is generally unrestricted unless the business is in a sector subject to Canadian ownership rules or the transaction meets the monetary thresholds requiring Investment Canada Act net benefit review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory requirement that export proceeds be surrendered to the Bank of Canada or any designated agent. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no central-Bank-approved trading window. Cross-border inbound payments for exported public-relations and communications services may be settled freely in CAD, USD, or another convertible currency. For statistical reporting, merchandise exporters are captured through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) customs declarations; service exporters are reported through Statistics Canada's International Transactions in Services survey when the firm is selected for survey coverage.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC is the monetary authority; Canada maintains no standalone Foreign Exchange Administration agency). Regulatory oversight of financial institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and cross-border transaction reporting is supervised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); portfolio and foreign-investment review oversight sits with Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada and the Investment Review Division under the Investment Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low. Foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) cannot independently hold accounts in the Canadian clearing and settlement system. A foreign EMI must either establish a Canadian money-services business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC, partner with a Canadian Schedule I bank or licensed credit union for settlement accounts, or operate as an authorized foreign bank representative office, which does not permit full deposit-taking activities. For a cross-border PR agency, a FINTRAC-registered MSB route is available but adds reporting obligations, Canada's Economic Sanctions compliance duties, and the requirement to maintain a Canadian presence. The optimal structure is to use a Canadian-tier-1 bank as the primary clearing institution and, if needed, a regulated global payments provider licensed under provincial money-services laws as a front-end collection and disbursement rail.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident directors and statutory officers residing outside Canada without verifiable physical locations, creating a regulator concern about accountability in a ULC

Root cause: Non-resident directors and statutory officers residing outside Canada without verifiable physical locations, creating a regulator concern about accountability in a ULC

Defense evidence: Provide notarized passports plus utility bills or bank statements for all directors; a centralized access-to-information protocol showing how books and records will be made available in Alberta on demand; a designated Alberta-based process agent or registered office confirmation; and a signed acknowledgment that the ULC can be dissolved only with the unanimous consent of all members and that statutory books will remain in Canada.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route for the offshore parent to inject ULC equity is a telegraphic transfer using SWIFT MT103/Customer Credit Transfer. The original message must contain the proper beneficiary account identifier (Canadian bank transit number, institution number, and CAD account number), a clean remittance information field stating 'capital contribution' or 'contributed surplus', and the ultimate originator's legal name and address. The receiving bank will perform value-dating through Payments Canada's LVTS or Real-Time Rail for high-value items. FINTRAC EFT-reporting thresholds apply ($10,000-for multiple transfers in 24 hours), and the bank will request the corroborating evidence described above. Canada does not require prior FX approval for the conversion of USD into CAD; conversion is effected by the receiving institution at a market spot rate, with the operational account credited in Canadian dollars.For trade settlement and operating disbursements, use SWIFT MT103 via a Canadian clearing bank for cross-border vendor settlements and media/trade payable; use Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary) where the overseas media placement or event agency needs payment assurance. For Canadian-dollar domestic vendors, use Payments Canada's EFT/ACH framework or Real-Time Rail. For USD payments, the bank may channel through its US clearing correspondent using Fedwire or CHIPS. Master / Local File threshold: CAD 500,000 for related-party transactions triggering local file requirements

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to File Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Penalty of $100 per day, capped at $25,000, plus accrual of interest on any unpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the penalties and interest
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 10% of the value of the non-arm's length transaction, or more in aggravated cases

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for any penalties assessed
Abusive Dividend Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the amount of improperly deducted or exempted income, plus interest

Director personal liability: Potential personal liability including fines and possible criminal charges under tax evasion provisions
Shell Company or Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of income, denial of deductions, and penalties up to 25% of the income

Director personal liability: Directors can face civil and criminal liabilities, including imprisonment for willful evasion
