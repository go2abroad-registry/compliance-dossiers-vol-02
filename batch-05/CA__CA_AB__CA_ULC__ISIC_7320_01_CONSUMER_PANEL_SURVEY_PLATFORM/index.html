Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration for GST/HST and Part I corporate income tax. Registration and filing are effected through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) gateway and the My Business Account (MyBA) portal. Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) administers the underlying ULC incorporation records; Alberta provincial corporate income tax is assessed federally by the CRA on the same corporate return under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the Alberta ULC through Service Alberta / Alberta Corporate Registry (Articles of Incorporation, NUANS name search, registered office in Alberta; non-resident ownership is permitted, though Alberta resident director/agent requirements must be verified). Step 2 — Obtain the Alberta corporate registry number, then enrol with CRA through BRO to open program accounts: corporate income tax account (RC0001), GST/HST account (RT0001), payroll deductions account (RP0001), and import/export account (RM0001) if cross-border goods movement exists. Step 3 — For a non-resident-owned ULC, complete CRA attestation of directors/officers and, if the ULC carries on business in other provinces, obtain extra-provincial registrations; the CRA Business Number (BN) is issued immediately upon BRO enrolment, with the GST/HST program account identified by the 'RT' suffix.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Alberta ULC is a 'taxable Canadian corporation' under ITA s. 2(1) and is entity-level taxed in Canada on worldwide income (residence-based territorial scope), but it remains eligible for U.S. check-the-box pass-through classification (disregarded entity or partnership) under Treasury Reg. § 301.7701-3 for U.S. federal tax purposes. No tax-exempt zone or preferential territorial regime applies; Alberta has no special economic zone or offshore tax classification for ULCs.

Available election forms

Available elections: (1) voluntary early GST/HST registration by a small supplier under ETA s. 240(2) before exceeding the CAD 30,000 threshold; (2) U.S. federal classification election on IRS Form 8832 to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for hybrid-mismatch planning (the ULC's Canadian corporate status is not overridden for Canadian tax purposes); (3) elections under the ETA input tax credit substantiation rules (e.g., all-or-nothing ITC calculation for registrants with exempt supplies) are inapplicable to a zero-rated SaaS exporter; Canada has no domestic check-the-box regime, and hybrid structuring is constrained by the GAAR (ITA s. 245), the foreign accrual property income (FAPI) regime, and the thin capitalization rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once the aggregate consideration for taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) made in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 either in a single calendar quarter or over the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters. Panelist incentive payments made by the platform are not consideration for taxable supplies and do not count toward the threshold. Voluntary registration before the threshold is permitted and is generally advisable to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST/HST applies: (A) Offshore services — Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 of the ETA zero-rates a supply of a service made to a non-resident when the non-resident is not a GST/HST registrant, the service is not rendered to a person in Canada, and the service does not relate to real property in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. A consumer-panel market-research SaaS contracted with foreign clients qualifies where the research is directed at non-Canadian markets and the service is not performed by or for Canadian-resident panellists in respect of Canadian property. If the survey panel explicitly recruits Canadian respondents or data collection occurs in Canada, that portion may be treated as rendered in Canada and taxable at 5%, so contractual scoping must clearly sever any Canadian-market deliverables. (B) Goods — ETA ss. 20-22 zero-rate goods exported by or on behalf of a non-resident, provided the goods are sold for export, shipped within a reasonable period (generally within 60 days of shipment), delivered to a destination outside Canada, and supported by prescribed evidence. Survey devices or branded merchandise shipped abroad qualify under this rule.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CEDAR export reporting via the carrier) evidencing physical exportation of goods
  • Commercial invoice / seller's export invoice stating Incoterms and foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the transporting common or private carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank IRC) or SWIFT/MT103 payment evidence showing FX repatriation from the non-resident buyer
  • Executed master service agreement / data-license contract identifying the non-resident recipient and the offshore market-research service scope
  • CRA-prescribed export evidence records (GST/HST Memorandum 17.5 schedule, including package identifiers and carrier tracking confirmations)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance API regime exists federally or in Alberta. CRA requires GST/HST registrants to maintain electronic invoices and supporting export records (ETA s. 286). Registrants with annual gross revenues above CAD 1,000,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) and remit by electronic funds transfer for amounts over CAD 10,000. Canada is progressively adopting the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (Peppol/CIUS) for business-to-government invoices; B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary, so no clearance code, stamping, or government API integration is mandated for this consumer-panel SaaS platform.

CRA may audit export zero-rating claims within the normal 4-year reassessment window, extended to 10 years for misrepresentation or neglect and without limitation for fraud. Where export documentation is insufficient or invoices are not repatriated within the platform's 180-day payment-rail policy, CRA will: (i) reverse the 0% rate and assess 5% GST on the full consideration; (ii) deny the related input tax credits; (iii) impose failure-to-register and failure-to-file penalties under ETA ss. 240/280 (up to 3% of unremitted net tax plus 1% per month for up to 12 months, subject to prescribed minimums); (iv) impose the false-statement gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 285.1 (not less than CAD 250 and up to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded); and (v) charge prescribed daily-compounding interest. Deliberate abusive export arrangements expose the ULC to criminal prosecution, GAAR re-characterization, and personal director/officer liability under ETA s. 323 for unremitted net tax. Although Canada imposes no fixed statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline for GST zero-rating, failure to remit export proceeds into the Canadian ULC account within the 180-day policy window undermines the evidentiary integrity of the zero-rating claim in audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has no foreign exchange control legislation; the former Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and Canada observes IMF Article VIII status. Both current account and capital account transactions are freely convertible, with no requirement for central bank approval, exchange permit, or capital import/export license. Profit remittance: a Canadian ULC may pay dividends or redeem shares to non-resident shareholders without regulatory authorization; the only fiscal impediment is Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory rate, reduced to 5% under the Canada-US treaty for a US corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of voting shares, provided treaty benefits are available). The ULC's ability to make distributions is governed by the Alberta Business Corporations Act solvency tests, not by FX rules. Canada maintains a statistics-based foreign direct investment reporting threshold under Statistics Canada's Balance of Payments Division, but this is inapplicable to a small market-research platform.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export or cross-border service receipts. The Alberta ULC may hold export proceeds in CAD or foreign currency accounts at any Canadian Schedule I bank or authorized foreign bank branch. Reporting obligations are limited to: (i) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports for wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) the T1135 Foreign Income Verification Statement if specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000; and (iii) CRA functional-currency reporting elections if accounts are maintained in USD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF supervisor under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The Alberta Treasury Board and Finance regulates provincially incorporated credit unions and provincial fiduciaries, but does not exercise exchange control.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

High feasibility. Canada does not recognise a distinct 'EMI' licence; non-bank payment providers operate as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Ltd and affiliate Wise Europe EMI regulated by the National Bank of Belgium) and PayPal (PayPal Canada, a FINTRAC-registered MSB) are fully viable for cross-border mass payout and API disbursement rails. Legal constraints: (i) the ULC must be active on the Alberta Corporate Registry and pass enhanced beneficial-ownership checks; (ii) the Retail Payment Activities Act (RCAA) requires final payment-service providers to register with the Bank of Canada, and both Wise and PayPal must be registered PSPs at the time of use; (iii) ULC unlimited-liability status produces prolonged legal-entity review by MSB compliance teams; (iv) fintech platforms generally do not provide commercial credit, overdraft, or escrow deposit insurance, so a Schedule I bank account remains mandatory for capital escrow and settlement liquidity.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility. Canada does not recognise a distinct 'EMI' licence; non-bank payment providers operate as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Ltd and affiliate Wise Europe EMI regulated by the National Bank of Belgium) and PayPal (PayPal Canada, a FINTRAC-registered MSB) are fully viable for cross-border mass payout and API disbursement rails. Legal constraints: (i) the ULC must be active on the Alberta Corporate Registry and pass enhanced beneficial-ownership checks; (ii) the Retail Payment Activities Act (RCAA) requires final payment-service providers to register with the Bank of Canada, and both Wise and PayPal must be registered PSPs at the time of use; (iii) ULC unlimited-liability status produces prolonged legal-entity review by MSB compliance teams; (iv) fintech platforms generally do not provide commercial credit, overdraft, or escrow deposit insurance, so a Schedule I bank account remains mandatory for capital escrow and settlement liquidity.
