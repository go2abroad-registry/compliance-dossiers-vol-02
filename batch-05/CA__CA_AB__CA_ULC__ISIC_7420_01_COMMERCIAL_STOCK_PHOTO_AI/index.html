Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, including the My Business Account portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the Alberta Treasury Board and Finance through CRA single-access registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Alberta ULC incorporated under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) must first obtain its corporate incorporation number from the Alberta Corporate Registry, then apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1, adding a GST/HST account if taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold. A non-resident ULC may register for a BN using a Canadian representative or agent; no minimum capital is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian-resident ULC is taxed as a corporation on worldwide income, with foreign tax credits available, and is not a pass-through entity under Canadian federal or Alberta income tax law. — A foreign-owned Alberta ULC incorporated under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) must first obtain its corporate incorporation number from the Alberta Corporate Registry, then apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1, adding a GST/HST account if taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold. A non-resident ULC may register for a BN using a Canadian representative or agent; no minimum capital is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian-resident ULC is taxed as a corporation on worldwide income, with foreign tax credits available, and is not a pass-through entity under Canadian federal or Alberta income tax law.

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box election exists under Canadian income tax law; a ULC remains a taxable Canadian corporation for Income Tax Act purposes. Available elections include the ETA section 156 election among closely related corporations for GST/HST, ITA section 85 rollover elections on qualifying transfers, and amalgamation elections. For U.S. federal tax purposes only, a Form 8832 election may classify the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership, but Canada does not recognize such election for Canadian tax characterization; hybrid mismatch rules under ITA sections 18.2 and 18.3 may apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); registration becomes mandatory once this threshold is exceeded, including for non-resident suppliers of digital services or intangible property to persons in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when sold by the supplier and exported by the recipient or by a common carrier to a destination outside Canada and possession passes in Canada solely for export. Services and intangible property, including digitized stock photos and AI-generated visual assets supplied electronically to a non-resident person, are zero-rated under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the supply is made to a non-resident and is not for use or benefit in Canada and does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The licensing of digital visual assets to non-residents is generally treated as a zero-rated supply of intangible personal property to a non-resident.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or equivalent electronic export report)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or transport document evidencing export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank FX confirmation (MT103), or equivalent evidence of repatriation of export proceeds

No national real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate currently exists in Canada. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically using CRA GST/HST NETFILE, TELEFILE, or EDI/XML for prescribed large filers; supporting invoices must be retained for audit but are not cleared or digitally stamped by the CRA in real time.

CRA may reassess and claw back GST/HST previously zero-rated if documentary evidence fails to prove physical export or non-resident receipt and off-Canada use. False statements or omissions attract civil penalties under Excise Tax Act section 273 (up to 25% of the claimed refund plus interest) and general penalties under section 280.1, with criminal prosecution available for wilful evasion. Directors and officers may be personally liable under section 323. Retrospective assessment, interest, penalties, and denial of future zero-rating claims are standard enforcement consequences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains an open capital account. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both current and capital transactions; no exchange control restrictions apply to commercial stock-photo royalties, dividends, or capital injections. Cross-border profit repatriation is subject only to Canadian withholding tax on dividends under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, generally 25% absent a treaty reduction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law does not impose surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may freely retain, convert, or disburse foreign currency via Canadian clearing channels.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. Offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) are permitted as payment facilitators but cannot serve as a Canadian bank for regulatory capital clearing purposes. An Alberta ULC may use these for merchant collection and global payouts, but primary treasury accounts must reside with an OSFI-regulated Canadian bank to satisfy corporate governance and AML expectations.
