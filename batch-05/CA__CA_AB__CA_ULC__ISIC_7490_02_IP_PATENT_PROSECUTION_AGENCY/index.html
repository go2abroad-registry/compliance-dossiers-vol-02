Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — My Business Account portal; GST/HST NETFILE; Alberta corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; Alberta has no provincial sales tax authority.

E-government filing portal

The foreign ULC must first obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 through the Business Registration Online service or mailing the completed form to CRA. Attach certified Alberta incorporation documents, the ULC operating or partnership-type agreement, and information on directors, shareholders, and registered address. For non-resident applicants, CRA may require a Canadian contact or representative for service of process and a Canadian address for records. Once the BN is issued, add the GST/HST program account using Form RC1 or My Business Account before making taxable supplies in Canada. The same BN is used for corporate income tax, payroll, and import/export accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Canadian federal and Alberta income tax law, the Alberta ULC is generally treated as a taxable corporation resident in Canada. However, for U.S. federal tax purposes, the ULC is eligible to make a check-the-box election to be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership, thereby achieving pass-through substance absent Canadian elective classification. Canadian domestic law does not provide a general entity-level pass-through election.

Available election forms

U.S. IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election under Treasury Reg. § 301.7701-3 permits the ULC to elect disregarded-entity or partnership status for U.S. tax purposes; no equivalent Canadian check-the-box election exists for federal income tax. Additional elections may include a GST/HST closely related corporation group election under Excise Tax Act s. 156, and joint election under s. 150 where applicable for non-resident withholding.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable revenues, measured over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, from supplies made in Canada (GST/HST small supplier threshold); a non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada exceeding this threshold or otherwise failing the small supplier exception must register and collect GST/HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Services supplied by the patent prosecution agency to a non-resident recipient may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the service is not excluded (excluding services relating to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and services of a type ordinarily rendered to a Canadian resident). PCT patent prosecution and international trademark registration services supplied to an offshore client are typically eligible for 0% GST/HST if the client is non-resident, the supply is performed outside Canada or to a non-resident, and contemporaneous documentary evidence is maintained. Goods physically exported from Canada may also be zero-rated when supported by export documentation proving removal from Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export declaration to the Canada Border Services Agency through the Canadian Export Reporting System or approved service provider) where tangible goods are part of the supply
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export for goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank evidence (SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice) showing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Detailed commercial invoice describing the patent prosecution services and identifying the non-resident recipient
  • Executed service agreement / engagement letter confirming the offshore nature of the services and the recipient's non-resident status
  • Supporting documentation of service performance (patent and trademark prosecution filings, correspondence, time records, USPTO/WIPO receipts)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government-approved e-invoicing. CRA accepts electronic invoices and digital records if legible and accessible. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1.5 million must file returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE; voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing and Peppol-based interoperability frameworks are available but not mandatory. Any electronic invoicing system must preserve transaction integrity and CRA audit access.

CRA may reassess disallowed zero-rating and require full 5% GST plus compound daily interest. Knowingly or negligently making a false statement on GST/HST filings triggers a civil penalty under s. 285 of the Excise Tax Act of 25% of the tax understated. Intentional evasion is subject to criminal prosecution under s. 327 of the ETA, fines, imprisonment, and public naming under CRA's taxpayer disclosure regime. For export zero-rating, CRA will scrutinize the documentary trail; insufficient proof of non-resident status, delivery, or payment may result in retrospective clawback of the 5% GST, penalties, and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains de jure full current and capital account convertibility under the Canadian Dollar floating exchange rate regime; no restrictions on conversion, repatriation, dividends, or profit remittance for non-resident shareholders, subject only to Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding tax and Canadian corporate solvency tests under the Canada Business Corporations Act and Alberta Business Corporations Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion regime exists in Canada; export proceeds may be retained in any currency. Reporting obligations arise solely under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, including FINTRAC reports for cross-border currency movement and electronic fund transfers exceeding CAD 10,000. A ULC receiving export-derived patent royalties is not required to convert or repatriate foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with legal constraints. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not authorized to accept deposits or operate as banks in Canada unless authorized under the Bank Act or registered as money service businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC. Cross-border digital platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) may be used as payment facilitators for operational disbursements, but cannot replace a Schedule I chartered bank for trust/capital clearing rails; they must maintain a partnership with a Canadian bank for local clearing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if total value of transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1 million for goods, CAD 500,000 for services, or CAD 250,000 for intangibles. Master File required if total revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form doctrine. A ULC must demonstrate real economic substance (e.g., employees, office, decision-making) to avoid recharacterization of transactions and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239: fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of gross negligence amount.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts if they failed to withhold or remit source deductions (s.227.1). Civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties up to 25% of tax avoided. CRA may also impose a 10% penalty for failure to file TP documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced in the sham arrangement; potential criminal charges for conspiracy to defraud the Crown.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly failed to implement required controls; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
