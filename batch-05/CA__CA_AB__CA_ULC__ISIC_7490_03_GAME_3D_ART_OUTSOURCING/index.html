Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — sole federal tax authority for GST/HST and Part I corporate income tax; also administers Alberta corporate income tax under the 2019 Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement (effective for Alberta taxation years commencing after 31 December 2019). Registration and filing portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), GST/HST NETFILE, Corporation Internet Filing (T2), and CRA My Business Account (canada.ca). Alberta Corporate Registry (Service Alberta) handles incorporation records but has no tax-assessment role.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the ULC under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) via Alberta Corporate Registry, obtaining a Corporate Access Number and confirming the majority-resident-Canadian director requirement of ABCA s.105(3). 2) Register the corporation for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with the CRA via BRO or Form RC1, selecting program accounts: RC (T2 corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll if employees). 3) Open the RT account within 30 days after the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded, or voluntarily at inception to recover input tax credits; Alberta CIT requires no separate provincial registration because CRA collects it on Alberta's behalf. 4) For a foreign-owned ULC, implement Part XIII withholding profiles (dividend/management fee remittances) at first capital distribution; the ULC remains a Canadian-resident corporation for Canadian tax purposes despite any US check-the-box election.

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Alberta ULC is always a 'corporation' resident in Canada under ITA s.250(4)(a), taxed under Part I on worldwide income at the federal general rate (15%) plus Alberta CIT (8%), with small-business rates (federal 9% / Alberta 2%) if eligible. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (US federal check-the-box only; Form 8832 disregarding the ULC is not recognized by Canada). Entity_Level_Taxation: the Alberta ULC is always a 'corporation' resident in Canada under ITA s.250(4)(a), taxed under Part I on worldwide income at the federal general rate (15%) plus Alberta CIT (8%), with small-business rates (federal 9% / Alberta 2%) if eligible. Territorial_Scope: worldwide (not territorial). Tax_Exempt_Zone: none in Alberta.

Available election forms

US check-the-box election (Form 8832): the ULC may be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership for US federal tax only; Canada taxes it as a corporation, creating a hybrid entity; the US owner may claim treaty relief under Article IV(6) of the Canada-US Convention (2007 Fifth Protocol).; GST/HST closely related corporation election (ETA s.156): taxable supplies between the registered ULC and registered affiliated group members, including a US parent carrying on business in Canada, can be treated as made for nil consideration, eliminating cash-flow GST.; GST/HST reporting-period elections (ETA ss.245-246): align the GST/HST filing period with the T2 fiscal year and elect monthly, quarterly, or annual remittance frequency, subject to ETA thresholds.; GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s.226): available to small registrants but rarely optimal for zero-rated exporters because it caps input tax credit recovery.; CRA Voluntary Disclosures Program (VDP): administrative remediation for late BN/RT registration, permitting penalty relief before CRA contact.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: mandatory GST/HST registration arises once taxable supplies of the ULC, aggregated with associated persons, exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter or the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; registration must be completed within 30 days. Voluntary registration is available immediately to recover input tax credits. Non-resident digital-service suppliers are subject to analogous CAD 30,000 remote-supplier rules, which do not apply to this Canadian-resident entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Part V of Schedule VI, ETA: (1) Services supplied to a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant (ETA Sch. VI s.9), provided the service is not rendered to an individual physically present in Canada and does not relate to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; (2) Intangible personal property (final 3D art assets, rigs, copyright, and digital game assets) supplied to a non-resident non-registrant (ETA Sch. VI s.10) where the property is not referable to Canadian situs; (3) Physical prototypes exported as goods, with proof of export tendered within 60 days. The ULC must maintain valid non-resident status evidence for each overseas game studio client. Canada prescribes no statutory FX repatriation deadline for export proceeds under the ETA or ITA; the 180-day figure in this dossier reflects bank/export-finance documentary benchmarks and CRA commercial-reasonableness review standards used in transfer-pricing audits.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada: Form B13A via the Canadian Export Reporting System / e-Export), required only where goods such as physical art prototypes or deliverables cross the border, including HS codes, value for duty, exporter BN, and destination.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing delivery to the non-resident consignee; a courier proof-of-delivery record is acceptable for low-value parcel exports.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice (SWIFT MT103) or FX conversion slip evidencing receipt and repatriation of export proceeds to the ULC's Canadian operating account.
  • Commercial invoice bearing the ULC's CRA Business Number, a 'Zero-Rated Export' statement, Incoterms, consideration, and the Schedule VI exemption citation, plus proof of foreign payment.
  • Transaction-file evidence: executed Service Agreement / Statement of Work, digital delivery certificate, non-resident status proof of the buyer (certificate of incorporation, tax residency certificate, or W-8BEN-E), and the asset royalty/distribution audit trail.

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, government API clearance, or mandatory PEPPOL/EDI e-invoicing regime. Invoices must contain: vendor name, CRA Business Number, invoice date, description of supply, consideration, GST rate and tax charged; zero-rated export invoices must state 'Zero-Rated – Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act'. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or the CRA XML/REST gateway; paper filing is restricted. Books and records must be retained for six years in English or French.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year period (ETA s.298), with no limitation period where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud is established. If zero-rating is reversed on audit, the ULC is liable for the uncollected 5% GST on the full consideration plus daily compound interest at the prescribed rate (currently roughly 7-8% for corporate overdue amounts). Civil penalties include 25% of the net tax amount under ETA s.280(1) for false statements/omissions or gross negligence, and ITA s.163(2) exposes the ULC to 50% of understated federal/Alberta tax if pass-through reporting is misapplied to Canadian-source income. Director and shareholder liability rules under ETA s.323 are materially magnified by the ULC's statutory unlimited-liability design. Standard CRA enforcement outputs include retrospective clawback of refunded input tax credits, denial of future refunds, and assignment of a high-risk audit file.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no general exchange controls, surrender requirements, or approval gates under Canadian federal law. Profit remittance is governed by corporate solvency tests, tax compliance, and non-resident withholding provisions of Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. Dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees may be remitted freely, subject to applicable Canadian withholding tax and treaty relief; a US-resident beneficial owner of a Canadian ULC may normally access a 5% reduced withholding rate on dividends under Article X of the Canada-US Tax Convention if the 10% voting ownership threshold is met. The ULC's unlimited-liability and US check-the-box flow-through characteristics do not remove the need for Canadian Part XIII withholding or transfer-pricing documentation on intercompany payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriating export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may hold foreign currency at Canadian banks, convert at commercial market rates, and set their own treasury timing. Foreign exchange gains and losses, however, must be tracked under section 39 of the Income Tax Act, and accounts must be reported in Canadian dollars for financial-statement purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Department of Finance Canada retaining ultimate policy responsibility. Canada has no separate foreign exchange administration or exchange-control authority comparable to an MFA-style regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. A foreign fintech or electronic-money institution is not a Schedule I Canadian bank and cannot hold itself out as a bank in Canada. To operate lawfully in Canada, a non-resident payment service provider or MSB must register with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and may be subject to provincial consumer-protection rules, including money-broker or payday-lender regimes. Such EMIs are acceptable only as ancillary payment facilitators, not as primary treasury hosts or liquidity depositories. A Canadian ULC should maintain its primary operating and escrow accounts with regulated Canadian banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from US shareholders should be routed via SWIFT MT103 in US dollars or Canadian dollars to the ULC's designated Canadian bank account. The remitter should use a clear payment reference such as 'Capital contribution -- ULC shares' and include the subscriber's name and ULC account number. Canada does not impose foreign-exchange control approvals, but the receiving bank will require the beneficial-ownership documentation and may require FINTRAC records for wire transfers above CAD 10,000. Non-resident capital contributions are not generally reviewable under the Investment Canada Act unless the transaction constitutes an acquisition of control of a Canadian business with a book-value or enterprise-value threshold; a newly formed ULC receiving its initial subscription is outside review. For real-time verification, the bank should be given the subscription agreement and directors' consent so the credit is posted as share capital rather than as a loan, preserving clean tax and treasury treatment.Outbound trade disbursements to overseas game studios and contractors should use SWIFT MT103 or, for large or documentary requirements, a commercial letter of credit issued by the Canadian bank. The ULC should use standard purpose codes accepted by Canadian banks, including 'service fees', 'royalties and IP licence fees', 'software development and outsourcing services', and 'management and administrative fees charged to the enterprise'. Each MT103 must reference the underlying invoice, and the bank may collect the transport, delivery, or service evidence under PCMLTFA record-keeping expectations. Canada's payments clearing system, Lynx, processes high-value CAD payments, while USD commercial payments clear through correspondent US banks; same-day settlement on the CLS or correspondent rails depends on the selected Canadian bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 transaction/series threshold for s.247(3) transfer pricing penalty exposure. Contemporaneous Local File / Master File documentation is expected for all reportable cross-border non-arm's-length transactions; Country-by-Country Reporting and Canadian Master File filing become mandatory where a Canadian ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue is at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. However, the CRA may recharacterize transactions without economic substance under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and deny treaty benefits under the OECD Principal Purpose Test. The ULC must maintain real Canadian presence — employees, office premises, bank accounts, contracts, risk management, and decision-making — to withstand transfer pricing audits and preserve any foreign tax classification election.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus up to five years imprisonment. Gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) applies at 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in, authorize, or acquiesce to the evasion may be jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 for unremitted withholdings; criminal conviction can produce restitution orders, travel bans, and imprisonment up to five years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Section 247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, plus interest and reassessment; treaty protection may be denied where documentation is absent or inadequate.

Director personal liability: Directors are not ordinarily liable for transfer pricing penalties alone, but if the CRA recharacterizes payments as dividends or royalties subject to Part XIII, directors may become personally liable for the failure to withhold and remit such tax under s.227.1.
Shell Company / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under s.245 to deny the tax benefit, gross-negligence penalty up to 50%, possible Part XIII withholding on recharacterized dividends, and denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Directors can face personal tax liability if corporate formalities are disregarded and the corporate veil is pierced; willful participation in a sham may also yield criminal liability and travel restrictions.
AML / Financial Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 100,000 per violation for entities, increasing to CAD 500,000 for repeat violations; criminal sanctions may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally prosecuted for knowingly failing to carry out PCMLTFA obligations, exposing them to fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years, in addition to travel bans.
