Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; national electronic portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO); provincial partner for Alberta: Alberta Treasury Board and Finance, with Alberta corporate income tax administered by the CRA through the Tax Administration Agreement and a single federal T2 filing.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the Unlimited Liability Corporation under the Alberta Business Corporations Act via the Alberta Corporate Registry and obtain the corporate access number. Step 2: obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA by filing Form RC1 Request for a Business Number or using CRA Business Registration Online; select Non-Resident Corporation status and open program accounts RP (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RC (payroll, if applicable). Step 3: register the Alberta provincial corporate income tax account through the same BN; no separate Alberta TIN exists. Step 4: apply for GST/HST registration; registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or the four preceding calendar quarters, and voluntary registration is available for sub-threshold entities to recover input tax credits; a non-resident applicant may be required by CRA to provide a Canadian address for service and post security under Excise Tax Act s. 289. Step 5: enroll in CRA My Business Account, authorize a tax representative where needed, and file T2 and GST/HST returns electronically.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible, with dual classification: for U.S. federal tax purposes, the Alberta ULC is an eligible entity that may elect under IRC § 7701 (Form 8832 tax classification election) to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity and is not a per se corporation. For Canadian domestic tax purposes, the default classification is Entity_Level_Taxation: the ULC is a corporation resident in Canada under Income Tax Act s. 250(2)(a), files a T2 return, and is subject to Part I tax on worldwide income; CRA does not recognize the U.S. check-the-box pass-through status for Canadian income tax or GST/HST purposes.

Available election forms

Canadian domestic law has no check-the-box election. Relevant elections and regimes: (1) U.S. Form 8832 tax classification election to make the ULC disregarded or partnership-status for U.S. tax, creating a hybrid structure; (2) GST/HST elections: Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for closely related corporations to charge nil consideration on intercorporate supplies, and ETA s. 240(4) GST/HST group registration; (3) hybrid mismatch rules: Income Tax Act ss. 18.2, 18.3 and 18.4 may disallow deductions or deem hybrid dividends where the U.S. shareholder treats the ULC as a disregarded entity, requiring annual review; (4) treaty-based relief election under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention, subject to the Limitation on Benefits article; (5) Alberta corporate elections under the ABCA, administered through the federal T2 system.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD) on both current and capital accounts; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since December 1952. No governmental approval, quota, licence, or registration is required for inbound foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, or the repatriation of profits. Dividend distributions by an Alberta ULC to a US parent are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5% under the Canada-US Tax Treaty for a corporate shareholder holding at least 10% voting rights), while arm's-length interest on shareholder debt is exempt from withholding under s. 212(1)(b) of the Income Tax Act. The CAD trades on a free float; the Bank of Canada does not target any exchange rate band.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no surrender, forced conversion, or mandatory repatriation timeline on export receipts, including fees earned by a clinical trial CRO from non-resident sponsors. Foreign-currency proceeds may be retained in any currency, held offshore, or converted freely. The only compliance hooks are (i) FINTRAC large electronic-funds-transfer reporting for each wire of C$10,000 or more, (ii) the CBSA cross-border currency declaration for physical cash movements of C$10,000 or more, and (iii) Form T1135 reporting for a Canadian-resident ULC holding specified foreign property in aggregate above C$100,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; no separate exchange-control administration exists. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises all federally regulated deposit-taking institutions. Canada abolished its formal exchange-control apparatus in 1952; foreign exchange policy is now administered by the Department of Finance Canada through the Exchange Fund Account.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary banking substitute; Medium as an ancillary payment rail. Canadian law reserves deposit-taking to OSFI-regulated chartered banks and provincial credit unions under the Bank Act and provincial trust-and-loan statutes; offshore digital EMI platforms may not lawfully hold the ULC's operating balances or escrowed milestone funds. An offshore EMI may be used only as a settlement aggregator for minor outbound vendor payments, and only if the EMI is registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada and clears through a licensed Canadian payment-service provider. All material trial balances must remain at a Tier-1 Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan injections are executed by SWIFT MT103 from the US parent to the ULC's Tier-1 Canadian account with an exact beneficiary-name match and remittance advice reading Capital Contribution or Intercompany Loan. Canadian banks require a payment purpose/reason code; equity draws are recorded against share issuance supported by board resolutions, while loans require a written intercompany agreement with arm's-length interest to preserve the s. 212(1)(b) withholding exemption and treaty eligibility. Any electronic transfer of C$10,000 or more is FINTRAC-reportable; capital inflows exceeding C$1 million trigger banker's inquiry and source-of-wealth verification prior to treasury deployment.Outbound settlement is cleared in CAD through Lynx (Bank of Canada RTGS) and in USD or third currencies through SWIFT MT103 via the Canadian bank's US correspondent on Fedwire/CHIPS. Site and subcontractor payments carry a unique end-to-end transaction reference (UETR), pre-validated beneficiary account information, and purpose codes describing CRO management services; no payment may be routed to a sanctioned entity. Imports of investigational medicinal products and trial supplies may be settled by commercial letters of credit under UCP 600 or documentary collection, with presentation of the Health Canada clinical trial authorization, commercial invoice, and transport documents.
