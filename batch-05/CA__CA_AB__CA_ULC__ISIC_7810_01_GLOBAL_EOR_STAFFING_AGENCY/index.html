Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca and CRA My Business Account (MyBA). Alberta provincial corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act, RSA 2000, c A-15 (Tax Collection Agreement); Alberta levies no provincial sales tax.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Unlimited Liability Corporation under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta), RSA 2000, c B-9, via the Alberta Corporate Registry (CorpSpace). Step 2: Obtain a federal Business Number (BN) in 9-2-4 format (9-digit BN + 2-letter program identifier + 4-digit reference) by filing Form RC1 through CRA Business Registration Online. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (hybrid entity: for Canadian federal and Alberta income tax purposes, an Alberta ULC is a 'corporation' under section 89(1) of the Income Tax Act and is subject to entity-level taxation; the pass-through attribute is realized only by foreign (typically U.S.) shareholders electing under the U.S. check-the-box regime, creating a hybrid entity circumstance).

Available election forms

No Canadian statutory pass-through election exists for a ULC. Available elections: (1) U.S. check-the-box election under U.S. Treasury Regulation section 301.7701-3, whereby U.S. shareholders may treat the Alberta ULC as a partnership or disregarded entity for U.S. tax purposes; Canada taxes the ULC as a corporation and applies hybrid mismatch recharacterization rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.2-18.7 where relevant. (2) GST/HST election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for closely related corporations (90% or more common ownership) to treat inter-corporate supplies as made for nil consideration. (3) No Alberta provincial sales tax election is required. (4) For payroll purposes, the employer may elect annual remittance filing thresholds under CRA payroll programs; no direct tax classification election is triggered.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in the total taxable supplies, worldwide, made by the person and any associated persons during the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold, Excise Tax Act section 123(1)); a ULC conducting an EOR/payroll business in Canada generally registers voluntarily even below this threshold to recover input tax credits through the GST/HST net tax calculation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a supply of a service to a non-resident recipient is generally zero-rated, unless the supply is in respect of real property situated in Canada, in respect of goods situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or is otherwise an excluded personal service rendered in Canada. For the Global EOR model, payroll administration, employer-of-record, and cross-border contractor payment services supplied to a foreign client are eligible for 0% GST when the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST and the service does not relate to Canadian real property or goods physically located in Canada. Substantiation requires documentary evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, including incorporation certificate or equivalent governmental registry excerpt, registered business address outside Canada, absence of Canadian GST/HST registration, the executed EOR service agreement, and bank records evidencing the cross-border consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Canadian Export Declaration filed through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CES/CAED) for goods exports exceeding CAD 2,000 per shipment; not applicable to pure cross-border services, for which the executed service agreement and performance records constitute the export evidence.
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill: transport documentation required for exported goods; for EOR services, the functional equivalent is the binding Global EOR master service agreement, schedule of employees/contractors, and service delivery records.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: Canada does not issue a statutory Inward Remittance Certificate; the functional equivalent is the banking evidence of receipt of foreign consideration, including SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advice, foreign exchange transaction records, and bank statements showing the non-resident payer.

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no government digital stamping, and no mandatory government API or PEPPOL clearance for GST/HST invoices. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1.5 million (GST/HST NETFILE or EDI), to corporations with gross revenue above CAD 1 million for the T2 corporate return, and to employers issuing 50 or more T4/T4A information slips, which must be transmitted electronically through CRA payroll systems. Books and records sufficient to substantiate zero-rated exports must be retained for six years under Excise Tax Act section 286.

Canada maintains no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter is applied as an internal compliance benchmark, but CRA and transfer-pricing rules (Income Tax Act section 247) require arm's length consideration and may reassess where payment is unreasonably deferred. Improper zero-rating exposure: CRA may reassess the supply at 5% GST together with interest compounded daily, and apply the Excise Tax Act section 285 gross-negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be avoided where a false statement or omission was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence. Failure to register carries the section 280 penalty of CAD 250. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under the Excise Tax Act can result in fines of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to two years. Reassessment periods are four years from the relevant date, extended indefinitely where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud exists (section 298 of the Excise Tax Act). Fraudulent export zero-rating claims additionally trigger clawback of input tax credits previously claimed and denial of refunds under the net tax calculation, with records required to be preserved for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a liberalized floating exchange-rate regime, with no exchange controls on either the current account or the capital account. Non-resident shareholders of the Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) may inject equity or shareholder loans in CAD, USD, or any freely convertible currency without central-bank approval. Profit and dividend remittances are unrestricted, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax on non-resident shareholders (default statutory rate of 25% on deemed ULC dividends, reduced to 10%-15% under Article X of the Canada-US Tax Convention where the US shareholder owns at least 10% of the voting stock and the ULC is treated as a corporation for treaty purposes). Key practical friction points: (i) the ULC deemed-residence rules under Income Tax Act subsection 94(3) - a wind-up, insolvency, or control event may trigger a deemed taxable disposition of the ULC's assets; (ii) foreign bank accounts and foreign situs assets of the ULC must be reported on CRA Form T1135 where the aggregate cost exceeds CAD 100,000; (iii) Statistics Canada inward-direct-investment reporting applies in any quarter in which accumulated non-resident investment exceeds CAD 1 million; (iv) the Alberta Employment Agency Regulation licensing must remain current for the Canadian bank to continue processing payroll. No capital-account license, no prior profit-repatriation approval, and no exchange equalization tax applies to this structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory conversion ratio, and no receivables-repatriation window for a Canadian-resident ULC. Foreign currency receipts from the EOR and staffing platform may be retained in USD or any foreign currency in a corporate foreign-currency account without BOC approval. For corporate tax purposes, the ULC may elect the Income Tax Act section 261 functional-currency reporting regime (typically USD) to stabilize EOR receivables and payroll payables; otherwise realized and unrealized FX gains are brought into income on ordinary rules. Notwithstanding the absence of surrender rules, the treasury blueprint requires a monthly CAD repatriation sweep to fund GST/HST net remittances and Alberta Employer Health Tax (EHT) obligations, and a quarterly USD balance cap to avoid T1135 and overly large unhedged translation exposure.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) as the sole central bank and monetary authority of Canada; prudential supervision of federally regulated financial institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); corporate tax administration by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and the Alberta Finance/Corporate Registry under the Alberta Business Corporations Act. Canada does not operate a SAFE-type foreign-exchange administration; the FX regime is administered through the Currency Act, the Income Tax Act (Part XIII withholding and s.18(4) thin capitalization), and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a supplementary disbursement rail only, never as the primary treasury account. Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex operate in Canada as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs), not as Schedule I banks; they are prohibited by the Bank Act from deposit-taking and cannot provide a true Canadian settlement or payroll trust account. The lawful structure for the licensed Alberta EOR/ULC is: (i) primary operating payroll account at a Schedule I bank holding client funds and employer payroll obligations; (ii) Wise Business deployed as a secondary batch-payment rail for small-value foreign contractor disbursements using its local-pool settlement model, with the API connector embedded in the bank's treasury management system. Legal constraints include: FINTRAC MSB registration and routine compliance audit of the EMI; the fee component of the employer-of-record billing must flow through the Schedule I bank account to avoid an unlicensed trust or payroll-agency characterization; CRA does not accept an EMI ledger alone as a statutory payroll record, so a mirror sub-ledger and daily reconciliation must be maintained.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant injection protocol: the non-resident shareholder wires CAD or USD via SWIFT MT103 from a US/EU bank to the ULC's segregated Capital Escrow Account at the primary Schedule I bank using field 50K for the ordering customer, field 57A for the receiving Canadian bank, field 59 for the ULC account, and the mandatory narrative 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN - CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - [Alberta Registry Number] - ULC'. Settlement occurs by finality transfer through the Bank of Canada Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); funds are released to the operational account only after the bank's compliance team verifies the source of funds. Regulatory attachments: (i) Statistics Canada quarterly inward-direct-investment report once cumulative non-resident investment exceeds CAD 1 million, using BoP purpose codes 7011 for equity and 7012 for loans; (ii) an executed shareholder loan agreement observing the 1.5:1 debt-to-equity thin-capitalization ratio in ITA s.18(4) to preserve interest deductibility; (iii) CRA T1135 filing for the overseas bank accounts of the ULC above CAD 100,000; (iv) for ISO 20022 CBPR+ corridors, an External Purpose Code of 'CACC' or 'INVE' appended to the settlement instruction so the credit is not misclassified as a taxable dividend.Layer 1 (domestic Canada payroll): Payments Canada ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) direct deposits for Canadian W-2/RL-1 employees, executed daily from a zero-balance payroll sub-ledger at the Schedule I bank, with Instant Payments via the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for termination and emergency payments. Layer 2 (cross-border US and international contractor payroll): SWIFT MT103 / gpi settlement through the bank's correspondent network (US Fedwire/CHIPS for USD, SEPA Instant for EUR), each payment bearing the ULC's employer-of-record reference and contractor identifier so foreign beneficiaries can reconcile filings as independent contractors; USD payroll is funded from the companion USD ledger against USD-denominated client receivables to eliminate double conversion. Layer 3 (trade and LC settlement): Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) issued against staffing-agency master service agreements, with confirmation facility at Scotiabank or BMO covering the EOR fee receivables; negotiated documents include progress invoices, terminated-employee reports, and payroll manifests. Layer 4 (fintech rail): Wise Batch Pay API for low-value high-volume freelancer disbursements in emerging-market currencies, with funds drawn from the segregated operational account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a supplementary disbursement rail only, never as the primary treasury account. Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex operate in Canada as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs), not as Schedule I banks; they are prohibited by the Bank Act from deposit-taking and cannot provide a true Canadian settlement or payroll trust account. The lawful structure for the licensed Alberta EOR/ULC is: (i) primary operating payroll account at a Schedule I bank holding client funds and employer payroll obligations; (ii) Wise Business deployed as a secondary batch-payment rail for small-value foreign contractor disbursements using its local-pool settlement model, with the API connector embedded in the bank's treasury management system. Legal constraints include: FINTRAC MSB registration and routine compliance audit of the EMI; the fee component of the employer-of-record billing must flow through the Schedule I bank account to avoid an unlicensed trust or payroll-agency characterization; CRA does not accept an EMI ledger alone as a statutory payroll record, so a mirror sub-ledger and daily reconciliation must be maintained.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% (15% federal general corporate rate net of the 10% General Rate Reduction and 13% provincial abatement, plus 8% Alberta corporate income tax on active business income). A non-resident-owned Alberta ULC is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation; therefore the Small Business Deduction is unavailable and the full 23% combined rate applies to active EOR and contractor payroll income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under ITA Part XIII (ss. 212(1)-(2)). Canada-US Treaty Article X(2)(a) reduces the rate to 5% for a US resident corporation beneficially owning at least 10% of the voting shares of the ULC; other treaty shareholders are entitled to 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5,000,000 aggregate fair market value of specified non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents in a taxation year triggers s. 247(3) penalty exposure; contemporaneous s. 247(4) documentation must exist by the T2 filing due date. Parent groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000 must also file the OECD Country-by-Country Report (CRA Form RC4649/RC4651) and maintain the Master File and Local File upon request.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations; the same effect is reached through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), the transfer-pricing substance requirement (s. 247(2)), hybrid mismatch restrictions (s. 18(t) and 93.1/94 approaches), and the Canada-US treaty conduct/Limitation-on-Benefits protections. An Alberta ULC that lacks its own employees, Canadian payroll records, board meetings in Alberta, hiring agreements, and invoice flow is exposed to being recharacterized as a sham, with Part XIII recapture at 25% and refusal of treaty relief for the US shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 200% of the evaded tax (ITA s. 239(1)); gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (s. 163.2); interest and civil re-assessment; criminal prosecution leading to imprisonment up to five years on indictment.

Director personal liability: Any director, officer, or agent who enforced or participated in the false return is jointly and severally liable (ss. 160 and 239); the veil is pierced, courts may order restitution and costs, and travel restrictions are effectively imposed by the criminal process
Source Payroll Non-Remittance

Statutory sanction: 10% penalty (s. 227(9)) plus prescribed daily arrears interest; a requirement to pay under s. 224(1.1); the CRA can freeze bank accounts and lien real estate without a court order.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for all unremitted source deductions (ITA s. 227.1); the only defence is the exercise of care, diligence, and skill to prevent the failure (s. 227.1(3)). Liability arises automatically upon non-remittance and the Minister's certificate can be enforced via the Federal Court even after the corporation is dissolved.
Transfer Pricing & Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: 10% penalty on the net upward adjustment (ITA s. 247(3)); the CRA may re-price the service under s. 247(2) and disallow losses or recharacterize debt; the Part XIII failure on management fees may add a separate 25% assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed standards-based T2 returns containing false statements under s. 163.2 become liable for the 50% gross-negligence penalty in addition to corporate tax; arranging cross-border payroll structures to evade Canadian withholding is an indictable offence, with extradition exposure and, on conviction, imprisonment up to five years and potential travel-bar restrictions upon sentencing.
