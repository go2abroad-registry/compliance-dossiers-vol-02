Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — national tax administration and the single entry point for the federal Business Number (BN), corporate income tax (T2 returns), GST/HST registration, and payroll accounts; Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca and the My Business Account portal serve as the electronic registration and filing interfaces. The CRA administers and collects Alberta's 8% corporate income tax on behalf of Alberta under the Alberta Corporate Tax Act; the Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (ATRA) retains only non-integrated provincial levies. Customs and export declaration matters are within the mandate of the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Provincial incorporation: Incorporate the ULC with the Alberta Corporate Registry (via the Alberta Corporate Account Registry) under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta), RSA 2000, c B-9, filing articles that expressly designate the corporation as an unlimited liability corporation; obtain the certificate of incorporation and Alberta corporate registry number. Step 2 — Federal Business Number: Apply through Business Registration Online or Form RC1 for the 9-digit BN and program accounts RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll if the ULC employs staff). An Alberta-incorporated corporation is deemed resident in Canada under section 250(1) of the Income Tax Act and must file annual T2 returns, including the Alberta corporate tax calculation, regardless of the residency of its directors, managers, or shareholders. Step 3 — GST/HST: Register for the RT program account once taxable supplies made in Canada equal or exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; BPO exporters with zero-rated foreign supplies normally register voluntarily at inception to recover input tax credits on software, telecommunications, and employment infrastructure. No separate Alberta sales tax or VAT registration exists because Alberta imposes no provincial VAT or retail sales tax. The BN is the sole statutory tax identifier for the ULC; non-resident shareholders and ultimate beneficial owners do not require a Canadian TIN.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial and investment transactions. Canada generally imposes no capital-account approval, capital import registration, mandatory repatriation, exchange surrender, or routine profit-remittance restrictions. A Canadian Alberta ULC may receive foreign equity funding, intercompany loans, service fees, dividends, interest, and other permitted payments subject to corporate-law authorization, solvency requirements, contractual restrictions, transfer-pricing rules, withholding tax, beneficial-ownership review, sanctions screening, and applicable tax filings. Canadian corporate residence and tax classification must be analyzed separately from U.S. check-the-box or other foreign tax classification; an Alberta ULC is not automatically tax-transparent under Canadian income-tax law. Cross-border payments to non-residents may require Canadian withholding tax, commonly at a statutory rate of 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty. Dividends may qualify for treaty-reduced rates, while interest, royalties, management fees, and other amounts require payment-specific analysis. Related-party financing and service charges must satisfy arm's-length transfer-pricing requirements. The recipient, payer, purpose, currency, and documentation should be validated before execution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or general deadline requiring foreign-currency receipts to be converted into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's treasury discretion. For a remote BPO, qualifying exported services may be zero-rated for GST/HST purposes where the statutory conditions are met, but zero-rating is distinct from foreign-exchange treatment. The company should retain customer contracts, invoices, service-delivery evidence, customer-residency evidence, payment confirmations, and foreign-currency valuation records. Foreign-currency transactions must be recorded in Canadian dollars for accounting and tax purposes using a reasonable consistently applied conversion methodology. Banking institutions may request transaction-purpose information, invoices, contracts, source-of-funds evidence, and sanctions or AML confirmations even though no central-bank conversion deadline applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no separate foreign-exchange administration imposing general exchange controls. Anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing supervision is primarily administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), while tax reporting and withholding matters are administered by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium to High for routine receivables and payables if the provider legally services Canadian businesses and completes Canadian AML, sanctions, beneficial-ownership, and source-of-funds checks. Wise Business and comparable providers may provide payment accounts or safeguarded payment services, but they are not substitutes for a Canadian deposit-taking bank and may impose country, currency, volume, prohibited-activity, and beneficiary restrictions. Offshore electronic-money institutions cannot lawfully conduct Canadian deposit-taking or represent themselves as Canadian banks without the required authorization. Payment service providers operating in Canada may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act and FINTRAC obligations, including registration, safeguarding, operational-risk, incident-reporting, and AML requirements, as applicable. Maintain a Canadian bank account as the primary settlement and contingency rail; use fintech accounts only after confirming contractual terms, Canadian availability, safeguarding structure, settlement finality, limits, data access, and account-closure procedures.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity contributions through the ULC's Canadian bank account from the named shareholder or an expressly documented group treasury entity. Use a traceable SWIFT wire, preferably ISO 20022 pacs.008 where supported, with the beneficiary's exact legal name, Alberta registered address, account number, transit and institution identifiers, currency, and a precise narrative such as 'equity contribution under [date] subscription agreement' or 'shareholder loan under [date] loan agreement.' Canada generally does not use a universal central-bank foreign-exchange purpose-code regime for ordinary corporate wires; the bank's own purpose, compliance, and regulatory fields must nevertheless be completed accurately. Before receipt, prepare the subscription or loan agreement, board approvals, capitalization table, source-of-funds evidence, payer relationship evidence, beneficial-ownership data, and expected-use schedule. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined general corporate rate (federal 15% + Alberta 8%); Alberta ULC is subject to this rate for active business income that does not qualify for the small business deduction, and the unlimited liability nature of the entity does not change Canadian tax classification or headline rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, imposed on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident of Canada.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed dollar threshold requiring a formal OECD Local/Master File is codified in the Income Tax Act; however, CRA requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for any cross-border non-arm's length transaction. Administrative penalty relief is available only if the tax adjustment does not exceed the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue, and the taxpayer has made reasonable efforts to establish arm's length prices. For BPO fee arrangements, intercompany service fees above CAD 1,000,000 should carry a formal transfer-pricing study.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the Cayman/Bermuda sense. CRA administers a substance-over-form doctrine, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), and anti-hybrid/surplus stripping rules. For a ULC operating a remote BPO call center in Alberta, economic substance is demonstrated by the presence of Canadian employees, physical or cloud-based call-center infrastructure, contracts executed in Canada, and board decisions referred to Alberta management. A ULC is always a resident taxable Canadian corporation for Canadian tax purposes, and the unlimited-liability attribute has no effect on Canadian tax substance requirements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239, an indictable conviction carries a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction carries a fine in the same range and imprisonment up to 2 years. Civil penalties include a 50% gross-negligence penalty on the understated tax, plus daily interest on arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted payroll source deductions and GST/HST if they knew or were negligent; liability may be enforced without proof of intent. A tax-evasion conviction can trigger personal fines, imprisonment, and an order of restitution to CRA; courts may pierce the corporate veil where the ULC is used as a mere facade for the director's personal tax evasion.
Shell Company / Artificial Avoidance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA) to deny tax benefits arising from an avoidance transaction, add a GAAR penalty of 25% of the tax benefit, and re-assess the ULC for the full corporate tax, Part XIII withholding tax, and interest. Non-resident corporations may additionally be challenged under Part XIV branch-tax rules or treaty abuse (principal purpose test).

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized distributions, payments, or transactions while the ULC had unpaid tax liabilities may be assessed personally under s.227.1/238. Civil restitution and director liability can extend to the full amount of the disallowed tax benefit. In cases of fraudulent conveyances, Alberta courts may disregard the ULC's limited liability mantle, and travel bans or asset-freezing orders may be issued in cross-border collection proceedings.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, FINTRAC can issue administrative monetary penalties for non-compliance; maximum penalties for entities have been raised in recent regulations to CAD 1,000,000 or more per violation, and for individuals CAD 500,000. Criminal prosecution for failure to register as a money services business, laundering proceeds of crime under Criminal Code s.462.31, or participation in a criminal organisation can result in imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who control AML compliance and knowingly arrange or direct transactions for the purpose of concealing proceeds may be charged personally under the Criminal Code and PCMLTFA, face imprisonment, be personally fined, and be ordered by the court to make restitution to FINTRAC or the Crown. Alberta authorities may impose a director disqualification order and deny the ULC's continued registration if senior management is complicit in AML breaches.
