UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Primary federal AML/CFT legislation is the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, SC 2000, c 17 (PCMLTFA), and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Regulations, SOR/2002-184. FINTRAC's beneficial-ownership requirements generally identify individuals who directly or indirectly own or control 25% or more of a corporation, or who exercise control through other means. The Canada Business Corporations Act, RSC 1985, c C-44, ss 21.301-21.304, requires a federal corporation to maintain an individuals-with-significant-control register, but those provisions do not automatically apply to an Alberta corporation. Alberta corporations must maintain corporate records under the Business Corporations Act, RSA 2000, c B-9, and its regulations; Alberta does not operate a generally public beneficial-ownership register for ordinary Alberta corporations. Corporate service providers, financial institutions and other reporting entities must comply with the PCMLTFA, FINTRAC guidance, identity verification, recordkeeping, suspicious-transaction reporting and source-of-funds requirements. Relevant sanctions legislation includes the United Nations Act, RSC 1985, c U-2, the Special Economic Measures Act, SC 1992, c 17, and the Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act, SC 2017, c 21.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

An Alberta corporation must maintain a registered office in Alberta and an address for service under the Business Corporations Act. A commercial virtual office may be acceptable if it is a genuine authorized address, receives and promptly forwards legal documents, maintains required corporate records or provides lawful access to them, and satisfies the registry agent, bank, tax authorities and licensing bodies. A mail-drop-only address, undisclosed nominee arrangement or address lacking operational access is high risk. For executive search and specialist technology headhunting, a virtual office may support registration, but the company should maintain documented Canadian management, client contracts, recruitment records, privacy controls, payroll or contractor controls where applicable, and evidence of actual business activity. A virtual address does not replace any Alberta employment-agency licensing, privacy, tax, banking or substance requirement.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains fully liberalized current-account and capital-account convertibility; exchange controls were abolished in 1951. There are no governmental approvals or FX-license hurdles to convert CAD, USD, or other major currencies for profit remittance. The only substantive barriers are tax-driven: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends paid by the Alberta ULC (statutory 25%, reduced to 5% or 15% under the Canada-US Treaty upon proper filing of NR301 and W-8BEN-E), thin-capitalization limits under ITA section 18(4) for non-resident shareholder debt (maximum 1.5:1 debt-to-equity), and hybrid-mismatch recharacterization risk where the ULC is disregarded for US tax purposes under Article IV(6) of the Canada-US Treaty. Treasury should document all capital contributions via share subscription or written intercompany loan agreements to preserve clear reclassification chains.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Canada. There is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion percentage, or surrender obligation for export or cross-border service receipts. The ULC may retain US dollars and other foreign currency indefinitely in onshore or offshore accounts. FINTRAC only imposes reporting thresholds: international EFTs of C$10,000 or more must be reported by the bank, and cross-border currency movements above C$10,000 require transporter declarations. For headhunting service exports, Statistics Canada's Census of International Transactions may require periodic service-revenue reporting, but this is statistical, not a surrender or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange-control agency. FX policy is administered under the Currency Act in full coordination with OSFI for prudential regulation, FINTRAC for AML/CFT supervision, and the Department of Finance for sanctions and capital-account policy. Canada is an IMF Article VIII member.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise B2B is rated High for low-value retainer receipts and contractor disbursements in USD, EUR, and GBP, and Medium as the sole treasury account. Wise Canada Ltd. is a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business, not a Schedule I bank; it is not CDIC-insured, cannot support commercial LCs, statutory licensing deposit custody, trust/escrow segregation, or high-value Lynx/LVTS clearing, and its virtual account details are not a full substitute for an operational bank account. Recommended architecture: Schedule I primary operating account plus Wise B2B as a supplementary payout rail for sub-CAD-50,000 multi-currency flows.
