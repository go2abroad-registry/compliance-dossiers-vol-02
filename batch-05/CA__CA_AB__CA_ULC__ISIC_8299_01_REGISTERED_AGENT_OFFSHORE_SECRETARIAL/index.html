Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), federal tax administration for GST/HST, Part I corporate income tax, and Alberta provincial corporate tax under the Alberta Tax Collection Agreement. Primary portal: CRA My Business Account. Alberta Corporate Income Tax is administered by the CRA, not a separate Alberta revenue authority.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that requires a Canadian tax identification number must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN). The standard procedure is: (1) Access CRA's 'Register a Business with the CRA' online portal or CRA's GST/HST NETFILE; (2) Submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' via mail or fax if not using the online portal; (3) Supply the foreign entity's legal name, country of incorporation, permanent establishment status in Canada, director/resident information, fiscal year-end, and NAICS/ISIC business activity code; (4) Indicate the required program accounts (e.g., GST/HST under account prefix 'RT', corporate income tax under 'RM'); (5) Upon successful registration, CRA issues the 15-character BN, which serves as the statutory TIN for both federal and Alberta tax purposes. A non-resident entity with a registered agent/offshore secretarial business may be required to name a resident representative for service of process. Registration can also be completed by filing Form RC1 after receiving a letter from CRA demanding a return. The BN is typically issued immediately online or within 3-5 weeks by mail.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (ULC may be treated as a pass-through or disregarded entity for certain foreign tax classifications, particularly under a US check-the-box election; however, for Canadian income tax purposes the ULC is taxed as an entity-level corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act. The provincial Alberta rate applies on taxable income allocated to Alberta.).

Available election forms

For cross-border classification, the unlimited liability corporation (ULC) may file Form 8832 (Entity Classification Election) with the US Internal Revenue Service to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity for US tax purposes. Under the Canadian Income Tax Act, there is no analogous 'check-the-box' election; a ULC is automatically a corporation for Canadian tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.4, 18.6, and 18.7 of the Income Tax Act may apply where non-resident shareholders use the ULC's hybrid character to avoid tax, and specific anti-avoidance rules may require a designation election under subsection 216(1) if the ULC provides services in Canada. Furthermore, an Alberta ULC may elect to have a representative share structure under the Alberta Business Corporations Act, but this is a corporate law election, not a tax status election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated and tax-exempt supplies) made over any rolling twelve-consecutive-month period. Once exceeded, the entity must register within 29 days. Voluntary registration is available, which may be advisable for an offshore secretarial provider to claim input tax credits on Canadian expenses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered supply is zero-rated as an export where (a) tangible personal property is exported from Canada in accordance with Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, or (b) a service is supplied to a non-resident who is outside Canada and the service is not performed in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. For offshore secretarial/registered agent services, zero-rating is available if the service recipient is a non-resident person, the service does not relate to Canadian real property or goods, and the service is not 'in respect of' taxable property or a service rendered in Canada. The supply must be made to a non-resident; the supplier must also be a GST/HST registrant. The services are zero-raised at 0% and claimed as input tax credit eligible; no GST/HST is collected from the offshore client.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Invoice or B13A Export Declaration filed with the Canada Border Services Agency for goods; for services, an equivalent contract/service order identifying offshore recipient)
  • Bill of Lading / Waybill (or alternative shipping document evidencing physical export of goods, where applicable; for services, retained records of offshore client identity and delivery of service)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt, or wire transfer/swift confirmation evidencing payment received from outside Canada and credited to the Canadian entity's bank account

Canada does not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoice pre-approval, or digital stamping through the CRA. GST/HST registrants must still issue valid invoices containing prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act (vendor name or trading name, BN, invoice date, description of supply, amount, GST charged). GST/HST returns are required to be filed electronically if the entity's total annual revenue exceeds CAD 250,000 (mandatory GST/HST E-file/EDI). The CRA is participating in the Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework built on the Peppol architecture, but submission through that network is voluntary; there is no statutory API clearance or fiscal stamp requirement.

CRA may reassess a claimant within four years from the original notice of assessment for ordinary negligence, and within ten years where a misrepresentation (including fraudulent or gross negligence) is established. Zero-rated export claims that lack documentary proof of export or that are based on offshore services mischaracterized as supplied in Canada will be subject to disallowance, reassessment of the GST at 5% (or HST where applicable), interest, and penalties. The understated liability penalty is 10% of the understated amount attributable to a false statement or omission made knowingly, or in circumstances amounting to gross negligence; if fraud is proven, the taxpayer may be liable to a minimum of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded. Criminal penalties for GST fraud include fines of up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment of up to five years. Additionally, failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day benchmark may be treated as evidence that no true export sale occurred, resulting in full reversal of the zero-rating claim plus arrears interest and potential civil penalties. For ULC entities, director and shareholder liability may extend to unpaid GST/HST subject to the Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible across current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls restricting capital inflows, profit outflows, dividends, or interest remittances. Legal impediments are limited to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (default 25% on dividends, reduced by treaty), FATCA/CRS reporting obligations, and AML/sanctions screening. Repatriation of registered capital and earnings is unencumbered, subject to OSFI or FINTRAC reporting of cross-border wire transactions exceeding CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory foreign exchange conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate export receipts to a designated bank account. Canadian exporters may hold foreign currency balances in resident banks; non-resident-controlled ULCs remain able to invoice and settle in contractually agreed currencies without surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Bangue du Canada); foreign exchange administration is carried out by the Bank of Canada, the Department of Finance, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), under the Currency Act and the Foreign Exchange Regulations, with no standing exchange control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for offshore digital EMI (e-money institution) status. Canada has no specific EMI license; foreign EMIs cannot onboard clients as deposit-taking or clearing institutions. A foreign entity licensing as a money service business (MSB) under FINTRAC may hold a money transmitter license in Alberta, but this does not permit banking or direct CAD clearing without a Schedule I bank sponsor. Such structures are feasible only as payment corridor facilitators, not as primary treasury repositories.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections, including shareholder loans and equity subscriptions, are transmitted via SWIFT MT103 with explicit purpose codes (e.g., OECD BPM6 code 'CF' for capital formation or 'ED' for external debt) and must reference the ULC escrow account. All wires over CAD 10,000 are reportable through FINTRAC cross-border wire report (GTR). Funds are subject to source-of-funds verification, OFAC/CATS sanctions screening, and anti-money-laundering review before credit to the Capital Escrow Account. CAD settlements use the CDSX clearing infrastructure; USD payments may clear through CHIPS/Fedwire or Canadian correspondent banks.Trade and secretarial retainer payments are effected either through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account service fees or through documentary commercial letters of credit for goods transactions.
