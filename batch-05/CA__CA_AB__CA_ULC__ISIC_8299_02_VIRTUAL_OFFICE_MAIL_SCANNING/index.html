Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). National corporate income tax, GST/HST and payroll authority. Main portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MyBA), GST/HST NETFILE, and T2 Corporation Internet Filing, accessible via canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate or continue the ULC in Alberta under the Business Corporations Act (Alberta) and obtain the Alberta Corporation Access Code from the provincial Corporate Registry. Step 2 - Register with the Canada Revenue Agency through Business Registration Online (BRO) using the access code, or file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' with the CRA, to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (statutory TIN) and, as applicable, the GST/HST account (RT), payroll account (RP), and import/export account (RM). Step 3 - Appoint an Alberta registered agent and maintain a registered office with a physical address, both mandatory for a foreign-owned ULC. Step 4 - File initial and annual T2 corporate income tax returns within 6 months after each taxation year-end and remit GST/HST according to the assigned filing cycle (annual, quarterly, or monthly) based on taxable-supply volume.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The Alberta ULC is a separate Canadian resident taxpayer for income tax and GST/HST purposes (entity-level taxation), but the unlimited liability share structure is expressly pass-through-eligible: a foreign shareholder may elect under its home-country rules (e.g., US IRC Section 7701 check-the-box) to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership. Territorial scope: federal GST at 5% in Alberta, with no provincial sales tax and no tax-exempt zone status.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain an internal check-the-box regime. The principal mechanisms available to a ULC are: (i) shareholder-level check-the-box elections under US IRC Section 7701 to create a hybrid entity; (ii) CRA administrative recognition of ULC hybridity (e.g., Income Tax Folio S4-F3-C1) and allocation of Canadian-source income under the Canada-US treaty tie-breaker; (iii) Income Tax Act elections, including the Section 216 optional net rental return for non-resident recipients and the Section 219(1) non-resident deemed-interest return for a ULC with non-resident shareholders; (iv) GST/HST elections, including the Section 156 ETA election for nil consideration between related registrants and the GST quick method election; and (v) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA sections 18.15 to 18.24, which operate principally as a deduction-denial regime rather than an elective regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (Canadian dollars) in annual worldwide taxable supplies, excluding supplies of financial services and capital assets, in any trailing 12 consecutive months; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. A mirror threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of cross-border digital services under the simplified GST/HST registration framework effective 1 July 2021.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered Alberta ULC may claim the 0% GST rate on exported supplies. Services supplied to a non-resident recipient (including virtual-office, mail scanning, and digital document processing) are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supply is made for the non-resident's consumption or use wholly outside Canada and is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Physical mail and goods forwarded abroad must in fact be exported and supported by the exporter's books and records. The CMRA must obtain the foreign client's identity, jurisdiction, and GST registration status, issue invoices clearly designating zero-rated supplies, and retain evidence that the benefit or use of the service occurs outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A Export Declaration, or Form A64 Mail/Commercial Export Declaration for postal shipments, with CBSA export transaction records)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air waybill) issued by the carrier evidencing movement of exported goods out of Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank/FX confirmation) evidencing receipt of the export consideration in foreign currency within the 180-day repatriation window
  • Zero-rated commercial invoice containing the supplier's BN/GST/HST number, invoice number, non-resident recipient address and status, and the zero-rated classification
  • Service agreement, purchase order, and written confirmation that the virtual-office, mail-scanning, or digital processing service is performed for and used wholly by the non-resident outside Canada
  • Electronic records, mail-scanning logs, and delivery receipts retained for at least 6 years under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act

No real-time government fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or per-invoice approval API is mandated in Canada. The CRA operates a self-assessment system requiring a registrant to issue a valid GST/HST invoice (full or simplified form) containing the prescribed particulars under section 223 of the Excise Tax Act, to retain electronic books and records for at least 6 years under section 286, and to file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or approved EDI/API channels. E-invoicing (including XML/EDI interchange and PEPPOL-style e-invoice) is accepted without advance CRA approval. For a virtual-office/mail-scanning business, the platform should emit recurring invoices showing the BN/GST/HST account number, consideration, and application of 5% GST or zero-rating on each supply.

The CRA may reassess a claim within 4 years of the original assessment, extended to 10 years where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is attributable to the taxpayer. Unsupported zero-rating is reversed with full tax clawback and compound daily interest. Civil penalties apply under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act for knowingly false or grossly negligent statements (typically 25% to 50% of the tax amount at issue) and under section 163.2 of the Income Tax Act for false statements and counselling; interest and penalties are mandatory. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act may result in imprisonment of up to 5 years and penal fines. Because the taxpayer is an Alberta ULC with unlimited liability, shareholders and directors remain jointly and severally liable for the entire CRA assessment, including interest and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible, and Canada generally imposes no exchange-control restrictions on current-account payments, capital transfers, foreign investment, or repatriation of profits. Transfers remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering requirements, tax withholding, corporate-law restrictions on distributions, bank policies, and applicable reporting. A Canadian ULC is a separate legal entity; its tax treatment is not automatically pass-through for Canadian tax purposes. A ULC may be treated as fiscally transparent by certain foreign jurisdictions, potentially creating hybrid-entity, treaty-benefit, withholding-tax, and foreign tax-credit issues. Obtain Canadian and relevant foreign tax advice before relying on pass-through treatment or treaty reductions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or general deadline requiring exporters to convert foreign-currency receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign currency or be converted through an authorized financial institution. Banks may request invoices, contracts, shipping or service records, and payment-purpose details under their AML, sanctions, and risk controls. Tax, customs, and statistical reporting obligations remain separate from currency-conversion rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada is Canada's central bank and conducts monetary policy. It does not administer general foreign-exchange controls. The Department of Finance Canada is responsible for federal financial-sector policy; FINTRAC administers Canada's anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing reporting regime. The Alberta Ministry of Treasury Board and Finance administers provincial financial-sector matters within its mandate.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and transfer services, subject to provider eligibility, Canadian regulatory status, and underwriting; low as a substitute for a Canadian domestic deposit account. An offshore electronic-money institution is not necessarily authorized to take deposits or provide regulated payment services in Canada. Verify whether the provider is registered or otherwise permitted under the Retail Payment Activities Act regime, where applicable, and whether it is an authorized foreign bank or uses a Canadian regulated partner. Confirm safeguarding, account ownership, insolvency treatment, CAD clearing access, card-acquirer settlement eligibility, and restrictions on Canadian CMRA activity. Do not represent safeguarded e-money as a Canadian bank deposit or assume deposit insurance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 must be filed no later than six months after the fiscal year-end (a December 31 year-end is due June 30). The balance of tax payable is due within two months of the fiscal year-end, or within three months for an eligible CCPC in the preceding year; a non-resident-controlled ULC generally qualifies only for the two-month rule.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within six months after the fiscal year-end; Schedule 17 must accompany the T2; contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation must be completed by the filing deadline.

Deadline: · Frequency:

NR4 slips and the NR4 Summary are due by the end of February following the calendar year in which the payment was made; electronic filing is required where more than 50 NR4 slips are issued.
