Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary federal portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs and export declarations; Alberta corporate income tax is administered and collected by the CRA under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement with Alberta Treasury Board and Finance; Alberta corporate registry portal: Alberta Corporate Account (https://www.alberta.ca/incorporate-business).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities operating in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and the applicable program accounts before commencing tax filings. Procedure: (1) complete Alberta incorporation for the ULC or, if the foreign entity is deemed to be carrying on business in Canada, register extra-provincially with the Alberta Corporate Registry; (2) apply for a BN via the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or file paper Form RC1; a non-resident corporation with no Canadian permanent establishment should use Form RC171; (3) open the GST/HST program account (RT) once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, or apply voluntarily; (4) open the corporate income tax program account (RC) for tax return filing; (5) open the payroll program account (RP) if the entity has Canadian employees; (6) open the customs/import-export program account (RM) where physical goods are exported or imported. The GST/HST account number consists of the BN plus the two-letter program code 'RT' and a four-digit reference number, and functions as the VAT identifier for all electronic invoicing and refund documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Canadian Income Tax Act, an Alberta ULC is a person and a corporation and is subject to entity-level taxation in Canada; there is no domestic 'check-the-box' election that converts the ULC into a partnership or branch for Canadian federal tax purposes. The pass-through eligibility arises in foreign tax jurisdictions, particularly the United States, where the ULC's unlimited liability allows the U.S. member to file IRS Form 8832 and classify the ULC either as an association taxable as a corporation or as a disregarded entity/branch solely for U.S. federal tax purposes. Canada does not provide a parallel election; hybrid mismatch outcomes are instead addressed through Canadian domestic rules, the Multilateral Instrument and the relevant tax treaty provisions. For GST/HST purposes, no ULC-specific election exists, although registered persons may use ordinary elections under the Excise Tax Act (e.g., section 273 for transfer of business assets) where statutory conditions are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide annual taxable supplies from commercial activity in Canada, or more than CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter; for non-resident suppliers of digital products or services into Canada, the same CAD 30,000 registration threshold applies on Canadian-source supplies; voluntary registration is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered Alberta ULC may claim the 0% GST/HST rate on supplies of goods that are exported, or delivered to an address outside Canada and exported without further processing. Services, including subscription access to a web-novel serialization platform, may be zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient and the supply is not excluded; exclusions include services in relation to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, and services rendered to an individual present in Canada at the time of supply. For digital platform subscriptions, eligibility should be documented by the recipient's ordinary residence outside Canada, non-Canadian billing address and payment method, device/IP geolocation outside Canada, and contractual terms limiting access to foreign subscribers. GST/HST must still be collected on subscriptions supplied to Canadian-resident readers.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or CBSA ACI eManifest for physical goods) or other CBSA-approved proof of export
  • Commercial invoice and subscription or licensing contract evidencing supply to a non-resident recipient
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill for goods; for digital services, transaction metadata including device IP address, billing address, payment method and platform dashboard records
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank SWIFT/telegraphic transfer record or equivalent foreign-exchange documentation proving receipt of payment from outside Canada
  • For digital services, CRA-compliant records demonstrating that the downstream service is not attributable to Canadian real property, tangible goods situated in Canada, or an individual present in Canada at the time of access

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime comparable to those found in some Latin American jurisdictions. There is no prior-approval API requirement before an invoice is issued. A GST/HST registrant must, however, retain invoices and records containing the prescribed Excise Tax Act information, including the registrant's BN, date, description, tax charged and applicable rate, and must file GST/HST returns electronically through CRA My Business Account or GST/HST NETFILE where the applicable filing threshold is met. For physical exports, electronic export declarations are submitted through the CBSA CARM portal (ACI/eManifest, Form B13A); for digital platform supplies, CRA requires platform operators to keep electronic transaction records sufficient to establish place of supply and recipient location, but relies on post-issuance audit rather than invoice pre-clearance.

False or unsupported export zero-rating claims expose the ULC to GST/HST reassessment at the full 5% headline rate plus interest, denial of refunds, and clawback of input tax credits previously claimed. CRA may extend the normal four-year reassessment period to seven years where misrepresentation occurred and may disregard the limitation period entirely where fraud or wilful default is established. Civil penalties include a 25% penalty on net tax sought to be evaded, gross negligence penalties, and penalties for failure to maintain adequate books and records; criminal prosecution can result in fines and imprisonment under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act. The exporter bears the burden of proof: incomplete customs documentation, missing B13A/export declaration, absent bill of lading, or unsatisfactory inward remittance evidence will cause the zero-rating to be overturned retrospectively.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; there are no statutory exchange controls or barriers to repatriate capital or remit profits. Dividend remittances from an Alberta Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) to a non-resident shareholder are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at a statutory rate of 25%, reducible under an applicable tax treaty, and to transfer pricing rules under Section 247 of the Income Tax Act. No advance approval, central bank permission, or ministerial license is required for profit repatriation, provided the entity is in good standing and arm's length transfer pricing documentation is maintained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory surrender obligation, mandatory conversion timeline, or minimum conversion ratio on export proceeds, including digital goods or subscription revenues. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency; however, for ULC treasury purposes, CAD conversion is recommended to satisfy Alberta corporate tax installments and to avoid foreign exchange exposure, but it is not legally mandated.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in concurrence with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) of Canada; exchange control policy is administered under the Currency Act and the Financial Administration Act, while bank prudential supervision rests with OSFI.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: D (Conditional) for primary treasury reliance. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, or Stripe Treasury are not chartered banks and are treated as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. They must be registered with FINTRAC; however, they are limited in Canada to e-money issuance, remittance, and settlement services, and cannot provide margin facilities, hold client funds as deposits, or serve as principal clearing accounts for a ULC's capital escrow requirements. They may be used for low-value author disbursements or cross-border payment execution, but prudent treasury policy requires a Tier-1 domestic bank as the sole CIP (Clearing and Settlement) account holder for capital injection and annuity subscription flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols must reference the 'SWIFT MT103' message type. For equity capital injection from a foreign shareholder, the remittance field (Field 50K) must cite the underlying entity name and account number; Field 72 (Sender to Receiver Information) must contain the beneficiary reference: '/POC/ Equity Injection - CA__CA_AB__CA_ULC__ISIC_9000_01_WEBNOVEL_SERIAL_PLATFORM'. The transfer must also include the 21-character purpose code 'CAN-EQFL-9000' in the user-defined section, signifying a non-resident equity foreign direct investment into a Canadian digital content platform under Statistics Canada's balance of payments coding. The Canadian receiving institution will require a KYC verification package matching the remittance name, a copy of the CBCA or AB Business Corporations Act contribution document showing number of shares issued, and an accountant's confirmation that the inward remittance is on capital account and not a financial arrangement subject to interest reporting. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5 million aggregate cross-border non-arm's-length transactions or CAD 50 million gross revenue triggers contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation and Form T106 filing obligations

Economic substance scope: No UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations apply; the CRA invokes the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s. 245 and judicial doctrine requiring real commercial substance and business purpose. A ULC cannot be an empty shell used solely to access Canadian treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Section 239 Income Tax Act: fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the amount of tax evaded; imprisonment up to 5 years for filing false returns

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions; CRA can assess directors personally for the corporation's unpaid penalties, with no minimum time limit for intentional evasion.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under the GAAR (s. 245) to deny treaty benefits and reattribute income to the foreign principal; interest and penalties up to 50% of net tax avoided

Director personal liability: Under s. 160, directors receiving transferred property are jointly liable; corporate veil may be pierced in criminal cases, supporting restitution orders, travel bans, and extradition in tax fraud cases.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Penalty of $24,000 per month for failure to furnish documentation (maximum $240,000) plus a 10% transfer-pricing adjustment penalty on net adjustment amounts exceeding a $5,000,000 threshold

Director personal liability: Corporate veil is pierced if directors knowingly supervised non-compliance; CRA may recover from directors using assessment provisions under the Income Tax Act.
Anti-Money Laundering / E-Commerce Payment Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 2,000,000 for entities; criminal fines at court's discretion and imprisonment up to 5 years for corporate officers

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable if consent or participation facilitated the offence; travel is restricted via court orders and director bank accounts may be frozen during investigation.
