Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Alberta unlimited liability corporation; a nominal subscription for one common share is sufficient. No escrow account or capital deposit requirement is imposed under the ABCA for a standard share subscription; subscription funds may be held in the incorporator's trust account until issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Alberta ULC: (1) incorporate or extra-provincially register under Alberta Business Corporations Act; (2) obtain federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online; (3) register for GST/HST account (mandatory if taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or voluntary); (4) register for import/export (RM) account if cross-border goods; (5) file Alberta corporate income tax registration with CRA (CRA acts as agent for Alberta). Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Alberta ULC is a Canadian-resident corporation taxed on worldwide income under Income Tax Act s.2(1); no Canadian flow-through election. Pass_Through_Eligible: for foreign shareholders, the ULC may be elected as a transparent entity under foreign tax law (e.g., US check-the-box).

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box election. For US purposes, Form 8832 can elect partnership/disregarded-entity tax classification. Canadian anti-hybrid rules (ITA s.18.6) and Canada-US Treaty Art. IV(6) may restrict hybrid mismatch benefits; non-resident shareholders may need a Canadian tax ruling/opinion on the ULC's treaty residence and limitation-on-benefits eligibility.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — taxable supplies made in Canada in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; for non-resident digital suppliers, an additional threshold applies when taxable supplies to Canadian recipients exceed CAD 30,000 in any 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated (0% GST/HST) under ETA Schedule VI Part V, ss.23 (services) and 25.1 (intangible personal property) for supplies made to a non-resident person where the supply is not for use in Canada and does not relate to Canadian real estate or tangible personal property situated in Canada. Digital webtoon licensing through Apple/Google IAP qualifies when the platform licensee/consumer is outside Canada and usage occurs outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration via CBSA/Canadian International Trade Single Window (for physical goods only; not required for pure digital deliveries)
  • Commercial Invoice / digital licensing agreement evidencing supply to non-resident
  • Bills of Lading or airway waybill (if tangible goods embedded)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT / payment ledger proving foreign currency receipt
  • Non-resident status evidence (e.g., W-8BEN-E, foreign jurisdiction registration, IP address/geolocation records for digital consumers)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance API requirement in Canada. Mandatory electronic GST/HST filing and remittance for registrants with annual net tax remittance of at least CAD 50,000; CRA supports API/XML filing via GST/HST NETFILE and Represent a Client.

CRA scrutinizes export zero-rating claims; failure to keep sufficient documentation triggers reassessment at 5% GST/HST, with gross negligence penalties under ETA s.285 (25% of tax understated), interest, and potential criminal prosecution. False export refund claims may be clawed back with fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment under Criminal Code s.380.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible, free-floating currency. Both current account and capital account transactions are generally unrestricted, including dividend distributions, management fees, interest payments, and repatriation of contributed capital. No approval is required for conversions between CAD and foreign currencies. However, all cross-border electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and intentionally structured transactions below the threshold to avoid reporting are prohibited. For corporate tax purposes, the CRA may disregard unfunded or informal shareholder injections if they are not supported by proper board resolutions, subscription agreements, loan notes, or capitalization records.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose export proceeds surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios, or statutory timelines for repatriating export receipts. A Canadian exporter, including a webtoon syndication ULC, may hold export proceeds in foreign currency accounts offshore or domestically and may convert or use them freely. Daily receipts and disbursements of C$10,000 or more must be captured in FINTRAC reporting by Canadian financial institutions or money services businesses. For accounting and tax, foreign currency receipts must be reported in Canadian dollars using the prevailing exchange rate or the CRA functional-currency rules where eligible.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy is carried out by the Department of Finance Canada, with prudential oversight of financial institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada applies a liberalized, non-discretionary exchange regime under IMF Article VIII, and there is no centralized foreign exchange administration requiring general transaction permissions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Ramp Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional — operationally viable for payment collection and payout aggregation, but legally constrained. A foreign fintech EMI (e.g., an FCA-licensed UK EMI or an EU e-money institution) may be used as a payment processor by the Canadian ULC if the EMI is not itself carrying on business in Canada as a money services business. If the EMI solicits Canadian clients, holds Canadian-origin funds, or provides cash-to-cash or account-to-account conversion services in Canada, it must register with FINTRAC as a money services business and may also trigger provincial money-lender licensing. The Canadian ULC should maintain its primary treasury and clearing account with a Canadian Schedule I bank, while the EMI is used solely as an ancillary collection and disbursement rail with segregated sub-accounts and a contractual audit trail. Any virtual-currency settlement or rapid off-ramp through an offshore EMI will raise transaction-monitoring alerts and should be replaced by direct bank wires wherever commercially feasible.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from foreign shareholders or parent companies should be executed via SWIFT MT103 wires referencing the correct purpose codes and underlying transaction documents. For equity contributions, state the remittance as 'Contribution to Capital' and attach the board resolution, subscription agreement, and share allotment record. For shareholder loans, use 'Loan Repayment' or 'Intra-Company Loan' with a signed loan agreement, maturity schedule, and interest-rate document. The wire should denominate in CAD or USD and must be sent to the CA-ULC's account at a Canadian Schedule I bank through the bank's correspondent clearing network. For any single transfer of C$10,000 or more, the receiving institution will report to FINTRAC, so the ULC must maintain originator and beneficiary identity evidence, beneficial ownership records, and source-of-funds statements. If the injection is connected to an acquisition, the CRA may require completion of Form T2032 or, more commonly, a corporate tax information notice if there are any cross-border non-resident dealings under section 212 of the Income Tax Act. The ULC should avoid splitting a single injection into multiple smaller wires to sidestep reporting, as that constitutes prohibited structuring.For trade settlements and royalties involving foreign licensors, artists, and distributors, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for a single high-value transfer and SWIFT MT101 for batch payment initiation. For physical merchandise or where documentary compliance is necessary, a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by a Canadian chartered bank and advised through a foreign correspondent bank may be used, though digital webtoon syndication typically requires no LC. For lower-value recurring licensing payments, the ULC can use ACH International (IAT) transactions, Fedwire or CHIPS for USD clearing via correspondent banks, and the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or successor Real-Time Rail (RTR) for high-value CAD settlements. All outbound disbursements above C$10,000 are FINTRAC-reportable, and the ULC must include precise payment descriptions, beneficiary legal entities, and supporting contracts to prevent monitoring false positives.Outright deliverable forward contracts in CAD/USD and other freely convertible crosses executed with a Canadian Schedule I bank, Schedule II bank, or a registered derivatives dealer under the Bank Act and applicable provincial securities exemptions.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts on CAD crosses are permitted in Canada, but because CAD is fully convertible, NDFs are generally used only when a counterparty bank is subject to a non-convertible jurisdiction or when a deliverable settlement is operationally impractical; the NDF must be entered with an OSFI-authorized financial institution and included in the ULC's FX mandate.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for medium-term treasury matching, limited to counterparties with an OSFI-recognized credit rating and an executed ISDA/CSA or Canadian Master Agreement.Plain vanilla European-style FX call and put options transacted through a registered Canadian bank or investment dealer, with strike prices and tenors consistent with the ULC's commercial exposure.Exotic or structured forwards containing embedded options, cancellable features, or leverage are permissible only if the ULC maintains a documented FX hedging policy, the instrument is sold to the ULC as a sophisticated commercial counterparty, and the bank confirms in writing that the hedge is reasonably correlated with identified webtoon revenue and licensing exposures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to file corporate tax return

Statutory sanction: Penalty of $50 per day, capped at $5,000; plus interest on unpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for corporate tax arrears; potential director's liability orders under provincial law
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the underpaid tax; recharacterization of transactions under GAAR

Director personal liability: Criminal liability for willful evasion; possible imprisonment up to 2 years and fines
Improper profit repatriation or dividend withholding

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 50% of unpaid WHT; interest charges; potential criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Personal liability for unpaid taxes; disqualification from office; possible jail time for evasion
Shell company or artificial structure

Statutory sanction: GAAR application to deny tax benefits; penalties for tax shelter

Director personal liability: Imprisonment for 2 years, substantial fines, and personal liability for tax debts
