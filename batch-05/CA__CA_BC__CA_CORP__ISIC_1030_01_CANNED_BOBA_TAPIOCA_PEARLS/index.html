Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administration for Part I corporate income tax, GST/HST and payroll. Registration is handled through the federal Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, and ongoing compliance is managed in the CRA My Business Account (MyBA) portal. British Columbia administers the separate 7% Provincial Sales Tax (PST) via the BC Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC / PST Account). The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs, export declarations and the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the British Columbia Business Corporations Act (BC Corporate Online) — the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) is then issued automatically by the CRA through BRO for the corporation's income tax account; 2) add the statutory accounts to the BN: GST/HST account (account type RT) once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or voluntarily from an earlier effective date; payroll account (RP) if employees are hired; import/export account (RM) required before any customs clearance activity; 3) register separately with BC eTaxBC for a PST vendor account before making PST-collectable sales in British Columbia; 4) for a foreign-parented Canadian subsidiary: appoint resident-Canadian directors (at least 25% of the board for a BC corporation), file the initial notice of directors, and submit Form RC1 to add/confirm accounts; the corporation is itself the registered taxpayer and no extra-territorial registration of the foreign parent is required beyond CRA's non-resident carrying-on-business rules.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act with no pass-through to shareholders; Canada has no check-the-box classification regime. Canada applies residence-based worldwide taxation (not a territorial regime): the entity's active business income in British Columbia is subject to a combined approximate rate of 27% (federal general rate 15% after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% general reduction, plus BC general corporate rate 12%). A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) would qualify for the CAD 500,000 small-business limit (federal 9% + BC 2%); a foreign-controlled Canadian corporation is not a CCPC and is taxed at the 27% general rate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act with no pass-through to shareholders; Canada has no check-the-box classification regime. Canada applies residence-based worldwide taxation (not a territorial regime): the entity's active business income in British Columbia is subject to a combined approximate rate of 27% (federal general rate 15% after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% general reduction, plus BC general corporate rate 12%). A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) would qualify for the CAD 500,000 small-business limit (federal 9% + BC 2%); a foreign-controlled Canadian corporation is not a CCPC and is taxed at the 27% general rate. Shareholders are separately taxed on dividends under Canada's dividend gross-up and integration mechanism.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists for a Canadian business corporation; CRA treats an entity with corporate attributes as a corporation even where a foreign jurisdiction (e.g., a foreign LLC) has elected flow-through status. Available statutory elections relevant to this entity: (i) GST/HST group registration election under Excise Tax Act s. 156, allowing related resident corporations to be treated as a single registrant for GST/HST purposes; (ii) associated-corporation business-limit allocation agreement under Income Tax Act s. 125(3) (CCPCs only); (iii) voluntary GST/HST registration election for a small-supplier exporter in order to claim input tax credits on export inputs (ETA s. 240(2)); (iv) for hybrid-mismatch situations, the Income Tax Act ss. 18.2/18.21 anti-hybrid rules (in force for payments made after 30 June 2022) disallow deduction/non-inclusion outcomes for cross-border arrangements — they contain ordering rules but no elective reclassification of entity status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — worldwide taxable (non-zero-rated) supplies made in a calendar quarter plus the three preceding calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148). Registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded and is voluntary below it. There is no GST threshold for a non-resident carrying on business in Canada. BC PST vendor registration is required as soon as PST-collectable sales or imports for sale in BC commence, with an out-of-province vendor rule applying where remote sellers' taxable sales into BC exceed CAD 10,000 annually; an exporter shipping only outside Canada is not a PST vendor if it holds proper export documentation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Canned/aseptic tapioca pearls exported from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 5 of the Excise Tax Act where the supplier (a) sells the goods under a written contract contemplating export to a destination outside Canada; (b) ships or delivers the goods outside Canada within a reasonable time after the supply (CRA practice generally accepts 60 days for routine ocean freight); (c) retains documentary proof of export; and (d) has no knowledge that the goods will be diverted back into the Canadian market. Offshore services supplied to non-residents (e.g., formulation, toll production or aseptic packaging services for an overseas boba brand) qualify for zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 where the service is performed outside Canada, or is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada. Domestically, tapioca pearls sold as 'food or beverage for human consumption' may qualify as zero-rated basic groceries under Schedule VI, Part III; however, if the canned product is characterized as a ready-to-drink beverage with added sweeteners, it is taxable at 5% GST and 7% PST in BC — exporters should therefore rely strictly on the export evidence chain rather than product characterization to sustain 0% GST.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A filed electronically via the Export Reporting System / CARM) for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 or any controlled goods — include HS 1903 (tapioca pearls and starch-based substitutes), commodity description, weight, value, origin and destination
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or Cargo Control Document / Forwarder's Cargo Receipt) evidencing departure from Canada and overseas delivery of the shipment
  • Commercial Invoice and signed sales contract specifying Incoterms, payment terms and foreign destination (CRA looks to the full chain of commercial documents per GST/HST Memorandum 4.2 'Exports')
  • Certificate of Origin (e.g., CPTPP or Canada-Chile preference claim) where the foreign buyer or the Canadian exporter requests preferential tariff treatment at destination
  • Freight forwarder evidence of arrival and delivery overseas, together with the SWIFT/telegraphic-transfer inward payment receipt or letter-of-credit settlement confirmation evidencing remittance of export proceeds

Canada has no national real-time electronic-invoicing clearance or digital-stamping mandate (no continuous transaction controls). GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by registrants with annual taxable revenue above CAD 1 million, and any net remittance exceeding CAD 10,000 must be paid by electronic funds transfer through MyBA; ordinary commercial invoices in accepted formats remain the statutory support. At the border, CBSA's CARM portal is the statutory single-window platform: export declarations (B13A), customs accounting, eManifest pre-arrival data and commercial accounting declarations are filed electronically through CARM or agency EDI/API integrations. A paper-based 'exit' stamp is not required; the export evidence pack is the transactional control for zero-rating purposes.

CRA scrutinizes export zero-rating claims on the principle that documentary proof must exist at the time of supply. Where the s. 5 (Schedule VI, Part V) conditions are not met, or goods are diverted to the Canadian market, CRA reassesses the supply as taxable at 5% GST, assesses uncollected tax on the exporter (which is commercially unrecoverable from the foreign buyer), and adds daily compound interest under ETA s. 280 and late-filing/remittance penalties. False statements or omissions in a GST/HST return, made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence, attract a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the tax or refund misstated (ETA s. 285). Criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 326 (and ITA s. 239) carries a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years on indictment. CBSA enforcement may also impose administrative monetary penalties for false export declarations. While Canada imposes no statutory deadline for repatriation of export proceeds, the 180-day benchmark is the conservative compliance window given the SWIFT LC / TT payment rails used by this entity; receipts materially beyond that period should be documented as genuine trade credit granted under a written agreement to avoid shareholder-benefit or presumed-service recharacterization. Related-party export pricing will additionally attract transfer-pricing review under ITA s. 247, with a 10% penalty where the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of 10% of gross revenue and CAD 5 million.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full convertibility for both the current and capital accounts under Article VIII of the International Monetary Fund Articles of Agreement. The Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bank of Canada Act impose no restriction on the acquisition, holding, conversion, or repatriation of foreign currency or CAD. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely; the Bank of Canada's intervention capability is limited and no fixed parity is maintained. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, interest, royalties, management fees, and shareholder loan repayments may be paid to non-residents freely in any currency, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act and to FINTRAC reporting for electronic funds transfers (EFTs) of CAD 10,000 or more (or foreign equivalent). No statutory approval from OSFI, the Bank of Canada, or the CRA is required for capital repatriation; no black-market premium or dual-exchange-rate mechanism exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose a surrender, repatriation, or conversion timeline for export proceeds. Exporters of canned boba tapioca pearls may retain export receivables in USD, CNY, or any freely convertible currency in any domicile without obligation to convert to CAD or to bring funds into Canada. The sole reporting obligation is the FINTRAC EFT cross-border report for the transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, which is automatically filed by the remitting or receiving financial institution; no exporter self-filing is required. Foreign exchange transactions at Canadian banks must be executed at a commercially reasonable market rate, subject to the Federal Retail Payment Activities Act (R.S.C., 2021, c. 8) where applicable to payment service providers, but no centralized conversion rate or surrender ratio exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), administered in conjunction with the Minister of Finance for monetary policy and currency issuance. Banking operations and foreign-exchange dealing are supervised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions Act. Cross-border financial transaction reporting is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control law and imposes no exchange-control permit regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium — supplementary rail only, primary account prohibited. Foreign digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not recognized as banks under the Bank Act and are legally barred from accepting Canadian deposits unless authorized as a bank under the Bank Act or licensed as a money service business (MSB) under PCMLTFA and registered with FINTRAC. Since September 2024, the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada requires any payment service provider that holds, uses, or directs funds in a retail payment function — including foreign EMIs processing payments for Canadian users — to register with the Bank of Canada; operating without registration is an offense. An overseas EMI may not issue a Canadian-domiciled bank account number, may not participate in Lynx or Payments Canada clearing, and may not serve as the account of record for tax remittance, payroll, CRA payments, or letter-of-credit issuance. Use a Schedule I bank (Big Five) as the primary treasury and clearing house; use EMIs exclusively as an auxiliary low-value disbursement channel for suppliers in mainland China or Southeast Asia that do not require LC documentary settlement.
