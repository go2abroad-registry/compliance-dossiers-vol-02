Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; British Columbia Ministry of Finance — provincial sales tax (PST), employer health tax, property transfer tax and other BC levies. Portals: CRA My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services) and BC eTaxBC / BC Corporate Online.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not resident in Canada may apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) through CRA's Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1. The BN is the statutory tax identification number for GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll, import/export, and potential excise duties. For a federal or provincial Business Corporation, the BN is normally issued upon incorporation, and separate GST/HST account registration is required once taxable supply revenue exceeds CAD 30,000 in a quarterly cumulative basis or if voluntarily registered. Non-resident vendors may use the CRA simplified GST/HST registration for foreign suppliers (Form GST20 and specialized portal) and must file via CRA's approved electronic channels. A BC PST registration with the BC Ministry of Finance is required separately when a vendor sells taxable goods or services in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box election or a general entity classification election. A Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to federal and provincial income tax on its worldwide income in Canada and is not eligible for pass-through treatment. Available statutory elections relevant to a foreign-owned Canadian corporation include the Income Tax Act s.216 election for non-residents to file returns on Canadian rental income, s.217 non-resident election for certain trust/custodian income, s.216.1 deemed disposition election on ceasing Canadian residence for tax purposes if applicable, and the Excise Tax Act s.238.1 simplified GST/HST accounting election for non-resident suppliers. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act are mandatory and cannot be elected away.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST small supplier registration threshold is CAD 30,000 in gross worldwide revenue from taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in any one calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters under Excise Tax Act ss.148-149. Once the threshold is exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. For BC PST, there is generally no equivalent small supplier exemption for non-resident vendors making taxable sales into British Columbia; a PST registration must be obtained before making a taxable supply in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods made in Canada is eligible for zero-rating under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the goods are exported as soon as reasonably possible after the supply is completed, normally no later than 30 days, and the supplier obtains sufficient documentary evidence of export. Services supplied to a non-resident of Canada who is not registered under GST/HST, for use wholly outside Canada, are likewise zero-rated. For the UHT plant operation, packaged oat milk sold to a foreign buyer and physically shipped from Canada may qualify for 0% GST if the supplier retains the export declaration, carrier evidence, and proof of payment. Food and beverage products intended for human consumption may also fall within Schedule VI Part III basic groceries zero-rating rules where the product is not otherwise excluded by statute.

  • Canada Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A transmitted through the Canadian Export Reporting System or equivalent CERS/ACI reporting)
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order or export sales contract
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the exporting carrier
  • CBSA proof of exit / carrier report / customs transit document
  • GST/HST zero-rated tax invoice referencing Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act
  • Packing list and certificate of origin or food safety/phytosanitary certificate where applicable
  • Inward remittance certificate or equivalent bank settlement proof such as SWIFT MT103 or bank advice evidencing transfer of export proceeds

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or government-issued digital stamp requirements on invoices. Businesses are permitted to issue electronic invoices where all prescribed GST/HST invoice particulars are retained and accessible in English or French. CRA supports the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL network on a voluntary basis for exchange of e-invoices, but there is no statutory obligation to use an API clearance system. GST/HST returns and non-resident registrations are accepted through CRA's EDI/XML protocols. BC PST records must be kept and filed via eTaxBC where applicable, but no real-time fiscal encryption or clearance is required.

CRA retains the right to audit zero-rating claims and to require production of complete export documentation. Where supporting documents are inadequate or fraudulent, CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess GST/HST at 5% plus statutory interest and penalties under the Excise Tax Act. The normal reassessment period is four years, but may be extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or culpable conduct. Penalties for making false statements or omissions can amount to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; criminal conviction for tax evasion may attract fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment for up to five years. Directors and officers may be held personally liable for unremitted GST/HST under ETA s.323. Retroactive clawbacks may include disallowance of export zero-rating, denial of input tax credits, interest from the original filing due date, and transfer-pricing adjustments on delayed or inadequately documented repatriation of export proceeds.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Bank Act, the Currency Act, and the federal Financial Administration Act do not impose exchange controls on foreign currency dealings by a resident Canadian business corporation. Inbound equity capital, loan proceeds, and trade-related payments may be received and held in Canadian dollars or any foreign currency without prior approval from the Bank of Canada or the Department of Finance. Profit remittances by a British Columbia-resident business corporation in the form of dividends are unrestricted but remain subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), unless reduced or eliminated by an applicable income tax treaty, together with the solvency-test requirements of the Canada Business Corporations Act or the British Columbia Business Corporations Act. Purchasing goods, paying foreign suppliers, and settling commercial debts cross-border do not require unrealistic declaratory permits; however, Canadian resident corporations must file an annual Foreign Income Verification Statement (Form T1135) for specified foreign property with a cost amount over CA$100,000, and all cross-border wire transfers of CA$10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose export proceeds surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios, or statutory remittance timelines for receipts earned from the export of manufactured food products. An oat milk packaging and UHT plant may retain export receivables in the invoiced foreign currency, hold them in Canadian-chartered bank foreign-currency accounts, or convert them at the prevailing market rate. For Canadian income tax purposes, export proceeds must be brought into income using the Bank of Canada's published daily exchange rate on the date of receipt or such other rate that is consistent with the subsection 261(2) functional-currency rules if the corporation elects to compute income in a functional currency. All export receipts must nevertheless be traceable through normal corporate records, and any currency transaction amount of CA$10,000 or more must be tracked and reported to FINTRAC in accordance with Canadian anti-money-laundering regulations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low as a complementary payment rail, not a replacement for a Tier-1 Canadian Schedule I bank account. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex may legally serve a Canadian entity only if they comply with Canada's MSB registration requirements under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and FINTRAC registration. Unregistered foreign EMIs soliciting business from Canadian customers would be operating in violation of Canadian AML law, and Canadian corporations using such unregistered entities create significant treasury and regulatory risk. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from a foreign parent or investor into the BC operating company should be routed via SWIFT MT103 through the investor bank's correspondent relationship to the Canadian Tier-1 bank's SWIFT BIC and the BC company's Canadian operating account. The wire must include the legal corporate name and Canadian bank account number in field 59, the Caucasian BC corporation or BN/RC identifier, and a clearly stated field 70 remittance note: 'Equity contribution for common share subscription — UHT oat milk plant — ISIC 1050 — BC Corporation Number [XXXXXXX]'. For balance-of-payments statistical reporting, the receiving Canadian bank will classify the inflow as Foreign Direct Investment in Canada under the equity capital component. Where the investment exceeds the Investment Canada Act notification or review thresholds, the transaction must be reported and, if applicable, reviewed by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada prior to implementation; for WTO-origin investors the direct acquisition control threshold in 2025 is CA$1.386 billion, while all other direct acquisitions are subject to a CA$594 million enterprise value threshold. No exchange approval or export-license is required to convert USD, EUR, or other capital proceeds into Canadian dollars, but the bank will obtain a source-of-funds declaration and may request evidence of the share subscription agreement.For trade settlements from the Canadian UHT plant to foreign suppliers, the primary clearing rails are (1) SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments of oats, enzymes, flavoring systems, packaging laminate, and aseptic filling machine spare parts, using the beneficiary bank BIC, IBAN, and the contract or invoice reference in the remittance field; (2) an irrevocable documentary commercial letter of credit issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank and advised/confirmed by the supplier's bank, transmitted via SWIFT MT700, governed by UCP600, and settled by sight payment or deferred payment; and (3) CAD-denominated domestic disbursements via Lynx (the successor to LVTS) for high-value Canadian payments.
