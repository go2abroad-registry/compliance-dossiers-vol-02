Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the BCBCA. Shares may be issued for nominal or non-cash consideration, and no mandatory escrow account rule applies to a standard business corporation. The corporation may not validly issue shares until at least one director is appointed and a registered office in British Columbia is established; the subscribed consideration must be actually received before share certificates are issued (BCBCA ss. 33–34).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the local fire marshal or building inspector verifying fire suppression systems, emergency exits, and ammonia or refrigerant gas alarm systems; structural load inspection and engineering sign-off by a British Columbia licensed professional engineer for the blast-freezer, cold-storage, steam, and production bays; Technical Safety BC clearance permits for steam boilers, pressure vessels, electrical installations, and refrigeration plant; and WorkSafeBC occupational clearance confirming compliance with the Workers Compensation Act, including machinery guarding, lockout/tagout, cold-stress protection (CSA Z94.1 and applicable cold-environment standards), and a written occupational health and safety program.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) - My Business Account Portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity deriving income from carrying on business in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN). Registration for GST/HST is initiated through the CRA's My Business Account portal by submitting Form RC1A (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or through the 'Register for a GST/HST Account' workflow. Upon approval, the CRA issues a confirmation letter containing the BN and the associated GST/HST program account number (RT suffix). Non-residents must provide documentary evidence of Canadian business presence, including the foreign registration certificate, a Canadian agent or physical address, and an estimated declaration of annual taxable supplies in Canada.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity deriving income from carrying on business in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN). Registration for GST/HST is initiated through the CRA's My Business Account portal by submitting Form RC1A (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or through the 'Register for a GST/HST Account' workflow. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Available statutory elections include: (1) Subsection 85(1) of the Income Tax Act rollover election for transfer of eligible capital property to a Canadian controlled private corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (2) the GST/HST Quick Method election for streamlined sales tax accounting; (3) the Section 211 GST/HST election for deemed supplies between closely related corporations; and (4) the net tax calculation election for non-resident registrants to simplify GST/HST remittances.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports). A person who makes taxable supplies exceeding CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters is a mandatory registrant. Below this threshold, registration is voluntary but required to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported from Canada where the exporter satisfies the conditions of Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act (ETA). For the taxable supply to be zero-rated, the goods must be exported by the supplier or a subsequent recipient within prescribed timelines and proper documentation must attest to exportation. Services supplied to a non-resident for use exclusively outside Canada qualify under Schedule VI, Part V, Section 7, provided the non-resident does not receive the benefit of the service in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A electronically filed through CBSA CAED)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill issued by the carrier
  • Commercial Invoice evidencing the sale and terms of trade
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Credit Confirmation from a Canadian chartered bank

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance (no CFDI-style clearance). However, GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed data: supplier's BN, date of invoice, recipient's name, total taxable amount, the tax rate applied, and the tax amount. Export shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 must be reported electronically via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) through a CBSA-approved service provider, with Form B13A (Export Declaration) filed before export. Digital stamping is not required, but an audit trail of fiscal records must be retained for six years.

CRA may reassess and claw back GST/HST refunds together with compound interest if export documentation is deficient. The penalty for knowingly making a false statement or omission in an export refund claim is 25% of the overclaimed amount (gross negligence penalty), and the penalty for fraud is 50% of the understated tax. Criminal tax evasion is punishable by fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment for up to five years. Retrospective audits target recurring patterns of overstating export supplies, and failure to maintain adequate books and records in Canada imposes an additional penalty under ETA Section 89.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. The Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) establishes CAD as legal tender, and the Bank of Canada Act imposes no licensing or approval requirement on foreign-exchange transactions. Profit and dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted; the practical hurdles are confined to (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., 5%/15% under the Canada-US Treaty and 10%/15% under the Canada-China Treaty), (ii) CRS/FATCA reporting classification, and (iii) FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting of CAD 10,000 or more. A British Columbia Business Corporation may freely pay dividends to foreign shareholders only if it satisfies the solvency test in s. 68 of the Business Corporations Act (SBC 2002, c. 57). No capital-account licence or repatriation permit applies to frozen-food manufacturing (ISIC 1074 / NAICS 311412).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada repealed the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951 and imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export receipts. Proceeds from exports of frozen steamed dim sum and dumplings may be retained in USD, CNY, EUR, or CAD, used to settle import payables, or converted freely through any authorized dealer. The only compliance obligations are CFIA food-safety licensing, foreign-country import documentation, and CBSA export declarations; no exchange-control reporting of proceeds arises.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority and manager of Canada's official international reserves; prudential regulation of clearing banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); anti-money-laundering supervision is administered by FINTRAC. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, supplement-only. Offshore digital EMIs cannot lawfully operate as standalone settlement banks in Canada. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23) and Bank of Canada regulations in force since late 2024, any foreign payment service provider must register as a Retail Payment Service Provider (RPSP); FINTRAC additionally requires Money Service Business (MSB) registration before a foreign EMI may transmit funds into or out of Canada. An offshore EMI with no Canadian nexus is legally constrained from holding CAD settlement accounts at a chartered bank and must partner with a Tier 1 institution for custody. For this factory, foreign EMIs are usable as an invoicing and supplier-payout overlay only; the primary treasury node must remain a Canadian chartered bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route is a SWIFT MT103 international wire from the parent bank to the British Columbia account using Canada's 9-digit routing convention (5-digit institution code plus 4-digit transit code) instead of IBAN, e.g., 'Transit 00010, Institution 003'. Field 70 must carry the remittance purpose, e.g., 'PAID-IN CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION FOR COMMON SHARES - BC BUSINESS CORPORATIONS ACT SOLVENT', and the intermediary bank's corporate-purpose code should be mapped to 'CAP' or 'BUS'. All cross-border electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reported to FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Verification is completed via MT103 confirmation and, for land or building acquisition, through the lawyer's or notary's trust-account escrow. The Investment Canada Act should be screened: the 2024 WTO-investor enterprise-value threshold is CAD 1.326B; generally a capital injection of that size will not require review, but notification and national-security screening must be assessed because the factory involves industrial real property.All international trade settlements run through SWIFT MT103 (direct remittance) or MT700 (commercial import Letter of Credit under UCP600, unconfirmed or confirmed for Chinese suppliers). Domestic Canadian obligations (payroll, packaging, utilities, intra-BC suppliers) settle via Payments Canada's Lynx real-time high-value system using ISO 20022-pacs.008 messages, with batch items through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). The treasury desk will execute FX conversion to invoice currency (USD, CNY, EUR) and release one-leg MT103 to the vendor. Equipment and packaging-material imports should be collateralized under the bank's trade line as an import LC or documentary collection (D/P, D/A).
