Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope: Canadian-resident corporations (incorporated in Canada or central management and control located in Canada) are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source income attributable to a permanent establishment in Canada. No pass-through status is available for corporations. Self-heating food production is a taxable commercial activity; no special tax-exempt-zone regime applies.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; corporate residence is determined by de jure (place of incorporation) and de facto (central management and control) tests. Available elections include: Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 216 election for non-residents to pay net-basis tax on Canadian rental/royalty income; ITA s. 216.1 election to waive treaty relief; ITA s. 93(2) election for foreign affiliate dividend distributions; ITA s. 88(3) wind-up election; ITA s. 256(1) loss-transfer elections within corporate groups; and Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156/157 closely-related corporation elections for GST/HST zero-rated inter-corporate supplies. Hybrid mismatch defensive rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6, 20.2) apply and disallow deduction-without-inclusion outcomes; no elective hybrid-mismatch regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 worldwide taxable supplies threshold (small-supplier exemption) for both residents and non-residents supplying goods/services to Canada; mandatory registration once exceeded. BC PST: no general de minimis threshold; any vendor making taxable sales in BC in the course of a business must register, subject to isolated-sale exemptions. Registered non-resident vendors may be required to post security.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported by the supplier qualify for 0% GST/PST where the supplier exports the goods directly to a destination outside Canada within a reasonable time (generally 60 days) under ETA s. 6; services supplied to non-residents qualify under ETA s. 7 if not related to Canadian real property or goods in Canada. Self-heating instant meals are also zero-rated domestically as 'basic groceries' under Schedule VI, Part III, ETA, but export zero-rating requires documentary proof of export. The supplier must be the exporter of record or have commercial responsibility for export; BC PST does not apply to goods delivered by the vendor outside BC.

None. Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (Peppol BIS 3.0). Export transactions are reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) platform using electronic export declarations (Form B13A / G21) for shipments valued over CAD 2,000 or controlled goods. All books, invoices, and customs records must be retained for six years (ETA s. 286).

CRA may deny zero-rating on audit, claw back claimed Input Tax Credits and assess unpaid GST with arrears interest and penalties. Gross-negligence penalty is 25% of the tax that was sought to be evaded or misstated (ETA s. 285; ITA s. 163(2)); failure to file and false statements attract penalties under ETA s. 280. The standard four-year reassessment limitation period is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full currency convertibility of the Canadian dollar under IMF Article VIII. There is no distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility for practical banking purposes: the CAD floats freely, and non-residents may remit dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, and intercompany debt repayments without Bank of Canada or Finance Canada approval. The operative frictions are Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, transfer pricing documentation (T106/T1134), thin capitalization rules, and FINTRAC reporting of large cross-border wire transfers. Foreign direct investment into a British Columbia operating entity may flow as equity or shareholder loans without exchange-control permissions, but the receiving bank will apply standard source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rule. Exporters of self-heating food products may retain export receivables in CAD or a foreign currency, convert them freely into CAD, or deploy them directly to settle trade payables. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no statutory timeline for repatriation, and no export-licence bottleneck solely for FX purposes. For tax accounting, export receipts are recognized at the Bank of Canada noon spot rate or the contracted rate under commercially reasonable accounting policies acceptable to the Canada Revenue Agency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering surveillance and reporting are enforced by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium as a supplementary treasury rail, but low as a primary clearing relationship. An offshore electronic money institution (EMI) is not licensed to take deposits or offer deposit accounts under the Bank Act. It may provide foreign-exchange execution and payment processing for peripheral disbursements, but any activity involving Canadian customers or the holding or clearing of CAD balances will generally require FINTRAC registration as a money services business (MSB) unless an express exemption applies. The compliant approach is to use a Schedule I bank for all substantive CAD clearing, LVTS wire settlements, and trade finance, while an EMI can support faster supplier payments in selected low-risk jurisdictions if the bank's treasury policy permits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route for a non-resident parent injecting capital into the British Columbia entity is a SWIFT MT103 wire to the Canadian bank's correspondent account. Reference field 70 must state 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION', 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION', or 'INTERCOMPANY LOAN' as applicable, and the wire should identify the ultimate remitter and beneficiary precisely. CAD inbound wires clear through Payments Canada LVTS for real-time high-value settlement; USD wires clear through US correspondent banks via Fedwire or CHIPS; EUR wires clear through TARGET2. The receiving bank will verify the source of funds and will file a FINTRAC electronic funds transfer report for any transfer exceeding C$10,000 or multiple linked transfers. Corporate records should mirror the wire by booking the capital injection as shares or loan principal under a signed subscription agreement or promissory note to satisfy both CRA and bank audit requirements.The primary clearing rails for trade settlements are SWIFT MT103 for cross-border beneficiary disbursements, Fedwire or CHIPS for USD clearing through US correspondent banks, LVTS for same-day CAD domestic payments, and TARGET2 for EUR payments. For documented trade transactions, confirmed irrevocable commercial letters of credit under UCP600 are available through the Canadian bank's trade finance desk and should be used when counterparties require bank payment undertaking. Documentary collections, open account MT103 payments, and batch EFT credits are permissible for recurring low-value disbursements.
