Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) – Business Number, Income Tax and GST/HST accounts; British Columbia Ministry of Finance – Provincial Sales Tax (PST). Portals: CRA Business Registration Online / My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and BC eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) with the relevant CRA program accounts before making taxable supplies in Canada. File RC1 (Request for a Business Number) either online through CRA Business Registration Online or by mail; for a non-resident corporation, submit certified articles of incorporation/registration, director identification, registered office address, a Canadian representative/agent if no Canadian address, and bank/TIN proof. The CRA will issue a 9-digit BN and program accounts, e.g., RT for GST/HST, RM for customs/import-export, and RC for payroll if employees are in Canada. PST vendor registration is separate and is completed through BC eTaxBC/BC Corporate Online before sales to B.C. consumers or businesses; registration is effective from the date taxable supply activity begins, with voluntary GST/HST registration available below threshold. Books and records supporting registration and Canadian nexus must be kept for six years.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election or pass-through entity classification exists for a business corporation; corporate status under the Canada Business Corporations Act or BC Business Corporations Act is determinative for CRA purposes. A BC unlimited liability corporation is Canadian-resident for tax law but may be treated as disregarded under U.S. check-the-box (not applicable to this ordinary CA_CORP). Available statutory elections affecting tax recovery include: GST/HST section 156 election for closely related corporations to file as one filer; GST/HST net-file and simplified method elections for small suppliers; ITA section 85 rollover election to transfer property into a corporation on a tax-deferred basis; and provincial permanent-establishment allocation via Part IV/ETC provisions. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are recharacterized under the Income Tax Act and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: mandatory registration when taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters, or the immediately preceding calendar quarter (CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; voluntary registration allowed below threshold). BC PST: registration is mandatory for all vendors making taxable sales in B.C.; a limited small-seller exclusion applies to non-resident online sellers only when B.C. taxable sales are CAD 10,000 or less and total Canadian taxable sales are CAD 100,000 or less in the preceding 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of freeze-dried pet food and kibble are zero-rated for GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act (Schedule VI) when the supplier exports or delivers the goods to a non-resident buyer outside Canada, exports the goods as part of the supply, and retains proof of export. The zero rate applies to goods shipped outside Canada; services supplied to a non-resident buyer are zero-rated only if each condition in section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI is satisfied. Domestic Canadian sales remain taxable at 5% GST plus 7% PST (12% combined).

  • Customs Export Declaration / CBSA export report (CERS/CARM transaction number, for prescribed shipments)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of goods from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign-exchange receipt or SWIFT confirmation evidencing non-resident payment
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and any CFIA export registration/certificate for pet food
  • Foreign customs import entry/documentation from the consignee's country (where available)

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system for invoices. Electronic GST/HST invoices are accepted if CRA information-retention requirements are met; the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL network is voluntary. BC PST returns and registrations are filed through eTaxBC without API stamping. Export declarations are reported electronically to the CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or CARM portal as applicable, and export invoices are transmitted in standard commercial formats.

The CRA and CBSA apply export verification controls; zero-rated treatment is clawed back where the supplier cannot show proof that goods were actually exported to a non-resident within a reasonable period (commonly 180 days). A disallowed zero-rating results in GST/HST assessed at 5% on the supply, plus interest, penalties for false statements or misrepresentations, and possible prosecution for wilful evasion. PST vendor audits can similarly assess uncollected tax against the seller, with penalties for failure to register, file or remit. Records must generally be retained for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains fully unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no central-bank quotas, no prior approval requirements for capital inflows, and no restrictions on profit remittance. The only material remittance hurdles are fiscal: Canadian dividend withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced if the shareholder is treaty-entitled), NR4 reporting for non-resident payees, transfer-pricing documentation for cross-border related-party transactions, and earnings-stripping/thin-capitalisation rules. The CAD/USD exchange rate is freely floating.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no prescribed conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, held offshore, or converted to CAD at the exporter's discretion. Banking requirements such as FINTRAP reporting of suspicious transactions and large electronic funds transfers are anti-money-laundering obligations and do not constitute exchange controls.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada has no separate foreign-exchange administration; monetary and FX policy operate through the Bank of Canada, prudential bank supervision is consolidated with OSFI, AML/ATF supervision is consolidated with FINTRAP, and economic sanctions administration sits with Global Affairs Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary settlement rail; Low as the primary Canadian banking and clearing solution. A foreign electronic-money institution licence is not passportable into Canada. An offshore EMI may provide payment processing to a Canadian business only if it is properly registered as a money services business with FINTRAP and complies with Canada's AML/ATF regime; it cannot take deposits or offer regulated banking services in Canada. The BC corporation should maintain its primary operating account with a Schedule I bank and use EMIs only for platform-pay receivables or small-balance international disbursements after satisfactory FINTRAP due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: direct SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank account to the BC corporation's CAD operating account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The remittance-field narrative should clearly identify 'share capital subscription / issued capital contribution - CA_CORP'; when the bank's portal requires a purpose category, select the equivalent of 'capital contribution / equity investment' or use ISO purpose code 'EQUI' if available. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 200% of tax evaded; criminal prosecution with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if they fail to exercise due diligence; travel bans and asset freezes possible
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the entity for tax purposes, reassess income, and impose penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; treaty benefits denied

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in the sham arrangement; potential criminal charges
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations; criminal penalties including imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned if they knowingly fail to comply; travel bans may be imposed
