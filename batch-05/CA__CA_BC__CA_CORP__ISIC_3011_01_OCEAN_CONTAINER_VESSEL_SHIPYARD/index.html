Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); applicable federal tax portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online at www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. The British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered by the Consumer Taxation Branch of the BC Ministry of Finance; relevant portal: BC eTaxBC at etax.gov.bc.ca.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian Business Corporation must obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN), which serves as the unified identifier for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and customs/import-export accounts. Apply by filing CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), by telephone through CRA Business Registration, or by electronic registration through CRA Business Registration Online / BizRegister BC. A BC PST vendor number must be obtained separately through eTaxBC before making PST-taxable sales in British Columbia. For a newly formed federal or BC corporation, the incorporating authority supplies the corporate registration number, which is distinct from the CRA BN; the corporation should be registered for GST/HST if its worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold or if it wishes to claim input tax credits on export-related inputs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. A Business Corporation is legally classified as a separate taxable entity and assessed at the corporate entity level; no pass-through election is available. Canadian domestic law and the Canada-US tax treaty use legal residence and place of effective management rather than a flexible elective classification. Hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act operate automatically to deny deductions or require inclusion for payments giving rise to deduction-without-inclusion outcomes; there is no elective hybrid-mismatch opt-in. Limited statutory elections available to a corporation include choosing a non-calendar taxation year under Income Tax Act section 249.1 and, where applicable, filing under section 216 for non-resident income of a passive nature, which is not the operating classification for an active vessel construction shipyard.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST registration threshold: CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) earned in Canada over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. BC PST: no comparable general small-supplier threshold; a vendor making taxable sales in British Columbia must obtain a PST number and collect, remit, and administer PST obligations. The combined nominal BC retail rate is 12% (5% federal GST plus 7% BC PST), but an export sale of an oceangoing vessel may be zero-rated for GST/HST and exempt from BC PST if all prescribed export conditions are met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A commercial shipyard supplying a newly built ocean container vessel to a non-resident purchaser may claim zero-rating under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supply is an export of tangible personal property. The conditions include: (i) title and risk in the vessel pass to the non-resident purchaser; (ii) the vessel is delivered to a common carrier, freight forwarder, or other export agent for carriage out of Canada, or is physically sailed or towed out of Canadian waters promptly after delivery; (iii) the vessel is not used or consumed in Canada before export; (iv) the shipyard obtains and retains documentary evidence showing the vessel left Canada and was delivered to the foreign buyer; and (v) the supply is reported as a zero-rated export on the GST/HST return. Where the vessel is constructed under contract for a non-resident buyer and title transfers abroad, the construction services may also qualify for zero-rating as a supply to a non-resident if the service is performed on tangible movable property destined for export. For BC PST, a sale of goods to be exported out of British Columbia is generally exempt when the vendor obtains an export declaration or equivalent evidence that the vessel was exported from Canada within the applicable period; without such evidence, BC PST may be assessed on the full sale price.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canadian Export Declaration / eManifest export report filed by the shipyard or authorised customs broker)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or through bill of lading evidencing the vessel's carriage from Canada to the non-resident purchaser)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank SWIFT MT103 / foreign exchange credit evidence demonstrating payment repatriation by the non-resident buyer)

Canada does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax authority approval, or digital stamping for GST/HST/PST invoices. Electronic invoicing is permitted if the invoice is generated, retained, and accessible in a clear and legible format. CRA requires a GST/HST registrant to issue invoices containing prescribed information for taxable supplies, including zero-rated export supplies, and to keep all source documents, customs records, bills of lading, and payment evidence available for audit. BC PST invoices must substantiate exemptions such as export sales and, for a vessel export, should include the buyer's non-resident status, delivery terms, export destination, and supporting export documentation.

CRA and the BC Ministry of Finance apply strict documentation-based scrutiny to zero-rated export claims. If the required export evidence is not timely produced, CRA may reverse the zero-rating, reassess the vessel sale at 5% GST (and potentially impose BC PST under provincial law), issue a clawback of input tax credits, and assess interest. Normal GST/HST reassessments generally occur within four years; the period is unlimited where the export claim involves misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default. Penalties can include a gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the misstated net tax or refund, a false-statement penalty, BC PST penalty and interest, and criminal prosecution for GST/HST evasion under the Excise Tax Act. Exporters are expected to retain all export bills, customs acknowledgements, proof of foreign payment, and foreign-exchange repatriation records for at least six years to avoid penalties and retrospective clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility; no exchange controls on profit remittance or capital movements. Non-resident shareholders may repatriate dividends and capital subject to Part XIII withholding and applicable tax treaty relief. CAD/USD/EUR trade freely in the interbank market; no Canadian restriction on holding foreign-currency accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio; export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts. FINTRAC reporting applies to cross-border electronic fund transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000; Canadian customs export reporting under the Customs Act and Statistics Act is required for vessel deliveries.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal constraints. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may be used as collection agents and low-value operational payment conduits, but cannot support maritime LC issuance, escrow settlement, or deposit-taking in Canada. They are required to register as money services businesses (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and with FINTRAC for any Canadian-facing remittance activity; British Columbia requires extra-provincial registration. For Stage 4 treasury operations, EMIs should be limited to non-critical operational transfers and must be backed by a Canadian bank-grade clearing rail for capital injections and trade settlements.
