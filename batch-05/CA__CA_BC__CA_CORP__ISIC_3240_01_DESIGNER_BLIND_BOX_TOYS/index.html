Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), GST/HST account and Business Number administration via My Business Account portal; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch for PST registration and administration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or registering for GST/HST must obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number. The corporation files Form RC1 or applies online through the CRA Business Registration Online service, selecting the GST/HST account and, if applicable, BC PST account. The BC PST account is obtained through the BC Consumer Taxation Branch's eTaxBC portal. Proof of foreign incorporation, Canadian address of records, and directors' information are required. A non-resident corporation may register for GST/HST even if not required under the small supplier threshold, and must register if it makes taxable supplies and is not a small supplier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have an IRS-style check-the-box election. A federal or provincial business corporation is automatically a Canadian corporation subject to entity-level federal income tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act and provincial corporate income tax in British Columbia. Relevant elections may include: functional currency election under section 261, capital cost allowance election under regulation 1100, GST/HST annual filing election, GST/HST election under section 156 for closely related corporations, and treaty-based non-resident withholding relief elections. Hybrid mismatch rules in section 18.9 and 18.20 of the Income Tax Act constrain cross-border tax arbitrage, and no hybrid entity classification election is generally available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD in taxable supplies (GST/HST small supplier threshold over any four consecutive calendar quarters); BC PST registration generally required if annual specified revenue from PST-taxable sales exceeds $10,000 CAD unless an exemption applies for small sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property are generally zero-rated for GST/HST when the goods are exported from Canada and evidence establishes that the goods were delivered or made available outside Canada. The supplier must charge 0% GST/HST and claim an input tax credit for related inputs. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated if they relate to property situated outside Canada or are performed wholly outside Canada. For designer blind box toys sold as goods shipped to foreign customers, the supplier must retain proof of export, including carriers' receipts and export documentation. BC PST does not apply to goods exported by the seller for delivery outside British Columbia.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or CBSA-authorized electronic export declaration)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill showing delivery outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or banking evidence of foreign payment and repatriation

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoicing clearance, or digital stamping for GST/HST or BC PST. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records, but no certificate of digital fiscal authorization is required. Export clearance is conducted through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) using electronic customs export declarations and the Canadian International Trade Single Window. BC PST is administered through periodic returns without transaction-level real-time clearance.

The CRA and CBSA may audit export claims for documentary validity. Failure to satisfy the documentary exported-goods test results in reassessment of the supply at 5% GST and disallowance of input tax credits, with interest and penalties. False or fraudulent zero-rating claims may attract penalties under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act, gross negligence penalties up to 50% of the net tax, and the Excise Tax Act contains no statutory limitation bar for misrepresentation. BC PST obligations on improperly exported or untaxed domestic sales may also be assessed with interest and penalty under provincial legislation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. The Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) imposes no general exchange controls; capital subscriptions, intercompany loans, dividends, and profit remittances may be freely executed without Bank of Canada or Department of Finance approval. Inbound non-resident investment is screened only under the Investment Canada Act: non-cultural manufacturing activities (ISIC 3240 / NAICS 339930) are below the WTO review threshold (C$1.342 billion enterprise value in 2024) and require only post-closing notification. Outbound dividend remittances are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory rate, potentially reduced under an applicable tax treaty) and, under the BC Business Corporations Act, must satisfy the statutory solvency test. No currency matching or local financing requirements apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timelines on export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts and convert exposure at market rates. For tax purposes, foreign exchange gains/losses on export receivables must be computed under section 39(1) of the Income Tax Act, and all cross-border flows above C$10,000 trigger FINTRAC electronic funds transfer and currency reporting obligations. Cash or monetary instruments crossing the border require FINTRAC Form (Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report, PCMLTFA s. 12).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with exchange administration functions shared across OSFI (prudential oversight), FINTRAC (AML/transaction reporting), and the Department of Finance Canada (sanctions and investment policy). No separate foreign exchange control agency exists; Canada operates a free-floating currency regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. Canadian fintech platforms (Shopify Payments, Stripe, Payoneer, Wise) operate as registered money services businesses under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and, since 2024, are subject to Bank of Canada retail payment activity registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed as Canadian deposit-taking institutions and cannot offer true CAD deposit-account functionality; they may only serve as FX/payment gateways or pre-funded card issuers. Treasury dependence on unregistered offshore EMIs creates correspondent-de-risking exposure with Schedule I banks and is therefore treated as a secondary rail rather than the primary clearing channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity-capital injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 to the bank-designated receiving account at a Schedule I bank, with remittance field 70 clearly referencing 'Capital Injection — Subscription to Shares under BC Business Corporations Act'.
