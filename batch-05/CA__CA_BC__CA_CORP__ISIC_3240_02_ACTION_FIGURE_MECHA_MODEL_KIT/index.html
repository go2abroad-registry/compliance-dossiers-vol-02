Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA/ARC), 'My Business Account' portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Provincial (BC): BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, eTaxBC portal (https://etax.gov.bc.ca) for BC PST registration and returns. Corporate registries: Corporations Canada (CBCA) and BC Registry Services (BCBCA).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the CBCA (Articles of Incorporation with Corporations Canada; at least 25% of directors, or one where fewer than four directors, must be resident Canadians) or under the BCBCA (Notice of Articles, Form 1; no director-residency requirement). Step 3: Register for BC PST via eTaxBC before the first taxable BC sale (no small-supplier threshold). Step 4: For non-resident corporations not incorporated in Canada, obtain a BN via CRA Form RC1; the GST/HST program account must be opened within 30 days after the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act; Canada has no pass-through or check-the-box classification regime for corporations. Territorial_Scope: a CBCA/BCBCA corporation is resident in Canada and taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident-owned manufacturing corporation earning Canadian-source active business income is taxed on that Canadian-source income only. Tax_Exempt_Zone: none applicable; BC has no income-tax exemption zone for toy/action-figure manufacturing.

Available election forms

No US-style entity classification election. Available mechanisms: (i) ITA s. 261 functional-currency election (reporting in USD/JPY), suited to an export-invoiced model-kit business; (ii) GST/HST elections under ETA s. 156 (group registration) and s. 156.1 (closely related corporations); (iii) ITA s. 85 tax-deferred rollover of property into the corporation; (iv) non-resident elections under ITA s. 216 (rents/royalties) and s. 217 (employment income); (v) CCPC small business deduction status (federal 9% + BC 2% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income) is a factual status, not an election, and is unavailable if non-residents directly or indirectly control more than 50% of voting shares; (vi) hybrid-mismatch response rules (ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3, 20.2, 21.2, 247.12) govern cross-border deduction characterizations and require contemporaneous documentation of the foreign entity's tax treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters. BC PST: no threshold; registration is mandatory effective on the first taxable sale of tangible personal property in BC (including action figures and model kits sold through Canadian fulfillment).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating under Part V, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act: section 1 - goods (plastic snap-fit mecha model kits and action figures, HS 9503.00) delivered by the supplier to a common carrier or courier for export; section 16 - drop-shipments to non-resident purchasers for delivery in Canada; section 5 - services rendered to non-residents, provided the service is not performed in connection with Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada at the time of performance and the foreign recipient is not a GST registrant carrying on business in Canada. Full input tax credits are claimable on resin pellets, steel molds, packaging, and production machinery. BC PST: goods delivered by the vendor outside BC are exempt from PST, subject to retention of an exemption certificate; production machinery and equipment used primarily in manufacturing may qualify for the BC PST exemption.

  • CBSA Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CESR), formerly Form B13A, required under the Reporting of Exported Goods Regulations for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000, with HS 9503.00 classification
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or Cargo Control Document evidencing the physical movement of goods out of Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or equivalent bank settlement record) proving receipt of foreign currency export proceeds within the 180-day benchmark
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matched to the export declaration, the GST return, and the proof-of-export evidence retained under the Proof of Export (Identity and Exported Property) Regulations, SOR/2007-202

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoice validation, or digital stamping regime in Canada. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST Internet File Transfer; mandatory for registrants with annual worldwide taxable supplies over CAD 1.5 million). The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (PCI), built on the PEPPOL network, is being phased in as a voluntary framework per Federal Budget 2024; XML, PDF, and paper invoices remain acceptable evidence if they satisfy the record-keeping requirements of ITA s. 230 and ETA s. 286.

CRA audits proof of export on a post-assessment basis; failure to substantiate physical export reclassifies the sale as taxable at 5% GST, disallows the offsetting input tax credits, and triggers reassessment with compounded daily arrears interest. Normal reassessment window: 4 years for GST/HST (ETA s. 298) and 4 years for corporate income tax (ITA s. 152(4)), extendable by waiver. Penalties: ETA s. 281.1 (false statements/omissions): CAD 250 minimum or 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded, whichever is greater; ITA s. 163(2) (gross negligence): 50% of the understated tax; criminal evasion (ITA s. 239 / ETA s. 327): on indictment, fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; on summary conviction, fines up to 100% and imprisonment up to 2 years. Open intercompany export receivables persisting beyond 180 days attract transfer-pricing scrutiny under ITA s. 247(2) and potential Part XIII deemed-interest/dividend recharacterization. Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST and payroll source deductions under ETA s. 323 and ITA s. 227.1.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has not maintained exchange controls since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951; the Currency Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-52) makes CAD fully legal tender and freely convertible across both current and capital accounts. No government approval is required for profit repatriation, capital injection, or dividend remittance; the only hurdles are (i) Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders (25% statutory, treaty-reduced to typically 5%-15% under applicable treaties such as Canada-China or Canada-Japan), and (ii) corporate-law solvency tests and board resolutions under the BC Business Corporations Act (BCBCA). Foreign-currency bank accounts may be opened without central-bank approval; corporate tax and accounting records must be maintained in CAD unless a functional-currency election under Income Tax Act s. 261 is filed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds. A BC manufacturer may hold export receipts in USD, JPY, or CNY deposit accounts at a Schedule I bank indefinitely. Compliance duties are limited to (i) monthly export reporting to Statistics Canada through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) administered with CBSA, and (ii) translation of all foreign-currency income into CAD for T2 purposes using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or, absent a published rate, a CRA-acceptable rate; timing differences are marked-to-market with no punitive revaluation rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) as prudential regulator under the Bank Act (Canada); FINTRAC as AML/trade-monitoring authority under the PCMLTFA

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: HIGH as an ancillary payment-service provider for small disbursements and marketplace collections; LOW as a primary operating/capital account. Canadian law prohibits non-bank EMIs from taking deposits (Bank Act s. 536(1)); offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may operate in Canada only as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses, without CDIC deposit insurance and without broker-inclusive settlement guarantees. Funds held via pooled settlement accounts expose the corporation to third-party insolvency and FX conversion spread risk. The optimal arrangement is: Schedule I bank holds the capital escrow and trade-finance facility; one EMI is onboarded solely for low-value supplier wires (<CAD 10,000 threshold monitored for FINTRAC triggers).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: inbound wiring instructions prepared as SWIFT MT103; field 70 (Remittance Information) must state 'Contribution to Paid-In Capital - BCBCA' and a recognized purpose code (Canadian banks map to ISO 20022 purpose 'CFDI' or legacy 'G' for capital transfers). Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within six months after the end of each taxation year (corporate fiscal year-end). For a December 31 year-end, filing is due June 30 of the following calendar year. Payment of any balance of tax is due within two months (or three months for certain CCPCs qualifying under the graduated rate estate rules) after the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 CAD of gross revenue combined with transactions with non-resident related parties exceeding CAD 1,000,000 (for Local File) and CAD 50,000,000 gross revenue threshold (for Country-by-Country Report filing) per CRA TPM-03 transfer pricing memorandum; Master File required for multinational groups meeting the CbC threshold of consolidated revenue of CAD 50,000,000 or equivalent foreign group threshold.

Economic substance scope: The general economic substance requirements derive from the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, which denies tax benefits arising from avoidance transactions lacking economic substance. British Columbia does not have a separate standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime (ESR) of the type seen in the UAE; but the federal GAAR plus transfer pricing penalties under subsection 247(3) are the operative substantive doctrine. A company must have board meetings, decision-making records, and real operational substance in order to support the Canadian PE attribution and to prevent treaty-shopping.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty under paragraph 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; interest and penalties on tax arrears; and criminal sanction under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act for tax evasion of a fine up to the greater of 200% of the tax attempted to evade and CAD 25,000 (on summary conviction) or up to 200% and CAD 100,000 (on indictment) plus possible imprisonment of up to two years (summary) or five years (indictment).

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for corporate tax, but are jointly and severally liable for unremitted employee source deductions, GST/HST, and certain corporation tax if the corporation fails to remit; the CRA can assess directors for 100% of unremitted amounts after a certificate issued. For evasion, a director who participated in, assented to, or acquiesced in the offence may be a party under subsection 239(1) and subject to the same fine and imprisonment. A director can be criminally prosecuted as a natural person even if the corporation is also prosecuted. Directors may face travel bans and asset seizures in the context of cross-border prosecutions and extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Transfer Pricing / TP documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty under subsection 247(3) of the Income Tax Act equal to the lesser of (i) 10% of the gross transfer pricing adjustment; and (ii) 10% of the gross revenue of the taxpayer from non-resident non-arm's-length transactions. If a correction is not reported, interest and the misrepresentation penalty of subsection 163(2) may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed if they sign or cause to be signed the T2 return containing a false statement, or if the penalty arises under subsection 163(2) for gross negligence, and can be criminally liable under subsection 239(1). Directors of a multinational group who knowingly approve or implement artificial prices can be held as parties to the false statements and may be assessed personally for penalties in exceptional circumstances, but the direct penalty is on the corporation.
Shell Company / Economic Substance failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, application of section 245 GAAR to recharacterize transactions and deny tax benefits, additional tax and interest, and the 50% penalty for false or misleading statements under subsection 163(2). The CRA may also impose penalties under the Excise Tax Act for participation in a sham arrangement. A 'sham' declaration may cause the CRA to reassess the corporation's entire tax position for all open years.

Director personal liability: Outside of bankruptcy and specific statutory regimes, directors of a shell corporation can be personally liable if they knowingly participate in the preparation of false returns (joint and several liability under subsection 239(2) for knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence reporting a false return), and if the corporation is used to commit fraud against creditors or the Revenue, the corporate veil will be pierced upon proof of fraud, dishonesty, or act of the director for personal benefit.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals, criminal prosecution for money laundering under subsection 462.31 of the Criminal Code carrying imprisonment up to 10 years, and asset forfeiture under proceeds-of-crime provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly launder proceeds of crime, fail to implement compliance obligations, or allow the corporation to transmit illicit funds can be criminally prosecuted in their personal capacity and imprisoned for up to 10 years; they may also be subject to civil restitution and asset seizure orders.
