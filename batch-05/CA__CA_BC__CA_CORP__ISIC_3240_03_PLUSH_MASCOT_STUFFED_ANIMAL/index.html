Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital self-service portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (canada.ca/cra-business-account). British Columbia provincial sales tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, via the eTaxBC portal (etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the British Columbia Business Corporations Act; the incorporation event (via Corporations Canada or BC Corporate Online) generates the CRA-issued 9-digit Business Number (BN). No minimum capital, statutory licence, or domestic-ownership condition is required for registration of a plush-toy OEM manufacturer. Step 2 - Establish CRA program accounts on My Business Account: RC0001 (corporate income tax), RT0001 (GST/HST), and, if employees are engaged, RP0001 (payroll). Step 3 - Register the GST/HST account through BRO within 30 days of ceasing to be a small supplier (taxable supplies in Canada exceeding C$30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters); voluntary early registration is available to recover input tax credits (ITCs) on fabrics, embroidery inputs, and ultrasonic-seaming equipment. Step 4 - If retail sales of finished plush goods occur in BC, register for PST as a vendor through eTaxBC; sales delivered outside BC or Canada remain outside BC PST scope. Step 5 - Register as an exporter with the CBSA/Statistics Canada via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) using the BN and file export declarations (B13A) for shipments above C$2,000. Step 6 - File the combined federal/BC T2 corporate income tax return within six months of each fiscal year-end, together with the annual GST/HST return where the corporation has elected an annual filing calendar.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Canadian corporate entity is a taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act separate from its shareholders; no S-corporation or LLC-style pass-through is available; a Canadian-resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income, with BC provincial income tax applied through the combined T2 return; non-resident-owned manufacturing entities are taxable on Canadian-source business income at the federal and BC entity level and may be subject to Part XIV branch tax on repatriated earnings).

Available election forms

Not applicable - no US-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian law. Available elections and factual status elections: (1) ETA s. 156 closely-related-corporations election (Form GST 25) for simplified GST/HST settlement; (2) ETA s. 167 qualifying-transfer-of-a-business election (Form GST 173); (3) hybrid-mismatch-arrangement rules effective 1 July 2020 (ITA ss. 12.6, 18.2-18.5, 20(1)(r.1), and 247.12-247.13), including self-hybrid and imported-mismatch elections; (4) ITA s. 93.1(1) election treating dividends from foreign affiliates as taxable dividends; (5) drop-shipment zero-rating certificate mechanism (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 10; Form GST 350) for goods delivered in Canada to a non-resident principal for export; and (6) factual Canadian-controlled-private-corporation (CCPC) status giving access to the federal small business deduction and reduced BC provincial small business corporate rate through the T2 combined return. British Columbia has no provincial check-the-box or elective entity tax classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters (the GST/HST small-supplier threshold); registration is mandatory for a non-resident carrying on business in Canada once the threshold is exceeded and is permitted voluntarily below the threshold to claim input tax credits. BC PST registration applies to any vendor making retail sales of taxable tangible personal property in BC on a physical or economic nexus basis; there is no low-value exemption, but export deliveries with possession transferred outside BC or Canada are not taxable BC retail sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods zero-rating: ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 zero-rates a sale of tangible personal property (plush mascots, stuffed animals, custom embroidery and ultrasonic-seamed products) exported by or on behalf of the supplier as soon after the supply as is reasonable in the circumstances, provided that possession of the goods is transferred to the buyer outside Canada, or, if possession is transferred in Canada, the supplier ships the goods to a destination outside Canada and exports them; title must not pass before shipment to the export destination. Services zero-rating: separately charged OEM design, embroidery programming, and quality-control services supplied to a non-resident customer are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 unless the services relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. Drop-shipment zero-rating: where a non-resident principal directs delivery of finished goods in Canada to a carrier for export, the supplier may rely on a drop-shipment certificate (Form GST 350) under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 10. Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline because Canada has no exchange controls; the 180-day benchmark in this dossier is a documentary-nexus target for closing export files and preserving CRA audit readiness.

None mandatory - Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing clearance. A registrant invoice must contain the prescribed GST/HST information under ETA ss. 169(4) and 223 (supplier BN, invoice date, total consideration, tax amount or statement that the supply is zero-rated). GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through NETFILE, E-file, or My Business Account once the electronic-filing threshold is met. Optional interoperability exists through the Pan-Canadian Peppol BIS 3.0 network (voluntary procurement pilots in Ontario and Quebec); British Columbia has enacted no Peppol or e-invoicing mandate. No fiscal production stamps, excise product marks, or toy-specific tax clearance requirements apply to plush dolls and stuffed animals.

CRA statutory limitation exposure: ETA s. 298 (GST/HST) and ITA s. 152(4) (corporate income tax) ordinarily permit reassessment within four years of filing, with no time limit where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud is established. Where export documentary proof is deficient or goods are not exported within a reasonable time, CRA will claw back the zero-rating, re-assess the supply at 5% GST, and, if possession of the plush goods passed in British Columbia, assert the vendor's liability for the 7% BC PST, with interest under ETA s. 322 at prescribed rates from the original remittance date. False-statement and negligence penalties: ETA s. 285(1) - the greater of C$100 and 25% of net tax. Wilful evasion: ETA s. 280(1) and (2) - a fine of not less than 100% and up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment of up to five years; deliberate fabrication of export declarations may also be prosecuted as fraud under Criminal Code s. 380. The 180-day repatriation figure is a compliance target rather than a statutory Canadian exchange-control deadline; failing the benchmark does not itself invalidate zero-rating, but stale receivables and missing remittance evidence weaken the evidentiary nexus and increase clawback risk.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible; Canada observes Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Both current account transactions and capital account transactions are generally open without exchange-control permits. Profit and dividend remittances by a BC corporation to non-resident shareholders are not blocked, but Canadian withholding tax may apply under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, and a branch profits tax may apply to non-resident corporations operating a permanent establishment in Canada. Sanctions screening, source-of-funds documentation, and standard bank compliance review remain applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada does not impose statutory export proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation deadlines. Export receipts may be retained in CAD, USD, or other currencies in permitted bank accounts. Exporters must retain supporting invoices, customs declarations, bills of lading, and payment records for Income Tax Act audit and FINTRAC record-keeping requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); the monetary authority for CAD and fiscal agent for the Government of Canada. Canada does not operate a dedicated foreign exchange control administration. OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) supervises federally regulated banks, and FINTRAC administers AML/CFT obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary collection/payment processor; Low as the primary treasury settlement account. Offshore digital EMI service providers are not authorized under the Bank Act to accept deposits or operate a deposit-taking account in Canada. A BC corporation may use a registered payment service provider for card collection or money movement only if the provider is registered as a money services business with FINTRAC and, where applicable, registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act regime. The final treasury concentration and FX settlement should remain at a tier-1 Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 for inbound equity contributions or shareholder loans from the foreign parent or beneficial owner. Canada does not prescribe a formal central-bank purpose code; the remittance information should clearly state Shareholder Capital Contribution or Shareholder Loan, identify the BC corporation by legal name and incorporation number, and include the ultimate originator details. The receiving Canadian bank will route the wire through its correspondent network and settle in CAD or USD. Funds may be held for three to five business days pending FINTRAC-related identity verification and source-of-funds checks. The required trade document package must include the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, certificate of origin, and, where applicable, inspection certificate. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined general corporate rate 27% (federal 15% + British Columbia 12%); combined small-business rate 11% (federal 9% + BC 2%) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income earned by a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents; treaty-reduced rates: commonly 5% for the beneficial-owner non-resident corporation controlling at least 10% of the Canadian payer's voting shares, and 15% for all other portfolio dividends (e.g., Canada-US, Canada-UK, Canada-EU DTAs).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for any single transaction or series of transactions with a non-arm's length non-resident in a taxation year (for taxation years beginning after 31 December 2022) triggers Form T106 and the mandatory contemporaneous Local File; Master File (Form T1063) is required for MNE groups with consolidated global revenue of at least CAD 1 billion; taxpayers must nevertheless prepare the Local File before the filing due date even below the threshold if the CRA examines the transaction.

Economic substance scope: No standalone ESR registration framework exists in Canada or BC comparable to the UAE or BVI regimes; substance is enforced indirectly through: (a) the permanent establishment and 'central management and control' concepts for residency; (b) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA) and treaty-shopping provisions (s. 94.1 and the OECD Principal Purpose Test) which deny treaty benefits where the structure lacks real economic activity; (c) the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL, ss. 18.2/18.21) capping net interest deductions at 30% of EBITDA for large CFC groups; and (d) for the plush-toy OEM, CRA audits will verify premises, payroll, manufacturing machinery, utility consumption, and operating expenses as evidence of genuine business activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 200% of the tax evaded plus a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understatement; s. 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: summary conviction fine from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; indictable offence: imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA may seize and sell assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate, assent, or acquiesce can be taxed personally for unremitted amounts under s. 227.1; criminal prosecutions can trigger travel bans, asset-freezing orders, and bail restrictions; restitution orders can attach personal residences and personal bank accounts.
Failure to Remit Payroll and Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: 10% late-remittance penalty on the first occurrence and 20% on repeat defaults; prescribed daily compound interest; provincial enforcement through the BC Employment Standards Branch for unpaid wages (directors liable for up to 6 months of wages).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the entire unremitted source deductions plus interest and penalties, without a due-diligence defence; CRA commonly issues s. 227.1 notices of assessment directly to directors; bankruptcy does not discharge such liability.
Shell Company / Sham Domicile

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustments under s. 247; 10% adjustment penalty; loss of the foreign affiliate exemption under ss. 113/91; GAAR nullification of all tax benefits; 50% gross-negligence penalty on the recharacterized income; potential deregistration under the Canada Business Corporations Act for false annual returns.

Director personal liability: A director who directed the profit shift can be jointly assessed personally for the resulting corporate tax liabilities under the s. 227.1 framework; knowingly signing false statutory declarations constitutes perjury and can lead to imprisonment and a travel ban during prosecution.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for a corporation (first offence) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; Criminal Code laundering offences carry fines up to CAD 2,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years; FINTRAC may suspend registrations and issue naming-notice penalties.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who directed, participated in, or were wilfully blind to the laundering face personal criminal liability and asset-confiscation proceedings; courts can impose travel bans, asset freezes, and up to 10 years imprisonment; professional disqualification from acting as directors follows conviction.
GST/PST Fraud and Registration Threshold Abuse

Statutory sanction: 6% per annum interest plus late penalties of 5% and 1% per month (maximum 25% on repeated failures); Excise Tax Act s. 280 fraud penalties: up to 200% of evaded tax and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; BC PST administration penalties up to 10% of unpaid PST plus civil recovery of all amounts and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted GST/HST under s. 323 of the Excise Tax Act; similar liability attaches to officers and trustees for BC PST; a serious evasion conviction allows CRA to register a personal tax lien against directors' properties and issue a departure-prevention order (travel ban).
