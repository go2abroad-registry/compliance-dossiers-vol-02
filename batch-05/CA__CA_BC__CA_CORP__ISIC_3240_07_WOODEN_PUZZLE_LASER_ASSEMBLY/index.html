Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) for federal GST/HST and corporate income tax, including BC provincial corporate income tax administration; BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the Ministry of Finance, Province of British Columbia. Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and BC eTaxBC / OneStop (https://www2.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity engaging in permanent activity in Canada must incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia). Upon incorporation, CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN). To collect GST/HST, the registrant must activate the BN as an RT program account using Form RC1 or through the CRA's Business Registry online. A non-resident corporation carrying on business in BC must also complete extra-provincial registration with BC Corporate Registry, obtain a BC business number, and register for BC PST with the BC Ministry of Finance if it regularly sells or leases tangible personal property in the province.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or pass-through election for a business corporation. Available statutory instruments include: (1) Form RC25 election to treat taxable supplies among closely related corporations as made for nil consideration under the GST/HST regime; (2) Income Tax Act s. 216(1) election for non-resident recipients of Canadian-source rental or royalty income to treat the income as taxable Canadian income; (3) non-calendar fiscal year election under Income Tax Act s. 249.1 subject to CRA approval. Hybrid mismatch and branch loss rules are governed by Code-like directed provisions and cannot be overcome by entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD GST/HST small-supplier threshold based on worldwide taxable supplies; no general exemption threshold for mandatory BC PST registration where the business regularly makes retail sales of taxable goods in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, tangible personal property (wooden puzzles and similar goods) physically exported by the supplier or delivered by the supplier or at the supplier's direction to an address outside Canada is zero-rated for GST/HST. The supplier must retain documentary evidence that the goods left Canada within a reasonable time after supply and that ownership passed to a non-resident purchaser. Services performed on exported goods, including custom CO2 laser cutting/assembly of wooden puzzles, may also be zero-rated when the underlying goods are exported in accordance with the conditions of Schedule VI, Part V. BC PST does not apply to eligible direct export sales when the supplier has proof that the goods were delivered for use outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA GEMINI/CAED where shipment value exceeds C$2,000)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill showing origination in Canada and delivery outside Canada
  • Commercial Invoice with export destination and non-resident purchaser details
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange receipt evidencing payment from a non-resident
  • Customs Broker or Freight Forwarder Certificate of Export

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based invoice authorization for GST/HST. Taxpayers file returns electronically through CRA NETFILE/GST Express or EDI. BC PST requires prescribed invoice and record-keeping content but does not impose pre-clearance of invoices. CRA has announced a PEPPOL-based e-invoicing roadmap, but participation remains voluntary as of the current tax year.

Canada does not impose an explicit statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for GST/HST export zero-rating; the 180-day period reflects a common commercial banking benchmark for export proceeds. CRA may reclassify zero-rated exports as taxable supplies if the documentation is insufficient, resulting in assessment of GST/HST not collected, interest, and retroactive denial of input tax credits. Penalties include gross negligence penalties under Excise Tax Act s. 280 (greater of C$100 and 25% of the net tax amount), wilful evasion fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded, and potentially imprisonment. Export documentation must be retained for six years under subsection 286(1) of the Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls or restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, or capital proceeds for non-resident shareholders of a British Columbia business corporation, provided that applicable corporate tax obligations under the Income Tax Act (Canada) are satisfied and a Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) clearance certificate is obtained for non-resident vendor dispositions in asset sales. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to standard anti-money-laundering reporting thresholds, tax withholding on deemed or actual distributions (Part XIII withholding tax, generally 25% subject to treaty reduction), and the requirement that the corporation be resident in Canada for tax purposes with no disqualifying cross-border migration. For the target wooden puzzle laser assembly business, no sectoral restriction on the repatriation of funds exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements in Canada. Canadian exporters are not required to convert export receipts into CAD within any timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts at authorized financial institutions, subject to normal bookkeeping and tax reporting requirements under the Income Tax Act and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), including FINTRAC reporting for large virtual currency or cash transactions where applicable. For the ISIC 3240 wooden puzzle assembly operation, export proceeds from cross-border e-commerce platforms (Amazon Pay, Stripe) may be held in USD or CAD operational accounts without any surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML oversight; foreign exchange administration is not centralized as Canada maintains a liberalized, market-based FX regime without general exchange controls, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates federally chartered banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for payment facilitation but Medium for treasury-grade principal banking. Legal constraints: Canada does not issue passportable Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licenses equivalent to the EU; non-resident digital EMIs must either rely on domestic payment service provider partnerships under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) or operate as authorized foreign banks with a representative office. A BC corporation may open settlement accounts with regulated Canadian fintechs such as Wealthsimple or Koho, but these entities are not full-service commercial banks and cannot provide credit, FX forwards, or SWIFT direct membership. For the target Amazon Pay and Stripe settlement rails, the most legally robust structure is a dual-rail approach: a Tier 1 commercial bank as master treasury account and an RPAA-registered payment service provider for e-commerce payouts. The use of unregulated offshore EMIs is discouraged because FINTRAC reporting and KYC equivalence cannot be assured under the PCMLTFA.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols require all inbound capital injections to be remitted via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire for USD) from a bank account titled in the name of the shareholder or parent corporation. The wire must include a purpose code consistent with the OECD Balance of Payments Manual and the Canadian Financial Monitoring for FINTRAC: for equity capital injection, use BOP code 'E002 – Equity capital, shares' or where applicable 'A060 – Trade credit and advances' for intercompany loans; a corporate resolution and subscription agreement must support the transfer. Verification protocols: (i) the receiving Canadian bank will apply Rule 33A correspondent banking checks, (ii) the beneficial owner must match the BC corporate registry, (iii) for USD receipts exceeding CAD 10,000, the bank will complete an Large Cash Transaction Report (LCTR) where applicable and a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) threshold analysis, and (iv) evidence of source of funds must be retained for five years under the PCMLTFA. The capital account should be maintained at a Schedule I bank to avoid delays in clearing through the Canadian dollar bulk settlement system. For the target laser puzzle corporation, a minimum initial capital injection of CAD 50,000–100,000 is recommended, all routed through the BMO capital escrow account before transfer to the operational account.Outbound trade disbursements for raw wood materials and laser components will be settled through (a) SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for supplier payments under USD/CNY or CAD/CNY, (b) commercial letters of credit (L/C) for large consignments exceeding USD 50,000 with a confirming bank in the supplier jurisdiction, and (c) SWIFT MT202/MT202 COV for correspondent bank coverage where the beneficiary's bank is a secondary correspondent. For cross-border e-commerce disbursements from Amazon and Stripe, the clearing rails are card network settlement files (ACH via Stripe) and Amazon's disbursement engine, which initiate an ACH credit to the BC operational account in CAD or USD. In both cases, the transaction must be recorded with the correct BOP code: 'A120 – Goods' for merchandise imports and 'A061 – Trade services' for freight and logistics. The bank's Foreign Exchange Online (FX Online) platform is recommended for real-time conversion of USD trade settlements into CAD, with all conversions supported by underlying commercial invoices (customs Form B3, bill of lading, and supplier commercial invoice) to satisfy FINTRAC's 'reasonable measures' standard. Disbursement thresholds over CAD 10,000 require EFT reports to FINTRAC if the transaction is structured to evade reporting, which is illegal; no fragmentation of payments is permitted.Outright FX forwards (CAD/USD and CAD/CNY) with delivery up to 12 months, permitted only with a Schedule I bank counterparty which has an ISDA 1998 FX and Currency Option Definitions agreement or a standard FX Master Agreement; underlying exposure must be demonstrated by purchase orders or projected Amazon payouts.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only in jurisdictions where the settlement currency is non-convertible; since CAD is fully convertible, NDFs are not necessary but may be offered by Canadian banks for CAD/CNY hedges with offshore counterparties, subject to OFSI capital adequacy treatment and prudent risk limits.Cross-currency swaps (CAD/USD and CAD/CNY) for matching medium-term working capital exposure, requiring an ISDA Master Agreement and Collateral Support Annex, with mandatory mark-to-market valuation and collateralization at 100% of threshold where the counterparty is an unrated offshore entity.Plain vanilla FX options (call/spread) to hedge downside risk on USD revenue from Amazon US marketplace, cleared through the bank's derivatives desk; options must be recorded in the corporation's treasury policy and approved by board resolution if notional exceeds CAD 1 million.Delivery-linked currency swaps under a bilateral trade agreement: when importing wood materials from US or Chinese suppliers, a bank-guaranteed FX swap may be embedded in the letter of credit structure, but a standalone speculative derivative is prohibited under Canadian business corporation prudent investment standards.
