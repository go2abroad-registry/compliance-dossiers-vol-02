Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate federally via Corporations Canada online or provincially via BC Corporate Online; non-resident ownership is permitted. 2) Apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) through BRO or Form RC1 (non-residents may use RC4591), activating program accounts RP (T2 corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST) and RC (payroll if Canadian employees exist). 3) File T2 federal+BC combined corporate returns within 6 months of fiscal year-end; GST/HST returns are due quarterly or annually for registrants below CAD 1.5M gross revenue. 4) Register for BC PST through eTaxBC before the first taxable retail sale (no registration threshold). 5) Activate a CBSA exporter/importer account linked to the BN for future B13A export declarations via CERS.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange-control permits for inbound/outbound capital flows, trade payments, dividends, or royalties. Non-resident direct and portfolio investment is generally free; however, controlling acquisitions of a Canadian business may trigger the Investment Canada Act notification or net-benefit review, and all financial transfers must satisfy FINTRAC reporting thresholds and Global Affairs Canada sanctions screening. Profit remittance through dividends is permitted without central bank approval, subject to non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act and applicable tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain foreign-currency receipts in a multi-currency corporate account and manage the FX exposure in-house. The only obligations are FINTRAC records for any transaction of CAD 10,000 or greater, customs and trade reporting, and a demonstrable source-of-funds trail in the corporate accounting system.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as federal banking regulator; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/ATF reporting authority; no dedicated foreign exchange control administration under Canadian law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) and digital challenger banks are not authorized as deposit-taking institutions in Canada and cannot receive deposits, provide bank-level corporate accounts, or access the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or Real-Time Rail clearing infrastructure. Such entities may operate only as limited-purpose money-services businesses under provincial registration and FINTRAC MSB licensing, strictly prohibiting deposit acceptance and limiting FX execution. They are acceptable only as a supplementary payment acceptance rail, not as a primary treasury clearing bank. A Canadian Schedule I chartered bank is mandatory for lawful cross-border liquidity management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections must be wired via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the commercial bank’s Canadian clearing participant, using the recipient account number, bank identifier (institution number and transit number), and BIC. Payment may be settled in CAD or via a USD clearing corridor through CLS or correspondent bank netting. The sender must annotate the payment with a source-of-funds narrative, e.g., 'Shareholder Equity Injection' or 'Direct Investment Subscription', and the Canadian receiving bank will apply an ISO 20022 purpose code appropriate to a direct-investment capital contribution. There is no central-bank approval, but any amount of CAD 10,000 or more is reportable to FINTRAC, and the transaction must be screened against the Special Economic Measures Act and Global Affairs Canada sanctions lists. For non-resident controlling acquisitions, the corporate secretariat should concurrently file the Investment Canada Act notification via the federal ISED portal if applicable; failure to do so does not block the wire but exposes the entity to administrative penalty.For recurring trade settlements involving fabrics, synthetic wig fibers, dyes, fasteners, and packaging supplies, the corporate treasury must use SWIFT MT103 for low-value or high-frequency disbursements, and an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (LC) for high-value cross-border purchases. With an LC, the bank’s trade finance desk transmits the LC to the beneficiary bank via SWIFT MT700 and settle through correspondent banking; subsequent drawings are paid under MT202 via the CAD/USD correspondent network. For all outbound wires, execute export/import control screening against the Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List, the Special Economic Measures Act, and UN/OFAC-type restrictions where the counterparty is a US-linked vendor. Payment in USD from a matched USD account avoids unnecessary FX conversion; otherwise, spot FX is executed within the bid-offer spread of the clearing bank. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general rate (federal 15% + British Columbia 12%); 11% combined small-business rate on first CAD 500,000 active business income (federal 9% + BC 2%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by Canadian-resident corporations to non-resident shareholders; reduced by applicable tax treaty (e.g., 15% portfolio dividend rate and 5% corporate beneficial-owner rate for a US shareholder owning at least 10% voting stock under the Canada-US Treaty).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File / Master File equivalents) is required when the aggregate value of controlled transactions with non-residents in a taxation year equals or exceeds CAD 1,000,000 (ITA s. 247(4)). For large multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million (approximately CAD 1.34 billion), a Country-by-Country Report and Master File must be filed with CRA.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, substantive economic activity is enforced through the Income Tax Act's general anti-avoidance rule (s. 245), transfer-pricing recharacterization (s. 247), treaty entitlement and LOB/PPT clauses in Canada's treaties and the OECD Multilateral Instrument, and the substance-over-form jurisprudence. A corporation or offshore entity without real staff, premises, assets, decision-making, or operational risk in its jurisdiction is exposed to denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and penalty assessments for artificial tax avoidance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Intentional Misstatement / Concealment

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s. 239: on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; criminal prosecution may be pursued by CRA.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid taxes, penalties, and interest; individual prosecution may result in personal fines, confiscation of assets, travel restraints, and imprisonment.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Non-Resident Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s. 227(8), the payer is liable for a penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount plus arrears interest computed on a daily basis; the unremitted amount is held in a deemed trust for the Crown and constitutes a first-priority charge over corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1 for the full unremitted Part XIII amounts, including penalties and interest, without the need to prove negligence; CRA may issue director liability assessments and enforce through bank freezing, garnishment, and travel-ban applications.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Artificial Offshore Shifting

Statutory sanction: CRA may re-determine the arm's-length price under ITA s. 247(2), assess additional tax plus gross-up interest, and impose a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the upward adjustment under s. 247(3); GAAR may deny the entire tax benefit and trigger s. 245 penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in mispricing may face gross-negligence penalties under s. 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; personal liability may be enforced against their personal property, and travel bans can be imposed for attempts to leave Canada with unpaid tax debts.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Treaty relief is denied under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) or Limitation on Benefits clause; the Canadian payer is reassessed for the full 25% Part XIII withholding deficiency plus interest; administrative penalties under ITA s. 162 and s. 163 may apply; under the CbC and substance-based reporting rules, false declarations create criminal exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the Canadian corporation to sign an incorrect NR301/NR302 certificate or who intentionally structure the shell to misrepresent substance are liable civilly for the withheld tax shortfall; personal fines of up to 200% of the avoided tax and imprisonment for fraud can be imposed; court-ordered travel bans and asset seizures may be used to enforce director liability.
