Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll and excise; Ministry of Finance, British Columbia (BC Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC) — BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) and BC corporate income tax (administered federally by CRA under the Canada–British Columbia Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement). Portal: CRA My Business Account / Business Registration Online (BRO); BC eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

A BC Business Corporation must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by telephone. Mandatory sequence: (1) file Articles of Incorporation under the BC Business Corporations Act (or the Canada Business Corporations Act with BC extra-provincial registration); (2) obtain the BN; (3) open Income Tax program account RC0001 for T2 corporate tax; (4) open GST/HST program account RT0001 by filing GST1 or through BRO, selecting standard quarterly or annual filing cycle; (5) register separately for BC PST through BC eTaxBC (Form FIN 419) before making any taxable retail sale in BC, as PST has no small-supplier exemption; (6) open Payroll account RP0001 when hiring employees; (7) if the corporation is controlled by non-residents, file the ITA s. 153 information return and, where required, appoint a resident representative under ITA s. 221.2.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-controlled private corporation eligible for the federal small business deduction — 9% federal rate on active business income up to CAD 500,000 — and the BC small business rate of 11% on the same CAD 500,000 eligible income; resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income under ITA ss. 2, 3 and 250; no US-style pass-through or check-the-box treatment is available; non-resident-owned investment corporations and public corporations are subject to different rate structures). — A BC Business Corporation must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by telephone. Mandatory sequence: (1) file Articles of Incorporation under the BC Business Corporations Act (or the Canada Business Corporations Act with BC extra-provincial registration); (2) obtain the BN; (3) open Income Tax program account RC0001 for T2 corporate tax; (4) open GST/HST program account RT0001 by filing GST1 or through BRO, selecting standard quarterly or annual filing cycle; (5) register separately for BC PST through BC eTaxBC (Form FIN 419) before making any taxable retail sale in BC, as PST has no small-supplier exemption; (6) open Payroll account RP0001 when hiring employees; (7) if the corporation is controlled by non-residents, file the ITA s. 153 information return and, where required, appoint a resident representative under ITA s. 221.2.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-controlled private corporation eligible for the federal small business deduction — 9% federal rate on active business income up to CAD 500,000 — and the BC small business rate of 11% on the same CAD 500,000 eligible income; resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income under ITA ss. 2, 3 and 250; no US-style pass-through or check-the-box treatment is available; non-resident-owned investment corporations and public corporations are subject to different rate structures).

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. Available elections include: (1) capital cost allowance elections under ITA s. 13 and Schedule II; (2) tax-deferred rollover elections on transfers under ITA s. 85, amalgamations under s. 87, and winding-up under s. 88; (3) Small Business Deduction allocation agreement among associated CCPCs on Form T1009 under ITA s. 125(3); (4) GST/HST elections under ETA s. 211 (closely related corporations) and s. 273 (nil consideration for intercompany taxable supplies); (5) GST/HST Quick Method election under ETA s. 227; (6) fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1; (7) where applicable, the s. 216(1) election for non-resident rental income; hybrid mismatch exposure is limited by ITA ss. 18.1 and 18.2, with no domestic hybrid entity election comparable to a check-the-box regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of gross worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports) measured over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or any single calendar quarter (ETA s. 240(1) small-supplier threshold); once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration applies from the month following the excess. PST: no registration threshold — every vendor carrying on business in BC must register with the BC Ministry of Finance before making retail sales, irrespective of revenue. BC Employer Health Tax registration is only required once BC payroll reaches CAD 500,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Canada are zero-rated under ETA s. 22 where the supplier exports the goods, or delivers them to a common carrier or customs broker for export, within 60 days of the supply (or such longer period as CRA policy permits). Services supplied to non-resident recipients outside Canada, including e-commerce support services for offline tabletop gaming products such as handcrafted epoxy resin liquid-core dice, qualify for zero-rating under ETA Sch. VI Part V s. 7. The exporter must demonstrate delivery outside Canada through a bill of lading, customs export declaration, and contract terms, and must remain able to substantiate a true foreign-resident recipient in order to claim input tax credits and refunds of GST paid on inputs.

  • Canadian Export Declaration filed with CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System / ACI eManifest, where a formal export declaration is required
  • Bill of Lading (Master or House) or Air Waybill evidencing physical delivery to an overseas consignee
  • Commercial Invoice annotated as zero-rated export under ETA s. 22 / Sch. VI Part V
  • Packing List and cargo manifest for the resin dice shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT confirmation, or bank/FX receipt evidencing repatriation of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Customs broker instructions or Power of Attorney, if a licensed broker is used
  • Kickstarter/Stripe or PayPal transaction records, customer shipping addresses, and IP-based evidence establishing non-resident recipient status

Canada operates no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing clearance platform comparable to Brazilian NF-e or Italian SdI. CRA accepts paper, PDF, and any electronic invoice format provided the disclosure rules in ETA s. 221 and GST/HST Information Requirements are satisfied. Registrants with taxable sales above CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE within CRA My Business Account; below that threshold, paper filing is permitted. Unless a registrant exits the small-supplier regime, there is no pre-clearance API obligation, but the registrant must preserve complete invoice and export documentation for six years under ETA s. 286 and make them available to CRA on demand.

CRA may reassess export zero-rating claims within the normal four-year period, which is unlimited where a return was omitted or where misrepresentation, fraud, or willful neglect is established. Failure to retain a valid export documentation bundle recharacterizes the supply as taxable at 5% GST plus 7% PST, giving rise to net tax reassessments, interest, and refund clawbacks of previously credited input tax credits. Civil penalties include: ETA s. 280(1) false statements penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax evaded; s. 285(1) repeated non-compliance penalty; s. 280(2) failure to file or remit penalty; statutory interest charged at the prescribed rate (2025 annual rate 9%, compounded daily) on all unremitted and clawed-back amounts; and penalties for failure to keep books and records under ITA s. 238. Where an exporter knowingly fabricates bills of lading or inward remittance certificates, criminal tax evasion charges may be laid under ETA s. 327(1) and ITA s. 239, exposing the corporation to fines in addition to any evaded tax and, for directors and officers who directed the fraud, imprisonment of up to five years. Clawback adjustments must be reported as a debit to net tax in the period in which reassessed and disclosed in the corresponding T2 corporate income tax filings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both capital and current account transactions are fully convertible in Canada. The Canadian dollar floats freely with no exchange-control permits, no surrender requirements, and no limitations on profit remittance by a non-resident shareholder. The only practical hurdles are indirect: (i) outgoing dividends are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% or a treaty-reduced rate, and (ii) the receiving Canadian bank must complete standard AML and tax-identification formalities before wiring funds abroad. For the target business of digital tabletop gaming dice sales, bank transfer payments or crowdfunding payouts by Kickstarter/Stripe/PayPal may be treated as current-account receipts and are freely convertible into CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. An exporting British Columbia corporation may retain and use foreign-currency proceeds without a requirement to repatriate or convert them into CAD. Any receipt is nonetheless reportable for GST/HST and income tax, and for foreign exchange gains/losses, under the Income Tax Act and applicable CRA compliance forms.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central monetary authority; formal exchange control administration is not a standalone agency under Canadian law. Operational FX oversight is distributed among the Global Affairs trade finance directorate, the Canada Border Services Agency for cross-border currency reporting obligations, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for regulated financial institutions. The Governor of the Bank of Canada does not pre-authorise commercial FX transfers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, with legal restraint. Canada does not issue an EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) licence; an entity accepting deposits must be a federally regulated bank or be supervised by OSFI. Offshore digital EMIs are acceptable only as non-deposit payment facilitators (PSP-like vendors) and cannot operate as the primary treasury account for an escrow in British Columbia. A local Schedule I bank account remains mandatory for capital escrow, corporate identity, and tax reporting. If a fintech rail such as Wise/Payoneer or Stripe Treasury is used, the Canadian entity must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, appoint a Canadian compliance officer, adopt a written AML/CFT program, and report large cross-border electronic funds transfers (over CAD 10,000).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contribution by a non-resident shareholder is executed by wire transfer from the offshore bank to the BC entity’s Canadian bank account. The remittance should use SWIFT MT103 with an OECD-BPM6 clear purpose code, e.g. 'equity investment (521)' or 'capital contribution to trading operation'. The receiving Canadian bank must be informed via a deposit slip note or secured transactional reference such as 'ISSUANCE OF SHARES' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN'. A board resolution approving the share subscription or intercompany loan must be kept on file along with the subscriber’s source-of-funds documentation. Because Canada has no FX allocation or exchange control approval, the only execution hurdles are completion of the bank’s legal-entity identification and AML checks.For global consumer sales, the primary rail is the Stripe/PayPal settlement of crowdfunding and webstore payouts direct to the Canadian CAD account. For international trade with resin suppliers, beading agent and packaging houses, the standard rail is a Canadian bank international wire in SWIFT MT103 / MT202 COV with a transaction purpose code such as 'supplier payment to a resin manufacturer' or 'advance import payment'. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27.0% combined headline rate (federal 15.0% + British Columbia general provincial rate 12.0%); reduced combined rate of 10.0% (federal 9.0% + BC 1.0%) applies to Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) active business income up to CAD 500,000 gross per year

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% on dividends paid or credited to non-residents under subsection 212(2) of the Income Tax Act; the rate is reduced under applicable tax treaties (e.g., 5% for a substantial corporate shareholder under the Canada-US treaty, 10% under the Canada-China treaty, and 15% for portfolio shareholders under most Canadian DTAs)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian transfer-pricing rules under subsection 247(4) require contemporaneous documentation to be prepared and filed within three months after the CRA requests it; the statutory T106 disclosure is mandatorily filed when non-arm's length transactions with a single non-resident exceed CAD 1,000,000; a Local File is expected by CRA practice where related-party transactions with any one non-resident exceed CAD 1,000,000, and a Master File is generally expected for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 10,000,000 or more; Country-by-Country reporting applies where consolidated group revenue is CAD 1.1 billion or more; for the present dice manufacturing operation, a prudent Local File should be maintained even below these thresholds using CUP benchmark prices for raw resin procurement and end-product comparables

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the manner of zero-tax jurisdictions, but economic substance is constitutionally enforced through: (1) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act; (2) the foreign accrual property income (FAPI) rules under sections 91-95; (3) the arm's length transfer-pricing rules under section 247; and (4) CRA's audit doctrine under Information Circular IC 87-2R; a BC-incorporated dice manufacturer must maintain demonstrable substance in British Columbia — physical premises, employees engaged in mould design, casting, quality control, fulfilment and billing — and cannot channel profits to a zero-tax haven without meaningful people functions, assets, and risk assumption; the CRA will re-characterize intercompany agreements that lack commercial plausibility and allocate income to the jurisdiction where the core value-creating activities actually occur

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under subsection 163(2): 50% of the understated tax or overclaimed credits, plus prescribed daily compound interest; criminal conviction under section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years; the CRA may also revoke the corporation's GST/HST registration and reassess any statute-barred year where misrepresentation is established.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling officers who knowingly participate in filing a false return face personal criminal liability with sentences of up to 5 years of imprisonment; the CRA can issue assessment against the individual under subsection 160(1) for transfers of property made to defeat tax claims, issue garnishment and asset-freezing orders, and impose travel bans through court orders or immigration hold proceedings when the accused is a non-citizen or at risk of leaving Canada.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR denies the tax benefit and re-characterizes the arrangement based on economic substance; CRA reallocates income to the Canadian entity under section 247 and imposes 25% Part XIII withholding on deemed dividends or royalties; a 25% branch tax under Part XIV can be applied if the substance is found in Canada through a foreign enterprise; administrative monetary penalties under the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act may also apply for false schedules submitted in relation to the shell arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors face joint and several liability for the reassessed corporate tax, withholding shortfalls, and penalties; in egregious cases, criminal charges under section 380 of the Criminal Code for fraud can result in imprisonment; the BC Registrar can issue a dissolution order for a shell corporation failing to demonstrate BC substance; individuals may be barred from re-entering Canada and subject to international arrest notices where fraud or money laundering is connected to the shell structure.
Money Laundering and FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: up to CAD 500,000 per contravention for corporations and CAD 100,000 per contravention for individuals; criminal recourse under the Criminal Code for laundering proceeds of crime leads to fines of up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 10 years; the CRA may also apply gross negligence penalties of up to CAD 100,000 on unreported foreign-source income connected to the laundering activity.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face personal administrative penalties for failing to implement FINTRAC controls; conviction for laundering offences exposes individuals to up to 10 years of imprisonment; courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and forfeiture orders; Fusion-centre investigations frequently lead to non-resident directors being denied Canadian entry and to extradition proceedings for related fraud.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Default

Statutory sanction: Penalty under subsection 227(8) of the Income Tax Act: 10% of the amount required to be withheld if the failure was not deliberate, rising to 20% if the failure was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence; the CRA also charges daily compound interest on the unremitted amounts; the tax, penalties, and interest are recoverable from the Canadian corporation; where a person transfers assets to avoid payment, section 160 assessments attach to the transferee.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted non-resident withholding tax under section 227.1, irrespective of whether the corporation itself can pay; the CRA must issue a liability notice within two years after the director ceases office, but the collection remedies extend to the director's personal assets; in fraudulent cases, directors can face criminal sanction and imprisonment; travel bans and passport seizures are available to compel attendance and prevent dissipation before judgment; the corporation's BC registrar may also refuse to issue a certificate of good standing until the withholding obligations are discharged.
