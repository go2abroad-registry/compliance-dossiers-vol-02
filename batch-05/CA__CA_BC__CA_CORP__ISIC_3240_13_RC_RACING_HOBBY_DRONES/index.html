Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal; British Columbia Ministry of Finance for PST registration

E-government filing portal

A Canadian business corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the British Columbia Business Corporations Act must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a separate GST/HST account from CRA. Application is made through Business Registration Online (BRO) using Form RC1 or through BC Registry's combined BC-REG system. For GST/HST, the corporation must choose an effective date and may be required to file an RC1 even if it is a small supplier; for PST, registration is effected separately with the BC Ministry of Finance through the BC one-stop business registration, unless exempt. Non-resident corporations without Canadian incorporation must also complete CRA Form RC391 and may be required to provide a waiver or security, but this dossier covers a resident Canadian corporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident in Canada by incorporation, subject to CRA assessment on worldwide income; BC provincial corporate income tax at the provincial level; no pass-through eligibility for the corporation; not a tax-exempt zone entity; not on a territorial scope, but foreign-source business income is generally taxable in Canada for resident corporations

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime. Specific statutory elections exist under the federal Income Tax Act, including the capital dividend account election under subsection 83(2), a deemed dividend election on winding-up or share redemption, and an associated corporations election under subsection 256(1) to allocate taxable capital. For GST/HST, an eligible corporation can elect the Quick Method (GST/HST) or, where applicable, the election for nil consideration intercompany supplies. BC PST provisions include a number of administrative elections for direct sellers and lease/rental arrangements. No hybrid mismatch election is available under Canadian domestic law except as referenced in the OECD Multilateral Instrument when relevant to cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of tangible personal property exported directly from Canada or supplied to a non-resident for delivery outside Canada; also applies to the provision of a service performed exclusively outside Canada, and to services performed in Canada in respect of exported tangible personal property when substantially all of the service is for such property. The manufacturing and sale of carbon-fiber FPV racing drone parts and integrated motors qualifies as zero-rated if the goods are physically exported and documentary evidence is retained. The exporter must be a GST/HST registrant, must obtain proof of export (typically CBSA export declaration B13A, bill of lading, freight invoices and commercial invoices) and must retain evidence of receipt of payment from the non-resident purchaser. Services supplied to a non-resident in connection with the export of goods may also be zero-rated if they are supplied to a non-resident and are not for use in Canada. A zero-rated exporter may input tax credits and may receive a net GST refund if the input tax credits exceed output tax.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Declaration (B13A) or proof of export
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank wire/payment proof

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API interoperability, or digital stamping for invoices. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records provided they are readily accessible and contain prescribed information. The federal government has adopted a voluntary e-invoicing framework using the Pan-European Public Procurement OnLine (Peppol) standards; no business is currently required to use a government fiscal clearance gateway. GST/HST registrants must retain source documents, including invoices and export documentation, for six years after the reporting date or as otherwise prescribed. BC PST laws require vendors to retain complete sales and purchase records for at least six years, but do not require pre-clearance.

CRA can reassess and claw back GST/HST zero-rating refunds if export documentation is insufficient or lost. The normal reassessment period is four years from the day the tax became ascertainable; it extends to seven years (or eight years for GST/HST net tax assessment under ETA subsection 298(3)) where there has been misrepresentation, fraud or neglect. A taxpayer claiming zero-rating without maintaining the mandatory export and remittance documentation may be assessed GST/HST plus arrears interest. Civil penalties include the gross negligence penalty equal to 25% of the misreported amount under Excise Tax Act subsection 285.1(1) (or 50% in cases of subsequent repeat failure), and a penalty under subsection 285.1(2) for facilitating false claims. Willful evasion can lead to criminal prosecution under subsection 327(1) of the Excise Tax Act, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment, pursuant to subsection 327(2). Any refund claimed on fraudulent export documentation is subject to immediate reassessment, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. No exchange control restrictions operate on current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, repayment of shareholder loans, and foreign direct investment flows are unrestricted. The only hurdles are tax reporting obligations (e.g., CRA Part XIII withholding at 25% on dividends, reducible to 15% under the Canada-US tax treaty in many cases), corporate filing requirements (T2/T106), and compliance with FINTRAC record-keeping and large transfer reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may hold foreign currency receipts in onshore or offshore accounts, subject only to CRA transfer pricing documentation and the requirement that invoices and financial records substantiate the revenue.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) through the Department of Finance; exchange control and AML/CFT enforcement are delegated to FINTRAC and OSFI, with no central-bank foreign exchange licensing or permit regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. Canada does not legally recognize the EU-style 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licence. A foreign EMI cannot independently open a CAD clearing account unless it registers with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB), obtains provincial money-transfer licensing where required, and enters into a sponsor/bank-account arrangement with a Canadian Schedule I bank. Offshore 'digital-only' EMIs operating without a Canadian banking sponsor are viable only for ancillary payment facilitation, not for settlement of the company's primary treasury account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 – total cross-border non-arm's length transactions exceeding this amount trigger mandatory filing of Form T106 and CRA's contemporaneous documentation expectations equivalent to Local File / Master File support

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations regime; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under s.245 of the Income Tax Act disregards transactions lacking economic substance and bona fide commercial purpose. A British Columbia corporation functioning solely as a passive invoice conduit without employees, premises, or operational control over drone manufacturing will be treated as an abusive tax arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Upon conviction under s.238(1) of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA may also impose gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of understated taxes and reassess beyond the normal limitation period.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed or knowingly acquiesced in the false filings are jointly liable under s.227.1 for unremitted corporate withholdings and may face personal fines, restitution orders, and imprisonment in cases of criminal fraud.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA will invoke GAAR to deny the tax benefit, recharacterize the arrangement, impose penalties under s.247 or s.163.2, and may issue a deemed dividend adjustment resulting in Part XIII withholding liabilities; failure to maintain a BC registered office can lead to administrative dissolution under the Business Corporations Act.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for transfer price adjustments and deemed dividends if they signed false undertakings, failed to maintain corporate records, or permitted distribution of assets while the corporation was insolvent; courts may pierce the veil where the company is a sham.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act of up to CAD 2,000,000 per contravention for corporations; criminal convictions carry fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment; FINTRAC may also issue compliance orders halting operations.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for AML program failures where they failed to designate a compliance officer, failed to train staff, or deliberately obstructed a FINTRAC examination; willful non-compliance can result in imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Section 247(3) imposes a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment; CRA may deny offsetting adjustments and reassess both federal and provincial income tax; failure to file Form T106 can incur penalties per day and cumulative foreign-reporting penalties of up to CAD 12,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or failed to document intercompany pricing may be assessed as joint and several obligors for the corporation's s.247 penalty and related interest; serious misconduct can amount to culpable negligence supporting personal liability.
Personal Liability of Directors / Withholding Obligations

Statutory sanction: Under s.160 (transferee liability) and s.227.1 of the Income Tax Act, directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST and source deductions; civil liability includes restitution equal to the amount of tax diverted; criminal tax conviction may add fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierceable: directors may be personally assessed for all unpaid withholdings, subjected to asset freezes and travel prohibitions pending tax enforcement, and, in flagrant cases, sentenced to up to 5 years of imprisonment.
