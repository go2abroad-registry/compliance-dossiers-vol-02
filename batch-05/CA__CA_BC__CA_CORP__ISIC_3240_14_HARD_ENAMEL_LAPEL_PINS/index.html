Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada is the national tax authority for federal corporate income tax and GST/HST, accessible through CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, via the eTaxBC portal (https://www.etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

A corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the BC Business Corporations Act is automatically issued a nine-digit Business Number (BN) by CRA upon incorporation. The owner must then activate program accounts through CRA My Business Account or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts): account RC for corporate income tax, account RT for GST/HST, and account RM for import/export (customs) purposes. For foreign non-resident corporations registering from outside Canada, the applicant submits Form RC1 to the CRA Sudbury Tax Services Office and receives a BN and any required non-resident GST/HST program accounts; the same BN is used for GST/HST and customs. Registration for BC PST is completed separately through the BC eTaxBC portal when the vendor has a permanent establishment in BC or regularly delivers taxable goods into BC for sale. No minimum capital is required, and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted under Canadian law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and is not eligible for pass-through treatment. There is no Canadian equivalent of the US check-the-box regime; a corporation incorporated in Canada is resident for tax purposes, and foreign-owned Canadian corporations are subject to Canadian federal and provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income, with British Columbia corporate income tax allocated by permanent establishment.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Canadian federal or provincial tax law; hybrid-mismatch rules are applied statutorily under subsection 18.2(8) of the Income Tax Act. Relevant elections available to an enamel-pin exporter include: (i) Excise Tax Act subsection 156(2) election to treat supplies between closely related corporations as made for nil consideration for GST/HST purposes; (ii) election under the Excise Tax Act to change the GST/HST reporting period to monthly, quarterly, or annual; (iii) Income Tax Act subsection 249.1(1) election to establish a non-calendar corporate fiscal year-end for tax purposes (the first tax year cannot exceed 53 weeks); and (iv) BC PST election permitting a group of registered corporations to file consolidated PST returns. A non-resident corporation may also elect under Income Tax Act section 184 to make a branch tax election for Canadian-source business income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (the small supplier threshold): a vendor must register for GST/HST if, in any single calendar quarter or cumulatively over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters, the value of taxable supplies made in Canada, including sales of hard enamel lapel pins, is greater than CAD 30,000. Registration is compulsory once the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is available to non-resident vendors. For BC PST, a non-resident or resident vendor must register with the BC Ministry of Finance when it has a permanent establishment in BC, makes retail sales of taxable goods in BC, or causes taxable goods to be brought into BC for sale or rental.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of hard enamel lapel pins qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when sold to a non-resident and physically exported from Canada. The vendor must establish that: (i) possession of the tangible goods was transferred to the foreign buyer outside Canada, or the vendor delivered the goods to a carrier, courier, or freight forwarder in Canada for shipment outside Canada; (ii) the goods were exported as soon as reasonably possible and in any event within 60 days after delivery to the carrier; (iii) the sale was made pursuant to a genuine contract of sale to a person who is not resident in Canada; and (iv) the vendor retains satisfactory evidence of the actual export, such as a customs export declaration and carrier waybill. Related international services supplied to the non-resident buyer, including design, enamel colour separation, polishing, and plating performed for use wholly outside Canada, may also be zero-rated under section 7 of Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A Export Declaration or the Canada Border Services Agency e-manifest/AES record containing the Commercial Invoice references and export particulars)
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean) or Air Waybill (Air Waybill showing the actual port or airport of exit from Canada and the foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or CRA-accepted equivalent evidence of foreign funds receipt, including Etsy Direct Pay payout reports, Stripe settlement statements, SWIFT credit advices, or bank receipts showing the export proceeds were received from the foreign buyer)

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval, or digital stamping regime for GST/HST invoices. Invoices must satisfy the information requirements of the Excise Tax Act, including the vendor name, Business Number and GST/HST registration number, transaction date, and tax charged. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE on a monthly, quarterly, or annual cycle, with no API-based clearance or cryptographic stamping. Pan-Canadian e-invoicing using the Peppol-BIS standard is voluntary and is used primarily in business-to-government procurement; no CRA authentication API or clearance stamp is required for cross-border exports of enamel pins.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims by examining the exporter's sales contracts, shipping records, customs declarations, and banking evidence. If the exporter cannot substantiate that the goods physically left Canada within the required 60-day export window and that the sale proceeds were received from outside Canada, with CRA administrative practice generally expecting open foreign receivables to be settled within 180 days, the 0% rate is clawed back retroactively and the full 5% GST is reassessed together with compound daily interest. Penalties for false or fraudulent export claims include: (i) a penalty under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act equal to 25% of the tax understated or the rebate, refund, or credit overstated when the false statement was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence; (ii) extension of the normal four-year reassessment limitation period indefinitely where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established; (iii) administrative monetary penalties imposed by the Canada Border Services Agency for inaccurate export declarations under the Customs Act; and (iv) criminal prosecution under section 327.3 of the Excise Tax Act, carrying fines of up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment of up to five years for indictable offences, with similar liability under the Income Tax Act for wilful evasion.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. No registration, approval, currency rationing, or incremental FX licence is required for profit conversion, dividend remittance, or equity repatriation. Statutory constraints are limited to: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties paid by a Canadian payer (25% statutory rate; treaty-reduced — under the Canada–US Income Tax Convention, generally 15%, with a 5% rate for a US corporate recipient owning 10% or more of voting shares); (ii) thin-capitalisation and interest-withholding rules under ITA ss. 18(4), 18(5), and 212 regarding shareholder loans; and (iii) the B.C. Business Corporations Act solvency test (s.186) applicable to any capital reduction or distribution from an escrowed capital reserve. For a boutique ISIC 3240 hard-enamel lapel-pin manufacturer, the Investment Canada Act review threshold is inapplicable at this size. Etsy Direct Pay and Stripe trade receivables are ordinary current-account items; USD balances may be held indefinitely in a Canadian bank USD operating account with no surrender obligation or conversion mandate. Profit remittance is executed as an ordinary dividend supported by a directors' resolution, financial statements, and an NR4/T5 tax record.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or forced surrender for export receipts in USD, EUR, or CNY. All proceeds from die-struck brass cloisonné enamel badge exports may be retained in a foreign-currency account at a Schedule I bank or converted voluntarily at spot. Persistent compliance duties: (i) GST/HST zero-rating under Excise Tax Act Sch. VI Part V (s.1) requires retained export documentation — Etsy/Stripe settlement reports, bills of lading, customs B3 entry or US import entry, and supplier purchase orders — for a 6-year retention period; (ii) T1135 Foreign Income Verification Statement must be filed if the aggregate cost amount of specified foreign property (USD bank balances, foreign receivables, shares) exceeds CAD 100,000; (iii) FINTRAC reportable transaction rules apply to any electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; (iv) books and records must be maintained in CAD under ITA s.230 unless the corporation validly elects USD as its functional currency under ITA s.261, which is appropriate for a USD-heavy Etsy/Stripe revenue model.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — sole issuer of Canadian banknotes, fiscal agent for the Exchange Fund Account, and operator of the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS, now Lynx). Formal exchange-control authority rests with the Minister of Finance / Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates Schedule I/II banks; FINTRAC administers AML/CTF reporting; Payments Canada governs retail clearing rails. Canada has had no exchange-control regime since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951, so no central-bank FX approval, allocation, or licensing mechanism applies to cross-border treasury flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for equity injection from a non-resident shareholder: instruct the remitting bank to send a SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pain.001) in CAD or USD with the narrative 'Share capital contribution — subscription of common shares of BC corporation — no loan' in Field 72, together with the beneficiary bank account exactly matching the corporate banking master. Use the receiving bank's transaction purpose code for non-resident direct investment equity injection — typically ISO 20022 purpose 'GDDS' (Equity purchase) or 'CAPC' where the bank's code table so provides. The receiving Schedule I bank will verify the beneficiary legal name, run FINTRAC EFT reporting for any amount at or above CAD 10,000, and may issue a non-resident direct investment declaration for Statistics Canada / Bank of Canada balance-of-payments data. If funds arrive in USD, either (i) maintain a USD operating account to preserve currency and defer conversion, or (ii) execute a standing conversion order at a recorded spot rate with an automatic sweep to CAD. Splitting a single capital contribution into multiple sub-CAD 10,000 wires to evade reporting is prohibited structuring. The corporation must update its share register and, where applicable, file a Notice of Alteration with the BC Registry for any change in directors or registered office within statutory timelines. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined Canadian federal (15%) + British Columbia general corporate income tax (12%); 11% combined on first CAD 500,000 of active business income for an eligible Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) under the federal and BC small business deduction rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to a non-resident individual or corporation. British Columbia does not levy a separate provincial dividend withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no statutory dollar-value threshold before transfer-pricing documentation is required; the documentation duty attaches to every non-arm’s-length cross-border transaction. For practical audit-risk management, a Local File should be maintained where aggregate intercompany transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000, and Form T106 requires reporting when reportable transactions with a single non-resident related party exceed CAD 50,000. Master File global allocation is recommended for large cross-border manufacturing, home-office allocation, and trademark royalty streams.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulation exists in BC. Economic substance is nevertheless reinforced through Canada’s General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245 of the Income Tax Act), PE attribution rules, anti-double-dip and anti-deferral rules, and CRA’s substance-over-form doctrine. An enamel-pin enterprise using a BC holding entity to receive treaty-protected dividends or royalties must demonstrate real business substance: permanent physical premises, employees or independent contractors performing material functions, active boards, operational decision-making, business risk, and meaningful local production or distribution activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statement

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; evading assessment after reassessment; criminal liability under section 239 of the Income Tax Act with fines up to CAD 200,000 on indictment and imprisonment for up to five years; significant prescribed-rate daily interest on unpaid amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be charged criminally as parties to the evasion, subjected to restitution orders for unpaid taxes, banned from cross-border travel through CRA enforcement warrants, and personally liable where the court pierces the corporate veil to recover hidden distributions.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Inadequate Documentation

Statutory sanction: CRA may redetermine income under section 247(2); subsection 247(3) imposes a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where documentation was not prepared or provided within specified timelines; the CRA may assess adjusted taxes, interest, and penalties without a cap.

Director personal liability: Directors are generally shielded from TP penalty unless they knowingly participated in the mispricing or used the corporate form as a sham; controlling shareholders who divert profits can face civil restitution, reversal of corporate distributions, and personal liability in veil-piercing cases.
Failure to Withhold / Unremitted Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: CRA may assess the full unwithheld amount under section 215 plus interest and penalties; knowing failure to remit is an offence under section 227/239 with fines, imprisonment up to two years (summary conviction) or five years (indictment), and CRA may use garnishments and bank freezes.

Director personal liability: Directors are strictly and personally liable for unremitted third-party withholdings under subsection 227.1(1) without the need to pierce the corporate veil; civil restitution follows the assessment and may be enforced against personal assets, travel bans may issue for persistent non-compliance, and directing mind liability may create exposure to imprisonment.
