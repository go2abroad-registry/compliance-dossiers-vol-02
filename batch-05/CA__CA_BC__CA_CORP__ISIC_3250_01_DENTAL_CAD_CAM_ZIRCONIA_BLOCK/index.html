Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act via BC Corporate Online or Corporations Canada (continuation, if federal); obtain the incorporation number. Step 2: Apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) through CRA Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or via the BC OneStop Business Registry; the BN is Canada's single tax identifier. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Worldwide_Resident_Global_Taxation_With_Foreign_Tax_Credit (a BC business corporation is a separate taxpayer; directors and shareholders are not pass-through participants; taxable Canadian corporation subject to Part I federal tax and BC provincial corporate income tax under the federal-provincial collection agreement; non-resident corporation with a permanent establishment in Canada is similarly taxable on Canadian-source business income with treaty relief; dividends paid to a non-resident parent are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory 25% rate, reduced to 5% under the Canada-US treaty where applicable).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime exists under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)). Available elections and integrity mechanisms: (i) s. 216 election to file a Part I return for a non-resident's net rental income in lieu of Part XIII gross-basis withholding; (ii) treaty-based reduced withholding via CRA Form NR301/NR302; (iii) s. 85 rollover election permitting transfer of property to a Canadian corporation on a tax-deferred agreed-value basis; (iv) s. 184 'ordinary' disposition and capital-dividend elections (s. 83(2)) for corporate tax-free distributions, where applicable; (v) hybrid mismatch integrity rules in ss. 18.4, 18.5 and 94 (mandatory, non-elective) denying double-deduction and deduction-without-inclusion outcomes for MNE groups; (vi) s. 256(4) associated-corporation allocation is an administrative statutory allocation, not an election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 gross revenue from taxable supplies in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act s. 240); the threshold applies only to GST/HST registration; BC PST registration is required from the first taxable sale with no small-supplier exemption for manufacturing, import, or export activities

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1) qualify for the 0% GST rate where the supplier is the exporter, the goods are delivered outside Canada 'as soon as practicable' and are not further processed in Canada; dental zirconia ceramic blocks and milling discs (HS 9021.29 dental fittings; alternatively HS 6909.19/6909.90) shipped directly to a non-resident buyer qualify; BC PST is also exempt on export under the Social Service Tax Act, R.S.B.C. 1996, c. 369, s. 74 where the seller ships the goods outside BC/Canada; eligible offshore services (e.g., product design, CAD/CAM engineering) supplied to a non-resident client with no Canadian use may also be zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, s. 9 if all conditions are met

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System CERS; paper G13 where ESA/AA provisions apply)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical delivery outside Canada to the named foreign purchaser
  • Commercial Invoice and sales contract identifying the non-resident buyer, Incoterms and export sale price
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) / SWIFT MT103 / telegraphic transfer (TT) advice demonstrating foreign-currency consideration received from outside Canada
  • Packing list, HS classification and, for shipments to the United States, a USMCA Certificate of Origin when claiming preferential duty treatment on re-imports

Canada operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing network (no federal fiscal stamping, no real-time clearance server, no CRA clearing house); mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE with a Web Access Code or Business Gateway EDI/API; all registrants must retain source documents, invoices, and electronic records per ETA s. 286; GST invoices must contain the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act Regulations (requisite information: supplier name/BN, recipient name, date, description, tax amount); BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC on a monthly/quarterly frequency assigned by the Consumer Taxation Branch; CRA may require XML/print-script invoices for ITC substantiation but no digital stamping or pre-clearance API is mandated; a federal e-invoicing framework was announced in Budget 2024, expected to build on the PEPPOL infrastructure on a voluntary basis

CRA GST/HST audits concentrate on input tax credit claims supported only by export sales lacking complete export documentation; if zero-rating is disallowed, the full 5% GST is assessed on the sale, and BC may apply 7% PST plus interest from the original invoice due date; CRA reassessment power generally extends four years under the Excise Tax Act; penalties are material: gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the net tax understatement (ETA s. 285), fines up to CAD 10,000 plus imprisonment for up to 5 years for willful evasion or false statements under ETA s. 280, and director/officer personal liability under ETA s. 323 for unpaid GST; where export consideration is not repatriated to Canada within 180 days (a prudent administrative benchmark, as Canada maintains no formal exchange-control statute), CRA may challenge the true consideration and recharacterize unpaid foreign receivables as an interest-free shareholder loan to the non-resident parent, crystallizing a deemed benefit under ITA ss. 15(2) and 80.4(2) at the end of the taxation year; such deemed benefits are subject to additional Part XIII withholding tax on distributed or adjusted amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely convertible G10 currency and Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; both current-account and capital-account transactions are liberalized with no exchange-control licensing, no repatriation quota, and no pre-departure approval for profit remittance. Profit repatriation in the form of dividends is subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5–15%, e.g., 0–15% under the Canada–US treaty depending on ownership), plus the corporate-law solvency test of section 48 of the British Columbia Business Corporations Act. No stabilization levy, surrender charge, or cross-border dividend restriction exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio apply. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds indefinitely in freely usable foreign-currency accounts maintained at a Schedule I bank or a Schedule III foreign bank branch in Canada; the statutory consolidation timeline is 0 days for conversion, with funds normally available for disbursement in 1–2 business days following receipt. Statistical and customs reporting obligations are triggered under the Customs Act (Form B13 export declaration) and Statistics Canada trade-return rules, but these are informational and do not constrain treasury liquidity.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; prudential banking supervision is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada maintains no dedicated retail foreign-exchange-control administration; the Bank of Canada governs monetary circulation and wholesale settlement, while FINTRAC enforces cross-border wire reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Conditionally viable as a secondary settlement rail only; offshore digital EMIs are not a compliant primary treasury platform for a British Columbia manufacturing corporation. Wise, Payoneer, Stripe, and comparable EMIs are treated as money services businesses under the PCMLTFA/FINTRAC registration regime and as payment service providers subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (effective 2024, supervised by the Bank of Canada). They cannot offer deposit-taking in CAD without a Canadian chartered-bank partner, do not carry CDIC deposit insurance, and their pooled settlement structures create legal and AML complications for high-value trade wires, LCs, and FX term instruments. Feasibility rating: LOW for primary liquidity and capital escrow; MEDIUM for receivables collections, low-ticket distributor payouts, and speed-to-market cross-border disbursements under CAD 10,000 per transaction.

Route the injection as a SWIFT MT103 through a Schedule I Canadian bank's correspondent network, settling in CAD over Payments Canada Lynx for domestic bank-side credit; the wire instruction should carry the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (equity injection) and a remittance information field citing the share-subscription and board-resolution reference. FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting applies at CAD 10,000 or more, so the onboarding file must contain the shareholder's bank reference, source-of-funds narrative, and certified corporate authorization. Canadian banks also require confirmation that the investment is a greenfield business establishment under the Investment Canada Act, with an ISED notification (not review) filed by the non-resident founder; funds may not originate from sanctions-listed jurisdictions or be routed through an unregulated third-party wallet.Domestic CAD disbursements settle via Payments Canada Lynx high-value wires (MT103-style structured DX messages) and via PAD/ACH rails for recurring payroll and Vancouver-region vendor payments. Cross-border USD trade payments should execute through the bank's New York clearing house, typically via SWIFT MT103 with 'SHARED' charges and a UETR correlation ID, clearing over CHIPS or Fedwire. For capital-intensive equipment and international dental-distributor settlements, use a commercial letter of credit issued under SWIFT MT700 and governed by UCP600, with Incoterms 2020 documents against payment at sight or up to 90-days usance, including a clean on-board bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and third-party inspection certificate to eliminate prepayment exposure.Deliverable USD/CAD vanilla forward contracts with tenors up to 24 months, documented under an ISDA / Canadian FX Master Agreement with a Schedule I bank and backed by a committed treasury credit line.Plain-vanilla OTC USD/CAD options (call or put) used strictly as budget-rate insurance for export receivables, with an evidentiary underlying commercial exposure.Cross-currency swaps (USD principal against CAD principal) permitted for balance-sheet redenomination of trade payables or ECU-financed equipment, subject to board approval and documented cash-flow exposure.CME Group listed USD/CAD futures and options for tenors up to 12 months, held in a segregated brokerage account and margined in accordance with OSFI permissible end-user rules.Non-deliverable forwards are not the standard instrument for USD/CAD because the CAD is fully deliverable; an NDF would only be permitted with express credit-committee approval and a documented deliverability-override rationale.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Conditionally viable as a secondary settlement rail only; offshore digital EMIs are not a compliant primary treasury platform for a British Columbia manufacturing corporation. Wise, Payoneer, Stripe, and comparable EMIs are treated as money services businesses under the PCMLTFA/FINTRAC registration regime and as payment service providers subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (effective 2024, supervised by the Bank of Canada). They cannot offer deposit-taking in CAD without a Canadian chartered-bank partner, do not carry CDIC deposit insurance, and their pooled settlement structures create legal and AML complications for high-value trade wires, LCs, and FX term instruments. Feasibility rating: LOW for primary liquidity and capital escrow; MEDIUM for receivables collections, low-ticket distributor payouts, and speed-to-market cross-border disbursements under CAD 10,000 per transaction.
