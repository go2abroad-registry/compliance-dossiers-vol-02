Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal corporate income tax and GST/HST administration; corporate registry via Corporations Canada or BC Corporate Registry (BCe-File); BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administered by BC Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch, eTaxBC portal). Key CRA portal: My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca.

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign entity must first be incorporated (or extra-provincially registered in BC under the Business Corporations Act, SBC 2002, c. 57) before obtaining a statutory tax identifier. The CRA Business Number (BN) is the single statutory TIN (9-digit base number plus program accounts: RC corporate income tax, RT GST/HST, RP payroll). Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada, or having Canadian-source taxable activities, register using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or online through BRO (accessible with proof of identity and incorporation documents). GST/HST account registration is mandatory under s.240(1) of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) for non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada (other than zero-rated exports) or those with a substantial presence; export-only entities may register voluntarily to claim input tax credits (ITCs). BC PST registration is handled separately through the eTaxBC portal; BC requires non-resident vendors selling taxable goods/software to register if they have a physical presence, solicitation, or delivery nexus in BC. No minimum capital requirement, but foreign corporations must file an annual extra-provincial report with the BC Registrar of Companies.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a BC-incorporated corporation is a Canadian resident for tax purposes (de jure) and is subject to Part I federal income tax under the Income Tax Act (ITA) on worldwide income at the entity level; no pass-through treatment. Non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source income (business income via permanent establishment, or Part XIII withholding on passive/qualifying income). The entity is eligible for the general corporate rate (15% federal) and, if it is a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) with active business income, the small business deduction (SBD) reduces the federal rate to 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (subject to BC provincial SBD allocation and the CAD 15 million/50 million business limit grind). BC provincial corporate tax: 12% general rate; 2% lower rate for CCPC active business income eligible for SBD. Territorial scope: BC/Canadian-source income taxed by Canada; foreign income taxable with foreign tax credits available. No Tax_Exempt_Zone status is available; the corporation is fully subject to federal and provincial taxation. — A foreign entity must first be incorporated (or extra-provincially registered in BC under the Business Corporations Act, SBC 2002, c. 57) before obtaining a statutory tax identifier. Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation — a BC-incorporated corporation is a Canadian resident for tax purposes (de jure) and is subject to Part I federal income tax under the Income Tax Act (ITA) on worldwide income at the entity level; no pass-through treatment. Non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source income (business income via permanent establishment, or Part XIII withholding on passive/qualifying income). The entity is eligible for the general corporate rate (15% federal) and, if it is a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) with active business income, the small business deduction (SBD) reduces the federal rate to 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (subject to BC provincial SBD allocation and the CAD 15 million/50 million business limit grind). BC provincial corporate tax: 12% general rate; 2% lower rate for CCPC active business income eligible for SBD. Territorial scope: BC/Canadian-source income taxed by Canada; foreign income taxable with foreign tax credits available. No Tax_Exempt_Zone status is available; the corporation is fully subject to federal and provincial taxation.

        
Available election forms
Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' regime; entity classification follows legal form and the ITA. Available elections relevant to a BC manufacturing corporation include: (i) s.85 ITA tax-deferred rollover election for transfers of eligible property to the corporation; (ii) s.256(1) associated-corporation election/allocation for SBD limits; (iii) s.184(3)/s.185 private corporation dividend election (for Part IV refundable tax); (iv) s.216(1) ITA election (non-resident investors in Canadian real property); (v) GST/HST election under s.156 ETA for eligible supplies between closely related registrants (deemed taxable supply threshold); (vi) GST/HST annual filing election and Quick Method accounting election (s.227 ETA); (vii) election to have a non-calendar fiscal period for GST/HST; (viii) foreign tax credit timing elections and treaty-based relief elections; (ix) hybrid mismatch rules under s.18.2/18.3 ITA (BEPS Action 2) disallow deduction mismatches unless an election/conformity adjustment applies; (x) for cross-border tax-transparent vehicles (e.g., US LLC), Canada applies its own 'fiscally transparent entity' analysis — CRA guidance and bilateral treaty tie-breaker rules must be used; no statutory blanket election exists.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
5%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CAD 30,000 — a person (including a non-resident) becomes a GST/HST registrant when annual worldwide taxable supplies (from a Canadian nexus, excluding zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; below this threshold the entity is a 'small supplier' and is not required to charge GST, but may voluntarily register to recover ITCs. BC PST: no parallel turnover threshold — registration is required before making any taxable retail sale in BC; out-of-province manufacturers with BC customers must collect and remit PST on the full selling price for taxable goods delivered into BC unless an exemption (e.g., manufacturing exemption certificate) applies.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exports of goods by a GST/HST registrant are zero-rated under s.23 ETA and Schedule VI, Part V (s.1) provided the exporter can demonstrate that the goods were delivered to a non-resident person and exported 'in a timely manner' — generally within 60 days of delivery. For manufactured titanium orthopedic implants that are not exported but supplied domestically, zero-rating may also apply as a 'medical or assistive device' under Schedule VI, Part II (item 15) if the goods are designed for human medical use (e.g., hip joint prosthetics), come with a prescription/medical authorization as applicable, and meet ETA conditions; however, the export route remains the primary basis for 0% GST. Offshore services (e.g., contract manufacturing services performed for a non-resident) are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 when supplied to a non-resident and not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. A refund (i.e., full ITC entitlement) arises where GST is charged in error or where the zero-rated export is not substantiated; the normal ITC recovery mechanism applies for embedded GST on inputs. PST export exemption applies upon removal from BC via the exporter's PST exemption certificate (FIN 521) or by providing a bill of lading for direct shipment; no PST is payable on goods for export.

Canada has NO real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing regime comparable to Latin American jurisdictions. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE or through approved EDI/API software) for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1 million; paper filing permitted below this threshold. Mandatory electronic T2 corporate income tax filing applies where gross revenue exceeds CAD 1 million. Imports into Canada are cleared through the CBSA (Canada Border Services Agency) using the Single Window Initiative (SWI) / Canada Border Services Agency Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) system; exporters must report exports electronically via the Canadian Export Reporting System (GFTD/CAED) or through the CBSA eManifest portal. For GST/HST, self-assessment on importation and ITC recovery is processed on the corporate GST/HST return; no tax invoice stamping is required. Canadian invoices must contain registrant's BN, but there is no central invoice clearance database.

      
CRA and CBSA audit export claims intensively; exporters must retain substantiation for six years from the end of the tax year (s.286 ITA; s.88 ETA). CRA has a four-year reassessment window (extendable to eight years or unlimited for misrepresentation/fraud) to reverse improperly claimed zero-rating, disallow ITCs, and assess net tax. Penalties under the ETA include: failure to file (s.280) — 1% plus ¼% per month, up to 12 months; false statements or omissions (s.285.1) — greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the amount; gross negligence penalty (s.285) — 50% of the understated net tax / overclaimed refund; and fines/interest on arrears (daily compound interest at prescribed rates). For export documentation failures, CBSA may impose administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) of CAD 100–CAD 500 per violation for non-compliance with the Reporting of Exported Goods Regulations (missing, false, or incomplete export declarations) and seize goods. Criminal prosecution is possible for fraudulent zero-rating or ITC fraud under s.327 ETA (fine up to CAD 25,000 and/or up to 2 years' imprisonment). Retroactive clawbacks: CRA may reassess the full GST that should have been charged (5% of the selling price) plus interest and penalties, and disallow ITCs attributable to the export if supporting documentation is incomplete. For PST, BC may assess unremitted PST (7%) plus 10% penalty and interest on out-of-province sales diverted to BC consumers. A robust export compliance dossier — including the inward remittance and customs filings — is mandatory to withstand CRA's risk-rule targeting high-value medical-device exporters.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Canada maintains full current account convertibility with no restrictions on profit remittances, dividends, or capital repatriation for resident corporations. Capital account transactions involving non-residents are subject to Foreign Investment Review Board (FIRB) review only for investments exceeding CAD 5 million in non-exempt sectors; medical device manufacturing qualifies for general exemption. Profit remittances are subject solely to standard 25% withholding tax on dividends to non-resident shareholders, with no additional FX surrender or prior approval requirements.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory surrender or forced conversion. Exporters must file a Canadian Customs Invoice with customs authorities and report transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 to the Bank of Canada via the Electronic Data Interchange Reporting (EDIR) system. Proceeds may be converted freely into foreign currency upon receipt without surrender obligations.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
27% combined federal and British Columbia statutory general corporate income tax rate, being 15% federal Part I rate plus 12% BC rate.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax imposed under ITA s.212(2) on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 — total fair market value of all reportable transactions with non-resident non-arm's-length persons triggers mandatory Form T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under CRA administrative policy. A separate consolidated group revenue threshold of CAD 750,000,000 triggers Country-by-Country Reporting; Canada does not statutorily prescribe 'Local File' or 'Master File,' but CRA adopts OECD TP Guidelines requiring equivalent documentation upon reassessment.

      
Economic substance scope: British Columbia is not an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) jurisdiction such as the UAE or BVI; however, Canada imposes an equally material substance standard through the judicial business-purpose test, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), and the OECD permanent-establishment threshold. Titanium orthopaedic implant manufacturing in BC must reflect substantive activity: a real assembly/3D-printing facility, qualified staff, board decisions in Canada, R&D records, and genuine economic risk, especially when claiming treaty benefits or intercompany IP royalties.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and False or Incomplete Corporate Filing
        
Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239(1), tax evasion may lead to summary conviction with a fine of 50%–200% of the evaded tax and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years. Reassessment periods are extended indefinitely for fraud or misrepresentation. Transfer-pricing penalties under ITA s.247(3) can reach 10% of net adjustments, plus gross-negligence penalties under s.163(2).

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally prosecuted as participants in tax fraud. Under ITA s.227.1 and s.323, directors are personally liable for unremitted source deductions, GST/HST and Part XIII withholdings. CRA may seek civil restitution, freeze personal assets, issue travel bans via collection orders, and courts can pierce the corporate veil in cases of fraudulent conduct.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Artificial Profit Repatriation Structure
        
Statutory sanction: CRA will apply GAAR (s.245) to disallow a transaction's tax benefits, impose Part XIII withholding on deemed dividends or deemed interest, levy transfer-pricing penalties of 10% of the gross adjustment, and apply a 25% penalty on aggressive planning positions. Courts may disregard the subsidiary's separate legal personality and re-tax the beneficial owner directly.

        
Director personal liability: Where a subsidiary is held to be a sham or agent of the foreign parent, the BC directors of the corporate vehicle may face direct liability for unpaid Canadian federal and BC tax. Directors who knowingly orchestrate circular financing or IP migration may be subject to civil restitution, asset-seizure proceedings, travel restrictions and director bans under the BC Business Corporations Act.

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / FINTRAC Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) imposes administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation; Criminal Code laundering offences carry imprisonment up to 10 years. The Canada Border Services Agency and FINTRAC can freeze corporate bank accounts, require compliance receiver-ship, and disqualify the company from federal contracts or hospital procurement.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers are separately accountable for AML compliance. If a director knowingly fails to establish FINTRAC controls, makes false STR conclusions, or directs transactions designed to circumvent reporting, they may face misdemeanour/felony liability, criminal fines, travel bans, and personal asset forfeiture. Serious cases can result in imprisonment of up to 10 years and loss of the right to act as a director of a BC-registered corporation.
