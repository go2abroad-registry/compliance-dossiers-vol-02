Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal corporate registry via Corporations Canada (ISED); British Columbia corporate and PST administration via BC Registry Services and BC Ministry of Finance; CRA online portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Canada Business Corporation federally (Corporations Canada online) or as a BC Business Corporation (BC Corporate Online) and obtain the incorporation number and certificate. Step 2: Apply to the CRA for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 or CRA Business Registration Online; CRA opens program accounts: RC (income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) and RM (import/export) as required. Step 3: Where the entity has BC nexus, register for BC Provincial Sales Tax (7%) and BC Employer Health Tax (payroll threshold) through BC eTax administered by the BC Ministry of Finance. Step 4: If the Canadian corporation is controlled by non-residents, file Form RC147 (Corporate Status Declaration) to establish non-CCPC status and applicable withholding/treaty rules. Step 5: For export operations, activate the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) credentials with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and link the BN's RM account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation | Territorial_Scope: a resident Canadian Business Corporation is taxed on worldwide income; the foreign-owned Canadian subsidiary is a private corporation but a non-Canadian-controlled private corporation (non-CCPC), ineligible for the small business deduction. Canadian-source business income of a non-resident corporation is subject to Part I tax plus a 25% Part XIV branch tax (treaty-reduced where applicable); no tax-exempt zones apply.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a check-the-box regime. Relevant elections and statutory mechanisms: (i) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident rental/royalty income to be taxed on a net Canadian basis; (ii) hybrid-mismatch restrictions in ITA s. 18.4 and s. 18.5 (effective July 1, 2022) that limit interest-deduction mismatches and require CRA-prescribed disclosures; (iii) CRA administrative entity-classification guidance for foreign entities (e.g., U.S. LLCs) claiming treaty benefits under the Canada-US LOB provisions; (iv) ITA s. 247 advance pricing arrangements and transfer-pricing documentation elections for intercompany export sales; and (v) CCA class elections for capital cost allowance schedules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CA$30,000 annual GST/HST small-supplier threshold based on total taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters; non-residents carrying on business in Canada are normally subject to the same threshold, though a non-resident soliciting orders in Canada may be required to register regardless. BC PST has no general numeric small-seller threshold; every vendor selling taxable goods or services to BC consumers must register, collect and remit 7% PST unless an exemption applies, with marketplace-facilitator rules applicable to remote and online sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI: (i) Part V s. 20 zero-rates tangible personal property (including the color Doppler ultrasound console) exported by the registrant as part of the supply, provided export occurs within a reasonable time and documentary proof of export is retained; (ii) Part V s. 7 zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident person, other than services relating to Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada; (iii) Part II medical device provisions may zero-rate or relieve qualifying hospital supplies of a licensed Health Canada medical device (SOR/98-282). For the export model, Part V zero-rating is the operative mechanism; GST-bearing input tax credits are claimed periodically to create net refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A filed electronically via CERS; mandatory for shipments valued over CA$2,000 and for controlled goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (carrier-issued evidence that the goods left Canada)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (supply terms, destination, export value)
  • Export Permit or Medical Device Establishment Licence (Health Canada / CBSA-controlled goods requirements, if applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank FX conversion receipt evidencing payment by the non-resident buyer; recommended evidence for CRA audit purposes though not separately mandated by the Excise Tax Act)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous API-based e-invoicing. The CRA accepts electronic invoices (XML/EDI/PDF) subject to six-year records retention under ETA s. 286. A voluntary pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on PEPPOL BIS 3.0 was launched for B2G transactions from 2024. For goods exports, CBSA requires electronic reporting via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) using the B13A export declaration for most shipments exceeding CA$2,000.

CRA may reassess and claw back zero-rating if goods or services did not in fact leave Canada or evidence is insufficient; the resulting assessment includes uncollected 5% GST, compound daily interest at prescribed rates, and, for goods diverted domestically, any applicable BC PST. The normal ETA reassessment window is four years (ETA s. 298) and is unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, neglect, or failure to file. Civil penalties: ETA s. 285.1 gross-negligence false statements (greater of CA$100 and 50% of the understated tax or overclaimed refund); ETA s. 280 failure-to-register (greater of CA$250 and 1% of taxable supply consideration); late or inaccurate returns attract penalties and interest under ETA ss. 280-282. Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST under ETA s. 323. CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalty System (AMPS) penalties may reach CA$25,000 per export reporting violation. Criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 or ITA s. 239 for evasion can impose fines of 50%-200% of evaded amounts and imprisonment. The 180-day figure aligns with ITA s. 17(2): an interest-free trade receivable from a non-arm's-length non-resident is deemed to generate prescribed-rate interest income unless collected within 180 days; no separate GST/HST export-proceeds repatriation deadline exists.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current- and capital-account convertibility. Canada repealed the Exchange Control Act in 1951 and accepted IMF Article VIII obligations in 1952; the CAD is freely floating. No government permit or central-bank approval is required for inward equity, intercompany loans, or dividend remittance from British Columbia. Practical frictions are documentary only: Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting for international transactions exceeding CAD 25,000 per quarter; FINTRAC large-transaction reporting over CAD 10,000; Part XIII withholding on dividends to non-residents (default 25%, reduced to 15% under the Canada-China DTA where the beneficial owner holds at least 25% of voting shares); and CBSA export declaration for goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more. Profit repatriation is otherwise unencumbered; no blocked-capital or remittance-permit regime applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion regime exists in Canada. The Export and Import Permits Act does not require exporters to convert foreign-currency proceeds, and no statutory repatriation timeline applies to CAD conversion. Export receipts may be held indefinitely in USD, EUR, or other currencies in a Schedule I operating account. Remaining obligations: (i) FINTRAC large-transaction reporting for any receipt or series of related receipts exceeding CAD 10,000 within 24 hours; (ii) CBSA export declaration in the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for shipments of CAD 2,000 or more; and (iii) accounting recognition at the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate. Colour Doppler ultrasound consoles classified under NAICS 334510 / ISIC 3250 are not on Canada's Export Control List and require no export permit unless a variant carries defence or dual-use technology; the exporter should nonetheless record exemption code NIL on each declaration.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada maintains no dedicated central-bank foreign-exchange control department; FX and monetary-movement supervision is administered jointly by Global Affairs Canada (sanctions and export permits), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC; PCMLTFA reporting), the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA; export reporting), and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI; prudential bank conduct).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderate feasibility. Canada confers no separate 'electronic money institution' licence; non-bank payment providers operating in or from Canada must register as money-services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Offshore EMIs (for example Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex) may execute foreign-currency payment runs and hold segregated client balances, but they cannot operate a Bank Act deposit-taking business or provide CDIC-insured CAD deposits. The Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime has transitional deadlines into late 2025 for payment service providers, so any selected EMI must evidence RPAA registration or FINTRAC MSB status before handling Canadian-dollar flows. Recommended treasury posture: keep statutory capital, escrow, and LC-drawing accounts with a Schedule I bank; use EMIs only for low-value disbursements and reference FX pricing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 annual aggregate of non-arm's length cross-border transactions with non-residents, triggering the obligation to file Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) and to prepare contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. CRA may require a Local File/Master File package for entities of multinational groups meeting the large-case audit threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. However, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 ITA denies tax benefits from transactions lacking genuine commercial substance, and treaty benefits require actual beneficial ownership and substantive economic activity. A British Columbia resident corporation used solely as a shell to obtain treaty advantages may have its arrangements recharacterized, and treaty relief can be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: CRA penalty of 50% of understated tax under section 163(2); upon conviction under section 239(1) ITA, a fine of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Criminal proceedings may include restitution orders and seizure of proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or participate in evasion may be prosecuted criminally and held personally liable; courts can impose fines, imprisonment, and travel restrictions such as passport surrender while charges are pending. Knowing involvement pierces the corporate veil, and the director may also be jointly liable for corporate tax liabilities under civil fraud principles.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may impose a transfer-pricing penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where reasonable documentation was not prepared or reasonable efforts were not made to determine arm's length prices. Form T106 late filing penalties reach CAD 100 per day up to CAD 25,000, plus unpaid tax interest and compounding arrears.

Director personal liability: Corporate directors are not automatically liable for TP penalties, but if the recharacterized transaction creates deemed Part XIII dividends or withholding tax, failure to withhold may trigger director liability under section 227.1 ITA, making directors personally liable for un-remitted amounts.
Withholding Tax on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable under section 215 ITA for the full un-withheld amount, plus interest at the prescribed rate and daily arrears penalties. Wilful failure to remit can escalate to criminal charges with fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for un-remitted Part XIII withholding tax under section 227.1 ITA, irrespective of corporate dissolution. If the directors evade the assessment, CRA may seek civil judgment and travel bans may follow where fraud is suspected.
Treaty Shopping / Shell Company / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR under section 245 ITA permits the CRA to deny the tax benefit, reassess on a recharacterized basis, and apply a penalty of 10% for large corporations in addition to accrued interest. Treaty benefits are denied for non-beneficial owners, and the entity may be deemed non-resident or ineffective for treaty purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in sham arrangements face personal civil liability for resulting assessments and penalties in cases of fraudulent transfers or misrepresentations. Criminal convictions may result in restitution, imprisonment, and travel restrictions attached to arrest warrants.
