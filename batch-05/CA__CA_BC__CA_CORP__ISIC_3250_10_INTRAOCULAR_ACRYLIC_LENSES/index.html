Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses.html) and My Business Account; customs and export controls: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the Canadian Integrated Import Declaration and Export Reporting; British Columbia PST: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (gov.bc.ca/pst).

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act; resident Canadian corporations pay federal corporate income tax at the general rate of 15% plus BC provincial corporate income tax of 12%, for a combined top statutory rate of 27%; foreign-controlled corporations are not eligible for the Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) small-business deduction (combined 11% rate) unless control and Canadian-resident ownership tests are met; Territorial_Scope: resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada are taxed on Canadian-source business income and, in addition, are subject to 25% Part XIV branch tax (reducible under tax treaty); Tax_Exempt_Zone: none — no Canadian tax-exempt zone applies to intraocular acrylic lens manufacturing; medical device manufacturing does not qualify for any general federal/provincial tax holiday.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a 'check-the-box' regime. Key statutory elections: (i) Income Tax Act s. 261 functional-currency election for corporate reporting in USD or another eligible currency; (ii) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident to report Canadian rental/royalty income on a net basis; (iii) ITA s. 219/Part XIV branch tax election filed with Form T2-NR and Schedule 29 for a non-resident corporation with a Canadian branch; (iv) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for supply of taxable goods/services between closely related corporations (GST/HST intercompany election); (v) ETA s. 150 divisional election for a single corporate unit; (vi) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3, 18.4 and Reg. 6808) mandate disallowance of deductions on hybrid and reverse-hybrid arrangements, with limited conformity elections for foreign branch losses and no general discretionary alignment election; US LLC treatment under the Canada-US Treaty follows the competent authority arrangement rather than a unilateral check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD in worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (GST/HST 'small supplier' threshold); a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must register once the threshold is exceeded, and may register voluntarily below it; BC PST has no small-supplier exemption comparable to GST — registration and collection are required once the non-resident carries on taxable retail activity in BC, with PST applying to most tangible personal property sold or leased in BC at 7%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported goods under Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act section 1 (0% GST) applies when the intraocular acrylic lenses are physically delivered outside Canada by the supplier or through a common carrier, and the supplier retains evidence satisfactory to the CRA that the goods were exported. To claim 0% GST, the exporter must: (i) hold documentary proof of export before reporting; (ii) be a GST/HST registrant to claim input tax credits on manufacturing inputs; (iii) maintain the goods' HS classification (e.g., HS 9021.39 as intraocular lenses / 9002.19 optical elements, subject to CBSA tariff ruling) and any Health Canada medical device licence or establishment licence file; and (iv) for services exported to non-residents, ensure the services are performed wholly or partly outside Canada or the benefit is realized exclusively outside Canada (Schedule VI Part V, s. 7). Although Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline, the CRA applies a 180-day commercial benchmark for inward remittance evidence; a deviation from the benchmark does not by itself invalidate zero-rating if other export evidence is satisfactory.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic ACI eManifest export reporting) for shipped goods valued over CAD 2,000, plus any government export permit or licence if required for medical devices
  • Commercial invoice and packing list stating product description, HS tariff classification, quantity, unit value, currency, and ultimate consignee details
  • Transport evidence: original Bill of Lading, air waybill, or courier receipt evidencing physical movement of goods across the Canadian border to a non-resident consignee
  • Inward remittance proof: Swift wire confirmation, bank inward remittance certificate/advice, Letter of Credit documents, and the non-resident purchaser's purchase order/contract, all reconciling to the export invoice and demonstrating receipt of proceeds in Canada

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, Continuous Transaction Control (CTC) mandate, or API-based digital stamping regime. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding $1,000,000 CAD must file returns electronically (NETFILE/EFILE/EDI) and remit electronically; GST/HST returns claiming zero-rated exports are filed through the CRA GST34 return with no invoice-level pre-clearance. The CRA has adopted a voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing standard based on Peppol PINT architecture, but B2B e-invoicing remains elective for intraocular lens exporters and their customers. Records, including export evidence, must be retained for six years.

CRA may reassess within four years of the return filing date (extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, negligence, wilful default, or fraud is shown), deny zero-rating, and claw back input tax credits if export evidence is missing or inadequate. Penalties include: ETA s. 285 false-statement/omission penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax (increased to 50% for gross negligence); ETA s. 280 late-filing penalties; interest at prescribed rates on assessed amounts; criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 for wilful tax evasion with significant fines and imprisonment; and additional civil monetary penalties for false customs declarations under the Customs Act. Exporters should retain all export documentation for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada imposes no foreign exchange controls. Both current account (trade, services, dividends) and capital account (equity injections, loans, M&A) transactions are fully convertible. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are unrestricted subject to corporate law solvency tests, tax compliance (Part XIII withholding), and FINTRAC reporting thresholds. No prior government approval for cross-border capital movements is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency accounts with licensed Canadian banks. FINTRAC reporting obligations apply for cross-border electronic transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, but no repatriation timeline is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acting as the Government's fiscal agent; Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange administrative authority. Foreign exchange administration is governed by the Financial Administration Act, the Currency Act, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for regulated financial institutions, with FINTRAC overseeing AML/CTF compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low immediate viability. Canada does not grant EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licenses for non-resident fintechs. Offshore digital EMIs must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, for BC operations, must comply with the British Columbia Money Services Businesses Act. High-risk medical device manufacturing involving intraocular lenses will require a fully regulated Canadian-chartered bank for escrowed capital and trade finance. EMIs can be used supplementary for migration of operating payments after core banking relationships are established.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Federal + 12% BC = 27% combined general corporate income tax rate; 13% Federal manufacturing/processing rate applicable to qualifying intraocular lens manufacturing, yielding 25% combined.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under ITA Part XIII (s.212(2)) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 information return threshold CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate non-arm's length transactions with non-residents in a taxation year. CRA contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (OECD-aligned, Local/Master File-equivalent under CRA TP Guide) must be prepared for related-party transactions; practical high-risk threshold: maintain for intercompany transactions exceeding CAD 250,000 to avoid s.163.2 penalty exposure.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no named ESR regime, but GAAR (ITA s.245) and OECD BEPS substance requirements apply; CRA will re-characterize artificial arrangements and deny DTA treaty benefits. The Health Canada Medical Device Establishment License (MDEL) and BC provincial licensing for intraocular lens manufacturing require physical site, Canadian responsible person, and active business operations, evidencing economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misstatement

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1): fine of 50%-200% of tax evaded and up to 2 years imprisonment; civil reassessment with interest and s.163(1) gross negligence penalty of 50% of additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or participate in tax evasion can be personally convicted; subject to imprisonment, criminal fines, civil restitution, and travel bans. CRA may issue proceeding certificates against property.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Gross Valuation Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty under s.163.2: 10% of the amount of the adjustment where adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 5,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross income; unreported T106 penalty CAD 100/day up to CAD 2,500.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for tortious fraudulent misrepresentation and, in deliberate false documentation, criminal liability; their personal knowledge of incorrect TP returns can pierce the corporate veil.
Unremitted Source Deductions / Director Liability

Statutory sanction: Section 227.1 director's liability certificate in the full amount unremitted plus interest; CRA can garnish bank accounts, attach land and assets; failure to remit adds 10% late-payment penalty and compound interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for 100% of the unremitted amounts if they failed to use due diligence; corporate veil pierced; personal property lien and seizure, bank garnishment, and 100% assessment; deficient director cannot plead corporate limited liability.
AML / Sanctions Abuses in Medical Device Payments

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act violations: up to CAD 2,000,000 fine for regulated entities; Criminal Code s.462.31 laundering offence imprisonment up to 5 years; FINTRAC administrative penalties and compliance orders.

Director personal liability: Officers who approve or structure sanctioned conduits risk criminal laundering convictions, personal fines, imprisonment, extradition/travel restrictions, and asset seizure beyond corporate veil.
Conduit Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR re-characterization of transactions at arm's length, loss and deduction denial, interest on reassessed tax; denial of treaty benefits under anti-abuse interpretation; CRA may levy penalty under s.163(1) for misrepresentations.

Director personal liability: If the shell is a sham, CRA may assess shareholders/directors under s.160(1) for asset transfers to the shell; veil-piercing allows personal liability for unpaid corporate tax; control persons may receive travel restrictions in cross-border tax probe.
