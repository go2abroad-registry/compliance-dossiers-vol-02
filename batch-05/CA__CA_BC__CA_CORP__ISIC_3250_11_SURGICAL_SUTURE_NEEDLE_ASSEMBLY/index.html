Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The BCBCA imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement; a company may be incorporated with nil paid-up capital and no statutory escrow account is required. Each subscriber must subscribe for at least one share; shares are issued without nominal or par value, and working capital may be introduced via shareholder loans or paid-up capital contributions recorded in the share subscription ledger.

Funding & unlock path: Pre-operational clearance requires: (1) building permit and structural/occupancy load inspection issued by the local municipal building department confirming the floor loading capacity of the assembly and sterilization rooms; (2) fire safety certification under the BC Fire Code from the local fire commissioner or fire prevention division, including suppression systems, chemical storage separation, and egress plan; (3) a WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety inspection and clearance letter covering machine guarding, ethylene-oxide gas monitoring, needle-handling injury controls, and emergency wash stations; and (4) final occupancy clearance from the municipal building inspector before production commences.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - federal tax and GST/HST administration; portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE. Provincial indirect tax administration: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (ePST self-service portal; gov.bc.ca/pst). Corporate/BN integration: Corporations Canada (Federal) or BC Registry Services (Provincial) issue the corporate number and trigger CRA Business Number (BN) issuance upon incorporation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or British Columbia Business Corporations Act (BCBCA); concurrently file the initial registered office address and first director list. Step 2: CRA automatically issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a corporate income tax account (RC prefix); a separate GST/HST account (RT suffix) is opened by filing Form RC1 or via My Business Account once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); a non-resident parent shipping into Canada must register if its Canadian taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or if CRA later compels registration under Excise Tax Act s. 241.1 for specially registered non-residents. Step 3: Register for BC PST through the ePST portal before making taxable retail sales in BC, unless the seller qualifies as a small seller (taxable sales of CAD 10,000 or less in the preceding 12 months). Step 4: Appoint an authorized representative for any non-resident owner under Form RC59 and file the applicable non-resident information returns if control is foreign; BC-incorporated companies are by law resident in Canada and therefore obtain BN directly through the integrated incorporation process without a separate non-resident registration gateway.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian corporations are per se taxable entities; no check-the-box or hybrid classification election exists for CBCA/BCBCA corporations. Available elections: (i) Excise Tax Act s. 150 election for nil consideration on supplies between closely related registrants for GST/HST; (ii) ETA s. 156 election to file GST/HST returns annually with accelerated instalments for large registrants; (iii) ETA s. 244 election for financial institutions (special attribution method is optional); (iv) Income Tax Act s. 216(4) election for non-resident owners of Canadian rental or timber property to file on a net-income basis; (v) ITA s. 17(8) election to treat related foreign receivables as loans with fixed repayment schedules to mitigate deemed-interest charges; (vi) ITA ss. 18.15 and 18.16 hybrid-mismatch rules operate automatically with no elective relief; (vii) transfer-pricing commensurate-with-income documentation can be filed under ITA s. 247 to resolve transfer-pricing adjustments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST small-supplier threshold: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (aggregated with associated persons) in the immediately preceding calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, with voluntary registration available below it. BC PST registration: required of every vendor making taxable sales in BC, except a small seller whose taxable sales in the immediately preceding 12 months are CAD 10,000 or less; once the threshold is exceeded, PST registration is compulsory. Export sales are excluded from the PST taxable-sales threshold computation but remain included in the GST small-supplier numerator.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: supplies of surgical sutures and needle assemblies are zero-rated under Excise Tax Act s. 16 and Schedule VI, Part V where the registrant transfers ownership of the goods to the purchaser and exports the goods as soon as is reasonable after delivery to a common carrier, or supplies them to a non-resident purchaser who is not a GST registrant and who arranges export; export must be evidenced by a bill of lading and CBSA export records. Services: services (including assembly, quality-control and inspection services) supplied to a non-resident entity are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 if the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST and the service is not performed on tangible personal property located in Canada. BC PST: an exported supply is exempt from PST only where the vendor ships or delivers the goods outside Canada on the vendor's or purchaser's behalf, or supplies goods to a foreign purchaser who holds a PST export exemption certificate (Form FIN 432); the vendor must retain proof of export within the applicable timeframe.

  • CBSA Automated Export Declaration (AER) / Canada Customs Coding Form B13A or eManifest export filing
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms (FOB/CIF/DAP), product HS classification, unit prices and destination country
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / Cargo Control Document evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • SWIFT MT103 inward remittance certificate or Letter of Credit settlement documentation evidencing FX collection
  • Forwarder or customs broker export instructions, container tracking and port-of-exit records
  • Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) and Export Certificates under Medical Devices Regulations ss. 55-58 for surgical suture shipments
  • GST/HST zero-rating proof file per CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17-4 (ETA s. 16 and s. 20 evidence)

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping exists for GST/HST or BC PST; the CRA operates a self-assessment returns-based system with EDI/XML filing via TELEFILE-GST and NETFILE. Canada is voluntarily adopting the Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard, with CRA launching validation services on an optional basis during 2024-2027; no mandatory API clearance is currently enforced. Regardless of the invoicing medium, all registrants must issue invoices satisfying Excise Tax Act s. 223: vendor BN, invoice date, GST/HST registration status, tax charged or zero-rated status, and foreign-currency reference; BC PST invoices must additionally show the PST registration number and retain supporting exemption certificates for exports.

Export zero-rating claims are scrutinized for both legal and physical export: if documentation does not prove export within a reasonable time, CRA and BC assessments claw back the 5% GST and 7% PST differential plus interest from the date the return was filed, within the normal 4-year reassessment window (unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud exists). Additional penalties: ETA s. 280 - false statements (CAD 7,500 plus 10% of unreported tax); ETA s. 285 - gross negligence penalty of 50% of net tax; ETA s. 327 - criminal evasion with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment; ITA s. 163(2) applies to income-tax filings. BC law: Provincial Sales Tax Act ss. 150-158 imposes a 10% penalty on unreported PST plus interest and director or partner liability under PST Act s. 149. Intra-group foreign receivables not collected within 180 days of maturity trigger deemed-interest and transfer-pricing consequences under ITA s. 17; the 180-day repatriation benchmark is accordingly embedded in this dossier as a financial transfer-pricing control.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized foreign exchange regime administered by the Bank of Canada, granting unrestricted convertibility for current account transactions including trade settlements, service payments, and profit remittances. Capital account transactions such as direct equity investments and portfolio flows are generally permitted without prior approval for standard corporate amounts, subject to notification thresholds and FINTRAC reporting. Profit remittance by Canadian-resident corporations is unrestricted following corporate tax compliance and beneficial ownership disclosure, with no capital controls on repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canadian exporters are not subject to mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds to authorized dealers. Funds may be retained in foreign currency accounts with licensed banks, provided all transactions are reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Repatriation to Canada is recommended within 90-180 days for tax residency and filing purposes, without statutory conversion ratios or timelines.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions are not directly accessible to Canadian corporations due to FINTRAC licensing mandates and provincial regulatory oversight. Cross-border liquidity must be channeled exclusively through correspondent banking relationships with licensed Schedule 1 Canadian banks.
