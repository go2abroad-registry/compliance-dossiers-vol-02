Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — GST/HST and corporate income tax registration administered through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (CRA MyBA); the British Columbia Ministry of Finance administers the Provincial Sales Tax (PST) through the eTaxBC registration portal.

E-government filing portal

A BC-incorporated business corporation is automatically assigned a 9-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) at incorporation via BC Corporate Registry / OneStop; to activate tax accounts the corporation files GST/HST registration (Form RC1 or secure BRO enrolment, adding an RT program account suffix) and BC PST registration (BC Registrant Application, Form VST.051, adding the BC account) before the first taxable sale; a foreign non-resident supplier without Canadian incorporation obtains a BN by filing CRA Form RC360 (Application for Non-Resident Registration) with a certified foreign certificate of incorporation, registered agent details, proof of Canadian permanent establishment or agent (Form RC362), and the foreign tax identification number, and files BC PST Form VST.051 through eTaxBC; statutory timelines require registration within 30 days of exceeding the applicable threshold and before invoicing for taxable supplies in Canada.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A BC-incorporated business corporation is automatically assigned a 9-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) at incorporation via BC Corporate Registry / OneStop; to activate tax accounts the corporation files GST/HST registration (Form RC1 or secure BRO enrolment, adding an RT program account suffix) and BC PST registration (BC Registrant Application, Form VST.051, adding the BC account) before the first taxable sale; a foreign non-resident supplier without Canadian incorporation obtains a BN by filing CRA Form RC360 (Application for Non-Resident Registration) with a certified foreign certificate of incorporation, registered agent details, proof of Canadian permanent establishment or agent (Form RC362), and the foreign tax identification number, and files BC PST Form VST.051 through eTaxBC; statutory timelines require registration within 30 days of exceeding the applicable threshold and before invoicing for taxable supplies in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box election; every corporation incorporated or continued under Canadian law is statutorily a taxable Canadian corporation (ITA s.89(1)) taxed at entity level, with no pass-through eligibility. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss.18.4 and 18.5, effective for transactions after 31 March 2019) are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions, not elective classifications. Available elections relevant here: (i) ITA s.216 election for non-resident taxpayers to file on a net rental/royalty basis; (ii) GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s.225.1) and streamed-simplification elections for qualifying businesses using the net tax calculation; (iii) ETA s.273 joint election for closely related corporations to use a single GST/HST return; (iv) voluntary BC PST registrant election for non-residents enabling recovery of PST paid on inputs; and (v) capital cost allowance elections embedded in the corporate return (ITA s.13(29) and s.1106).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST basic threshold: CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (small-supplier exception); non-residents must register once taxable supplies to Canada exceed the threshold or where they carry on business in Canada; BC PST: no small-supplier exemption for BC-based sellers, registration is mandatory from the first taxable retail sale; out-of-province and foreign remote sellers of specified digital goods or services must register when annual taxable sales into BC exceed CAD 10,000, and marketplace facilitators are statutory collecting agents with no threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Multilayer thermoformed dental clear aligner sheets (tangible personal property) qualify for zero-rated GST/HST treatment under paragraphs 1(a) and 1(b), Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act when: (i) the supplier sells the goods to a non-resident or to a resident purchasing for export outside Canada; (ii) the goods are delivered outside Canada via common carrier or the supplier's own conveyance in accordance with ETA Part V and CRA Memorandum 17-10-2; (iii) the goods are not acquired for consumption, use, or supply in Canada; and (iv) the supplier retains contemporaneous evidence of export (export declaration, bill of lading, carrier certificate) within the prescribed record period. Ancillary services such as export packing and overseas freight may be zero-rated under Part V ss.7 and 8 only if contracted with the non-resident customer and billed separately; the intermediate aligner sheets are not within the zero-rated medical device listings of Schedule VI Part I, thus the general export rules exclusively govern.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA electronic eManifest/export report for goods exceeding CAD 2,000 or controlled items)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill with foreign destination port/airport evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank FX settlement statement evidencing collected foreign-currency receivables
  • Canada Customs Commercial Invoice (exporter statement, zero-rated designation at 0%, HS classification 3921.90 or 3926.90)
  • Exporter's evidence-of-export log (CRA Form GST11) and carrier certificates retained for the six-year audit window

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API invoice-certification regime exists under GST/HST or BC PST. E-invoicing is voluntary in Canada, using the pan-Canadian Peppol BIS/UBL standard facilitated by certified service providers (not CRA-certified). Statutory invoice requirements under ETA s.169 and Regs 242 to 242.1 mandate the supplier GST/HST registration number (BN/RT account), invoice date, seller name, buyer name, total consideration, and tax status line showing 0% zero-rating or exemption; PST invoices must separately include the BC PST number and PST amount charged; records must be retained for at least six years per ETA s.286 and ITA s.230.

False or unsupported export zero-rating claims trigger CRA reassessment within the normal four-year period (unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud exists), disallowance of input tax credits, and restitution of the uncollected 5% GST with compound interest at the prescribed rate. Civil penalties under the Excise Tax Act include ETA s.280(1) (5% of the federal portion of tax evaded or attempted) and ETA s.285(1) (false statements: CAD 250 per occurrence to a maximum of CAD 7,500, or CAD 25,000 for a corporation), plus ETA s.280.1 gross negligence penalty equal to 5% of any misstated amount exceeding the correct tax; criminal prosecution under ETA s.328 and Income Tax Act s.239 carries imprisonment and fines up to 200% of the evaded amount. BC PST applies similar scrutiny for alleged diversion of exports into the domestic market, with penalties of 10% of unremitted PST plus arrears interest, director and officer liability, and potential audit enforcement against affiliated U.S. corporate parents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act and the Exchange Fund Act; no exchange controls, transaction limits, or prior-approval gates apply to foreign-currency conversion, and CAD and USD may be used freely for trade and treasury operations. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders require no Foreign Exchange Administration approval and are blocked only by the General anti-avoidance and dividend-solvency rules; the statutory burden is a 25% Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, generally reduced (e.g., 5% on substantial voting-stock dividends and 15% on portfolio dividends) under the Canada-United States Tax Convention and other bilateral treaties. Dividend payments must be supported by a directors' resolution, issued against positive retained earnings, and reported and remitted to the Canada Revenue Agency on Form NR4.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender, repatriation, or conversion obligation on export receipts. A British Columbia clear-aligner producer may freely retain USD or other convertible foreign currency in offshore or domestic foreign-currency accounts. Export proceeds are taxable on a Canadian-dollar translated basis under the Income Tax Act, with elective functional-currency reporting under section 261 available for qualifying Quebec, BC or federal corporations; foreign exchange gains and losses are recognized for tax purposes. Export compliance requires a Canada Border Services Agency B13 export declaration for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000, zero-rated GST/HST treatment of exported goods under Part V.1 of the Excise Tax Act, and conformity with the Health Canada Medical Devices Regulations for commercial distribution of the aligner products.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), constituted under the Bank of Canada Act, is the monetary authority and operates the national large-value payment and settlement systems, including Lynx. Prudential supervision of clearing banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); registration and reporting requirements for foreign-exchange dealing, money services businesses and payment service providers are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Canada maintains no separate exchange-control regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary rail purposes only. Canada does not license electronic money institutions (EMIs) as a standalone banking category; non-bank payment providers must register as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC and, when performing retail payment functions, as payment service providers with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act effective 2024. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled in the EU or UK cannot serve as the primary depository or clearing institution in Canada because they lack a federal bank charter, but they may operate as supplementary conversion and payout rails provided treasury funds are ultimately swept to a Tier-1 Canadian operating account for regulatory reporting and trade settlement.

Annual meeting records or unanimous members’ consent resolution in lieu of annual meeting (corporate record, not a registry filing) AGM or unanimous consent resolution must occur no later than 15 months after the previous AGM; intended for annual director and financial statement approval. $0 USD No registry fee applies; shareholder may apply to court to compel a meeting, and chronic failure to document annual meetings supports a finding of director mismanagement or involuntary dissolution proceedings.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined 27% (Federal general rate 15% + British Columbia general rate 12%). For a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), the combined rate is 11% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (Federal 9% + BC 2%). A BC subsidiary controlled by a US parent is a non-CCPC and pays the 27% combined general rate on all taxable income.
