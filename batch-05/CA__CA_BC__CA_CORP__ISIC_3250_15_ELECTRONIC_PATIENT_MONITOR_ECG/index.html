Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — My Business Account online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export reporting; British Columbia Ministry of Finance — Consumer Taxation Branch (eTaxBC portal, https://etax.gov.bc.ca) for Provincial Sales Tax (PST).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the business corporation (federal or British Columbia) and obtain the certificate of incorporation. 2) Obtain a single Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account (RT suffix) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; non-resident corporations must file RC1 and may appoint a Canadian representative. 3) Register the Import/Export (RM) program account with CBSA for customs export reporting. 4) Register for the BC PST vendor number via eTaxBC before the first taxable sale in BC; no turnover threshold applies to PST registration. 5) File the T2 corporate income tax return within six months after fiscal year-end and remit any balance due within two months (or three months for certain Canadian-controlled private corporations), subject to prescribed instalment and interest rules.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — 1) Incorporate the business corporation (federal or British Columbia) and obtain the certificate of incorporation. 2) Obtain a single Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account (RT suffix) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; non-resident corporations must file RC1 and may appoint a Canadian representative. 3) Register the Import/Export (RM) program account with CBSA for customs export reporting. 4) Register for the BC PST vendor number via eTaxBC before the first taxable sale in BC; no turnover threshold applies to PST registration. 5) File the T2 corporate income tax return within six months after fiscal year-end and remit any balance due within two months (or three months for certain Canadian-controlled private corporations), subject to prescribed instalment and interest rules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. A Business Corporation is per se taxed at entity level; profits distributed to non-resident shareholders are additionally subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reducible). Available elections include: Income Tax Act s.83(2) capital dividend election (Form T2054); ITA s.216 election for non-residents to file a Canadian return on net rental/farm income; Excise Tax Act s.156 closely-related-corporation election (Form GST25) to disregard GST/HST on inter-corporate supplies; ETA s.159 joint election for supplies of a business as a going concern. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed compulsorily under ITA ss.18.2–18.4 and s.247 (structured and dual-inclusion mismatches), not through elective classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST small-supplier threshold measured on worldwide taxable supplies including exports; registration is compulsory once exceeded, and voluntary registration is available to exporters for nil-rated supplies; BC PST vendor registration is mandatory from the first taxable sale in BC with no monetary threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods (e.g., multi-parameter bedside telemetry and ECG patient monitors, NAICS 334510 / ISIC 3250 / HS 9018.10–9018.19) are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, ss.1–2 of the Excise Tax Act where the registrant ships or delivers the goods to an export point as soon as is commercially feasible and retains the prescribed documentary proof of export within the time limits set by the Act and the GST/HST Regulations. Services performed in respect of exported goods, and offshore services supplied to non-residents (ETA Sch. VI Part V s.23), qualify for 0% GST provided the non-resident is not registered under the ETA, no person in Canada is the primary beneficiary, and the services do not relate to Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada at the time of supply. BC PST similarly applies a 0% rate to tangible personal property permanently exported on production of export evidence. Domestic zero-rating for licensed medical devices under Schedule VI Part II (requiring the Health Canada device licence number on the invoice) is a separate regime and must not be conflated with export zero-rating.

  • CBSA B13A Canada Customs Export Declaration / electronic export report filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System
  • Bill of Lading / Sea or Air Waybill naming the Canadian exporter and the non-resident purchaser
  • Commercial invoice (bilingual EN/FR) with HS classification (e.g., 9018) and stated export sale terms
  • Packing list and foreign destination manifest
  • Carrier certificate or freight forwarder proof of export evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank foreign-exchange receipt evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency payment

No continuous transaction control (CTC), real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada as of the applicable pipeline year. CRA requires registered persons to issue and retain prescribed GST/HST tax invoices and records (ETA s.223) and mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE, EDI/XML API) for virtually all registrants. CBSA export declarations are filed electronically under the Canadian Export Reporting System (B13A). Pan-Canadian e-invoicing interoperability is voluntary; BC PST returns and remittances are processed through eTaxBC, with digital records subject to CRA audit standards.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period (ETA s.298), extended indefinitely where fraud or misrepresentation is shown. Civil penalties: ETA s.280 — 25% supplementary penalty for false statements or omissions (minimum CAD 250); ETA s.285.1 — 50% gross-negligence penalty; ETA s.327 — evasion offence punishable by a fine of not less than 100% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to five years. On reversal of export zero-rating, CRA retroactively disallows input tax credits attributable to the exported goods, reassesses GST/HST on the supply with compounding interest under ETA s.280.1, and adds the applicable civil or criminal penalties. The BC Ministry of Finance administers parallel PST interest, late-filing penalties, and audit assessments where export exemptions are improperly claimed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has a fully floating Canadian dollar and observes Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Capital and current account transactions are freely convertible; there are no exchange controls restricting the conversion, transfer, or repatriation of funds into or out of Canada. Profit remittances through dividends, interest, royalties, or branch remittances are permitted without foreign-exchange authorization, subject only to Canadian corporate solvency tests, the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) requirements, and applicable non-resident withholding tax (generally Part XIII tax on dividends, interest, rents, and royalties).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export proceeds. Exporters may retain receivables in foreign currency and repatriate them at their own commercial discretion, subject only to standard tax reporting, customs declarations, and voluntary statistical surveys (Statistics Canada export reporting).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank and monetary authority; prudential supervision of banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada maintains no specialized exchange-control administration, and foreign exchange policy is coordinated between the Department of Finance Canada and the Bank of Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for treasury facilitation, Low for primary statutory capital custody. Non-bank electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Brex, or UK/EU-licensed fintechs may provide cross-border payment rails, multi-currency disbursements, and virtual IBANs; however, they are not authorized Canadian Schedule I banks and cannot accept deposits under the Bank Act. Any EMI operating in Canada must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and, in British Columbia, satisfy any applicable provincial registration. For capital escrow and statutory corporate accounts, a Canadian bank-based relationship remains the sole fully compliant primary custody solution.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million in aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions triggers the contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation requirement under subsection 247(4) of the Income Tax Act; CRA also expects an OECD-aligned Master File and Local File for substantial multi-year files; Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 1 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada is not a jurisdiction with standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under subsection 245(2) of the Income Tax Act and common-law substance-over-form principles. The BC medical-device manufacturing corporation must maintain actual production plant, calibrated equipment, technical staff, board decisions, and commercial risk in British Columbia; otherwise, the entity may be recharacterized as a conduit/shell and treaty benefits or DEAs may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239(1) – on summary conviction, a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment for up to 5 years; on indictment, penalties are greater. Civil penalty under subsection 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax plus daily compound interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate in the evasion may be personally convicted; the corporate veil may be pierced for fraudulent conduct, exposing personal assets, CRA travel restraint certificates, restitution orders, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Penalty

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act subsection 247(3) imposes a transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, not less than CAD 1,000; additional gross-negligence penalties under subsection 163(2) may apply where the taxpayer knowingly made false statements or omitted relevant facts.

Director personal liability: No direct statutory TP penalty applies to directors as individuals, but directors who sign false T2 returns or authorize intentionally non-arm's-length transactions can be jointly liable for fraudulent preference under corporate law and criminally exposed under section 239 for abetting tax evasion.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping / GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR under subsection 245(2) denies the tax benefit and reassesses the tax as if the avoidance transaction had not occurred; Part XIII non-resident withholding tax is applied at 25% where treaty reduction is denied; a non-resident conduit may be deemed to be carrying on business in Canada and become subject to Part XIV branch tax at 25%; taxpayer penalties apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement a sham treaty-shopping structure may be held personally liable for amounts paid as dividends or royalties under fraudulent conveyance laws; travel bans may be sought where the directors attempt to leave Canada pending criminal or tax-collection proceedings.
Regulatory & AML Red Flags for Medical Device Manufacturers

Statutory sanction: For selling a medical device without a licence, court fines can reach CAD 5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years on indictment under the Food and Drugs Act and Medical Devices Regulations; FINTRAC administrative penalties under the PCMLTFA may reach CAD 500,000 for reporting failures; CRA can assess the 50% gross-negligence penalty and forfeiture of amounts found to be unreported revenue.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally prosecuted for failing to exercise due diligence to prevent the unlicensed sale or AML violation; if the court finds that the corporation was a façade, directors’ personal assets may be subject to seizure, travel bans, asset-freezing orders, and imprisonment for up to 2 years.
