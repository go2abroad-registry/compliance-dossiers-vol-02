Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - national tax and GST/HST authority; portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business.html (Business Registration Online 'BRO' and My Business Account). Provincial indirect tax: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Provincial Sales Tax Act (PST) portal: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst. Customs and export clearance: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) CARM portal: https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca.

E-government filing portal

A Business Corporation (federal CBCA or BC Business Corporations Act) must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Form RC1 or online via BRO. Procedure: (1) incorporate and file articles; (2) apply for the BN and select RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) and RM (import/export) program accounts; CRA normally issues the BN immediately for online registrations and a corporate tax account is auto-created from incorporation data; (3) for a foreign-owned Canadian corporation, disclose the ultimate non-resident parent, jurisdiction and shareholding percentage; (4) if operations are run from outside Canada, a non-resident GST/HST registration can be made using Form RC1A with a special NR program account; (5) open the CBSA export account and post CARM security (surety bond or cash deposit) to clear bulk vessel exports; no minimum capital or CRA fees are required for BN issuance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation is deemed resident in Canada under ITA subsection 250(4) (incorporation in Canada) and is separately taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income; it cannot elect pass-through or partnership classification for Canadian tax purposes. Territorial scope: worldwide income with foreign tax credits; combined federal and BC Part I rate approximately 27% (15% federal general rate plus 12% BC general rate). For GST/HST, the corporation is an entity eligible to register and, if it makes zero-rated export supplies, can recover all input tax credits. — A Business Corporation (federal CBCA or BC Business Corporations Act) must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Form RC1 or online via BRO. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation is deemed resident in Canada under ITA subsection 250(4) (incorporation in Canada) and is separately taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income; it cannot elect pass-through or partnership classification for Canadian tax purposes. Territorial scope: worldwide income with foreign tax credits; combined federal and BC Part I rate approximately 27% (15% federal general rate plus 12% BC general rate). For GST/HST, the corporation is an entity eligible to register and, if it makes zero-rated export supplies, can recover all input tax credits.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' election exists under the Income Tax Act. Available elections relevant to a sugar export trading entity: (1) GST/HST group registration election under ETA s. 240(1)(c) to treat related Canadian corporations as a single registrant; (2) closely related corporation election under ETA s. 156 to disregard GST/HST on intercompany supplies at nil consideration; (3) quick-method election under ETA s. 227(1) for registrants with annual taxable supplies up to CAD 400,000, generally inadvisable for export traders because it caps input tax credit recovery; (4) ITA s. 216(4) election is not applicable to sugar sales but is listed for completeness as the main non-resident rental income election; (5) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s. 18.4 apply unilaterally to deny or defer deductions on hybrid payments where a foreign jurisdiction grants a mismatch, and foreign check-the-box classifications of the Canadian entity do not alter Canadian entity-level taxation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with unrestricted capital account convertibility and free current account transactions. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without prior approval, subject only to standard corporate tax filings and FINTRAC anti-money laundering reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds required under Canadian law. Foreign currency export receipts may be converted freely on the spot market. All export transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC within ten business days using prescribed forms.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
