Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Federal tax registration and GST/HST administration; online portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) and, if required, a GST/HST registration account. The standard procedure is to file the RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) with the CRA by mail, fax, or online through the CRA's Business Registration Online tool. Supporting corporate documents (certificate of incorporation, foreign registration evidence, and information on the Canadian place of business) may be requested. The BN is a 9-digit federal tax identifier, to which program accounts such as the GST/HST account (RT) are attached. For non-resident registrants, a Canadian representative or agent may be required for service of process; a simplified registration form (RC1, RC2 in some circumstances) applies. CRA registration is generally effective immediately, but a registrant must maintain records in English or French.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a 'check-the-box' classification system comparable to the U.S. or certain other jurisdictions. A Business Corporation incorporated federally or under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act is a separate taxpayer subject to entity-level taxation under the Income Tax Act (Canada). Relevant statutory election mechanisms for a corporation include: (i) election as to fiscal year-end under subsection 249.1(1) (restricted to certain farming or fishing corporations or specified corporations); (ii) associated corporation elections under subsection 256(2) for allocation of the small business deduction; (iii) non-resident corporation elections under section 216 for net-basis tax on rental income from Canadian real property or timber rights; and (iv) certain hybrid mismatch elections under the Income Tax Act in conformity with the Multilateral Instrument and BEPS Action 2, where applicable. No election is available to convert an entity-level taxed business corporation into a transparent/partnership entity for Canadian federal income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The mandatory federal GST/HST registration threshold is CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (other than zero-rated supplies, supplies of financial services, and certain other exempt supplies) over a trailing four-quarter calendar period. A non-resident that carries on a commercial activity in Canada and is not a small supplier must register; non-residents making zero-rated export supplies may voluntarily register to recover input tax credits. The threshold is applied on a gross revenue basis and is exclusive of the GST/HST itself.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, the supply by a GST/HST registrant of tangible personal property (including dried goji berries and herbal botanicals) is zero-rated when the supplier exports the property 'as soon as reasonable' after delivery to a destination outside Canada, or transfers ownership to a non-resident and exports under the conditions set out in section 1 (Part V). The seller must be a registrant, must maintain contemporaneous evidence that the goods were shipped/exported from Canada, and must have a commercial invoice, purchase order, or contract documenting the sale to a non-resident customer. Services supplied to non-residents that are directly linked to exported goods may also qualify for zero rating only if the services are performed in Canada and are supplied to a non-resident who is not registered under the ETA. The burden of substantiating the export rests on the supplier.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Reporting System / Form B13A or equivalent electronic export report via CERS)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Ocean or Air Carrier Transport Document evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Commercial Invoice (sales contract, purchase order, and payment terms)
  • Proof of Payment / Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire confirmation showing foreign currency transfer from the non-resident buyer

Canada does not currently mandate a national real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime. Electronic invoices are legally permissible if they are legible, intelligible, and retained in an accessible format. CRA does not pre-clear or fiscally stamp invoices. Registrants may use CRA's GST/HST NETFILE / My Payment portals for returns and remittances; EDI or API-based bulk transmission is available only for specified prescribed information returns and customs reporting, not for invoice clearance. However, exporters must submit electronic export data through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for controlled, regulated, or goods valued above CAD 2,000. Retaining digital records of invoices, bills of lading, and customs declarations is mandatory under the Income Tax Act and ETA.

CRA may reassess GST/HST returns for up to four years from the date of assessment, with an extended limitation period where a misrepresentation was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence. Claiming zero-rating on exports without valid export documentation subjects the exporter to reversal of zero-rating, reassessment of tax, interest, and penalties. The Excise Tax Act imposes a penalty of 5% of the unpaid tax plus an additional 1% per full month of arrears (not exceeding 12 months) for failure to remit; a false statement or omission penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax (or overstated refund/ITC) is applicable. For dishonest or fraudulent behavior, criminal prosecution and fines up to CAD 500,000 and/or imprisonment are possible. CRA also exercises a normal audit scrutiny of cross-border shipments, requiring the exporter to produce the documentary bundle above, and any export documentation discrepancies may trigger full clawback of input tax credits previously claimed on the exported goods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Residents and non-residents may freely convert, transfer and repatriate funds related to trade, investment and financial operations. Repatriation of corporate earnings is permitted, but outbound dividends, interest, royalties and certain management fees are subject to non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (typically 25% without treaty relief, reducible under the Canada-US treaty or other applicable tax treaties). Non-resident sellers of taxable Canadian property are subject to the Part XIII / section 116 clearance procedure to obtain a certificate of compliance or an amount withheld from gross proceeds. No exchange-rate rationing, allocation requirements or prior import/export licensing of foreign exchange exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios or repatriation timelines applicable to export receipts under Canadian federal or British Columbia law. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency and deployed directly to settle offshore obligations. However, the commercial invoice must reflect the transaction in Canadian dollars or in the agreed foreign currency, and income recognition for Canadian tax purposes must be computed in Canadian dollars based on the prevailing Bank of Canada exchange rate conversion. Exporters are expected to retain underlying export documentation for audit and banking compliance purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential and institutional supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering and terrorist financing supervision is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); there is no separate foreign-exchange control administrative body because Canada operates under a freely floating exchange-rate regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for an operational corporate account at an offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) unless the EMI is separately licensed as a Canadian money-services business and registered with FINTRAC. An offshore EMI that is not a chartered bank, authorized Canadian foreign bank branch, or registered MSB cannot hold deposits in Canada and cannot provide payment clearing through the Canadian banking system. Use of such an EMI would create settlement risk, KYC deficiencies and potential violations of the Bank Act and Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. The preferred structure is a domestic operational account at a Tier 1 bank plus an optional separate FINTRAC-registered payment-service provider for client collection only.

Defense evidence: Executed purchase orders, supplier invoices, warehouse receipts, phytosanitary or organic certification, insurance documents, logistics contract and a projected 12-month cash-flow/working-capital statement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions are executed via SWIFT MT103 wires denominated in CAD or USD to the BC corporate's Tier 1 operational or capital escrow account. The wire remittance information should state 'equity contribution / paid-in capital for working capital and trade settlement' and include the company legal name, address, financial institution account number and institution number (5 digits) plus branch/transit number for Canada (e.g., XYZ Bank, transit XXXXX, account XXXXXX). The inbound wire should be accompanied by a board resolution approving the capital call, a share subscription agreement where applicable, and proof of the source-of-funds in the remitting bank. No exchange-control approval is needed; however, the receiving bank will complete a FINTRAC large transaction report (for cash transactions over CAD 10,000) and may require an updated source-of-funds questionnaire. The remittance purpose code as used by correspondent banks should be classified as 'capital transfer (5100 series)' or equivalent, and supporting documents must be provided to clear correspondent account verification.Trade payments for procurement and logistics may be cleared through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers, documentary letters of credit (LC), and documentary collection under INCOTERMs. For LC-backed shipments, the BC exporter should open an irrevocable, confirmed LC with a Canadian Tier 1 issuing bank and instruct the advising bank via standard SWIFT MT700 messages. Outbound freight, insurance and customs-bond payments may be made by wire or corporate commercial card. All outbound disbursement instructions should include Canada's standard clearing identifiers: institution number, transit number, account number and SWIFT/BIC. For cross-border payments to suppliers, each MT103 should carry the invoice number, shipment reference, INCOTERM, product classification (HS heading for dried goji berries / herbal botanicals) and full supporting documents to satisfy the receiving bank's AML and sanctions screening.
