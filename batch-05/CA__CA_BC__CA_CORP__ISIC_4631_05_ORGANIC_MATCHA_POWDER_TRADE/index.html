Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA)/Agence du revenu du Canada - federal GST/HST and Part I corporate income tax; BC Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC) - British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) and BC provincial corporate income tax collected by CRA under the Provincial Tax Collection Agreement. E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - Canadian Business Corporation taxed under ITA Part I (s.2) on worldwide income; no pass-through or elective flow-through exists. A CCPC (not controlled by non-residents) qualifies for the small-business deduction (federal 9% on first CAD 500,000; BC 2%; combined 11%); non-CCPC status (typically where foreign shareholders control) triggers general combined rates (federal 15% + BC 12% = 27%). Canada is a worldwide-residence regime, not territorial; export matcha-trading profits are taxed on the exporter before treaty relief applies in the buyer's jurisdiction.

Available election forms

No IRC 7701 check-the-box or equivalent exists in Canada; entity-level taxation is mandatory. Applicable mechanisms: (1) ETA s.156 election - closely associated Canadian corporations may elect to treat inter-corporate supplies as made for nil consideration for GST/HST (useful between procurement and distribution affiliates); (2) ITA ss.18.2/18.3 and 92.1 hybrid-mismatch rules - mandatory deduction denial where cross-border payments exploit characterization differences (BEPS Action 2), no elective opt-out; (3) ITA s.125(3) - associated-CCPC agreement allocating the CAD 500,000 small-business limit; (4) ITA s.216 election for non-resident net-basis rental income (not relevant to matcha trading); (5) zero-rating is evidenced, not elected, under ETA s.22 and Schedule VI Part V; no export processing zone or tax-exempt-zone income-tax designation applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies - worldwide (including zero-rated export supplies for threshold testing, excluding certain financial services) over any single calendar quarter or four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s.123(1) 'small supplier'; ETA ss.240/241). Registration is compulsory once the threshold is exceeded and may be voluntary below it; for a pure export trader, voluntary registration is tax-positive because it unlocks ITC recovery on matcha procurement and logistics inputs. BC PST registration carries no threshold and is required from the first BC taxable sale.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of matcha powder exports is claimed under ETA ss.16 and 22, Schedule VI Part V items 1 and 20. Conditions: (a) the supply is a sale of tangible matcha inventory (HS 0902.10/0902.20) by the Canadian corporation; (b) the goods leave Canada 'as part of the supply' - export must be completed within 60 days of the time of supply (ETA s.22 and CRA GST/HST Memorandum Series); (c) the exporter retains contemporaneous evidence of export (export declaration, transport documents, delivery to the non-resident's foreign location); (d) the purchaser is a non-resident and no taxable supply is made in Canada; (e) the matcha is not consumed or further supplied in Canada. Freight and logistics services directly arranged for the offshore buyer are separately zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V when the 'eighty-per-cent rule' is met. If documentation fails, the supply is re-characterized as taxable at 5% GST and correlating input tax credits on procurement are lost.

  • Customs Export Declaration - B13A / Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) electronic acknowledgment for exit from Canada
  • Bill of Lading - original ocean/air waybill evidencing carriage from Canada to the offshore destination and the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank credit advice or SWIFT MT103 confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing organic matcha powder, quantity, value, HS classification, and export destination
  • Cargo Control Document or carrier manifest and border exit crossing record
  • Procurement audit trail - producer/supplier invoices, pre-export purchase orders, and lot-level traceability for the exported matcha
  • Related-party documentation - CRS/FATCA self-certification and transfer-pricing support for the offshore buyer and any foreign parent

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital tax stamp, and no statutory API e-invoicing clearance for private-sector B2B exports. Obligations: (1) GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (NETFILE) by registrants with annual revenue above CAD 1 million; (2) CBSA requires electronic export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) for goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more; (3) the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing network is mandatory for federal-government suppliers and is the emerging B2B standard; (4) large GST/HST filers (revenue above CAD 2 million) must use EDI/API-enabled returns; (5) BC PST returns are filed via eTaxBC.

CRA verifies export zero-rating on audit using the B13A/CAED record, transport documents, and the inward-remittance trail. Where evidence is absent or the 60-day export window is missed, CRA reassesses the supply as taxable at 5% GST, reverses input tax credits on the matcha procurement, and assesses arrears interest and penalties. Penalty exposure: ETA s.280 (false statement or gross negligence - greater of CAD 250 and 50% of net tax sought to be evaded); ETA s.285 (failure to comply with a demand - CAD 250 per day, capped at CAD 10,000); ITA s.163(2) (50% of the income-tax shortfall for gross negligence); ITA s.239 and Criminal Code s.380 (tax evasion - fine and imprisonment up to five years). BC PST audit penalties apply for mischarged taxable supplies, with a four-year normal reassessment period, unlimited on misrepresentation or fraud. The 180-day FX-repatriation benchmark governs prudent export-credit and banking compliance; CRA itself imposes no fixed statutory repatriation window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada operates a free-floating exchange-rate regime with no exchange controls, no restrictions on the purchase or sale of foreign currency, and no prior regulatory approval for profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment, or trade settlement. Non-resident-owned BC corporations may repatriate funds freely; however, they remain subject to CRA reporting rules, the Income Tax Act transfer-pricing arm's-length standard, FATCA/CRS self-certification, FINTRAC reporting obligations for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and the anti-money-laundering regime of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. For incoming foreign investment, the Investment Canada Act requires a simple post-establishment notification for a new greenfield Canadian business; the asset-threshold review regime applies to acquisitions of existing Canadian businesses and is not triggered by de novo incorporation of a matcha distribution company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to bring export proceeds onshore. A BC exporter may hold proceeds in any currency in any jurisdiction, subject to: (a) CRA Form T1135 foreign-income-verification reporting where aggregate specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000; (b) FINTRAC electronically transmitted funds reporting for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; and (c) bank-specific anti-money-laundering documentation of the economic basis of foreign-currency retention. Where the upstream matcha supply originates in a jurisdiction with capital controls (e.g., Japan), the Canadian seller must ensure that the foreign supplier receives payment through compliant local banking channels, but the Canadian side faces no surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central bank and monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential regulator of federally licensed banks; the Department of Finance Canada holds residual policy authority over the exchange rate regime. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration comparable to a SAFE-type agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. Canada does not recognize the EU/UK electronic money institution (EMI) authorization; foreign EMIs are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Canada and cannot independently open domestic accounts, hold insured deposits, or clear CAD-denominated transactions. Such providers may be used only as execution-only payment channels routed through a Canadian Schedule I bank or a registered money-services business (MSB) under FINTRAC, subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act registration regime led by the Bank of Canada. For a BC matcha importer, sole reliance on an offshore EMI creates unacceptable risk of disrupted clearing, delayed sanctions screening, and refusal of onward settlement by Japanese recipient banks; the blueprint therefore mandates a direct banking relationship with at least one Canadian Schedule I bank as the recognized legal entity for all CAD liquidity, with any fintech rail used only for small-value ancillary FX conversion and never for principal trade settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 (for amounts under the swift/telegraphic threshold) or SWIFT MT202 COVER (for larger correspondent transfers) from the foreign shareholder's account to the BC company's CAD account at the designated Canadian Schedule I bank. Canada does not maintain a mandatory wire-purpose code registry; however, the bank's compliance narrative field should state 'Capital Contribution – Share Issuance' and include the BC company's incorporation number and a reference to the board resolution authorizing the issuance. The remitting shareholder must provide source-of-wealth documentation, a CRS/FATCA self-certification, and confirmation of the investment's commercial purpose. Because greenfield establishment of a distribution business is not subject to Investment Canada Act review, no ministerial approval is required, but the company must file the post-incorporation notification under section 11 of that Act. Same-day settlement is achievable through the LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) if the transfer is in CAD; foreign-currency inbound wires are settled on the swift/correspondent cycle (typically one to two business days) and should be converted to CAD only under the board-approved treasury policy to minimize FX slippage.Outbound settlement for imported matcha powder should employ, in order of preference: (a) SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfer for open-account payments to the supplier after inspection and documentary verification; (b) documentary collection with SWIFT MT750/MT752 advice for initial transactions or moderate-value orders, protecting both parties through documents-against-payment terms; (c) irrevocable confirmed commercial letter of credit issued via SWIFT MT700, with the BC bank's confirming bank in Japan, for first-time supplier relationships and high-value ceremonial-grade matcha purchases; and (d) fully tested telex/SWIFT authorization for urgent disbursements. All payment instructions must include the supplier's full legal name, account number, Japanese bank branch and SWIFT BIC, invoice number, and the goods description 'organic matcha powder' under HS codes 0902.10 or 0902.20 for customs and sanctions screening. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium feasibility. Canada does not recognize the EU/UK electronic money institution (EMI) authorization; foreign EMIs are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Canada and cannot independently open domestic accounts, hold insured deposits, or clear CAD-denominated transactions. Such providers may be used only as execution-only payment channels routed through a Canadian Schedule I bank or a registered money-services business (MSB) under FINTRAC, subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act registration regime led by the Bank of Canada. For a BC matcha importer, sole reliance on an offshore EMI creates unacceptable risk of disrupted clearing, delayed sanctions screening, and refusal of onward settlement by Japanese recipient banks; the blueprint therefore mandates a direct banking relationship with at least one Canadian Schedule I bank as the recognized legal entity for all CAD liquidity, with any fintech rail used only for small-value ancillary FX conversion and never for principal trade settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local-file-style documentation is mandatory where aggregate controlled transactions with a single non-resident related party exceed CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year, or where any category of controlled transaction exceeds CAD 500,000. A group Master File is expected for multinational groups with consolidated annual turnover exceeding CAD 50 million (or equivalent).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) as in offshore finance centres, but the doctrine of economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), the permanent-establishment concept and treaty-limitation rules. Shell companies without real Canadian employees, business premises, purchase functions, decision-making or risk exposure can be disregarded for treaty and tax purposes, causing denial of treaty relief, reallocation of profits, and imposition of gross-negligence penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s. 239: fine equal to 50%–200% of evaded tax plus imprisonment up to five years; civil reassessment without limitation period; gross negligence penalty up to 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be made personally liable for unremitted withholdings under ITA s. 227.1; courts can order civil restitution, freezing of personal assets, travel bans and imprisonment for individuals who participated in or assented to fraudulent filings.
Failing to Maintain Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: CRA adjusts profits to arm's length and applies a 10% penalty on the amount of any adjustment when required contemporaneous documentation is not provided within 60 days of a written demand; plus late-filing penalties and interest; double taxation unless competent-authority relief is obtained.

Director personal liability: Officers who ignore TP documentation obligations may be assessed personally for withheld source taxes or for any corporate tax liability if they acted in a manner they knew to be unreasonable; travel restrictions and injunctions can issue during serious administrative investigations.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Treaty relief denied, dividends subject to full 25% WHT, GAAR re-assessments, penalties of 50% on misstatements; potential revocation of corporate status and winding-up orders where the entity is used as a sham.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate a façade company can be held civilly liable for any withheld taxes re-assessed, be subjected to personal curia judgments, denied travel privileges under court supervision, and face criminal prosecution for fraudulent misrepresentation.
Anti-Money Laundering / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: up to CAD 1 million (first) / CAD 2 million (repeat) per violation; designated criminal offences may lead to imprisonment up to ten years; CRA will deny tax deductions for non-compliant cash-based expenses.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors may be personally fined under FINTRAC's penalty framework and barred from re-entering key jurisdictions; travel bans can be imposed pending money-laundering investigations; in serious cases corporate veil is pierced leading to personal criminal liability.
