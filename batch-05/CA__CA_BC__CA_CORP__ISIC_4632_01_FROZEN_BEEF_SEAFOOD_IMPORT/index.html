Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO). In British Columbia, provincial sales tax (PST) is administered by the BC Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch) through eTaxBC; customs duty, GST/HST on import and other border measures are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) Portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain federal incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act through Corporations Canada (or a BC incorporation under the Business Corporations Act, BC), and complete the necessary extra-provincial registration. Step 2: Apply through BRO (or Form RC1) for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and select the RT program account for GST/HST and the RC program account for federal/BC corporate income tax; add an RM account when registering with CBSA as importer of record. Step 3: Register for the BC PST account via eTaxBC to secure the PST collector number before any sales of goods supplied in BC. Step 4: If the parent is a non-resident corporation, file Form RC138 (Application for a Non-Resident Corporation Registered to Perform Commercial Activity in Canada) and maintain a Canadian address for service. Standard issuance: virtually immediate confirmation for clean domestic online applications; 2 to 6 weeks for paper or multi-step non-resident cases.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Business Corporation under Canadian law is a distinct legal person and is not a pass-through entity, so Part I of the Income Tax Act applies to all of its world-wide income if resident in Canada, or to Canadian-source business income if non-resident and carrying on business in Canada. A corporation controlled by Canadian residents can be a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) and access the small business deduction on active business income up to the federally allocated Business Limit; with non-resident shareholding or in a wholesale import vehicle, the deduction is usually unavailable, and the income is simply taxed at the general federal and BC corporate rates. No Canadian tax-exempt zone applies to commercial wholesale operations of this kind.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists; the Canadian corporation is a per se taxpayer, and for United States upstream parents it is not a disregarded entity. Available elections include: (i) the association/control election under ITA subsection 256(2) for separation of business limits among connected corporations; (ii) the ETA subsection 273 joint election, which allows closely related corporations to treat taxable intercompany supplies as made for no consideration so that GST/HST neutrality is achieved within a consolidated cold-chain group; (iii) ITA section 216 and section 216.1 elections for non-residents receiving rents/timber royalties or treaty-protected income; (iv) a branch-tax election for the Canadian establishment of a foreign corporation under ITA Part XIV; and (v) the GST/HST quick method election under ETA section 230 for small registrants. Hybrid mismatch rules in ITA sections 18.4, 18.5 and Part VI.1 deny deductions where outcomes are hybrid-arbitrage driven (e.g. a payment giving a deduction in Canada without corresponding inclusion in the US parent).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: a GST/HST registrant must register once global taxable supplies (including taxable supplies made in Canada) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the aggregate of any four consecutive calendar quarters; for a non-resident importer, voluntary registration is economically essential because the GST/PST remitted at the border cannot otherwise be recovered as input tax credits. The BC PST regime has no small-supplier exemption; any person acquiring or importing goods for use in BC must register and self-assess the 7 percent PST before the goods enter the provincial consumption stream.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported by the registrant are zero-rated under ETA section 16, and goods delivered to a non-resident purchaser and removed from Canada within 60 days of delivery may be zero-rated under ETA subsection 16(1.1) when the required export evidence exists. For the ISIC 4632 frozen beef and seabound fish wholesale operation, the 0 percent rate is available both (i) upon re-export of imported goods held in cold storage and (ii) upon direct export of goods sourced and packaged in Canada for a non-resident buyer; the registrant must retain and produce contemporaneous proof of international transport, because zero-rating is conditional, not automatic.

  • CBSA B13 Goods Exported From Canada declaration (national export reporting for controlled/valued goods) or the corresponding carrier report of export
  • Commercial invoice showing HS 0202 frozen bovine carcasses/cuts or HS 0303/0304 frozen fish, quantity, weight, value and INCOTERM
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill with evidence of delivery to an international carrier and terminal handling at a Canadian seaport/airport
  • Carrier certificate of export and container/cold-storage receipt with temperature logger data evidencing continuous cold-chain custody
  • Foreign inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 / letter of credit documentation confirming exchange-control compliant receipts of sale proceeds
  • GST/HST quarterly return (Form GST34/GST62) and the GST/HST export evidence file compiled under CRA Memorandum 9-4 and 9-5

Canada does not require real-time fiscal clearance, API tax stamping, or continuous transaction controls for B2B invoices. A GST/HST registrant must issue a tax invoice containing its BN, the property/service description, and the tax charged, and retain electronic records in readable order; CRA is progressing a Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and Peppol-style pilots, but adoption is voluntary. Import accounting, however, is mandated entirely through the CBSA CARM Portal (single-window Canadian Single Interface): each shipment is matched to the importer's BN/CARM account, duties and PST/GST are secured and paid electronically, and non-compliance blocks future shipments.

CRA may reassess an export zero-rated supply as taxable at 5% GST where the documentation bundle is incomplete, goods cannot be traced out of Canada, or payment has not crystallized within the export-supporting period; interest under ETA section 280 and civil penalties for gross negligence under ETA section 285 may reach 25% or more of the net tax understated. Deliberate false statements or fabricated export records are indictable offences under ETA section 239 and ITA sections 238.1 to 244, carrying fines of up to 200 percent of the excise tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; CBSA can additionally issue AMPS penalties and impose refusal-of-release measures, while ETA section 222 grants CRA a deemed trust over receivables and inventory in priority to ordinary creditors for collected-but-unremitted GST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada does not maintain exchange controls on either current or capital account transactions; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Profits, dividends, interest, royalties, and invested capital may be repatriated freely, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (dividends generally 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty), Income Tax Act transfer-pricing documentation, and, for acquisitions of control, the Investment Canada Act net-benefit review. The Bank of Canada operates an inflation-targeting free-float regime and does not require prior administrative approval for payments. Bank-level verification and FINTRAC reporting are the only practical hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory percentage surrender, and no mark-to-market conversion obligation on export receipts. Exporters may retain, borrow, or invest foreign currency freely. For a BC-based importer, the only related obligations are accurate Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) customs declarations, CFIA import documentation for frozen beef and seafood, and FINTRAC reports for electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange and AML administration is exercised through the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and Global Affairs Canada for economic sanctions; no dedicated Canadian exchange control authority exists in law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs do not hold Canadian banking licences and cannot directly offer deposit accounts protected by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC). A foreign fintech must register as a money services business with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act framework, and use a Canadian Schedule I licensed PSP or bank for settlement. An unlicensed offshore EMI is legally unsuitable for corporate cash management or capital escrow; it may be used only for ancillary FX conversion through a compliant Canadian partner after rigorous due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Canadian corporation in BC, non-resident capital should be injected as share subscription, shareholder loan, or convertible debt. Use a SWIFT MT103 payment with the remitter in field 50K, the Canadian beneficiary bank in field 57A, and field 70 reference reading 'Share subscription CA/BC Corp [file reference]'. If the foreign currency is not CAD, convert through the Canadian bank FX desk or receive it into a USD account through the bank US correspondent. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (federal 15% + British Columbia general corporate income tax 12%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% of gross dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder (Part XIII Income Tax Act), unless a Canada income tax treaty reduces the rate, often to 15% generally and 5% for a foreign parent owning at least 10% of the Canadian corporation's voting shares.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation and T106 filing; additionally, MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000 are required to file Country-by-Country Reports and maintain OECD-aligned Master File and Local File documentation in practice.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. The functional equivalents are the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), the transfer-pricing rule (ITA s.247), the common-law sham doctrine, and the statutory provisions for denial of tax benefits. A BC-incorporated importer of frozen beef/seafood must demonstrate actual employees, office premises, inventory risk, decision-making authority, and contract risk in Canada; otherwise CRA may recharacterize the Canadian entity as a shell and attribute the import income or expense to the foreign principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer-Pricing Mispricing and Absent Documentation

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess using the arm's-length principle under ITA s.247(2), deny expense deductions, apply the 50% gross-negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2), charge compound interest on deficient tax, and extend the normal reassessment period for undisclosed non-arm's-length transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 for unremitted withholding amounts when the corporation fails to pay; where directors sign false transfer-pricing documentation or create sham services, the corporate veil may be pierced and directors can be assessed for civil restitution and prosecuted for tax evasion.
Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s.239 carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and, on indictment, imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil reassessment and the 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) apply independently of criminal proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directing minds who design or authorize fraudulent invoicing can be prosecuted personally; personal bank accounts, assets, and passports may be restrained; travel bans and extradition are possible where proceeds are moved abroad.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA s.245 permits CRA to deny all resulting tax benefits and assess a 50% GAAR penalty; the sham doctrine allows the court to disregard the corporation and assess the true economic owner; statutory anti-avoidance rules may treat distributions as directly taxable income.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers knowingly using a shell to evade Brazilian? No, to evade Canadian tax may be personally liable for restitution of diverted corporate assets, jointly and severally for tax debts under provincial corporate law, and subject to imprisonment for fraud or theft in commercial matters.
Trade-Based Money Laundering and Customs False Declarations

Statutory sanction: False customs declarations violate the Customs Act and can result in forfeiture of goods, administrative penalties, and criminal fines; money-laundering under the Criminal Code is an indictable offence punishable by up to 14 years in prison. FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties for unregistered or non-compliant entities can reach up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors who order, direct, or assist in trade-based money laundering or false customs filings face personal criminal liability; personal assets may be restrained and forfeited as proceeds of crime, and a compliance order may bar the director from managing any Canadian corporation.
Repatriation Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Canadian payer is liable under ITA s.215 for the entire amount of withholding tax not deducted, plus daily interest; recovery may be made directly from the corporation's assets. Conviction under s.238 or s.239 can result in additional fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be assessed personally for unremitted Part XIII taxes under s.227.1 if the corporation is insolvent or unable to pay; deliberately structuring distributions to avoid withholding exposes directors to criminal culpability and personal seizure of bank accounts and directors' fees.
