Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via My Business Account portal; provincial indirect taxation administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance under the Consumer Taxation Act and Provincial Sales Tax Act

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC3487 (Application for a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number) with the CRA; a GST/HST account (RT) is obtained simultaneously if the corporation is required to register or voluntarily elects to register. For British Columbia PST registration, the corporation must file the BC Provincial Sales Tax Application for Registration and obtain a provincial PST account if it is a 'seller' making taxable sales of goods in BC. Since the target activity is export wholesale distribution outside Canada, no PST registration is required, but GST/HST registration is recommended to claim zero-rated input tax credits and refunds. A prescribed non-resident address must be maintained and evidence of foreign incorporation must be filed with corporations registry prior to tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a 'check-the-box' regime; every business corporation is a mandatory separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act. Relevant statutory elections include: GST/HST group registration (s. 240 Income Tax Act / s. 215 GST/HST regulations) permitting a foreign parent to elect with its Canadian subsidiaries for fiscal-period reporting; CRA form RC2821 for GST/HST groups; section 85 rollover election for property transfers; section 256 amalgamation elections; capital cost allowance Class 14.1 re-elections; and treaty-based hybrid entity elections via filing Form NR3010 to assert treaty benefits in respect of non-resident withholding. There is no federal 'partnership tax classification' election available for a Canada Business Corporation, but foreign hybrid entities holding shares may claim tax-treaty limitations using Form NR3010/901.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies in Canada for residents; non-resident corporations that make taxable supplies in Canada must register unless all supplies are zero-rated, but registration is prerequisite to claiming input tax credits on goods/services supplied in Canada

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating applies to the supply of tangible personal property (including pre-packaged spicy tallow hotpot base bricks) that is exported from Canada by the supplier or a related subsidiary, or delivered outside Canada to a non-resident customer, provided possession transfers outside Canada. For agent-brokered wholesale export, the supplier must maintain proof that the goods were delivered to a destination outside Canada within a reasonable period under CRA policy P-051R and GST/HST Memorandum 20-1. The exporter must not be considered an 'exporter' merely by virtue of delivering to an export warehouse inside Canada without actual export. Food products requiring CFIA regulatory approval for export must also demonstrate compliance with the Safe Food for Canadians Act and, for pork tallow, compliance with animal-origin export permits.

  • Completed B13A Export Declaration or Canada Border Services Agency-approved electronic export report
  • Commercial invoice identifying the overseas importer, HS classification of the product, quantities, and sale price in foreign currency
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment from Canada to the foreign destination port
  • Cargo control document / forwarding agent instruction and port status showing goods left Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank wire statement proving receipt of foreign exchange payment from the non-resident buyer (where applicable; Canada does not issue a statutory 'inward remittance certificate' but a bank confirmation is standard)
  • Foreign customs entry or importation clearance documentation from the destination country demonstrating goods entered the foreign market

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing network, digital stamping, or Continuous Transaction Controls exist in Canada. CRA-administered GST/HST returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE; EDI/XML based reporting to CRA is available. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) operates the ACI/eManifest portal for electronic pre-arrival border data and exports are reported through G7 Export Return or CBSA B13A Export Declaration for food products that require customs control.

CRA may reassess zero-rated treatment retroactively and deny input tax credits if the exporter cannot substantiate actual export within the audit period, resulting in GST/HST assessed at 5%, plus arrear interest and penalties. Penalties under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act may apply to false statements, with fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment for severe fraud. Unjustified zero-rating coupled with intentional misrepresentation attracts the gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated net tax. If a beneficiary of the export concession participates in a scheme to claim refunds without actual shipment, CRA may invoke the anti-avoidance regime under section 274 of the Excise Tax Act and rescind refunds for up to four years. In addition, CBSA export data mismatches can trigger Customs Act penalties and suspension of export privileges. The 180-day outward remittance deadline is not a statutory Canadian tax deadline; however, CRA administrative practice accepts a 180-day outward remittance window as reasonable proof of monetary settlement for cross-border export transactions, and a failure to repatriate proceeds within 180 days is treated as heightened audit risk for value and currency manipulation, especially in sales to tax-haven or opaque jurisdictions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates under IMF Article VIII and maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar is freely floating; no exchange control approval is required for foreign investment, profit/dividend remittances, or cross-border payments by a British Columbia business corporation. Remittance hurdles are limited to Part XIII withholding taxes on dividend payments to non-residents, arm's-length transfer pricing documentation under section 247 of the Income Tax Act, and FINTRAC reporting of international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. Non-resident capital contributions are subject to Investment Canada Act notification or review thresholds when applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in Canadian chartered bank accounts, authorized foreign bank branches, or offshore accounts. However, every international electronic fund transfer of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by the financial institution to FINTRAC as a Large Electronic Fund Transfer Report. Physical cross-border transportation of currency or monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported on the Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report (E677).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - Canada does not maintain a designated foreign exchange control administration; prudential banking supervision is conducted by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. Canada does not licence electronic money institutions as deposit-taking banks. Foreign digital EMIs may operate only as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC; they cannot issue traditional prime-bank deposit accounts, commercial letters of credit, or CDIC-insured balances. A fintech/EMI corridor is viable only for low-value FX execution and disbursement, subject to the corporate treasury maintaining a primary clearing relationship with a Canadian Schedule I chartered bank. Legal constraints include mandatory MSB registration, beneficial ownership disclosure, and prohibition of unregistered cross-border money transmission to or from Canadian clients.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined headline rate composed of 15% federal Part I corporate income tax plus 12% British Columbia provincial corporate income tax for general corporations; eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) pay 11% combined (9% federal + 2% BC small business rate) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (Income Tax Act, s.212(2)) on any dividend or deemed dividend paid by a corporation resident in Canada to a non-resident shareholder; a lower treaty rate applies where the shareholder is a resident of a treaty country. Under the Canada-US Tax Convention, dividends are limited to 15% for portfolio shareholders and 5% where the US resident beneficial owner holds at least 10% of the voting shares of the Canadian payer.

The T2 return must be filed no later than 6 months after the corporation's taxation year-end. The final balance of tax payable is due 2 months after the taxation year-end for general corporations, or 3 months after year-end for an eligible CCPC with a qualifying balance-due day; quarterly instalments are due on the prescribed instalment dates rather than at filing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of specified non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with non-resident related parties in a taxation year (Income Tax Act, s.247). When the threshold is exceeded, the taxpayer must prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, in the OECD-aligned form used by the CRA, before the T2 filing deadline; Canada has no formal statutory Local File and Master File, but the CRA expects documentation equivalent to OECD BEPS Action 13 content as a defense against the s.247(3) penalty.
