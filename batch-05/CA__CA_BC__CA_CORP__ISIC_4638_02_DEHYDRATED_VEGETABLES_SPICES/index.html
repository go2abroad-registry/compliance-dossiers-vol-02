Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed under the Business Corporations Act; a company may be incorporated with unlimited share capital and shares may be issued for any lawful consideration fixed by the directors. No escrow account or capital deposit requirement applies in British Columbia. Non-resident ownership is permissible without federal net benefit review.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html (My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE). British Columbia corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement; BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through the eTaxBC portal. Customs and export filings are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the BC Business Corporations Act and obtain the federal or provincial incorporation number. Step 2: Apply to the CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or the CRA online business registration service; open the GST/HST program account (BN with 'RT' suffix), the corporate income tax program account (BN with 'RC' suffix), and, if employees are engaged, the payroll account (BN with 'RP' suffix). A non-resident corporation without Canadian incorporation uses the CRA non-resident registration procedure, including the simplified GST/HST registration for non-residents and, where required, the appointment of a Canadian representative. Step 3: Register as an exporter with the CBSA by linking the exporter account to the BN and activating electronic export reporting (CAED/RMDEL) for outbound shipments of dehydrated vegetables and spices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; a Business Corporation is mandatorily an entity-level taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Income Tax Act (British Columbia), taxed on worldwide income if resident and subject to Part XIV branch tax at 25% if a non-resident carrying on business in Canada. Available statutory elections include: ITA s. 85 tax-deferred rollover on transfers of property to the corporation; specified aligned-interest and associated-corporation rules are statutory rather than elective; and Canada's hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3, implementing BEPS Action 2) apply mandatorily to disallow deductions or impose inclusions, without an elective check-the-box feature. The BC unlimited liability company (ULC) form remains available where a foreign parent seeks US check-the-box disregarded-entity treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST small-supplier threshold): registration is compulsory where total taxable supplies from Canada and worldwide exceed CAD 30,000 in either a single calendar quarter or the last four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available below the threshold. Non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Canada are subject to the same threshold, with special distribution and drop-shipment rules for imported and expensed goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) and Schedule VI, a supply of tangible personal property is zero-rated at 0% GST/HST if the goods are exported by the supplier or the recipient, delivered to a carrier for export, or deemed exported, provided there is no taxable use or consumption in Canada. For bulk dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper shipments sold on FOB/CFR/CIF terms to non-resident buyers, 0% zero-rating is available where the exportation is fully documented. Where the foreign purchaser is a GST/HST registrant, certain B2B zero-rating rules may apply. Exports are also exempt from BC PST where goods are delivered to a carrier or dispatched for export outside Canada and the supplier retains a prescribed export certificate or equivalent evidence.

  • CBSA Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or the electronic CAED/RMDEL declaration accepted by the Canada Border Services Agency
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment to the non-resident consignee
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract or purchase order (including SWIFT LC or TT payment terms)
  • Inward remittance evidence (e.g., bank SWIFT MT103 or credit advice) confirming receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • Packing list, weight certificate, and optional certificate of origin, retained with the exporter's books for CRA audit

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control regime. The CRA mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns (NETFILE / My Business Account) for virtually all registrants and requires electronic T2 corporate income tax filing, but does not pre-approve or digitally stamp individual invoices. The federal government is promoting voluntary adoption of the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard based on the PEPPOL interoperability framework; no API-based clearance and no mandatory e-invoice stamping obligation currently apply.

On audit, the CRA and the BC Ministry of Finance verify export documentation, the identity of the foreign purchaser, and the timing and amount of repatriated proceeds. If zero-rating is not substantiated, GST/HST at 5% and, where applicable, BC PST are reassessed on the full sale amount, input tax credits are clawed back, and arrears interest runs from the original return due date. Knowingly false or grossly negligent statements attract a civil penalty under ETA s. 285 of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be avoided, with parallel ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalties for income tax (the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax). Criminal prosecution is possible under the ETA and ITA fraud provisions. Although Canada imposes no codified FX-repatriation deadline for export zero-rating, unrepatriated or artificially deferred export proceeds beyond 180 days may trigger ITA s. 247 transfer-pricing adjustments and reallocations of Canadian-source income, compounding interest and penalty exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction; the Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange-control approvals for inbound equity, trade settlements, profit repatriation, or liquidation proceeds. The only material cost of remitting profits to non-residents is Part XIII withholding tax: 25% on dividends, generally reduced to 15% under an applicable income tax treaty (e.g., Canada-EU Convention), reinforced by transfer-pricing and thin-capitalization rules under sections 247, 212.3, and 18(4) of the Income Tax Act. The CAD/USD and CAD/EUR markets are deep and unrestricted; no conversion license or central bank waiver applies to a BC trading corporation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exist in Canada. Exporters of dehydrated vegetables and spices may retain USD/EUR proceeds in a Foreign Currency Account at a Schedule I bank, or convert freely into CAD. Foreign-currency gains and losses on conversion or on translation of monetary items are recognized under Income Tax Act section 39(1); a corporation that elects under section 261 may use a qualifying functional currency for tax reporting. Imported and exported goods are zero-rated for GST/HST purposes. CBSA export reporting (B13A via the eBSB portal) applies within 15 days of export where the prescribed shipment threshold (generally CAD 2,000 or a regulated commodity trigger) is crossed; no foreign-exchange surrender certificate or customs-linked conversion ratio is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa; regulatory and enforcement oversight for currency and cross-border payments is shared with the Federal Minister of Finance, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for physical currency reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium viability with clearly bounded legal capacity: foreign digital EMIs operating into Canada (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Multy) are FINTRAC-registered money services businesses, not Schedule I banks under the Bank Act (Canada). They cannot issue documentary letters of credit, provide deposit insurance through CDIC, or extend trade loans. They are operationally effective for supplier payouts, small-value collections and FX conversion, but must be paired with a Schedule I bank account as the primary settlement and escrow rail. A second-tier BC credit union (Vancity, Coast Capital Savings) is viable for provincial deposit routing but has narrower LC capacity than the big six banks.
