Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal corporate income tax and GST/HST administration, accessible through Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca; British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance through the eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian business corporation obtains a nine-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA, together with program accounts including RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RM (import-export reporting), and RP (payroll) where applicable. Registration is completed online via BRO or by submitting Form RC1; no minimum paid-in capital is required for incorporation, and non-resident ownership is unrestricted under the Canada Business Corporations Act. The BC PST registration is secured separately through eTaxBC where the corporation has a place of business in BC, delivers taxable tangible personal property into BC, or makes taxable sales to BC consumers. Required particulars for BN registration include the registered office address, date of commencement of Canadian commercial activity, authorized or issued share structure, director and officer identification, fiscal year-end, and a business activity description aligned with NAICS 423460 (optical goods merchant wholesalers).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a federal/provincial Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act and is not fiscally transparent. The corporation pays federal and provincial corporate income tax on its worldwide income, subject to foreign tax credits and the foreign affiliate active-business exemption (which provides territorial-style relief for active foreign business income); non-resident shareholders receiving dividends are subject to Part XIII withholding tax unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election; corporate status is determined statutorily by incorporation. Pertinent elections affecting the tax identity of a wholesale distribution corporation include: section 216 of the Income Tax Act (non-resident electing to file a Part I return in respect of Canadian rental or timber receipts), section 85 and 86.1 rollover elections, section 88 winding-up elections, section 93(1) exempt-surplus dividend designation elections, and the section 218 joint election for a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada. Hybrid mismatch rules introduced through Budget 2021 (Bill C-59, Part 2) disallow deductions in structured arrangements involving hybrid entities or hybrid instruments and do not offer elective accommodation; no pass-through election is available for a Canadian incorporated entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding capital receipts and financial services) over four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident supplier whose only Canadian supplies are zero-rated may be excused from mandatory registration but can voluntarily register to claim input tax credits. BC PST: no fixed turnover threshold for out-of-province sellers; registration with the BC Ministry of Finance is triggered when the seller has a place of business, agent, or fulfilment presence in BC, transfers title or possession of tangible personal property in BC, or ships goods to BC consumers under the direct-sales rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a wholesale supply of acetate ophthalmic eyeglass frames is zero-rated at 0% GST where the supplier ships or delivers the goods, or causes them to be delivered by a common carrier under its contract, to a destination outside Canada and the goods are exported as soon as reasonably possible thereafter (Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, section 1); the supplier must retain satisfactory evidence of export proving physical export and transfer of ownership outside Canada. Services: ancillary offshore services supplied to a non-resident, such as international logistics coordination, marketing, or quality assurance, are generally zero-rated under Schedule VII, section 7 of the Excise Tax Act where the non-resident is not a GST/HST registrant, the service relates neither to real property nor tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and the benefit is not enjoyed by a resident of Canada. Zero-rating is asserted directly on the periodic GST/HST return, allowing full input tax credit recovery on export-cost inputs and generating a net refund where creditable tax exceeds GST collected.

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API pre-stamping, or digital stamping regime for invoices. CRA may reassess the GST/HST return within the applicable limitation period and, where export documentation fails the satisfactory-evidence-of-export test, deny zero-rating, retroactively tax the supply at 5% (and subject any provincial PST exemption to the same BC audit scrutiny), disallow related input tax credits, and charge arrears interest. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII obligations for both current-account trade and capital-account transactions. There is no permission regime for purchase of foreign currency, no central bank approval threshold, no limit on overseas dividend or profit remittance, and no restriction on retaining export revenue in USD/EUR at Canadian financial institutions. Profit repatriation is subject only to Canadian income tax at the entity level and, for distributions, Part XIII withholding tax of 25% on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, which is typically reduced to 5%-15% under Canada's tax treaties (e.g., Canada-US Article X). Transfer-pricing documentation under Income Tax Act section 247 and adequate paid-up capital mechanics are necessary so that management fees, royalties, and intercompany services can be remitted without recharacterization as taxable dividends.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose export proceeds surrender, conversion-ratio, mandatory repatriation, or settlement-timeline rules. The wholesale exporter of acetate ophthalmic eyewear frames may leave export proceeds in a foreign currency account (e.g., the bank's USD sub-account) indefinitely or convert them at any authorized dealer rate. The only binding obligation is the PCMLTFA: any physical cross-border movement of cash or monetary instruments equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 must be declared to the CBSA on FINTRAC's Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Report (ECDR), and electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more credited to a Canadian account are reportable as Large Electronic Funds Transfer (LFET) by the financial institution. The B13A Export Declaration and matching bank credits constitute the compliance record for the export-receipt audit trail.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act as the central bank and operator of Lynx, the high-value payment and settlement system. Canada has no dedicated foreign exchange control authority; the former Foreign Exchange Control Board was abolished in 1951. Financial-transaction reporting is supervised by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and federal bank chartering, capital, and risk supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act. Capital contributions into Canada remain subject to Investment Canada Act net-benefit review thresholds, but this does not constitute FX conversion control.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Conditional Medium feasibility. Canadian law does not define a sui generis Electronic Money Institution; any fintech issuing stored value or executing payments in CAD must register with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) and, when offering lending, satisfy BC provincial money-lender licensing requirements. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or XTransfer can lawfully operate as payment-only facilitators by sponsoring into Schedule I banks, but they have no direct Lynx settlement entitlement and cannot open CAD nostro settlement accounts without Canadian MSB registration. For this wholesale business they are viable only for low-value Platform_Pay collections below the CAD 10,000 EFT reporting threshold; high-value trade settlement must remain on SWIFT MT103 rails through RBC/CIBC to preserve the documentary chain and avoid second-tier settlement risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection executes as a SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's financial institution to the BC corporation's Schedule I bank account in Vancouver. The message should carry the instruction in field 70: 'Capital Contribution CA__CA_BC__CA_CORP__ISIC_4646_01_OPHTHALMIC_EYEWEAR_FRAMES', and the remitter must be the legal shareholder of record, not a third party, to prevent AML misclassification. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Conditional Medium feasibility. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No monetary exemption: contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared under ITA s. 247(4) for every cross-border non-arm's length transaction before the T2 filing deadline. Country-by-Country Report and Master File are mandatory for MNE groups whose consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000; the CRA also expects a Local File for all related-party transactions involving a Canadian entity, with the draft OECD-aligned documentation rules introducing an explicit Local File threshold of CAD 10,000,000 in Canadian gross revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations akin to the UAE, but a non-resident-owned BC wholesale corporation must demonstrate genuine substance for treaty and PE purposes: BC-based decision-making, local board meetings, employees, warehouse operations and commercial contracts. The CRA may apply the general anti-avoidance rule (s. 245) to recharacterise a conduit arrangement, deny treaty benefits, and impose Part XIV branch tax (25% of after-tax Canadian business income) if the non-resident is deemed to carry on the business directly in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1): on summary conviction, a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment of up to 2 years; on indictment, imprisonment of up to 5 years. The civil gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2) adds 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or acquiesce to the evasion are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 (unremitted withholdings) and s. 160 (below-market transfers to non-arm's length persons); face CRA civil restitution claims, court-imposed travel bans as bail conditions, and potential extradition where they depart Canada.
Failure to File and Deficient Reporting

Statutory sanction: Penalty under s. 162(1)/(2): 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month up to 12 months; information return penalties of CAD 100 per day up to CAD 8,500; a court order can compel filing with a fine of up to CAD 10,000 and imprisonment of up to one year under s. 238(1).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for corporate source deductions and penalties if the CRA cannot collect from the corporation, and may be prohibited from managing a corporation; travel bans can be ordered where criminal charges for obstruction or evasion arise.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under s. 247(2) to the arm's length price with interest; a specific 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s. 247(3) applies to the net understatement of income where the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to determine and document the arm's length price.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause to be filed false transfer-pricing certificates may be prosecuted for counselling tax evasion, face personal assessments under s. 160, and incur director liability orders requiring personal restitution of corporate tax debts.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245) disallows the abusive arrangement and re-assesses as if the non-resident carried on business directly in Canada; Part XIV branch tax of 25% is imposed on post-tax Canadian income; treaty withholding relief is denied; interest and penalties accrue from the original filing date.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the corporate veil to be used as a sham may be personally reassessed on a substance-over-form basis, face CRA civil seizure of personal assets, and in flagrant cases criminal charges carrying travel bans and imprisonment for fraud or evasion.
AML and Financial Transaction Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act of up to CAD 500,000 for entities (CAD 100,000 for individuals); criminal money-laundering under Criminal Code s. 462.31 carries up to 10 years imprisonment; assets may be frozen or forfeited.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct payment structuring or divert corporate receipts to personal offshore accounts face criminal money-laundering liability, personal asset seizures, travel bans pending trial, and incarceration; corporate veil is pierced for laundering proceeds.
