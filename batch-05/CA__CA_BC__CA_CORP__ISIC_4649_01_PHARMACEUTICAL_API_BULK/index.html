Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal GST/HST and corporate income tax are administered through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and Represent a Client portals. BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch) via eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian-incorporated business corporation (CA_CORP) is deemed resident in Canada under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 250(4) and must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll if employees), and RM (import/export) where applicable. Application is made via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), attaching Form RC2 (Corporation Account Authorization) and the certified articles/certificate of incorporation. A foreign parent with no Canadian permanent establishment should not itself obtain a TIN unless it is carrying on business in Canada; the Canadian subsidiary must hold the GST/HST RT account to zero-rate API exports and claim input tax credits. Non-resident corporations registering directly for GST/HST must file Form RC1, provide a Canadian filing address or appoint a representative through CRA Represent a Client, and may be required to post security under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 240. The BC PST account is applied for separately through eTaxBC once wholesale activity commences in British Columbia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity-classification election; incorporation creates deemed residence under ITA s. 250(4) and entity-level taxation of worldwide income. Relevant elections: (i) corporate fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 permitting a non-calendar taxation year; (ii) Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status and aggregation elections under ITA s. 256; (iii) partnership income-allocation elections under ITA s. 103 where a Canadian partnership is interposed; (iv) GST/HST Quick Method election for small suppliers under ETA s. 227.1 and the associated Remission Order; (v) elective capital cost allowance pooling under the Income Tax Regulations. For hybrid mismatch arrangements, Canada's BEPS Action 2 rules, effective for payments made or arising after 1 July 2023, operate automatically: deduction/no-inclusion mismatches, imported mismatches, and double-deduction arrangements are denied under ITA Part I without any general elective or check-the-box relief; corresponding reportable-transaction disclosure obligations under ITA s. 237.3 may apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (ETA small-supplier threshold measured on worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four calendar quarters and the current quarter). A person exceeding the threshold, carrying on a taxable commercial activity in Canada without being a small supplier, or supplying certain imported taxable supplies to Canada must register for GST/HST. BC PST registration is mandatory for every vendor carrying on business in BC with no monetary threshold; sales of pharmaceutical APIs to PST-registered manufacturers or resellers are generally exempt from PST when the purchaser supplies a valid PST registration number and acquires the goods for resale or as ingredients/raw materials.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported bulk pharmaceutical APIs are zero-rated under Schedule VI to the ETA: s. 1 where the supplier exports the goods; s. 5 where goods are supplied to a non-resident for delivery outside Canada; and s. 11 where goods are consigned to an exporter and exported within prescribed periods. Claiming 0% GST requires the physical removal of tangible personal property from Canada, passage of title, and contemporaneous documentation of export intent; the supplier must be a GST/HST registrant, charge 0% GST, and preserve refund eligibility for input tax credits attributable to the exported goods. Ancillary offshore services, such as freight forwarding, foreign regulatory dossier preparation, and logistics management performed wholly outside Canada for a non-resident, may be zero-rated under Schedule VI s. 7; intangible property supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada qualifies under Schedule VI s. 23. BC PST does not apply to exported goods, and PST-registered wholesalers evidence exempt sales for resale or manufacture through valid PST exemption certificates.

  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill showing Canadian port of loading, foreign destination, and non-resident consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer

Canada imposes no continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate. Effective for reporting periods beginning after 2023, all GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically (NETFILE, EDI, or API); the prior mandatory electronic filing threshold was annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million. Export shipments are reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Export Reporting System (ERS) or legacy Form B13A; real-time pre-approval of individual invoices does not exist. Large pharmaceutical trading partners commercially require EDI (EDIFACT or ANSI X12) or Peppol BIS 3.0 e-invoices, but such exchange is contractual, not statutory.

CRA audits export zero-rating on a transaction-by-transaction basis. Failure to substantiate the export and the receipt of foreign currency triggers retrospective GST/HST reassessment at 5% plus BC PST (7%) where applicable, with daily interest under ETA s. 280 and ITA s. 161(1). Penalties escalate for culpable conduct: the gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 285 is the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax at issue, and under ITA s. 163(2) the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of understated income tax. Fraudulent export invoicing and false zero-rating claims are prosecutable offences under ETA ss. 327/328 and ITA s. 239, carrying fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and possible imprisonment. Transfer-pricing gross-valuation misstatements on export invoices attract an additional 10% penalty under ITA s. 247(3). Non-arm's-length exporters failing to repatriate proceeds within the 180-day benchmark face recharacterization of the transaction as a domestic supply, denial of zero-rating, and expanded CRA audit document demands under ITA ss. 231.1 and 231.2.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no exchange controls on profit, dividend, interest, royalty, or equity repatriation. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible, and foreign currency accounts are permitted. However, payment and trade transactions must comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), sanctions administered by Global Affairs Canada, and Health Canada trade controls on pharmaceutical products and APIs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no Canadian statutory surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may hold proceeds in any foreign currency. Accounting records must be maintained in Canadian dollars for tax and excise reporting, and certain API exports may require an export permit or controlled-drugs authorization under the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act and the Food and Drugs Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no standalone foreign exchange administration in Canada. Trade-related licensing falls under Global Affairs Canada (Export and Import Permits Act), health product regulation under Health Canada, and AML/KYC supervision under the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury counterpart. Canada does not operate the EU EMI passport regime; an offshore digital EMI holding no Canadian bank licence cannot act as a principal clearing and settlement bank, issue commercial letters of credit, or guarantee incoming/outgoing cross-border flows. An offshore EMI may operate only as a FINTRAC-registered money services business (MSB) for certain payment service functions, subject to strict AML/beneficial-ownership diligence and limited to ancillary low-value transfers. It is not an acceptable replacement for a Schedule I chartered bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan contributions are routed by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 with MT103 cover) to the Canadian operating account. Canada does not require a national purpose code, but the sending institution should state purpose using ISO 20022 codes such as EQUI (equity securities) or GINT (general inter-company transfer) in the remittance information field. Verification requires a signed board resolution authorizing the capital contribution, shareholder loan agreement, share subscription agreement, UBO ownership map, source-of-funds evidence, and confirmation that neither sender nor beneficiary is a sanctioned party. Incoming international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC reporting obligations. There is no minimum capital requirement for a BC business corporation, but the bank may request a projection of anticipated cash flows and funding purpose.Outbound API trade disbursements use two main rails: (1) open-account settlement through SWIFT MT103 wires to suppliers, and (2) cross-border shipping under irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 and settled against presentation of compliant trade documents. CAD wires clear through Payments Canada's large-value transfer system; USD wires clear through correspondent banks using CHIPS or Fedwire. Each outward cross-border EFT equal to or above CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC. An offshore EMI may operate only as a FINTRAC-registered money services business (MSB) for certain payment service functions, subject to strict AML/beneficial-ownership diligence and limited to ancillary low-value transfers. It is not an acceptable replacement for a Schedule I chartered bank.
