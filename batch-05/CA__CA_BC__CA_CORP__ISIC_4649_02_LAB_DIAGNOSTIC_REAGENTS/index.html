Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/business-number-registration.html) and GST/HST NETFILE suite; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Revenue Division — eTaxBC PST registration portal (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst).

E-government filing portal

Any newly incorporated Canadian business corporation must obtain a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) from CRA, together with program accounts: RT for GST/HST, RP for Part I corporate income tax, and RC for payroll where employees are resident in Canada. Procurement is effected either online through Business Registration Online or by paper Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) executed by an authorized officer, with the BC or federal Articles of Incorporation and, where required, a certified power of attorney for foreign principals. CRA mails the BN confirmation within approximately 15 business days. Non-resident corporations that are not carrying on business in Canada but make taxable supplies in Canada must still register for the RT account, provide records access under ETA s. 286 and, where CRA so requires, post security under ETA s. 307. For BC PST, registration is mandatory in the eTaxBC portal before taxable sales commence in British Columbia; the PST registration is a separate BC PST number distinct from the federal BN, with no de minimis threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian-incorporated business corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation within the meaning of subsection 89(1) of the Income Tax Act (ITA), treated as a separate taxable entity taxed on worldwide income under Part I; the territorial scope is residence-based, so foreign-source active business income remains taxable in Canada subject to foreign tax credits and treaty relief. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian-incorporated business corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation within the meaning of subsection 89(1) of the Income Tax Act (ITA), treated as a separate taxable entity taxed on worldwide income under Part I; the territorial scope is residence-based, so foreign-source active business income remains taxable in Canada subject to foreign tax credits and treaty relief. The combined federal / BC general corporate rate is approximately 27% (federal 15% plus BC 12%) before the Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) small business deduction, which applies to the first CAD 500,000 of active business income at roughly 11%; no branch-level tax classification is available for a BC domestic entity, and dual-residency risk (central management and control) must be monitored closely.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box or partnership-classification election; all corporations are mandatorily entity-level taxpayers. Hybrid-mismatch positions are addressed through the Multilateral Convention rules effective after 2023, under which a Canadian entity may elect to apply the imported-mismatch rule and document treaty-favourable results in the transfer-pricing file. Domestic elections available include: (i) the GST/HST closely related corporation election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act, rendering inter-affiliate supplies non-taxable; (ii) the election under subsection 89(11) of the ITA to deem shares of a foreign affiliate to be capital property; (iii) the section 85 rollover election on incorporation or reorganization of a proprietorship; and (iv) the amalgamation and loss-absorption elections under ITA s. 256.2 for intra-group losses. For the PST regime no election is available — registration, collection and remittance are compulsory for any BC taxable sale.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 — the small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1) on total worldwide taxable supplies, measured quarterly (current quarter or preceding four quarters); registration is mandatory once turnover exceeds CAD 30,000, but voluntary registration is available and prudent for exporters in order to recover input tax credits. BC PST: no registration threshold — registration is required before any taxable sale in British Columbia, except for supplies of exported goods and supplies covered by a valid PST exemption certificate (e.g., sales to hospitals, provincial health authorities, or licensed medical practitioners of in-vitro diagnostic reagents classified as medical diagnostics).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of clinical ELISA assay kits and diagnostic reagents qualifies for the 0% rate under section 16, Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act where the supplier ships or delivers the property to a destination outside Canada pursuant to a contract of carriage or its own transportation, and title and all incidents of ownership pass to the non-resident purchaser in the ordinary course of trade before any use in Canada. Offshore services connected with the distribution (e.g., cold-chain logistics, calibration or quality-control certification) are zero-rated under section 7, Part V, Schedule VI when rendered to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST, provided the service does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. Any domestic installation, validation or use of the reagents before export destroys eligibility; CRA applies the exports zero-rating audit guide and a clear six-factor evidentiary analysis covering contractual supply, delivery terms, transfer of possession and risk, payment provenance, carrier evidence, and non-resident status of the purchaser.

  • CBSA Canada Export Declaration (B13A / CAED) filed via the Canada Export Reporting System or by a licensed customs broker (required for goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more)
  • Export Sales Contract or Purchase Order evidencing the sale to the non-resident purchaser, including Incoterms (EXW/FCA/CPT/CIP/DAP)
  • Commercial Invoice (in English) describing the diagnostic reagent batch, HS classification, value for duty, and the buyer's GST/HST registration status
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Master/House Air Waybill (air) evidencing the foreign destination and consignee
  • Packing list, cold-chain temperature logs, carrier pickup confirmation, and CBSA release documentation from the port of export
  • Final FX settlement evidence: Swift Wire / TT bank advice, bank statement crediting proceeds, or an Inward Remittance Certificate where the buyer's jurisdiction requires one
  • BC PST exemption certificate (where applicable) confirming that the goods are exported and not diverted to BC consumption

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate for commercial GST invoices or PST receipts. CRA record-keeping obligations (ITA Regulation 5800 and ETA s. 286) require electronic invoices, cash-register data and export clearance reports to be retained in readable format and made available electronically on demand; non-resident suppliers must either keep records in Canada or provide an approved electronic access arrangement. Pan-Canadian e-invoicing (Peppol-based) is voluntary. The mandatory clearance document in the export chain is the CBSA Canada Export Declaration (B13A) filed through the Canada Export Reporting System; for shipments valued at CAD 2,000 or more, failure to file generates administrative monetary penalties (AMPS) of up to CAD 25,000 per violation and triggers CRA audit that can rebut the zero-rating documentation bundle.

Under ETA s. 298, CRA may reassess GST/HST within the normal four-year assessment window; where export zero-rating is disallowed for insufficient substantiation, the supply is re-rated at 5% GST plus applicable 7% BC PST if the goods are diverted into BC consumption, with daily compounding arrears interest under ETA s. 280 assessed from the filing-due date. For fraudulent or careless conduct, the reassessment period is unlimited, and gross-negligence penalties are statutorily set at 25% of the tax sought to be evaded under ETA s. 285 for GST purposes and up to 50% of the understated tax under ITA s. 163(2) for income tax purposes. Criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 239 exposes the corporation to court-imposed fines, imprisonment of up to five years on indictment, and a 100% civil penalty on unreported export proceeds. CRA has also clawed back input tax credits previously claimed on exported reagents where the exporter's documentation bundle failed the six-factor evidentiary test; records must be retained for at least six years after the export, and if an export payment is received after the assumed 180-day repatriation benchmark, the exporter should prepare a written explanation of extended commercial credit terms to pre-empt audit disallowance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully and freely convertible for both capital and current account transactions. Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime without exchange control restrictions under the Currency, Exchange and Banking Act; there are no separate capital control or current account convertibility approvals. Profit remittance (dividends, branch profits, interest, royalties) is generally unrestricted subject to withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and applicable tax treaties, provided that the payor is not in default of Canadian tax obligations and the recipient provides the necessary forms for reduced treaty withholding. No exchange control permission is required for repatriation of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation in Canada to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory converted threshold, and no timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Canadian exporters may retain foreign currency in an offshore or domestic account; however, for Canadian income tax purposes, unrealized foreign exchange gains or losses may be recognized on a revenue-account or capital-account basis depending on the nature of the receivables. All export documentation must comply with the customs reporting requirements of the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and Statistics Canada, but free disposal of export proceeds is unrestricted.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating in co-ordination with the Canadian Department of Finance; foreign exchange policy is administered by the Minister of Finance and the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), with no dedicated foreign exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with strict regulatory limitations. A foreign electronic money institution (EMI) not licensed or registered under Canada's Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and not supervised by FINTRAC may not lawfully provide digital payment, money transfer, or cross-border settlement services to Canadian residents. An entity that engages in foreign exchange transfers, remittance, or payment processing must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, if in British Columbia, may be subject to provincial consumer protection regimes. Viable only where the EMI has a Canadian MSB registration or operates through a registered and regulated Canadian partner bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection into the British Columbia corporation must be effected via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the foreign corporate parent to the Canadian settlement account at a Schedule I bank. The wire must clearly state the purpose, generally referencing 'Direct Investment — Equity Subscription' or 'Injection of operating capital under Canada Customs and Revenue Agency guidelines.' For Statistics Canada and Bank of Canada balance-of-payments classification, the transaction should be coded as Direct Investment (equity injection) or Direct Investment (intercompany loan), depending on the subscribed share structure. The Canadian receiving bank shall require the remitter and beneficiary to confirm source of funds and the underlying corporate resolution; funds must be verifiable as originating from the bank account of the foreign parent and not from an unregulated third-party aggregator. For amounts above CAD 10,000, the bank will complete a FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction/International Electronic Funds Transfer report automatically; there is no currency conversion restriction upon arrival.Trade settlements for imported diagnostic reagents and wholesale distribution disbursements to foreign manufacturers should be made via (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct wire payments; (ii) commercial letters of credit (L/C) issued under UCP600 subject to the ICC rules, advised or confirmed by the Canadian bank or a correspondent bank; or (iii) documentary collections (CAD/DP) where adequate trade history exists. For payments in USD, a Canadian dollar to US dollar conversion is executed at the bank's prevailing spot rate or under a forward contract; the CAD is not subject to exchange surrender. The Canadian entity must maintain a fully documented trade file including purchase orders, supplier invoices, shipping documents, and customs entries. Export disbursements should include clear payment purpose codes (e.g., 'Imports of Goods' / 'General Merchandise') and comply with the Foreign Exchange Reporting Regulations established by the Bank of Canada Act and Statistics Canada reporting thresholds. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal and British Columbia general corporate income tax rate (15% federal Part I general rate + 12% BC general rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident payee, unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed within 6 months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. Tax payable is due 2 months after fiscal year-end for non-CCPCs and 3 months after fiscal year-end for CCPCs with eligible Canadian controlled private corporation status; non-resident corporations are subject to the 6-month filing rule and the general payment due dates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of reportable non-arm's length transactions with each non-resident person or related enterprise in a taxation year generally triggers mandatory T106 filing and robust transfer pricing documentation expectations under CRA guidelines.

Economic substance scope: British Columbia does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to Caribbean or Gulf ESR jurisdictions. However, the CRA applies the arm's-length standard under subsection 247(2), the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245, and the substance-over-form doctrine in treaty benefit determinations. A BC entity lacking real distribution capacity, inventory risk, employees or substantive decision-making functions may be disregarded or recharacterized for transfer pricing and permanent establishment purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False or Misleading Statements

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act subsection 239(1), on summary conviction a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment a fine of not less than 100% and not more than 300% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years, plus reassessment and arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorized, participated in, or acquiesced to the filing of false returns may be jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid source deductions under subsection 227.1(1), and may face criminal prosecution as principals under section 242 of the Income Tax Act, including fines and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty under subsection 247(3) equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, applied without reducing other tax liabilities; CRA may also impose gross negligence penalties under subsection 163(2) and deny offsetting adjustments, producing full reassessment plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be named for GST/HST and payroll withholding liabilities arising from unreported inventory payments, and may be assessed personally where they directed or permitted the related-party payment scheme to avoid Canadian tax.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Avoidance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 to re-determine tax consequences, deny treaty benefits on dividend/royalty flows, impose transfer pricing adjustments, and assess gross negligence penalties; an avoidance transaction under the GAAR may also trigger a punitive benefit tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of a deemed sham or shell cannot shield the distribution activity through the corporate veil where the corporation is used as a device to commit tax fraud; civil restitution and potential criminal liability may extend to individual board members.
Unremitted Part XIII Withholding Tax on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The Canadian payer remains liable under subsection 215(2) for the entire amount that should have been withheld; late remittance penalties and daily prescribed interest are added, and repeated failures may trigger criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the withholding tax amount and related penalties under subsection 227.1(1) if the corporation fails to deduct, withhold, or remit; this liability may be enforced by CRA directly against personal assets.
AML / FINTRAC Reporting and Wire Transfer Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under s.462.31 of the Criminal Code is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years; FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties can reach significant amounts for missing reports; property derived from the offence is subject to freezing and forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors participating in or directing laundering of proceeds through corporate bank accounts may be prosecuted personally; travel bans, asset freezing orders, and personal seizure of assets are available remedies under the Criminal Code, alongside civil liability for restitution.
