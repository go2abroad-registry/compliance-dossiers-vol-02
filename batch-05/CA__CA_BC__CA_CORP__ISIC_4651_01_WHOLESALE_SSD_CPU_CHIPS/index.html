Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal income tax, GST/HST and payroll administration via Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MyBA) and GST/HST NETFILE portals; customs administration via Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) Portal; British Columbia provincial indirect tax window: BC Ministry of Finance / Director of Provincial Sales Tax via eTaxBC for the Provincial Sales Tax (PST).

E-government filing portal

A BC-registered business corporation obtains its federal Business Number (BN) automatically once the BC Corporate Registry transmits incorporation data to the CRA. For a foreign-owned or newly formed corporation carrying on business in Canada: (1) incorporate under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) via BC Corporate Online; (2) activate BN programme accounts (GST/HST, payroll, corporate income tax) on MyBA; (3) if CRA does not auto-issue the BN, file RC1 (Request for a Business Number); (4) register for a GST/HST account when worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, or upon voluntary registration to claim input tax credits (ITCs); (5) register with CBSA CARM to obtain an importer/exporter number for customs reporting; (6) for wholesale sales within British Columbia, register as a PST collector only if the corporation maintains a vendor location or permanent establishment in BC — wholesale sales of SSD/CPU chips to registered resellers are generally exempt from PST or are zero-rated sales under the PST framework.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide check-the-box classification; the CRA taxes corporations as separate legal persons under the Income Tax Act (Part I) and Excise Tax Act (Part IX). Relevant statutory elections include: (i) ETA s. 156 election among closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; (ii) ETA s. 150 election to group financial services supplies; (iii) ETA s. 211 election to reduce the repayment of ITCs; (iv) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident corporation to file a net-basis Part I return on Canadian rental or resource property income; (v) ITA s. 250(5.1) deemed-residence election for eligible non-resident corporations; (vi) treaty permanent establishment declarations via CRA Forms RC4900 and RC4645 where relevant to a US or other treaty residence; (vii) hybrid mismatch arrangements are not elective — ITA ss. 18.3 to 18.4 (including expanded 2023 rules) override deductible mismatches and no foreign classification election is recognised.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold) measured by worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada; registration becomes mandatory on the earlier of (a) the day the threshold is exceeded in a single calendar quarter, or (b) the day following any of the four consecutive calendar quarters in which cumulative taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 (ETA s. 148). A non-resident corporation whose only nexus is the export of goods out of Canada is not required to register because its supplies are made outside Canada or zero-rated; registered non-resident suppliers of electronic services may utilise the simplified ETA s. 241.1 registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 5 to 28 of the Excise Tax Act applies when a GST-registered supplier sells or transfers goods to a non-resident and the goods are shipped directly by the supplier by common carrier to a destination outside Canada, or the non-resident purchaser exports the goods within the statutory timeline (generally 30 days when exported by the supplier's carrier, or 60 days when exported by a carrier retained by the purchaser, with strict documentary identification of the exporter and destination). Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 when received exclusively for consumption outside Canada, provided the service is not performed in relation to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada and is not supplied to a non-resident individual present in Canada. For wholesale SSD/CPU trade, the exporter must evidence ownership, hand-off to an international carrier, shipment routing outside Canada and receipt of settlement from the non-resident buyer; sales made to Canadian resident intermediaries or to foreign buyers who take delivery inside Canada attract GST at 5%.

  • Commercial Invoice with HS classification (HS 8471.70 for solid-state storage devices; HS 8542.31-8542.33 for processors) and INCOTERMS terms

Canada does not operate a mandatory continuous transaction control (CTC) regime, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or government-side invoice validation for GST/HST. The CRA mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns for non-individual registrants for reporting periods beginning after 1 January 2023, filed via GST/HST NETFILE or MyBA; paper returns are accepted only on concession. Electronic payment is required for large CRA remittances. E-invoicing is evolving voluntarily under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard based on PEPPOL. For exports, the CBSA CARM Portal is the single electronic window for commercial accounting, and export cargo data (ACI for air, CCS for marine, eManifest for highway/rail where applicable) must be cleared before or at departure; the B13A Export Declaration is filed electronically and forms the basis of CBSA export statistics.

The GST/HST refund review programme and CBSA Trade Compliance Verification jointly audit export zero-rating claims. Substantiation failures cause the zero-rating to be denied with full refund clawback, assessment of net tax, arrears interest under ETA s. 298 and a false-statement penalty under ETA s. 285(1) equal to the greater of CAD 1,000 and 25% of the tax that would have been payable absent the false claim. Gross negligence or fraudulent billing structures additionally attract ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the income tax avoided, criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239 and ETA s. 327 with fines between 50% and 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Repeated or sham export invoicing in the semiconductor wholesale sector may be referred to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada; for false documentation submitted to CBSA, the Customs Act also authorises seizure, monetary penalties and criminal sanctions. The 180-day repatriation benchmark is not a statutory deadline in Canadian tax law, but is retained as a treasury and transfer-pricing control: CRA expects payment terms and settlement timing to reflect arm's length commercial behaviour, and unrepatriated sale proceeds may be recharacterised as shareholder loans, deemed dividends or Part XIII taxable payments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bank of Canada Act impose no exchange-control permit, approval quota, or repatriation licensing for dividends, management fees, loan repayments, or capital withdrawals. The material frictions for a non-resident-owned BC trader are: (i) Part XIII Income Tax Act (Canada) withholding on outbound remittances (25% dividends, 25% statutory interest/royalties subject to treaty relief; arm's-length interest may be exempt under the Interest Act carve-outs); (ii) transfer-pricing documentation under s.247 ITA for related-party cross-border transactions; and (iii) FINTRAC reporting of all electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. No currency conversion quota or non-resident deposit restriction applies to the SSD/CPU wholesale brokerage model.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no repatriation ratio on export proceeds realized by a Canadian-resident exporter. Export receivables may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, CNY, or other foreign currency in OSFI-supervised accounts. The only compliance obligations are (i) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting for each wire of CAD 10,000 or above, and (ii) Statistics Canada / Bank of Canada balance-of-payments and international investment position surveys (monthly, quarterly, or annual) when the entity is selected, given the cross-border payment volume characteristic of semiconductor brokerage.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank licensing and supervision and with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border transaction surveillance; Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration authority because the Canadian dollar operates under a fully floating, convertible regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as the primary treasury rail and Medium as a supplementary outbound vendor payment channel. Canada does not recognize the EU EMI passport, and foreign digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) are treated as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) required to register with FINTRAC; they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions and cannot legally host the capital escrow rail or L/C operations. An EMI is acceptable only for low-value operational disbursements under CAD 10,000 per instruction, and the bank's compliance desk will apply an enhanced UBO and source-of-funds review to any wire sourced from or destined to an EMI account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank treasury aggregation) from the parent's bank to the BC corporation's Tier-1 account. Mandatory wire elements: beneficiary account with Canadian institution and transit numbers, recipient BIC, the 9-digit Canada Revenue Agency business number (BN) in field 72, and narrative 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION FOR WORKING CAPITAL - SSD/CPU WHOLESALE TRADING; NO GOODS SHIPPED; NON-RESIDENT DIRECT INVESTMENT'. The receiving bank converts at the Bank of Canada daily rate. No prior exchange-control approval is required, but contributions exceeding CAD 1,000,000 or triggering a BC Registry paid-up capital filing will subject the entity to Statistics Canada inward direct investment position reporting and FINTRAC large-value wire reporting.Three compliant clearing rails for SSD/CPU procurement settlement: (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer to the exporter's account with invoice number, Incoterms (CFR/FCA), and HS 8471/8542 reference in the narrative; (2) Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP600 (MT700 issuance, typically 30-90 days sight with an inspection certificate condition) opened at the BC trade finance desk; (3) Escrow Inspection Wire, where the bank holds funds in a segregated sub-account and releases only upon presentation of a third-party inspection certificate (SGS/TUV) and conformed bill of lading. Best practice for semiconductor brokerage is a 2-of-3 release condition (bank, buyer, and independent quality inspection house), with documentary collection (D/P 30 days) adopted for established counterparty relationships to reduce L/C charges.USD/CAD Vanilla FX Forwards (unlimited tenor, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex, permitted only against identified trade payables or receivables)FX Swaps (spot-forward or forward-forward rollovers) for managing USD working capital timing mismatches between procurement and resale cyclesCross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (USD borrowing versus CAD operating liquidity) subject to the bank's derivatives product approval and documentary proof of underlying trade exposureEuropean-Style Currency Options (premium settled, buy-side only) providing downside protection on USD payables without naked option writingNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) permitted only for settlement-inconvertible supplier currencies (e.g., restricted RMB/CNH or KRW market access) and only upon documented exception approved by the bank's risk committee and the BC treasury policy committee

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium as the primary treasury rail and Medium as a supplementary outbound vendor payment channel. Canada does not recognize the EU EMI passport, and foreign digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) are treated as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) required to register with FINTRAC; they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions and cannot legally host the capital escrow rail or L/C operations. An EMI is acceptable only for low-value operational disbursements under CAD 10,000 per instruction, and the bank's compliance desk will apply an enhanced UBO and source-of-funds review to any wire sourced from or destined to an EMI account.
