Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST registration and Business Number portal (CRA My Business Account / Represent a Client); British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration via eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a Canadian Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1/RC1C or through the CRA online registration portal; for GST/HST, a non-resident must apply using Form GST494 (Application for Non-Resident GST/HST Registration) after assessing the CAD 30,000 worldwide small-supplier threshold; BC PST registration for out-of-province sellers is handled separately through the BC Ministry of Finance PST Registration portal, generally required when the entity makes taxable sales or leases in British Columbia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a Business Corporation incorporated federally or provincially is a separate taxpayer and does not qualify for pass-through taxation under the Income Tax Act.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canadian federal tax law; available elections include the GST/HST net tax election, the election to use the rebate method for non-residents, section 217 election for non-residents to file GST/HST returns, and provincial PST registration elections; hybrid mismatch rules under section 18.6 of the Income Tax Act require appropriate consequential adjustments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 worldwide annual taxable supplies of the non-resident; BC PST: no general PST small-supplier exemption threshold, registration required for taxable sales or leases in British Columbia arising from carrying on business or systematic solicitation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of crude oil and refined marine fuel products are zero-rated under section 1 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier exports the goods and possesses documentary evidence reasonably showing that the goods were removed from Canada, normally within 60 days of delivery or payment; qualifying services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated under section 7 of Schedule VI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A/B13 or Canadian Export Reporting System electronic declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, rail, or truck manifest) evidencing physical removal from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract evidencing sale of marine fuel oil or refined petroleum products to a non-resident
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of foreign-currency payment received in Canada

No real-time e-invoicing fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada; CRA permits electronic invoicing and generally mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns for registrants. BC PST returns are filed electronically through eTaxBC.

CRA may reassess and deny zero-rating if export substantiation is inadequate, resulting in uncollected GST plus interest and penalties. False statements and fraudulent GST/HST claims attract penalties under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act (up to 20% of net tax plus interest), and egregious export schemes may trigger criminal prosecution and full retrospective clawback of refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible; Canada maintains no exchange-control regime on either current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance is not subject to FX approval, but non-resident dividend withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act applies (25% statutory, treaty-reducible). All cross-border fund movements require FINTRAC electronic fund transfer reporting and income-tax documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign-currency balances; only standard Customs/Statistics Canada export declarations and accrual-based foreign-exchange gain/loss recognition for tax purposes are required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore EMIs. Canadian deposit-taking is reserved for banks and regulated credit unions under the Bank Act; any money-services activity requires FINTRAC MSB registration. Offshore EMIs may serve only as supplementary payment corridors, never as principal clearing or custody rails, and must operate through a Canadian Schedule I bank or a FINTRAC-registered MSB payer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate value of transactions with non-resident related parties in the tax year (mandatory Form T106); contemporaneous Local/Master File documentation is expected where transactions are material (CRA Information Circular 87-2R and OECD Chapter V); no de-minimis threshold for the arm's-length penalty.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulation in Canada; tax residence and PE are determined by factual management and control and operational presence. CRA applies GAAR (s.245) and, for treaty benefits, Limitation-on-Benefits/principal-purpose tests. Shell entities with no BC office, bank account, staff, or business risk are presumptively attacked; substance documentation (contracts, risk allocation, decision records) is essential for cross-border fuel trades.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: on indictment, fine up to 200% of evaded tax and/or 5 years' imprisonment; summary conviction fine up to C$50,000 and/or 2 years; civil reassessment with s.163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax plus compound prescribed-rate interest.

Director personal liability: Directors prosecuted as principals (Criminal Code s.21); personal restitution orders and asset-freezing orders (s.462.33); Criminal Code s.380 fraud over C$5,000 punishable by up to 10 years; travel bans imposed as bail/orders.
Shell Company / GAAR / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment of the entire tax benefit; penalty under s.163.2 for false statements (up to C$100,000 plus 100% of the misstated tax); denial of treaty withholding reduction; revocation of BC Business Corporations Act registration; T1134/T1135 penalties for undisclosed foreign affiliates/property.

Director personal liability: Directors personally assessed under s.227.1 for unremitted GST/HST, PST, and payroll; prosecution for participation in the sham arrangement and criminal forfeiture of diverted property.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Income inclusion adjustment of the full net transfer-pricing adjustment; s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the net adjustment; late T106 filing penalty C$100/month capped at C$2,500; interest accrual on reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: No direct statutory TP penalty on directors, but wilful false T106 filings supporting fraud charges; unremitted taxes flowing from TP adjustments remain director-assessable under s.227.1.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalty up to C$1,000,000 per violation for a corporation under the PCMLTFA; Criminal Code s.462.31 money-laundering offence punishable by up to 10 years' imprisonment; account freeze and forced dissolution risk.

Director personal liability: Directors/officers individually liable up to C$500,000 in FINTRAC AMPs; criminal charges for laundering proceeds of crime with extradition and travel-ban exposure.
Failure to Remit Withholding Taxes

Statutory sanction: CRA director-liability certificates; 100% interest and penalty on unremitted amounts; immediate collection via garnishment/seizure; NR4 late-filing penalties and criminal non-remittance charges.

Director personal liability: Joint and several personal liability under s.227.1 without limitation or corporate-veil protection; personal bankruptcy does not discharge these amounts; travel bans and asset-restraint orders issued to enforce collection.
