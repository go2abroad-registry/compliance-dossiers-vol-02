Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a BC business corporation is a separate taxable entity under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the BC Income Tax Act. No pass-through eligibility exists. A Canadian-resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident-owned corporation carrying on business in Canada is taxed on Canadian-source business income through a permanent establishment. Federal general corporate rate is 15% after provincial abatement, plus BC general corporate income tax at 12%; a Canadian-controlled private corporation may use the small business deduction (federal 9% + BC 2%) on active business income up to CAD 500,000. No tax-exempt zone applies to bulk cement clinker wholesale.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election or US-style entity classification. Available elections relevant to this structure: (1) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156 election between closely related corporations to treat inter-corporate supplies of taxable property as otherwise than by way of sale, eliminating GST; (2) GST/HST Quick Method of accounting for registrants with annual taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000; (3) ETA s. 141.2 election to allocate input tax credits on mixed-use inputs; (4) hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4-18.6, effective for payments after July 1, 2022) impose deduction disallowance with no elective relief absent ministerial designation; (5) BC PST offers no election mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold). For the non-resident wholesaler, only Canadian-source taxable supplies count; zero-rated exports count as taxable supplies for the threshold test. BC PST: mandatory registration upon making taxable retail sales in BC with no general threshold for vendors with a BC place of business; remote sellers exceeding CAD 10,000 in annual BC taxable sales must register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of bulk portland cement clinker is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 if the goods are exported by or on behalf of the supplier in the course of commercial activity. Conditions: (a) the supplier delivers the goods to a common carrier (ocean vessel, rail, or truck) for export; (b) the bill of lading names a non-resident buyer as consignee; (c) the goods depart Canada in the same commercial transaction; and (d) the supplier retains adequate proof of export. Ancillary services supplied to a non-resident for use entirely outside Canada may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7. BC PST does not apply to goods shipped by common carrier to a destination outside BC; for domestic wholesale sales to other PST registrants, a valid BC PST resale exemption certificate must be obtained (Form FIN 427).

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no digital stamping, and no API-based invoice approval. CRA accepts standard electronic invoices and records. Every GST registrant must issue an invoice containing the registrant's BN, transaction date, and tax charged when consideration exceeds CAD 30. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or approved EDI for registrants with annual taxable revenues exceeding CAD 1,500,000. Export declarations must be transmitted electronically to CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) using CUS-EXP-01 before the ocean vessel is loaded; no visa or stamp is issued for commercial export documents.

CRA GST auditors test export substantiation transaction-by-transaction; inadequate documentation results in reversal of zero-rating and assessment of 5% GST on gross consideration, plus compound daily arrears interest (prescribed corporate rate, reset quarterly, typically 7%-9%). The normal reassessment window is 4 years from the filing date (ETA s. 298), extended to 7 years for misrepresentation or fraud. Penalties: failure to register attracts the greater of CAD 250 and 1% of consideration, increased by 25% per full month of default (maximum 12 months); net tax understatement due to carelessness attracts 6% plus 4% per month on the shortfall; gross negligence or false statements attract the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of tax avoided (ETA s. 285); fraudulent export filings are indictable offences under Criminal Code s. 380, punishable by up to 14 years imprisonment; directors and officers may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST under ETA s. 323 where they knew or ought to have known of the failure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible with no exchange controls under the Currency Act and the repealed Exchange Control regulations. Both current account and capital account transactions are generally free from approvals or restrictions. Profit remittance to non-residents is permitted without exchange-admin approval; however, dividends and certain payments to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally 25%, subject to applicable income tax treaty reduction). No Central Bank approval is required for inward equity investment or outward trade-related transfers, unless sector-specific restrictions, sanctions, or Investment Canada Act review thresholds are triggered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency receipts into CAD. Exporters in British Columbia may hold export receipts in foreign currency accounts with Canadian chartered banks. For Canadian tax and GST/HST reporting, foreign currency amounts must be converted to CAD using generally acceptable exchange rates; however, no percentage or timeline conversion mandate applies under Canadian federal or provincial law.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering reporting to Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium viability as a sole primary banking rail for a Canadian-domiciled wholesale entity. Non-resident electronic money institutions (EMIs) and unlicensed fintech payment firms generally cannot provide Canadian clearing directly without registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act as a money services business, and they must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act regime administered by the Bank of Canada when operational. A credible structure is to use a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank for the core corporate treasury account and to integrate a regulated fintech partner solely for expedited cross-border foreign currency settlement. Direct use of an unregulated offshore EMI exposes the entity to FINTRAC reporting, sanctions screening, and banking integrity risks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital injection must be effected via a standard SWIFT MT103 wire from the foreign parent/investor to the corporate CAD-denominated operating account or capital escrow account held with a Schedule I bank. Canada does not require a statutory foreign exchange purpose code, but the remitter should include a precise remittance information field (field 70/Remittance Information) such as 'CA_CORP share subscription - injection per board resolution dated YYYY-MM-DD' to establish traceability. The receiving bank will perform sanctions screening, FATF-compliant originator/beneficiary verification, and FINTRAC reporting for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more. For inbound foreign direct investment, Investment Canada Act notification may be required if the transaction exceeds the review threshold, and the bank may request evidence of the completed or planned filing. All inbound transfers should be pre-notified to the compliance relationship manager to avoid unnecessary hold or refusal.Outbound trade settlements for bulk portland clinker purchases and vessel freight payments should use SWIFT MT103 corporate-to-supplier wires for open account transactions. For documentary credit transactions, the appropriate rail is a commercial letter of credit issued under UCP600, transmitted via SWIFT MT700 (issue) / MT707 (amendment), with settlement via SWIFT MT202/MT202COV for bank reimbursements. Domestic Canadian-dollar liquidity transfers between branches or with Canadian counterparties can be cleared over Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the newer Real-Time Rail (RTR), while USD-denominated trade settlements should be cleared via CHIPS/Fedwire through the bank's US clearing correspondents. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory dollar threshold requiring a Local File or Master File under the Income Tax Act; documentation must be prepared on a reasonable-efforts basis for transactions exceeding CAD 1 million in certain categories for penalty purposes. For multinational groups with significant Canadian operations, CRA's Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed annually when the aggregate value exceeds CAD 1 million or when share/loan transactions exceed specified balances.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply EU-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the general anti-avoidance rule and transfer-pricing rules require genuine economic substance, demonstrable business purpose, and arm's-length pricing for intercompany transactions; the CRA may recharacterize arrangements lacking commercial substance and assess penalties of 10% of the net adjustment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act and Criminal Code; fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, restitution orders, and up to 5 years imprisonment per count.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions, GST/HST, and unpaid corporate taxes under the Income Tax Act, Excise Tax Act, and BC Business Corporations Act; civil restitution orders, asset freezes, travel bans under tax enforcement, and potential imprisonment for contempt or fraud convictions.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment plus a 10% penalty on the net adjustment for gross negligence; interest compounded daily; denial of deduction for documentation failures.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they directed, assented to, or acquiesced in the misstatement; CRA civil collection proceedings against personal assets.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the entity under the general anti-avoidance rule; reallocate income to the true economic owner; reassess penalties up to 25% of the tax benefit; dissolution under BC law for non-compliance with annual reports.

Director personal liability: Nominee or passive directors remain responsible for corporate compliance, tax filings, and truthful information; personal liability arises for authorizing sham arrangements, with restitution and cancellation of director authorization under the BC Business Corporations Act.
Anti-Money Laundering / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties to CAD 10,000,000 per violation for entities; criminal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; freezing and forfeiture of property.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or fail to prevent money laundering can be prosecuted; personal asset seizure, travel restrictions pending charges, and custodial sentences.
