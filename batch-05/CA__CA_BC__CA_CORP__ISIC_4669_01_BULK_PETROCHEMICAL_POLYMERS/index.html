Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance for storage occupancy is required under the British Columbia Fire Code, including compliance with the plastic-storage height, spacing, and sprinkler requirements of NFPA 400 for combustible solids; the local fire authority must issue an occupancy or fire inspection certificate for the warehouse. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — national tax authority for federal income tax and GST/HST, with principal digital portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html); GST/HST returns filed via GST/HST NETFILE; British Columbia provincial sales tax (PST) is administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through the Tax Central/eTaxBC portal (https://etax.gov.bc.ca/).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies that trigger registration must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' with the CRA or by completing the GST/HST registration questions at the time of federal incorporation. Registration is mandatory within 30 days after the entity ceases to be a small supplier (i.e., once taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold) under Excise Tax Act (ETA) sections 238-240. A non-resident with no permanent establishment in Canada may also voluntarily register under ETA section 240 if it makes taxable supplies in Canada; the CRA may in that case require security under ETA section 307. For British Columbia, the entity must separately register for PST with the BC Ministry of Finance through Tax Central before making taxable retail sales or leasing tangible personal property in the province; there is no BC PST small-supplier threshold for in-province retail sales.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Canadian business corporation (federally or provincially incorporated, including BC) is a separate taxable person subject to federal Part I corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act (ITA) and BC provincial corporate income tax under the BC Income Tax Act. Canada does not apply pass-through or US-style check-the-box classification to business corporations; income is taxed at the legal entity level in accordance with ITA section 2. A corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident in Canada under ITA section 250. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Canadian business corporation (federally or provincially incorporated, including BC) is a separate taxable person subject to federal Part I corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act (ITA) and BC provincial corporate income tax under the BC Income Tax Act. Canada does not apply pass-through or US-style check-the-box classification to business corporations; income is taxed at the legal entity level in accordance with ITA section 2. A corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident in Canada under ITA section 250. Canada does not operate tax-exempt zones or special territorial tax-holiday regimes that would alter the standard corporation tax classification.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election for corporations; classification follows statutory incorporation form. Available elections relevant to a Canadian wholesale distribution corporation include: (i) ITA section 85 rollover election (Form T2057) to transfer eligible property into the corporation on a tax-deferred basis at incorporation; (ii) ITA section 22 election (Form T2201) to have cumulative doubtful debts treated as realized on a sale of accounts receivable; (iii) ITA section 256(1) associated-corporation agreement (Form T2013) to allocate the small business deduction limit among associated corporations; (iv) GST/HST elections under ETA section 156 (close-associate election, Form A-1) and ETA section 157 (joint election for nil consideration) to treat intra-group supplies as not taxable and to suppress invoicing obligations between closely related registrants; and (v) hybrid-mismatch rules effective for certain arrangements from 1 July 2023 and 1 October 2023 under ITA sections 18.2, 18.3 and 18.3(8) and related regulations, which include prescribing elections and transitional relief for cross-border arrangements involving fiscally transparent entities or permanent establishments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST small-supplier threshold under ETA section 148(1); registration becomes mandatory within 30 days of exceeding the threshold under ETA sections 238-240. A registered exporter can still fully recover input tax credits associated with zero-rated export supplies and can claim refunds of GST paid on domestic purchases. BC PST has no registration exemption threshold for in-province retail sales; a wholesaler making retail sales or leases of tangible personal property in BC must be registered and charge 7% PST before the activity commences.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available on tangible goods (e.g., virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules) supplied pursuant to a contract of sale where the supplier ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada, or where the goods are exported by the recipient or at the direction of the recipient in accordance with ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 1, provided the goods are exported within the prescribed period (generally 60 days after delivery) and the supplier maintains proof of export acceptable to the CRA. Services supplied to a non-resident who is not in Canada at any time the service is performed, and where the service is not performed in respect of real estate or tangible personal property located in Canada, are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7. The exporter must retain independent corroboration of physical export: customs export declarations, bills of lading, carrier waybills, warehouse gate records, terminal outbound reports, and foreign-currency remittance evidence.

  • CBSA export declaration (B13A/CARM Export Report) — mandatory when the export value is CAD 30,000 or more or the commodity is controlled/regulated; must include HS code, customs jurisdiction codes, exporter/importer identifiers, and quantity in metric tonnes
  • Commercial invoice (with INCOTERMS, detailed polymer grade and commodity description, quantity, unit price in CAD or agreed currency, and itemized GST/PST lines where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill issued by the ocean carrier or freight forwarder showing the named foreign consignee, discharge port, and final country of destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds through the banking channel within the 180-day repatriation window
  • Packing list and weighbridge or terminal receipt (third-party evidence of quantity and physical movement from the Canadian storage facility or port of exit)
  • Warehouse outbound report or inventory removal log evidencing segregation of exported product from the Canadian domestic supply chain

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal-clearance mandate, government digital stamping, or mandatory API pre-clearance for invoices at the GST/HST level. E-invoicing is voluntary and commonly uses PEPPOL/PAN-XML or EDI-ASC X12 formats; the CRA accepts paper, PDF, or structured electronic records provided the ETA section 286 record-keeping requirements are met (generally six years of retention, with certain exceptions for non-residents). On the export side, the CBSA operates the CARM Export Reporting portal (successor to B13A/CAED), requiring advance electronic export declarations for goods valued at CAD 30,000 or more or for controlled/regulated products. GST/HST registrants with total annual revenue exceeding CAD 1 million must file returns and remit amounts electronically via GST/HST NETFILE and the My Payment portal. BC PST returns and payments are submitted through the eTaxBC/Tax Central portal, with no per-invoice government stamping requirement.

The CRA audits export zero-rating claims with heightened scrutiny of documentary proof of physical export and of arm's-length realization of export proceeds. Where evidence is inadequate, the CRA reassesses the supply at 5% GST on the full invoice value, disallows input tax credits connected with the export, and imposes compounded daily interest under ETA section 281 at the prescribed rate (FTARR plus 2 percentage points, approximately 9% per annum in the current rate cycle). False statements or omissions attributable to culpable conduct attract a penalty of 25% of the tax amount under ETA section 285, and gross negligence attracts a 50% penalty under ETA section 285.1. In egregious cases, the CRA may prosecute under Criminal Code section 380 for fraud over CAD 5,000, punishable by imprisonment of up to 14 years and fines. The ordinary GST reassessment period is 4 years from the filing due date under ETA section 298, extended to 7 years where misrepresentation is shown, and unlimited in cases of fraud or wilful default. Product diverted onshore after a zero-rated export is subject to full self-assessment of GST/HST and BC PST, plus interest, penalties, and potential suspension or revocation of registration privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. There are no exchange-control restrictions on capital inflows, dividend remittances, interest payments, or profit repatriation by non-resident shareholders of a British Columbia business corporation. Non-resident ownership of CA_CORP is permitted without prior regulatory approval. Cross-currency transactions in USD, EUR, CNY, and other major currencies may be settled freely through authorized chartered banks and reporting dealers. The only compliance gates are FINTRAC reporting thresholds (e.g., records and reporting for electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, large cash transaction reports) and OSFI prudential requirements on the banking counterparty. Profit remittances are subject solely to corporate tax compliance and, where applicable, Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents under the Income Tax Act (Canada).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exported goods and proceeds are captured for statistical purposes via the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration (B13A) and Statistics Canada trade reporting, and foreign currency accounts may be held with Schedule I banks or authorized foreign banks; full retention of proceeds in the currency of invoicing is permitted without a conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); regulatory oversight of financial institutions is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Department of Finance Canada; cross-border transaction reporting and AML enforcement rest with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no centralized foreign-exchange control authority analogous to a dedicated exchange administration; FX flows are governed by the Currency Act, the Bank Act, and general financial-sector regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., UK FCA-authorised e-money institutions or EU PSD2 licences) are not licensed as banks under the Bank Act (Canada) and cannot hold CA_CORP's core operating liquidity or issue documentary credits. They may be used only as ancillary payment-corridor tools if registered as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada, provided settlement ultimately clears through a Payments Canada member bank or a Schedule I bank; sole reliance on an off-shore EMI would fail the corporate treasury and LC issuance requirements for bulk petrochemical trade.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfers into CA_CORP's Canadian bank account are executed via SWIFT MT103 with a precise purpose code (e.g., 'f900' for other capital transfers, or 'EQUI' for equity capital injection depending on the correspondent bank's coding) and a narrative referencing the share-subscription agreement or shareholder loan. USD inbound transfers clear via the respondent US bank through Fedwire or CHIPS; CAD inbound wires clear through Payments Canada Lynx using the institution number (e.g., 003 for RBC, BMO 001), transit number, and the corporate account number. The bank requires LEI (Legal Entity Identifier) plus the completed FINTRAC EFT record for amounts of CAD 10,000 or more; funds are held in the Capital Escrow Account or Operating Account pending economic-substance verification and the filing of any required Form under the Investment Canada Act if the investor is a non-Canadian controlling party.Trade settlements for bulk polyethylene and polypropylene granular purchases are executed through irrevocable documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700, with MT707 amendments) issued by the Canadian bank in favour of foreign suppliers, accepted through the supplier's confirming bank, and settled via SWIFT MT103/MT202 payments. CAD domestic disbursements use Payments Canada Lynx RTGS; USD settlements use the bank's US correspondent via Fedwire/CHIPS. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Return must be filed no later than six months after the corporation's taxation year-end. For a December 31 year-end, the T2 due date is June 30. Any balance of federal and BC tax owing must be paid no later than two months after year-end (three months for a CCPC qualifying for the small business limit).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, the CRA will test economic substance through the general anti-avoidance rule (s.245 Income Tax Act), the pre-existing sham transaction doctrine, and treaty-shopping jurisprudence. A British Columbia corporation claiming treaty benefits or transfer-pricing margins must demonstrate real Canadian substance: an office, independent director(s), substantive purchase/sales contracts, risk-bearing capital, and local management/warehouse operations. A mere letterbox company will fail CRA tax-residency and permanent-establishment analysis, and profits may be reattributed to the ultimate beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under s.239 Income Tax Act, the penalty is a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and/or imprisonment for up to five years. The CRA will re-assess tax plus daily compound interest and may impose a s.163 gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understatement.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or ratify a false return are personally exposed to criminal liability; the Crown may seek restitution orders, and directors face disqualification under the BC Business Corporations Act. The CRA may also assert director's liability under s.227.1 for unremitted source deductions connected with payroll or deemed payments.
Transfer Pricing Failure / Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Under s.247(3), a 10% penalty applies to the net upward transfer-price adjustment if the taxpayer fails to provide complete and accurate documentation within 90 days of the CRA's written demand. The CRA will recharacterize or adjust the price to an arm's-length equivalent, assess additional Part I tax, BC tax, and interest, and may disallow interest deductions under the thin-capitalisation rule where debt-to-equity exceeds 1.5:1.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve transfer-pricing positions without a contemporaneous benchmarking report and who sign the T2 may be found negligent. Although s.227.1 does not generally apply to corporate income tax, it does make directors personally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding tax on cross-border dividends, interest, or royalties if the corporation defaults and required filings were made.
Shell Company / Sham and Sanctions / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: The CRA may disregard the shell under the sham doctrine and the GAAR, reattribute the true economic profits to the non-resident beneficial owner, and impose a 25% Part XIV branch tax on unremitted Canadian-source profit. Failure to file annual BC registry reports leads to administrative dissolution. Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, a corporate service provider or payment intermediary lacking AML registration is subject to fines of up to CAD 1,000,000 on summary conviction and CAD 5,000,000 on indictment, plus asset-freezing orders under the Canada Economic Action Plan? (referring to assets under sanctions).

Director personal liability: Directors of a dissolved shell company may be personally liable for company debts where the company acted despite being not able to pay its obligations. Directors can face personal criminal charges for money laundering, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to five years. The BC Business Corporations Act permits the court to order personal payment of corporate taxes and to prohibit a director from acting as a director for up to five years.
