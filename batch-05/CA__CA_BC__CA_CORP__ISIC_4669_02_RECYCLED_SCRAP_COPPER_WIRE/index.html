Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A B.C. company is statutorily required to maintain a registered office and a records office at a physical address within British Columbia at which process may be served; the registered office may validly be the office of a licensed B.C. registered agent or law firm holding a virtual-office service. However, for AML/CFT and commercial-substance purposes, a virtual-office-only structure is insufficient for a Medium-Risk Enhanced scrap-copper wholesale entity seeking documentary LC and Swift/TT banking rails, municipal scrap-metal dealer licensing, and FINTRAC compliance. The entity should maintain a physical leased or owned operating office or yard in British Columbia with staff, records, and operational infrastructure proportionate to the stated cross-border consignment business.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a British Columbia business corporation. Common shares may be issued without par value for such consideration as the directors determine. No mandated paid-up capital escrow account is required by the Business Corporations Act; however, if shares are issued for property or past services, directors must comply with fair-value consideration rules and record the valuation in the company's records.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST registration and Business Number issuance via My Business Account portal; British Columbia Ministry of Finance – Provincial Sales Tax administration via eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) and, if applicable, a GST/HST account. Application is made online via My Business Account (with appropriate authorization), by mail, or by fax using Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number'. The entity must provide corporate documents, proof of non-resident status, and details of Canadian commercial activities. If conducting taxable activities in British Columbia, a separate PST account must be obtained through the Province of BC's eTaxBC system, supported by statutory declaration and business identification documents.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) and, if applicable, a GST/HST account. Application is made online via My Business Account (with appropriate authorization), by mail, or by fax using Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number'. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Canadian business corporation is subject to entity-level federal and provincial income tax. Available statutory elections include the GST/HST closely related corporation election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act, the functional currency election under subsection 261(2) of the Income Tax Act, and the annual corporate tax year election under section 249.1. No US-style check-the-box regime exists; hybrid mismatch rules in sections 12.5 and 12.6 of the Income Tax Act may restrict certain entity classification outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) over any 12-month period; PST: no small-supplier threshold for registration, required as soon as the entity carries on business or makes taxable sales in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of shredded non-ferrous scrap copper wire qualifies as zero-rated under section 10 of Part V of Schedule IX to the Excise Tax Act when the goods are sold and delivered outside Canada. The supplier must retain documentary evidence establishing that the goods were physically exported, typically through a common carrier or by direct shipment, and that the sale was made to a non-resident purchaser. The GST/HST registrant may then claim input tax credits on purchased inputs and apply for net refunds where eligible.

  • B13 Export Declaration (Customs Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control regime. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via a CRA-approved service provider or directly through My Business Account. British Columbia PST returns are due through eTaxBC. There is no statutory API-based invoice clearance mandate, but suppliers must issue tax invoices containing the supplier's Business Number, GST/HST registration number, and, where applicable, PST registration number, and must retain electronic records for audit purposes.

CRA audits zero-rated export claims by requiring contemporaneous evidence of physical export and foreign payment. Failure to substantiate may result in disallowed zero-rating, reassessment of GST/HST, interest, and penalties. Civil penalties include up to 20% of any net tax amount for false statements or omissions, plus a separate $10,000 penalty for failure to provide documents. Willful evasion of GST or fraudulent export claims may lead to criminal prosecution, fines up to double the tax evaded, and imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks are permissible within the normal reassessment period, which is generally four years, or without time limit in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a market-determined floating exchange rate with full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. There are no statutory exchange controls, no central bank approval hurdles for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation, and no distinction between resident and non-resident capital movements for FX approval purposes. Payments in CAD or foreign currency must be made through a Canadian chartered bank or an authorized foreign bank branch. Transactions equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC as large cash or electronic funds transfer reports. Cross-border dividend and profit remittances are subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, but not to FX authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory surrender or conversion timeline on export proceeds. Exporters may retain receivables in any convertible currency or hold them in foreign-currency accounts. Mandatory compliance arises solely through customs/statistical channels: exports of shredded non-ferrous scrap copper wire must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) when the shipment value exceeds the applicable threshold (CAD 2,000 for most destinations; CAD 2,500 for shipments to the United States). The export declaration must identify the commodity, net weight, value, and HS code 7404.00. Export proceeds should be reconciled with CAED filings to satisfy income tax and transfer-pricing documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — no separate central foreign-exchange administration; OSFI, FINTRAC, and CBSA exercise auxiliary regulatory and reporting mandates.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium (conditional). Offshore digital EMIs licensed in the UK, EU, or Singapore may be used for ancillary FX conversion and cross-border remittance, but they cannot serve as the primary CAD clearing or escrow account and are not eligible for documentary letter-of-credit issuance. Such entities must register as Money Service Businesses with FINTRAC before offering remittance services to Canadian clients. For trade settlement, escrow, or high-value clearing involving recycled scrap copper wire, a Canadian Schedule I bank is required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident shareholders must be initiated as SWIFT MT103 or SWIFT gpi payments to the corporate's CAD operating or escrow account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The beneficiary must be identified by full legal name, SWIFT/BIC, and Canadian account number. The remittance information field must clearly state 'unsolicited share capital contribution / free capital injection' and reference the corporate's legal name and account number; do not use commodity or trade descriptions to avoid misclassification as trade receivables. For injections greater than CAD 10,000, the receiving bank must file a FINTRAC EFT report. If the corporation issues shares for the injection, a CRA Schedule 50 or corporate records entry must document the paid-up capital. The originator's bank account name must match the shareholder's legal name to satisfy source-of-wealth verification.Trade settlements for cross-border purchases of shredded non-ferrous scrap copper wire may be executed using SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers or through commercial letters of credit. The Canadian bank issues the LC (MT700/MT710) in favour of the overseas seller; the confirming or advising bank negotiates documents; settlement occurs via MT103 or local clearing after compliance presentation. For TT payment, the corporate must provide a purpose narrative referencing the purchase contract, invoice date, cargo description 'shredded non-ferrous scrap copper wire', HS code 7404.00, country of origin, and shipment port. CAD payments use Lynx or Retail Settlement; USD payments clear over Fedwire/CHIPS via the Canadian bank's USD correspondent network. All trade disbursements must be supported by commercial invoices, transport documents, and evidence of goods received to satisfy FINTRAC and customs reporting obligations.Deliverable CAD/USD forward contracts with a Canadian Schedule I bank, settled via CLS or Payments Canada, used to hedge non-ferrous scrap sales proceeds.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or less-liquid currencies such as CNY, INR, or BRL, used only when the underlying trade settlement currency is not freely deliverable.Cross-currency swaps (CAD against USD or EUR) to convert foreign-currency operating liabilities into CAD term funding while stabilizing cash flow.Plain vanilla European-style FX options (call or put) purchased for hedging purposes only, with no speculative option-writing, subject to bank limits and derivatives qualification.
