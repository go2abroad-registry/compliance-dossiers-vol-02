Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST Division and Corporate Income Tax Branch; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (BC PST). Primary national portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account; BC portal: eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident corporation carrying on taxable supplies in Canada: (1) Determine compulsory GST/HST registration status under Excise Tax Act s.240 based on the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; if exceeded in any four consecutive calendar quarters, register via CRA Business Registration Online or by sending Form RC1 through an appointed Canadian representative. (2) Obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and separate GST/HST program account number (RT) for remitances and filings. (3) For British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST), file the BC PST registration application through eTaxBC before making taxable sales into or within BC; PST registration is independent of CRA and no small-supplier turnover exemption applies for ordinary retail/wholesale sellers. (4) For federal corporate income tax, resident corporations obtain an automatic BN upon incorporation; foreign corporations carrying on business in Canada must separately obtain a BN by Form RC1 and file a Canadian income tax return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – A Canadian or provincially incorporated business corporation is treated as a separate taxable entity under Income Tax Act, Part I, and is not eligible for pass-through taxation. Income is taxed at the corporate level and shareholders are taxed only when distributions, dividends, or deemed benefits are received.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election for entity classification. CRA determines foreign entity status under its legal-characteristics analysis (Income Tax Folio S4-F18-C1). Available targeted elections include: (a) the s.216(1) election for non-resident corporations with Canadian-source rental or timber income; (b) the s.217 election allowing non-resident employees to file a Canadian return; (c) associated-company elections affecting the shared small-business deduction limit; and (d) hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA s.18.2/18.3 that disallow certain deductible amounts involving transparent entities without providing elective relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies for GST/HST small-supplier exemption (or CAD 50,000 for charities and public institutions); no PST small-supplier threshold applies in BC – PST registration is required as soon as a seller carries on taxable business activities in BC unless the vendor has an export-only or otherwise exempt operation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, the export of tangible personal property (including granular elemental sulfur) qualifies for a 0% GST/HST rate if the goods are: (a) shipped outside Canada by the supplier to a foreign destination in the ordinary course of business; (b) delivered to a common carrier or a customs-bonded warehouse for export without use or consumption in Canada; or (c) sold to a non-resident who is not registered under GST/HST and is exported by the supplier or the non-resident within 60 days of delivery. Supplies of services rendered to non-residents, including offshore transport brokerage and freight-forwarding services related to the export of goods from Canada, will also be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, where the service is not tied to Canadian real property and not performed for a person resident in Canada. BC PST does not apply to export sales; the seller must retain evidence of export to claim the PST exemption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A or electronic equivalent) with Cargo Control Number
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or multimodal) evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/credit advice proving repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • CBSA Export Cargo Reporting (ECR) receipt or carrier acknowledgment
  • Proof of non-resident purchaser status (e.g., foreign tax identification number and registered address)

Canada does not currently require real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping. E-invoicing is permitted and CRA recognizes electronically generated records as sufficient for GST/HST audit. The federal government is progressively rolling out a voluntary Pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoicing standard for B2B and government procurement; no mandatory API-based fiscal clearance exists at the federal or BC provincial level. For PST, electronic sales records must be retained in an auditable format but no pre-approval or stamping is required.

CRA audits zero-rated export claims by verifying the documentary bundle; absent sufficient proof, GST/HST at the prevailing rate (5% federal, plus PST if applicable) is reassessed, together with interest and statutory penalties. The normal reassessment period is four years, extended indefinitely in cases of careless, false, or misleading statements. Gross negligence penalties under ETA s.285.1 and ITA s.163.2 equal 25% of the resulting tax or understated income. Fraudulent export-zero-rating schemes, false customs declarations, or failure to repatriate foreign currency within the prescribed period can trigger tax-evasion charges under ITA s.239 and ETA s.326, with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment for up to two years. CRA also evaluates transfer-pricing and commercial-substance aspects where an export sale is made to a related foreign buyer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for current and capital account transactions; no central bank approval is required for foreign exchange conversion, dividend remittance, or capital repatriation. Dividend/profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (generally 25% statutory, reduced under tax treaties, widely 5%–15%) and is not restricted by any FX licensing regime. Cross-border funds transfers above CAD 10,000 require FINTRAC reporting (Electronic Funds Transfer Reports) and can be blocked only under sanctions, terrorism-financing, or money-laundering provisions of the Criminal Code, United Nations Act, and Special Economic Measures Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender obligation exists in Canada. Granular elemental sulfur exporters are free to hold receipts in USD or any other convertible currency in domestic or offshore accounts. No mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline applies. Tax recognition follows the functional-currency election under section 261 of the Income Tax Act (Canada); FX gains are taxable and losses deductible on realization, and inventory may be valued in Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada's daily exchange rate if no functional-currency election is made.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is not centralized and resides with OSFI for prudential supervision of banks, FINTRAC for anti-money-laundering compliance reporting, and the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act; Canada accepted IMF Article VIII obligations in 1952 and maintains no Article XIV exchange controls
