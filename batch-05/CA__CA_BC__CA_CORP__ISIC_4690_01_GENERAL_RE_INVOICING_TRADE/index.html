Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Corporate Registry, Ministry of Finance, Province of British Columbia — operating via the Corporate Online electronic filing system (OneStop BC).

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act, SBC 2002, c. 57, as amended — ss. 10(1) (incorporation), 11 (memorandum), 51 (notice of articles), 177 (registered office), 190 (filings with registrar); supplemented by the Business Corporations Act General Regulation, BC Reg 278/2004.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation Application (Form 1) and Notice of Articles (Form 10), resulting in the electronic issuance of a Certificate of Incorporation by the Registrar. Official filing fee: CAD 350 (approx. USD 259), plus CAD 30 name reservation fee if a named company is used.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 34 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital requirement under the BCA; authorized capital may be one or more classes of shares without par value. No share capital escrow or government deposit is required for incorporation; non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the issued shares.

Funding & unlock path: WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety registration and clearance; BC Fire Code (Fire Safety Act) inspection and fire exit certification; building occupancy permit and structural load clearance for the leased commercial suite; municipal fire marshal inspection of heating, electrical, and emergency systems.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital requirement under the BCA; authorized capital may be one or more classes of shares without par value. No share capital escrow or government deposit is required for incorporation; non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the issued shares.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary digital portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO); for provincial BC matters: BC Ministry of Finance / BC Taxpayer Services (PST and Provincial Employer Health Tax registration separately through BC eTaxBC).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business or making taxable supplies in Canada must first apply for a federal Business Number (BN) — the statutory TIN for GST/HST, payroll, corporate income tax and import/export accounts. SOP: (1) Determine registrant status; non-resident is required to register for GST/HST where taxable supplies exceed the $30,000 CAD small supplier threshold on a worldwide basis or where the non-resident holds a permanent establishment in Canada; (2) Submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or use CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) with the foreign entity's legal name, home-jurisdiction corporate registration number, registered office address, and beneficial ownership particulars; (3) Obtain the 9-digit BN plus program accounts (RT for GST/HST, RC for corporate income tax, RP for payroll); (4) For a foreign corporation without a Canadian director, provide proof of home-country good standing; CRA may assign a non-resident account with a foreign address and no Canadian nexus; (5) For BC-only activities, also complete BC incorporation extra-provincial registration and obtain a BC PST number via Bureau de services / eTaxBC; no separate VAT ID exists — the BN/RT is the single GST/HST identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian business corporations are taxable entities under Part I of the Income Tax Act; Canada does not apply a check-the-box pass-through regime; a foreign LLC or limited partnership claiming non-resident status may be recharacterized under Regulation 9503 / s. 248.1 based on the 'all or substantially all' test, but the standard Canadian federal or provincial Business Corporation is taxed at the entity level regardless of shareholder residency; provincial BC corporate income tax applies to corporations with permanent establishment in BC at the same entity level).

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. Statutory elections relevant to a Canadian re-invoicing corporation include: (1) S. 216 Election under the Income Tax Act (ITA) permitting a non-resident with rental or timber income to file a return on a net basis instead of being subject to Part XIII withholding; (2) S. 216.1 election for a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada to base Canadian tax on its Canadian-source business income; (3) GST/HST 'substantially all election' between a registrant and non-resident clients under ETA s. 34.2 for drop-shipment and re-invoicing chains; (4) Financial institution/group elections under ITA s. 89(11) and GST/HST ETA s. 156/289; (5) Hybrid mismatch alignment: Canada applies BEPS Action 2-aligned rules — ITA s. 18.6 (overall interest expense rule), s. 18.7, s. 18.8 (dividend deductibility), s. 12.8/12.9 (withholding on hybrid instruments), and Regulations under s. 248.1 for foreign entity classification — no full check-the-box, but targeted elections may be made to characterize a foreign entity for domestic law purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA s. 148(1)(a); measured as the total worldwide taxable supply revenues in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and in the current calendar month; once the threshold is exceeded, registration is mandatory within 29 days; for non-residents making taxable supplies outside a permanent establishment, non-resident small suppliers are not required to register unless they solicit for sale, advertise, deliver, or accept orders in Canada. For re-invoicing trade where the Canadian entity makes zero-rated export supplies, registration may still be elected to claim input tax refunds although it is not mandatory for small suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Canadian re-invoicing and export trade intermediary may claim 0% GST/HST on exported goods under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 16 (supply of goods to a non-resident who is not a GST registrant where the supplier ships the goods outside Canada), s. 16(i) (goods delivered outside Canada without importation into Canada — applicable where the goods are located abroad at the time of supply), and s. 19–21 (supplies to prescribed non-residents for export). Offshore services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 if the supply is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the services are performed, and the non-resident is not a GST registrant. To substantiate, the exporter must hold documentation proving the goods were physically exported at the earliest possible time after the supply and that they were not diverted to Canadian use; where goods never enter Canada on a pure re-invoicing back-to-back model, the supplier must prove the goods were outside Canada throughout and the buyer is a non-resident; for export refunds, input tax credits (ITCs) on export-related overhead and procurements are available via the ordinary ITC regime in ETA s. 169, subject to the 'evidence of export' Regulations (ETA s. 24/25 and the Excise Tax Act Export Evidence Regulations).

  • Customs Export Declaration (CERS/CAED Form B13A — Canadian Export Reporting System transmission for shipments above the Statistics Canada export reporting threshold, including the exporter's BN, commodity H.S. code, destination, and value)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air/highway carriage document showing the consignor and consignee, port of loading in Canada or third-country, port of discharge, and shipment date evidencing the physical export movement)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign exchange bank advice or letter of credit settlement record demonstrating receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency from the non-resident buyer)

Canada imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime for B2B/GST/HST purposes; export and domestic invoicing may be issued on standard commercial formats with the minimum disclosure elements listed in ETA s. 221 and the GST/HST Information for Recipients of Taxable Supplies Regulations. The federal government has adopted the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL network (2023–2025 rollout) mandating e-invoicing for B2G invoices above procurement thresholds, using the Canadian PEPPOL Access Point registration; all CRA GST/HST returns for registrants with annual revenues above their prescribed threshold must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or a certified ERP integration. The CRA operates an invoice validation portal for B2G PEPPOL compliance; failure to file electronically may attract penalties under ETA s. 280.1.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted convertibility of the Canadian dollar for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no foreign-exchange controls, no central-bank approval gates, and no restrictions on the transfer of funds into or out of a British Columbia entity. The operative constraints on a re-invoicing hub are fiscal rather than monetary: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends and certain payments, at the 25% statutory rate or a lower treaty rate; (ii) transfer-pricing and permanent-establishment exposure under the Income Tax Act; and (iii) the requirement that all tax and GST/HST calculations be converted to Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender, mandatory conversion, repatriation timeline, or minimum-holding requirement for export proceeds. A BC-incorporated re-invoicing hub may retain full currency balances in its operational accounts. Compliance obligations are limited to: (i) FINTRAC reports for international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) annual foreign-income verification disclosures through CRA Form T1135 where the aggregate specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000; and (iii) consistent Canadian-dollar reporting of income and expenses at the Bank of Canada spot rate for tax and GST/HST purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the Canadian monetary authority. Foreign-exchange administration is decentralized; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) supervises cross-border funds-transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), while the Department of Finance Canada retains policy authority over sanctions, currency, and payment-system infrastructure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM — ADVISORY. Offshore digital EMIs and fintech payment institutions are not recognized as 'authorized foreign banks' under the Bank Act (Canada) and may not accept deposits from the Canadian public unless separately licensed. They can serve only as an ancillary settlement corridor for outbound payments to non-Canadian suppliers, never as the primary treasury rail. Mainstream Canadian Schedule I banks will apply heightened scrutiny, enhanced due diligence, and possible account restrictions to any transaction flowing through such EMIs. Primary liquidity, capital escrow, and cross-border clearing must remain exclusively on tier-1 Canadian banking rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocol: funds are transmitted by SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfer, or ISO 20022 payment message where supported, from the overseas parent's banking institution directly to the BC hub's tier-1 Canadian operational account. The payment must be accompanied by the approved capital-contribution or shareholder-loan purpose code prescribed by the receiving bank, plus a structured remittance field referencing the financing instrument. Supporting verification comprises: (i) a board resolution authorizing the capital injection; (ii) the share-allotment return filed with the BC Corporate Registry, or the signed intercompany loan agreement if structured as debt; (iii) a source-of-funds statement certified by the remitting bank; and (iv) confirmation of the Canada Revenue Agency business-number and GST/HST registration status. International wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reported to FINTRAC. Once cleared, funds may be credited to a capital escrow sub-account until the enhanced KYC verification file is accepted by the bank's compliance committee.Primary clearing rails for supplier settlements: (i) SWIFT MT103 priority telegraphic-transfer wires with the commercial-payment purpose code and explicit invoice reference for time-critical payments; (ii) irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank under UCP 600, either at-sight or usance 90-to-120-day tenor, for high-value initial transactions and new counterparties; (iii) documentary collections under URC 522 (D/P or D/A) for established low-risk repeat suppliers; and (iv) bank-proprietary trade portals for electronic handling of trade documents. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for services; CAD 5,000,000 for goods; or if total gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million, full documentation required.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific ESR regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions require genuine economic substance. Re-invoicing operations must demonstrate real business purpose, staff, and decision-making in Canada to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 50% of tax evaded, plus interest. Criminal conviction: fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they failed to withhold or remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for willful participation.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR may recharacterize transactions, disallow deductions, and impose penalties up to 50% of tax avoided. Criminal charges possible for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in a sham arrangement can be held personally liable for penalties and face criminal prosecution.
AML

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for individuals include fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for fines if they authorized or acquiesced to the non-compliance; potential travel bans and imprisonment for serious offenses.
