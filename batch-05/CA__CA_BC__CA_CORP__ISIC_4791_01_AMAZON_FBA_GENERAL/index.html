Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and BC Income Tax Act. Non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source income (territorial scope for non-residents).

Available election forms

No check-the-box or elective entity classification regime; classification is statutory. Except for procedural elections such as ITA s. 216 for net-basis taxation of Canadian rental income, there is no pass-through election. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 apply automatically and cannot be opted out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For GST/HST, CAD 30,000 in consideration for taxable supplies in Canada in a calendar quarter or four consecutive quarters (small supplier threshold). BC PST has no small-supplier monetary exemption; registration is required where a vendor has physical, agent, or directed-sales nexus in BC. Marketplace platform deeming rules may shift GST/HST collection obligations to operators such as Amazon.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act when exported in accordance with regulations and the supplier retains prescribed evidence such as CBSA Form B13A, bill of lading or air waybill, and commercial invoice showing the goods left Canada. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated when the recipient is not registered for GST/HST in Canada and the service is not primarily in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange settlement record

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoicing pre-clearance, or mandatory digital stamping regime. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1.5M must file electronically; voluntary Pan-Canadian Peppol-based e-invoicing is used for B2B transactions.

CRA may deny zero-rating, reassess within four years for ordinary errors and indefinitely for misrepresentation or fraud, and recover uncollected GST/HST plus interest and penalties. False statements or omissions in GST/HST returns may be subject to penalties under Exfise Tax Act s. 280 up to 250% of tax evaded, as well as criminal prosecution. Export evidence and accounting records must be retained for six years. Canada does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline; the operative controls are documentary evidence and accounting records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility in law and practice for both current and capital account items; Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and the Canadian dollar is free-floating. Profit remittance out of a British Columbia corporation is legally unrestricted, but four compliance gates apply: (i) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) imposes 25% withholding on dividends, interest, rents, or royalties paid to non-residents, treaty-reduced under the Canada-U.S. Income Tax Convention to generally 15% on portfolio dividends, 5% on a U.S. corporate shareholder owning 10% or more, and generally 0% on arm's-length interest; (ii) the corporation's bank, not the corporation, reports electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour period to FINTRAC; (iii) the payment must not breach sanctions lists administered under the Special Economic Measures Act and the Criminal Code; and (iv) corporate treasury policy must document the funding chain to avoid recharacterisation of shareholder loans as unremittable or tax-non-compliant equity. There is no repatriation permit, no currency allocation, and no central-bank clearance for outbound treasury payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export proceeds, including Amazon Seller Central payout receipts in CAD, USD, or other marketplaces. A Canadian exporter may retain proceeds in any currency. Ancillary compliance includes: (a) CBSA export declarations for physical exports from Canada above the current Customs Act reporting threshold of CAD 2,000; (b) for FBA inventory purchased offshore and shipped directly to Amazon fulfilment centres, the Canadian seller must act as importer of record and instruct a licensed customs broker, but no import licence is required for general merchandise; and (c) for Canadian income tax purposes, foreign-currency balances must be translated in the financial statements and gains/losses from retranslation of foreign-currency receivables and payables are taxable or deductible under subsection 39(1) of the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting jointly with the Department of Finance Canada on exchange-rate and monetary policy; Canada has abolished statutory foreign-exchange controls (the Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951). The operative regulatory counterparts are the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally incorporated banks, FINTRAC for cross-border transaction surveillance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and the Bank of Canada as administrator of the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) effective 1 November 2024.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility for settlement and FX conversion, subject to legal constraints. Airwallex (Canada) Ltd., Wise Solutions Canada Inc., and Payoneer Inc. (Canada branch) are all FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses and either hold or are in process of holding Retail Payment Activities Act registration with the Bank of Canada. They may lawfully hold client funds in safeguarded trust accounts at Schedule I banks, provide multi-currency accounts (USD, CAD, CNH), and connect to Amazon disbursement rails. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a deposit-taking institution under the Bank Act and cannot offer deposit insurance or term deposits; (ii) the RPAA registration obligation applies to any payment service provider operating in Canada, so a foreign EMI without Canadian registration cannot legally facilitate the seller's receipts; (iii) the EMI must remain a payment mechanical layer only - the BC corporation must remain the merchant of record and registered GST/HST registrant for supply under the Excise Tax Act, since the EMI is not a reseller and cannot alter place-of-supply; and (iv) if Amazon payouts are routed through an EMI, the seller should obtain an IRS W-8BEN-E and the EMI's 'local receiving account' details to evidence that Amazon is paying the Canadian entity and not an unregistered intermediary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder-loan protocol: (i) Corporate authority - a board resolution of the BC corporation authorising the share subscription or the shareholder loan, and confirmation that the Notice of Articles authorises the share class to be issued; BC law imposes no minimum paid-up capital, and the wire may be credited directly to the operational account or to a designated Capital Escrow Account. (ii) Wire standard - SWIFT MT103 containing field 50K (ordering customer: the foreign shareholder), field 59 (beneficiary: CA_CORP at the Canadian Schedule I bank, full account number and BIC), and field 70 (remittance information, e.g. 'SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - RESOLUTION DATED - SHARE CERTIFICATE NO. X' or 'LOAN PRINCIPAL UNDER PROMISSORY NOTE DATED'). For USD wires originating in the U.S., use Fedwire or CHIPS through the bank's U.S. affiliate; for CAD wires, use LVTS or the emerging Real-Time Rail. (iii) Purpose codes - Canada has no statutory purpose-code directory; the receiving bank internally classifies the credit as 'capital contribution' or 'intercompany loan' for AML purposes, and the sender should reference the transaction ID or loan agreement number. (iv) Verification and reporting - the bank collects the corporate KYC pack and, for any single or aggregated cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more in 24 hours, the bank files the FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report; the non-resident shareholder should provide Form W-8BEN-E or a treaty-residency certificate to support future interest or dividend withholding relief. (v) Tax-compliant credit - a shareholder loan must bear interest at the CRA prescribed rate to preserve deductibility and avoid deemed-benefit rules; share-subscription proceeds are not taxable and no stamp duty or capital duty applies; if the shareholder is a U.S. person, the issuance should comply with the BC Securities Act private-issuer exemption under NI 45-106 (restriction on transfer and a maximum of 50 security holders).Trade clearing rails: (i) Supplier T/T - SWIFT MT103 from the Canadian bank to the beneficiary supplier bank in USD, CNH, or CAD; field 70 must include the invoice number, purchase-order number, and the phrase 'PURCHASE OF INVENTORY - TRADE PAYMENT' to avoid misrepresentation; the bank applies OSFI-required sanctions screening, and FINTRAC EFT reporting triggers at CAD 10,000 or more in 24 hours. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility for settlement and FX conversion, subject to legal constraints. Airwallex (Canada) Ltd., Wise Solutions Canada Inc., and Payoneer Inc. (Canada branch) are all FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses and either hold or are in process of holding Retail Payment Activities Act registration with the Bank of Canada. They may lawfully hold client funds in safeguarded trust accounts at Schedule I banks, provide multi-currency accounts (USD, CAD, CNH), and connect to Amazon disbursement rails. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a deposit-taking institution under the Bank Act and cannot offer deposit insurance or term deposits; (ii) the RPAA registration obligation applies to any payment service provider operating in Canada, so a foreign EMI without Canadian registration cannot legally facilitate the seller's receipts; (iii) the EMI must remain a payment mechanical layer only - the BC corporation must remain the merchant of record and registered GST/HST registrant for supply under the Excise Tax Act, since the EMI is not a reseller and cannot alter place-of-supply; and (iv) if Amazon payouts are routed through an EMI, the seller should obtain an IRS W-8BEN-E and the EMI's 'local receiving account' details to evidence that Amazon is paying the Canadian entity and not an unregistered intermediary.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal and British Columbia general corporate income tax rate (15% federal Part I basic rate after provincial abatement and general rate reduction + 12% BC provincial rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on dividends paid or credited to a non-resident of Canada, subject to reduction under an applicable income tax convention.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in annual aggregate non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents requires mandatory Form T106 and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (local-file equivalent). A multinational group consolidated revenue threshold of CAD 750,000,000 triggers Master File and Country-by-Country Report filing obligations.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act, s.245), the common-law 'sham' doctrine, and residency rules based on central management and control. A Canadian e-commerce corporation that is a letterbox or 'post-box' entity, without real board activity, employees, contracts, or commercial decisions in Canada, is exposed to recharacterization by CRA. Amazon FBA storage alone in a foreign jurisdiction does not, without more, create a foreign permanent establishment; however, the substantive control of the seller must remain in Canada to sustain Canadian-resident status and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willegal Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under section 163(2) of the Income Tax Act of 50% of the understated tax; daily compound interest on the unreported balance; criminal conviction under section 239(1) for tax evasion carries imprisonment up to 5 years upon indictment and fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded. Repeated failure to file carries added s.162(1) penalties of 10% plus 2% per month to a 50% ceiling.

Director personal liability: Where the corporation is used as a mere instrumentality, the court and CRA will pierce the corporate veil; directors and officers who participated in the evasion become personally liable for the corporate tax debt, are subject to civil restitution proceedings, can be criminal defendants, and may face travel bans or seizure of passports under CRA collection measures.
Transfer Pricing Fraud / Non-Arm's-Length Arrangements

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment to an arm's-length compensation under s.247(2); transfer-pricing penalty under s.247(11) equal to 10% of the net upward adjustment where the taxpayer did not prepare adequate contemporaneous documentation. Failure to file T106 is an additional CAD 100-per-day penalty up to CAD 25,000; interest on the reassessment is mandatory.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer-pricing certificates or who orchestrate the artificial transfer of assets are personally liable under criminal fraud and conspiracy provisions. The corporate veil is pierced, allowing CRA to trace and seize the director's personal assets and to impose civil penalties equal to the unpaid corporate tax.
Shell Company / Sham Pure Device

Statutory sanction: The transaction or entity is disregarded under s.245 (GAAR), and income is reattributed to the actual controlling foreign person. The resulting deemed dividends or royalties to non-residents attract Part XIII withholding tax at 25%, plus gross-negligence penalties and interest. The sham determination is retroactive.

Director personal liability: Limited liability is lost when the corporation is no longer a genuine commercial enterprise. Shareholders/directors become personally liable for the full Canadian tax liability; CRA may register a lien against personal assets, impose an emigration/exit ban, and seek personal civil recovery where the controlling person has absconded with corporate funds.
Anti-Money Laundering / Foreign Asset Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Under s.162(7), T1135 non-filing or false reporting is subject to a penalty of CAD 2,500 per failure, with CAD 25,000 for gross negligence. Under the Criminal Code of Canada, money laundering carries a maximum fine of CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years; FINTRAC administrative penalties can reach CAD 500,000 for money-services businesses.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly divert or hide foreign financial assets are personally exposed to criminal money-laundering prosecution and civil liability. CRA can freeze their personal accounts, issue travel bans, and pierce the corporate veil where the corporation served as a laundering conduit; the director is jointly and severally liable for all unremitted source deductions and income tax arising from the scheme.
