Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca; corporate income tax and GST/HST filings via My Business Account. Provincial counterpart for indirect retail tax: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch — eTaxBC. Export declarations: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the Business Corporation federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act; no minimum capital requirement; non-resident ownership and non-resident directors are permitted. 2) Obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA via Business Registration Online (BRO) or, for non-resident applicants, Form RC1. 3) Register program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll if employees are engaged). 4) Register with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC for a BC PST account; remote sellers are required to register when taxable sales delivered into BC exceed CAD 10,000 in the preceding 12 months. 5) A non-resident registrant without a Canadian address must appoint an authorized representative with a Canadian contact address. 6) No statutory business licence is required for ISIC 4791 / NAICS 454110 internet DTC retail; banking AML risk tier is Low_Risk_Standard.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian Business Corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation (or a non-CCPC if non-resident controlled) subject to Part I federal income tax and BC provincial corporate tax on worldwide income. Combined headline rate: 27% (15% federal general + 12% BC general); 11% (9% federal small business deduction + 2% BC small business rate) on qualifying active business income up to CAD 500,000 where the corporation is a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC). Non-resident-controlled corporations cannot claim the SBD. No flow-through or check-the-box pass-through eligibility exists for domestic Canadian corporations. No tax-exempt zone applies in British Columbia.

Available election forms

Available elections include: Income Tax Act s. 85 rollover election (tax-deferred transfer of eligible property to a taxable Canadian corporation); s. 83(2) capital dividend election (tax-free capital dividend account distributions); s. 216 election for non-resident property income; Excise Tax Act s. 150 election for filing GST/HST via NETFILE; GST/HST quick method election for small suppliers (RC4614); s. 225.2 security election and joint elections for closely related corporations. Canada has no domestic check-the-box regime; entity classification is statutory. For cross-border structures involving a US LLC, the US Form 8832 check-the-box election is respected under Canada-US Treaty Article IV(6) only to the extent the LLC is treated as fiscally transparent. Canadian hybrid mismatch and excessive interest rules (Income Tax Act s. 18.2) apply mandatorily to payments made after July 1, 2022 and offer no elective exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s. 148 and Schedule IX, measured by taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident remote vendors selling taxable electronic commerce supplies to Canadian consumers must register once the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded in a 12-month period, subject to marketplace facilitator deeming rules that may shift GST/HST collection liability to platforms such as Shopify. BC PST: registration is mandatory for remote sellers when taxable sales delivered into British Columbia exceed CAD 10,000 in the preceding 12 months; no PST small-supplier exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, Excise Tax Act where the registrant exports the property 'as soon as reasonably practicable'; DTC e-commerce shipments to cross-border consumers must be supported by carrier export documentation and Incoterms placing transit risk outside Canada. Services supplied to non-resident recipients are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 where the services are not performed in connection with real estate located in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or services to be performed wholly in Canada. Input tax credits (ITCs) attributable to zero-rated export sales are fully recoverable, generating net GST refunds where ITCs exceed output tax. CRA administrative guidance ordinarily expects physical export of goods within 60 days of the supply.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) export declaration — ACI eManifest export filing
  • Commercial invoice / Canada Customs Invoice reflecting export delivery terms (FOB, Incoterms 2020)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier (courier POD records acceptable for small parcels)
  • Proof of payment from the non-resident buyer (SWIFT or banking remittance advice; Canada does not issue a statutory Inward Remittance Certificate)

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, tax-stamping, or mandatory API-inspection regime. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE on monthly, quarterly, or annual cycles; BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC. E-invoicing is voluntary, with the Pan-Canadian Peppol BIS 3.0 standard designated as the national interoperability framework. Registrants must retain invoices, books, and export evidence for six years under Excise Tax Act s. 286.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess output GST at 5%, with BC PST exposure where export substantiation is deficient. Normal reassessment window is 4 years, extended to 8 years for misrepresentation or fraud. Penalties: Excise Tax Act s. 285 false statements — up to 10% of net tax; s. 280 failure to provide information; s. 327(1) criminal fraud — fines up to CAD 250,000 and/or imprisonment. Interest at CRA prescribed rates accrues on clawed-back amounts. Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds; the 180-day figure is a data-model benchmark, not a legal condition of zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no statutory restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distributions, royalty payments, intercompany loans, or capital repatriation to non-residents. The capital account is entirely open; no licence, ministerial approval, or administrative clearance is required for inbound equity or debt funding into a British Columbia business corporation. An offshore non-resident investor may remit net profits and exit proceeds freely in CAD, USD, or any fully convertible currency, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax and transfer-pricing documentation. Settlement in non-fully-convertible emerging-market currencies, such as CNY, INR, IDR, or PKR, is available only through non-deliverable or narrowly permitted onshore channels governed by the counterparty jurisdiction's restrictions, which do not constitute Canadian exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and applicable Canadian federal law do not impose any statutory surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation timeline, or holding-structure requirement for export receipts. DTC retail sales proceeds received through Shopify Payments, PayPal, or Stripe may remain in foreign currency indefinitely, are recognized as operating revenue for income-tax purposes, and are subject only to FINTRAC cross-border currency reporting for amounts at or above CAD 10,000 and to ordinary accounting and transfer-pricing documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada (monetary authority, banknote issuance, wholesale clearing, and retail payment activity registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act); prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act; anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision of financial intermediaries is exercised by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration and no exchange-control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Foreign-origin digital EMI and payment providers, including Airwallex, Wise Business, and Payoneer, are legally classified in Canada as money services businesses or unregistered payment service providers and cannot take deposits or hold themselves out as a bank under section 545 of the Bank Act. As of 1 November 2024, the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) requires any payment service provider operating in Canada to register with the Bank of Canada and to safeguard end-user funds during the transitional window ending 1 November 2026; provincial MSB registration may also apply for operations in British Columbia. An offshore EMI may be used as a non-banking FX aggregator and vendor-payment aggregator, but the principal treasury account, customer settlement account, and capital escrow account must remain with a CDIC-insured Schedule I bank to maintain deposit protection, regulatory clarity, and clean audit trails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are compliantly routed via SWIFT MT103, or MT202 COV for interbank transfers, to the Schedule I bank's clearing infrastructure. The receiving bank identifies the beneficiary through Payments Canada standards: the three-digit institution number, five-digit transit number, nine to twelve-digit account number, and the receiving branch SWIFT/BIC code (for example ROYCCAT2, BOFCCAT2, TDCATTTT, CIBCCATT, or NABCCATT). The wire narrative and purpose code must unambiguously characterize the commercial intent, such as 'Capital contribution / share subscription', 'Shareholder loan / intercompany financing', or 'Escrow-funded project equity' and must include the BC company's fifteen-digit Business Number (BN-15) where required. Cross-border flows at or above the equivalent of CAD 10,000 trigger FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting; the account-opening file must identify all non-resident beneficial owners. Non-resident investors transferring from a foreign EMI or cryptocurrency venue will face enhanced due diligence; treasury policy therefore directs such investors to instruct a domestic bank transfer from their home banking institution or to use a licensed Canadian dealer for digital-asset conversion before wiring. Consumer card settlement from Shopify Payments, PayPal, or Stripe is recognized as operating revenue and is not a capital-injection channel; the treasury policy strictly prohibits depositing merchant settlement balances into the capital escrow account and reserves that account exclusively for restricted or escrowed funds.Outbound supplier and trade disbursements use three primary clearing rails. First, SWIFT MT103 direct credit transfers for supplier payables, with CAD domestic clearing through Payments Canada Lynx RTGS, USD clearing through New York Fedwire or CHIPS correspondent accounts, and EUR or GBP clearing through the respective local payment systems. Second, confirmed commercial letters of credit issued under ICC UCP 600 for high-value import purchases, with bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and insurance certificate examined by the issuing or confirming bank. Third, merchant-specific settlement rails managed by Shopify Payments, PayPal, and Stripe for refunds, chargebacks, settlement corrections, and partial-capture reversals. A DTC brand with a high volume of small-ticket Canadian refunds may use the Payments Canada Real-Time Rail or Interac e-Transfer for domestic vendor credits, while global supplier settlements above a defined USD-equivalent threshold are batched to reduce wire fees.
