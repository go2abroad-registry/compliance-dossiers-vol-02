Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available for Canadian business corporations; they are automatically taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Available elections include: GST/HST Section 156 election for closely related corporations (taxable supplies at nil value), functional currency election (ITA s. 261), capital cost allowance elections, and provincial partnership elections. Hybrid mismatch rules under BEPS Action 2 apply mandatorily; no elective hybrid mismatch regimes exist.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies for GST/HST registration (small supplier exemption); BC PST has no small supplier threshold – registration with the BC Ministry of Finance is mandatory before making retail sales of taxable goods in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by the supplier from Canada qualify for 0% GST/HST under Excise Tax Act s. 22. Services supplied to a non-resident may qualify under s. 23 if the service is not performed in connection with Canadian real property or goods and is not part of a commercial activity performed in Canada. The supplier must retain evidence of exportation within two years; for sales via TikTok Shop, the platform's payout records and shipping documents should be preserved.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) or electronic reporting in the Canadian Integrated Customs System
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment from Canada to foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice with Incoterms and foreign buyer name/address
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement showing foreign currency payment completion
  • Exporter's zero-rating certificate (e.g., completed Form GST11 or comparable declaration)

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. Canada does not mandate API integration or government e-invoice authorization. GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed information under Excise Tax Act sections 221-223; invoices for BC PST must meet provincial requirements. Electronic invoicing is accepted, but business records must be retained for six years.

CRA and the Canada Border Services Agency together scrutinize zero-rated export claims. If proof of export is not produced within two years, the supply is reassessed at 5% GST (plus BC PST if applicable) with statutory interest. Penalties under ETA s. 285 for false statements are 5% plus 1% per month on the net tax liability, up to 25% for gross negligence. Intentional evasion may result in criminal prosecution, director liability, and unlimited reassessment periods (10 years for careless, 12 years for fraud). Refund claims may be denied or redetermined, with clawbacks plus penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility on both the current account and the capital account. Exchange controls were substantially dismantled by 1951, the Canadian dollar floats freely, and no approval, registration, or repatriation licence is required for inbound equity, intercompany debt, dividend repatriment, or trade settlement. Profit remittance hurdles are exclusively tax-driven: dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% under the Income Tax Act, subject to treaty relief (e.g., 5-15% under the Canada-US or other double-tax treaties); a Part IV refund for private corporations must be computed; and CRS/FATCA reporting may attach. FX liquidity is deep, and there are no currency-restriction premia, surrender windows, or dual-rate mechanisms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canadian law imposes zero statutory surrender requirements for export proceeds: no mandatory timeline for repatriation, no minimum percentage conversion into Canadian dollars, and no designation of a government-mandated settlement account. A BC-incorporated TikTok Shop retail exporter may retain foreign currency in commercial bank accounts indefinitely. Relevant obligations are limited to FINTRAC large-cash transaction reports (CAD 10,000 threshold), CRA/Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys, and income attribution under the accrual method in the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing oversight by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada has no centralised foreign-exchange administrative authority equivalent to a SAFE-style exchange-control bureau; the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) confirms Canadian dollar legal tender, and the Minister of Finance retains residual powers under the Investment Canada Act and certain sanctions regimes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for payment aggregation and small-ticket TikTok Shop settlement; Limited for principal treasury function. Wise B2B (Wise Payments Ltd), Payoneer, and Airwallex are registered or regulated as money-service businesses under PCMLTFA and maintain Canadian funding partnerships, enabling cheaper cross-border payout rails, virtual IBANs, and API-driven reconciliation. They are, however, not Schedule I banks, do not hold CDIC-insured Canadian-dollar deposits, and cannot substitute for a Tier 1 licensed bank for escrow, LC issuance, overdraft lines, or large-value FX. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Execute all equity and shareholder-loan injections via SWIFT MT103 with GPI tracking, using field 72 structured references such as /INV/ EQUITY CONTRIBUTION or SHAREHOLDER LOAN and stating the BC corporate registry number and beneficiary account in the field 59 details. The sending shareholder must identify ultimate ownership and source-of-funds on the wire, and the BC entity must match the incoming remittance to a signed board-approved capital schedule. Canal funding is cleared through Payments Canada Lynx for large-value CAD and through the correspondent network for USD; no deposit-control or exchange-approval clearance is needed.
