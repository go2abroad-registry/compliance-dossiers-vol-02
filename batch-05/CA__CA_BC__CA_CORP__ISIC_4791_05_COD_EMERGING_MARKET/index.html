Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal GST-HST and Part I corporate income tax administration via CRA My Business Account portal (canada.ca). BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance under the Social Service Tax Act (portal: eTaxBC). Customs and export border controls: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate as a Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (1-day online filing with Corporations Canada) or under the BC Business Corporations Act (BC Corporate Online); no minimum capital requirement and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted. Step 2: Obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) automatically issued by CRA upon federal incorporation or by filing Form RC1 / Business Registration Online; the BN is the sole statutory tax identifier. Step 3: Add program accounts: RT for GST-HST (mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any quarter or four consecutive quarters; voluntary registration is available earlier) and RC for Part I corporate income tax. Step 4: Register for BC PST through eTaxBC when taxable retail sales in BC exceed CAD 10,000 in the immediately preceding 12 months (small-seller exemption); sellers with wholesale or manufacturing activities must register irrespective of volume. Step 5: For cross-border COD flows, open the Airwallex collection/FX account and the local logistics remittance rail; link settlement records to the BN and retain non-resident vendor/tax identification documentation for the destination emerging market.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box pass-through regime; a Business Corporation is taxable at entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Available elections for this structure: (1) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156 election for closely related corporations to treat intercorporate taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; (2) ETA s. 167(1) joint election for zero-rated supply of a business/going concern between registrants; (3) ITA s. 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfer of business property into the corporation; (4) hybrid-mismatch reporting and elective deference under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 and ETA s. 21.1 for cross-border payments involving related entities in emerging markets. Elections are made on prescribed forms or by written agreement retained with the registrant's books; late elections require CRA administrative relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies for GST-HST registration (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148); registration becomes mandatory as of the earlier triggering day and is retroactive to that date. BC PST registration threshold: CAD 10,000 of taxable sales sourced to BC in the four preceding calendar quarters; wholesalers and manufacturers must register without any threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is governed by Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, principally s. 5 (supplier ships tangible personal property to a destination outside Canada) and s. 10 (supply to a non-resident for export within 60 days). For ISIC 4791 mail-order COD retail: (a) the supply must be of tangible personal property made by a registered supplier; (b) goods must be consigned to a foreign address and physically removed from Canada; (c) the purchaser must not be a GST-HST registrant (if registered, the reverse-charge rules apply); (d) COD collection through Airwallex or local logistics remittance does not alter the zero-rate but must be traceable to the specific export invoice; (e) related freight, handling, and delivery services performed in Canada in respect of exported goods are zero-rated under Schedule VI ss. 7-8. Canada has no currency-control statute, so no statutory FX repatriation deadline applies; the 180-day figure below reflects best-practice COD settlement substantiation for emerging-market payment rails.

  • B13A CBSA Export Declaration (or CES e-manifest filed by the carrier/courier) for goods exceeding CAD 2,000 or controlled goods; low-value mail-order shipments may rely on the carrier's consolidated export manifest
  • Commercial invoice / zero-rated GST tax invoice showing the BN, HS codes, foreign consignee address, incoterms, and zero-rated export annotation
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / postal customs label evidencing removal from Canada and delivery abroad (destination-country customs stamp or courier delivery signature)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank SWIFT MT103 or Airwallex settlement statement evidencing collection of COD funds, currency conversion, and invoice-level reconciliation
  • Destination-market import declaration or customs clearance record (critical for emerging-market audit trails and for any export-refund or VAT-reclaim claims abroad)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance API, or digital-stamping regime exists in Canada. The pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework is PEPPOL-based (voluntary phased adoption for B2G/B2B, 2024-2026; acceptance-only for small vendors). Mandatory invoice data under ETA s. 223: supplier name and BN, date of invoice, total consideration, and GST/HST rate and amount (zero-rated export invoices must show the BN, a 0% rate, and the export destination). Simplified invoices (omitting receiver identity) are permitted for supplies under CAD 150. Paper or electronic tax invoices must be issuable at the point of delivery for COD transactions, and all records must be retained for six years (ETA s. 286 / ITA s. 230). Border clearance applies to imports via the CBSA CARM portal and to exports via CES e-manifest; no government digital seal is affixed to export invoices.

CRA export-verification reviews use risk-scored selection for COD e-commerce exporters. A zero-rating claim that fails substantiation is reassessed at 5% GST and may additionally attract BC PST where goods were sold or delivered into BC. Consequences: (i) net tax deficiency plus compounded daily arrears interest at CRA quarterly rates; (ii) ETA s. 280(1) civil penalty of 6% of unremitted net tax, escalating to a 25% gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 280(1.1); (iii) ETA s. 285 criminal prosecution for false or deceptive statements (summary conviction: fine up to CAD 25,000 and 12 months imprisonment; indictment: fine up to CAD 100,000 and 5 years); (iv) ITA s. 239 tax-evasion penalties of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment of up to 5 years; (v) extension of the normal 4-year reassessment window to 7 years for misrepresentation or fraud; (vi) clawback of input tax credits and suspension of refunds until export documentation is produced; and (vii) FINTRAC penalties up to CAD 500,000 for money-services businesses where COD proceeds are settled through non-regulated remittance rails failing AML/KYC reporting.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account purposes. No prior approval is required for inward capital injection, loan repayment, dividend distribution, or profit repatriation. However, payments to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada): 25% on dividends, interest, and royalties, reduced to as low as 5%/15% under the Canada-USA tax treaty or relevant treaty concluded with the emerging-market counterparty. The CA_BC corporation must file NR4 returns, obtain treaty-reduced-rate certificates (NR301/NR302) where applicable, and ensure non-resident logistics partners are not inadvertently triggering Canadian permanent establishment exposure. Section 116 certificates are required for non-resident disposals of taxable Canadian property if any equity sale occurs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, repatriation, or settlement timeline for export proceeds or inbound cross-border sales receipts. Receipts may be retained in any freely convertible currency in a Canadian Schedule I bank or authorized foreign bank branch, subject solely to FINTRAC transaction reporting and standard anti-money-laundering record keeping.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) as monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF supervisor. Canada operates no statutory foreign exchange control authority; banks under the Bank Act (Canada) manage FX freely.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High operational feasibility with constrained legal status: Canada does not recognize the 'Electronic Money Institution' licensing category; Airwallex and similar platforms operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, holding funds as stored-value/money-transfer balances rather than Canadian bank deposits. Their cross-border payment rails are legally viable for the ISIC 4791 COD e-commerce model provided that: (i) the platform maintains FINTRAC MSB registration and an authorized Canadian sponsor bank; (ii) provincial money-services licensing or a clear statutory exclusion is confirmed; and (iii) a sponsorship/escrow agreement prevents commingling of merchant settlement funds. Local logistics remittance corridors must be screened for sanctions exposure and correspondent-banking restrictions. Client funds held with an unlicensed offshore EMI would NOT constitute 'deposit insurance' under the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation Act and carry heightened restitution risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward equity capital from the emerging-market parent/shareholder is effected through SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank credit) with a proper purpose code evidencing 'direct investment/equity capital subscription', routed to the Canadian bank's account. The bank verifies the payer name appears on the board/CFO-sanctioned schedule; then the CA_BC corporation files a shareholder subscription record and issues shares in compliance with the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia). The remitting bank must complete FINTRAC-mandated source-of-funds disclosure; no BoC approval is needed. To avoid reverse money-laundering flags, the injection must be supported by the executed subscription agreement, board resolutions, opening balance-sheet projections, and a capital-receipt ledger. Funds may be held in a Capital Escrow Account pending operational launch and converted to CAD at a licensed dealer.Cross-border trade settlement uses SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier/logistics payments, with SWIFT GPI tracking for end-to-end visibility; commercial letters of credit (UCP600) are preferred for high-value inventory procurement to provide documentary protection. CAD-denominated clearing runs on Payments Canada's Lynx high-value system and ACSS for retail batch settlements; USD and other currencies clear via correspondent banking networks. COD collections from emerging markets are aggregated by local logistics remittance corridors and repatriated by wire or fintech batch payout into the Canadian operating account. Every outbound disbursement must carry a valid purpose code, link to an underlying invoice or LC document, and pass the bank's transaction-monitoring screening against OFAC, UN, EU, and Canadian sanctions lists.
