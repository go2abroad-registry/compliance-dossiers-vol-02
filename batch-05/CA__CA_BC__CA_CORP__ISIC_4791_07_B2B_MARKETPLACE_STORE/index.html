Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal corporate income tax (Part I), Part XIII withholding, and GST/HST administration. Integrated portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register-options.html). Provincial counterpart for British Columbia: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (Provincial Sales Tax / PST) via eTaxBC portal (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the federal/provincial business corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the BC Business Corporations Act; if a federal CBCA corporation carries on business in BC, file an Extraprovincial Registration with the BC Registrar of Companies via Corporate Online / OneStop Business Registry. Step 2: Procure the federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO); non-resident applicants without a Canadian mailing address must appoint a resident representative (Form RC59) and may paper-file. Step 3: Register as a PST vendor with BC Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC / OneStop; there is no monetary threshold for PST, and BC marketplace facilitator rules may deem the platform to be the vendor for facilitated taxable sales in BC. Step 4: If employing staff, register for CRA Payroll (RP) account, BC WorksafeBC, and applicable provincial employer accounts. Step 5: Coordinate the TIN equivalents: BN / RT0001 corporate income tax program account and RT0001 GST/HST account; for non-resident corporations earning only Canadian-source passive income, evaluate Part XIII withholding registration and any required section 116 certificate for dispositions of taxable Canadian property.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian federal/provincial business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act. A resident corporation (central management and control situated in Canada) is taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada is taxed on Canadian-source business income under ss. 2(3) and 115, with additional Part XIV branch tax of 25% (treaty-reduced where applicable). The foreign affiliate regime (ss. 95-113) applies at entity level, providing exempt-surplus treatment for active foreign business income. No pass-through eligibility exists for corporations. Territorial scope for indirect tax: B2B cross-border wholesale platform services (ISIC 4791 / NAICS 425120) are generally zero-rated for GST/HST when supplied to non-residents, while domestic Canadian sales attract 5% GST and BC PST as applicable.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory and treaty elections relevant to entity classification and hybrid/mismatch arrangements include: (a) treaty transparent-entity recognition for US LLCs and comparable fiscally transparent foreign entities under Canada-US Treaty Article IV(6) and analogous treaty provisions; (b) hybrid mismatch limitation rules under ss. 18.4-18.6, 94-94.4 of the Income Tax Act, which constrain deduction outcomes and require substantiated factual classification of offshore entities; (c) section 216 election permitting non-residents with Canadian rental income to compute net Part I tax instead of Part XIII gross withholding; (d) section 219(3) elective branch-tax base adjustments for non-resident corporations with Canadian branches; (e) GST/HST section 191 election for closely related corporations, deeming intercorporate taxable supplies to be made for nil consideration; (f) elections respecting foreign affiliate surplus/profit entitlement designations under s. 113 and Income Tax Regulations 5900-5907.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD $30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports) measured over four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, mandatory registration, collection, and remittance are required; voluntary registration is permitted for export-oriented businesses. BC PST: no general monetary threshold; vendor registration is due within 30 days of the first taxable sale in BC, and marketplace facilitator rules impose deemed-vendor obligations on cross-border e-commerce platforms facilitating taxable BC sales. BC PST registration is required for this entity only to the extent of tangible personal property or software supplied to BC end-consumers; pure B2B exports to non-residents remain zero-rated and PST-exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For a B2B cross-border wholesale marketplace (ISIC 4791 / NAICS 425120) operating from Canada: (1) services supplied to a non-resident (platform access, listing fees, transaction commissions, data services) are zero-rated under Excise Tax Act s. 23(1) provided the supply is not in respect of real property in Canada, goods situated in Canada at the time of supply, or a service rendered to an individual present in Canada; (2) digital and remote services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under ETA s. 23.1; (3) tangible goods sold through the platform and exported from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V (supply of goods delivered outside Canada) where export documentation is retained; (4) the place-of-supply rules (ETA ss. 13-23.1 and Selected Listed Financial Institution / VBE Rules) govern Canadian nexus; (5) the exporter must hold contemporaneous evidence of non-resident recipient status and physical movement of goods or offshore provision of services; zero-rated exporters retain full input tax credit recovery.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Declaration (CAED/GTR) or electronically filed export report for goods exceeding CAD $2,000 in value or for controlled/prohibited goods
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / cargo receipt evidencing export from Canada and delivery to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, and B2B platform contract identifying the non-resident customer and supply terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice or other bank proof of receipt of funds from abroad
  • Place-of-supply analysis memorandum under ETA ss. 13-23.1 and VBE Regulations documenting non-resident status of the recipient and the offshore supply analysis
  • Non-resident status attestation (certificate of residence or treaty residency declaration) collected from marketplace merchants

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping. CRA accepts standard electronic invoices (PDF, EDI, PEPPOL-based exchange) without prior clearance or approval; the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework is voluntary. GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD $1,500,000; others may paper-file). Records must be retained for six years in an electronically readable and readily accessible format with a preserved audit trail. BC PST returns are filed electronically via eTaxBC; no fiscal invoice clearance or cryptographic stamping requirements apply.

CRA may deny zero-rating, claw back previously allowed input tax credits, and reassess GST/HST at 5% (or 12% where BC HST/PST interaction applies) if export substantiation is deficient. Default reassessment window is four years under ETA s. 298; the period is unlimited where misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default is established. Penalties: 50% of any false refund claimed under ETA s. 285.1; gross negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD $250 and 25% of the tax under ETA s. 285; daily compound interest on unremitted amounts at the prescribed rate plus 2% under ETA s. 280; criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ETA s. 327 carrying fines up to CAD $100,000 on indictment and possible imprisonment. Additional provincial exposure may arise if BC PST was improperly claimed as exempt or zero-rated; directors and officers may be liable for corporate GST/PST debts in cases of gross negligence.
