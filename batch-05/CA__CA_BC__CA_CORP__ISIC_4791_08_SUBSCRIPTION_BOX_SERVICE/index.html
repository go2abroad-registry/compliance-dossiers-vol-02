Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Corporate Income Tax and GST/HST administration; Federal portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). British Columbia Ministry of Finance — Consumer Taxation Branch; BC Tax Self-Service portal (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity or a Canadian business corporation must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 or using the online 'Register a business with the CRA' service. When the entity carries on business in Canada or makes taxable supplies (other than zero-rated supplies) in Canada, it must open a separate GST/HST account under the same BN. For a cross-border subscription box business exporting physical goods from Canada, voluntary GST/HST registration is strongly recommended to claim zero-rating and input tax credits. The British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) account is procured separately from the BC Ministry of Finance and is required before conducting taxable sales into BC as a 'vendor' under the PST Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a British Columbia or federal jurisdiction business corporation is a separate legal person and taxpayer; under the Income Tax Act (Canada) it is taxed on its worldwide income, and under the Excise Tax Act (Canada) it is treated as a distinct registrant for GST/HST purposes. There is no general pass-through eligibility for corporations in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. A business corporation's income tax classification is statutory. Available GST/HST elections include the closely related corporation election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act (allowing intercompany taxable supplies to be treated as made for no consideration), and provincial PST vendor status elections. Hybrid mismatch planning is limited because Canadian corporations are not eligible for pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 in taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies but excluding financial services and capital) made over any four consecutive calendar quarters, measured on a worldwide basis; non-residents making only zero-rated exports may register voluntarily. BC PST: no general low-value vendor exemption; a non-resident vendor making taxable sales of goods in BC must register as a PST vendor before making such sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a GST/HST registrant may zero-rate (0% GST) a supply of tangible personal property that is sold and exported by way of sale from Canada in a commercial transaction. Eligibility requires that the registrant owns the goods at the time of export, the goods are removed from Canada pursuant to a contract of sale, they are delivered to a common carrier, freight forwarder, or bridge/crossing operator for export, no further processing takes place in Canada, and the registrant retains documentary proof of export. Payment may be received in foreign or Canadian currency; the tax treatment does not depend on settlement method.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B15/A or approved electronic export declaration)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent shipping waybill
  • Packing List and exporter/importer records
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank wire or payment-rail settlement record evidencing receipt of funds from the foreign customer
  • Carrier export receipts, customs entry records, and destination-confirmation documents (e.g., foreign address proof and delivery confirmation)

Canada has no mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime for GST/HST or BC PST. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies above C$1,000,000 are required to file returns electronically through CRA-approved gateways. All registrants must retain readable electronic records supporting every tax invoice and export transaction. The BC PST system uses the BC Tax Self-Service portal for registration and remittance; there is no government API invoice-clearance mandate.

CRA will scrutinize export zero-rating claims by matching exports to shipping records, sales contracts, customer invoices, and payment receipts. If proof of export is inadequate, CRA reclassifies the supply as taxable GST at 5% and BC may assert PST liability for goods delivered into BC. Penalties under sections 280-285 of the Excise Tax Act include a penalty equal to 25% of any unremitted tax for knowingly making false statements, up to 200% of tax evaded, and criminal fines and imprisonment for tax evasion. Retrospective reassessment may extend beyond the normal four-year limitation period where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, or wilful default is established; interest runs from the original due date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible, freely floating fiat currency. Canada maintains no exchange control laws; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and no statutory restrictions exist on current account or capital account transactions. Profit remittance from a British Columbia Business Corporation is unrestricted, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, commonly treaty-reduced) on dividends and to arm's-length transfer pricing rules under Income Tax Act section 247.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or repatriation timeline applies to export receipts in Canada. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency bank accounts or CAD operating accounts. The only binding obligations are the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) client identification, record-keeping, and FINTRAC reporting duties for transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 or structured patterns.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. Cross-border digital EMI-style providers (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) may operate as payment service providers in Canada but are not licensed as banks and cannot offer deposit insurance. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), effective September 2024 with transitional registration requirements, payment service providers must be registered with the Bank of Canada. Such providers are viable for ancillary cross-currency disbursements but are not a substitute for a Schedule I chartered bank account for capital escrow, letter-of-credit facilities, or FINTRAC-compliant corporate onboarding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pain.001 for electronic Canadian-dollar credits) from the remitting bank to the BC corporation's CAD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank, using purpose code 'Capital contribution / equity injection' with full reference to the share subscription agreement. Verification requires the corporate minute-book resolution authorizing the share issuance, a FINTRAC transaction report if cumulative credits exceed CAD 10,000, and the receiving bank's OFAC and sanctions screening.
