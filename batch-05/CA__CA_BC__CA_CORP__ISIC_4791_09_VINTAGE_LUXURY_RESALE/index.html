Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Canada maintains fully convertible current-account and capital-account regimes under IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no exchange controls, no central bank approval requirements for capital injections, and no restrictions on the conversion or repatriation of profits, dividends, interest, or liquidation proceeds. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating currency. The only compliance hurdles are indirect: cross-border payments above CAD 10,000 trigger FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting; dividend payments to non-resident shareholders are subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty; and transactions with listed sanctioned jurisdictions or persons must be screened under the Special Economic Measures Act and United Nations Act.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
There are no statutory surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios, or designated timelines for the repatriation of export proceeds in Canada. Exporters may lawfully maintain foreign-currency bank accounts and retain earnings offshore. However, the exporter must report certain cross-border electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and must keep accurate books and records to substantiate income inclusion for Canadian tax purposes under the accrual method. For vintage luxury resale, all sale proceeds from online transactions must be traceable through the intermediary platform's settlement reports to satisfy anti-money-laundering (AML) and tax record-keeping obligations.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank and monetary authority); foreign exchange surveillance and financial-crime compliance oversight are jointly administered by the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not maintain a dedicated exchange-control authority and has no general system of foreign-exchange licensing.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High for auxiliary treasury operations, with important legal constraints. Canadian-chartered banks remain the primary liquidity and clearing partners because they provide settlement certainty under the Canadian Payments Act and access to the Lynx system. Offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may support operational foreign-currency accounts and cross-border payment execution, but they generally cannot offer a Canadian-dollar clearing account routed through Lynx unless they partner with a Canadian bank or hold a FINTRAC money services business (MSB) registration for remittance services. Any foreign EMI operating as a payment service provider to Canadian residents must assess whether it crosses the threshold for FINTRAC MSB registration; Canadian corporate clients using foreign EMIs remain fully responsible for record-keeping, source-of-funds documentation, and tax compliance. For a vintage luxury resale business holding consumer-facing settlement funds, segregated fiduciary accounts held at a Tier-1 Canadian bank are the legally safer routing than unregistered offshore EMI accounts.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Luxury resale e-commerce model flagged as high-risk because of secondhand goods, cross-border counterparties, and multiple payment channels.
        
Root cause: Luxury resale e-commerce model flagged as high-risk because of secondhand goods, cross-border counterparties, and multiple payment channels.

        
Defense evidence: Provide audited transaction history from the online marketplace (Stripe, Escrow/Pay), a detailed seller verification policy, authentication certificates for luxury items, third-party appraisal reports, chain-of-custody documentation for consignment inventory, and a written AML risk self-assessment evidencing that each consignor is identified, that provenance is verified, and that no cash-intensive activity is present.

      

      
      

        Non-resident director or shareholder, particularly originating from a jurisdiction perceived as high-risk or non-cooperative for tax purposes.
        
Root cause: Non-resident director or shareholder, particularly originating from a jurisdiction perceived as high-risk or non-cooperative for tax purposes.

        
Defense evidence: Provide a certified copy of a valid passport, proof of residential address, professional reference letters from a regulated financial institution, a tax-residency certificate, a corporate organigram disclosing the full beneficial-ownership chain, and a comfort letter from a Canadian legal counsel explaining the commercial rationale and source of funds.

      

      
      

        Large inbound capital injection from an offshore jurisdiction inconsistent with the stated equity capital and start-up profile.
        
Root cause: Large inbound capital injection from an offshore jurisdiction inconsistent with the stated equity capital and start-up profile.

        
Defense evidence: Provide executed share subscription agreements, bank statements of the ultimate transferring account, board resolutions approving the capital contribution, completion certificates for funds verification (SWIFT MT103 confirmation), proof of the transferor's source of wealth (tax returns, audited financial statements, or sale-and-purchase agreements), and a notarized declaration of funds.

      

      
      

        Cross-border trade disbursements to vendors in jurisdictions with known sanctions or money-laundering vulnerabilities.
        
Root cause: Cross-border trade disbursements to vendors in jurisdictions with known sanctions or money-laundering vulnerabilities.

        
Defense evidence: Provide a counterparty sanctions-screening report, know-your-customer (KYC) and know-your-buyer (KYB) documentation for each vendor, supplier invoices, authentication reports for the vintage goods purchased, transport and logistics bills of lading, and an internal compliance gateway confirming that all counterparties are absent from the Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List, the UN sanctions list, and OFAC SDN list.

      

      
      

        Escrow payment and consumer refund flows creating perceived layering, commingling, or unverified third-party payments.
        
Root cause: Escrow payment and consumer refund flows creating perceived layering, commingling, or unverified third-party payments.

        
Defense evidence: Provide a detailed flow-of-funds diagram separating buyer payments, seller disbursements, escrow holdbacks, refunds, fees, and platform settlement reports; execute a written escrow agreement; maintain a segregated client-transaction ledger; and provide monthly reconciliation reports as well as a written confirmation from the payment processor that the merchant account is verified and owned solely by the Canadian entity.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The compliant routing for inbound equity or subordinated-loan capital is a SWIFT MT103 international wire transfer from the foreign investor's bank to the Canadian corporation's operational or escrow account at a Tier-1 Canadian bank. The beneficiary account should be identified with the Canadian bank's institution number, transit/branch number, and account number; the beneficiary legal name must exactly match the corporate name in the articles. The wire must be accompanied by an inward remittance advice specifying the purpose as 'equity contribution / share subscription' or 'loan advances' in plain business language. Although Canada does not issue exchange-control purpose codes, the bank's automated compliance system will capture the remittance narrative, so the narrative should contain a reference to the corporate name and the share subscription agreement. For an injection exceeding CAD 10,000, the receiving bank is required to file a FINTRAC EFT report; the corporate treasury must therefore be prepared to provide source-of-wealth documentation and the board resolution. All capital should be routed to the same account from funding accounts owned by the exact shareholders recorded in the corporate register; third-party payments should be avoided outright or accompanied by a clear collateral assignment and declaration of trust.Outbound trade payments from the Canadian entity will be executed by SWIFT MT103 for direct vendor payments, or via a commercial documentary letter of credit (LC) issued by the Canadian bank for high-value consignment purchases requiring shipment security. Domestic Canadian-dollar payments may be cleared through the Lynx real-time gross settlement system for large amounts or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for standard payments; partner-financed legacy rails are not recommended. For cross-border USD payments, the Canadian bank may route through its United States correspondent or use the bank's US-dollar clearing account. Acceptable clearing instruments include: (a) SWIFT MT103 for pre-paid vendor invoices and authenticated luxury-goods purchases; (b) SWIFT MT700 / MT707 for irrevocable documentary credits with vendor-favorable performance and transport documents; and (c) escrow-initiated disbursements via SWIFT MT103 supported by the escrow agent's release instructions. The corporate treasury must maintain a documentary archive for each disbursement: invoice, contract, transport insurance, export/import customs declaration, authentication certificate, and FINTRAC EFT report record where applicable.Deliverable FX forward contracts with a Canadian Schedule I bank for committed USD/CAD purchases or salesNon-deliverable currency forwards (NDFs) where CAD is the reference currency and settlement is netted in USD when counterparty restrictions applyCross-currency interest rate swaps (CIRS) for aligning debt-service currencies and minimizing refinancing riskFX swaps and rollover forwards used for short-term working-capital liquidity matchingPlain-vanilla FX options (call or put) purchased to hedge uncertain future trade flowsCommodity or luxury-good price hedges are irrelevant; currency hedges must be specifically documented as treasury risk-management transactions and should not create speculative trading positions under bank internal policies

Any foreign EMI operating as a payment service provider to Canadian residents must assess whether it crosses the threshold for FINTRAC MSB registration; Canadian corporate clients using foreign EMIs remain fully responsible for record-keeping, source-of-funds documentation, and tax compliance. Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under ITA s. 239; fines up to $5 million; imprisonment up to 2 years

        
Director personal liability: Personal liability for unpaid taxes; possible director disqualification under CBCA; criminal charges and travel bans

      

      
      

        Shell Company
        
Statutory sanction: Penalty up to $10,000 per day for failure to file; possible revocation of corporate status under Business Corporations Act

        
Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; personal liability for taxes, debts, and obligations; potential imprisonment for willful non-compliance

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Penalty up to $500,000 for failure to file TP report; reassessment of income and penalties for underpayment

        
Director personal liability: Personal liability if willful; possible fines, imprisonment, and director disqualification
