Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal fiscal authority; registration and filing portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Provincial component for British Columbia: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, portal: eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca/btmspa/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on commercial activity in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Canada Business Number (BN) and activate a GST/HST tax account. Procedure: (1) apply online through CRA 'My Business Account' or via Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Tax Accounts'; (2) supply legal entity name, registered address, date of establishment, foreign registration documentation, director/officer details, and description of Canadian business activity; (3) select the GST/HST account and declare the effective date of the first taxable supply; (4) receive the BN followed by the two-letter account identifier (e.g., RT0001); (5) if PST collection is required, register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance through the eTaxBC portal or by paper application using the relevant BC PST registration form, indicating Canadian BN and evidence of economic nexus in British Columbia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or elective entity classification exists under the Income Tax Act; a Business Corporation is statutorily taxed at the entity level. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2–18.8 and 28.1) apply mandatorily, without elective opt-outs for standard arm's-length or related-party cross-border structures. Within GST/HST, a qualifying group election under Form RC4616 can deem intra-group qualifying supplies to be non-taxable; under BC PST, no analogous elective classification is available for entity tax status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold measured over the calendar quarter and the preceding four quarters; BC PST: CAD 10,000 annual remote-seller threshold for taxable sales delivered in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: dietary supplement products qualify for GST/HST zero-rating when the supplier exports the goods, or supplies them to a non-resident for delivery outside Canada, and the supplier retains documentary evidence of export within the prescribed record-keeping period. Services: offshore services to non-residents may be zero-rated when the services are not performed for the benefit of a Canadian resident and are not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. For e-commerce supplies, zero-rating is available only where title and possession pass outside Canada and shipment documentation demonstrates foreign destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CERS or postal/courier outward manifest) showing HS code, value, exporter, and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of lading / airway bill / courier consignment note evidencing delivery outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of funds from the foreign purchaser
  • Proof of customer location (IP address, billing address, shipping address) for cross-border e-commerce zero-rating claims
  • Inventory and shipment trace records demonstrating goods departed Canada

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital fiscal validation requirement. GST/HST invoices must be issued for most taxable supplies exceeding CAD 30 and preserved in electronic or paper format; CRA accepts electronic records without pre-clearance. E-invoicing is optional, including Peppol-based exchange. BC PST requires invoices to disclose the PST charged; there is no digital stamping mandate or government clearance portal for B2B or B2C invoices.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export sales for four years from the date of assessment with a further extension for wilful misrepresentation, and may apply GST/HST plus interest where export substantiation is insufficient. Gross negligence or fraudulent claims attract a 50% civil penalty on the tax evaded under Excise Tax Act s. 285. Intentional failure to remit GST/HST is a criminal offence carrying fines up to CAD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to two years on summary conviction, or CAD 500,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years on indictment. BC PST audits may reassess uncollected PST with interest and provincial penalties; false declarations under the Provincial Sales Tax Act are subject to administrative penalties and may constitute an offence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible in both the current and capital accounts with no statutory exchange-control gateways. Dividends, management fees, royalties, and loan repayments may be remitted offshore freely, subject only to Canadian Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (generally 25% for dividends, 15% for interest and royalties, reducible by treaty) and box-6 CRA reporting. No pre-approval is required for cross-border fund transfers; banks independently screen for sanctions, money-laundering, and tax-transparency compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes zero statutory export-proceeds surrender or conversion requirements. A Canadian exporter may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in USD/EUR/other foreign-currency bank accounts in Canada or offshore, and no timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies under the Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act, or the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates all federally incorporated chartered banks and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces AML/CDD. Canada maintains no foreign-exchange licensing or administrative approval regime; CAD is a freely floating currency traded on open global markets.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High as a supplementary rail but not a substitute for a Canadian bank account. Foreign-regulated electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) may hold multi-currency balances and issue USD/EUR routing numbers; however, they cannot provide cheque-clearing, LVTS access, or CAD correspondent bank accounts in Canada, and the entity must still maintain a domestic OSFI-regulated account. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward equity capital should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or shareholder's bank to the Canadian corporation's BMO/RBC operational account. The payment must carry the Bank of Canada Balance of Payments function code 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (BPM6 'E102') and a wire-reference note 'Share Capital Contribution under the Canada Business Corporations Act'. The beneficial-owner bank will require a signed capital-introduction letter from the recipient, a certified copy of the share subscription agreement, and a CRA-prescribed 'New Business Establishment Notification' if the parent is a non-Canadian investor. The Canadian bank will independently confirm that the sender's name matches the UBO declaration and that the funding is sourced from a bank account held in the shareholder's country of residence; if the inbound transfer exceeds CAD 10,000, a FINTRAC Large Virtual Currency Transaction/Cross-Border Electronic Funds Transfer Report is triggered by the bank, but no central-bank approval or exchange-control permit is required.Supplier payments settle via SWIFT MT103 (single corporate treasury message) or via the bank's online treasury platform for payments to raw-material APIs, bottle/encapsulation vendors, and contract manufacturers. For purchase orders exceeding USD 250,000, a standard irrevocable Documentary Letter of Credit per the UCP 600 (issued via MT700 and advised via MT710/MT720) is recommended with required documentation including supplier invoice, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading and CoA. Domestic Canadian obligations (payroll, VAT/GST remittances, intercompany loans) settle in real-time through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and the Canadian Real-Time Rail (RTR) for card payouts; US-denominated payables may be cleared either through SWIFT USD cross-border rails or via the bank's US correspondent network (e.g., BMO Harris N.A., RBC Bank Georgia) using a US-based clearing account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal 15% + British Columbia provincial 12% = 27% general corporate income tax rate; reduced to 11% on active business income up to CA$500,000 for a Canadian-controlled private corporation (9% federal Small Business Deduction rate + 2% BC small business rate). Non-resident-controlled Canadian corporations are not eligible for the SBD and are taxed at 27%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 filing deadline is six (6) months after the corporation's fiscal year-end (e.g., December 31 year-end = June 30 filing deadline). Balance-due day is two (2) months after year-end for a non-CCPC, extended to three (3) months for a CCPC eligible for the SBD. Instalments are due the last day of each month; T106 must be filed no later than six (6) months after year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 information return and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation are mandatory when the aggregate fair market value of cross-border non-arm's-length transactions (sales, purchases, royalties, services) with any non-resident exceeds CA$1,000,000 in a taxation year; Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) is required for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of CA$750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a designated 'Economic Substance Regulations' statute (as in UAE/Bermuda), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) recharacterizes avoidance transactions lacking economic substance or a bona fide business purpose, and imposes a 25% (pre-2024) or 50% (post-2024 Budget) penalty on the tax benefit. For cross-border e-commerce of regulated health products, the CRA and Health Canada require real local substance: physical inventory, warehousing, employees, books and records, and management in BC; a letter-box entity controlled from abroad will be challenged for PE allocation, treaty entitlement, and Part XIII assessment. The 'substance' analysis is also applied through the GAAR, the OECD BEPS minimum standards, and judicial doctrines on sham and corporate veil piercing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil: gross negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax plus interest. Criminal conviction under ITA s. 239 and Criminal Code ss. 380-382: summary conviction fine of 50%-200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; indictable offence fine of 100%-200% and imprisonment up to 5 years; restitution orders; asset seizure under the BC Civil Forfeiture Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be held personally liable for the corporation's unremitted source deductions under ITA s. 227.1 and GST/HST; criminal liability attaches to directing officers; CRA may obtain judgments, enforce travel bans against foreign directors, impose exit blocks, and red-flag financial institutions for AML review.
Shell Company / GAAR and Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR re-determination of all tax consequences under ITA s. 245; penalty of 25% (transactions before April 2024) or 50% (post-April 2024) of the tax benefit; denial of treaty benefits under BEPS MLI Art. 7/PER; reassessment interest; publication of taxpayer names on the CRA's voluntary disclosures/conviction lists.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil if the company is an 'agent' or 'sham' of the shareholder, exposing the foreign parent and its directors to Part XIII assessments, civil restitution, travel restrictions, and potential extradition for fraud-related offences.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: TP penalty under ITA s. 247(3) equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment (minimum CA$500); T106 late filing penalty of CA$100/day up to CA$7,500; upward assessment of the Canadian taxpayer's income by the CRA eliminating double-dip deductions; interest at prescribed rates; heightened audit/examination risk.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, certify, or condone the TP policy face personal recourse under director liability certificates; CRA criminal referral for 'culpable conduct' under s. 247(2) may result in jail time for wilfully false documentation.
AML and Health Canada Regulatory Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, penalties of up to CA$2,000,000 per violation and imprisonment up to 10 years for laundering; Health Canada/Food and Drugs Act prosecution: summary conviction fine of up to CA$10,000 per day and imprisonment up to 2 years, broadened to CA$200,000 per day and 5 years on indictment for serious health-product offences; BC Consumer Protection Act fines for deceptive e-commerce promotion.

Director personal liability: Personal prosecutions of directors, senior officers, and beneficial owners; civil forfeiture of company assets; airport stop-list/travel bans; designation as a 'high-risk' beneficial owner blocks Canadian banking, merchant processing, and future immigration/visa privileges.
