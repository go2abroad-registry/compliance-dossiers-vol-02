Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada for federal GST/HST and corporate income tax; CRA's Business Registration Online and 'My Business Account' portal serve as the electronic registration and filing gateway. E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and is not a flow-through entity; it is subject to federal Part I tax on worldwide income, and in British Columbia to provincial corporate income tax on its permanent establishment income. A BC branch of a foreign corporation is likewise subject to entity-level tax on Canadian-source business income computed under the same statutory rules.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election is available under Canadian federal or British Columbia law. Classification as a corporation, partnership, or trust follows the legal and commercial attributes of the entity under applicable corporate or foreign law. Hybrid mismatch provisions in the Income Tax Act (as enacted following BEPS Action 2) operate as mandatory anti-avoidance rules rather than elective tax regimes; therefore, there are no elective check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections for a Canadian business corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies for a resident supplier (small-supplier threshold); non-resident suppliers of digital products or services to Canadian consumers must register for the simplified GST/HST framework once Canadian sales exceed the same CAD 30,000 threshold. BC PST: no material economic threshold applies; a person who makes taxable retail sales of tangible personal property in BC, including a non-resident vendor with a physical or agency presence or soliciting sales through employees/agents in BC, is required to register for PST before carrying on taxable selling activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, where tangible personal property (including cosmetics) is supplied in Canada, possession is transferred to the recipient, and the goods are exported by the supplier or on behalf of the recipient as a condition of the supply. For DTC beauty retail, goods dispatched by courier or postal service from Canada to a foreign address can qualify as zero-rated exports provided the exporter retains proof of export from Canada; services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part VII when they are supplied to a person who is not resident in Canada and are not delivered or performed in Canada for use/consumption in Canada. The exported goods must leave Canada within a fixed period after supply, normally 60 days, and the seller must retain complete documentary evidence to support the zero-rating claim.

  • B13A/B13 Export Declaration report filed through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) when the export volume/value requires filing, or verified exemption from CERS filing
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order identifying the imported/exported cosmetics, quantities, selling price, Canadian exporter, and destination customer
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, Canada Post/courier consignment note, or equivalent carrier document proving physical export from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign currency payment receipt from Shopify Payments, PayPal, or the relevant payment rail showing receipt in the exporter's account
  • Customs brokerage export confirmation or carrier certification of export, where applicable
  • Destination-country customs entry documents, when available, evidencing importation into the consignee jurisdiction

None. Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping for GST/HST at the federal level, and British Columbia has no e-invoicing or mandatory tax clearance mandate for PST. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or Represent a Client API, and PST returns are filed via eTaxBC; overall record-keeping and audit requirements apply under the Excise Tax Act and BC Provincial Sales Tax Act, but electronic invoices are acceptable without CRA clearance.

CRA and CBSA may conduct post-export audits of zero-rated supplies and deny zero-rating where the exporter fails to maintain sufficient documentation showing the goods left Canada or the services were supplied to a non-resident for consumption outside Canada. If zero-rating is disallowed, the supplier is assessed GST/HST at the applicable rate together with arrears interest and may incur late remittance penalties. Knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence making a false statement in a GST/HST return attracts civil penalties, including the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the net tax understated, and tax evasion is criminally prosecutable under the Excise Tax Act with fines and imprisonment. BC PST clawback may also apply if exported goods are later returned to Canada and consumed or used in British Columbia, in which case PST and interest become payable. All export records should be retained for a minimum of six years in accordance with the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act record-keeping obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII status. Both capital-account and current-account transactions are liberalized; there are no domestic exchange controls, no central bank approval gates for equity injections, and no restrictions on profit repatriation. A British Columbia business corporation may freely remit dividends, management fees, or intercompany loan repayments to non-resident shareholders through any Schedule I bank. Such remittances remain subject only to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (default 25 percent, treaty-reduced where applicable), to FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting thresholds, and to standard GST/HST and corporate tax filing obligations. No advance Bank of Canada authorization is required to convert CAD receipts or to purchase foreign currency for trade settlements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: not applicable. Canada imposes no mandatory timeline for repatriation or surrender of export proceeds. Mandatory conversion ratios: none; exporters may retain 100 percent of CAD or foreign-currency proceeds in any Canadian operating account, escrow account, or non-resident account without remittance to the Bank of Canada. Documentary compliance: income tax and customs records must be retained for 6 years under subsection 230(1) of the Income Tax Act and section 40 of the Customs Act; FINTRAC cross-border currency movement reporting applies at CAD 10,000 or more; CBSA export reporting is required for goods valued above CAD 2,000 unless exempt. The beauty retail scope here involves digital services and low-value cross-border parcels, so the applicable obligation is principally the 6-year documentary retention rule plus FINTRAC incoming/outgoing EFT reporting where the bank executes wires.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the central bank, with supporting oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking supervision, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT supervision, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for cross-border trade reporting. Canada has no centralized foreign exchange administration comparable to China's SAFE; foreign exchange policy functions rest jointly with the Bank of Canada and the Department of Finance under the Currency Act and the Exchange Control Act (largely repealed), leaving the FX market to float freely.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a supplementary treasury rail only, never as the primary regulated settlement bank for the BC operating entity. Foreign fintech EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Nium, Payoneer) may legally provide multi-currency nostro/IBAN services, CAD foreign-exchange clearing, and mass-payout functions to a BC corporation, provided they do not hold themselves out as carrying on deposit-taking banking business in Canada without an OSFI bank license or a provincial credit-union license. Legal constraints: (i) an offshore EMI performing FX dealing or international electronic funds transfers for customers in Canada must register as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; (ii) client funds must be safeguarded in segregated accounts, typically held through a Schedule I bank correspondent; (iii) the BC corporation must not commingle EMI settlement inflows with Shopify/PayPal settlement inflows without clear source-of-funds reconciliation given the bank's e-commerce AML profile rules; (iv) the EMI rail cannot be used to circumvent CAD clearing through Lynx or ACSS for high-value disbursements above CAD 100,000. For this Low_Risk_Standard beauty DTC scope, the EMI rail is best restricted to foreign supplier payments in USD and EUR below CAD 50,000 per counterparty per month.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if non-arm's length transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 (goods) or CAD 500,000 (services, interest, royalties). Master File required if consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions require genuine economic substance for tax benefits. A shell company without real business activity may be challenged under GAAR or treaty shopping rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under s.227.1 if they fail to withhold or remit; civil restitution and potential travel bans.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the entity under GAAR, reassess tax, and impose penalties up to 50% of tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in the scheme; possible criminal charges for conspiracy.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations; criminal penalties up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly failed to comply; travel bans and seizure of assets possible.
