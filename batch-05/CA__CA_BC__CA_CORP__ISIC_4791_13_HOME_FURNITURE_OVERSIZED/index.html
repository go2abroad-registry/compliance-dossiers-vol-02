Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) is the national tax authority responsible for the federal Business Number (BN), the Goods and Services Tax / Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) registration and returns, and Part I corporate income tax (T2). The primary national portal is canada.ca/en/revenue-agency, with Business Registration Online (BRO) available for BN and program-account enrolment. British Columbia levies a separate 7% Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administered by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, whose digital portal is eTaxBC (www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst). The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers the customs program through the CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) online portal for RM (importer/exporter) accounts and export declarations.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-parent group incorporating a Canadian Business Corporation (federal under the Canada Business Corporations Act or BC under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act) for online retail of oversized furniture (ISIC 4791 / NAICS 449110): Step 1 - Confirm corporate residency: a corporation incorporated in Canada is de jure resident under ITA s. 250(4) and taxable on worldwide income; Step 2 - Apply for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by paper Form RC1; a foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business in Canada uses Form RC4306 (Request for a Business Number for a non-resident business) and must nominate a Canadian agent or service address; Step 3 - Activate the RT (GST/HST) program account; registration is mandatory within 29 days if the corporation exceeds the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold on worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada, and voluntary registration is permitted for corporations that carry on a business in Canada; Step 4 - Activate the RM (customs export) account with CBSA via CARM so that cargo/export declarations (B13A / G7) can be filed electronically for goods exported from Canada; Step 5 - Register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC for the Provincial Sales Tax (PST) vendor account; BC imposes no small-supplier exemption for furniture sold at retail in BC, so PST registration must be completed before the first taxable sale in BC; Step 6 - If employees are engaged in Canada, obtain an RP payroll account with CRA and register with WorkSafeBC, the Employer Health Tax program (if payroll exceeds CAD 500,000), and the BC Provincial Nominee Program if applicable; Step 7 - File the T2 corporate income tax return annually no later than six months after the fiscal year-end, remit corporate instalments in accordance with ITA s. 157, and remit GST/HST net tax via CRA My Business Account on the prescribed monthly, quarterly, or annual cycle; Step 8 - Maintain the BN, GST/HST account, PST account, and CBSA exporter records permanently on file as the core tax identity baseline for cross-border e-commerce, including for refund and export-zero-rating substantiation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a federal or BC Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under ITA s. 89(1) and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income at the general federal rate of 15% (after the 38% statutory rate, 10% provincial abatement, and general rate reduction), plus the BC general corporate income tax rate of 12%, for a combined general rate of 27%. A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) qualifies for the Small Business Deduction on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income, reducing the combined BC and federal rate to approximately 11% (9% federal plus 2% BC). Territorial_Scope: Canada employs a worldwide taxation model for resident corporations, with foreign tax credits, foreign affiliate (FAPI) rules under ITA ss. 91-95, and no territorial exemption; a non-resident corporation carrying on business through a permanent establishment in Canada is taxed under ITA s. 115 on Canadian-source business income plus a Part XIV branch tax of 25% (treaty-reduced) under ITA s. 219. Tax_Exempt_Zone: none applies; the entire Province of British Columbia is within the ordinary GST and PST net, save for listed zero-rated or exempt supplies of goods and services.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box classification; legal corporate status is conclusive for tax purposes. Available statutory elections relevant to a Canadian corporation and its non-resident parent include: (i) ETA s. 156 election to zero-rate otherwise taxable supplies between GST/HST registrants (including intercompany administrative and financing services); (ii) ETA s. 167(1) election to treat the sale of a business as a going concern as outside the scope of GST/HST; (iii) ETA s. 273 joint election for closely related corporations to treat certain intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration; (iv) ITA s. 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of property into the corporation; (v) ITA s. 219 branch tax election (Form T1533) by a non-resident corporation to exempt certain debt-financed Canadian branch interest from withholding tax; (vi) ITA s. 216(4) election for non-residents on rental income from Canadian real estate; and (vii) the hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 (SC 2022, c. 10), which deny a deduction in Canada for a payment that creates a hybrid mismatch (deductible in Canada without a full ordinary inclusion in the recipient jurisdiction), an election-driven regime that restricts abusive hybrid structures despite the absence of check-the-box classification. Any GST/HST election must be filed with CRA on the prescribed form before the election becomes effective, and intercompany transfer-pricing documentation under ITA s. 247 must support the terms of all cross-border transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 for GST/HST (the small-supplier limit measured on worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada in the current calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, registration is mandatory and must be completed within 29 days; non-resident vendors that carry on business in Canada apply the same threshold to their Canadian business supplies). For the BC PST there is no monetary threshold: every vendor making taxable sales of tangible personal property in BC, including online sellers of oversized furniture and home decor, must register for a PST vendor account through eTaxBC before the first taxable sale is made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered corporation may claim the 0% GST rate on exports of tangible personal property under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, item 1 where the supplier ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada, including oversized furniture sold online and exported from a Canadian fulfillment warehouse to a non-resident customer. For drop-shipment arrangements, ETA s. 179.1 can zero-rate certain supplies of goods made by a registrant to a non-resident drop-shipper, provided title and risk pass outside Canada. Services supplied in respect of the online retail platform (logistics, software, storage) are zero-rated only if they qualify under ETA s. 7 (services performed on tangible personal property for export), s. 23 (services delivered to a non-resident for consumption wholly outside Canada), or s. 23.1 (services in respect of property situated outside Canada). BC PST is not charged when goods are delivered or made available outside BC. Substantiation requires contemporaneous proof that the recipient is a non-resident and that the goods physically left Canada within a reasonable period, ordinarily within 60 days of sale, as applied in CRA administrative practice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CBSA CARM G7 export reporting record) naming the Canadian exporter BN, HTS classification, value, and foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or truck) or Master/House Air Waybill naming the Canadian vendor as shipper and the foreign buyer as consignee, showing the foreign port of discharge
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement record (SWIFT MT103/MT202, FX contract, or CAD credit voucher) evidencing receipt of settlement funds from the non-resident purchaser
  • Commercial invoice expressly stating 'GST/HST 0% - Export' and citing ETA Schedule VI, Part V, item 1
  • Packing list and final delivery proof (signed POD at the foreign destination or foreign customs entry document) confirming goods left Canada
  • For zero-rated services: written contract with the non-resident recipient and evidence that the service is performed and enjoyed wholly outside Canada

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), API tax-stamping, or digital-clearance mandate for invoices. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and supported by the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service based on the PEPPOL network, launched by the CRA in 2024 for voluntary adoption. GST/HST registrants are nevertheless required under ETA s. 286 to retain all books, records, and invoices in readable electronic or paper form, and electronic records must be preserved and retrievable in Canada on demand. Exporters of goods are subject to the CBSA electronic export reporting mandate: export declarations (B13A / Canada Customs Coding Form or the CARM G7 single-window record) must be filed electronically for goods with a value over CAD 2,500 or goods in regulated categories, naming the exporter BN, the HTS classification, and the foreign destination. There is no digital stamping or invoice-clearing API in the Canadian GST system.

The CRA maintains a dedicated Trade Verification / Export Verification program under which zero-rated export claims are matched against customs declarations, carrier records, payment flows, and foreign destination documentation; any missing or inconsistent evidence will trigger reassessment of the supply at 5% GST. Consequential clawback and penalty exposures include: (i) ETA s. 280(1) penalty equal to 10% of the tax not collected, plus arrears interest; (ii) ETA s. 285(1) penalty of 25% of the amount assessable where a false statement was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence; (iii) ETA s. 285.1 penalties up to 50% of the tax amount for misrepresentation in rebate or net-tax claims; (iv) ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; (v) criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 carrying fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years; (vi) retroactive loss of input tax credits claimed on expenses allocable to the zero-rated export transaction; and (vii) application of the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245) and sham doctrine where export documentation is contrived or the transaction lacks commercial substance. The normal CRA reassessment period is four years, extended to ten years or longer where a return involved misrepresentation, neglect, fraud, or failure to file (ITA s. 152(4); ETA s. 298), and director liability for unremitted GST can be asserted under ETA s. 316. Although Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-currency repatriation deadline, the 180-day convention is the strongest audit-safe settlement standard for open-account cross-border trade; payments outstanding materially beyond that benchmark may cause the CRA to question whether a genuine export sale occurred and to subject the receivable to transfer-pricing scrutiny under ITA s. 247.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Canada is an IMF Article VIII member; there are no exchange control restrictions, no licensing gates, and no prior approval for capital injections, dividend repatriation, profit remittance, or trade settlement. The operative constraints for a foreign-owned BC corporation are: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends, interest, rents, and royalties (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced; e.g., 5% on dividends under the Canada-US treaty for qualifying corporate holders in a substantial holding, 0%-15% on arm's-length interest); (ii) sanctions screening under Global Affairs Canada regulations (e.g., Russia, Iran, North Korea, Syria) that may freeze counterparty payments; (iii) Investment Canada Act review for foreign investments in sensitive sectors and national security reviews; and (iv) CBSA import duty and excise compliance for physical goods. Online retail of oversized home furniture (ISIC 4791 / NAICS 449110) is not a regulated or restricted activity and encounters no remittance or conversion barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency bank accounts held with authorized Canadian banks. Informational reporting applies at the aggregate level: all non-consumer international electronic transfers are reported through the authorized dealer bank's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) filings with Statistics Canada / Balance of Payments framework, and direct investment positions and transactions (generally where a shareholder holds 10% or more voting interest) are reportable under the Statistics Canada Survey of Direct Investment. For oversized-furniture import-oriented e-commerce, export proceeds are not a material compliance item; the more relevant obligations are CBSA import declarations, customs duties, and GST/PST collection on domestic sales.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange control functions are residual, with regulatory oversight shared by the Department of Finance Canada, Global Affairs Canada (economic sanctions), FINTRAC (AML/ATF administration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, PCMLTFA), and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for cross-border trade compliance. Canada repealed the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951, and no centralized exchange control authority exists for day-to-day currency approval.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment rail, Low as the primary treasury account. Canada does not offer a European-style E-Money Institution (EMI) licence; non-bank payment providers must register with FINTRAC as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), as Payment Service Providers with the Bank of Canada. Offshore digital EMIs (UK or EU e-money licence holders) may legally service the entity for payouts, supplier disbursements, or multi-currency float, but they cannot accept CDIC-insured deposits, cannot clear CAD directly through Payments Canada (Lynx), and will commonly reject businesses with high cross-border trade flows due to correspondent-bank de-risking. Recommended architecture: maintain primary operating and escrow accounts with a Schedule I bank; use Stripe for consumer card acceptance and Affirm BNPL payouts; use Wise or Currencycloud only for low-value foreign supplier payments, with daily sweeps to the Canadian bank.

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment rail, Low as the primary treasury account. Canada does not offer a European-style E-Money Institution (EMI) licence; non-bank payment providers must register with FINTRAC as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), as Payment Service Providers with the Bank of Canada. Offshore digital EMIs (UK or EU e-money licence holders) may legally service the entity for payouts, supplier disbursements, or multi-currency float, but they cannot accept CDIC-insured deposits, cannot clear CAD directly through Payments Canada (Lynx), and will commonly reject businesses with high cross-border trade flows due to correspondent-bank de-risking. Recommended architecture: maintain primary operating and escrow accounts with a Schedule I bank; use Stripe for consumer card acceptance and Affirm BNPL payouts; use Wise or Currencycloud only for low-value foreign supplier payments, with daily sweeps to the Canadian bank.

Compliant protocol for equity or shareholder-loan injection: (i) execute a share subscription agreement or a written shareholder loan agreement, and pass a board resolution; (ii) remit by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign source account to the named Canadian operating account with exact beneficiary legal name and account number; (iii) use the SWIFT remittance field 70 to specify 'Equity injection / share capital' or 'Shareholder loan', and include the corporate registration number (BC company number) to prevent returns due to reference mismatch; Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate rate (15% federal + 12% British Columbia provincial); 11% combined small business rate (9% federal + 2% BC) on first CA$500,000 of active business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on gross dividends paid to non-residents (Part XIII tax), subject to treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$5,000,000 aggregate value of related-party transactions in the tax year AND CA$100,000,000 gross revenue of the Canadian entity triggers mandatory Local File/related-party transaction reporting; Master File is required for groups with consolidated revenue above CA$500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in the UAE, but the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and Canadian residence rules rest on real economic presence, management and control in Canada, and substance-based definitions of permanent establishment. A lack of Canadian administrative substance may cause CRA to deny treaty benefits under the principal purpose provision (Article 29 of the OECD-based DTA), and to recharacterize related-party transactions under Section 247.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to CA$2,000,000 (over 400% of the surcharge) — actually criminal fine up to CA$1,000,000 for any count and imprisonment up to 5 years (Section 239(1) only applies to indictable offence; CRA prosecutions use maximum 5 years + fine up to 200% of tax evaded). For BC provincial offences, penalties under the BC Income Tax Act can reach 200% of the evaded amount plus fines up to CA$250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under Section 160 of the Income Tax Act for amounts transferred at non-arm's length to evade tax; CRA may issue director's liability assessments under Section 227.1 for employee source deductions/AIT; criminal charges can trigger personal fines, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment for up to 5 years.
Shell Company Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR applies to deny the tax benefit and to adjust the transaction to arm's length result; treaty relief may be refused under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) in Article 29, resulting in a 25% withholding tax on payments; CRA can also impose Part VI.1 tax on dividend payments to non-residents at 25% if transactions are structured as dividend substitutes.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders who knowingly participate in a sham can be pursued under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil; CRA may assess civil penalties equal to 100% of the evasion amount plus interest. Non-resident directors may face travel bans and extradition if criminal tax charges are laid.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Over-Under-Pricing

Statutory sanction: CRA can adjust price under Section 247 and apply penalties of 10% of the gross transfer-pricing adjustment in a corporation's taxable income; interest runs on the net amount; documentation deficiencies trigger a rebuttal presumption of negligence. Late-filed T106 forms for related-party transactions carry CA$500 per month penalties (maximum CA$8,000).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing documentation may be subject to civil penalties of up to CA$24,000 under the Excise Tax Act (Section 285); CRA also has director liability for unremitted GST/HST and source deductions on e-commerce payroll, leading to personal bank account garnishment and travel restrictions while on assessment.
Dividend WHT Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Non-resident withholding tax is 25%; failure to withhold and remit attracts 10% penalty plus 5% penalty per month (maximum 12 months) under section 227(8); interest at prescribed rates; CRA also may assess a 15% penalty for claiming treaty benefits if the TRC is not timely filed.

Director personal liability: Directors of a Canadian company are personal sureties for unremitted Part XIII tax, and CRA can exercise a director's liability assessment under section 227.1 if there is gross negligence. The director of the non-resident parent can be prosecuted for an abusively structured dividend scheme as an accessory, resulting in fines up to CA$100,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years.
