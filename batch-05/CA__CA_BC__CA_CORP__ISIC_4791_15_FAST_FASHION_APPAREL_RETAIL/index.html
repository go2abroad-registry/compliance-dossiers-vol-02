Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; a BC business corporation may be incorporated with zero or nominal issued share capital. No escrow or deposit account is required under the BCBCA. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered by the BC Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation and Reporting System, https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA, which is the statutory TIN. Register online through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) using Form RC1, identifying legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, directors/officers, and Canadian business activities. CRA then establishes the corporate income tax program account (RC0001) and, where registration is required or voluntary, the GST/HST program account (RT0001). For BC PST, a non-resident must separately register via the BC Ministry of Finance eRegister service using the CRA BN as the base identifier; the PST account number is a linked sub-account of the BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box regime. A Business Corporation is automatically a taxable entity at the federal and provincial levels; however, statutory elections relevant to non-resident shareholders or cross-border arrangements include: ITA section 216 (non-resident’s elective net-basis Canadian tax return on rental or timber royalties), ITA section 217 (non-resident’s election to be taxed at Canadian resident graduated rates on certain Canadian-source income), ITA subsection 93(1) (election on disposition of shares of foreign affiliate), and ITA subsection 115.3 (optional election respecting Canadian business income of non-resident affiliates). Hybrid mismatch relief is constrained by ITA sections 18.4, 18.6, and 163.2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 over any four consecutive calendar quarters (or more than C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter when a business is carried on in Canada). BC PST: non-resident/out-of-province vendors must register for PST when taxable sales delivered into British Columbia exceed C$10,000 over a rolling 12-month period (economic nexus rule effective 2023); resident Canadian businesses with a BC place of business are required to register regardless of sales volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under GST/HST applies to supplies of tangible personal property (e.g., apparel and footwear) that are exported by the supplier in the course of the supply, provided the supplier maintains documentary proof that the goods have been removed from Canada. Goods shipped from Canada to a foreign address (including via courier, postal, or freight) are entitled to 0% GST provided export occurs within a reasonable period (CRA practice: generally within 90 days of sale). Services supplied to non-residents may qualify for zero-rating under Sched. VI, Part V if the services are performed outside Canada or are directly attributable to an exported good. Fast-fashion e-commerce operations shipping internationally from Canadian fulfilment centres must secure customer export shipment evidence to claim 0% GST, while BC PST generally does not apply to goods shipped for final consumption outside BC.

  • B13A Export Declaration (or electronic export report filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing Canadian origin and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the national zero-rated supply nature
  • Proof of payment (bank inward remittance certificate, SWIFT confirmation, or card settlement record) evidencing the export sale in foreign currency

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, pre-issuance digital stamping, or compulsory API clearance. GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed information (tax registry number, date, vendor name, amount, tax paid or payable); zero-rated and exempt supplies require simplified invoice details. CRA accepts electronic filing via GST/HST NETFILE and My Business Account; API connectivity is available for certain third-party filing services. Pan-Canadian e-invoicing (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is being piloted on a voluntary basis only. BC PST requires standard sales invoices; no separate e-invoice clearance or government pre-approval applies.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess GST/HST at 5% where export documentation is incomplete, inaccurate, or wilfully fabricated. Normal reassessment period is four years, but is unlimited where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default is established. Specific penalties under the Excise Tax Act include: s.280 (25% penalty on net tax deficiency), s.285 (penalty for false statements up to 25% of the amount of tax sought to be evaded), and s.327 (criminal tax evasion — fines up to 200% of the evaded amount plus imprisonment up to five years). Interest under s.280 accrues at prescribed rates. In BC, the Provincial Sales Tax Act imposes additional penalties (25% of the tax amount or C$100, whichever is greater) for failure to collect/remit PST, and the Director may retroactively claw back zero-rated export treatment if goods are subsequently brought back into BC within a relevant period or the export sale is found to be a sham.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. No exchange control legislation restricts cross-border movement of CAD or foreign currency for trade, e-commerce settlement, or investment. The Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985) establishes CAD legal tender; the Bank of Canada Act governs monetary policy without imposing approval gates. A BC-incorporated fast-fashion e-commerce corporation may freely invoice in foreign currency, hold foreign-currency bank accounts abroad, and repatriate capital or remit profits subject only to (i) Part XIII Income Tax Act non-resident dividend withholding (statutory 25%, commonly reduced to 15% under the Canada-US treaty or 5% for qualifying treaty shareholders), (ii) FINTRAC reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000+ large cash transactions, electronic funds transfer reports of CAD 10,000+ inbound/outbound), and (iii) corporate law solvency tests. Practical hurdles are commercial rather than regulatory: settlement currency conversion spreads on Shopify Payments and Klarna BNPL flows, and documentation burden for foreign-currency accounting under IFRS/ASPE.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no repatriation deadline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts from apparel and footwear sales may be retained in USD, CAD, or any freely convertible currency in any eligible deposit account. The company must, however, maintain underlying commercial documents for GST/HST zero-rating of exported goods (Excise Tax Act) and for FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting for transactions over CAD 10,000. No central bank approval is required for retention or deployment of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). No standalone Foreign Exchange Administration exists; monetary and prudential oversight is split among these three bodies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility as a supplementary liquidity rail, Medium as a primary settlement account. Canada has no dedicated EMI license; foreign digital banks and e-money institutions (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Relay) operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) that must register with FINTRAC and, in British Columbia, must satisfy provincial money-lending and remittance registration requirements. Legal constraints: (i) no CDIC deposit insurance for funds held with non-domestic EMIs; (ii) EMIs cannot directly clear CAD through Payments Canada Lynx/ACSS; (iii) Klarna BNPL merchant settlement must be routed to a bank account, and most BNPL schemes favor a Schedule I bank account for reconciliation; (iv) Shopify Payments payouts settle via a linked Canadian bank account using standard EFT. Recommended deployment: hold only operating float (max 7 days of disbursement needs) in Wise or Payoneer for cross-border supplier payments to China and Southeast Asia; keep primary treasury and compliance relationships at a Tier 1 Schedule I bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Non-resident or tiered beneficial ownership structure (offshore parent or foreign investor holding shares in the BC corporation)

Defense evidence: Full organizational chart; BC Registry extracts showing registered office and directors; Register of Individuals with Significant Control (ISC Form 8); notarized passports of all shareholders above 10%; source-of-funds statements from the investing entity's bank; Canadian tax residency certificates for the BC corporation; audited or reviewed financial statements (if available); written explanation of the commercial rationale for the holding structure.

Root cause: BNPL and e-commerce chargeback velocity triggering merchant acquiring flags (Klarna receivables)

Rapid inbound capital injection before generation of meaningful revenue or trade history

Root cause: Rapid inbound capital injection before generation of meaningful revenue or trade history

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for equity or shareholder-loan injections: (i) Execute governing instrument (share subscription agreement or written shareholder loan agreement with arm's-length interest rate to avoid Income Tax Act section 15 and thin-capitalization issues); (ii) board resolution authorizing issuance or borrowing; (iii) routing via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's bank with ISO 20022 purpose code for capital contribution (e.g., code 'CUST' or 'EQUI' or bank-specific 'CADT' for direct investment) and a beneficiary account at the Canadian Tier 1 bank; (iv) for USD injections, funds clear through a US correspondent intermediary (e.g., RBC Bank Georgia or TD Bank N.A.) then via Payments Canada Lynx for same-day CAD settlement; (v) the receiving bank will apply FATCA/CRS verification and FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting for amounts at or above CAD 10,000; (vi) archival evidence must include the subscription agreement, board minutes, share certificates issued, incoming wire confirmations, and ledger posting to share capital or shareholder loan account. Payments above CAD 50,000 may trigger enhanced source-of-wealth review; advance submission of the capital deployment schedule materially reduces clearing delay.Primary rail for supplier settlements: SWIFT MT103 URDG-compliant telegraphic transfer in USD, EUR, or CNY via the Tier 1 bank's correspondent network; CAD disbursements to domestic suppliers via Payments Canada ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) batch EFT with T+1 settlement, or Lynx RTGS for immediate value. For larger confirmed purchase orders (above CAD 250,000), use a commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700) issued by the Canadian bank in favor of the apparel supplier, with full set of documents (invoice, bill of lading, packing list, inspection certificate). Open-account terms with trade credit insurance (EDC or private insurer) are recommended for recurring vendor relationships. All outbound wires must use the applicable ISO 20022 purpose code (e.g., 'TRAD' for goods trade) and FINTRAC reporting applies at CAD 10,000 or more. Klarna BNPL and Shopify Payments settlement flows arrive via standard card-network and ACH/EFT clearing, batched by the payment processor, and are swept daily to the operating account; treasury policy should set a maximum aggregate float of CAD 250,000 in the payment processor to limit counterparty and insolvency exposure.Outright CAD/USD forwards (deliverable, up to 12 months tenor) with execution by the Tier 1 bank trade desk; fully permissible for hedging committed inventory purchases.Outright CAD/CNY forwards (deliverable or non-deliverable depending on counterparty policies; NDFs are permissible but economically unnecessary because CNY has an offshore deliverable market in Hong Kong).CAD/USD FX swaps for rolling short-term liquidity positions (legally permissible under Canadian banking law as no options dealer license is required for end-users).Vanilla exchange-traded or bank-OTC FX options on CAD/USD and CAD/CNY, purchased only on a zero-premium-collateral basis to hedge committed receivables; naked option-selling is restricted by OSFI prudential guidance for non-financial end-users.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CAD/USD basis swaps) for asset-liability management of the USD operating account, subject to board-approved treasury policy and IFRS 9 hedge accounting documentation.Prohibited: leveraged speculative derivatives without underlying trade exposure, structured products containing barrier features, and any forward settlement in excess of 12 months for non-capital-account transactions without board authorization.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility as a supplementary liquidity rail, Medium as a primary settlement account. Canada has no dedicated EMI license; foreign digital banks and e-money institutions (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Relay) operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) that must register with FINTRAC and, in British Columbia, must satisfy provincial money-lending and remittance registration requirements. Legal constraints: (i) no CDIC deposit insurance for funds held with non-domestic EMIs; (ii) EMIs cannot directly clear CAD through Payments Canada Lynx/ACSS; (iii) Klarna BNPL merchant settlement must be routed to a bank account, and most BNPL schemes favor a Schedule I bank account for reconciliation; (iv) Shopify Payments payouts settle via a linked Canadian bank account using standard EFT. Recommended deployment: hold only operating float (max 7 days of disbursement needs) in Wise or Payoneer for cross-border supplier payments to China and Southeast Asia; keep primary treasury and compliance relationships at a Tier 1 Schedule I bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 in aggregate cost or receipts for non-arm's length cross-border transactions triggers mandatory Form T106 disclosure and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File-level) under ITA s.247; consolidated group revenue of C$750 million triggers OECD Country-by-Country Reporting (master file may be requested by CRA, though no separate statutory form is filed).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply U.A.E.-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the CRA and OECD-guided GAAR under ITA s.245 operate as a de facto economic substance requirement. A BC fast-fashion apparel corporation must demonstrate real commercial substance—Canadian employees, client-facing decisions, inventory risk, platform contracts, and board meetings in BC—to support both primary residency and PE attribution. Merely holding a BC certificate with an offshore manager is a 'loophole-factory' red flag; CRA can reattribute profits to a Canadian PE of the non-resident, disallow intercompany payments, and impose double taxation plus interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Revenue Reporting

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2): 50% of federal and BC income tax understated; criminal conviction under ITA s.239(1) provides fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA applies compound daily arrears interest at prescribed rate plus 4%.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate are jointly and severally liable for unremitted payroll/GST/HST held in trust, while controlling minds face personal criminal liability for wilful evasion, civil fraud, and CRA-issued travel bans prohibiting leave from Canada to offshore tax havens.
Shell Company / Artificial Permanent Establishment Avoidance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessments under ITA s.245 deny tax benefits of the shell structure, reallocating profits to the BC enterprise; transfer pricing adjustments under s.247(2) plus s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the net adjustment if documentation is defective; daily compound interest runs from the original filing due date.

Director personal liability: Directors and nominee shareholders who sign false statutory declarations can be pursued for GST/HST and payroll director liability, and under British Columbia Business Corporations Act, the veil may be pierced for fraudulent abuse of corporate form, resulting in personal restitution, restrained travel, and potential regulatory prosecution.
AML / False Statements to Revenue Authority

Statutory sanction: False statements penalty under ITA s.163(2): 50% of tax understated; where no tax understatement, unspecified penalties between C$100 and C$25,000; Criminal Code fraud (s.380(1)) carries imprisonment up to 14 years when the value exceeds C$1,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or authorize false filings are criminally liable as parties to the offence; CRA may issue a civil collection order preventing travel outside Canada and charge a director personally for all monies deemed held in trust for the Crown (payroll and GST/PST), piercing the corporation's limitation shield.
