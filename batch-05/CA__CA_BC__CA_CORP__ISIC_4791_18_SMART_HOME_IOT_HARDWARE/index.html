Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — single national tax administrator for GST/HST, federal corporate income tax, and BC provincial corporate income tax (collected under a federal–provincial tax collection agreement); online portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account at canada.ca. Provincial counterpart: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, eTaxBC portal for the 7% BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) before commencing Canadian commercial operations. Procedure: (1) file Form RC1, Request for a Business Number, either online via BRO or by mail/courier, attaching certified evidence of incorporation and proof of foreign registered office; (2) select the required program accounts — 'RT' for GST/HST (sales tax), 'RC' for corporate income tax, and 'RP' if payroll remittances are anticipated; (3) if the foreign corporation has no Canadian physical presence, it must designate an authorized Canadian representative/agent with a domestic address, and may be required to provide a security deposit under s. 240 ETA at the CRA's discretion; (4) CRA issues the BN (9-digit base plus two-letter program identifier and four-digit reference, e.g., 123456789 RT 0001) within 5–10 business days for paper filings and immediately for online filings. A separate BC PST vendor account number is obtained via eTaxBC for PST collection on taxable retail sales in BC; no filing fee applies. The GST/HST registration is also required for non-resident vendors under the 2021 deemed-supplier rules where the vendor's platform or fulfillment network supplies goods to Canadian consumers and the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope — a business corporation incorporated federally or in British Columbia is resident in Canada and subject to federal Part I tax (Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c.1) on worldwide income, plus BC provincial corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act (BC); foreign-incorporated corporations may become Canadian-resident where central management and control is situated in Canada. Canada does not operate a check-the-box or pass-through classification regime; an entity's classification for Canadian tax purposes generally follows its legal form under the foreign jurisdiction, with hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance rules under ss. 18.4–18.5 ITA and the EIFEL (Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation) provisions constraining structures that create double deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope — a business corporation incorporated federally or in British Columbia is resident in Canada and subject to federal Part I tax (Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c.1) on worldwide income, plus BC provincial corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act (BC); foreign-incorporated corporations may become Canadian-resident where central management and control is situated in Canada. Canada does not operate a check-the-box or pass-through classification regime; an entity's classification for Canadian tax purposes generally follows its legal form under the foreign jurisdiction, with hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance rules under ss. 18.4–18.5 ITA and the EIFEL (Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation) provisions constraining structures that create double deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes. For this ISIC 4791 smart-home IoT retailing enterprise, all cross-border retail sales revenue is Canadian-taxable at the corporate level, subject to foreign tax credits for source-country withholding taxes.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada; the operative elections are: (1) s. 216(4) ITA election for a non-resident to report Canadian rental net income on a net basis; (2) s. 217 ITA election for a non-resident to file a Part I return on Canadian-source income; (3) s. 87 ITA amalgamation and s. 88(2) ITA winding-up elections; (4) ETA s. 211 election (supplies between closely related corporations treated as made for nil consideration) and ETA s. 273 election (exempt supplies between related persons); (5) EIFEL joint election under the proposed excessive finance expense rules; and (6) GST/HST voluntary registration election for a non-resident to opt into the simplified non-resident registration regime (s. 242.1 ETA). Elections must be filed in prescribed form with CRA before the relevant filing due date to be valid.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST registration is mandatory where taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold, s. 148 ETA); for non-resident vendors, the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies with an additional economic-nexus test for deemed-supplier platforms under Part IX of the ETA (effective July 1, 2021). BC PST registration is mandatory for every vendor carrying on business in BC (including online sellers with inventory located in BC or over CAD 10,000 of annual taxable sales into BC) irrespective of the GST threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exports is available under s. 1 (goods) and s. 7 (services) of Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. For smart home IoT hardware: (a) goods must be delivered by the supplier or the supplier's agent to a common carrier or shipped by mail/courier to a destination outside Canada; (b) for goods exceeding CAD 30 in value, the supplier must obtain evidence that the goods were exported within 60 days of the supply (CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17.3); (c) title must pass to a non-resident customer outside Canada; (d) services supplied to non-residents (e.g., software activation, cloud-connectivity services bundled with IoT devices) are zero-rated where the service is not performed for a person present in Canada and is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property. The foreign destination country's import VAT/GST (including U.S. retail delivery taxes or EU IOSS) is outside Canadian zero-rating and must be administered separately. Where goods are shipped to a Canadian freight forwarder for consolidation, zero-rating applies only if the supply is made before export and the forwarder's export documentation is retained by the supplier.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Form B13A / B13A via an authorized customs broker, mandatory for exports with commercial value over CAD 2,000
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / courier manifest showing the consignee and destination outside Canada
  • Commercial Invoice / Sales Invoice with supplier's BN, GST zero-rate notation, and Incoterms
  • Proof of Export — CRA Form GST11 (Export Certification) or carrier/customs broker certificate confirming the goods left Canada within 60 days
  • Packing list, freight invoice, and insurance documents corroborating the export movement
  • Foreign exchange settlement records (CAD conversion, payment gateway remittance detail from Stripe/Shopify Payments) — Canada's fully liberalized FX regime does not require an inward remittance certificate for export substantiation

Canada imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based continuous transaction controls, and no digital stamping regime. GST/HST registrants must issue a sales invoice containing the supplier's BN, invoice date, recipient name, the total consideration, and the GST paid/collectible (or, for zero-rated exports, a clear indication that the supply is zero-rated under Schedule VI to the ETA); invoices may be issued in paper or electronically. E-filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million (GST34 electronic remittance via CRA My Business Account or approved third-party software). The CRA has adopted voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing standards aligned with the Peppol framework, piloted in 2024–2025, but issuance and clearance remain optional; e-commerce platform operators must collect and remit GST/HST under the deemed-supplier regime, with platform-level filing using the GST34 e-service.

Canada has no statutory forced-FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day default in the schema represents the administrative safe-harbour period adopted by CRA for reasonable commercial settlement cycles in cross-border IoT retail. CRA audit teams verify zero-rating claims against CBSA export declarations, carrier records, and platform payment data; failure to produce the documentary bundle within the four-year normal reassessment window (ten years on misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect) results in reassessment of the GST at 5% on gross revenue plus prescribed interest (currently 7% for corporations, compounded daily). Penalties include: s. 280(1) ETA false-statement penalty of the greater of CAD 5,000 and 60% of the tax sought to be evaded; s. 285 ETA administrative penalty of CAD 250 for each false or omitted invoice amount; s. 239(1) ITA criminal tax evasion — fines of 50% to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years; clawback of input tax credits (ITCs) claimed in respect of export inventory, since export zero-rating and ITC entitlement rise and fall together; and the s. 245 ITA General Anti-Avoidance Rule may re-characterize circular export–reimportation structures designed to create artificial GST refunds or provincial PST avoidance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a pure floating exchange-rate regime with unrestricted CAD convertibility on both current and capital account; the Foreign Exchange Control regime was abolished in 1951 and no exchange-control licence or Bank of Canada authorization is required for any commercial transfer, dividend remittance, royalty, intercompany loan, or repatriation of equity by non-residents. Canada accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, confirming the absence of restrictions on payments and transfers. The practical profit-remittance hurdles are tax and corporate-law gates, not FX gates: (i) the CBCA/BCBCA solvency test before dividends are declared; (ii) Part XIII withholding tax of 25% on dividends and certain cross-border payments, treaty-reduced to 5% (US parent holding at least 10% of the voting stock) or 10% (PRC/HK treaty shareholders); and (iii) FATCA/CRS reporting by the bank plus the corporation's own reporting under CRA Form T1135 for specified foreign property held in excess of CAD 100,000 (e.g., offshore vendor-deposit balances).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), constituted under the Bank of Canada Act; prudential bank supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act of Canada; AML and financial-intelligence enforcement by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); cross-border payments clearing by Payments Canada (Lynx high-value real-time gross settlement and the Automated Clearing Settlement System, ACSS); payment-service-provider registration by the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) effective November 2024.
