Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and corporation income tax, with digital portal 'My Business Account' and GST/HST NETFILE; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST), with portal 'eTaxBC'.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishing a Canadian business corporation must first incorporate federally or provincially under the Canada Business Corporations Act or British Columbia Business Corporations Act. The new corporation then obtains its federal Business Number (BN, also used as GST/HST registration number and corporate income tax program account) by registering with CRA through Form RC1 or online via CRA's Business Registration Online. For PST purposes, the corporation must separately register as a PST vendor with the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC before making taxable sales of tangible personal property in British Columbia. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada are entitled to the same BN/GST/HST registration process; a Canadian bank account, authorized representative, or Canadian mailing address may be required for CRA correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Canadian business corporation is a taxpayer separate from its shareholders, resident in Canada, and taxable on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act. No pass-through treatment is available; corporate income is taxed at the entity level and distributions are separately subject to Canadian dividend withholding rules where applicable.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. The Income Tax Act provides specific statutory elections, including the taxable Canadian corporation election under section 256.1 for control group reattribution, section 85 rollover elections for property transfers to corporations, and dual-resident corporation treaty-tie-breaker equivalences. For indirect tax purposes, closely related corporations may elect under subsection 156(1) of the Excise Tax Act to self-assess GST/HST on certain supplies, and BC PST rules do not provide a general election to reclassify entity type. Canadian hybrid mismatch rules apply under OECD BEPS Action 2 and do not permit elective pass-through status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible, and Canada maintains an open capital account with no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital transactions. An online auto-parts e-commerce business (ISIC 4791) may freely receive export-sales revenue, pay foreign suppliers, repatriate capital, and remit dividends without Bank of Canada licensing or central-bank approval. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are not restricted by FX law but are subject to Canadian Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, typically reduced to 15% or 5% under applicable tax treaties, e.g., Canada-US Treaty Article X), and cross-border wire transfers of C$10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting duties on the financial intermediary.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule exists in Canada. There is no mandatory repatriation timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no requirement to channel export receipts through a designated domestic bank account. An exporter may retain foreign currency balances in Canadian or offshore accounts at its discretion. However, all financial intermediaries must file FINTRAC large-transaction and suspicious-transaction reports and maintain prescribed records under the PCMLTFA; voluntary conversion of USD or CNY receipts into CAD is executed at the prevailing interbank rate through a tier-1 bank or registered money services business.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting with the Department of Finance Canada as the federal monetary and exchange-rate policy authority; prudential banking supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering and terrorist financing supervision is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (conditional). Canadian-registered fintechs and money services businesses (MSBs), including Wise Canada, Nuvei, Stripe Treasury, and Payoneer, are viable as ancillary payment or settlement rails for an auto-parts e-commerce treasury. Offshore unregulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not viable as primary clearing counterparties: they cannot access Payments Canada systems (Lynx or Real-Time Rail), cannot maintain a CAD settlement account at a tier-1 Canadian bank without FINTRAC MSB registration, and are subject to provincial money-services licensing requirements. Legal constraints comprise PCMLTFA registration, provincial consumer-protection licensing, bank counterparty concentration limits, sanctions screening obligations, and the requirement that the operating entity retain a tier-1 Canadian bank account as the anchor treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the beneficiary corporation's CAD operating account at a tier-1 Canadian bank. The credit package must include the 3-digit institution number (e.g., RBC 003, TD 004, BMO 001, CIBC 010, Scotiabank 002), the 5-digit branch transit number, the bank's SWIFT BIC, and the corporation's legal name exactly as registered. USD-denominated injections may be held in a foreign-currency account or converted to CAD at the bank's prevailing rate with settlement through Payments Canada Lynx (formerly LVTS). The remitter must designate the purpose as 'Equity Contribution' or 'Shareholder Loan' and the compliance file must contain the board resolution, subscription or loan agreement, and source-of-funds evidence. Box 700 Provincial tax payable (BC) BC provincial tax applies to income attributable to a permanent establishment in BC. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 3% per month up to 12 months; interest on unpaid amounts at prescribed rate (currently 8% for 2024).
Box 800 Total tax payable (federal + provincial) Include any instalment payments and credits. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 3% per month up to 12 months; interest on unpaid amounts at prescribed rate (currently 8% for 2024).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for services; CAD 5,000,000 for other transactions

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s. 245 of the Income Tax Act and treaty abuse provisions (s. 94.1) require genuine economic substance. A shell company without real business activity may be challenged for treaty benefits and subject to reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s. 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties: gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under s. 227.1 if they fail to withhold or remit source deductions. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for criminal conviction.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits, reassess income, and apply GAAR penalties (25% of tax benefit). Administrative penalties for failure to provide documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for tax liabilities if they knowingly participated in a sham arrangement. Personal liability for penalties and interest.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations; criminal penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly failed to implement AML compliance programs. Travel bans and imprisonment for willful non-compliance.
