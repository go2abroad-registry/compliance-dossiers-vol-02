Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Business Registration Online and My Business Account (canada.ca); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export and import reporting; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, eTaxBC for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST).

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Canadian corporation: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act; (2) request the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Business Registration Online or Form RC1; (3) open required CRA program accounts, including GST/HST (RT) and, if exporting goods, the CBSA import/export program account; (4) register separately for BC PST through eTaxBC when taxable sales are made in British Columbia; (5) use the BN as the statutory tax/VAT identifier on all invoices and customs documents. GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration may be obtained earlier to claim input tax credits and facilitate export zero-rating.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification regime; a Business Corporation is an entity-level taxpayer subject to Part I corporate income tax. Available mechanisms include the capital dividend account election, the associated-corporation allocation designation for the small business deduction, and GST/HST elections for closely related corporations and the quick method. Hybrid mismatch rules are implemented as automatic deduction-denial and inclusion-denial provisions, not as elective check-the-box classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in a single quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; BC PST: registration required once a vendor makes taxable sales in British Columbia, with no general small-supplier exemption for non-resident sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible musical instruments and studio audio gear are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act when the registrant supplies goods that are delivered outside Canada or to a common carrier for export and the goods are exported without further processing in Canada. Digital audio products and offshore services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated when the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not performed or enjoyed in Canada. The seller must retain sufficient export evidence to claim the 0% rate and to support input tax credits on related purchases.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration / Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) report
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill issued by the common carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign-exchange receipt evidencing export payment
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Foreign destination customs entry or import certificate
  • Freight forwarder certificate or carrier certificate of export

Canada has no national real-time e-invoicing clearance or fiscal stamping mandate. GST/HST invoices must identify the vendor's Business Number, date, and amounts of tax; electronic invoices are accepted. CRA does not pre-clear export invoices; exporters must retain contemporaneous documentary proof of export. BC PST is administered through eTaxBC filing and remittance, without transaction-level digital stamping.

CRA and CBSA cross-check export declarations, courier manifests, payment rails, bank statements and destination-country customs records. If the export is not substantiated, CRA re-assesses the supply as taxable in Canada, recovering 5% GST plus interest, and may apply civil penalties for false statements or gross negligence; wilful evasion can lead to criminal prosecution. BC PST liability may also be reassessed with interest and penalties if goods are found to have been delivered or used in British Columbia.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account transactions. No exchange control legislation or restrictions exist on profit remittance, dividend distributions, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders, subject to corporate solvency tests and normal tax reporting. Canada subscribes to OECD capital movement liberalization standards, and no prior regulatory approval is required for legal current or capital payments in CAD or foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or convert foreign currency receipts into CAD. Exporters may maintain foreign currency bank accounts in Canada or abroad, and retain balances indefinitely, provided that all transactions are reported for tax purposes and FINTRAC reporting thresholds are met for wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank) and Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF compliance; currency administration follows the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal boundary. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not authorized to open Canadian domestic bank accounts or provide deposit-taking services. They may operate as money services businesses (MSBs) in Canada only if registered with FINTRAC, and, depending on activities, may require a provincial money-lending licence. Payments to and from such EMIs are possible as commercial fund transfers, but settlement finality and customer protection are inferior to tier-1 Canadian bank accounts. Use of an unregistered offshore EMI is a compliance blocker.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to a Canadian business corporation are executed via SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire in USD, using the corporate operational account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The sending bank must reference the corporate legal name, CAD bank account number, and the appropriate purpose code. For direct investment from affiliates, purpose code 'D-30' (direct investment) or 'C-10' (capital transfer) should be cited; for shareholder loans, use purpose code 'L-12'. The receiving Canadian bank applies no exchange control restriction but will require a beneficiary account in good standing and may request a copy of the subscription agreement or shareholder resolution as an underlying document. For equity subscriptions, securities law exemptions (such as the family, friends, and business associates exemption under National Instrument 45-106) must be validated if the investor is a Canadian resident.Trade disbursements for musical instrument and audio equipment purchases use standard international clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for outright supplier payments; SWIFT MT202 for cover or interbank settlements; and documentary commercial letters of credit (LC) issued by the Canadian bank, usually irrevocable, confirmed or unconfirmed, against shipping documents such as the bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin.
