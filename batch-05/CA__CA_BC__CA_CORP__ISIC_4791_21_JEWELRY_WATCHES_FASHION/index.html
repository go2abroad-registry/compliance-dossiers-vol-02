Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada or engaged in taxable supply in Canada must register for the Canadian GST/HST account via the CRA Business Registration Online service (via Represent a Client or My Business Account). The entity must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA, then establish a GST/HST program account. For British Columbia provincial corporate registration, the extra-provincial or domestic corporation must also register with the BC Corporate Online registry (https://www.bcregisters.ca) for a BC incorporation number or extraprovincial registration number. The GST/HST registration threshold is CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies; if exceeded, registration is compulsory. A foreign entity may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits and zero-rate exports.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Canadian Business Corporation (federal or provincial) is a separate taxpayer subject to entity-level federal and provincial corporate income tax; no check-the-box pass-through election exists for Canadian corporations. Elective mechanisms relevant to indirect tax include the GST/HST quick method election, annual filing election, and the ability to elect to have a branch or division treated as a separate segment for GST/HST registration. For cross-border hybrid entities, Canada applies hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act that may recharacterize payments or deny deductions where a foreign jurisdiction treats the Canadian entity as fiscally transparent; specific statutory elections are limited to the CRA administrative forms for GST/HST registrant status and for selected listed financial institutions (not applicable to e-commerce).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports) over any four consecutive calendar quarters, or CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter; mandatory registration applies once the threshold is surpassed. No threshold applies to non-residents who solicit orders in Canada or operate a permanent establishment in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI, Part V applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada by the supplier, or delivered outside Canada by the vendor or its agent, provided the recipient is a non-resident and the goods are exported as soon as practicable. For jewelry and watches, the supply is zero-rated if the seller maintains evidence of export, the goods are exported within 60 days (or as reasonable), and the sale is made under a written contract with an export clause. Services that are physically performed outside Canada, or that relate to exported goods but are supplied to a non-resident, may also be zero-rated; online marketplace sales to non-residents are generally zero-rated when the tangible goods are shipped from Canada to a foreign address.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Invoice / CUSMA or CERS transmission)
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill showing shipper and consignee
  • Proof of shipment and customs clearance from Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for goods exported
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract bearing the export statement
  • Receipt of payment from the non-resident buyer (bank transfer or SWIFT proof) and, where applicable, Inward Remittance Certificate from the receiving financial institution

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government-approved API clearance for B2B or B2C e-invoices. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices with prescribed information (supplier name, BN, customer name, description, tax payable shown) under Excise Tax Act section 238, but transmission may be paper or electronic. The CRA requires electronic records retention in machine-readable format. For e-commerce, platform operators may be deemed agents for GST/HST purposes under the Digital Services Economy; no pre-clearance invoice authorization exists.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rated supply to taxable status if documentation is deficient, retroactively clawing back input tax credits and assessing GST/HST, plus interest and penalties. Under the Excise Tax Act, knowingly making a false or deceptive statement can incur penalties of up to 25% of the tax evaded, or 8% for negligent errors, plus interest; gross negligence penalties may be CRA-discretionary up to 100% of the amount in contestation for wilful participation. Records must be retained for six years. Failure to substantiate export within prescribed timelines results in reversal of zero-rating, additional assessment, and potential prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime. Canada does not impose exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions; profit remittance is lawful and does not require central bank approval, but it may attract Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act. Cross-border capital inflows, dividend distributions, and trade payments can be freely routed through SWIFT, subject only to standard bank-level AML/KYC and sanctions screening. Investments by non-residents may trigger notification under the Investment Canada Act, with transactions below the prescribed monetary thresholds requiring no regulatory approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules in Canada. No Central Bank approval, conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline is mandated. Export proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency account with a Canadian chartered bank or converted at commercial discretion. CBSA declarations and Statistics Canada export reporting obligations still apply for customs and statistical purposes, but they do not impose currency conversion requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank and monetary authority); foreign exchange policy coordination with the Department of Finance Canada, prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/ATF supervision and registration by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (secondary rail only). Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex are viable for small-ticket payment collection, supplier disbursement, or FX conversion, but they are not a substitute for a Schedule I Canadian bank treasury relationship. In Canada, such providers are generally regulated as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, requiring FINTRAC registration and compliance with AML/ATF obligations. They do not have membership in Payments Canada for direct access to the Large Value Transfer System or core CAD clearing, and deposit accounts with them are not eligible for Canadian deposit insurance. For a medium-risk cross-border e-commerce merchant selling jewelry and watches, an unregistered offshore EMI is not legally permissible for primary treasury operations and may create money transmission risk; only a Canadian-registered MSB or a chartered bank should be used as the primary clearing and counterparty settlement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a shareholder capital contribution into the Canadian operating entity: (1) Execute a share subscription agreement or shareholder loan agreement and board/shareholder resolutions; (2) Originate a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's own bank account, with the payment narrative identifying 'share capital contribution' or 'intercompany loan disbursement' and reference to the agreement; (3) Use the Canadian bank's BPM6-aligned purpose category for direct investment equity or intercompany lending—Canadian banks do not use a single mandatory numeric purpose code but require a clear narrative; (4) Ensure the investor's account is in the jurisdiction of the investor and not a third-party payor; (5) Provide the corporate registry extracts, source-of-funds evidence, and beneficial-ownership pack to the receiving Canadian bank; (6) The bank will conduct sanctions, OFAC, and PEP screening, and may credit the funds to a capital escrow or operating account only after verification. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate rate (Federal 15% + British Columbia 12%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII rate on dividends paid to a non-resident; treaty-reduced to 15% portfolio / 5% direct-investment (10% voting-interest shareholder) under the Canada-US Tax Convention and Canada-China DTA; 5% applies to genuinely operating corporate shareholders under most Canadian DTAs.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Income tax payment due within 2 months after fiscal year-end (3 months for a CCPC claiming the small business deduction in the year); T2 return must be filed no later than 6 months after fiscal year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 300 Net income before taxes: accounting profit from worldwide business operations adjusted under Part I of the Income Tax Act (depreciation, reserves, inventory, FX gains/losses, non-deductible expenses). The non-resident-owned BC corporation files the full T2 for income attributable to its Canadian business; foreign-source passive income and foreign head-office income are excluded. Late T2 filing penalty under ITA s. 162(2): 5% of the unpaid balance of tax plus 1% per complete month of delay up to 12 months (17% cap); a repeat offender in any of the three prior years is penalized at 10% plus 2% per month. Not available: a corporation controlled directly or indirectly by non-residents is not a CCPC and is taxed at the full 27% combined rate on all Canadian-source business income. CRA prescribed-rate interest of 7%-9% compounds daily on unpaid tax and instalments; director liability under s. 227.1 attaches to unremitted source deductions included in the penalty base.
Schedule 5 (British Columbia) Tax Calculation Supplementary (BC): allocates taxable income between provinces using the PE sales/wages/property formula and computes BC provincial corporate tax at 12% general or 2% CCPC small-business rate. The corporation must file Schedule 5 and pay BC tax if it has a permanent establishment in British Columbia; a treaty-protected PE may be exempt but the treaty position must be fully disclosed on the T2. Late T2 filing penalty under ITA s. 162(2): 5% of the unpaid balance of tax plus 1% per complete month of delay up to 12 months (17% cap); a repeat offender in any of the three prior years is penalized at 10% plus 2% per month. CRA prescribed-rate interest of 7%-9% compounds daily on unpaid tax and instalments; director liability under s. 227.1 attaches to unremitted source deductions included in the penalty base.
Form T106 Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents: discloses every cross-border related-party transaction in goods, services, loans, guarantees, and intangible property, including the transfer-pricing methodology applied. Filing duty rests solely on the Canadian entity; the foreign parent does not sign or file T106. Late filing is penalized at CAD 24 per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500) and failure to provide contemporaneous documentation can trigger a 10% transfer-pricing adjustment penalty. Late T2 filing penalty under ITA s. 162(2): 5% of the unpaid balance of tax plus 1% per complete month of delay up to 12 months (17% cap); a repeat offender in any of the three prior years is penalized at 10% plus 2% per month. CRA prescribed-rate interest of 7%-9% compounds daily on unpaid tax and instalments; director liability under s. 227.1 attaches to unremitted source deductions included in the penalty base.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File under ITA s. 247(4) is mandatory where the aggregate value of any single category of non-arm's-length transactions (goods, services, loans, guarantee fees) with one non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the fiscal year; a Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated global revenue of at least CAD 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting applies at the EUR 750,000,000 group-revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: British Columbia has no separate Economic Substance Regulation; the federal General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) disregards transactions lacking genuine economic substance, and treaty-shopping defences are blocked by Limitation-of-Benefits clauses and the OECD Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test. CRA audit practice examines physical premises, employees, inventory custody, board decision-making, and bank management in BC; multinational groups with revenue over EUR 750M are also subject to the OECD Pillar Two Global Minimum Top-up Tax effective for fiscal years beginning after 31 December 2023.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Omission

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1): fine of 50%-200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment for up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s. 163(2); failure-to-file and arrears interest accrue at the 7%-9% prescribed rate.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct or assist the concealment are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, CRA civil assessments with the corporate veil pierced on fraud grounds, asset freezes, and international travel bans while under criminal investigation; CRA may also assess directors under s. 227.1 for any unremitted source deductions embedded in the scheme.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA re-determination at the arm's-length price under s. 247(2); a 10% transfer-pricing adjustment penalty under s. 247(3) applies to the net upward adjustment (capped only by the amount of the adjustment); T106 late-filing penalties of CAD 24/day up to CAD 2,500; arrears interest from the year the amounts would have been taxable.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false or misleading T106 schedules can be prosecuted under s. 239(1); an aggressive TP adjustment can render the corporation insolvent, exposing directors to unpaid-payroll liability under s. 227.1 and creditor claims, with veil-piercing available where the pricing arrangement is found to be a sham.
Shell Company / Sham Agency Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization under s. 245 and case-law substance analysis: denial of the small business deduction with full 27% combined tax retroactively assessed; CRA may deem the corporation a bare agent of the foreign principal and allocate the entire profit to the offshore principal, triggering 25% Part XIII withholding on deemed distributions; penalty interest compounds daily.

Director personal liability: Local nominee directors are personally assessed for Part XIII tax they failed to withhold on deemed dividends; travel bans and criminal charges under s. 239 apply where the arrangement is fraudulent; the corporate veil is pierced where the corporation is a mere instrumentality of the offshore principal.
Anti-Money Laundering / Dealer in Precious Metals and Stones

Statutory sanction: Administrative Monetary Penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act of up to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 2,000,000 for corporations per violation; knowing non-compliance is a criminal offence carrying fines up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The designated compliance officer and directors may be personally named in FINTRAC penalty assessments; willful blindness to laundering through the e-commerce platform supports money-laundering conspiracy charges and personal criminal exposure, with restraint orders freezing corporate bank accounts in Canada.
Disguised Dividend / Shareholder Loan Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA recharacterization under s. 15(1) and Part XIII as a deemed dividend: 25% withholding tax assessed against the payer plus 7%-9% daily interest; late filing penalties under s. 162(2); imputed interest on shareholder-benefit loans under s. 80.4(1).

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the sham loan are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid Part XIII withholding under s. 227.1; CRA can freeze the company's payment-facilitator accounts and issue director liability letters; knowing participation converts the civil assessment into criminal tax-evasion exposure under s. 239(1).
