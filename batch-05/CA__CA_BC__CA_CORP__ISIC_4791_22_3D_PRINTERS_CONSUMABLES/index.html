Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); provincial sales tax administered by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance (BC PST administration); online filing portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or British Columbia must register for a Business Number (BN) with the CRA. For GST/HST purposes, the foreign entity applies for a GST/HST account using Form RC1 or through the CRA My Business Account portal. Registration requires legal entity identification, proof of identity of directors, principal business address, and designation of a Canadian representative if non-resident. Once issued, the BN becomes the TIN for both GST/HST and corporate income tax filing. BC PST registration must be performed separately through the BC Taxpayer Portal (eTaxBC) when the entity makes taxable retail sales or leases of goods in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada adopts a full entity-level taxation regime without a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election. For income tax, a business corporation is a separate taxable entity, and rollover relief is available under subsection 85(1) of the Income Tax Act for transfers of eligible property to a corporation by shareholders. GST/HST elections are available to streamline compliance, including the election for closely related corporations under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act and the quick method and annual filing election thresholds. No hybrid mismatch election is applicable; Canada applies mandatory hardwired rules under sections 12.2 and 18.2 of the Income Tax Act addressing cross-border hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (for non-resident persons, the threshold also includes supplies of real property, corporeal moveable property, and services in Canada); once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act applies to goods supplied in Canada and exported by the supplier or a related commercial freight forwarder, and to services supplied to non-residents where the service is not performed in Canada (or is incidental to exported goods). A GST/HST registrant may claim an input tax credit for embedded Canadian GST while charging 0% on qualifying exported goods. For the sale of 3D printers and resin filaments via e-commerce, the supply must be physically exported from Canada within a fixed time frame (generally 60 days from the date of invoice) and evidence of export must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A) as received and validated by the Canada Border Services Agency
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill with consignee name, destination, and export date
  • Commercial invoice showing description, quantity, value, and export origin
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment in Canadian currency from the foreign buyer
  • Freight broker or carrier packing list
  • Proof of export (carrier notification or customs release document)
  • Sales contract or purchase order identifying the offshore recipient

Canada operates no national real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for invoices. GST/HST registrants are required to file periodic GST/HST returns electronically via CRA NETFILE when annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1,000,000 or when requested by the CRA. In BC, retailers must file PST returns electronically via eTaxBC; there is no fiscal receipt license, but ordinary invoice record-keeping obligations apply. Emerging e-invoicing adoption is through voluntary business-to-business electronic data interchange; no government platform API clearance is mandated.

The CRA and CBSA may audit export transactions and jointly apply penalties under the Excise Tax Act and the Customs Act. For fraudulent export zero-rating claims, the CRA can reassess undeclared tax retroactively (four-year statute period extended to ten years for negligence or misrepresentation), impose a penalty of 5% of the net tax understatement plus 1% per month (to a maximum of 10%), and charge synthetic interest. Criminal prosecution for willful evasion carries fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years. BC PST refund claims also may be assessed, with interest and penalties if export documentation is insufficient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions; the Currency Act, Bank of Canada Act, and provincial business-incorporation laws impose no exchange restriction, licensing, or prior-approval requirement on cross-border payments, equity injections, intercompany loans, or dividend repatriation by a British Columbia business corporation. The operative remittance hurdles are fiscal and administrative: a 25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents (treaty-reduced typically to 5-15%), a CAD 10,000 international-EFT reporting threshold that obliges the receiving bank, and source-of-funds documentation expectations when the inbound remitter is a non-resident shareholder. For an online 3D-printer retailer, USD and EUR platform settlements may be retained in multi-currency accounts without conversion requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for export-proceeds repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio; a BC exporter may lawfully hold USD, EUR, or CNY receipts in domestic or offshore accounts indefinitely. The only binding obligations are indirect: the remitting/receiving financial institution must file a FINTRAC international-electronic-funds-transfer report for each inflow or outflow of CAD 10,000 or more, and the corporation must substantiate the commercial origin of funds if the bank's transaction-monitoring system flags high-velocity platform payouts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central bank and retail-payment supervisor under the Retail Payment Activities Act; no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration exists in Canada. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially supervises Schedule I chartered banks, FINTRAC enforces AML/CTF registration and international-electronic-funds-transfer reporting, and Global Affairs Canada administers sanctions and export-permit policy under the Special Economic Measures Act and the Export Control List.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Offshore and digital EMI providers (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) are lawful and operationally attractive for sub-ledger and foreign-currency settlement, but a provider that services CAD currency or Canadian residents must register with FINTRAC as a money services business and, where it performs retail payment functions, with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Because most offshore EMIs lack a Canadian clearing number, CAD-denominated accounts must be issued through a sponsoring Schedule I bank, and the e-commerce seller should not itself seek MSB registration since it is the merchant of goods rather than a remitter. Recommended architecture: Tier 1 chartered operating account as the master account, with a FINTRAC-registered EMI sub-ledger for fast USD/EUR platform payouts and inter-entity liquidity routing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended protocol: an inbound SWIFT MT103 (ISO 20022 pacs.008.001.08) from the non-resident shareholder's bank directly into the BC corporation's designated CAD operating account, with field 70 remittance information stating 'Equity subscription - common shares of [legal name] - BC Business Corporations Act' and a clear source-of-funds narrative for FINTRAC. The bank will request the directors' resolution approving the share issuance, the share-subscription ledger, and proof of the remitter's identity; the receiving bank is obligated to report the international electronic funds transfer to FINTRAC where the amount equals or exceeds CAD 10,000. Injections above CAD 100,000 should be pre-notified to the relationship manager to prevent a 3-5 business day compliance hold, and Statistics Canada direct-investment transaction reporting obligations must be assessed where a non-resident acquires a voting interest. Where injection currency is USD or EUR, conversion into CAD should occur at settlement-day spot under an executed FX authorization, preserving conversion transparency.Ranked clearing rails for trade settlement: (1) SWIFT MT103 with gpi tracking for open-account supplier payables, each payment supported by purchase order, commercial invoice, and packing list; (2) irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued under UCP600 by CIBC or BMO for recurring resin and hardware component purchases, with sight drafts and confirmation by the beneficiary's Asian negotiating bank; (3) documentary collections (documents against payment) for smaller one-off orders; and (4) open-account settlement supported by supplier-receivables financing or reverse factoring for seasonal inventory builds. USD-denominated payables should settle from the USD sub-ledger to avoid double conversion; final settlement in CNY, EUR, or TWD should be pre-covered by a forward or swap booked under the approved treasury policy, and every counterparty and clearing corridor must be screened pre-execution against the Canadian sanctions lists.Outright CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/CNY forwards with Schedule I Canadian banks under a bilateral ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex, subject to board-approved hedging limitsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market receivables or payables currencies where offshore deliverability is absent, including CNY and TWDCross-currency swaps for multi-year intercompany loan or equity-funded capital structures where material foreign-currency liabilities are amortized over more than one yearPlain-vanilla and cap/barrier over-the-counter FX options used only for downside protection on committed but not yet settled platform revenue streams, with no option-writing authorityMoney-market natural-hedge arrangements under which USD platform receivables are matched against USD supplier payables, eliminating the need for transaction-level conversion and reducing FINTRAC-reporting friction
