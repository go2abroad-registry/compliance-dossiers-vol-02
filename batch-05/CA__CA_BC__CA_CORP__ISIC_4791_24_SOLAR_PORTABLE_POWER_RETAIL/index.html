Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll and import/export program accounts; portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html (My Business Account). British Columbia Ministry of Finance — Provincial Sales Tax (PST), Employer Health Tax, carbon tax and motor fuel tax; portal: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes (eTaxBC).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the foreign entity is carrying on business in Canada and whether its Canadian taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters; if yes, mandatory GST/HST registration is required; voluntary registration under ETA s. 240 is available and commonly recommended to claim input tax credits and preserve export zero-rating. Step 2: Incorporate or register as an extra-provincial company, then obtain the CRA Business Number (BN) through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, or by mailing Form RC1/RC1A; open program accounts: RC (income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) and RM (imports/exports). Non-residents must provide a Canadian address for service or appoint a Canadian tax representative, and CRA may require security (ETA s. 244) as a condition of registration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification regime; a Canadian business corporation is treated as a separate taxpayer subject to federal Part I corporate income tax and BC corporate income tax. Statutory elections available include: (i) GST/HST s. 156 election allowing closely related corporations to file and remit GST/HST as a single group; (ii) GST/HST s. 157 joint election for related corporations on capital property supplies; (iii) ITA s. 216 election for non-residents to compute tax on a net basis for Canadian rental and timber royalties; (iv) ITA s. 166.2 election to extend the normal reassessment period; and (v) controlled foreign corporation and hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2/18.4) that operate automatically and cannot be elected around. No provincial election equivalent exists for BC PST.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold; below-threshold entities may register voluntarily; non-resident platform sellers may rely on marketplace facilitator collection but should verify their own obligations). BC PST: CAD 10,000 in taxable BC retail sales over four consecutive calendar quarters (effective July 1, 2013; prior non-resident rule required registration regardless of volume).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods may be zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V where a GST/HST registrant sells goods for delivery outside Canada and the goods are exported within 60 days of the sale, or where possession of the goods is transferred to a non-resident and the goods are subsequently exported in the ordinary course of business. Services performed wholly outside Canada are zero-rated, and intangible/digital property (e.g., embedded firmware updates or app-based monitoring services) supplied to a non-resident recipient outside Canada qualifies for zero-rating where the supplier holds foreign address and payment-method evidence. BC PST does not apply to goods delivered by the seller to a destination outside Canada; however, domestic BC consumer sales of portable solar generators and batteries attract 7% PST plus 5% GST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13) or CERS electronic export report for goods valued above CAD 2,000 or controlled goods
  • Bill of Lading or courier airway bill showing foreign destination, consignee name and date of export
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms, zero-rating endorsement, foreign purchaser details and HS classification
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign exchange record, including Form TFC (Transaction and Export Confirmation certificate) issued by a Canadian chartered bank, evidencing inbound funds for shipped goods
  • Carrier or CBSA electronic cargo evidence (eManifest/CESAR) confirming physical exit of goods from Canada

Canada does not operate a continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate, real-time CRA fiscal clearance API, or digital stamping for GST/HST invoices. GST/HST registrants must electronically file returns (GST NETFILE is mandatory for most registrants; paper filing is restricted to registered charities and registrants with annual taxable supplies below CAD 15,000). Electronic records are acceptable and must be retained for six years (ETA s. 286). For customs and export trade, the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) system and Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) provide digital filing and electronic cargo reporting (eManifest and CESAR), with API/EDI integration for high-volume shippers. The GST/HST digital platform economy rules (in force July 1, 2021) require marketplace facilitators to collect and remit GST/HST on qualifying electronic commerce supplies made by non-resident sellers through their platforms, which generally shifts the collection burden to the platform while the seller remains liable for its own direct supplies.

CRA audits place the burden of proof on the exporter to substantiate export zero-rating through retained transportation, customs, banking and customer records. Failure or misrepresentation triggers: (1) reassessment denying the zero-rating and assessing GST/HST at 5% plus statutory interest compounded daily; (2) a false statement penalty under ETA s. 281.1/285 equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the improperly claimed amount; (3) a gross negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2) of 50% of the tax understated where export income affects taxable earnings; (4) criminal prosecution for willful evasion under ETA s. 327 or ITA s. 239 with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years; and (5) retrospective clawbacks for up to ten years where the reassessment is based on misrepresentation, neglect or fraud. BC PST similarly requires proof of export to claim the exemption; failure to substantiate results in assessment of 7% PST plus 5% GST, together with penalties and interest under the Provincial Sales Tax Act (SBC 2002 c. 2).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, no FX licensing or approval requirements for remittances, and no restrictions on repatriating share capital or retained earnings. Profit remittance risks arise primarily from tax withholding, not FX: dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statututory 25%, generally reduced by an applicable tax treaty; US treaty rates may be 15%, or 5%/0% in qualifying substantial-holding situations). Intercompany royalties or management fees also face 15–25% withholding absent treaty reduction, and transfer-pricing must be documented. Share capital reductions and redemptions are permitted if the corporation meets solvency tests under the applicable CBCA or BC Business Corporations Act framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory holding period before export receipts can be disposed of. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency, held in Canadian or offshore bank accounts, or freely converted in the market. Canadian tax residents remain liable to report worldwide income, and FINTRAC reporting obligations apply only to large cross-border currency/EFT transactions, not to disposition timing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — Canada does not maintain a separate foreign exchange administration; the Department of Finance, OSFI, FINTRAC and the Bank of Canada collectively exercise monetary, supervisory, AML and FX-reporting mandates.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low–Medium. Offshore digital EMIs cannot lawfully carry on deposit-taking or e-money issuance in Canada without appropriate Bank Act / federal financial services authorization and, where applicable, registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act and FINTRAC MSB requirements. An offshore EMI may still function as a payment-facilitation rail for a Canadian corporate e-commerce company, but Canadian Schedule I banks generally discount or reject EMI-held funds for treasury clearing and settlement. EMI funds are not CDIC-insured, and reliance on an unregulated EMI raises regulatory and operational risk for supplier disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a SWIFT MT103 from the overseas investor to the BC operating corporation's CAD-denominated bank account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The Canadian receiving details must include the 3-digit institution number, 5-digit transit number, account number, and SWIFT BIC; for USD inbound, obtain the Canadian bank's correspondent ABA/CHIPS routing instructions. The purpose description should state 'Share Capital Contribution', 'Paid-Up Capital', or 'Director/Shareholder Loan'; if a shareholder loan is used, execute a formal written loan agreement before remittance. Provide to the receiving bank: incorporation certificate, registered office address, director and UBO list, board resolution authorizing bank account opening and capital subscription, and source-of-funds evidence. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate income tax rate (15% federal Part I general rate + 12% British Columbia provincial general rate); 11% combined small business rate (9% federal + 2% BC) applies to the first CAD 500,000 of active business income earned by a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents; treaty-reduced rates apply, including 15% portfolio and 5% direct-dividend rates under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention for corporate beneficial owners holding at least 10% of voting shares.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than six (6) months after the fiscal period end (e.g., a December 31 year-end return is due by the following June 30); the balance-due day for corporate tax instalments is two (2) months after fiscal period end, extended to three (3) months for a CCPC eligible for the Small Business Deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 is mandatory where aggregate annual non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000; contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation consistent with OECD Chapter V is required for penalty relief with no separate dollar threshold; Country-by-Country Report filing applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: British Columbia has not enacted Gulf or Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); the Canadian functional equivalent comprises the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), which recharacterizes transactions lacking genuine economic substance and undertaken primarily for tax avoidance, and ITA s.247, which requires all intercompany dealings to reflect the arm's length principle on commercial substance; BC courts additionally apply the sham and facade doctrines in corporate veil-piercing analysis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal)

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; aggravated schemes under s.239(1.1) subject to imprisonment up to 14 years; prosecution conducted by the Public Prosecution Service of Canada on referral from the CRA Criminal Investigations Program.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or participate in evasion face personal criminal prosecution; the corporate veil may be pierced where the corporation is a sham or a mere facade for the director's personal profit; CRA may obtain freezing orders, property restraint orders, travel bans on fugitive taxpayers, and civil restitution judgments.
Failure to File and Gross Negligence

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of 5% plus 1% per full month up to 12 months; gross negligence penalty of 50% of the misstatement under ITA s.163(2); daily compound prescribed-rate interest; CAD 25 per day penalty for failure to file information returns.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted GST/HST and payroll source deductions held in trust; civil recovery of diverted corporate funds is enforceable through BC court orders; culpable conduct may trigger third-party penalties under s.163.2 and director disqualification proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Upward transfer-pricing adjustment to the arm's length price plus a 10% penalty under ITA s.247(3) where the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue; statutory interest on reassessed tax; denial of penalty relief where contemporaneous documentation is absent.

Director personal liability: Where intercompany arrangements are a sham or conduit, CRA recharacterizes the structure and BC courts may disregard separate legal personality; directors who sign knowingly false T106 returns face s.163.2 culpable-conduct penalties and may be personally exposed for corporate tax arrears in insolvency or fraudulent conveyance scenarios.
