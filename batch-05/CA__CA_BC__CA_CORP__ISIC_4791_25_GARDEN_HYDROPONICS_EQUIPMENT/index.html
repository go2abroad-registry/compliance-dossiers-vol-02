Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal fiscal authority for GST/HST, payroll, and federal corporate income tax (portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency; My Business Account at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html). The Province of British Columbia administers the separate Provincial Sales Tax (PST) and BC corporate income tax via the Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch; portal: eTaxBC at etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

A domestically incorporated CBCA/BCBCA corporation is auto-issued a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with an 'RC' corporate income tax program account upon incorporation, transmitted by Corporations Canada or the BC Registry to the CRA. The entity must then register for the GST/HST 'RT' program account through Business Registration Online (BRO) or My Business Account; registration is compulsory once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the aggregate of the four preceding consecutive quarters (Excise Tax Act s. 148 small supplier rule), and is otherwise available voluntarily. A foreign non-resident entity soliciting orders or carrying on business in Canada obtains a BN by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) through BRO and registers for GST/HST in the same manner, or, if selling tangible goods through a Canadian fulfillment warehouse, under the 2021 GST/HST e-commerce vendor registration rules. For BC PST, the seller must register with the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC using the same BN; registration is mandatory once taxable sales in BC exceed C$10,000 in the preceding 12 months (Provincial Sales Tax Act small seller exemption), with marketplace facilitators deemed to be the PST-collecting seller for marketplace orders from May 1, 2023. Payment processors such as Stripe and PayPal are not 'marketplace facilitators' and do not relieve the seller of registration obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Business Corporation is a corporation resident in Canada under the Income Tax Act, subject to tax on worldwide income at the federal (15% general / 9% small business) and BC provincial (12% general / 2% small business) rates; no pass-through eligibility. If Canadian-controlled and private (CCPC), the first C$500,000 of active business income qualifies for the federal/BC small business deduction, subject to the aggregate business limit and specified corporate group rules. The tax base is residency-based worldwide taxation; there is no Canadian territorial remittance election for corporations.

Available election forms

There is no check-the-box or entity-classification election in Canadian tax law; foreign entity tax classifications (e.g., US disregarded-entity elections) have no CRA effect. Available elections include: (1) voluntary GST/HST registration by a non-resident not carrying on business in Canada (Excise Tax Act s. 240(2.1)); (2) the Excise Tax Act s. 156 'nil consideration' election between closely related corporations (Form GST25-1); (3) the GST Quick Method election (Form GST74) and annual filing elections; (4) the Income Tax Act s. 216 election for a non-resident's Canadian rental or timber income; and (5) for BC PST, the small-seller voluntary registration and vendor allowance election. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4–18.6, enacted 2024) impose a mechanical deduction-denial framework without taxpayer elections, and relevant hybrid instruments are recharacterized by statute.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 CAD — triggered when a registrant's taxable supplies made in Canada exceed C$30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the aggregate of the last four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold). BC PST: C$10,000 CAD — a 'small seller' whose taxable sales in BC in the preceding 12 months do not exceed C$10,000 is exempt from PST registration; registration becomes mandatory above this threshold, and the threshold applies to the seller's own sales before any deemed marketplace-facilitator shift.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% GST/HST applies to exported goods under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V (section 1) where a GST/HST registrant supplies goods to a non-resident purchaser, ships or delivers the goods to a common carrier, postal service, or courier destined to a point outside Canada, the goods are exported as soon as the circumstances permit, and the registrant retains contemporaneous export evidence. Services (e.g., software configurations) are zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident not registered under the ETA and the benefit of the service is received outside Canada (Schedule VI, Part V, section 7). Hydroponics and gardening equipment (HS 9406, 8419, 8421) are not export-controlled, but an Export Control List / General Export Permit screening should be documented. BC PST does not apply to goods validly exported, and PST-registered vendors may claim refunds of PST paid on goods subsequently exported within the prescribed periods under the BC Provincial Sales Tax Act.

  • CBSA export report under the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES/Y50), mandatory for exports valued over C$2,000 or controlled goods; for US-bound parcel traffic, courier ACI export data or AES proof in lieu
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or courier manifest showing delivery to an outside-Canada destination
  • Commercial invoice and itemized packing list
  • Purchase order or sales contract with the non-resident buyer
  • Inward remittance evidence — bank SWIFT advice, foreign-invoice remittance certificate, or Stripe/PayPal settlement statement in CAD-equivalent reflecting receipt of export proceeds
  • GST/HST return and working papers supporting the 0% rate and input tax credit claims

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoice stamping, or tax-authority API validation regime at either the federal or BC level; indirect tax compliance is self-assessed on periodic GST/HST/PST returns. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding C$1.5M (GST NETFILE) and to corporations with gross revenue exceeding C$1M for T2 returns. The voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (Peppol PINT standard) is optional and does not displace the Excise Tax Act invoice-content and records-retention duties (ETA ss. 236 and 286).

Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; for this dossier the 180-day benchmark is adopted as the internal export-evidence control, requiring export proceeds to be reconciled and documented within 180 days of shipment. In audits, CRA scrutinizes zero-rating substantiation in GST/HST net-file reviews and post-assessment verification programs. Failure of documentation reverses the 0% rate, yielding assessments of 5% unremitted GST plus daily compound arrears interest, with BC PST assessments for supplies not validly exported, and: (1) a 50% false-statement penalty under ETA s. 285(1) for knowing or grossly negligent misstatements; (2) a 50% evasion penalty under ETA s. 280(1) (minimum C$100); (3) an unlimited look-back period under ETA s. 298(4) overriding the normal four-year limitation where misrepresentation or fraud exists; (4) criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 or Criminal Code s. 380 with fines up to twice the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; and (5) BC PST civil penalties up to 25% of tax evaded, plus interest and potential registration cancellation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible current and capital account regime with no material exchange controls. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is freely permitted under the Canada Business Corporations Act and Income Tax Act, subject only to corporate tax clearance (CG-28 or equivalent non-resident withholding tax compliance) and anti-money-laundering documentation. Dividend repatriation may be affected by Part XIII withholding tax unless reduced by treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirements exist in Canada. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted freely; no mandatory conversion ratio or holding-period constraint applies. Reporting to the Canada Revenue Agency is limited to income tax and GST/HST obligations, and no foreign-exchange repatriation declaration is required under federal law.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); currency issuance and monetary policy reside with BoC, while financial institution supervision falls under OSFI.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for low-risk e-commerce treasury operations, subject to full money services business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC and provincial registration where required (e.g., British Columbia's Money Services Act if regulated). Non-resident EMIs must establish a Canadian legal entity, appoint a compliance officer, implement a FINTRAC-approved AML/ATF program, and maintain a clearing relationship with a Canadian bank. Remote-only EMIs may be used for pay-in/pay-out, but must not hold client funds in Canada without appropriate licensing; a regulated Schedule I bank account remains the primary treasury anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity injection is executed via SWIFT MT103 wire from the foreign shareholder's bank to the Canadian corporation's operational account at a Schedule I bank. The wire message must clearly indicate the corporate legal name, Canadian account number, bank institution number and transit number, and a reference to the share subscription or shareholder loan agreement. There is no mandatory Canadian purpose code, but the bank may request a letter of explanation and a board resolution to satisfy internal AML/CFT controls. For incoming payments exceeding CAD 10,000, the Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) and Common Reporting Standard (CRS) self-certification must be on file. The bank will verify the entity's registration with Corporations Canada or the British Columbia Registrar of Companies, and may ask for supporting audited financials for capital validation.Outbound trade settlements for garden hydroponics equipment are cleared primarily via bank-to-bank SWIFT MT103 transfers for low-value spot payments and Documentary Letters of Credit (LC) for high-value inventory procurement. LCs are issued under UCP600 rules through Canadian confirming banks for suppliers in Asia or North America. The treasury operation should maintain a CAD-denominated operational account for local disbursements, a USD-denominated account for cross-border supplier settlements, and make use of payment rails such as Visa Direct, Fedwire, and EFT for non-SWIFT mass payouts. All outbound wire instructions require AML triggers such as recipient name, address, account, and, where applicable, the supplier's business identification number.
