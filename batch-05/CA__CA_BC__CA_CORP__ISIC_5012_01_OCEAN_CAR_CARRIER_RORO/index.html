Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), GST/HST Registration and Business Number program; online portal: CRA My Business Account / Business Registration Online (BRO). For British Columbia's Provincial Sales Tax, the competent provincial authority is the BC Ministry of Finance (Tax and Revenue Administration).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity seeking a Canadian tax identity must: (1) obtain federal or provincial incorporation/extra-provincial registration; (2) apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (RT suffix) through CRA's Business Registration Online or by submitting Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts'), providing legal identification, jurisdiction of formation, and, if applicable, evidence of a permanent establishment or Canadian agent; (3) for BC PST, separately register with the BC tax registry via the BC Government's PST registration system or OneStop if required; (4) use the BN/RT number as the GST/VAT registration identifier for invoicing and export zero-rating claims.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity seeking a Canadian tax identity must: (1) obtain federal or provincial incorporation/extra-provincial registration; (2) apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (RT suffix) through CRA's Business Registration Online or by submitting Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts'), providing legal identification, jurisdiction of formation, and, if applicable, evidence of a permanent establishment or Canadian agent; (3) for BC PST, separately register with the BC tax registry via the BC Government's PST registration system or OneStop if required; (4) use the BN/RT number as the GST/VAT registration identifier for invoicing and export zero-rating claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box entity classification election. A Canadian Business Corporation is automatically taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. Available elections are limited to corporate reorganization and timing rules, such as the Income Tax Act s. 85(1) rollover election on qualifying transfers to a corporation and amalgamation elections under s. 87(6) and 88(1); no election can convert a corporation into a pass-through entity. Hybrid mismatch deduction disallowances under Income Tax Act s. 18.2 apply automatically and are not subject to elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 - taxable supplies made in Canada, including zero-rated export supplies, in a single calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold. Registration may be made voluntary for exporters below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The RoRo international ocean carriage can be zero-rated under the export provisions of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supply is an export of goods or a transport/freight service in respect of goods moving from a place in Canada to a place outside Canada, provided the supplier is registered and retains proof that the vehicles were delivered to the non-resident consignee or foreign port within the prescribed export period. Eligibility requires that the service be supplied to a non-resident or performed in respect of goods being exported, that the goods are not consumed, used, or supplied in Canada after delivery, and that the supplier maintains documentary evidence connecting each voyage or load to the consideration invoiced. A registered exporter may also claim input tax credits and receive a net GST refund or credit through normal periodic filings.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (electronic export report via Canada Border Services Agency, or Form A1/A6 where required)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (Ro-Ro through bill of lading or straight bill issued by the carrier)
  • Cargo manifest and Ro-Ro port terminal/sailing report evidencing departure from Canada
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms, HS codes, and motor vehicle identification details
  • Service contract, voyage charter party, or freight agreement with the non-resident customer
  • Documented foreign currency remittance or SWIFT/Wire/Letter of Credit payment advice from the consignee or freight payer
  • Vessel schedule or certificate showing the foreign destination port outside Canada

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-connected e-invoicing, or digital stamping for GST/HST. Taxpayers may issue paper, PDF, or EDI invoices that satisfy the minimum information requirements under Excise Tax Act s. 223 (e.g., supplier BN, transaction description, tax amounts). CRA supports optional GST/HST NETFILE and XML APIs for return transmission, but no government clearance authorization is triggered at invoice issuance. Books and records, including electronic records, must be available in a readable and accessible format upon CRA request.

CRA may audit export zero-rating claims and reclassify the supply as taxable if the documentary bundle is incomplete, inconsistent, or fails to establish actual export within the permitted time. The consequence is reassessment of GST/HST at the 5% rate on the gross consideration originally zero-rated, plus statutory interest. For false or misleading statements made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence, Excise Tax Act s. 285.1 imposes a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the understated net tax; s. 327 provides criminal liability for willful evasion, with fines and imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks generally extend four years, but the period is unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Director liability and injunctions may also apply in cases of control, participation, or failure to withhold.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account for residents and non-residents; the residual controls of the former Foreign Exchange Control Act were dismantled by 1952. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment, capital repatriation, and branch transfer of funds are unrestricted and require no Bank of Canada or ministerial approval. The operative limitations are: (i) Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding at 25% statutory rate on dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents, commonly reduced under tax treaties (e.g., 5% on dividends to a 10%-shareholder under the Canada-US treaty; 0%-15% on interest depending on recipient and instrument); (ii) notification and, above the 2024 WTO-investor threshold of CA$1.124 billion for an acquisition of a Canadian business, net-benefit review under the Investment Canada Act — a newly incorporated BC RORO shipping company triggered only notification, and non-resident ownership of a provincially incorporated corporation is otherwise unlimited; (iii) CRS/FATCA self-certification and FINTRAC large-transaction reporting; and (iv) balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Statistics Canada/Bank of Canada. Non-resident shareholders may lawfully hold 100% of the equity, and the company may invoice globally in USD or EUR, maintain foreign-currency accounts, and settle through correspondent banks without exchange-control consent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, forced repatriation, conversion, or minimum-holding obligation on export proceeds or foreign-service revenues. There is no mandated timeline or conversion ratio for USD/EUR freight receipts. Ocean-freight income of the RORO carrier is an international service export captured administratively, not through customs goods declarations: the company must report service revenue in the International Transactions in Services Survey (Statistics Canada/Bank of Canada) and retain commercial documentation for six years under the Customs Act. FINTRAC reporting applies to any cross-border electronic funds transfer or net transfer of CA$10,000 or more in a single 24-hour period, and CBSA currency reporting applies to physical currency of CA$10,000 or more crossing the border. Exporters may hold foreign currency indefinitely in a Schedule I bank account or abroad, and any conversion to CAD is at the commercial spot or forward rate, with hedge documentation preserved for Income Tax Act s. 39(2) characterization.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and operator of the Lynx high-value payment system; prudential supervision of banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); statutory foreign-currency and legal-tender powers reside in the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-52), with the Bank of Canada acting as fiscal agent for the Exchange Fund Account; AML/CFT supervision of financial intermediaries is exercised by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Canada has no central exchange-control board, no exchange-control licensing, and no prior-approval mechanism for inbound or outbound cross-border payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium - conditional and supplementary only. Federally registered Canadian payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23, s. 238), including Wise Business, Airwallex, Corpay, and OFX, can support low-value crew payroll, agency disbursements, and spot conversion of freight collections, and they segregate client funds in safeguarding accounts. However, such entities (i) are not Schedule I/II banks and carry no CDIC deposit coverage; (ii) cannot issue or confirm commercial Letters of Credit, standby credits, or shipping guarantees under UCP600; (iii) cannot act as principal counterparties for ISDA/CSA FX forwards, NDFs, or cross-currency swaps because they lack a bank or authorized investment-dealer exemption; and (iv) may impose lower transaction caps and repetitive sanctions screening on multi-jurisdiction shipping strings. Offshore UK/EU-licensed digital EMIs are usable for EUR/GBP collections but cannot provide Canadian legal-entity banking status, Lynx CAD clearing, or FINTRAC-compliant audit evidence. The core capital-escrow, USD clearing, LC, and hedging rails must remain with Schedule I chartered banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from the non-resident parent or UBO is routed by SWIFT MT103 to the designated Capital Escrow Account at the chosen Schedule I bank's Vancouver branch. Wire discipline: populate field 57A with the beneficiary bank's BIC, field 50K with the ordering institution and ultimate-originator account, field 70 with 'SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - [board resolution reference]', and field 71A with charges 'OUR' to guarantee full net settlement. The bank's compliance team should be advised 48 hours in advance and provided the corporate authorization package, CRS/FATCA self-certifications, and source-of-funds file. No exchange-control approval is required, but FINTRAC EFT reporting applies at the CA$10,000 threshold, and the bank's screening engine will validate the wire against the filed UBO register. A USD subscription can be held as USD without conversion or converted at the bank's spot or forward rate; the CAD equivalent must be recorded at the bank's daily rate. Tax discipline: credit the subscription receipts to paid-up capital (PUC) at fixed par value in the minute book so that later redemption or wind-up cannot trigger the ITA s. 84(1) deemed-dividend rule; any side-pocket shareholder loan must be documented at a commercial rate and kept below a 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio to avoid ITA s. 17 imputed-interest and thin-capitalisation scrutiny.Disbursements settle over three interoperable rails: (i) SWIFT MT103/MT103-STP for ordinary operational wires (charter hire to owners, stem-ordered bunker payments, port/agency fees in foreign ports, crew wages, classification-society dues, and P&I calls), with full remittance-reference discipline in fields 70/77B; (ii) commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707/MT720 under UCP600 and eUCP 2.0) for ship-repair deposits, newbuilding milestone payments, and material procurement, issued by the Schedule I bank or confirmed through a New York/London correspondent; and (iii) SWIFT MT202/MT202COV for bank-to-bank cover and nostro/loro rebalancing when the beneficiary's bank receives in a different currency. CAD domestic obligations clear through Lynx (Bank of Canada RTGS); USD clearing in Canada is effected through each bank's integrated US correspondent - RBC via City National Bank (Los Angeles), BMO via BMO Bank N.A. (Chicago), TD via TD Bank N.A., Scotiabank via its New York branch, and CIBC via CIBC Bank USA - or via Fedwire/CHIPS. All outbound wires require dual control under board delegation, pass the internal sanctions and customer-risk engine before release, and retain full records for FINTRAC (5 years) and CRA (6 years).Deliverable outright forwards (USD/CAD, EUR/CAD, GBP/CAD) under a standard ISDA or Canadian Master Agreement with a Schedule I bank, fixing freight-revenue conversion for up to 24 months.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on currencies with settlement or convertibility frictions (e.g., USD/CNY for China port and yard charges), cash-settled on the bank's screen fixing.Cross-currency swaps (long-dated FX swaps) to refinance USD equity into a CAD operating pool or to hedge a stage-5 debt-service currency mismatch.Vanilla OTC FX options (calls and puts), premium-paid, with a chartered bank or authorized investment dealer, to establish participating hedges on bunker-related currency volatility.Exchange-traded FX futures and options listed on the Montreal Exchange (TMX) for transparent, collateralised hedge execution where transaction volumes qualify.Rolling or stacked forward tenor ladders covering sequential voyage revenue cycles, designated hedge-accounting eligible under IFRS 9 and documented in the board-approved treasury policy so that gains and losses are recognized on income account under ITA s. 39(2).

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium - conditional and supplementary only. Federally registered Canadian payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23, s. 238), including Wise Business, Airwallex, Corpay, and OFX, can support low-value crew payroll, agency disbursements, and spot conversion of freight collections, and they segregate client funds in safeguarding accounts. However, such entities (i) are not Schedule I/II banks and carry no CDIC deposit coverage; (ii) cannot issue or confirm commercial Letters of Credit, standby credits, or shipping guarantees under UCP600; (iii) cannot act as principal counterparties for ISDA/CSA FX forwards, NDFs, or cross-currency swaps because they lack a bank or authorized investment-dealer exemption; and (iv) may impose lower transaction caps and repetitive sanctions screening on multi-jurisdiction shipping strings. Offshore UK/EU-licensed digital EMIs are usable for EUR/GBP collections but cannot provide Canadian legal-entity banking status, Lynx CAD clearing, or FINTRAC-compliant audit evidence. The core capital-escrow, USD clearing, LC, and hedging rails must remain with Schedule I chartered banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50 million in gross revenue or CAD 5 million in cross-border transactions with non-residents (per CRA guidance).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. The CRA may challenge transactions lacking economic substance, and the corporation must demonstrate real business purpose and operational substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the tax evaded, plus interest. Criminal prosecution may result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they fail to withhold or remit source deductions, or if they participate in evasion. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment may apply.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income and impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment if the adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue. Interest accrues on the adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the transfer pricing mispricing. Personal liability may include restitution and travel bans.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR may apply to recharacterize the transaction, resulting in additional tax, interest, and penalties of up to 50% of the tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for negligence or willful blindness. Civil restitution and travel bans may be imposed.
AML/Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and CAD 1 million for corporations. Criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for failure to implement adequate AML controls. Civil penalties and travel bans may apply.
