Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — My Business Account portal

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada or registering for GST/HST must apply for a CRA business number (BN) via the CRA's online registration service, by completing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or through the authorized third-party registration service. The application requires corporate identification (certificate of incorporation, articles), director/officer details, and a Canada-based representative or agent if no Canadian presence. The CRA issues the BN with associated program accounts: GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM) as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not offer UNCITRAL-style check-the-box classification; Canadian-resident corporations are taxed at the entity level. Section 256.1 of the Income Tax Regulations and subsection 256.1(1) of the Income Tax Act govern deemed joint ventures for GST/HST. Certain hybrid mismatch rules under section 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act may apply to structured arrangements. A non-resident corporation may elect to be deemed a resident under subsections 250(5.1) or 250(6) in limited circumstances. For GST/HST, a non-resident can elect to use a Canadian agent under section 178 of the Excise Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST) in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; failure to register when required results in obligation to pay tax plus penalties and interest. For non-resident e-commerce fulfillment operators, registration is required if carrying on business in Canada and the threshold is met; a non-resident may also voluntarily register. British Columbia's PST registration threshold for non-resident vendors is CAD 10,000 in taxable sales into BC over 12 months; note this is a separate BC provincial obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under subsection 6(1) of the Excise Tax Act, goods exported by a registrant are generally zero-rated. The supplier must retain evidence of export within a prescribed period (generally within 60 days of the sale, or such longer period acceptable to CRA under IC 71-3). Services provided to a non-resident who is not a GST/HST registrant and where the service relates to real property outside Canada or is wholly performed outside Canada may be zero-rated under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI. For cross-border e-commerce fulfillment through the subject ISIC 5210 operation, warehousing, order fulfillment, and logistics services performed in Canada for non-resident persons may be zero-rated if the conditions in section 7 (services performed wholly outside Canada) or section 23 (transportation services) are met, but note services performed in Canada in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada are generally taxable unless specific zero-rating applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form A1/Customs Export Document or CAED)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air) or Waybill (courier)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing Slip
  • Evidence of shipment from Canada to a destination outside Canada
  • Proof of payment and inward remittance or bank settlement records
  • GST/HST export documentation and zero-rating certificate (e.g., Form GST11, Declaration of Export)
  • BC PST exemption certificate where applicable

Canada does not mandate a national real-time e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping regime. GST/HST registrants must issue and retain valid invoices (or simplified receipts for small supplies) containing required information. The CRA uses post-audit electronic data matching and can require electronic records through Emailing/MyBA. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) requires electronic customs accounting documents through the Single Window Initiative for imports/exports. There is no mandatory API-based fiscal data exchange with the CRA at the point of invoicing. For BC PST, no mandatory e-invoicing stamping exists; PST returns and remittances are filed electronically through the BC eTaxBC portal.

The CRA can reassess GST/HST net tax within the normal four-year limitation period, extendable for misrepresentation or neglect, and can apply gross negligence penalties of 50% of the net tax understatement under subsection 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act and section 327.5 of the Excise Tax Act. Civil penalties may also apply under the Customs Act for false export documents (up to CAD 25,000 per violation and forfeiture of goods). Failure to substantiate zero-rating may result in denial of zero-rating, reassessment to 5% GST/HST on the sale, interest, arrears interest, and penalties. Provincially, BC PST penalties of 10% of unpaid tax plus 1.5% per month interest can be assessed on unsubstantiated exemptions; knowingly making false statements can result in fines up to CAD 10,000 or imprisonment under BC's Provincial Sales Tax Act. Criminal fraud charges can apply where there is intentional misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 1952 and maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar for both current-account (trade and services) and capital-account (equity, loans, repatriation) transactions. There are no Canadian exchange-control approvals for inbound equity injection, dividend or branch-profit remittance, intercompany loan servicing, or liquidation proceeds, and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted. The only relevant frictions are: (i) the Investment Canada Act notification obligation for establishing any new Canadian business by a WTO investor; (ii) Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding on remittances to non-residents, generally 25% on dividends and treaty-reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US and Canada-China tax treaties, plus a 25% branch tax for unincorporated non-resident operations; and (iii) FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting at C$10,000 and above. No prior central-bank authorization is required for any FX or liquidity operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds. USD and CNY receivables of the BC cross-dock and 3PL entity may be held freely in Canadian foreign-currency accounts or offshore. Residual obligations are confined to CBSA customs and trade-statistics declarations, FINTRAC large-value and EFT reports, CRA foreign reporting where an affiliate or specified foreign property interest exists, and standard corporate record-keeping under the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) administers monetary policy and, since the Retail Payment Activities Act registration regime took effect on November 1, 2024, supervises retail payment service providers; it does not administer exchange controls. Prudential regulation of Schedule I banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while anti-money-laundering and cross-border transaction reporting is enforced by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

High for regulated digital EMIs but constrained by licensing. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing begins with a SWIFT MT103 share-subscription remittance (or Fedwire for USD) to the Canadian operating account using the bank's standard settlement instructions, with the 70 tag citing 'Equity capital contribution / share subscription' and the corporate account identifier. There is no mandatory Canadian purpose-code system, but the bank will record the narrative purpose; FINTRAC EFT reporting triggers at C$10,000. The BC entity must file the Investment Canada Act new-business notification on the ISED portal, update the BC central securities register and annual report, book the receipt as paid-up capital or contributed surplus for CRA purposes, and reconcile the flow against the Airwallex multi-currency sweep ledger to preserve a clean source-of-funds audit trail.Two complementary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct and correspondent-bank wires for high-value supplier and logistics payments, with irrevocable documentary credits under UCP 600 (MT700) for large Chinese supplier contracts; and (ii) Airwallex and Wise local-in-settlement rails for low-value recurring disbursements, channeling US ACH and Fedwire payments to American warehouse and cross-dock partners, and CIPS or local-currency agent transfers for CNY supplier payments in China. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File if gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million; additionally, Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions) must be filed if transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million in aggregate.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime, but the CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and the 'substance over form' doctrine. Transactions must have genuine economic substance and business purpose; otherwise, they may be recharacterized and penalties imposed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution may result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if they were aware of the evasion and failed to act. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess and deny treaty benefits, impose a 10% penalty on grossed-up amounts, and apply GAAR to recharacterize transactions. Fines up to CAD 100,000 for non-compliance with documentation requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for taxes and penalties if they signed false declarations or knowingly participated in the sham arrangement. Criminal charges may lead to imprisonment.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for first violation, and up to CAD 1 million for subsequent violations. Criminal penalties for willful non-compliance include fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years if they knowingly fail to implement AML controls.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable income and impose a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (if the adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue). Interest accrues on the additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for the penalty if they were involved in the transfer pricing decision and failed to exercise due diligence. In severe cases, criminal charges for tax evasion may apply.
