Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); registration portals are Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account for post-registration administration; BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains its statutory tax identifier through the federal Business Number (BN) program; incorporation is completed federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the BC Business Corporations Act; the corporation must obtain a BN and register for GST/HST and payroll accounts via BRO, or by filing Form RC4601 (Request for Non-Resident Corporation Tax Account) when no domestic incorporation exists; a BC PST account is obtained through eTaxBC if taxable tangible personal property is sold at retail into British Columbia; the BN serves as the TIN and is established using the entity's articles of incorporation, registered office address, director information, and Canadian representative details.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian business corporation is a taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act; a corporation resident in Canada is taxed on worldwide income, while a non-resident corporation is taxed on Canadian-source business and property income; British Columbia imposes provincial corporate income tax at the 12% general rate and the 2% small business rate for eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations; no pass-through treatment is available for corporations.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity classification election exists under Canadian tax law; a corporation is categorically a separate taxpayer; available elections include the s.216 Income Tax Act election for non-residents to file net-basis returns on Canadian rental and timber income, the s.219 branch tax election for non-resident corporations with a Canadian permanent establishment, the s.85 rollover election on transferring property to a corporation, s.125 allocation elections for associated corporations sharing the small business deduction, the Excise Tax Act s.273 election to exclude supplies between closely related corporations from GST/HST, and the s.179 joint venture election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters (the small supplier threshold under s.148 of the Excise Tax Act); registration becomes mandatory on the date the threshold is exceeded; non-residents carrying on commercial activity in Canada are subject to the same threshold and may register voluntarily; economic nexus is established through carrying on business in Canada or engaging in GST/HST taxable activity in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating is claimed under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act; goods exported by the supplier as part of the supply qualify under s.1; freight services transporting goods from Canada to an outside destination qualify under s.6; services made to a non-resident in respect of tangible personal property located outside Canada at the time of performance qualify under s.7; kitting, assembly, packaging and storage services physically performed in Canada on Canadian-situated goods are generally taxable at 5% GST because they constitute specified services, so the 3PL operator must charge GST unless the goods are located outside Canada at the time the service is performed or the sale of goods is made by the operator as principal and the goods are exported as part of the supply; documentary evidence proving export and payment must be retained by the supplier for at least two years from the date the GST/HST return is filed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) where exports exceed CAD 2,000 or are controlled goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and kitting or packing list
  • Inward remittance evidence (proof of foreign payment received by wire or bank transfer)
  • Non-resident status certificate or written confirmation of the customer's non-resident status for GST/HST purposes

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping mandate currently applies in Canada; the CRA is phasing in a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard as announced in the 2024 Fall Economic Statement; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE once annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million; electronic records must be retained for two years for GST/HST zero-rating evidence and six years for income tax purposes; no government API clearance is required on individual invoices.

CRA may reassess an improperly zero-rated supply at 5% GST if export evidence is not retained within the two-year statutory evidence period; gross negligence penalty equal to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (minimum CAD 250) applies under s.285.1 of the Excise Tax Act; false statements and omissions attract a 25% net tax penalty under s.280; criminal tax evasion is punishable by a fine up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to five years under s.327(1)(c); the standard reassessment window is four years, extended to seven years or indefinitely for fraud or misrepresentation; interest accrues at prescribed CRA rates from the original due date; third-party penalties up to CAD 25,000 apply to counselled false statements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible current and capital accounts; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and imposes no general exchange controls. Profit remittance is unrestricted but a 25% Part XIII withholding tax (Income Tax Act (Canada)) applies to dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents, subject to income-tax-treaty rate reduction (e.g., 5% dividends under the Canada–US treaty); FATCA/CRS reporting applies to non-resident account holders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline for foreign-currency export proceeds; exporters may retain receivables in foreign-currency accounts with authorized financial institutions. Reporting obligations are limited to FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reports (CAD 10,000+ cross-border transactions) and CBSA Cross-Border Currency Reports (CAD 10,000+ in currency or monetary instruments).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada operates no centralized foreign-exchange-control regulator. Prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision of money services businesses is performed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) enforces physical cross-border currency reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High for payment-rail aggregation. Airwallex Canada and similar FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs, also licensed under BC's Money Services Businesses Act by the BC Financial Services Authority) are viable for cross-border disbursements, multi-currency wallets, and supplier FX, but are not licensed deposit-taking institutions; funds carry no CDIC insurance, settlement finality rests with the sponsoring Schedule I bank, and MSBs cannot legally hold escrow balances under Canadian escrow practice. Treat EMIs as a parallel clearing layer, never as the designated escrow custodian.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined for the 2025 taxation year (Canada federal general rate 15% + British Columbia provincial general rate 12%); 11% combined for qualifying Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) active business income up to C$500,000 (federal 9% + BC 2%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax applies to dividend payments made by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline: 6 months after the end of the fiscal period (e.g., a December 31 year-end is due by June 30). Tax payment deadline: within 2 months after year-end for most corporations; 3 months after year-end for a CCPC that is eligible for the SBD and has no substantial investment income.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Page 1, Line 1 Business income or loss from the warehousing, kitting, and fulfillment operations. Report only income attributable to a business carried on in Canada (Income Tax Act s.253); attach Schedule 125 GIFI financial data and Canadian-basis financial statements. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous local transfer pricing documentation (Canada's 'reasonable efforts' standard, s.247(4) ITA) is generally expected when the aggregate gross value of transactions with non-arm's length non-residents exceeds C$1,000,000 in a taxation year. A Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least C$750,000,000; Canada has not enacted a separate statutory Master File threshold but expects OECD-aligned Master File documentation for large MNE groups.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations regime of the UAE type. Substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, s.245 ITA), the Supreme Court of Canada's substance-over-form and beneficial ownership jurisprudence (Canada Trustco 2005 SCC 54; Alta Energy Luxembourg 2021 SCC 49), and transfer pricing rules. A BC fulfillment/kitting entity must demonstrate real premises, payroll, inventory risk, substantive decision-making, and genuine commercial purpose; a nominal 'shell' with artificial intercompany arrangements is vulnerable to GAAR recasting, treaty denial, and transfer pricing penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Omission

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239 ITA with fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment or 2 years on summary conviction; civil liability for the evaded tax, a 50% gross negligence penalty under s.163(2), and daily prescribed-rate interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, approve, or benefit from the evasion are prosecuted as parties regardless of the corporate veil; CRA may assess directors personally for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST (s.227.1 ITA / s.323 ETA), and asset freezes, departure orders, travel bans, and extradition cooperation can be sought.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization disallowing the claimed tax benefit; denial of treaty benefits under anti-beneficial-ownership rules; reassessment of income, interest, and a possible 50% gross negligence penalty if the arrangement involves false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors who strip corporate assets or knowingly maintain sham structures can be traced as beneficiaries for civil restitution; criminal exposure arises under s.239 ITA and the Criminal Code for fraudulent manipulation of corporate records, with imprisonment and personal monetary damages.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment to the arm's length price; penalty under s.247(3) of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment when the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts; daily prescribed-rate interest compounding on retroactive adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's length contracts that leave the corporation insolvent can be joined in civil proceedings; if the arrangement also drains payroll source deductions, personal director liability under s.227.1 ITA applies.
Late Filing / Information Return Defects

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of 5% plus 1% per month (max 12 months); repeated failures increase to 10% plus 2% per month (max 24 months); Schedule 50 penalties of C$25 per day per shareholder; GIFI and other information return penalties of C$100 per day per form.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unremitted withholdings where the corporation's delinquency includes source deductions or GST/HST; personal travel restrictions and director tax clearance certificates are commonly required before leaving Canada.
AML / Money Laundering and Sanctions

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 for reporting breaches; Global Affairs Canada sanctions violations carry fines up to C$25,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years under the Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act and related sanctions regulations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate laundering or sanctions evasion face criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code s.462.31; personal bank accounts and assets can be restrained or seized, travel bans imposed, and imprisonment of up to 14 years ordered.
