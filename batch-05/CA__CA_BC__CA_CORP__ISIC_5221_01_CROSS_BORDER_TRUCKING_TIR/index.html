Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST, corporate income tax, and payroll source deductions; BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch – British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administration; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) – import/export accounts and customs reporting; Corporations Canada – federal business incorporation registry.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate federally or extra-provincially register in British Columbia, obtaining a Corporations Canada/BC Registry filing. 2) Obtain a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO), using the non-resident registration pathway if the foreign corporate head office is outside Canada. 3) Register for a GST/HST account under the same BN if the entity is not a small supplier or if voluntary registration is desired to claim input tax credits on zero-rated exports. 4) Apply through CRA/CBSA for an Import/Export (RM) account under the BN to obtain a CBSA client number, Canada Customs coding, and the ability to file electronic export declarations and TIR Carnet movements. 5) Register with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch through eTaxBC for PST if the entity makes taxable sales, leases, or service transactions in BC that are not exempt or zero-rated. 6) Appoint a BC registered office address and, if applicable, a resident agent; maintain BC extra-provincial company registration as a prerequisite to carrying on business in the province.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. A business corporation is a distinct taxpayer for federal and provincial income tax purposes. For GST/HST, non-resident registrants with limited Canadian taxable supplies may elect to use a simplified annual or quarterly GST/HST return filing method. No comparable election exists for BC PST. Canada's hybrid mismatch and excessive interest financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) rules in the Income Tax Act (ITA ss. 18.4–18.6, 18.2–18.3) may disallow deductions arising from hybrid mismatches, including payments by Canadian entities to non-resident affiliates in cross-border trucking operations. A non-resident corporation may also elect under ITA s. 216 (net basis for rental/royalty income) or s. 217 (employment income), although these are less relevant to trucking income which is typically sourced based on where services are performed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – annual worldwide taxable supplies for federal GST/HST small-supplier exemption; mandatory registration above CAD 30,000, voluntary registration below. For BC PST there is no small-supplier threshold: registration is required as soon as the entity makes taxable sales, leases, or services in BC, unless the transaction is exempt or zero-rated as an export.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods that are exported from Canada are zero-rated for GST/HST (Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V) when the supplier provides proof of export and the goods are exported by the supplier, the purchaser, or a delivery service. Cross-border long-haul trucking services provided by a carrier that transport goods out of Canada to a destination outside Canada are zero-rated if the transportation begins in Canada and the goods are destined outside Canada. The zero-rating also extends to transportation services in respect of exported goods if the carrier retains contemporaneous evidence that the goods physically left Canada. BC PST does not apply to goods delivered by the vendor to a destination outside British Columbia or Canada, provided the export transaction is documented. Availability of the zero rating does not depend solely on the TIR Carnet status; the Carnet facilitates customs transit dressage, but GST/HST zero-rating requires independent export proof.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration or electronic export declaration accepted through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) / GTR
  • TIR Carnet (international customs transit document) covering the Canadian goods movement to the U.S. border
  • Commercial invoice or waybill identifying the goods, quantity, value, shipper, consignee, and export destination
  • Bill of Lading (international through bill or straight bill of lading) evidencing carriage from a Canadian origin to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/FX documentation evidencing the foreign currency proceeds received for the exported goods or services
  • Border crossing proof, including CBSA export exit record and the U.S. CBP entry or e-Manifest release (CBP Form 7523 or in-bond/TIR control document)
  • Carrier's CBCSA/CBP registration and evidence of any export permit or licence required for bonded highway freight under TIR

Canada and British Columbia do not impose mandatory real-time electronic e-invoicing, fiscal stamping, or API-based tax clearance for GST/HST or PST. GST/HST tax invoices must nevertheless contain prescribed information (ETA Part IX and regulations). For export movements, CBSA mandates electronic export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) or the Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) via the GTR portal. TIR Carnet shipments are processed electronically through CBP in the United States and CBSA in Canada, requiring advance border data and customs release messages under the TIR Convention.

The CRA may deny GST/HST zero-rating if export proof is not obtained and retained within a reasonable time, generally considered to be 180 days from the date of export or from invoicing. If zero-rating is denied, the CRA can reassess the exporter for the full GST/HST, charge interest, and impose penalties. Knowingly making false or misleading statements in an export claim can result in penalties of up to 25% of the net tax amounts, fines, and imprisonment under ETA ss. 280, 282, and 283. CBSA may also impose administrative monetary penalties under the Customs Act for inaccurate export declarations or failure to report, and any subsequent input tax credit recovery based on a reversed zero-rated export may be clawed back with interest. Retrospective reassessments are possible within the normal assessment period, extended under the ETA for misrepresentation, and in egregious cases the conduct may trigger the general anti-avoidance rule or criminal tax-evasion provisions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains de jure and de facto full current and capital account convertibility under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. No exchange control approvals are required for trade-related repatriation, profit remittance, dividend payments, or foreign currency conversion by a British Columbia business corporation. However, transactions with sanctioned entities or jurisdictions, cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, and cash-based operations may trigger FINTRAC reporting, sanctions screening, and enhanced due diligence. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floatable, major currency without any central bank restriction on clearing or settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory export proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline under the federal Foreign Exchange Regulations or the Bank Act. A BC corporation engaged in cross-border trucking may retain earned forex (USD or otherwise) in offshore or domestic accounts, use it for payment of trade obligations, or convert it in the open market at freely negotiated commercial rates, providing the treasury functions comply with general corporate record-keeping, transfer pricing, and FINTRAC large-transaction reporting rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML/ATF oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium - Feasibility rating: Conditional use as a secondary rail. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut) may service a BC corporation only if registered with FINTRAC as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Even when registered, they are legally constrained from offering primary Canadian clearing functionality (e.g., access to the Large Value Transfer System) and cannot act as a meaningful home for TIR bond collateral or high-value freight settlements. Furthermore, any EMI that remains unregistered or has no Canadian branch is ineligible for a BC corporate account. Therefore, a tier-1 chartered bank relationship is mandatory for statutory and commercial reasons.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital or shareholder loan injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 messages from the remitting bank to the beneficiary account at a Canadian tier-1 bank. Accurate wire codes are essential: the field 50K must show the remitting shareholder's full legal name and bank; field 56A/57A must identify the Canadian correspondent bank; field 59 must show the corporate legal name exactly as registered under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia), including the BC incorporation number. Purpose tags must reference 'Capital Injection – Share Allotment' or 'Shareholder Loan Agreement – BC Corp CA_CORP' to prevent misclassification for Part XIII non-resident withholding or Canadian GST/PST audit. For wire values exceeding CAD 10,000, FINTRAC's Large Transaction Report requirement will apply automatically. If the injection originates from a related US company, the BC corporation should provide an intercompany loan agreement or equity subscription resolution to the bank's compliance office prior to execution. No Exchange Control approval is needed for CAD/USD conversion, but a bank's sanctions officer may hold wiring outside the Canada-US-Mexico corridor for enhanced verification.Clearing rails for trade disbursements: (1) SWIFT MT103 is the default instrument for supplier payments, fuel card reconciliation, third-party trucking settlements, and TIR bond précis payments; all MT103s must carry the corporate legal name and a precise value date. (2) Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 are suitable for high-value or contractually required payments, particularly for cross-border goods accompanied by TIR carnet and customs seals; the LC must name the BC corporation as applicant and use a Canadian bank's branch to issue, with a confirmed or unconfirmed advising bank at the beneficiary's country. (3) Domestic CAD legs can be settled through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day finality on amounts over CAD 50,000. (4) All outbound FX-related wires must have a trader name and transaction reference that corresponds to the freight contract invoice number, so that FX audit trails remain transparent to OSFI and FINTRAC.
