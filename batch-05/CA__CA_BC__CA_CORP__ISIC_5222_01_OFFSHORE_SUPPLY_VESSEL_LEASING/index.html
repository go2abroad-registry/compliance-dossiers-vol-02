Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full free convertibility of the Canadian dollar for both current account and capital account transactions. No central bank approval, exchange control permit, or repatriation authorization is required for foreign direct investment, dividend remittance, loan servicing, charter hire, or sale proceeds. The CAD is a freely floating G10 currency. Profit remittance hurdles arise exclusively through taxation: non-resident dividends are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% statutory rate, typically reduced by treaty (e.g., 5%–15% under the Canada–China and Canada–Hong Kong DTAAs), and non-resident dilution or share redemptions may trigger Part XIII XE or deemed dividend rules. Non-resident acquisitions of control in Canada should be screened under the Investment Canada Act, with the 2025 WTO trade-agreement investor review threshold at approximately C$1.6 billion and the state-owned-enterprise threshold at approximately C$716 million, subject to a net benefit review. There are no legal restrictions on holding foreign currency accounts in Canada or abroad; however, Canadian-resident corporations must file Form T1135 for specified foreign property with cost amount exceeding C$100,000 and report controlled foreign affiliate income via section 91 FAPI of the Income Tax Act where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline imposed by the Bank of Canada, OSFI, or any exchange control ordinance. Charter hire and other export-equivalent revenues may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, CAD, or any other currency and may be applied directly against foreign loan obligations. The only binding obligations are reporting: FINTRAC monitoring of all incoming and outgoing wire transfers of C$10,000 or more, annual Form T1135 foreign-property disclosure, transfer-pricing documentation under sections 247/247A of the Income Tax Act for non-arm's-length charter payments, and anti-money-laundering record retention for a minimum of five years.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), 234 Wellington Street, Ottawa, Ontario, K1A 0G9, acting as the central monetary authority, together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking supervision, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/CFT reporting and supervisory authority. Canada no longer maintains any dedicated foreign exchange control ministry or central-bank exchange licensing bureau; the Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for operational disbursements and payment-channel diversification only; Low for capital escrow or client-trust holdings. Canada does not issue a U.K.-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence. Non-resident digital payment firms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Convera) are regulated instead as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and, upon full commencement of the Retail Payments Activities Act (RPAA), must register with the Bank of Canada as retail payment service providers. Funds held with such MSBs are not deposits: they are not CDIC-insured, are not segregated as client trust funds under Canadian law, and expose the corporation to counterparty and operational risk. Accordingly, the primary treasury structure should use a Schedule I bank for all capital and escrow balances, with a single FINTRAC-registered MSB approved as a secondary disbursement conduit for small operational vendor payments where the corresponding bank has cleared the counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an injection of share capital or shareholder subordinated debt into the BC corporation: the foreign parent should transmit a SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian bank's correspondent corridor — for USD funds, through the bank's New York clearing correspondent; for EUR, through Target2; for GBP, through CHAPS; the beneficiary leg must identify the CA_CORP's Canadian deposit account using the 5-digit transit number and 3-digit CCPA institution code. The Field 70 narrative should state 'CAPITAL INJECTION — CA_CORP OFFSHORE SUPPLY VESSEL LEASING', and the messaging should include ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI for equity or INVE for inbound investment, with the underlying source-of-funds evidence (share subscription resolution, directors' resolution, and the loan or subscription agreement) pre-uploaded to the bank's transaction monitoring desk. The Canadian bank will apply FINTRAC source-of-funds verification for amounts at or above C$10,000; provisional credit is normally immediate for qualifying CAD wires but can be delayed under enhanced due diligence for wire amounts exceeding C$200,000. For escrow-type inbound routing, funds should be directed to a segregated escrow trust account governed by a written escrow agreement under BC law, with fulfilment instructions tied to vessel delivery or registration conditions.For operational disbursements and charter-related payments, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 where the beneficiary is outside Canada and MT202/MT202COV where cover payments are required in correspondents; for documentary trade settlements, the bank should issue commercial letters of credit via SWIFT MT700, with MT707 amendments and MT760 standby/guarantee messages for advance-payment or performance bonds in newbuild or vessel-purchase financings. Domestic Canadian disbursements above the C$1 million equivalent should be routed via LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) real-time gross settlement, and ordinary operational payments below that threshold via the Automated Clearing Settlement System or instant payment rails. OSV charter hire is customarily invoiced monthly and settled by direct wire to the charterer's nominated bank, often USD-denominated, in which case the Canadian bank's USD clearing account is debited and a corresponding CAD buy applies for replenishment.
