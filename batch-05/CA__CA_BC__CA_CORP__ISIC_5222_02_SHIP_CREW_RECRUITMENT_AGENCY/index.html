Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax authority administering the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST). Principal CRA portals: My Business Account, Represent a Client, and GST/HST NETFILE. In British Columbia, the BC Ministry of Finance administers the Provincial Sales Tax Act (PST) and the Employer Health Tax Act, with BC eTaxBC and BC Corporate Registry (BCeID) portals. Because BC has a tax collection agreement with the CRA, CRA administers both federal and BC corporate income tax accounts under a single Business Number (BN) and T2 return.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the Business Corporation federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia); if incorporated federally, register extra-provincially in BC under the BC Extra-Provincial Registration procedure to acquire a BC corporate registry number. Step 2 — Apply to the CRA for the statutory tax identifier, the 9-digit Business Number (BN), using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts'; select the RP account for corporate income tax and, where GST/HST registration is required, the RT account for GST/HST. Step 3 — A Canadian-resident Business Corporation files an annual T2 corporate income tax return through CRA's e-file systems using the BN/RP account. Step 4 — Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada obtain a BN and register for GST/HST through the same RC1 procedure; a non-resident corporation that does not carry on business in Canada but makes taxable supplies may elect the CRA 'Simplified GST/HST Registration for Non-Resident Persons' using Form RC171. Step 5 — Register with the BC Ministry of Finance for a PST vendor account (obtaining a PST number) if making taxable sales in BC; register for BC Employer Health Tax where a permanent establishment in BC has BC-source payroll payments. Step 6 — Activate CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE credentials and, for BC, the eTaxBC portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box classification regime; a corporation is a statutory taxpayer subject to entity-level taxation and there is no elective pass-through for a Business Corporation. Available elections affecting this maritime crewing agency include: (a) joint election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act to treat supplies between closely related corporations as made for nil consideration; (b) non-application of GST/HST on transfer of a business as a going concern under ETA section 167; (c) quick method of accounting election under ETA section 172 and the streamlined input tax credit method; (d) GST/HST group registration election under ETA sections 156 to 156.4 enabling consolidated filing by qualifying Canadian nexus corporations; (e) association and loss-utilization elections under the Income Tax Act (including subsection 256(1) associated-corporation rules and amalgamation continuity elections under sections 87 and 88); (f) hybrid-mismatch limitation rules under ITA sections 18.3 and 18.4 allow no elective deduplication of amounts where Canadian tax results would otherwise be artificially reduced; and (g) special non-resident elections — ITA section 216 election to file on net Canadian rental income and ITA section 219 branch tax election applicable to non-resident corporations operating in Canada. CCPC (Canadian-controlled private corporation) status is statutory, not elective, and depends on the 50% Canadian resident beneficial ownership test.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: Canada's small supplier threshold is CAD 30,000 of total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies but excluding supplies of financial services, goodwill, and capital real property) computed over the current calendar quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters; registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded and is also mandatory for non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada. Voluntary registration is available for persons below the threshold and is generally advisable for a startup crewing agency to recover input tax credits on vessel-related operational costs. BC PST: the small seller carve-out applies where a vendor's total taxable sales in the preceding calendar year are CAD 10,000 or less; a vendor exceeding CAD 10,000 of taxable sales into BC must register, collect and remit the 7% PST. Crew placement and manning services performed outside Canada for non-resident clients generally do not attract BC PST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under ETA section 7 and the Export and Import of Goods Regulations where the goods are exported by the vendor or on behalf of the vendor with documentary proof routed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or ACI eManifest. Services: crew placement, manning, payroll administration and crew-management services supplied by the BC agency to a non-resident shipowner qualify for zero-rating under ETA section 21 where the supply is not performed in connection with real property situated in Canada and is not made in connection with tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Services performed entirely outside Canada are outside the GST/HST system; services performed in Canada for a non-resident are zero-rated rather than exempt, and the vendor must report the zero-rated supplies in the GST/HST return. Special rules: manning services supplied in respect of vessels engaged in international voyages are zero-rated under the place-of-supply rules; the agency must ensure payroll administration is substantively part of the crewing service and not a separate 'financial service', which would be exempt and would deny input tax credits. The zero-rating is not elective; it requires contemporaneous documentation of the non-resident recipient, the commercial service, and the receipt of export consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration transmitted via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or ACI eManifest for any goods movement accompanying the crew service
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the international carriage and export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank foreign-exchange (FX) credit advice proving receipt of the export consideration from the non-resident shipowner
  • Master crew management agreement, crew placement contracts, and payroll manifests demonstrating the commercial substance of the offshore manning supply
  • Certificate of non-residence of the client (e.g., W-8 BEN-E, corporate tax residence declaration, or vessel flag registration certificate)
  • Zero-rated invoices bearing the vendor's BN/RT number, the 'zero-rated — ETA s.21' notation, and the currency and foreign-exchange details

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no tax-authority invoice pre-approval, and no digital stamping regime. CRA operates a voluntary, PEPPOL-based pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework under the 'E-Invoicing in Canada' initiative. Statutory invoice content requirements under ETA sections 169 and 223: a registered vendor must issue an invoice showing the vendor's name and Business Number, the date of invoice, the date of supply, a description of the supply, the consideration, and the GST rate or amount; invoices must be issued no later than 15 days after the end of the month in which the supply is made. GST/HST returns are remitted electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or EDI, and electronic filing is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies in excess of CAD 1.5 million. Records must be retained in paper or electronic form for six years. Payment remittance is accepted by wire transfer, CRA My Payment, online banking, and pre-authorized debit. BC PST returns and remittances are processed through the eTaxBC portal.

CRA GST/HST audits cross-match CERS export declarations with bank foreign-currency receipts and may disallow zero-rating where export proceeds are not repatriated within the documentary window. Consequences: (a) civil false-statement or omission penalty under ETA section 285 equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the understated net tax or overstated refund; (b) gross negligence penalties and, for income tax understatement, the 50% gross-negligence penalty under ITA section 163; (c) compound daily arrears interest on the tax, penalties, and interest under ETA section 280.1; (d) criminal tax evasion under ETA section 327, carrying a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to two years; (e) directors' and officers' joint and several liability for unremitted GST/HST under ETA section 323; (f) reassessment limitation — CRA may reassess within the ordinary four-year period, and the limitation is displaced entirely where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, or fraud is established, permitting retrospective clawback without time limit; and (g) if export zero-rating is denied, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST, with additional BC PST exposure where a BC nexus is found, together with penalties and interest from the original due date.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current account and capital account convertibility. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely in the foreign exchange market; no surrender, conversion, or repatriation approval is required for either current account payments or capital transfers. Profit remittances by foreign-owned BC corporations are unrestricted, subject to the standard 25% dividend withholding tax (reduced under tax treaties), the solvency test in the British Columbia Business Corporations Act, and the provision of adequate source documentation. Cross-border funds are only monitored under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and must be reported to FINTRAC when transactions exceed CAD 10,000 in a 24-hour period.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None — Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender rule, no statutory conversion ratio, and no specific timeline for repatriation of export earnings. Exporters may retain receipts in foreign currency indefinitely in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency domestic bank accounts. The only obligations are (i) statistical trade reporting to Statistics Canada through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for goods and services; (ii) certain declaration requirements under the Export and Import Permits Act for controlled goods; and (iii) FINTRAC obligations to report suspicious transactions and cross-border currency movements exceeding CAD 10,000, which may trigger enhanced monitoring for seafarer-agency cross-border payrolls.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority. Foreign exchange administration is conducted jointly by the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), which charters and regulates Canadian banks. There is no separate foreign exchange administration requiring exchange licences or approval for cross-border transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM. International fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) offer swift multi-currency collection and payroll disbursement but cannot serve as the principal treasury vehicle for corporate lending, letters of credit, or LVTS clearing into the Canadian banking system. Offshore EMIs that are not FINTRAC-registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) or that lack a Canadian-chartered bank partner cannot clear CAD directly through the Large Value Transfer System. For a BC crew recruiting agency, such EMIs are acceptable only as auxiliary receiving channels for certain foreign shipowner receivables; all significant payroll disbursements should route through a Canadian Schedule I bank to maintain statutory withholding (PST/GST, EI, CPP, WCB) compliance and to avoid unexplained cash-flow red flags.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection is routed via the SWIFT MT103 wire protocol from the non-resident shareholder's account to the BC corporation's operational account at a Schedule I bank. For USD payments, intermediary correspondent banks (e.g., U.S. Fedwire/ABA routing) may apply; both sources must reference the purpose of payment as 'Equity Injection – British Columbia Business Corporation Act Capital Contribution'. The receiving bank will require the corporate registration certificate, Certificate of Incumbency, and a board resolution approving the injection. All prescribed FINTRAC reporting thresholds are automatically captured by the bank's transaction monitoring; additional declarations are required when the transaction exceeds CAD 10,000 or when the source is a high-risk jurisdiction. A source-of-wealth report on the funding shareholder should also be provided to the bank to avoid AML escalation.Crew salaries and statutory Canadian payroll deductions (EI, CPP, WCB) are cleared via the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for domestic beneficiaries and via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border seafarers. For services rendered to shipowners and charterers, settlement occurs through the invoice-payment route with purpose code 'Trade in services (crew manning agreement)', using the same SWIFT infrastructure. Where a shipowner requires a documentary guarantee for advanced payments, a commercial letter of credit (CCT302) may be opened through the bank's trade finance desk, but this is rarely necessary for standard crew recruitment; more common are bank guarantees for performance bonds and payroll guarantees. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: MEDIUM. International fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) offer swift multi-currency collection and payroll disbursement but cannot serve as the principal treasury vehicle for corporate lending, letters of credit, or LVTS clearing into the Canadian banking system. Offshore EMIs that are not FINTRAC-registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) or that lack a Canadian-chartered bank partner cannot clear CAD directly through the Large Value Transfer System. For a BC crew recruiting agency, such EMIs are acceptable only as auxiliary receiving channels for certain foreign shipowner receivables; all significant payroll disbursements should route through a Canadian Schedule I bank to maintain statutory withholding (PST/GST, EI, CPP, WCB) compliance and to avoid unexplained cash-flow red flags.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal 15% general corporate rate + British Columbia 12% general corporate rate = 27% general corporate income tax rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Canada imposes Part XIII withholding tax of 25% on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder; no separate British Columbia dividend withholding tax applies. Tax treaty relief may reduce the rate to 15%, and to 5% for a corporate shareholder that is the beneficial owner of at least 10% of the voting stock under the Canada–US treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The T2 return must be filed within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. The balance-due date is two months after year-end for non-CCPCs and three months after year-end for CCPCs eligible for the Small Business Deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not prescribe a fixed local currency threshold for Local File or Master File preparation. However, Form T106 must be filed for non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents when the aggregate annual value of specified transactions with a non-resident exceeds C$1 million. Country-by-country reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of C$750 million or more. All controlled cross-border transactions, including crew supply fees, payroll recharges, and management charges, must be supported by contemporaneous OECD-aligned transfer pricing documentation sufficient to respond to a CRA documentation request under ITA section 247(4).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime used in certain offshore financial centres. Instead, the CRA may invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA section 245, the transfer-pricing rules under section 247, and treaty anti-abuse principles to disregard, recharacterize, or deny benefits where a British Columbia corporation lacks genuine economic substance, real employees, premises, management activity, or a legitimate commercial purpose. A crew recruitment agency structured as a conduit with no local substance is a high-risk target for reassessment and treaty benefit denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA section 239 carries a fine of not less than 50% and up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment of up to two years on summary conviction; CRA may also impose a 50% gross-negligence penalty plus reassess the full tax and interest. Indictable fraud under the Criminal Code can expose responsible parties to higher incarceration terms.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed, authorized, assented to, acquiesced in, or participated in the evasion are personally exposed to civil penalties, director liability certificates for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST, and criminal liability. Courts may impose travel restrictions as a bail condition and, upon conviction, a term of imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR under ITA section 245 to recharacterize the arrangements, deny treaty benefits, assess Part XIII withholding on amounts recharacterized as dividends or fees, and impose transfer-pricing adjustments and penalties under ITA section 247. A sham finding can also result in the corporation's separate identity being ignored for tax purposes.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate form is a sham or vehicle for fraud, courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors and shareholders personally liable for unpaid tax, interest, and penalties; CRA may pursue civil restitution and the individuals may face travel bans and imprisonment in the criminal context.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the consideration to an arm's-length amount under ITA subsection 247(2); a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment may apply for gross valuation misstatements or failure to provide documentation within the statutory deadline. T106 non-filing penalties and daily compounded interest on reassessed tax also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate in false transfer-pricing documentation can be personally assessed under the gross-negligence penalty regime and, in aggravated cases, may face criminal liability and asset-freezing orders, travel bans, and imprisonment.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties may reach C$500,000 for a corporation and C$100,000 for an individual per violation, with criminal fines and imprisonment for knowing or reckless non-compliance. CRA and FINTRAC may also refer matters for money-laundering prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who consented to, participated in, or failed to exercise proper oversight over AML systems can be personally penalized and may be subject to fines, travel restrictions, and imprisonment; corporate veil protection is not available for deliberate breaches of AML legislation.
