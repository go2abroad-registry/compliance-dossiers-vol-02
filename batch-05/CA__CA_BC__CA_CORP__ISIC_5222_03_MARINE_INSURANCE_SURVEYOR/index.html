Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - the single federal authority for corporate income tax and GST/HST; CRA Business Registration Online and CRA My Business Account portal at canada.ca; British Columbia corporate income tax is administered federally by CRA under the tax collection agreement; BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered by the BC Ministry of Finance (Revenue Division, taxing.bc.ca); BC Registries administers extraprovincial corporate registration; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers export declarations and customs reporting.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate under the BC Business Corporations Act or the Canada Business Corporations Act (online via BC Corporate Online or Corporations Canada) to obtain the incorporation number. Step 2 - Register with CRA through Business Registration Online to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and the required program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RP (BC PST, only if PST-taxable goods or services are supplied), RC (payroll), and RN (import/export account); issuance is immediate online. Step 3 - For a non-resident shareholder corporation, extraprovincial registration in BC is required within 30 days of carrying on business in the province; non-resident corporations must also register for a CRA BN and T2 filing obligations. Step 4 - Register for GST/HST once taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or elect voluntarily. Step 5 - File T2 corporate income tax returns within 6 months of the fiscal year end and remit any Part I corporate tax. Required evidence: certificate of incorporation, registered office address, authorized share capital, directors and officers list, and the non-resident ownership declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a BC or federal business corporation is a separate legal person and a 'taxable Canadian corporation' taxed at entity level on worldwide income if resident (central management and control in Canada), or on Canadian-source income if non-resident; no pass-through for shareholders. The corporation may qualify as a Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) eligible for the federal small business deduction (9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income) and the reduced BC small business rate (2%); the BC general corporate rate is 12% and the federal general rate is 15%, for a combined general rate of 27%. Territorial scope: resident corporations taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations taxed on Canadian business income and capital gains from taxable Canadian property.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime for domestic corporations; entity-level taxation is mandatory. Available elections for a marine surveying CCPC include: (i) taxation year-end and 53-week fiscal period election under ITA s. 249; (ii) associated-CCPC allocation elections under ITA s. 125(3)-(7) to apportion the CAD 500,000 small business deduction limit; (iii) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST74) for annual taxable supplies up to CAD 400,000; (iv) GST/HST annual filing election (Form GST105) for revenues up to CAD 1,500,000; (v) documentation-based zero-rating treatment for services supplied to non-resident insurers under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI Part V; (vi) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 are mandatory and non-elective, with no opt-out for cross-border hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - GST/HST registration is mandatory when worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 240(1)(a)); voluntary registration below the threshold is permitted and is recommended for exporters to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A marine insurance surveyor can claim the 0% GST export rate under ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7(a) where: (1) the recipient is a non-resident of Canada and not registered for GST/HST; (2) the service is performed in respect of tangible personal property (vessel, hull, machinery, or cargo) situated outside Canada at the time the service is performed; (3) the service is not in respect of real property in Canada; and (4) the recipient is not present in Canada for the purpose of consuming the service. If the surveyed vessel or cargo is physically in Canadian waters, the service may still qualify under Schedule VI s. 9 if the non-resident recipient exports the goods as soon as reasonably possible after the service. Supplies to Canadian-resident underwriters or for vessel/cargo located in Canada are standard-rated at 5% GST. The zero-rating claim shifts the burden to the registrant to prove non-resident status, location of the property, the date of survey, and the export chain; input tax credits on related professional and travel expenses remain fully claimable, generating net refunds where ITCs exceed output tax.

  • Customs export declaration (CBSA Integrated Export Declaration / B13A) - required only where physical instruments, samples, or parts cross the Canadian border; not required for purely offshore service delivery
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the movement of the surveyed cargo or vessel equipment out of Canada where applicable
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank credit advice in respect of the foreign underwriter's payment (SWIFT MT103, Underwriter_Wire, or Wise foreign exchange advice)
  • Signed service contract and detailed marine survey report identifying the insured vessel, IMO number, cargo, survey location, and date
  • Proof of non-resident status of the insurer or underwriter (foreign corporate registration, certificate of good standing, or Form W-8BEN-E for US counterparties)
  • GST/HST return schedules and the input tax credit allocation worksheet supporting the net refund claim

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or government digital-stamping regime exists in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable through the Pan-Canadian Peppol PINT standard; the federal government has announced a future e-invoicing mandate for suppliers to the federal government beginning in the 2027-2028 period. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies once annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1,500,000; remittances over CAD 10,000 must be made electronically. For physical export movements, CBSA requires electronic Integrated Export Declarations through the CARM exporter portal with no paper stamping.

CRA treats zero-rated export supplies as a high-risk audit area; the registrant bears the onus of maintaining contemporaneous export evidence under ETA s. 286. Unsubstantiated zero-rating is reassessed within the default 4-year limitation period, adding the uncollected 5% GST, prescribed interest compounded daily, and failure-to-remit penalties of 6% of the amount plus 1% per full month up to 12 months (ETA s. 280). Gross negligence or false statements in GST/HST returns attract an additional penalty of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded (ETA s. 285). Criminal evasion is punishable on summary conviction by fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years, and on indictment by imprisonment of up to 10 years and fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax (ETA s. 327). The CRA Voluntary Disclosures Program may reduce penalties only if disclosure precedes audit contact. Clawback also applies to ineligible input tax credits on non-export services within the same reassessment window; the 180-day inward-remittance benchmark is applied administratively to confirm the reality of the export and to distinguish genuine offshore supplies from artificial avoidance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility. Canada has accepted Article VIII status under the IMF Articles of Agreement, which prohibits exchange controls on current international transactions. Both current-account items (export survey fees, reinsurance commissions, sub-contractor payments) and capital-account items (parent equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend repatriation) are fully transferable in any currency without central-bank licensing or approval. Statutory frictions are limited to: (i) Part XIII withholding tax at 25% statutory on dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents, generally treaty-reduced (e.g., 15% under the Canada-UK, Canada-USA treaties; dividend rate 5% for substantial shareholders); (ii) Income Tax Act reporting of foreign-source receipts where service income is earned abroad; (iii) mandatory FINTRAC EFT reporting of CAD 10,000+ transfers crossing Canada's border; (iv) FATCA and CRS self-certifications; and (v) Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) anti-money laundering / anti-terrorist financing (AML/ATF) diligence that may delay non-standard transfers. Profit remittance by a marine insurance surveyor is, therefore, procedurally unimpeded but must be titled accurately (e.g., 'Survey Fee Remittance', 'Part XIII Dividend Distribution') to avoid bank-level compliance hold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement exists. Canada imposes neither a mandatory conversion ratio nor a statutory deadline for repatriation of export or service receipts. Marine surveyors and other professional service exporters may keep receipts in original foreign currency indefinitely, either in a domestic foreign-currency bank account held at a Schedule I bank or in an offshore correspondent account, provided the income is reported on the corporation's tax return in Canadian dollars unless the corporation elects functional-currency reporting under subsection 261(2) of the Income Tax Act. Under Section 261 the qualifying condition is that the taxpayer must keep its records and books of account in Canadian dollars or in the elected functional currency and derive at least 90% of its revenue in that currency or from related operations. For ISIC 5222 marine claims surveyor activity, where fees are invoiced in USD to foreign underwriters, USD functional-currency reporting is often available and eliminates the need for frequent spot conversions; no Canadian exchange-control certificate is required for the settlement of invoices.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Monetary Authority) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI, prudential regulator), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC, AML/ATF supervisor), and the Department of Finance Canada (foreign-exchange and payments policy). The Bank of Canada operates the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and administers the national clearing settlement infrastructure, while the Exchange Control administration itself is a residual function retained under the Currency Act and the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act; however, no Canadian Exchange Control Order is currently in force.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Viability rating: High for outbound operational disbursements and foreign-currency invoice collections up to CAD 500,000 annually; Medium as the primary treasury custodian. Wise Canada (a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business, MSB) offers a business multi-currency account, supports 40+ currencies, and settles in CAD via ACSS; it is lawful for paying foreign sub-contractors, receiving underwriter fees in USD, and batch-disbursing reinsurance premium pass-through. However, Wise is not a CDIC-insured deposit institution and cannot provide Letters of Credit or LVTS membership. Offshore digital EMIs (UK FCA e-money institutions, EU EMI passport holders) are not recognized as depository institutions under the Bank Act; they cannot open Canadian operational accounts, cannot clear via ACSS/LVTS, and must transact through a licensed Canadian MSB or a Schedule I bank. Legal constraint: any foreign entity providing money-transfer services in Canada must register with FINTRAC as an MSB under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); operating without registration constitutes a criminal offence. Therefore the recommended treasury stack is a Schedule I operating account (settlement and custody) plus Wise Business (low-value collection/disbursement efficiency), with all Wise activity mapped to the underlying files in the corporate ERP.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a capital contribution from a foreign parent or foreign underwriter equity investment: (i) execute a board resolution authorizing the issuance of shares or the creation of a shareholder loan; (ii) remit proceeds via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign bank (originating bank) to the BC corporation's CAD-denominated operating account at a Canadian Schedule I bank (beneficiary bank), using the 59-field beneficiary name 'CA_CORP (BC Business Number)' and the 72-field bank-to-bank information 'CORP INVOICE SURVEY REF'; (iii) include a clear end-to-end transaction reference (e.g., 'EQUITY INJECTION 2025 SERIES A' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN'), as Canadian banks do not mandate a Government of Canada purpose code but will perform an EFT report to FINTRAC for any international transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; (iv) furnish the bank a share-registry extract, the BC registry confirmation, and a signed FATCA/CRS Form to confirm non-reportable status of the Canadian entity; (v) where the payment is a claim-settlement pass-through from a foreign underwriter, label the MT103 as 'Survey fees pursuant to policy claim NNN' to distinguish it from equity proceeds; (vi) applicable clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 over the LVTS or Canada-USA cross-border clearing service; domestic CAD legs settle via ACSS. No Exchange Control approval or surrender requirement applies; the bank, however, reserves the right to return funds if the originating jurisdiction is FATF-monitored and the underlying documentation is incomplete.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 for all foreign currency disbursements. Domestic settlement rails include the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day irrevocable CAD inter-bank payments over CAD 50,000, and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for standard CAD EFT/ACH credits under that threshold. For large equipment purchases or multi-jurisdictional survey gear: commercial letter of credit (documentary credit) issued by the BC bank via SWIFT MT700 and, where required, confirmed by the seller's bank; terms typically sight payment (CLAUSE 41a) or 60-day usance; underlying transport documents include the surveyor's inspection reports and insurance certificates. Standard disbursement categories and rails: (i) sub-contractor survey fees overseas — SWIFT MT103 with 70-field narrative 'SUBCONTRACT SURVEY FEE / REF'; (ii) calibration and equipment maintenance — Commercial LC or SWIFT MT103 depending on value; (iii) reinsurance premium pass-through to the underwriting agent — MT103 or Wise batch for low-value; (iv) global industry membership dues and local P&I club calls — Visa Business / Mastercard Corporate card rail or MT103; (v) domestic CAD obligations (rent, salaries, insurance premiums) — ACSS EFT or LVTS. The bank will require a registered recipient list for periodic batch payments and an AML screening of each beneficiary; all corporates must retain underlying contracts to satisfy MT103 audit trails.Outright USD/CAD foreign-exchange forward contracts, tenor 1-12 months, transacted under a prudent Master Foreign Exchange Agreement or ISDA 1998 FX Master Agreement with any Canadian Schedule I bank; no Exchange Control approval required; margin facility equal to 105% of anticipated settlement notional for non-rated corporates.Foreign-exchange swaps (two-leg settlements) executed over LVTS settlement windows with the bank's capital markets division, subject to OSFI B-7 derivatives and margin guidelines.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) where the surveyor receives fees in restricted-Tier currencies (e.g., offshore Renminbi CNH, INR, or KRW); available only through the bank's emerging-market desk; use discouraged for annual FX exposure below CAD 2 million equivalent because of documentation costs.Cross-currency swaps matched to material USD-denominated receivable books or asset/liability mismatches, requiring hedge-effectiveness documentation if IFRS 9 hedge accounting is to be upheld by the external auditor.Natural-hedge foreign-currency (USD) bank accounts held domestically at a Canadian Schedule I bank, eliminating derivative-hedging needs for annual third-party currency receipts below CAD 500,000.Currency-maturity matching by designation of the Canadian corporation's Subsidiary to bill underwriters in the same currency as the sub-contractor cost base, supported by the bank's multi-currency account overlay.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability rating: High for outbound operational disbursements and foreign-currency invoice collections up to CAD 500,000 annually; Medium as the primary treasury custodian. Wise Canada (a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business, MSB) offers a business multi-currency account, supports 40+ currencies, and settles in CAD via ACSS; it is lawful for paying foreign sub-contractors, receiving underwriter fees in USD, and batch-disbursing reinsurance premium pass-through. However, Wise is not a CDIC-insured deposit institution and cannot provide Letters of Credit or LVTS membership. Offshore digital EMIs (UK FCA e-money institutions, EU EMI passport holders) are not recognized as depository institutions under the Bank Act; they cannot open Canadian operational accounts, cannot clear via ACSS/LVTS, and must transact through a licensed Canadian MSB or a Schedule I bank. Legal constraint: any foreign entity providing money-transfer services in Canada must register with FINTRAC as an MSB under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); operating without registration constitutes a criminal offence. Therefore the recommended treasury stack is a Schedule I operating account (settlement and custody) plus Wise Business (low-value collection/disbursement efficiency), with all Wise activity mapped to the underlying files in the corporate ERP.
