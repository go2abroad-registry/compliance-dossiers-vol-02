Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), accessed through the 'My Business Account' portal and Business Registration Online at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; GST/HST returns are filed through GST/HST NETFILE; export declarations and customs clearance are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Automated Export Declaration System (CAED) / G7 electronic export reporting.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with program-account suffixes. Procedure: (i) determine whether taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or the trailing twelve months, or whether voluntary registration is preferable to recover input tax credits; (ii) file Form RC1 or use the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, electing 'Non-resident' status and opening the GST/HST (RT), customs (RM) and corporate income tax (RC) accounts as applicable; (iii) provide the foreign certificate of incorporation, registered office address, Canadian address for service, director and officer particulars, estimated annual Canadian taxable supplies and the elected fiscal period end under ITA s. 249.1; (iv) obtain the 9-digit BN with the RT program account, typically within five business days; (v) complete extraprovincial registration with the BC Corporate Registry under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act and register for BC PST through BC eTax; (vi) for an exporter whose supplies are exclusively zero-rated, voluntary GST registration is commercially essential in order to claim input tax credits on Canadian port inputs, tug services, and marine fuel procurement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a company incorporated or continued under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the British Columbia Business Corporations Act is a separate taxpayer subject to federal Part I income tax and BC provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment applies, and non-resident shareholding does not alter the entity's residence. A non-resident corporation carrying on business in BC through a permanent establishment may instead be subject to Part XIV branch tax and treaty-based relief.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style 'check-the-box' regime; classification follows the legal form recognized under domestic corporate law. Procedures available include: (i) the non-resident corporation fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1; (ii) the GST/HST closely-related corporation election under ETA s. 273, treating intercompany supplies between affiliated bunkering entities as made for nil consideration; (iii) branch-tax relief election under applicable double taxation treaties; (iv) foreign-affiliate and associated-corporation elections for allocating taxable income and capital among Canadian and related foreign group members; and (v) election and designation mechanisms under the Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2–18.8 and 20.2) intended to characterize outbound payments and curtail hybrid mismatches by adjusting the deduction/deemed-inclusion treatment of payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST registration is mandatory for a non-resident carrying on business in Canada once its taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act, s. 240). Voluntary registration is permissible and is commercially necessary for an exporter whose supplies are entirely zero-rated, as it permits recovery of input tax credits on Canadian inputs. For BC PST, a person making taxable retail sales in BC must register and collect PST irrespective of revenue size; no monetary registration threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Marine bunker fuel, lubricants and ships' stores delivered to vessels in British Columbia qualify for zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act where the goods are exported in connection with an international voyage. The criteria include: (i) the goods are shipped from a place in Canada to a place outside Canada and are exported as soon as reasonably possible (ETA Sch. VI, Pt. I); (ii) the recipient is a non-resident vessel operator or the vessel is engaged in international commercial navigation, with the supply not relating to goods situated in Canada at the time of supply (ETA Sch. VI, Pt. V); (iii) the supplier is a GST/HST registrant, including a voluntarily registered exporter, and holds contemporaneous documentary evidence of export; (iv) ancillary bunkering services rendered at a Canadian port, such as lightering, pilotage, and delivery charges, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident and not performed in respect of Canadian real property or Canadian-situated goods; and (v) BC PST does not apply to fuel or lubricants supplied as ships' stores for a vessel operating in foreign or international commerce under exemptions in the Provincial Sales Tax Act. Federal excise duties and BC carbon charges are likewise relieved or rebated for bunker fuel consumed in international commercial shipping. Supplies of bunkers to vessels engaged solely in domestic coastal voyages remain taxable at 5% GST and 7% BC PST.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration filed electronically through the CBSA CAED/G7 system (export control number) for each bunker delivery leaving Canada
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading and ship's manifest identifying the vessel, cargo, quantity and grade of marine fuel/lubricant, and the next foreign port of call
  • Bunker delivery note signed by the vessel's chief engineer and the supplier's barge or terminal representative evidencing the physical transfer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT wire confirmation, or bank statement evidencing settlement by the non-resident vessel owner or charterer
  • International voyage evidence, including port clearance, certificate of departure, or the vessel's itinerary confirming departure to a destination outside Canada

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government-side e-invoice validation mandate. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1 million via GST/HST NETFILE or XML; the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing framework is being adopted voluntarily. On the export side, the CBSA requires electronic transmission of an export declaration through CAED/G7 for goods shipped from Canada with a value exceeding CAD 2,000, filed prior to departure, and the exporter must retain contemporaneous records of the underlying commercial transaction.

CRA may reassess to deny zero-rating within the normal four-year assessment period, and without time limitation where misrepresentation or fraud is established, imposing 5% GST plus arrears interest under ETA s. 280. A false statement or omission penalty under ETA s. 282 applies for the greater of CAD 5,000 and 10% of the disallowed input tax credits or uncollected tax. Willful evasion is prosecutable under ETA ss. 327–329, carrying significant fines (up to double or in certain cases 250% of the evaded tax) and imprisonment (on indictment up to five years). CRA also conducts targeted export-substantiation audits of bunker suppliers and may jointly and severally assess the exporter and the non-resident recipient for uncollected GST, cancel the GST/HST registration, and require repayment of input tax credits previously recovered where export evidence is deficient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Profit remittances and capital repatriation are freely permitted for resident and non-resident investors, subject to non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25% under the Income Tax Act, generally reduced by applicable tax treaty rates, for example 5%–15% under the Canada–United States Convention), solvency tests applicable to dividend distributions, and standard AML/ATF documentation. Currency conversion requires no prior governmental approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no required conversion ratio for export receipts, including marine fuel and lubricant export settlements. The Canadian dollar is a free-floating currency, and exporters may hold, convert, or disburse foreign currency without restriction. Cross-border trade transactions must nevertheless be reported in balance-of-payments surveys under the Statistics Act and are screened for compliance with the Canadian sanctions regime and PCMLTFA reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Canada maintains no stand-alone foreign exchange administration agency and does not impose exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-low feasibility. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) without FINTRAC registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) or without a Canadian money services business (MSB) licence cannot lawfully service a Canadian operational corporation. Canadian bank-embedded platforms and Schedule I digital banks are viable for ancillary, low-value clearing, but the bunkering credit architecture, SWIFT settlement, and standby letter of credit facilities require a regulated Schedule I or Schedule II Canadian bank, a Canadian branch of a foreign bank, or a federally regulated trust company. Pure offshore EMI rails should be restricted to secondary liquidity or rejected where SBLC issuance is contemplated.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if total value of transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1 million (goods) or CAD 500,000 (services, interest, royalties). Master File required if consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal ESR regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. A corporation must have real economic substance (employees, premises, business activities) to avoid recharacterization of transactions and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate tax under s.227.1 if they failed to withhold or remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization of transactions, denial of tax benefits, penalties up to 25% of tax avoided, and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to the arrangement; potential for criminal charges if fraud is proven.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations, criminal fines up to CAD 2 million, and imprisonment for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly fail to comply; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices, impose penalties of 10% of net transfer pricing adjustment (if adjustment exceeds CAD 50,000), plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the mispricing; civil restitution and potential criminal charges for tax evasion.
