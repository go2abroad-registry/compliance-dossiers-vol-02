Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; BC Ministry of Finance for provincial sales tax. Online portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account.

E-government filing portal

A BC-incorporated corporation obtains its tax identity by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or using CRA Business Registration Online. Non-resident entities carrying on business in Canada follow the same procedure. The CRA issues a 15-digit Business Number (BN); a separate GST/HST program account (RT) is created when the entity is a GST/HST registrant. In British Columbia, entities making taxable sales of goods or services in BC must also register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance for a Provincial Sales Tax (PST) number. The BN/RT account is the statutory tax identifier for GST/HST filings; the BN is also used for corporate income tax, payroll, and excise tax accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; corporate entity status is governed by statute and is opaque for tax purposes. Relevant elections include: ITA s.85(1) rollover election, s.83(2) capital dividend election, s.216(1) election for non-residents with Canadian rental or royalty income, and fiscal-period elections under ITA s.249.1. Hybrid-mismatch rules in Part I disallow deduction mismatches arising from hybrid entities or instruments; there is no general domestic election to treat a Canadian business corporation as transparent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 annual taxable supplies made in Canada (GST/HST small-supplier threshold). BC PST registration is required separately when the supplier has a physical presence or permanent establishment in BC or makes PST-taxable sales in BC; there is no small-supplier threshold equivalent for PST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part I where goods are exported by or on behalf of the supplier. Services supplied to a non-resident person are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7, unless the service is rendered to a non-resident individual present in Canada, relates to real property situated in Canada, or relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. Deep-sea towage and emergency salvage services performed wholly outside Canada for a non-resident vessel owner, charterer, or P&I club will satisfy this rule; associated salvage awards and LOF remuneration paid by non-residents are zero-rated accordingly. The supplier must maintain contemporaneous evidence that the supply was not consumed in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A/B13P or Canadian Automated Export Declaration data)
  • Signed Bill of Lading evidencing export / foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing FX payment received within 180 days
  • Original towage/salvage contract (including LOF 2011 or similar)
  • Marine cargo manifest and international voyage records
  • Certificate of non-resident status of the recipient (e.g., W-8, tax residency certificate)

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping in Canada. GST/HST registrants must issue tax invoices containing the supplier's Business Number and the GST/HST charged or a statement that the supply is zero-rated/exempt. Electronic invoices are accepted without prior CRA approval if the invoice format is secure, complete, and auditable. Books and records, including electronic invoicing data, must be retained for six years and provided to CRA upon demand.

CRA may reassess within four years (unlimited in cases of misrepresentation), recover unremitted GST/HST with arrears interest, and claw back input tax credits if export substantiation is inadequate. Penalties include late-filing penalties, gross-negligence penalties, and for willful evasion or knowingly making false statements, criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment up to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully and freely convertible. Both capital and current account transactions, including dividend remittances, interest payments, salvage remuneration transfers, and repatriation of subscribed capital, are unrestricted; no central bank approval, exchange-control permit, or profit-remittance certificate is required. Non-resident shareholders are subject only to Part XIII withholding tax at the applicable tax-treaty rate (typically 5%-15% depending on ownership structure under Canada's income tax conventions), which operates as a purely fiscal charge and not as a convertibility barrier. The BC towage and salvage corporation may lawfully invoice and hold foreign currency (USD, EUR, GBP); CAD/USD settlement clears through the continuous linked settlement (CLS) framework, and no forced conversion ratio or profit-repatriation hurdle exists for salvage awards, towage hire, or any export service proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts. Salvage awards, towage charter-hire, and other foreign-currency trade or service proceeds may be retained indefinitely in their original currency in domestic Canadian or foreign-currency accounts at authorized financial institutions. Compliance duties are limited to: (i) FINTRAC reporting of every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more within the prescribed timeframe; (ii) maintenance of transaction records for the statutory retention period of at least five years; and (iii) readiness to evidence source of funds under the enhanced due diligence expectations of OSFI Guideline B-8 (Deterring and Detecting Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential regulator of chartered banks; Canada operates a liberalized foreign exchange regime administered without separate exchange-control licensing under the Currency Act, while anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing surveillance is performed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) pursuant to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary payment corridor; Low as a sole primary banking solution. Offshore digital EMIs (including Airwallex, Wise, and Payoneer) can execute cross-border wires, hold CAD balances, and issue local payment instructions, but any service provider soliciting Canadian clients or providing payment services to a BC-resident corporation must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where engaging in retail payment activities, must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). A BC-incorporated offshore towage and salvage operator should therefore retain a Schedule I chartered bank as its principal clearing hub and restrict EMIs to expedient sub-disbursements such as small-port supplier payments and crew wages, because Canadian banks apply enhanced transaction monitoring when an entity's central treasury is maintained wholly outside the domestic regulated banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital injections must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with a narrative explicitly identifying 'Issuance of Common Shares / Share Capital Contribution' and referencing the BC Registrar of Companies file number. The bank-prescribed payment-purpose category denoting an equity capital contribution must be stated on the transfer application, and the inbound amount must reconcile to the share subscription records and the notice of allotment filed with the corporate registry. Each international transfer of CAD 10,000 or more is FINTRAC-reported; transfers exceeding CAD 1,000,000 attract enhanced transaction monitoring under the bank's OSFI-mandated compliance program. Where the parent is a Chinese entity, the parent must first clear the SAFE outbound ODI approval route and remit through its designated domestic bank to the BC account; where the parent is a US entity, the OCC- or Fed-regulated correspondent executes the transfer. The receiving BC bank will additionally conduct sanctions and vessel (IMO-number) screening against the originator's LEI and the corporate beneficial ownership declarations prior to crediting the escrow or operational account.Clearing rails comprise: (i) SWIFT MT103/MT202 single or batch credits for definitive cross-border payments of bunker invoices, port dues, subcontract towage fees, crew wages, and equipment purchases; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued under ICC UCP 600 for mobilization deposits, vessel repairs, and capital equipment supply contracts, with the BC bank acting as issuing or confirming bank; (iii) Standby Letters of Credit and demand guarantees under ISP98/URDG 758 for tender, performance, and advance-payment bonds in foreign ports; (iv) Lynx, the high-value real-time gross settlement system operated by Payments Canada as successor to the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS), together with the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for domestic CAD obligations; and (v) Lloyds Standard Form wires whereby escrowed salvage proceeds are released by SWIFT MT103 to the salvor, owner, or appointed security representatives upon certification of the final arbitration award or an executed Lloyd's settlement agreement. Offshore digital EMIs (including Airwallex, Wise, and Payoneer) can execute cross-border wires, hold CAD balances, and issue local payment instructions, but any service provider soliciting Canadian clients or providing payment services to a BC-resident corporation must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where engaging in retail payment activities, must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). A BC-incorporated offshore towage and salvage operator should therefore retain a Schedule I chartered bank as its principal clearing hub and restrict EMIs to expedient sub-disbursements such as small-port supplier payments and crew wages, because Canadian banks apply enhanced transaction monitoring when an entity's central treasury is maintained wholly outside the domestic regulated banking system.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal-provincial general corporate rate (15% federal + 12% British Columbia provincial); 11% combined rate for Canadian-controlled private corporation active business income within the C$500,000 small business limit (9% federal + 2% BC)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax is imposed on dividends paid or credited to non-residents of Canada, unless a tax treaty reduces the rate (e.g., to 5% for a beneficial shareholder owning at least 10% of voting stock, or to 15% for a portfolio shareholder under the Canada-US DTA).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed dollar threshold exists in the Income Tax Act for mandatory transfer-pricing documentation; however, CRA requires contemporaneous documentation wherever cross-border non-arm's length transactions occur. Schedule T106 filing is statutorily required for controlled transactions exceeding C$1,000,000 (fair market value) in a taxation year, including intercompany towage fees, salvage allocation arrangements, vessel management charges, and loans/advances.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer an annual Economic Substance Regulations notification regime analogous to the UAE, but economic substance is central to the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA), transfer-pricing adjustment rules (s.247), and the foreign affiliate attribution rules. An offshore towage and salvage corporation claiming treaty benefits or reduced withholding through a Canadian parent must demonstrate substantive BC-based management, vessel ownership or bareboat chartering, employment of marine crews, harbor operations, and genuine profit-bearing economic functions; transactions lacking such substance are recharacterized and may trigger GAAR penalties and loss of treaty protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil liability: penalty of 50% of the underpaid tax amount plus arrears interest; criminal liability: prosecution under s.239 of the Income Tax Act, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years for every count.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted employee source deductions (s.227.1 ITA) and may be assessed for corporate GST/HST amounts in certain circumstances. Where the corporate structure is a sham or a façade, courts pierce the corporate veil and directors face civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for fraudulent maritime billing or sabotage of a marine casualty.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: CRA will apply GAAR to recombine transaction steps, deny treaty-based reductions, assess Part XIII withholding at the statutory 25% rate on all dividends, and charge the appropriate accrual interest; anti-conduit rules may additionally impute Canadian income to shareholders.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure an interposed Canadian shell corporation for improper treaty relief risk personal liability under negligence and breach of fiduciary duties, personal monetary sanctions equal to restored withholding tax amounts, and potential criminal conspiracy charges where documentation falsification is proven.
Money Laundering / AML-Enhanced Marine Payments

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to C$2,000,000 for entities under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; enforcement may include suspended banking operations, seizure of settlement funds, and court-ordered forfeiture of vessels involved in laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers of the towage corporation face personally audited AML obligations; failure to implement a risk-based compliance program exposes them to fines and disqualification, while deliberate participation in money laundering through fabricated salvage invoices results in criminal prosecution, asset freezing, travel restrictions, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Section 247 adjustments permit CRA to recalculate taxable income using arm's length amounts and to recharacterize the transaction; the shortfall in taxable income is subject to a 10% transfer-pricing penalty in regard to amounts exceeding the prescribed threshold, plus interest on unpaid federal and BC taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve defective transfer-pricing documents without substantive review are exposed to indemnification limitations; severe cases where the manipulated arrangement constitutes a misrepresentation subject the directors to civil penalties and to personal liability for withholding obligations if the profits were shifted out of Canada with knowledge.
