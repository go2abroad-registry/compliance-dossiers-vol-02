A 'Certificate of Compliance of Premises' from the local building department may be necessary for leasehold improvements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full capital and current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. No prior exchange-control approval is required for inbound equity injections, cross-border lease rental receipts, or dividend remittance. Profit repatriation is subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (default 25%, treaty-redUCed to 0%–15% under applicable CUS/UK tax treaties) and is payable in freely convertible CAD or USD without central bank restrictions. The CAD/CAD exchange rate is a floating, market-determined rate; no surrender or approval hurdles exist for aircraft-engine dry-lease income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules. Exporters, including aircraft engine dry lessors invoicing in USD or other currencies, are not required to convert receipts, repatriate proceeds within any prescribed timeline, or surrender a mandatory ratio of foreign currency. Proceeds may be retained in the currency of receipt, held offshore, or converted freely. Only FINTRAC reporting thresholds apply for cross-border currency transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 and for all cash transactions exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange regulatory oversight is discharged jointly by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking supervision and by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/money-laundering controls under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury and escrow operations; Medium for ancillary disbursement channels. Offshore digital EMI/FX platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Qonto) are not authorized as Canadian banks and cannot provide insured CAD deposit accounts. They must be registered with FINTRAC as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and are subject to the PCMLTFA. Material legal constraints for an aviation-engine dry-leasing treasury include: (i) no access to Canadian high-value clearing systems (Lynx), (ii) inability to act as a Qualified Financial Institution for escrow or standby letters of credit, (iii) enhanced KYC expectations of major airline lessees are not met by EMI-level documentation, and (iv) risk of being treated as a prohibited foreign bank under the Bank Act if they hold Canadian deposits without authorization.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial share capital and equity intercompany contributions are remitted using SWIFT MT103 (for commercial amounts) or SWIFT MT202 COV (for wholesale/correspondent flows) into the Canadian Schedule I account. Under IMF BOP certification, recommended purpose codes are 'EQUI' for equity capital or 'DLOA' for intercompany direct investment loans. Inbound USD may arrive via FedWire or CHIPS, while CAD funds clear through Lynx, Canada's high-value payment system. Verification requires CRS/FATCA self-certifications, source-of-wealth evidence, an executed share subscription or equity contribution agreement, and, for amounts exceeding CAD 10,000 in cross-border cash or negotiable instruments, a FINTRAC report submitted by the bank.Trade settlements for engine maintenance, parts purchases, and sublease obligations are executed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 remittances, ICC-compliant Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) processed through the bank's trade-finance desk, and for domestic CAD-denominated suppliers via Lynx or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for EFT. For USD payments to international aviation suppliers, a USD SWIFT or FedWire transfer is made from the same Canadian concentration account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value for contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (local file) in controlled cross-border transactions; a Master File is generally expected for multinational enterprises with consolidated revenue over CAD 500,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting is required for groups with consolidated revenue over CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute. Non-arm's-length cross-border transactions must satisfy the arm's-length standard under ITA s. 247; a taxpayer claiming treaty benefits must demonstrate real economic nexus and business substance (employees, offices, capital, and decision-making in BC). A BC aviation-leasing company that is only a hollow 'letterbox' will be disregarded or reassessed under the GAAR (s. 245 ITA) and treaty abuse doctrines, and may lose treaty-based relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Income Tax Act s. 239

Statutory sanction: On indictment, imprisonment up to five years and fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax; on summary conviction, fines between 50% to 200% and up to two years' imprisonment. CRA also levies a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the misreported amount plus prescribed interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize or knowingly acquiesce are personally liable as parties to the offence; they can be imprisoned, subjected to travel bans, and personally assessed restitution or penalties. Directors are further joint and severally liable for unremitted source deductions under s. 227.1 ITA.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under s. 247(2) using the arm's-length principle. A penalty under s. 247(3) of 10% of the gross transfer-pricing adjustment (or CAD 4,500 minimum in the case of a corporation, if the adjustment exceeds CAD 100,000) can be imposed. Late or incomplete T106 filings also trigger information-return penalties under Part XV.

Director personal liability: Transfer pricing penalties generally attach to the corporation, but a director who signs false records or knowingly causes a loss of Canadian tax can be prosecuted for tax evasion; civil recovery against directors is available under corporate insolvency law and fraudulent-conveyance provisions.
Sham Company / Abusive Treaty-shopping / Shell Structure

Statutory sanction: CRA will invoke the GAAR (s. 245 ITA) to deny the tax benefit; the sham transaction is void at law. Additional penalties and interest apply, and the corporation can be deemed a conduit, causing the underlying shareholder to be exposed. BC's provincial registry may also strike a shell corporation for non-filing and enforce director bans.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham may be personally assessed for disallowed tax benefits if they are found to have orchestrated the arrangement; they may face corporate-veil-piercing claims, travel restrictions, and imposition of criminal fraud penalties if the sham is part of a criminal money-laundering scheme.
AML and Economic Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 2,000,000 per violation may be imposed on entities; Criminal Code s. 462.31 money-laundering conviction carries up to ten years' imprisonment; violations of the United Nations Act / Special Economic Measures Act can trigger additional fines and freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be prosecuted for directing or permitting money laundering and sanctions evasion, potentially leading to personal imprisonment, travel bans, personal fines, and disqualification as directors. Corporate veil-piercing is available where directors used the company as an instrumentality.
