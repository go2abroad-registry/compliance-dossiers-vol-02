Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) - CRA; portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services/business-login-services.html). Customs border authority: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Provincial indirect tax authority: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, eTaxBC portal (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) to CRA, either electronically through the non-resident registration mailbox or by mail with certified copies of foreign incorporation documents, articles, and proof of identity of directors. CRA issues a 9-digit BN and permits opening program accounts for GST/HST (RT) and import/export (RM). For British Columbia, the same BN is used to register for PST under the Provincial Sales Tax Act through eTaxBC; a BC address or authorized agent/attorney for service is required. The application must identify the non-resident's Canadian permanent establishment or representative and expected volume of taxable supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election; corporations are separate taxpayers and cannot elect pass-through or partnership treatment. Available statutory elections relevant to a foreign-owned NVOCC include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration before exceeding the small supplier threshold; (ii) election for a non-resident to file GST/HST returns through an appointed agent (Form GST20); (iii) GST/HST election for closely related corporations under s. 273 of the Excise Tax Act to treat certain transactions as supplies not made for consideration (Form GST25); (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under s. 126.3 of the Income Tax Act are mandatory and not electively avoidable; no branch-level deemed inclusion election applies at the Canadian federal level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. GST/HST small supplier threshold is $30,000 in worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) in any calendar quarter and over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. BC PST has no general small-supplier exemption for retailers; foreign freight forwarders rendering GST-zero-rated services may be registered solely to claim input tax credits and must satisfy CRA's carrying-on-business nexus test.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of GST applies to (i) supplies of goods exported from Canada where title transfers to a non-resident and the goods are shipped to a destination outside Canada; (ii) a supply by a common carrier of a service of transporting goods from a place in Canada to a place outside Canada; and (iii) a supply of an arranging-for-transportation service, including freight forwarding and NVOCC brokerage, in respect of goods exported from Canada where the supply is made to a person who exports the goods and the freight forwarder retains documentary evidence that the goods were shipped to a point outside Canada. The service must be supplied to the exporter, and the NVOCC must not merely provide domestic haulage or warehousing; reverse logistics and inland drayage within Canada may be taxable unless incidental to the international export movement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or CBSA electronic export reporting confirmation for each ocean export movement
  • Bill of Lading / ocean waybill issued by the ocean carrier or NVOCC evidencing the port of loading in Canada and foreign port of discharge
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/credit advice showing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident customer, supplemented by the commercial invoice and freight invoice

Canada does not mandate a real-time tax-authority fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime. Electronic invoicing and filing are mandatory for certain GST/HST registrants through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or approved software; CBSA mandates electronic advanced cargo data via eManifest and the Canadian Export Reporting System (B13A) using EDI/API for ocean carriers and freight forwarders. BC PST returns must be filed and paid electronically through eTaxBC; no API-based fiscal invoice validation is imposed.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST within 4 years (no limitation period in cases of misrepresentation, false statements, or fraud) and deny zero-rating retroactively if the exporter cannot produce B13A, bill of lading, freight invoice, and payment evidence. Penalties include the 25% gross negligence penalty, false statements penalties up to 50% of any understatement, interest on reassessed amounts, revocation/forfeiture of refunds, and criminal prosecution for GST/HST fraud or conspiracy. In BC, PST reassessments carry provincial penalties and mandatory collection from directors; an unsuccessful zero-rating claim will convert the supply into taxable 5% GST plus possibly 7% PST, resulting in full clawback of refunds, penalties, and compound interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada imposes no exchange-control regime, no prior governmental approval for inward equity injection, and no restriction on dividend or profit repatriation by non-resident shareholders. Dividend outflows are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) at a 25% statutory rate, generally reduced to 15% or 5% under applicable tax treaties. Interest deductible on non-resident shareholder loans is capped by the thin-capitalization rules in section 18(4) ITA at a 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio. Capital is freely movable in and out of British Columbia subject only to AML/CFT reporting and the corporation's own EDD obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose mandatory surrender, forced conversion, repatriation timelines, or minimum retention ratios on export proceeds. NVOCC freight revenues may be held in CAD or foreign currency as the corporation elects; however, for Canadian tax purposes the corporation's books and records must permit computation of income in CAD, and the bank's Medium_Risk_Enhanced profile for freight-forwarding operations will require an audit trail linking receivables to underlying bills of lading.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Foreign-exchange administration is not centralized in Canada: the Minister of Finance sets monetary and exchange-rate framework policy, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally chartered banks and deposit-taking institutions, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), including cross-border currency and electronic funds transfer reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: Medium as a complementary settlement tool, Low as a primary treasury account or for escrow/LC arrangements. FINTRAC-registered money services businesses (MSBs), such as Wise Canada and Payoneer, can execute low-value cross-border FX conversion and small freight disbursements, but they are not Schedule I/II chartered banks, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and do not provide direct access to Payments Canada's Lynx/ACSS clearing rails. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), payment service providers must register with the Bank of Canada; 'escrow-like' corporate fiduciary accounts remain the exclusive domain of federally or provincially licensed trust companies. A Tier-I chartered bank must anchor all escrow, capital injection, and carrier-settlement flows.

Line 200 Federal tax payable Compute federal tax at 15% on taxable income; apply treaty reductions if applicable. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month up to 12 months; interest on late payments at prescribed rates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: CAD 10 million gross revenue; Master File: CAD 100 million gross revenue

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions; no formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but substantive economic activity is required to avoid GAAR and treaty denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 200% of tax evaded; imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; corporate fines up to CAD 1 million.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST; civil restitution and travel bans may apply.
Shell Company / Sham

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under GAAR; denial of treaty benefits; penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit; potential criminal charges for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes reassessed if they knowingly participated in the sham; potential criminal liability.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly or negligently fail to comply; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
