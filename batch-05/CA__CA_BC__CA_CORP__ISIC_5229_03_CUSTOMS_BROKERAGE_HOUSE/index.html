Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current account and capital account, with zero exchange controls on capital inflows, profit repatriation, dividend remittances, intercompany loans, trade payments, or portfolio investment. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 1953, so no Bank of Canada or Department of Finance approval is required for any cross-border payment. The only statutory friction applicable to a BC customs brokerage house is Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada): dividends and certain passive payments made to non-resident shareholders attract withholding tax at 25% statutory, typically treaty-reduced to 5-15% under Canada's comprehensive income tax conventions (e.g., Canada-US, Canada-UK, Canada-China), collected via NR4 information returns and CRA remittances. Capital account transactions are unfettered, but an inbound controlling investment may trigger Investment Canada Act review where the enterprise value exceeds the 2025 prescribed WTO threshold (C$5.0M for direct acquisitions; C$1.33B for indirect WTO-enterprise acquisitions), which is irrelevant to a sub-threshold customs brokerage subsidiary; the only corporate procedural requirement is a valid share issuance resolution, issuance of share certificates, and a BC Corporate Registry filing. Profit remittance hurdles are therefore limited to: (i) solvency-test compliance under the BC Business Corporations Act before declaring dividends; (ii) withholding tax compliance; and (iii) FINTRAC EFT reporting for outgoing wires of C$10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender rules: there is no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, no designated export-proceeds account, and no obligation to convert foreign currency receipts into CAD. A British Columbia customs brokerage house may retain receipts in CAD, USD, or any freely convertible currency in a foreign-currency account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, and conversion to CAD is discretionary at prevailing market rates. The only cross-border compliance triggers are: (i) physical transportation of currency or monetary instruments of C$10,000 or more into or out of Canada must be reported to CBSA on the Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Report (Form E677); (ii) FINTRAC reports apply to large cash transactions (C$10,000+) and international electronic funds transfers (C$10,000+) under the PCMLTFA; (iii) exporters must retain underlying customs and sales records for a minimum of six years under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act; and (iv) where export services are rendered to non-resident affiliates, transfer-pricing documentation under section 247 of the Income Tax Act (Canada) must evidence arm's-length terms.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - Canadian monetary authority and sole issuer of Canadian bank notes; foreign exchange policy competence resides with the Department of Finance Canada, while the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers cross-border currency and monetary instruments reporting and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) supervises AML/CFT obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control board or exchange permit authority, and the Canadian dollar operates under a free float with IMF Article VIII status.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a secondary rail, Low as the sole treasury host. Canadian law does not recognize the European Economic Area 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licence; a non-bank payments firm operating in Canada must be registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, and offshore EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex cannot open clearing accounts at Payments Canada (Lynx or ACSS), cannot hold Canadian demand deposits, and cannot issue Canadian settlement instruments unless partnered with, or backstopped by, a Schedule I bank. For a Canadian customs brokerage, the legally viable configuration is: primary CAD/USD operational account and capital escrow account at a Schedule I bank; the FINTRAC-registered EMI may be used solely for auxiliary cross-border vendor disbursements, payroll in foreign jurisdictions, or small-value client payouts, provided each EMI transaction is mapped back to an underlying customs entry or brokerage invoice and the EMI's Canadian MSB registration number is verified annually.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for inbound equity financing from the non-resident parent to the BC customs brokerage: (1) the board of directors adopts a share-subscription resolution denominating the CAD-equivalent consideration and the class of shares to be issued; (2) the parent bank transmits a SWIFT MT103 with payment narrative 'Share capital subscription - [Corporate Legal Name]' - Canada mandates no statutory purpose-code table, so the free-format narrative must unambiguously evidence the capital nature of the transfer to satisfy FINTRAC EFT transparency and the bank's source-of-funds review; (3) CAD funds settle through the SWIFT network and Payments Canada Lynx in near real time, while USD funds may be credited to a segregated USD operating account or converted at the bank's prevailing spot rate with an optional welded forward; (4) within the statutory filing window, the brokerage issues the share certificates, updates the BC Corporate Registry via BC Online, and records the subscription in the share-capital ledger and minute book; (5) if the investment exceeds Investment Canada Act thresholds, the purchaser files the prescribed notification or application for review with Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada - not expected for a sub-threshold brokerage; (6) the AML file retains the subscription agreement, source-of-wealth statement for the transferring shareholder, bank credit advice, and the resolution, providing a complete audit trail that distinguishes equity from debt and avoids any mischaracterization as taxable Canadian-source income.Clearing rails for outbound disbursements and trade settlement: (i) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers for brokerage service fees, freight-forwarder payments, and client duty reimbursements - CAD-denominated payments settle via Lynx, while USD-denominated payments clear through the Canadian bank's US correspondent network (typically JPMorgan Chase, BNY Mellon, or Wells Fargo) chosen to minimize correspondent fees and float; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit (sight and usance) issued under UCP 600 and transmitted via SWIFT MT700 for high-value bonded-transit cargo and equipment settlements; (iii) Payments Canada ACSS for domestic CAD payroll, supplier payments, and direct deposits, with the modernized same-day settlement framework applicable as of the 2025 ACSS modernization; (iv) the CBSA CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) portal as the mandatory statutory rail for payment of duties, GST/HST, and penalties under the brokerage's approved financial security (surety bond) - duty payments are lodged as CARM financial obligations rather than conventional bank wires and must be separately reconciled; and (v) dedicated US-dollar clearing accounts maintained outside CDIC deposit insurance, allowing retention of USD receivables and duty floats without conversion spread leakage on North-American transactions.Outright deliverable FX forward contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP, CAD/CNH) with documented underlying trade, balance-sheet, or royalty exposure, typically available for tenors up to 24 months for a licensed corporate; governed by ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex.FX swaps (simultaneous spot sale and forward re-purchase, or forward-forward combinations) permitted for liquidity management and intercompany funding without incurring spot conversion costs.Cross-currency interest rate basis swaps (CAD/USD XCCY swaps) permitted for long-term hedging of intercompany debt or capital-account exposures, priced off the CORRA and SOFR benchmarks.Vanilla European-style FX options, including zero-cost collars and participating forwards, permitted under ISDA/CSA documentation for protecting duty-float and fee-income USD-CAD flows.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are NOT permissible for CAD and are not quoted by Canadian banks, because the Canadian dollar is fully convertible, freely deliverable, and supported by a deep onshore forward market; any request for a CAD NDF would be rejected on regulatory and market-convention grounds.Canadian dollar interest rate swaps and CORRA Overnight Index Swaps (OIS) to hedge the floating-rate exposure of the operational credit facility, classified as permissible treasury hedges under the bank's credit agreement and IFRS hedge-accounting rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File required if gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million; Country-by-Country Report required if consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 750 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance for treaty benefits; customs brokerage must maintain real business operations, employees, and decision-making in Canada

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of tax evaded; criminal prosecution with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they failed to exercise due diligence; travel bans and asset freezes possible
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income, denial of treaty benefits, application of GAAR, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for corporate tax debts if the company is a sham; may face criminal charges and imprisonment
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for individuals; suspension or revocation of customs brokerage license

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly or negligently fail to implement compliance programs; may face imprisonment up to 5 years
Unlicensed Customs Brokerage

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 50,000 per violation; seizure of goods; criminal prosecution for unauthorized practice

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for fines and penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to unlicensed activity; potential imprisonment
