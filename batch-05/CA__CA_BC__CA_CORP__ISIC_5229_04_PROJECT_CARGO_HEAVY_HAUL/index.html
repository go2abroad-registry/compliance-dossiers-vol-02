Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

British Columbia provincial tax counterpart: Ministry of Finance (British Columbia) via 'eTaxBC' for provincial sales tax (PST) registration and BC Corporate Online for Business Corporations Act filings; federal corporate registry is Corporations Canada.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Canadian-resident business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Income Tax Act s. 2, taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credit relief; no pass-through regime; a BC-active corporation is also liable to BC provincial corporate income tax; a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada is taxed under Part I on Canadian-source attributable income and generally remains subject to Part XIV branch tax unless treaty-reduced).

Available election forms

Canada does not employ a 'check-the-box' entity classification election; classification follows statutory incorporation law, and hybrid mismatch rules (enacted 2022 under the Income Tax Act) restrict deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction outcomes for cross-border entity mismatches. Available statutory elections include: (i) functional-currency reporting election (ITA s. 261); (ii) non-resident s. 216 election for rental income or timber royalties; (iii) Part XIV branch tax compliance mechanics under ITA s. 219; (iv) GST/HST election for nil consideration between closely related corporations and the small-supplier quick method; (v) GST/HST non-resident rebate applications under ETA ss. 252.1/252.2; and (vi) allocation of the federal CAD 500,000 small-business limit among associated corporations and the corresponding BC small-business deduction allocation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (ETA s. 148): GST/HST registration is mandatory once total worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available below the threshold and is commercially required to recover input tax credits on zero-rated international freight; BC PST registration carries no analogous minimum threshold for PST-taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% with full input tax credit recovery applies to (i) exported tangible personal property under ETA Schedule VI, including out-of-gauge heavy-lift machinery and project equipment, where the goods are delivered to a common carrier or customs office for immediate export and are exported within the statutory 60-day export window; (ii) international freight transportation and shipping services supplied from a place in Canada to a destination outside Canada under ETA Schedule VII, including combined cartage, drayage, and handling forming part of one international freight contract; and (iii) services supplied to non-residents that directly relate to goods destined for export. The domestic-only leg of a haul and storage before export remain taxable at 5% unless contractually subsumed in a zero-rated international shipping service.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration or automated filing through the Canadian International Trade Single Window evidencing departure from Canada
  • Customs-validated commercial invoice and packing list
  • Through Bill of Lading or multimodal transport document (or equivalent waybill) naming a destination outside Canada and evidencing export movement
  • Project logistics contract / purchase order with milestone, INCOTERMS, and Letter of Credit terms
  • Carrier attestation and proof of export (vessel/truck/rail/air waybill and arrival-confirmed documentation from the destination customs authority)
  • Destination-country customs release or PGA entry documentation confirming physical arrival abroad
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank FX proceeds report matching invoice value and proving repatriation of export proceeds
  • OOG route-specific permits (dimension and weight approvals, police escorts, port heavy-haul permits) where relied on to evidence the international consignment

Canada has no real-time electronic-invoice clearance, government API stamping, or digital fiscal stamp mandate. Invoices must contain the prescribed Excise Tax Act information (supplier name, BN / GST registration number, invoice date, description of supply, and tax payable), and books and records must be retained in CRA-acceptable format. Registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,000,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account; voluntary Pan-Canadian electronic invoicing is available via the PEPPOL network (BIS 3.0) but is not mandatory for domestic B2B supplies.

CRA applies heightened audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims using export verification checklists, customs reciprocity, and cargo-document cross-checks. Normal reassessment windows are four years under ETA s. 298 and ITA s. 152(4), extended indefinitely for misrepresentation, neglect, wilful default, or fraud. If zero-rating is overturned, CRA reassesses the supply at 5% GST, disallows and claws back input tax credits, charges daily compound prescribed-rate interest, and imposes penalties: false-statement penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of understated net tax; gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the additional tax under ETA s. 285 and ITA s. 163; criminal evasion prosecution under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 with fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment, plus director and officer exposure and potential revocation of the GST/HST registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no exchange controls, quotas, advance-approval requirements, or repatriation restrictions applicable to dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, branch profits, or loan repayments payable to non-residents. Remittance is limited only by the corporate-law solvency tests (Canada Business Corporations Act s. 42 and British Columbia Business Corporations Act s. 123) and by Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (statutory 25% on dividends to non-residents; treaty-reduced to 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for substantial-holding corporate dividends under the Canada-US and Canada-China income tax conventions). A BC corporation may freely hold, invoice, borrow, and settle in CAD, USD, or any convertible currency through Schedule I chartered banks without central bank approval, exchange-control licence, or balance-of-payments authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation quota, or surrender obligation on export receipts. ISIC 5229 project-cargo invoices may be settled and retained in foreign currency, converted at negotiated commercial rates, or netted through multi-currency operating accounts. The only related obligations are FINTRAC reporting of every cross-border electronic funds transfer exceeding CAD 10,000, Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export reporting through the Canadian Export Declaration (CAED) for goods shipped, and economic-sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA) and the United Nations Act where a counterparty or corridor is prescribed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary and currency authority under the Bank of Canada Act; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential supervisor of federally regulated banks and foreign bank branches; and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing supervisor under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administrator or SAFE-type licensing agency; oversight is distributed among the Bank of Canada, Payments Canada, and the Minister of Finance acting through the Exchange Fund Account.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a deposit-taking substitute; Medium as a payments and FX overlay. Canada does not recognize the UK/EU EMI licence construct: only a Schedule I or Schedule II bank, a Canadian federal credit union, or an authorized foreign bank branch under the Bank Act may accept deposits, and an offshore digital EMI that accepts Canadian-resident deposits without a Canadian banking licence risks an offence under s. 545 of the Bank Act. An offshore EMI may instead (i) register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, which permits domestic and cross-border money transfers, FX dealing, and payment services but not deposit-taking, or (ii) contract as a white-label Banking-as-a-Service partner to a Schedule I bank's regulated deposit account. Recommended structure for this project: hold escrowed capital and operational liquidity at a Schedule I bank; use an MSB-registered fintech only for corridor FX execution, cross-border payout orchestration, and multi-currency settlement; and obtain the fintech's FINTRAC registration number plus a written Canadian AML compliance program before onboarding funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound route for non-resident parent funding into the BC project-cargo company: (i) execute the share-subscription or shareholder-loan agreement with board resolutions and, for debt, a written promissory note at arm's-length market rates to pre-empt Canadian thin-capitalization and transfer-pricing adjustment risk; (ii) send the wire via SWIFT MT103 (GPI) from the parent's bank using the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' or 'CAPR' for share capital and 'LOAN' for intercompany debt, referencing the BC registry incorporation number and a unique payment reference; (iii) convert foreign currency to CAD through the receiving Schedule I bank's FX desk, or hold the account as a USD or multi-currency operating account; (iv) deliver the supporting verification bundle (articles of incorporation, share register, certificate of incumbency, good-standing certificate, and source-of-funds file) before or within five business days of value date to avoid funds holds; (v) report any transfer or associated transfers over CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC within the prescribed period; no Bank of Canada approval, exchange-control permit, or investment-authority authorization is required for inbound equity or shareholder loans. All disbursements over CAD 10,000 attract FINTRAC reporting; exports require CBSA CAED declarations; and complete audit trails (invoices, proof-of-delivery, carrier rate confirmations, and customs entries) must be retained for at least five years to support CRA and AML review.Deliverable over-the-counter FX forward contracts in CAD/USD and major G10 crosses booked under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the Canadian Master Agreement with a Credit Support Annex posted as credit supportVanilla FX swaps for principal buy-sell and re-exchange at maturity covering near-dated working-capital and milestone-timing mismatchesLong-dated cross-currency swaps hedging CAD/USD and CAD/EUR project cash-flow mismatches across the multi-year heavy-haul contract termFX options and risk-reversal structures including zero-cost collars, caps, and participating forwards for freight-rate and heavy-equipment acquisition hedgingExchange-traded CAD/USD futures and options listed on the CME as a transparent exchange-cleared alternative to OTC productsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited to hedging non-convertible tail currencies encountered on emerging-market project-cargo corridors; NDFs referencing CAD are not market-standard but are legally permissible where a Canadian bank counterparty agrees to offer themCross-currency interest-rate swaps where the project has a USD- or EUR-denominated debt tranche against CAD operating revenue, fully documented under ISDA with a Credit Support Annex and a hedging-eligibility attestation accepted by the Canadian Schedule I bank

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a deposit-taking substitute; Medium as a payments and FX overlay. Canada does not recognize the UK/EU EMI licence construct: only a Schedule I or Schedule II bank, a Canadian federal credit union, or an authorized foreign bank branch under the Bank Act may accept deposits, and an offshore digital EMI that accepts Canadian-resident deposits without a Canadian banking licence risks an offence under s. 545 of the Bank Act. An offshore EMI may instead (i) register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, which permits domestic and cross-border money transfers, FX dealing, and payment services but not deposit-taking, or (ii) contract as a white-label Banking-as-a-Service partner to a Schedule I bank's regulated deposit account. Recommended structure for this project: hold escrowed capital and operational liquidity at a Schedule I bank; use an MSB-registered fintech only for corridor FX execution, cross-border payout orchestration, and multi-currency settlement; and obtain the fintech's FINTRAC registration number plus a written Canadian AML compliance program before onboarding funds.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate rate (federal 15% + British Columbia 12%); a non-resident-controlled Canadian corporation does not qualify for the small business deduction and is subject to the full 27% on Canadian-source active business income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax under s.212(2) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; reduced to 5% under the Canada-US DTA for dividends paid to a US corporation that owns at least 10% of the voting shares, and to 15% for portfolio dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File/contemporaneous documentation: required where the aggregate value of transactions between the Canadian corporation and each non-resident related party exceeds CA$1,000,000 in the taxation year (ITA s.247(4) and CRA IC 87-2R). Master File and Country-by-Country Report: required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is CA$750,000,000 or more; the Canadian entity must file CbC Form RC4649 and the Master File in accordance with the CRA's 2023 TP documentation guidance.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations analogous to offshore jurisdictions; economic substance is enforced through the common-law sham doctrine, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), treaty Limitation on Benefits clauses, and the CRA's anti-treaty-shopping practice. A Canadian heavy-haul project-cargo subsidiary must maintain real substance in BC: a physical office, full-time operational and dispatch employees, contracts negotiated and executed in Canada, and board decisions documented in Canada. A shell entity without employees or decision-making functions will be disregarded and income reallocated to the non-resident principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - ITA s.239 and gross negligence s.163(2)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. Civil: gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated amount, plus interest compounded daily, full reassessment, and CRA audit and asset seizure powers.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, authorize, or assent to the evasion are prosecuted as parties to the offence; a conviction may trigger bail conditions restricting travel and CRA court orders freezing personal assets. Directors who receive corporate assets diverted in the course of the scheme are personally liable under the s.160 asset-transfer rule for the full market value received, enforceable by civil restitution and garnishment.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement - ITA s.247(3)

Statutory sanction: Reallocation and recharacterization of the non-arm's-length transaction; 10% transfer pricing penalty on the net adjustment with no dollar cap; additional 50% gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) for culpable conduct; prescribed interest compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the resulting unremitted corporate tax debt under s.227.1 where the corporation fails to pay, regardless of intent; where corporate assets are transferred to shareholders or affiliates before payment, s.160 permits the CRA to recover from directors as transferees; non-compliance may also trigger director travel restrictions and foreign-asset freezing orders.
Unremitted Part XIII Withholding - ITA s.212(2) and s.227(8)-(9)

Statutory sanction: The corporation is liable for the full amount that should have been withheld, plus a 10% late-repayment penalty (20% where the failure was made knowingly or under gross negligence), plus prescribed interest compounded daily; the NR4 failure penalty reaches CA$25,000 for repeat violations.

Director personal liability: Every director of the corporation is jointly and severally liable for the unwithheld Part XIII tax under s.227.1; CRA may issue a director's assessment after demonstrating the corporation is unable to pay, without any requirement of director knowledge or fault, and may enforce by garnishment, asset liens, and travel restrictions pending settlement.
Shell Company / AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach - CBCA, BCBCA, PCMLTFA

Statutory sanction: Under the CBCA/BCBCA: administrative dissolution or revocation of charter; fines up to CA$200,000 and 6 months' imprisonment for knowingly false beneficial ownership disclosure; banks are required to freeze and report suspicious transactions under the PCMLTFA; CRA may disregard the entity as a sham and attribute its income to the true foreign owner, with reassessment and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors of a dissolved or struck BC corporation are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate income tax, GST/HST, source deductions, and wages; directors continue to face personal assessment for unremitted withholdings after dissolution; directors may be prohibited from leaving Canada pending CRA collection proceedings and are exposed to criminal liability for money laundering where the pass-through structure facilitates the proceeds of crime.
