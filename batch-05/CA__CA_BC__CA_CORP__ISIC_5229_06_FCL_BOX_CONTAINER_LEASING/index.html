Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST registration, Business Number issuance, and federal income tax administration; portal: CRA My Business Account / Business Registration Online. British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax is administered by the BC Revenue Division, Ministry of Finance via eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a 9-digit Business Number by filing Form RC1 with CRA when it begins taxable Canadian activity; a domestically incorporated corporation obtains a BN during federal/provincial registration. The GST/HST program account is created through the same CRA registration process and is the BN plus the 'RT' suffix. BC PST registration is completed separately with the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC before taxable leases occur in BC. Registration may be made on a voluntary basis even when Canadian taxable supplies are below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian law provides no check-the-box partnership or disregarded entity election. Non-resident corporations may access treaty benefits and may use the Income Tax Act s. 216 election for rental income from Canadian real property. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 apply without elective carve-outs. Where supplies are made to non-residents, CRA permits certain joint elections or filings to avoid improper GST/HST application, but zero-rating of exported supply is mandatory, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, in the current calendar quarter or preceding four calendar quarters for the GST/HST small-supplier threshold; BC PST has no general small-supplier turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A lease of ISO shipping containers is zero-rated under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the container is exported by the lessor or on the lessor's behalf, delivered to a common carrier for immediate export, or taken out of Canada by a non-resident lessee before first use in Canada. The container must not be leased for use in Canada after export, and documentary evidence must prove the international carriage move. Where an ISO container is first leased for use in Canada, the lease attracts GST and BC PST, but subsequent export may still qualify the supply for zero-rating if the lessor makes the supply after export.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Canada Border Services Agency export-report or ACI eManifest record
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill / house bill identifying container number, port of loading, and foreign destination
  • Lease agreement and intermodal movement records including container pick-up, depot release, carrier voyage, and foreign redelivery statement
  • Commercial invoice stating lease fee, ISO container number, lessee details, destination country, and 'GST/HST zero-rated' notation
  • Inward remittance certificate or equivalent bank wire/ACH evidence confirming offshore lease payments

No Canadian real-time fiscal clearance, pre-authorization, or digital stamping mandate. Electronic invoices are accepted where the record is legible and retained with underlying export documentation for at least six years. CRA supports API/EDI filing mechanisms and the Pan-Canadian Peppol interoperability framework; zero-rated export invoices should state 'GST/HST zero-rated' and reference the container lease invoice and vessel or shipment details.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export treatment within the normal four-year reassessment period, and without limitation where there has been misrepresentation. The civil gross-negligence penalty is the greater of C$100 and 50% of the net tax amount under the Excise Tax Act; criminal tax evasion can attract fines up to 250% of the evaded tax and imprisonment. Improper zero-rating claims result in GST assessment, interest, ITC denial, and potential BC PST reassessment with penalties and interest for unremitted PST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a freely floating exchange rate administered by the Bank of Canada. Both current and capital account transactions are generally fully convertible without exchange control approval. Profit remittances, including dividends and branch repatriations, are not subject to convertibility hurdles; the only material constraints are statutory withholding tax requirements under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and applicable tax treaty relief under the Canada-US Tax Convention or other relevant treaties. Non-resident directors and shareholders may freely inject or withdraw capital in CAD or foreign currency without Bank of Canada authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in Canada. Exporters and lessors of shipping containers are not required to convert foreign currency receipts into Canadian dollars within any statutory timeline. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, distributed offshore, or used for trade settlement at the entity's discretion, provided such transactions are accurately recorded for income tax and transfer pricing purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential regulator

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary banking channel. Non-Canadian regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating through digital platforms do not generally hold a Canadian banking license and cannot provide formal CAD clearing account functionality or deposit insurance. They may be used ancillary payment collection or remote treasury overlay under an OSFI-compliant domestic bank arrangement, but must not be the sole custody or clearing rail for corporate capital or trade flows. Legal constraints under the Bank Act (Canada) require that deposit-taking activity be performed by a regulated Canadian bank; any unlicensed EMI accepting deposits would violate federal banking and payment system rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound investor equity or shareholder loan injections are routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the corporation's operational CAD account at a Toronto or Vancouver clearing center. There is no exchange control license requirement. Each incoming wire should carry purpose code COT (Capital Transfer - Equity) or a compliant Customs/Industry Code as requested by the receiving bank, typically Canadian Payments Association standard description 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION'. For loans, code 'INTRACOMPANY LOAN' is used. The bank will verify beneficiary naming and the corporate B.C. registry number. Supporting documentation should include a board resolution authorizing the injection, shareholder register update, and the executed loan agreement or equity subscription agreement. For high-value or documentary transactions exceeding CAD 500,000, the lessee may elect to open an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) under UCP 600 naming the British Columbia lessor as beneficiary. Additional penalties for failing to file an instalment, misrepresentation, or failure to comply with information return obligations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 – Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's-Length Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed if aggregate transactons with a non-resident exceed this threshold. CRA may request full contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, including a Local File and Master File, where the aggregate value of covered transactions is significant (commonly > C$5,000,000) or where the transfer-pricing adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue. A penalty under subsection 247(3) is not charged for smaller adjustments.

Economic substance scope: British Columbia does not maintain offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations. However, Canadian law enforces a substantive commercial purpose requirement: the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) can deny a tax benefit arising from an avoidance transaction lacking a bona fide business purpose. The BC Business Corporations Act (SBC 2002, c 54) and the Canada Business Corporations Act require registered offices, resident directors, financial records, and annual reporting. A shell company without real economic activity in the container leasing business may be challenged by the CRA under GAAR, have intercompany deductions denied, and may lose treaty benefits under the principal purpose test (PP T) where applicable.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Statements or Concealment

Statutory sanction: Under sections 238 and 239 of the Income Tax Act, fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years. Civil penalties include reassessment of tax, interest compounded daily, and gross negligence penalties up to 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid withholding taxes, unremitted GST/HST, and certain employee source deductions under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act. If evasion is proven, officers responsible for the misrepresentation may face criminal charges, director disqualification, and travel bans imposed by the court.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments are made under section 247. Penalty of 10% of the net adjustment when the adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 and 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue. Interest is compounded daily; no offset for tax paid to a foreign jurisdiction is permitted unless a treaty applies.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in preparing false transfer pricing documentation can be subjected to civil penalties for conspiracy or negligent misrepresentation. In serious cases, the CRA may invoke section 239 as aiding in tax evasion, exposing directors to personal fines and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Loss of tax treaty benefits under the principal purpose test; denial of deductions and loss carryovers under GAAR; social or administrative dissolution by BC Registry after repeated failure to file annual reports. Financial penalties can be up to C$10,000 for late annual report filings, and a resulting liquidation can accelerate tax liabilities.

Director personal liability: Directors of a dissolved company may be personally liable for unsatisfied corporate tax claims under the BC Business Corporations Act. Courts may pierce the corporate veil if the company is used as a sham to evade tax and impose equitable restitution or travel bans on non-compliant directors.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 for a corporation per violation; criminal sanctions upon conviction include fines of up to C$2 million and imprisonment for up to 10 years. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) can issue compliance orders and freeze assets.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and employees who knowingly participate in or direct money laundering activities can be prosecuted as parties to the offence, facing personal fines and imprisonment. Directors may also face civil liability for losses caused to the corporation or the Crown for failure to implement adequate AML controls.
