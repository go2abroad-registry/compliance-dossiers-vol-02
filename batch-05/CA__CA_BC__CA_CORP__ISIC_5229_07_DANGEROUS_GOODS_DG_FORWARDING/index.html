Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); GST/HST and federal income tax administration through the My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). The British Columbia Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch) administers the separate Provincial Sales Tax (PST) under the Provincial Sales Tax Act.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities and Canadian Business Corporations must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Procedure: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or provincially under the BC Business Corporations Act (BCBCA) via Corporate Online/BC Registry; (2) complete Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or use the CRA Business Registration Online portal to open the GST/HST account (program identifier 'RT'), Import/Export account ('RM'), and Payroll account ('RP'); (3) non-resident corporations soliciting orders in BC must appoint a resident agent for service and register the BN before carrying on business; (4) obtain a Carrier Identification Number (CIN) and register under the CBSA eManifest (ACI) portal for freight forwarding. A non-resident that solicits orders in Canada is liable to register for GST/HST and cannot rely on the small supplier exemption.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident under ITA s. 250(4)(a) and taxed under Part I on worldwide income. A non-resident corporation with a permanent establishment in BC is taxed only on Canadian-source business income. No pass-through or partnership attribution applies to BC corporations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident under ITA s. 250(4)(a) and taxed under Part I on worldwide income. A non-resident corporation with a permanent establishment in BC is taxed only on Canadian-source business income. No pass-through or partnership attribution applies to BC corporations. For GST/HST, the corporation is a separate registrant; for BC PST, the corporation is a vendor liable for PST registration on taxable sales.

Available election forms

Canadian income tax has no check-the-box regime; entity classification is driven by the incorporating statute. Available elections: (i) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident owners of rental real estate; (ii) ITA s. 261 functional-currency election (USD or CAD reporting); (iii) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations to file joint GST/HST returns; (iv) Quick Method of Accounting election (ETA s. 156.1) for small suppliers; (v) hybrid mismatch and excessive interest election/attribution rules under ITA ss. 18.2, 18.4-18.7 for inbound cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, measured quarterly (ETA s. 123(1) 'small supplier'); a non-resident who solicits orders in Canada loses the small supplier exception and must register for GST/HST, regardless of Canadian revenue level, once it makes any taxable supply in Canada (ETA s. 240(1.1)). BC PST registration is required for any seller, including non-resident remote sellers, making taxable sales to BC consumers above the PST small seller threshold of CAD 10,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible personal property from Canada is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (s. 1). Freight forwarding services supplied in respect of exported goods, including international transportation of dangerous goods, are zero-rated under Schedule VI, s. 7 where the service is supplied to a non-resident, the transportation is from Canada to a destination outside Canada, and the goods are in fact exported. The forwarder must demonstrate that the goods left Canada and that export was completed; GST paid on inputs (input tax credits) remains fully creditable even where revenue is zero-rated. DG forwarding additionally requires compliance with the Transport of Dangerous Goods Act, 1992 and IATA/IMDG packing and declaration rules, which do not affect the GST characterization.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration (or CARM eManifest equivalent A6/A8) filed within 15 days after exportation; mandatory for goods exceeding CAD 2,000 FOB value or controlled/dangerous goods; includes exporter number, UN/NA number, and HS classification.
  • Through Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (Master and House) evidencing the consignee outside Canada, port of discharge, and date of outward shipment.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT wire confirmation evidencing FX or CAD payment received from the non-resident consignee, traced to the corresponding export invoice, and bank statements showing repatriation into a Canadian financial institution account.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance e-invoicing mandate exists in Canada. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (NETFILE or GST/HST TELEFILE) where registrant revenue exceeds CAD 1.5M; mandatory electronic remittance and reporting applies. Customs and export transactions are cleared through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) platform and the Advance Commercial Information (ACI/eManifest) portal; freight forwarders must transmit electronic cargo and house bill level data, including dangerous-goods harmonized codes and UN numbers, in real time at the border. The Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (PCP) permits voluntary PEPPOL e-invoicing for B2B transactions but does not mandate it.

No statutory 180-day repatriation deadline exists under Canadian law; the CRA uses the 180-day benchmark as a business-reasonableness test for export proceeds, treating delayed repatriation as a shareholder/loan benefit subject to ITA s. 15.2 or s. 80.4. If the exporter fails to retain the export declaration, bill of lading, and remittance evidence, the CRA reassesses the supplies as taxable GST at 5%, denies related ITCs, and applies statutory arrears interest. False or fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under the Excise Tax Act and the Income Tax Act, exposing the registrant to gross negligence penalties, fines up to CAD 200,000 on indictment, imprisonment up to five years, and retrospective clawback of refunds and ITCs for a four-year reassessment window, extended indefinitely for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. The Customs Act additionally authorizes administrative penalties for inaccurate export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account items under a free-floating exchange-rate regime. No prior Bank of Canada approval, economic-needs test, or licence is required to receive, hold, or remit foreign currency. Freight invoices, charter payments, and LC proceeds may be denominated in CAD, USD, or EUR. Profit remittance: dividends may be paid freely cross-border, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (25% statutory; treaty-reduced to 5%-15% depending on shareholder residency, e.g., 5% for substantial holdings under the US/UK treaties, 10% for China). Practical reporting barriers are limited to FINTRAC reports on cross-border wires of CA$10,000 or more, CBSA customs declarations, and CRA foreign-reporting obligations (T1134/T1135) when thresholds are met.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export proceeds. A BC-based dangerous-goods forwarder may retain 100% of USD/EUR receipts onshore or offshore. Statutory obligations are documentary only: Transport Canada registration and shipping documents under the Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act and Regulations; CBSA export declarations for controlled or hazardous goods; retention of records for six years under the PCMLTFA; and automatic FINTRAC reporting for any incoming or outgoing wire of CA$10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and retail-payments overseer; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of federally chartered banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/ATF supervisor under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). No dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority exists; Canada abolished exchange controls in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as an ancillary collection and disbursement rail; Low as a primary or sole operating account. Canada does not grant EMI charters. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex) may operate only as money-service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, must rely on a Canadian Schedule I bank for CAD clearing, and must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada. Legal constraints: no deposit-taking, no CDIC insurance, no direct Lynx settlement, and frequent underwriting rejection of high-risk DG freight profiles. Use only for high-frequency, low-value disbursements (e.g., per-shipment courier or handling fees); never for payroll, tax remittances, or LC operations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined Canadian federal and British Columbia general corporate income tax rate (federal 15% plus BC provincial 12%); reduced to 11% for qualifying CCPCs using the small business deduction (federal 9% plus BC 2%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% non-resident dividend withholding tax imposed under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. Treaty rates may apply; for example, under the Canada-United States DTA, 5% for a corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of voting shares and 15% for portfolio holdings.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due no later than six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. Corporate balance-due tax is due two months after the fiscal year-end (three months for certain CCPCs eligible for the small business deduction).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada requires Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) for all reportable cross-border controlled transactions, regardless of transaction value; there is no statutory de minimis for T106. For contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation and Local File/Master File preparation, CRA expects documentation where any controlled transaction exceeds CAD 1,000,000 or where the aggregate value of controlled transactions is significant relative to the taxpayer's gross revenues. Country-by-Country Report filing and Master File, under the OECD CBCR framework, are mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated annual revenue above EUR 750,000,000 (approximately CAD 1.1 billion).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations similar to offshore Caribbean jurisdictions. BC and federal tax law instead apply the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR, Income Tax Act s.245), the economic substance/substance-over-form doctrine, and the OECD principal purpose test under the Multilateral Instrument to disregard transactions lacking genuine commercial purpose, real activity, personnel, or risk. A BC corporation engaged in actual dangerous-goods forwarding must demonstrate substantive management and operational control in BC to access treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Blindness

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment, interest, gross negligence penalty of 50% of additional tax payable under s.163(2), and criminal prosecution under s.239(1). Criminal sanctions: fine up to 200% of the evaded tax on summary conviction and up to 400% on indictment, plus imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally personally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/QST under s.227.1; active concealment can trigger personal civil restitution, asset freezes, travel bans pending criminal trial, and imprisonment for fraud-related tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment to arm's length results; 10% penalty on net adjustment under s.247(4) where no reasonable effort was made; interest on understated corporate tax; additional false-declaration penalties under s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer-pricing returns may face criminal liability for false statements, be required to repay wrongful dividends paid from inflated profits, and be disqualified from board service; CRA may pursue director personal assets through civil enforcement.
AML and Sanctions Violations (High-Risk DG Operations)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA up to CAD 1,000,000 for an entity per violation; enforcement under the Special Economic Measures Act and United Nations Act with fines up to CAD 1.5 million (or CAD 25,000 for individuals) and imprisonment up to 10 years; cargo seizure and forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally subject to FINTRAC administrative penalties where the failure is attributable to gross negligence; criminal fines and imprisonment can attach personally; travel bans may be imposed pending foreign corruption or sanctions inquiries.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under GAAR, denial of treaty benefits under the OECD principal purpose test, Part XIII withholding tax on deemed distributions, 50% gross negligence penalties on re-assessed income, and cancellation of import/export authorizations by the Canadian Security Intelligence Service or Transport Canada for false representation.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow artificial construction of a shell may be personally liable for return of distributions paid as fraudulent conveyances, s.160 tax liability for undervalue transfers, and criminal sanctions for signature on false statutory declarations; a travel ban restriction may be imposed as a bail condition in related fraud prosecutions.
