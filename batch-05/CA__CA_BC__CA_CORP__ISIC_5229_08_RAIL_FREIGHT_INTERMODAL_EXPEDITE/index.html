Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire safety certification and an occupancy permit are required for the office premises; structural load inspection is not required unless a rail-adjacent warehouse or heavy equipment transloading area is installed, in which case an engineer's structural assessment, WorkSafeBC occupational clearance, and fire code compliance certificate must be obtained.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via My Business Account portal and GST/HST NETFILE

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must, prior to commencing commercial activity, apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) either online through the Business Registration Online system, by mail using Form RC1, or by telephone. Where the corporation is incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or a provincial statute, the BN is issued after incorporation; a separate GST/HST account is mandatory for any person making more than CAD 30,000 in annual taxable supplies. Non-resident corporations may register directly using Form RC151 (GST/HST Application for Registration) and may designate a resident agent for service of notices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must, prior to commencing commercial activity, apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) either online through the Business Registration Online system, by mail using Form RC1, or by telephone. Where the corporation is incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or a provincial statute, the BN is issued after incorporation; a separate GST/HST account is mandatory for any person making more than CAD 30,000 in annual taxable supplies. Non-resident corporations may register directly using Form RC151 (GST/HST Application for Registration) and may designate a resident agent for service of notices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not employ a check-the-box regime; resident corporations are taxed at the entity level under Income Tax Act Part I. Elective mechanisms relevant to cross-border logistics include: (a) the GST/HST streamlined non-resident registration election for selected property and services; (b) the CRA deemed permanent establishment election for treaty relief; (c) election under section 216 to file on net rental income basis where applicable; and (d) hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.2–18.7 and 20.2 requiring timely designation elections for certain structured arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies worldwide per calendar quarter or four consecutive quarters; if exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported directly by the rail freight intermodal carrier, or delivered to a Canadian customs-bonded warehouse or foreign-bound vessel/aircraft, are zero-rated, as are transportation services for exported or imported goods provided to a non-resident where the service is performed partly in Canada but principally outside Canada. The supplier must retain written evidence of export, such as customs export documents, packing slips, and transport contracts. Services ancillary to exported goods (e.g., freight expediting, customs brokerage for export, and consolidation) are also zero-rated when the underlying service is supplied to a non-resident and the principal purpose relates to the export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency ACI/CBSA Export Reporting)
  • Bill of Lading (through or combined rail/sea B/L)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank/swift confirmation of offshore payment receipt)

No real-time fiscal clearance or API-based digital stamping regime exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants must file electronically via GST/HST NETFILE (or via the CRA My Payment portal for instalments) and issue standard invoices containing the BN, transaction details, and applicable tax. E-invoicing is voluntary, with the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and PEPPOL-based exchange being available but not mandated.

CRA audits may require proof of export within 180 days of invoicing; where zero-rating is claimed fraudulently or without substantiation, the supply is reassessed at 5% GST plus provincial PST exposure, and the exporter is liable for uncollected tax, interest under the Excise Tax Act sections 280 and 281, penalties up to 15% of the amount for wilful misrepresentation or gross negligence, and potential criminal prosecution and director liability for knowingly participating in false claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full current and capital account convertibility. There is no general foreign exchange control regime requiring central bank approval for cross-border payments, dividend remittances, or capital injections. Profit repatriation is freely permitted but may attract non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (typically 25 percent, subject to treaty reduction to 5 percent for active business dividends to treaty-resident shareholders). Cross-border payments exceeding C$10,000 are subject to FINTRAC large transaction reporting, and international electronic funds transfers must be accompanied by a six-part traveler's report under PCMLTFA when all details are not available.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain receivables in foreign currency, convert them to CAD at licensed banks or financial institutions, or use them for settlement of trade payables. Export documentation must align with customs declarations at the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), but no central bank conversion certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); on anti-money-laundering matters, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) exercise statutory authority over banks and money services businesses.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions with each non-resident related party in a taxation year. Above this threshold, CRA Form T106 must be filed and contemporaneous OECD-aligned transfer pricing documentation must be prepared and produced on demand. Canada does not require the Local File/Master File to be filed with the T2, but CRA expects documentation substantially aligned with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines when audited.
