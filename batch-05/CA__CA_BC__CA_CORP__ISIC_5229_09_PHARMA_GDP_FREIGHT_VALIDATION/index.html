Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box classification election equivalent to U.S. practice. A corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or British Columbia Business Corporations Act is automatically a taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada). Partnerships are flow-through unless incorporated. A U.S. LLC registered extra-provincially in BC is generally treated as a corporation for Canadian tax purposes; however, a limited election under section 216.1 of the Income Tax Act allows certain non-resident corporations (including U.S. LLCs) to compute Canadian-source business income tax as if they were non-residents, with no full entity-classification election. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.4 and 18.5 of the Income Tax Act apply to deny deductions in respect of certain hybrid arrangements, but no elective mechanism exists for a 'CA_CORP' entity to change its default corporate classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated export supplies) triggers mandatory registration. BC PST: No general low-value registration threshold for sellers making taxable sales into British Columbia; however, a small seller exemption applies where the seller's worldwide gross revenue from taxable sales is C$10,000 or less in the prior 12 months, in which case registration is not required unless the seller chooses to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for export of tangible personal property applies under section 6 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier (1) makes a taxable supply by way of sale, (2) ships the property to a destination outside Canada in the ordinary course of business, and (3) possesses documentary evidence of export. Zero-rating for services applies under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI when the service is supplied to a non-resident person not registered for GST/HST and not a consumer in respect of a supply made in Canada, or when the service relates to tangible personal property exported from Canada. For GDP pharmaceutical freight, the following can be zero-rated: international freight transportation services supplied by a common carrier (section 5, Part V, Schedule VI), freight services supplied to a non-resident in respect of exported goods (section 7), and related handling services only where they form part of an international supply. Domestic storage, handling, and distribution within BC remain subject to 5% GST and 7% PST unless a specific exemption applies.

  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing shipment to a destination outside Canada

Canada and British Columbia do not currently mandate real-time government tax clearance, e-invoicing fiscal stamping, or API-based continuous transaction controls for GST or PST. Electronic invoices are valid if they contain the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act and applicable regulations, including supplier name and BN, date, description, amount, tax rates, and tax charged. The CRA provides optional digital filing and remittance through GST/HST NETFILE and My Business Account, but not transaction-level clearance. BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC, with no mandatory national e-invoicing or clearinghouse requirement.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rated export supply within the normal reassessment period (generally four years; up to ten years for non-arm's-length or non-resident situations) if the exporter fails to retain adequate documentary evidence for six years as required under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act. For negligent or false statements, a 10% penalty may apply under section 285, with gross negligence penalties up to C$5,000 plus 50% of the tax sought to be evaded under section 285.1. Failed zero-rating results in full 5% GST liability on the export supply, denial of related input tax credits, interest on arrears at prescribed rates compounded daily, and potential criminal prosecution under sections 327 and 328 of the Excise Tax Act (fines up to C$25,000 and imprisonment up to two years). Where export transactions involve false invoicing, fraudulent documentation, or shell entities, the CRA may also apply Income Tax Act civil fraud penalties of 50%, and the Criminal Code fraud provisions may apply. BC PST audits under the Provincial Sales Tax Act may impose interest and penalties of 10% plus up to 25% for failure to collect or remit, with liability for uncollected PST on taxable sales into British Columbia where nexus is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a freely floating currency with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. No exchange-control licence, central bank permission, or prior administrative approval is required for equity injections, trade settlements, dividend remittances, or management-fee payments by a British Columbia business corporation. The only compliance constraints are (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on outward remittances at a 25% statutory rate, treaty-reduced generally to 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for substantial corporate shareholdings, and 10%/0% for arm's-length royalties and management fees; (ii) CRA Form T1135 disclosure when foreign financial assets exceed CAD 100,000; (iii) arm's-length documentation under section 247 of the Income Tax Act for intercompany and shareholder payments; and (iv) a post-closing Investment Canada Act notification where a non-resident establishes a new Canadian business. Profit repatriation and cross-border conversion are fully deliverable at market spot rates without surrender or quota.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion timeline, no invoice-matching requirement, and no retention regime for freight or logistics receivables. Proceeds may be retained in CAD or in a USD/EUR foreign-currency clearing account at a Canadian Schedule I bank or a foreign branch. Treasury must nevertheless (a) recognise foreign-exchange gains and losses under section 39 of the Income Tax Act; (b) preserve CBSA/CARM customs entries, invoices, bills of lading, and proof of GDP shipment integrity to substantiate trade-based flows; and (c) file CRA T1135 where aggregate foreign financial assets exceed CAD 100,000 in any given tax year.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acting with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision; FINTRAC enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); the Currency Act governs the legal tender and exchange framework; and Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) administers investment and foreign-directive compliance under the Investment Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Limited and supplementary. Overseas electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as UK/FCA-authorised firms cannot hold CAD clearing accounts or operate in Canada unless registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and compliant with provincial money-services legislation. Feasibility rating: Medium for low-value vendor disbursements, payroll sub-processes, and multi-currency payouts (e.g., Wise, Payoneer); Low as a primary cash-concentration, settlement, or liquidity rail. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Forward the equity or shareholder-loan injection by SWIFT MT103 to the BC operating account using the bank's SWIFT BIC (e.g., ROYCCAT2 for RBC, TDOMCATTTOR for TD, BOFMCAM2 for BMO, CIBCCATT for CIBC, NOSCCATT for Scotiabank), the three-digit Canadian institution/clearing code, and the five-digit Vancouver branch transit code. Reference the transfer as 'Equity Subscription - [Shareholder Name]' or 'Shareholder Loan - [Name]' and apply the ISO 20022 purpose code 'COVC' for general corporate cash or 'EQTY' for equity-type injections. FINTRAC automatically generates an EFT report for each wire exceeding CAD 10,000. The corporate must retain the shareholders' resolution, share-subscription minutes, updated BC share register, and, for intercompany loans, an executed promissory note with an interest schedule consistent with section 247 of the Income Tax Act. If the injection is received in USD, either direct funds into the linked USD clearing sub-account or instruct a same-value-day spot conversion at the bank's FX desk.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 for cross-border USD/EUR vendor and customs payments using purpose codes 'TRAD' (trade settlement) or 'SUPP' (supplier payment), with beneficiary bank, clearing code, and transit details included in field 72. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Limited and supplementary. Overseas electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as UK/FCA-authorised firms cannot hold CAD clearing accounts or operate in Canada unless registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and compliant with provincial money-services legislation. Feasibility rating: Medium for low-value vendor disbursements, payroll sub-processes, and multi-currency payouts (e.g., Wise, Payoneer); Low as a primary cash-concentration, settlement, or liquidity rail. The core treasury must retain a Schedule I bank account for commercial LC issuance, committed FX lines, CRA remittances, and Lynx real-time transfers; EMI balances are pre-funded, are not CDIC-insured, and should never exceed one week of operating disbursements.
