Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal corporate income tax and GST/HST, portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO); British Columbia Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch) for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST), portal: eTaxBC. The CRA Business Number (BN) is the statutory TIN.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned bakery chain must first incorporate a Canadian business corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia), maintaining a registered and records office in BC and a BC address for service. Next, register with CRA through Business Registration Online or Form RC1 to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN), then activate a GST/HST program account, a payroll deductions program account if staff are hired, and a BC PST program account under the same BN through eTaxBC for taxable retail supplies of prepared food. Non-resident shareholders and directors are permitted, provided a resident Canadian agent or BC address for service is maintained. Registration can be completed entirely online; CRA and eTaxBC issue proof of registration and the statutory filing frequency (monthly, quarterly, or annual) based on supply volume.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian business corporations are per se taxable at the entity level; no check-the-box or pass-through election is available. Available GST/HST elections include the Excise Tax Act section 156 election (intercompany services supplied for nil consideration), section 157 election (intercompany property supplies for nil consideration), the Quick Method of Accounting election for simplified remittance, and annual reporting-period elections for smaller registrants. For cross-border financing, hybrid-mismatch (OECD BEPS Action 2) rules apply as mandatory statutory adjustments, not elections. No BC PST election is available; the only relief is the statutory exclusion for goods shipped by the vendor out of BC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including sales of associated entities) in the current or any preceding four consecutive calendar quarters for mandatory GST/HST registration under the small-supplier rules; BC PST requires vendor registration for sales of PST-taxable goods and services into BC, subject to a small-vendor exemption for annual taxable sales not exceeding CAD 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST-registered suppliers may zero-rate (0%) exports of bakery and pastry goods under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act where the goods are shipped from Canada to a destination outside Canada as a result of the supply and prescribed export documentation is retained; ancillary transportation and insurance for the exported goods are likewise zero-rated. Master-franchise royalty, advisory, and central-kitchen services supplied to a non-resident franchisee who uses the benefit wholly outside Canada may also be zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V, provided the recipient is not GST-registered in Canada. Export sales are excluded from BC PST when the vendor delivers or ships the goods out of British Columbia.

  • CBSA export declaration (electronic export reporting through EDI or the Canadian Automated Export Declaration system for goods over CAD 2,000, or B13A declaration where required)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or common-carrier cargo control document proving movement from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice stating INCOTERMS 2020 terms, HS classification, quantities, and identifying the zero-rated export shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-currency credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Master franchise agreement and royalty calculation workings substantiating zero-rating of cross-border royalty and management services

No mandatory real-time invoice clearance, fiscal digital stamping, or B2B e-invoicing regime exists federally or in BC. GST/HST registrants self-assess and remit via periodic returns filed through CRA My Business Account or GST/HST NETFILE; BC PST returns are filed via eTaxBC. Electronic invoices (PDF, XML, EDI) are valid records if kept for six years, and CRA may require invoice-level transaction listings on audit. The federal and provincial governments are adopting the PEPPOL-based Pan-Canadian B2G e-invoicing standard, which will affect public-sector procurement only and is not a bakery retail compliance requirement.

CRA export verification programs require production of the complete documentation bundle on request; failure to substantiate the export leads to reassessment at 5% GST on the full invoice value, statutory daily compound interest, and late-filing or remittance penalties, plus a false-statement penalty equal to 50% of the net tax understated where documentation is fabricated or misrepresented. CRA may reopen returns within four years of filing, extended to ten years for negligent misrepresentation, and may retroactively claw back GST refunds. Criminal prosecution under section 327.3 of the Excise Tax Act for wilful export fraud can result in fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment of up to five years; BC PST false returns attract civil penalties of up to 25% of the tax due with interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible free-floating currency with no exchange control restrictions on current account or capital account transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriations are freely permitted without prior regulatory approval, subject only to corporate law solvency tests and tax withholding compliance (e.g., Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends, which may be reduced by tax treaty). Current account payments for royalties, management fees, and intercompany goods/services are unrestricted. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, are generally free, although inbound investments exceeding specified thresholds may require review under the Investment Canada Act, and certain transactions may require notification or approval for non-Canadian acquirers. Cross-border wire transfers are freely available and not subject to conversion surrender requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio requiring export proceeds to be surrendered or repatriated to Canada. The Bank of Canada operates no exchange control surrender regime. Exporters may hold foreign currency receipts in offshore or onshore foreign-currency-denominated bank accounts and may convert CAD to foreign currency and vice versa at will, provided that the underlying transactions are accurately documented and any taxable income is reported to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Trade settlement and intercompany remittances are subject to standard financial transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and FINTRAC reporting thresholds (e.g., CA$10,000 for large cash, and all wire transfers are reportable per regulatory requirements of financial intermediaries).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); exchange control administration is vested in the Minister of Finance and the Canada Border Services Agency, with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) overseeing reporting obligations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High viability for onboarding, but subject to legal restrictions. Canadian provincial and federal banking regulation reserves deposit-taking activity to banks regulated under the Bank Act (Canada), and thus non-bank fintech entities or foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) cannot accept deposits or operate as clearing institutions without obtaining an authorization under Canadian law. However, fully regulated foreign-based financial technology payment platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Mercury) may onboard Canadian businesses as clients for payment facilitation and cross-border disbursement services, but funds must ultimately be routed through a Canadian Schedule I or Schedule II bank or a recognized clearing house. The fintech/EMI account does not serve as a substitute for a domestic operating account and cannot be used for capital escrow purposes. Due diligence is complicated by corresponding banking restrictions and AML/CFT requirements; an EMI must be licensed in its home jurisdiction and demonstrate adequate sanctions screening and transaction monitoring systems.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from foreign shareholders or the master franchise parent entity must be routed through a regulated Canadian Schedule I or II bank account with proper wire transfer protocols. Payment must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 or an equivalent correspondent clearing mechanism, denominated in CAD/USD/CNY as settled, and the originating bank must state an accurate cross-border purpose code. For China-origin capital, required wire purpose codes include: Savings and Loans (Capital Injection) — use SAFE code 6920/6210 for investment inward remittance if routed through China, or the commercial code '1101' for operating/current account flows where legally permissible. Remittance narrative must identify the sender as shareholder or parent entity and state 'Contribution to Share Capital' or 'Shareholder Loan (Non-resident Lending)' as the case requires. The Canadian receiving bank will require a completed 'Large Value Transfer System (LVTS)' notification for transactions exceeding CA$50,000 and the bank's Standard Settlement Instructions (SSIs) must be followed. Verification includes corporate resolution authorizing the capital transaction, directors' resolution for debt/equity characterization, and a legal opinion as to compliance with the Investment Canada Act notification thresholds (e.g., enterprise value above applicable financial threshold). Intermediary banks in the US or UK should be listed explicitly in the MT103 to avoid routing ambiguity. The funds must be credited to the Capital Escrow Account if the funds are designated for equity issuance; otherwise ordinary operating current-account inflows will be characterized as shareholder advances and must respect transfer-pricing documentation.Outbound disbursements for trade settlement, equipment purchases, royal-tax payments, and franchise disbursements are cleared primarily via SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) or MT202 COV (bank-to-bank cover payment) through LVTS or the US Fedwire/CHIPS corresponding networks. For domestic Canadian settlements exceeding CA$25 million, LVTS is mandatory. Trade disbursements to China should preferably be settled through CNY clearing arrangements, available through Bank of China (Canada) with CNAPS/RMB RTGS connectivity, or in USD through a US correspondent. Commercial letters of credit (L/C) will be issued by the Canadian relationship bank (irrevocable, at-sight or term) where trade counterparty risk requires documentation-based settlement. For master franchise royalty and IP fee outflows, the disbursement instruction must include reference to the underlying license agreement, the CRA Withholding Certificate (if any), and be transmitted using the purpose code corresponding to 'Royalties and License Fees' (SAFE code 9260 for outward remittance from China where relevant). All outbound wires must pass bank screening against OFAC and Canadian sanctions lists (OSFI/DFATD); entity legal name, incorporation number, and registered office address must match all KYC records.
