E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows CBCA/BCBCA corporate law, and corporations are fully taxed at entity level under ITA Part I. Available statutory elections include: the GST/HST closely-related corporation election (ETA s.156) treating intercompany taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; ITA s.256 associated-corporation elections to allocate the small business deduction; ITA s.83(2) capital dividend account election; corporate fiscal-period and partnership elections; and hybrid-mismatch rate-differential elections with EIFE (excessive interest and financing expenses) designated-group elections under ITA s.18.4-18.9 for cross-border financing structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST small-supplier threshold: C$30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies, excluding sales of capital property and financial services) over the trailing four calendar quarters under ETA s.240; mandatory registration is triggered once exceeded, and meal sales through UberEats and DoorDash count as taxable supplies. BC PST: a vendor with a place of business in BC must register immediately; an extraprovincial (out-of-province) vendor must register once BC taxable sales exceed C$10,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under ETA Schedule VI, Part V: (i) tangible personal property (including packaged or prepared meals sold for export) is zero-rated if the supplier ships or delivers the goods outside Canada, normally within 60 days of the supply (s.1-5); (ii) services supplied to a non-resident person are zero-rated under s.7 where the service is not acquired for consumption in Canada and the non-resident is not a GST/HST registrant; and (iii) domestic meal supplies through Canadian delivery platforms remain taxable at 5% GST plus 7% BC PST, and no basic-grocery zero-rating applies to prepared food.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada GCA/CAARS automated export reporting or equivalent commercial export documentation)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / carrier certificate proving physical export from Canada within the prescribed 60-day window
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank payment records) evidencing FX settlement from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract identifying the foreign purchaser and HS classification of the meal products
  • Packing list and container manifest linking the specific consignment to the zero-rated sale

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoice stamping, or digital control-authority regime. Registrants must issue a valid GST/HST tax invoice under ETA s.221 containing the BN, transaction date, identification of the parties, taxable status of each supply, consideration, and the GST/HST amount or a tax-inclusive statement; electronic invoices are accepted. Mandatory electronic filing applies through GST/HST NETFILE and eTaxBC, and registrants with annual taxable revenues above C$1.5M must file electronically. Payment service providers, including UberEats and DoorDash payouts, are subject to the CRA Electronic Payment Transaction Reporting Regime (REEPT) for payments processed from 2024, giving CRA transaction-level visibility into ghost-kitchen revenue.

CRA has full reassessment and input tax credit clawback powers over fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating: gross-negligence penalty of the greater of C$325 and 50% of the tax sought to be evaded (ETA s.285); false-declaration and evasion penalties with daily penalties (ETA s.280.1 and s.281); retrospective reassessment of uncollected GST/HST plus arrears interest (ETA s.280); criminal prosecution under ETA s.283 and ITA s.239 with fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment of up to 5 years; and, for cross-border arrangements, a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment. Substantiation of physical export and FX repatriation within the 180-day ceiling is critical, as CRA audit staff use carrier export filings, customs data, and REEPT payment-platform reports to cross-match zero-rated export claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada has been an IMF Article VIII member and does not impose exchange controls, prior approval requirements, or restrictions on the conversion, transfer, remittance, or repatriation of funds for a British Columbia business corporation. Dividends, royalties, repatriated capital, intercompany loan repayments, and settlement proceeds may be converted and remitted freely in any currency. The principal compliance frictions are (i) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act non-resident withholding tax of 25% on certain cross-border payments, reduced by applicable tax treaties; (ii) FINTRAC large-value EFT reporting at or above C$10,000 entering or leaving Canada; and (iii) source-jurisdiction exit controls (e.g., SAFE, RBI, or other central bank approval regimes) that the foreign parent must satisfy before remitting equity. The CAD is the functional currency of the BC entity; the USD is the most liquid counter-currency, with same-day spot execution and no central-bank conversion declaration requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export proceeds. A British Columbia ghost-kitchen operator may hold delivery-aggregator settlement receipts and trade proceeds in CAD, USD, or any foreign currency without restriction, and may convert or deploy them at commercial discretion. However, every Canadian financial intermediary must report to FINTRAC any electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more entering or leaving Canada within 24 hours, and the treasury operator must maintain a documented reconciliation of marketplace settlements (UberEats and DoorDash payouts) to the operating account. For physical goods crossing the border, CBSA export declarations apply under the Customs Act; for services or digital platform revenue, no export declaration is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary and financial stability authority; foreign exchange administration and exchange control policy are vested in the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-52, with operational oversight shared by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border electronic fund transfer surveillance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for trade declaration data under the Customs Act, and Statistics Canada for Balance of Payments statistical reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium (ancillary use only). Canada does not issue an EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) licence; a non-Canadian digital EMI must either partner with a Canadian Schedule I Bank or register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where operating in British Columbia, comply with the federal PCMLTFA and the British Columbia Money Services Business Act (administered by Consumer Protection BC). Platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Airwallex hold Canadian MSB registrations and can serve as secondary rails for low-value foreign vendor payments and FX conversion, but they are not chartered deposit-takers and cannot provide credit, escrow, settlement finality, or access to LVTS. The recommended treasury architecture is a primary Schedule I bank account paired with one registered MSB account used exclusively for low-value cross-border disbursements; funds must not remain as unregulated float, and the MSB should route CAD payouts through a Canadian clearing member to preserve auditability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the foreign investor initiates a single SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 interbank credit) from its home bank to the BC operating account, denominated in CAD or USD, with the field-72 narrative 'equity subscription / share capital issuance - Ghost Kitchen Operating Co.' and with no cash-splitting across dates or banks. Canada maintains no mandatory purpose-code or prior-approval regime for inbound capital, but the receiving Schedule I bank will treat the transfer as a FINTRAC reportable EFT (threshold C$10,000) and will require: (i) a statement of purpose; (ii) the share subscription agreement; (iii) a board resolution authorizing the issuance of shares; (iv) CRS/FATCA self-certification of the investor (Form NR301); and (v) if the investor is in an exit-capital-control jurisdiction, the home-state approval reference (e.g., SAFE ODI registration, RBI CAP/LRS approval, or other central bank remittance clearance) stated in field 72. Funds must not be split into smaller tranches to avoid the C$10,000 reporting threshold. Credits arrive subject to the bank's financial-crime screening; after clearance, the funds are recorded in the corporate share register and in the Canada Revenue Agency paid-up-capital tracking to avoid deemed benefit or thin-capitalization issues. For an intercompany loan, an interest-bearing note must be executed and Form T106 filed where the lender is non-arm's-length.Outbound trade settlements use four primary clearing rails. (1) SWIFT MT103 with GPI UETR for supplier invoices and intercompany payments; each wire must carry the supplier's bank details, invoice number, and goods description with HS code to satisfy the bank's electronic funds transfer record-keeping and FINTRAC reporting rules. (2) Irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) issued by the Vancouver trade-finance desk for high-value imported kitchen equipment and fit-out contracts; a confirmed LC is recommended for USD or CNY settlement with overseas suppliers. (3) Domestic CAD business-to-business payments via the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day finality on rent, payroll tax remittances, and supplier payments above C$50,000; smaller recurring disbursements may use ACSS EFT or Interac RTP. (4) Delivery-aggregator settlements (UberEats and DoorDash payouts) arrive as aggregated EFT or ACH-style credits to the designated operating account per marketplace payout terms, often daily or weekly with a 7-30 day holdback; the treasury operation must maintain a sub-ledger allocating each settlement lump into the correct virtual brand management account, with marketplace fees, driver tips, and refunds tracked separately. Cross-currency conversion is centralized in the FX facility (forwards or spot) rather than executed inside the merchant account to reduce spreads. Cash, anonymous cards, and crypto rails are prohibited in this treasury architecture.Deliverable OTC FX forward contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/CNH) under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with Schedule and Canadian Credit Support Annex, executed with a Schedule I Bank dealing desk; suitable for hedging confirmed foreign-currency supplier payables and marketplace receivables.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for hedging exposures in restricted-convertibility currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, TRY, BRL) where physical delivery is impractical; permitted as a Canadian corporate end-user under the business-purpose exemption and subject to the bank's FX middle-office credit approval.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps) for matching a CAD-denominated operating line to USD-denominated revenue received from US-based meal-delivery aggregators, executed with a qualified counterparty and compliant with OSFI and Uncleared Margin Rules (UMR) threshold requirements where applicable.Plain-vanilla European FX options and zero-premium collars (CAD/USD) purchased from a licensed bank to protect minimum settlement rates on high-volume delivery payouts.CAD/USD futures and options on futures traded on a regulated exchange (CME) through a registered futures commission merchant, with hedge-designation documentation and variation margin funding recorded in the treasury ledger.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium (ancillary use only). Canada does not issue an EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) licence; a non-Canadian digital EMI must either partner with a Canadian Schedule I Bank or register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where operating in British Columbia, comply with the federal PCMLTFA and the British Columbia Money Services Business Act (administered by Consumer Protection BC). Platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Airwallex hold Canadian MSB registrations and can serve as secondary rails for low-value foreign vendor payments and FX conversion, but they are not chartered deposit-takers and cannot provide credit, escrow, settlement finality, or access to LVTS. The recommended treasury architecture is a primary Schedule I bank account paired with one registered MSB account used exclusively for low-value cross-border disbursements; funds must not remain as unregulated float, and the MSB should route CAD payouts through a Canadian clearing member to preserve auditability.
