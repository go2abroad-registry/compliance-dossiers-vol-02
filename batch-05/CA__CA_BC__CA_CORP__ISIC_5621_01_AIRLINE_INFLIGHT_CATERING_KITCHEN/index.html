Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); additionally, British Columbia Ministry of Finance for Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration and administration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation seeking to operate in British Columbia must first obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing the applicable registration form through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or by submitting Form RC348 (GST/HST Account) and Form RC4592 (Corporation Income Tax Account). If the foreign entity is an extra-provincial company, it must also register with the BC Registry using the OneStop Business Registry and obtain a BC PST number under the Provincial Sales Tax Act. The prescribed procedure involves: (i) determining whether the non-resident is required to or voluntarily elects to register for GST/HST; (ii) obtaining a BN as the single taxpayer identifier for GST/HST, income tax, and payroll; and (iii) completing the BC PST registration for taxable sales of prepared food and tangible property in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a domestic check-the-box regime for federal or provincial business corporations; such entities are inherently taxed at the corporate level. For non-resident entities, the Canada Revenue Agency applies Foreign Entity Classification rules to determine whether an entity is a corporation or a flow-through. Relevant statutory elections include: subsection 216(4) election for non-resident persons to file a net rental return, section 256.1 election to alter the fiscal year of a corporation, subsection 93(1)(b) and (c) elections, and the hybrid mismatch counterpart rules in sections 18.2 and 18.6 which operate to deny or recharacterize certain payments involving hybrid entities. There is no general election to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity for Canadian corporate income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold). Once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is required. BC PST registration is mandatory at the first sale of taxable prepared food or tangible personal property in BC regardless of the GST small supplier threshold, subject to specific PST exemptions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under section 23 of Part I of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act for tangible personal property supplied to a non-resident purchaser who exports the property, provided the supplier retains records establishing removal from Canada. For in-flight catering, meals produced in an airport bonded kitchen and delivered directly to the aircraft of a non-resident airline are zero-rated when the food is to be consumed or resold in the course of an international flight; supplies for domestic flights remain taxable at 5% GST. Additionally, services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI where the service relates to exported goods or the supply otherwise qualifies as a service performed for the non-resident in respect of international transportation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency) or approved equivalent consent documentation
  • Air Waybill (AWB) / Bill of Lading evidencing export of the catering goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-exchange bank credit statement confirming payment repatriation
  • Signed catering service order and flight itinerary identifying the aircraft's destination
  • CRA Form GST115 or equivalent evidence of export transactions if applicable

No real-time CRA fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based clearance mandate exists in Canada. GST/HST compliance is return-based and predominantly self-assessed. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants exceeding the electronic filing threshold and for corporate income tax returns of most corporations. The federal government is actively facilitating the Peppol e-invoice framework, but adoption is voluntary. CRA may require electronic exchange of invoice data in the course of an audit, but there is no continuous transaction control or systematic pre-approval of invoices.

Under the Excise Tax Act, knowingly or negligently claiming zero-rating on non-exported supplies or fraudulently obtaining GST/HST refunds attracts civil penalties under sections 280 and 285. A false claim for a refund or input tax credit is subject to a penalty of 25% of the amount claimed, compounded with interest, and gross negligence penalties can reach 50% of the overstated net tax. Criminal prosecution under section 327 can result in fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to five years. The CRA is empowered to reassess within four years of the original assessment, but this limitation period is waived where the taxpayer has committed misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default in the manner contemplated by subsection 298(1). Retrospective clawback of input tax credits previously allowed on zero-rated exports is a routine outcome in audits where documentation is insufficient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized floating-currency regime. Neither the Currency Act nor the Bank of Canada Act imposes exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Capital injections, trade settlements, intercompany loans, and profit repatriation are freely permitted without central-bank approval or prior notification. Outbound dividends, interest, and royalties are subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), statutory rate 25%, reduced under the Canada-US Tax Treaty to 5% for qualified dividends, 0% for arm's-length interest, and 10% for royalties; the reduced rates require a valid NR301/NR4 filing and Canadian beneficial-ownership declaration via Schedule NR604. For intra-EEA or Asia-Pacific counterparties, treaty rates vary. Banking reporting obligations apply under PCMLTFA: every inbound/outbound international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more is reported by the financial institution to FINTRAC; the entity retains a secondary record-keeping duty for five years.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion requirement exists. Export proceeds earned by a British Columbia catering entity in USD, EUR, GBP, or CNY may be retained in foreign-currency bank accounts, converted at market rate, or held offshore, entirely at treasury discretion. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no repatriation timeline. However, FINTRAC STR/EFTR reporting thresholds apply for cash transactions over CAD 10,000 and international transfers over CAD 10,000; additionally, CFIA export certification for cooked airline meals may be requested by foreign aviation customs authorities, and the entity must maintain the Safe Food for Canadians Act (SFCA) licence plus HACCP records to validate the commercial nature of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and financial stability authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of Schedule I banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/CFT supervisor; Department of Finance Canada as currency and exchange control policy lead; British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) for provincial credit-union oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium as a supplementary relay, prohibited as a primary settlement venue. Canada does not recognise the EU Electronic Money Institution (EMI) passport. An offshore digital EMI, e-wallet provider, or cross-border PSP must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) or Foreign Money Services Business (FMSB) with FINTRAC and, since the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) entered force, must also register with the Bank of Canada to provide payment functions to Canadian users. For a BC inflight-catering treasury, an offshore EMI may be used only for low-value collections (weekly ordering, cashless staff meals) and must not carry aviation contract settlements, payroll, or supplier disbursements; routing such funds through an unregistered PSP triggers regulatory penalties and undermines banking relationships with Schedule I institutions. A recommended compliant hybrid is to operate a FINTRAC-registered Canadian PSP (e.g., Stripe Canada, Payouts Canada, or Wealthsimple) as a collection-only rail that sweeps automatically into the RBC or TD operational account within 24 hours.
