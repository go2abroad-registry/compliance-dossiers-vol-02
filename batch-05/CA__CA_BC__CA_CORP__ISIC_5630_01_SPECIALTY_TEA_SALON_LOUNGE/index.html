Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Agence du revenu du Canada, federal tax administration for GST/HST and corporate income tax, accessed via the CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Provincial sales tax in British Columbia is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance via the eTaxBC PST portal (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst).

E-government filing portal

Canada uses the nine-digit Business Number (BN) as the statutory tax identifier. A Canadian business corporation obtains its BN and GST/HST registration concurrently either online through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1/RC1CO (Request for Business Number and Certain Accounts). Registration must be applied for within 30 days after the small-supplier threshold is exceeded (ETA s.242(1)). The CRA issues the BN plus program accounts: RC for corporate income tax and RT for GST/HST; RP payroll and RM import/export program accounts may be added at filing. A foreign-owned Canadian corporation may complete the application electronically without a fiscal representative, but CRA reserves the power under ETA s.241 to require a non-resident registrant to post security or appoint an agent on collection-risk grounds. BC PST vendor registration is a separate filing through the BC eTaxBC portal using the same BN and is required once the corporation begins selling taxable goods or services in British Columbia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; a Business Corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the British Columbia Business Corporations Act is mandatorily taxed as an entity at the corporate level. Available elections include: (a) the GST/HST closely-related corporation election under ETA s.156, which treats taxable supplies between associated registrants as made for nil consideration and can be applied to inter-company tea-stock transfers, royalties, and management services; (b) the functional currency election under ITA s.261 for an eligible Canadian corporation reporting in a prescribed foreign currency; (c) corporate reorganization rollover elections under ITA s.85 for transfer of business assets to a related corporation; and (d) the GST/HST Quick Method accounting election under ETA s.227.1 for small registrants with annual taxable supplies not exceeding C$400,000. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss.18.4(3)-(5) are self-executing disallowance/recharacterization provisions with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 - GST/HST small-supplier threshold under ETA s.240(1). Registration is mandatory once the person's worldwide taxable supplies, including those of associated persons, exceed C$30,000 in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or, once exceeded in the current calendar quarter, thereafter until the threshold is no longer exceeded. Zero-rated export supplies count as taxable supplies for the threshold; exempt supplies do not. For BC PST there is no general monetary small-supplier threshold: registration is required upon commencing a taxable business of selling goods or providing taxable services in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a supply of tangible personal property, such as packaged ceremonial teas, loose-leaf blends, tea ware, and ceramics, is zero-rated under ETA s.19(1) where the property is exported by the supplier or the non-resident recipient as part of the supply, ordinarily within 60 days of delivery to an international carrier, and the supplier retains the prescribed customs and shipping evidence. Services: a supply of services, including online tea-culture education, custom blend consulting, and cultural salon programming, is zero-rated under ETA s.22(1) where the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST, the service is not rendered to an individual present in Canada, and the service does not relate to real property in Canada or to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; the place-of-supply rules of ETA ss.18.1-18.2 must direct the supply outside Canada. A registrant making predominantly zero-rated exports may claim full input tax credits for GST paid on inputs, including imported tea leaf, kilns, and lease costs, and may recover excess ITCs refundable in the ordinary net GST return. In-store retail tea service at the BC salon remains taxable at 5% GST plus 7% PST and is never zero-rated.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) electronic export declaration (B13A / legacy G13 equivalent)
  • Commercial invoice with HS tariff codes, quantity, unit values, and country of destination
  • Bill of Lading / Through Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill / Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Packing list and freight or courier receipt confirming handover to an international carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice evidencing repatriation of export proceeds to Canada
  • Signed non-resident declaration and GST/HST non-registration confirmation from the foreign purchaser for zero-rated services

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no government pre-stamping, and no API-based clearance obligation for GST/HST invoices. A registrant must issue a GST/HST invoice containing the vendor's Business Number, invoice date, date of supply, total consideration, and GST/HST charged, and must retain full source documentation. Electronic invoicing, including PEPPOL-standard e-invoices, is legally valid and accepted by CRA where records are complete, legible, and accessible to audit. The Government of Canada has adopted PEPPOL for federal procurement digitization, but no B2B e-invoicing mandate applies to a specialty tea salon. The BC PST regime similarly requires itemized PST disclosure on invoices and retention of sales and export records, with no fiscal clearance or digital stamping requirement.

CRA and the Canada Border Services Agency jointly verify export zero-rating claims through documentary audits, carrier record matching, and cross-border data analytics. Failure to maintain the prescribed documentation bundle causes reversal of the zero-rating and assessment of uncollected GST at 5% (plus BC PST where applicable) for the entire review period, with statutory interest from the original due dates. The normal four-year reassessment limit is extended without limitation where the registrant has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default (ETA s.298(3)). Civil penalties for false statements or omissions in returns and rebate applications under ETA s.280(1) et seq. start at C$100 and may reach 50% of the tax shortfall or rebate overclaimed; repeated or systemic diversion of export documentation can trigger aggregated penalties. Wilful evasion or fraudulent suppression of export records is prosecutable under Criminal Code s.380 as fraud, carrying imprisonment of up to 14 years. Independently, CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalty System (AMPS) penalties apply to inaccurate or non-compliant export declarations, up to C$5,000 per violation, and CRA may require security or block further zero-rated treatment pending audit cooperation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000 (goods) or CAD 500,000 (services) or CAD 250,000 (interest). Master File required if group revenue exceeds CAD 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone ESR. However, CRA applies general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions; substance is required to claim treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to 50% of tax evaded; criminal prosecution with fines up to 200% of tax and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they failed to withhold or remit; travel bans and asset seizure possible
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, penalties for negligence, and potential criminal charges

Director personal liability: Directors liable for negligence; civil restitution and potential imprisonment for fraudulent schemes
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations; imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; asset forfeiture

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; travel bans and criminal prosecution for willful blindness
