Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and pays federal corporate income tax plus BC provincial corporate tax. If more than 50% of voting/equity control is held by Canadian residents, the corporation is a Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) eligible for the federal small-business deduction (9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income) and BC small-business rate (2%); otherwise general rates apply (federal 15%, BC 12%). Shareholders are taxed on dividends and capital gains at the shareholder level; no flow-through or look-through of corporate income is available.

Available election forms

Canada has no 'check-the-box' or elective pass-through classification for corporations. The OECD hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss.18.2, 18.3, 20.2) apply on a non-elective, mandatory basis. Available elections relevant to the portal include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration before crossing the CAD 30,000 threshold; (ii) ETA s.156 election for associated corporations to align fiscal years; (iii) ETA s.273 election to treat supplies among closely related corporations as made for nil consideration; (iv) election to file GST/HST annually where net tax remittance is below prescribed thresholds; (v) ITA s.85 rollover elections on asset contributions into the corporation. None of these alter entity-level corporate taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in the current or previous four consecutive calendar quarters for GST/HST (small-supplier threshold); CAD 10,000 in BC taxable sales per calendar year for BC PST registration, or immediately upon opening a permanent establishment or place of business in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital magazine subscriptions and sponsored-media placements are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V where supplied to a non-resident person (s.7 services; s.10 intangible personal property) and the property or service is not used or enjoyed in Canada. For advertising separately, Schedule VI Part V s.17 zero-rates advertising services supplied to non-resident advertisers only where the advertisement is not directed primarily at the Canadian market; ad placements targeting Canadian audiences are taxable at 5% GST. The portal must document at the time of supply: subscriber/advertiser billing address, payment-card country, IP geolocation, and contract statements. Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline for zero-rated digital exports; the 180-day benchmark in this dossier reflects the administrative settlement expectation used for CRA audit review. Input tax credits attributable to zero-rated exports are recovered through the regular net-GST filing cycle, producing a cash refund when ITCs exceed GST collectible.

  • Executed subscription, insertion order, or media-buy contract naming the foreign customer and stating the digital nature of the magazine supply or placement;
  • Payment-processor settlement ledger (Stripe / AdSense payout records) showing currency, card-issuer or bank-account country, and inward remittance amounts matching invoices;
  • IP geolocation, billing address, and device or session logs for each subscriber or advertiser evidencing non-Canadian residence and absence of benefit in Canada;
  • Customer self-attestation or statutory declaration of non-residency, plus the portal's internal zero-rating assessment log and CRA audit-evidence file aligned with CRA export substantiation practice (GST/HST Memorandum 4.5 evidentiary principles).

No mandatory real-time tax clearance, digital stamping, or government API clearance exists in Canada; e-invoicing is voluntary and interoperability is developing on the Peppol BIS 3.0 framework. Statutory invoicing rules (ETA s.223) require every GST/HST registrant to provide a sales invoice within 15 days after the end of the month in which the supply is made, showing the vendor's BN, invoice date, nature of the supply, and GST/HST amount; the recipient's GST/HST registration number must be stated where the recipient is a registrant. For zero-rated digital exports, the invoice should expressly state 'zero-rated export supply' and the portal must retain electronic evidence of non-resident customer status.

CRA scrutinizes zero-rating claims on digital exports through subscriber counts, ad-targeting configurations, payment flows, and server-location logs. The normal GST/HST reassessment period is 4 years (ETA s.298), extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default is established. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply is reclassified as taxable in Canada and GST at 5% is assessed against the portal as supplier, with interest from the original filing due date. Penalties include: failure-to-remit penalties (ETA s.280) of 6% per annum plus 1%/2% monthly instalment penalties; gross-negligence penalties (ETA s.285) of 25% of the net tax at issue; and criminal prosecution (ETA s.327) with court-ordered fines up to double the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years on indictment. BC PST imposes separate registration and reporting penalties, including interest at the prescribed rate and a 10% administrative penalty on unreported PST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada affords full current-account and capital-account convertibility, consistent with IMF Article VIII status, with no exchange-control statute restricting cross-border transfers, foreign-currency accounts, or settlement of trade payments. Profit remittance and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are freely permitted, subject only to Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) non-resident withholding tax of 25%, which is reduced to 15% (portfolio) or 5% (substantial-holding) under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention and similarly reduced under the Canada-UK Treaty. The Canadian dollar floats freely with no official parity band, and there are no prior-approval hurdles for the repatriation of invested capital; the Investment Canada Act imposes a notification/review obligation only where the enterprise value of the Canadian business acquired reaches the prescribed WTO threshold (CAD 1.335 billion in 2024), which is inapplicable to a digital magazine portal entity unless a change-of-control transaction occurs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds, including digital-content and advertising service exports (ISIC 5813). Receipts denominated in USD, EUR, GBP, or other currencies may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts maintained at Schedule I banks or authorized foreign bank branches. The only compliance obligations are: (i) the reporting to FINTRAC of large electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more per transaction, (ii) accurate recording of foreign-currency income in the corporation's books under IFRS for CRA audit, and (iii) Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting for direct investment and service exports above the applicable threshold. No customs export declaration is triggered for digital service deliveries.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2) and the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52), is the sole authority for monetary policy and Canadian-dollar issuance. Canada maintains no standalone foreign-exchange administration; exchange-rate policy is administered by the Minister of Finance with the Bank of Canada, while prudential regulation of all deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Banking licensing is exclusively federal under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); British Columbia does not license banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary use, Low as a primary settlement rail. The compliant structure requires a primary operating account at a Schedule I Canadian bank; offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Revolut Business, Stripe Treasury) may be used only as supplementary collection or contractor-payout rails if and only if the EMI is registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). An unregistered offshore EMI cannot lawfully receive the corporation's operating revenue or advertising payouts, because channeling funds through a non-registered foreign MSB violates the PCMLTFA registration regime and creates a severe CRA audit-trail and correspondent-banking taint. For this digital publishing portal, Stripe and Google AdSense settlement proceeds should be directed by ACH/SWIFT to the Canadian bank's USD remittance sub-account, not to an offshore EMI, to preserve clean audit evidence and maximize treasury visibility.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) controlling more than 25% through a foreign parent corporation with no evident Canadian nexus.

Root cause: Non-resident ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) controlling more than 25% through a foreign parent corporation with no evident Canadian nexus.

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the foreign parent's certificate of incorporation; notarized register of directors and shareholders; UBO declaration identifying each individual with a >25% direct or indirect interest; the BC transparency register extract filed under the BC Business Corporations Act; Canadian tax-residency certificate or completed CRA NR6/NR301 forms; original bank reference letter from the parent's foreign banker dated within 6 months; source-of-funds affidavit supporting the initial capital injection.
High-volume, low-value digital advertising and subscription receipts (Stripe, AdSense Payout) that algorithmically profile as aggregation, 'money mule', or unexplained volume patterns.

Root cause: High-volume, low-value digital advertising and subscription receipts (Stripe, AdSense Payout) that algorithmically profile as aggregation, 'money mule', or unexplained volume patterns.

Defense evidence: Current editorial business plan and revenue-model narrative; three to five executed advertiser/sponsor contracts; three months of Stripe and Google AdSense settlement statements with merchant ID and domain; a logical segmentation memo distinguishing subscription revenue from sponsored-media placements; platform terms-of-service evidence; a FINTRAC-compliance note confirming CAD 10,000 large-EFT reporting thresholds and absence of transaction structuring.
Use of nominee directors, bare trusts, or opaque private holding companies obscuring beneficial control.

Root cause: Use of nominee directors, bare trusts, or opaque private holding companies obscuring beneficial control.

Defense evidence: Fully completed beneficial-ownership register per BC Business Corporations Act s.119.2; trust deed and trust UBO declaration if any trust interest exists; signed statutory declaration confirming the absence of nominees; board resolution authorizing the bank account and identifying all signatories; complete minute book from incorporation; source-of-funds confirmation for all contributed capital; letter of explanation from Canadian legal counsel validating the corporate chain.
PEP status, politically connected shareholders, or sanctions-list hits on payees (ad-tech intermediaries, foreign editorial contractors) in the outbound payment ledger.

Root cause: PEP status, politically connected shareholders, or sanctions-list hits on payees (ad-tech intermediaries, foreign editorial contractors) in the outbound payment ledger.

Defense evidence: Executed PEP self-certification for all directors, officers, and UBOs; transaction-screening report confirming no OFAC, UN, EU, or Canadian autonomous sanctions-list matches; a written low-risk classification memo based on the published digital-publishing business model; a list of counterparties with jurisdiction and licensing status of each ad-tech or content-supplier platform; Canadian counsel diligence letter describing the anticipated inbound and outbound payment flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route all shareholder or foreign-parent capital contributions via SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) with the beneficiary name matching the bank profile exactly and account number indicated; include in the remittance information field the statement 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION – SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – [Shareholder Legal Name] – [Board Resolution Date]'. Canada does not mandate a statutory FX regulatory purpose code, but the receiving Schedule I bank's enhanced due diligence will require the share subscription agreement, the directors' resolution approving the share issuance from authorized capital, the register of allotments, and a source-of-funds declaration. Wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reported automatically to FINTRAC as large electronic funds transfers; amounts below CAD 10,000 remain subject to suspicious-transaction analysis. For inbound foreign direct investment, file the ISED notification (if any) under the Investment Canada Act online; for a digital magazine portal with an enterprise value below the threshold, a no-notification position is defensible.For cross-border supplier, editorial-contractor, and ad-technology settlements, use SWIFT MT103 via the Canadian bank's correspondent network (e.g., RBC's US clearing arm, BMO Harris Bank, TD Bank N.A., or KeyBank for CIBC) with the Canadian entity's USD account and US routing details for domestic-US payout; direct debit/ACH is permissible for recurring US payees. Commercial letters of credit under UCP600 are available through the bank's trade desk but are not standard for digital-publishing services and should be reserved for high-value content-acquisition or physical print-distribution inventory. Domestic Canadian disbursements under CAD 10,000 may clear through Payments Canada's ACSS/EFT or Interac e-Transfer; high-value domestic payments use LVTS/Lynx real-time gross settlement. To avoid 30% US FATCA/backup withholding on US advertising revenue or contractor payments, file W-8BEN-E (or W-8IMY for intermediaries) with the US payors and CRA NR301/NR302 as applicable; issue T4A or T5018 to Canadian resident contractors for tax-reporting symmetry.Vanilla over-the-counter (OTC) FX forward contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) transacted with a Schedule I Canadian bank under the Canadian Foreign Exchange Forward Agreement (FEFA) or ISDA master agreement, limited to underlying revenue and liability exposures.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) offered by Canadian bank trading desks for restricted or non-convertible settlement currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, BRL) where the digital advertising platform remits in a currency with no onshore deliverable forward market.Cross-currency swaps (CCS/CRS) CAD/USD for long-dated treasury balance-sheet hedging where a US-dollar capital injection or future acquisition liability creates multi-year currency risk.European-style FX options (call/put structures and zero-premium collars) purchased for downside protection on material USD-denominated advertising revenue conversions, subject to a board-approved no-speculation treasury mandate.Foreign-currency deposit accounts (USD, EUR, GBP) maintained at the primary Schedule I bank to reduce conversion frequency, manage settlement timing on publisher payouts, and provide natural hedging of foreign-currency operating costs.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary use, Low as a primary settlement rail. The compliant structure requires a primary operating account at a Schedule I Canadian bank; offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Revolut Business, Stripe Treasury) may be used only as supplementary collection or contractor-payout rails if and only if the EMI is registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). An unregistered offshore EMI cannot lawfully receive the corporation's operating revenue or advertising payouts, because channeling funds through a non-registered foreign MSB violates the PCMLTFA registration regime and creates a severe CRA audit-trail and correspondent-banking taint. For this digital publishing portal, Stripe and Google AdSense settlement proceeds should be directed by ACH/SWIFT to the Canadian bank's USD remittance sub-account, not to an offshore EMI, to preserve clean audit evidence and maximize treasury visibility.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (15% federal + 12% British Columbia provincial general rate); reduced to 11% for qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations on active business income up to CAD 500,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII Income Tax Act) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; treaty reduction typically 15% for portfolio shareholders and 5% for corporate shareholders holding at least 10% of the voting shares under the Canada-US DTA (equivalent rates under most comprehensive Canadian DTAs).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (aggregate cross-border non-arm’s length transactions with non-residents in a taxation year triggers mandatory contemporaneous documentation under s. 247(4); T106 Information Return must be filed when transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000, regardless of revenue size).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA) and transfer-pricing rules (s. 247) apply discretionary recharacterisation where transactions lack a bona fide business purpose or where the Canadian entity lacks real substance (i.e., a mailbox affiliate or non-resident management). For Canadian federal and provincial tax purposes, a corporation must demonstrate a physical presence (office, employees, tangible/assets) to sustain a permanent establishment and to obtain treaty benefits; otherwise CRA may assess higher penalty and deny treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s. 239(1) of the Income Tax Act and s. 380(a) of the Criminal Code: fine up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalty under s. 163(2) of 50% of the federal tax avoided, plus provincial penalty; gross-fault penalty for transfer pricing misstatements can be 10% of the net adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a fraudulent return or participate in concealment may be held personally liable under s. 227.1 (failure to withhold), s. 323.1 (directors’ liability for unremitted GST/HST), and under common law of civil conspiracy; criminal convictions trigger personal fines, travel bans, and imprisonment; asset diversion may be reversed via tracing and restitution orders.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Mandatory adjustment under s. 247(2) and a 10% penalty on net transfer-pricing adjustments exceeding the arm’s length range when no contemporaneous documentation exists; unpaid taxes attract prescribed interest (daily compounding) and gross negligence may add 50% of the avoided tax under s. 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors approving the TP policy may be assessable under s. 160 (transfers of property to a non-arm’s length person) and derivative actions under corporate law; if the manipulation is part of an intentional scheme, they may face criminal conspiracy charges and extradition risk; personal travel bans and seizure of personal assets are possible upon criminal conviction.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the arrangement under GAAR (s. 245) and impose tax as if the income accrued to the Canadian resident; treaty relief is denied under Limitation on Benefits and an additional 50% gross-fault penalty; penalties can reach 200% of the federal tax in criminal cases; failure to file T106 and Part XIII returns carries daily fines.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the unremitted corporate tax if they were aware that the structure was used to avoid tax and failed to correct it; fraudulent debtors to Her Majesty may have assets traced vertically and personal bankruptcy; directors of a BC corporation face statutory liability under the Business Corporations Act for authorising an act that causes prejudice to tax obligations.
Money Laundering / Failure to Comply with AML

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC Administrative Monetary Penalty: up to CAD 2 million for a corporation per serious contravention; criminal money-laundering offence under the Criminal Code carries imprisonment up to 10 years and fine at the director’s discretion; failure to register as a money services business can attract additional fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct or allow such transactions are personally liable under the Criminal Code and may be charged with laundering proceeds of crime; they may be subject to asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment; corporate veil piercing is automatic upon proof of culpable participation.
