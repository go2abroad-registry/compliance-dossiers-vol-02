Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - primary federal tax authority for GST/HST and federal corporate income tax, administered through CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html). British Columbia provincial sales tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes).

E-government filing portal

A Canadian-resident business corporation incorporated under Canadian or BC corporate law automatically receives a federal Business Number (BN) when incorporated, subject to CRA issuance procedures. For GST/HST, the corporation must open a GST/HST program account (BN + RT account) using CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1 if not created at incorporation. A foreign entity not incorporated in Canada may obtain a BN and register for GST/HST using CRA Form RC1 and the same online registration pathway; non-resident suppliers of digital products and services may use the simplified non-resident registration process once the CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold is exceeded. A BC business corporation must also maintain a valid BC corporate registry filing and, if making taxable sales into BC, register with the Consumer Taxation Branch for PST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Worldwide_Income / No_Pass_Through_Election / No_Tax_Exempt_Zone. A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and cannot elect pass-through treatment. It is taxed on worldwide income unless a specific statutory or treaty exception applies. No preferential export zone or offshore tax-exempt regime applies to an MMORPG publisher in Canada or BC.

Available election forms

Canada does not offer check-the-box entity classification elections comparable to the United States and does not permit hybrid-mismatch election mechanics; hybrid and anti-hybrid rules under the Income Tax Conventions Interpretation Act apply automatically. Available Canadian statutory elections relevant to a corporate publisher include the subsection 85(1) rollover for incorporating eligible assets, subsection 83(2) capital dividend elections, subsection 86(1) share reorganizations, and various capital cost allowance elections under the Income Tax Act. These are timing, rollover, and capital-account elections, not entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of total worldwide taxable supplies excluding zero-rated financial services in the preceding four calendar quarters for GST/HST small supplier status; exceeding this threshold makes GST/HST registration mandatory. BC PST has no equivalent general small-supplier threshold but requires vendors making taxable sales into British Columbia to register; online marketplace facilitator rules may shift PST collection obligations to Apple App Store or Google Play for sales to BC consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, exported goods are zero-rated when exported by the supplier. Services supplied to non-resident persons are zero-rated if performed outside Canada or not supplied as personal services performed in Canada. Intangible personal property, including digital game licences, downloadable MMORPG software, in-app currencies, subscriptions, and virtual content, is zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada and when the supply is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property located in Canada. For the target commercial model, revenue receivables from Apple App Store and Google Play payouts for end customers located outside Canada and outside BC are eligible for zero-rating; customer residency, IP geolocation, platform storefront region, and terms-of-service acceptance outside Canada should be documented. BC PST does not apply to digital property delivered or accessed for use outside British Columbia.

  • Customs export declaration (CRA/Canada Border Services Agency Form B3-3) - mandatory only for physical export shipments; not required for pure digital MMORPG delivery through app stores.
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or courier manifest - required only where physical media, promotional merchandise, or physical gift cards are exported.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/currency receipt evidencing receipt of foreign-source proceeds from Apple, Google, or other app store distribution platforms.
  • Platform payout report from Apple App Store and Google Play for each reporting period, identifying end-user country, gross sales, platform commission, net remittance, settlement currency, and non-resident customer indications.
  • Non-resident substantiation records: digital receipts, IP geolocation logs, storefront region, customer residency declaration, and contract/licence terms confirming that the supply is for use outside Canada and outside British Columbia.

Canada does not currently mandate nationwide real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls for GST/HST or BC PST. CRA requires certain registrants to file GST/HST returns electronically and supports API-based e-services through My Business Account. Commercial electronic invoicing is accepted if it accurately records tax, consideration, and transaction data. Records must be retained electronically in an accessible and auditable format; no provincial clearing requirement applies in BC.

CRA may reassess within a four-year window, and without time limitation where misrepresentation, negligence, or fraud is established, to deny zero-rating, claw back claimed input tax credits, and collect GST/HST at the applicable rate plus non-deductible interest. Failure to substantiate export zero-rating with contemporaneous documents exposes the taxpayer to reassessment at 12% on BC consumer sales and at 5% GST on other Canadian supplies. Knowingly making or participating in false statements in a GST/HST return attracts a gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the tax sought to be evaded, subject to a minimum fixed penalty. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion may also be commenced under the Excise Tax Act and the Criminal Code. BC PST audits may assess unremitted PST, interest, and penalties for unregistered sellers or misdeclarations, with marketplace facilitator liability applicable for BC in-province digital sales.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Canada accepts IMF Article VIII obligations, meaning there are no exchange controls on profit remittances, dividends, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds. Outbound dividends are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (typically 25% statutory, 5% under the Canada-US treaty for 10%+ shareholders and 15% otherwise); no prior regulatory approval is required. The Bank of Canada operates a flexible exchange-rate regime and intervenes only in exceptional circumstances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no mandatory repatriation timeline or conversion ratio for export receipts. A Canadian-resident corporation may retain foreign currency earnings in any authorized foreign-currency bank account in Canada or abroad, subject to underlying debt/equity capitalization restrictions under the Income Tax Act thin-capitalization rules, which do not apply directly to FX retention, and FINTRAC reporting for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Department of Finance Canada; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates federally chartered banks, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/ATF obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections into BC corporate accounts should be routed by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank transfers) with full remitter information and a clear narrative: 'Inbound equity injection to CA_CORP, ISIC 5820, BC business corporation'. The beneficiary bank must complete a KYC verification of the corporate legal name, BC registration number, and ultimate ownership. Where inbound investment exceeds CAD 1,000,000 (or when directly asked), both the company and the non-resident investor must file the applicable Statistics Canada Foreign Direct Investment and Canadian Foreign Investment reports (ST-1 undertaking and ST-2 transaction forms, including the 'Commercial Services and International Transactions' appendix) within 30 days. For amounts above the federal foreign-funded consideration threshold, competition act notification thresholds may also apply in the case of share subscription that constitutes an acquisition of controlling interest; however, comparative BC corporate subscription is typically exempt unless the investor receives control of an existing business.Trade settlements for MMORPG infrastructure, licensing royalties, and subcontractor game-development services clear primarily via SWIFT MT103 for payments up to CAD 500,000 per transaction, with an MT103+ with structured remittance data. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (federal general corporate income tax rate 15% + British Columbia general corporate income tax rate 12%; combined rate before small business deduction and credits)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filed within six months after the corporation's fiscal period end. Balance-due day: two months after fiscal period end for most corporations; three months for a CCPC claiming the small business deduction. Instalment requirements apply when Part I tax exceeds CAD 3,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (filing threshold for Form T106 and CRA's contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation expectation for controlled non-resident transactions); Country-by-Country reporting triggers at EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue, requiring CBC form RC4648 and CRA notification.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose a standalone Economic Substance Regulations filing as in offshore centres; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) and CRA substance-over-form assessments apply. For treaty benefits and non-resident WHT relief, the shareholder must have full beneficial ownership, business purpose, and real economic activity. Conduit or invoice-only arrangements will be recharacterized and assessed, including for transfer pricing and attribution of intangible-related income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; gross negligence penalty 50% of understated tax; civil reassessments extend to 4 years, or 8 years if income not reported.

Director personal liability: Directors who instruct, participate in, or acquiesce to tax evasion face joint and several liability for penalties and interest under civil assessment; criminal conviction leads to personal fines and imprisonment; CRA can obtain a writ to seize personal assets and impose travel restrictions during prosecution.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / IP Migration

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment plus s. 247(3) penalty equal to 10% of net transfer-price adjustment; traditional reassessment with compound interest; double taxation unless mutual agreement procedure invoked under DTAs.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for corporate tax amounts in respect of unremitted source deductions if payroll/withholding involved; civil veil-piercing after insolvency; transfer-pricing penalties can become director liability only if caused by criminal fraud, but CRA may add shareholders as secondary liable for sham transactions.
Unremitted Source Deductions and Non-Resident Withholding

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 227(2): penalties equal to unpaid amounts plus interest; a director's liability certificate can be registered and enforced against personal property; failure to remit on repeated occasions can lead to criminal prosecution and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors are absolutely liable for unremitted source deductions under s. 227.1 within two years after ceasing office, without requiring proof of fault; travel restrictions may be used where criminal prosecution is initiated; no bankruptcy discharge for such director liability.
Profit Repatriation / Treaty Shopping Conduit

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty reduced rate, reassess a further 25% Part XIII tax, apply GAAR, and impose s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty at 50% of unreported tax; section 212(2) and 212(3.1) deemed-refusal rules; reasonable assessment of deemed overpayment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false treaty-relief declarations (e.g., NR301) may be subject to fines or imprisonment for false statements under s. 239(2); civil liability for unlawful distribution of capital, return of dividends, and potential personal liability under BC Business Corporations Act for distributions made when insolvent.
Money Laundering / AML Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code s. 462.31 laundering offence: up to 10 years imprisonment; Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 1,500,000 for regulated entities; FINTRAC directives; seizure and forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are liable to administrative monetary penalties if they directed or contributed to non-compliance; personal criminal liability attaches if they knowingly launder proceeds; the corporate veil will be pierced for fraud; CRA can seek civil restitution for the funds plus double damages in indictable offences; travel bans may be ordered pending criminal trial.
