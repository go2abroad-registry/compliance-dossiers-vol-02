Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), federal tax administration portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; British Columbia Ministry of Finance (Provincial Sales Tax) portal: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first incorporate as a Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the British Columbia Business Corporations Act; then apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) via the 'Register for a business number' online service or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts). The entity must open corporate income tax (RC2) and Goods and Services Tax / Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) program accounts; a payroll (RP) account is required if Canadian employees are hired. Thereafter, the corporation must register for British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) through eTaxBC before making taxable sales of goods or software in BC, and enrol for BC Employer Health Tax if remuneration exceeds CAD 500,000. A non-resident corporation with Canadian-source income must obtain a BN to file Part I corporate returns and may be subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on cross-border payments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian-resident corporation is taxed in its own right under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income, with no pass-through treatment; a non-resident corporation is taxable on Canadian-source employment, business, and capital gains income, and on Canadian-source investment income subject to Part XIII withholding; BC has no separate corporate income tax and applies the federal provincial tax allocation system.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists. Available statutory elections include: section 216 election for anon-resident to compute tax on Canadian rental/timber income on a net basis; section 217 election for non-residents to pay Canadian tax on registered retirement savings plan income; section 39(2) functional currency election to report Canadian tax results in Canadian dollars; section 18(4)/18.5 hybrid-mismatch limitation rules to deny double deduction; GST/HST elections such as section 156 associated-persons election and the quick-method election for small suppliers under the Excise Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies to non-resident digital-services suppliers making taxable supplies to Canadian consumers; BC PST registration is required for any person making taxable sales in BC, without a general small-supplier exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act applies: (a) goods physically exported by the supplier and delivered to a destination outside Canada (sections 1-2); (b) services supplied to a non-resident provided the service is performed wholly outside Canada, or if performed in Canada the foreign recipient does not consume or use the service in Canada and no supply relates to Canadian real property or tangible personal property in Canada (section 7); (c) intangible personal property, including digital game licences, software rights, and Steam/PlayStation publishing rights, supplied to a non-resident for use exclusively outside Canada and not exploited in Canada (section 10). For a BC corporate publisher, sales of digital keys or publishing services to Valve/Steam or PlayStation Store operators for international customers are zero-rated if the contract, jurisdiction-of-use clauses, and non-resident status of the licensee support use outside Canada; domestic Canadian end-user downloads remain taxable. Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is used operationally as a CRA documentation-acceptance reference, and evidence of export and receipt within a reasonable commercial period is required.

  • CRA Export Declaration (Form B13A) or Canada Border Services Agency Automated Export System (CAED/ATLAS) filing for physical goods
  • Commercial Invoice and Publishing / Distribution Licence Agreement with Valve (Steam) or PlayStation Store operator
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or electronic delivery log evidencing movement of exported goods outside Canada
  • Carrier, customs broker, or freight-forwarder proof of export certificate (date-stamped)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103, bank statement, or payment processor payout evidence showing foreign-currency receipt and conversion
  • Certificate of Residence of the foreign licensee or Form W-8BEN-E demonstrating non-resident status for Canadian tax purposes

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government e-invoicing clearance regime. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable revenue above CAD 1.5 million must file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or CRA My Business Account); all invoices must be retained as original business records. The CRA promotes voluntary PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoicing interoperability for procurement but does not mandate any specific API or pre-clearance validation. Registered non-resident digital-services suppliers file simplified quarterly GST/HST returns electronically through My Business Account.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST net tax within the normal four-year limitation period under section 298 of the Excise Tax Act; the limitation extends to ten years where a taxpayer has been negligent or careless, and is unlimited in cases of fraud or willful misrepresentation. Specific penalties include: section 280 failure to register or collect GST/HST equals 15% of unreported tax; section 280(1.1) late filing penalty of 5% of unassessed net tax; section 285 gross-negligence penalty of 50% of any misstatement or omission; section 286 penalty for false refund claims equal to 50% of the overstated refund plus arrears interest. Zero-rated export claims are screened under the CRA Export Verification and Zero-Rated Supplies audit programs; where documentary substantiation fails, the CRA disallows the zero-rating, claws back refunds and input tax credits retroactively, adds interest from the date the refund was originally paid, and may refer fraudulent export filings for criminal prosecution under section 327.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility with a freely floating Canadian dollar (no general exchange-control permit regime since repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1952). Profit remittance is unrestricted at the FX level; practical hurdles are limited to: Part XIII withholding tax on dividends (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced typically to 5%–15%), CRA non-resident payment reporting and slip filing requirements, regulatory approval only for payments touching sanctioned persons/regimes, and currency-reporting thresholds for cross-border movements of monetary instruments exceeding C$10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed repatriation timeline applies to Canadian export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in any currency and in any authorized account. Only reporting obligations apply: FINTRAC cross-border currency and monetary instrument reports (C$10,000+), financial-entity large-value transaction reports (C$10,000+), and sanctions-screening obligations; no central bank declaration is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); FX-related statutory administration co-led by FINTRAC (PCMLTFA enforcement), Global Affairs Canada (sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act and United Nations Act), and OSFI (prudential supervision of authorized banks)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder equity must route as a SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pain.001 via API banking) from the foreign parent to the BC Corporation's CAD operational account with explicit purpose code 'Direct Investment–Equity Injection' and narrative 'Paid-up share capital contribution'; the entry is recorded as increased paid-in capital on the corporate ledger. For intercompany loans, compliance requires thin-capitalization adherence (debt-to-equity not exceeding 1.5:1 effective 2024) and documented term-sheet covenants. Any receipt exceeding C$10,000 triggers the bank's FINTRAC large-value transaction report; the bank performs sanctions screening on the remitter before crediting.Steam/Valve and PlayStation Store settlement flows are inbound: USD credited via US ACH direct deposit or SWIFT MT103 into the entity's US$ ledger account, then converted to CAD via the bank's FX desk. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 10 million gross revenue and CAD 1 million cross-border transactions with non-residents (per CRA guidelines)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific ESR, but CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) to deny tax benefits from transactions lacking economic substance. Treaty benefits may be denied under Limitation on Benefits (LOB) provisions if entity lacks substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% to 200% of tax evaded, plus interest. Criminal prosecution: fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for corporate tax debts if they participated in evasion; may face civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess and apply GAAR, adding tax, interest, and penalties up to 50% of tax avoided. Treaty benefits may be denied.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for tax if they knowingly participated; may face civil penalties and potential criminal charges.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for first offense; criminal charges up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly or negligently failed to comply; may face travel bans and imprisonment.
